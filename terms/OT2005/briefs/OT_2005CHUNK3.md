# 30. Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1084; 546 U.S. 418.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of an interlocutory preliminary injunction after the Tenth Circuit affirmed en banc.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-02-21 (historical Supreme Court calendar date used for scheduling).
* **Question Presented:** Whether RFRA permits the Federal Government to prohibit this church’s sacramental use of hoasca on general drug-control and treaty-compliance grounds, and which party bears the risk of evidentiary equipoise at the preliminary-injunction stage.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit, 389 F.3d 973, affirming preliminary protection against federal enforcement; the merits action remains pending.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks dissolution of the injunction; the church seeks continued protection for its regulated sacramental practice pending final adjudication.
* **Material Record:** The approximately 130-member American church uses tea containing Schedule I DMT. Customs seized a shipment and threatened prosecution. At the injunction hearing the Government conceded sincere exercise and substantial burden. Evidence about health risks and diversion was found evenly balanced. The injunction permits controlled religious use with regulatory safeguards. The record includes an existing federal peyote accommodation and general diplomatic declarations concerning the 1971 Convention.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The enforcement threat and seizure establish a concrete prospective dispute. Review concerns preliminary relief, not final entitlement to an unconditional exemption. The concession was made below; no oral-argument concession is assumed. Federal RFRA’s constitutional validity is not presented as a preserved independent question.
* **Relevant Dates:** Customs seizure in 1999; appellate en banc judgment in 2004; argument November 1, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Gonzales v. Oregon concerns the scope of administrative authority over medical practice, not RFRA’s individualized statutory defense. People of Guam v. Guerrero involved a disputed acquisition burden; the substantial burden here is conceded.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any actually adopted earlier same-term rule on administrative drug-control authority must be checked. Gonzales v. Oregon concerns a distinct statutory issue.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The applicable jurisdiction, review, preservation and proof requirements are stated below.

### Issue 1: Individualized RFRA justification and preliminary proof

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** People of Guam v. Guerrero requires proof of actual religious exercise and substantial burden, followed by governmental production and persuasion on a person-specific compelling interest and least restrictive means. Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization v. City of Clearwater and Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission supply that statutory sequence within valid fields of operation. City of Boerne v. Flores limits universal state and local RFRA coverage under section 5 while leaving federal RFRA outside that invalidation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The claimant proves coverage, sincerity and substantial burden; government must justify this application. Neither a drug-control label nor speculative lawful alternatives replaces evidence. Cutter v. Wilkinson preserves denominational neutrality, concrete institutional interests and others’ rights when administering statutory accommodation.
* **Other Governing Law:** RFRA, 42 U.S.C. §§2000bb-1 and 2000bb-3; the Controlled Substances Act; the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances. RFRA’s enactment responded to Employment Division v. Smith, whose neutral-law constitutional rule survives under governing law. RFRA supplies added statutory protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union addresses proof and less restrictive alternatives at the preliminary stage; its speech holding does not itself grant a drug exemption.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The individualized merits burden is settled. General applicability is no statutory defense.
* **Open Point:** How that burden operates when the preliminary record is evenly balanced, and whether this treaty and regulatory evidence meets it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Tenth Circuit sustained protection because the Government failed its application-specific evidentiary burden. The Government argues that Schedule I findings, diversion concerns, administrability and international commitments establish a compelling need for uniform prohibition; it also contends the preliminary-relief applicant must demonstrate likely success. The church answers that the acknowledged religious burden and the Government’s evenly balanced defense establish that likelihood, while the peyote exception undermines an asserted categorical impossibility of accommodation. The church’s rule still permits restriction where the Government proves a compelling application-specific danger and inadequate alternatives. The United States is the petitioner; no separate independently verified amicus proposal is necessary to identify its rule.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

One path sustains the injunction because the burden of persuasion at trial remains relevant to likelihood of success and undifferentiated regulatory concerns do not establish this defense. Its scope would extend RFRA’s existing allocation to preliminary proof, while leaving final findings and suitably supported restrictions open. Another dissolves or remands the injunction if the courts misallocated proof or failed to evaluate legally material treaty obligations and safeguards. That path would require identifying the actual error, not treating all controlled substances as automatically exempt from RFRA. Neither route establishes harmlessness of the drug, excuses diversion or gives every claimant the same exemption. The legal reach of the treaty and the evidence supporting future enforcement remain distinct. The verified materials do not support a final factual finding of universal safety.

# 31. Buckeye Check Cashing, Inc. v. Cardegna

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Buckeye Check Cashing, Inc. v. Cardegna
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1264; 546 U.S. 440.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the Florida Supreme Court’s final resolution of the federal arbitration question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-02-21 (historical Supreme Court calendar date used for scheduling).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a court or an arbitrator decides a challenge that a signed contract containing an arbitration clause is illegal and void as a whole, when the claimant does not separately attack agreement to the arbitration provision.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Florida Supreme Court, 894 So.2d 860, holding that the court must decide the asserted illegality before compelling arbitration; it reversed an intermediate appellate ruling.
* **Relief Requested:** Buckeye seeks enforcement of arbitration; the borrowers seek judicial adjudication of the asserted usury and invalidity.
* **Material Record:** Borrowers signed deferred-deposit agreements exchanging personal checks for cash plus finance charges. Each agreement contained broad arbitration terms. Their putative class complaint asserts unlawful interest and violations of Florida lending and consumer-protection laws. The challenge is to the transaction as a whole; the reported record identifies no forgery, lack of signature or distinct invalidity of the arbitration clause.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** State-court finality concerns the conclusively resolved federal arbitration issue, not a completed merits trial. Doctor’s Associates v. Casarotto preserves the relevant finality route without making all arbitration orders appealable. The existence of assent must not be silently converted into a mere validity objection. No factual finding of usury is identified.
* **Relevant Dates:** Florida Supreme Court decision in 2005; argument November 29, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Sarhank Group v. Oracle Corp. concerns whether a nonsignatory parent ever agreed to arbitrate, a foundational question different from illegality of an acknowledged signed transaction.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The applicable jurisdiction, review, preservation and proof requirements are stated below.

### Issue 1: Severability and allocation of whole-contract illegality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson makes FAA section 2 operative in state court while preserving generally applicable contract defenses. First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan and Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. require independent resolution of genuinely disputed foundational assent absent an established delegation. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle assigns procedural interpretation within an accepted broad submission to the arbitrator.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify an actual agreement and the challenge before allocating it. Arbitration receives neither a special formation preference nor a state-law disability. Substantive scope and foundational assent differ from administration of agreed arbitration.
* **Other Governing Law:** FAA §§2–4; Florida usury and contract law. Prima Paint Corp. v. Flood & Conklin Manufacturing Co. supplies the surviving severability rule for whole-contract fraud; Southland Corp. v. Keating’s state-court operation is retained by Allied-Bruce.
* **Material Other Authority:** PacifiCare Health Systems v. Book leaves interpretation of ambiguous remedial restrictions to the agreed forum before assuming statutory rights have been eliminated.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** State courts must honor covered agreements and ordinary contract defenses. A court must first establish that an arbitration bargain exists.
* **Open Point:** Whether the same severability rule reaches a whole-contract illegality theory described by state law as void from inception, rather than merely voidable.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Florida’s court treated criminal illegality as antecedent to an enforceable contract. Borrowers argue that section 2 cannot enforce a provision embedded in a nonexistent legal contract and that ordinary contract law preserves judicial review. Buckeye argues that the objection attacks the financing terms, not the separable arbitration bargain, and seeks the same allocation for void and voidable whole-contract claims. Buckeye must still establish an agreement and statutory coverage; borrowers retain their substantive illegality defense in the proper forum. Neither side can obtain a determination of the usury merits merely by winning the allocation question. No separate verified government proposal changes these paths.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may require arbitration of the whole-contract defense after distinguishing it from disputed assent and clause-specific invalidity. That would establish a generally applicable allocation rule, not validate illegal loans. It may instead hold that the asserted illegality defeats the existence of any enforceable arbitration bargain under section 2 and return the case for judicial resolution. A narrower remand is available if the complaint actually contains a preserved foundational or clause-specific defense omitted from the reported analysis. The available record identifies no such defense; one must not be invented. Class-procedure authority and monetary liability remain separate.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Prima Paint Corp. v. Flood & Conklin Manufacturing Co. extended from fraud to whole-contract illegality. Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson reaffirmed. First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan and Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. preserved for foundational assent.

# 32. Ministry of Defense and Support for Armed Forces of the Islamic Republic of Iran v. Elahi

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ministry of Defense and Support for Armed Forces of the Islamic Republic of Iran v. Elahi
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1095; 546 U.S. 450.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the appellate attachment-immunity ruling, limited to the identified FSIA distinction.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-02-21 (historical Supreme Court calendar date used for scheduling).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Ninth Circuit could permit attachment under the provision for an agency or instrumentality without deciding whether Iran’s Defense Ministry is the foreign state itself, and without a meaningful opportunity to litigate that distinction.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 385 F.3d 1206, sustaining a lien on an arbitral-award judgment under 28 U.S.C. §1610(b)(2), after rejecting the district court’s waiver rationale.
* **Relief Requested:** The Ministry seeks removal of the attachment; Elahi seeks access to the award to satisfy his terrorism-related judgment.
* **Material Record:** Elahi holds a substantial default damages judgment against Iran for the alleged murder of his brother. The Ministry obtained a Swiss arbitral award against Cubic and secured federal confirmation in California. Elahi intervened to attach that asset. The district court relied on waiver by the Ministry’s confirmation suit. The Ninth Circuit used an agency-or-instrumentality commercial-activity exception instead. Elahi’s brief conditionally mentioned that exception; the Ministry did not concede its own legal status.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Execution immunity is separate from jurisdiction over the original damages suit and confirmation proceeding. The appellate court’s alternative ground requires legally and factually established predicates and fair notice. Terrorism allegations and a valid damages judgment do not alone establish an execution exception. No later statutory amendment or subsequent Elahi litigation may be imported.
* **Relevant Dates:** California confirmation judgment in 2002; Ninth Circuit decision in 2004. Statutory law is fixed as of February 21, 2006.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Sarhank Group v. Oracle Corp. concerns consent to foreign arbitration, not execution immunity of sovereign assets. Neither confirms the other’s predicates.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The applicable jurisdiction, review, preservation and proof requirements are stated below.

### Issue 1: Foreign-state status and the applicable execution exception

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Republic of Argentina v. Weltover and Saudi Arabia v. Nelson distinguish commercial activity by its nature and require the nexus enacted for the particular exception. Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson enforces section 1603’s actual instrumentality conditions. None converts merits or adjudicatory jurisdiction into execution authority.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the particular FSIA category and statutory nexus; identify the relevant sovereign entity and property rather than relying on an undifferentiated government-related label.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1603, 1609 and 1610 in their then-effective versions. Section 1610(a) addresses property of a foreign state used for commercial activity; section 1610(b) separately addresses qualifying property of an agency or instrumentality engaged in commercial activity. Section 1609 supplies the baseline immunity from attachment and execution.
* **Material Other Authority:** Transaero v. La Fuerza Aerea Boliviana treats armed forces as ordinarily integral to a foreign state; this is persuasive lower-court authority, not a controlling holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The correct exception and its distinct conditions must be established. A litigant cannot concede the opponent’s disputed status for it.
* **Open Point:** The Ministry’s legal classification on a properly developed record and the resulting availability of any preserved execution exception.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court relied on waiver; the Ninth Circuit rejected waiver and affirmed under section 1610(b). The Ministry maintains that a defense ministry is the state itself and that its property therefore must satisfy section 1610(a)’s property-use requirement. Elahi supports attachment and argues the commercial award and confirmation activity suffice under the statutory exception. His conditional footnote does not establish an opposing concession. The Solicitor General proposes vacatur and remand to determine whether the Ministry is an inseparable part of the state, emphasizing the different statutory texts. That recommendation would remove the existing appellate justification without deciding all grounds for attachment.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance requires a supported classification and satisfaction of the right exception, with no unfair reliance on an unbriefed premise. Vacatur permits adversarial adjudication of status and preserved exceptions. A final immunity judgment is possible only if the record and preserved alternatives conclusively establish entitlement; the reported materials do not establish that. The plausible rule concerns correct entity classification and property-specific execution conditions, not immunity for all commercial awards or an automatic terrorism exception. The actual asset, sanctions treatment and any independent waiver must remain separately proved.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Schering-Plough Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** 402 F.3d 1056.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of an appellate judgment setting aside an FTC competition order.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-03-06 (assigned scheduling date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a patent settlement restraining generic entry is insulated from antitrust inquiry merely because the restraint falls within the patent’s asserted scope, and whether the FTC’s actual findings support its order under the proper legal standard.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 402 F.3d 1056, granting the manufacturers’ petitions for review and setting aside the FTC order. The Commission had reversed its administrative law judge’s dismissal.
* **Relief Requested:** The FTC seeks restoration of its competition order; Schering and Upsher seek to retain the judgment setting it aside.
* **Material Record:** Schering held a patent covering an extended-release potassium-chloride formulation. Settlements with Upsher and ESI fixed generic-entry dates before patent expiration and included transfers of value. The Upsher transaction also licensed other products. The ALJ credited commercial explanations and found insufficient proof of unlawful exclusion. The Commission treated payments as consideration for delayed entry and imposed prospective restrictions. The appellate court relied on patent scope and rejected the Commission’s economic inferences. Whether compensation bought genuine assets, avoided litigation costs, or purchased exclusion is contested, not an established fact common to both decisions.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review concerns the Commission’s final order and the judicial-review record, not a fresh patent-infringement trial. Legal classification is reviewed independently; agency facts receive statutory substantial-evidence review on the whole record, including contrary ALJ findings. The record must distinguish the two settlements. The Commission’s general remedial prohibition requires its own reasonable relation to a proved violation.
* **Relevant Dates:** Commission decision December 8, 2003; appellate decision March 8, 2005; listed Supreme Court event March 6, 2006 is organizationally assigned.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Illinois Tool Works concerns actual tying-market power. Its earlier listed date supplies no effective ruling. Patent-antitrust rules already effective in CSU v. Xerox and Monsanto v. McFarling apply independently.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An actual effective Illinois Tool Works decision could clarify patent-related proof, but none is identified. Texaco v. Dagher’s separate venture issue is not an adjudicated settlement precedent.

### Issue 1: Patent settlement and competitive restraint

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** CSU v. Xerox rejects conclusive patent-scope immunity and an exhaustive list of exceptional misconduct as the only entrance to antitrust review. Monsanto v. McFarling likewise requires ordinary antitrust elements notwithstanding an asserted lawful patent scope. Patent exclusion supplies a substantial justification, not automatic proof of every competitive consequence or immunity for an agreement with a potential rival. California Dental Association v. Federal Trade Commission requires an inquiry suited to the restraint and the credible explanation; State Oil v. Khan confines automatic categories to supported experience.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual exclusion, competitive alternatives and justification. The agency bears the elements of the invoked offense. Neither ownership of a patent nor payment to settle litigation alone establishes market power, unlawful exclusion or competitive injury.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sherman Act §1, FTC Act §§5 and 5(c), and the applicable patent and Hatch-Waxman framework. Settlement may conserve resources and license genuine technology, but private agreement cannot conclusively establish validity, infringement and the lawful counterfactual against outsiders.
* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the controlling patent-antitrust decisions and enacted framework identified above.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The categorical immunity defense is foreclosed. Ordinary liability requirements and substantial patent justifications remain.
* **Open Point:** How the particular payments, entry limits and asset valuations establish or rebut actual anticompetitive exclusion on this administrative record.

### Issue 2: Evidence and the Commission’s remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** California Dental Association requires a reasoned evidentiary connection between demonstrated conduct and competitive effects. It permits abbreviated proof where justified but does not authorize unsupported assumptions or judicial substitution for agency factfinding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Preserve supported agency factfinding while independently reviewing its legal premise and the lawful scope of relief.
* **Other Governing Law:** FTC Act §5(c) and Universal Camera require substantial evidence on the whole record. An ALJ’s credibility findings matter without becoming an automatic veto on the Commission. Agency remedial discretion depends on an established violation and justified prevention of recurrence.
* **Material Other Authority:** Universal Camera requires substantial-evidence review of the whole administrative record, including the significance of the hearing examiner’s findings.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Patent scope cannot truncate whole-record review of the Commission’s actual findings.
* **Open Point:** Whether the settlement-specific findings and prospective restrictions survive the correct legal framework.


## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Commission argues that substantial value transferred to a potential rival in return for delayed competition warrants scrutiny even if entry occurs before asserted patent expiration. It treats the ancillary licenses as insufficient explanations for the transfers and emphasizes the competitive opportunity surrendered. The manufacturers invoke the patent’s lawful exclusion, litigation uncertainty, negotiated earlier entry and valuable licensed products. They argue that the Commission imagined a costless earlier settlement without evidentiary support. The ALJ accepted important commercial testimony; the Commission drew contrary inferences; the appellate court both protected patent scope and criticized proof. Each disagreement must be separated rather than treating the existence of one as resolving the others. No adjudication of patent invalidity or noninfringement is assumed.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can rest on a properly reviewed failure of substantial evidence, but not categorical scope immunity. Reversal and automatic reinstatement of the FTC order would require resolution of the independent evidentiary and remedial objections. Vacatur and remand can require the appellate court to review the actual Commission findings under the governing noncategorical rule, with further agency proceedings only where ordinary review permits them. The Court may reject both per se illegality of every reverse payment and per se legality within asserted patent scope. The immediate uncertainty is the sufficiency of this record, not whether antitrust law may examine a patent-related agreement at all.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Patent scope does not establish settlement immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The legal immunity premise is reviewable on the existing administrative record. Ultimate liability remains tied to proved offense elements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether asserted patent scope conclusively protects a payment-and-entry settlement from antitrust review.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply CSU v. Xerox and Monsanto v. McFarling to the actual agreement, retaining the substantial justification afforded lawful patent exclusion.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule reaches patent settlements restraining potential competition, not every litigation payment or an automatic validity trial. A narrower drug-specific exception would leave the same erroneous immunity intact elsewhere; a broader presumption of illegality would discard genuine settlement and licensing benefits.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An agreement restricting a potential competitor’s entry does not receive conclusive antitrust immunity merely because its duration and product coverage fall within a patent’s asserted scope. The agreement must be examined under the otherwise applicable antitrust elements, with lawful patent exclusion and genuine settlement or licensing benefits given their demonstrated weight. This appellate judgment must be reconsidered under that rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The settlement restraints actually challenged and their competitive justification. Patent validity is not adjudicated simply by rejecting immunity.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Examine the payment, entry restraint and supported explanations together. Neither patent scope nor payment alone decides liability; actual purchased exclusion is subject to the ordinary offense elements.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A patent authorizes legally established exclusion; a settlement reflects private expectations and bargaining over uncertain rights. Those are related but not identical. CSU v. Xerox and Monsanto already reject transformation of the statutory right into a complete answer to independently alleged anticompetitive conduct. At the same time, a transfer can compensate for real assets or avoided expense, and an agreed date may improve access. Actual evidence must distinguish those explanations from purchased suppression.
* **Precedent Treatment:** CSU v. Xerox and Monsanto v. McFarling reaffirmed. California Dental Association applied to select the required evidentiary inquiry. Any contrary categorical rule in Valley Drug is rejected as lower-court law inconsistent with the controlling rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: The record and remedy need separate lawful review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Commission and ALJ disagreed materially. A legal correction does not itself establish the Commission’s version of the facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What follows for evidence review and the FTC order after eliminating patent-scope immunity.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Preserve whole-record substantial-evidence review and a remedy proportionate to proved competition violations.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this administrative disposition. A broader universal payment ceiling would require legislative or adequately justified adjudicative support absent here. A narrower reinstatement would bypass preserved objections.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A reviewing court rejecting patent-scope immunity must still determine whether each material Commission finding is supported by substantial evidence on the whole record and whether the order reasonably addresses the proved violation; neither ALJ disagreement nor judicial skepticism permits an unreasoned substitution of factfinding. Remand is required for that analysis here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The two settlement records and the challenged prospective restrictions, including the treatment of genuine consideration and litigation costs.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Review all material evidence, including evidence credited by the ALJ, under the substantial-evidence standard. Prospective restrictions must reasonably address the established violation and risk of recurrence.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The contested license valuation and inferred alternative entry dates matter to causation and justification. The whole record includes evidence supporting the commercial explanations as well as the transfer structure. The FTC may draw supported economic inferences, but an assumed better bargain is not evidence simply because a payment occurred. A categorical ban on receiving value also requires a reasoned connection to the unlawful conduct actually established.
* **Precedent Treatment:** California Dental Association and Universal Camera applied; no displacement of statutory fact-review deference.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Missing complete lawful review of contested findings: ultimate competitive effects and the precise permissible prospective restrictions require whole-record review under the corrected legal premise.
