# 77. House v. Bell

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** House v. Bell
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-8990; judgment below, 386 F.3d 668.
* **October Term:** 2005.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of denial of a first federal habeas petition after an evidentiary hearing and an en banc appellate judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-06-12. Historical calendar placement.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the supplemented record satisfies the Schlup crime-innocence gateway and what showing of factual innocence can support freestanding constitutional habeas relief.
* **Question Granted:** The merits question stated above; no broader question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit, en banc, affirmed denial of relief, treating the new evidence as insufficient to overcome default or establish an independent innocence entitlement. The district court had heard the new evidence and made historical and credibility findings.
* **Relief Requested:** House seeks consideration of his defaulted constitutional claims and independently a writ on innocence grounds. Tennessee seeks affirmance, emphasizing the surviving circumstantial case, credibility findings and the distinction between undermining proof and affirmatively demonstrating innocence.
* **Material Record:** House was convicted of Carolyn Muncey's 1985 murder and sentenced to death. Trial evidence included semen thought consistent with House and the victim's blood on his jeans. Later DNA testing identifies her husband as the semen source. New testimony and chain-of-custody problems cast serious doubt on how blood reached the jeans; missing autopsy blood and evidence of spillage support a contamination theory without establishing deliberate planting. Other witnesses recount the husband's abuse and admissions, with credibility disputed. Evidence against House remains: his presence near the concealed body, inconsistent accounts, his departure from his girlfriend's home that night, and injuries. The changed sexual-assault evidence matters to the overall identity narrative and an aggravating circumstance, although sexual contact is not an element of murder.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Gateway passage permits adjudication of an otherwise defaulted constitutional claim; it does not itself grant a writ. This is not a second or successive federal application. Statutory limits on receiving new evidence cannot be converted without their predicates into a higher burden governing an existing evidentiary record. The State's asserted deference and clear-and-convincing arguments must be kept distinct from the legal gateway determination.
* **Relevant Dates:** Murder: July 1985. Trial: February 1986. Federal application: September 30, 1996. En banc judgment: October 6, 2004. Argument: January 11, 2006. No development after the assigned merits date is used.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion requires an additional brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-06-12; precedent through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None established as necessary to decide the stated question under entering law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 2254 supplies the state-custody channel. Its merits restrictions, exhaustion and applicable factual presumptions retain their actual predicates. Schlup addresses procedural default; §2244(b)'s amended successive-application rules address a different filing category. Underlying constitutional merits and any preserved retroactivity defense remain separate from gateway success.

### Issue 1: The whole-record crime-innocence gateway

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Schlup v. Delo requires reliable new evidence and a showing that, considering the whole probative record, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. Carriger v. Stewart applies that inquiry while separately preserving supported state factual findings and the claim's proper legal regime. Neither case equates the gateway with acquittal, a proved constitutional violation or the reviewing judge's own reasonable doubt.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess old and new evidence together, including probative impeachment and material not admitted at trial, while evaluating reliability, delay and credibility. Do not require the prisoner to eliminate every incriminating circumstance separately. A possible acquittal is insufficient; the inquiry concerns the likely conduct of every reasonable juror under the proper reasonable-doubt instruction.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§2254 and 2244(b). The amended latter provision's clear-and-convincing and diligence requirements for a new successive application do not replace the Schlup default gateway. Section 2254(e)(2) conditions a qualifying request for a federal evidentiary hearing; it is not a universal higher innocence standard.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sawyer v. Whitley concerns factual ineligibility for death, a distinct gateway with a distinct burden. Jackson v. Virginia addresses trial-record sufficiency, not the impact of reliable new evidence on reasonable jurors.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Schlup and Carriger establish the governing crime gateway. Sawyer, statutory successive-application screening and hearing restrictions remain separate predicates rather than alternative versions of that same test.
* **Open Point:** Whether this record meets the gateway notwithstanding surviving incriminating evidence, and whether the lower court's underlying findings prevent an independent legal determination of that question.

### Issue 2: Freestanding innocence as a substantive constitutional claim

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Herrera v. Collins supplies only a majority-supported rejection of that record even assuming sufficiently persuasive innocence could bar execution. It neither recognizes nor rejects a general freestanding claim and establishes no controlling uniform burden. Carriger v. Stewart preserves that reservation. No uniform merits burden is settled.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A new freestanding entitlement must be identified as new and must distinguish affirmative factual innocence from the procedural gateway. Jurisdiction, an available claim and entitlement to relief remain separate; a substantive protection does not itself erase statutory or procedural conditions.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments and §2254 provide the constitutional and remedial setting. Teague's substantive-punishment exception is distinct from its new-procedure rule. Schriro v. Summerlin preserves that distinction: a class-of-persons punishment restriction is different from changing who makes an eligibility finding.
* **Material Other Authority:** Herrera's separate writings may inform competing proposed rules, but no common general test may be manufactured by combining them. A federal finding of new reasonable doubt does not alone establish affirmative innocence.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law leaves the existence and precise burden of this freestanding claim open. It does establish that the gateway and ultimate writ are different inquiries.
* **Open Point:** Whether to recognize the claimed substantive protection, the prisoner's burden and remedy, and whether the present evidence satisfies that burden.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

House argues that the new forensic evidence and evidence concerning the husband, considered together, satisfy Schlup and also warrant independent innocence relief. His merits brief relies on the rational-juror reasoning of Herrera's separate concurrence for the latter claim. Tennessee emphasizes the injuries, inconsistent accounts, discovery-area conduct and adverse credibility findings. It argues that new evidence need not be taken at face value and invokes statutory clear-and-convincing formulations. Herrera’s limited holding and the separate statutory categories govern those arguments.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can find the gateway met and remand for constitutional merits while separately rejecting a freestanding writ. It can find the whole record insufficient and affirm default-based denial, provided it uses the proper probability inquiry rather than a requirement to rebut every circumstance. It can recognize a freestanding right with a stated burden and resolve or remand its application; an immediate writ requires both an adequate innocence finding and satisfaction of independent remedy conditions. Any course must distinguish review eligibility from adjudicated innocence.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The developed evidence supports an actual gateway decision. The expressly granted freestanding question permits a proposed substantive rule and burden, but does not justify inventing factual findings or automatic statutory exceptions.

**Material Uncertainty:** Blood contamination and the husband's alleged admissions remain evidence to evaluate, not established prosecutorial fabrication or a judicial finding identifying the killer. The complete merits of the defaulted constitutional claims have not been adjudicated here. Schlup’s gateway does not displace the distinct successive-application restrictions, which do not govern this first petition. No oral-argument concession is attributed without a verified transcript.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the denial insofar as it refuses the Schlup gateway and remand for the underlying constitutional claims; deny an immediate freestanding innocence writ on the present record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court must adjudicate properly available constitutional claims under their actual standards and identify any genuinely remaining independent restriction. A proved constitutional violation receives its proper writ remedy. The gateway ruling alone supplies no retrial, unconditional release or automatic execution stay.
* **Remedial Reach:** The remand covers House's petition and custody. No class proceeding or proved statewide policy is before the Court.

If a qualifying violation is established, its writ must specify what Tennessee must correct and when custody must end absent lawful correction. No date or merits finding is fabricated here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The whole-record crime-innocence gateway

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the crime gateway on the developed record. Preserve historical findings without treating a district judge's ultimate confidence in guilt as the statutory or Schlup question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a conviction may remain procedurally insulated when reliable new evidence changes the force of its central forensic and circumstantial foundations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing Schlup probability standard and Carriger's separation of factual findings from the whole-record legal inquiry.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: restate and apply the existing gateway to materially revised forensic proof. A broader rule that any major evidentiary contradiction automatically opens review would eliminate the demanding whole-record burden. A narrower requirement to negate each remaining circumstance would turn the gateway into an absolute-innocence test the governing law rejects.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prisoner satisfies the crime-innocence gateway when reliable new evidence, assessed with the entire probative record, makes it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. House carries that burden: the forensic revisions and supported alternative-perpetrator evidence together alter the case's overall force despite remaining grounds for suspicion. He may pursue his defaulted constitutional claims under their independently applicable requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns defaulted crime claims, not merely sentence-selection mitigation, and requires no categorical acceptance of recantations or new expert opinions. It does not change a separate statutory gate where that gate actually applies.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts evaluate the changed force of the entire prosecution narrative; they may not require separate elimination of every incriminating circumstance or treat gateway passage as a writ. The applicable statutory merits and evidentiary conditions remain independent.
* **Transition:** Not applicable; no departure from the entering transition rules is proposed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Schlup deliberately separates a new-evidence probability assessment from Jackson's trial-record sufficiency inquiry. Carriger preserves that separation even where credibility findings require respect. The DNA result removes the asserted semen link, while the blood-handling evidence makes reliance on that second forensic link substantially less secure. The new accounts concerning the husband must be weighed critically, not presumed true, but their significance changes when considered with those forensic revisions. House's inconsistent explanations and suspicious presence remain in the analysis. They do not, on the whole supplemented record, restore the strength needed for a reasonable finding beyond reasonable doubt. This conclusion opens review; it does not declare who committed the murder.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Schlup v. Delo and Carriger v. Stewart without changing their burden. Preserve Sawyer v. Whitley's separate eligibility test and Jackson v. Virginia's trial-record function. The three bounded gate, finding and statutory rules retain distinct predicates; Stone would not merge them into a single heightened innocence test.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying constitutional claims and their remedy require merits adjudication and findings not supplied by the gateway ruling. Independent statutory restrictions remain for the lower court only where their actual predicates are established.

### Issue 2: Freestanding innocence as a substantive constitutional claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the expressly granted freestanding question, while denying an immediate writ on this submission. No state merits adjudication, waiver or unexhausted remedy is invented to avoid the question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What reliable showing of factual innocence makes continued punishment unconstitutional through an otherwise available habeas channel.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose a new substantive holding in the space Herrera reserved: a lawful conviction cannot authorize continued punishment after affirmative factual innocence is established by clear and convincing evidence. Herrera did not adopt this rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad: recognize the substantive limit and give it a definite burden and remedy within the presented innocence claim. A broader rule converting every successful Schlup showing into a writ would confuse reasonable doubt with affirmative innocence. A narrower refusal ever to recognize relief would permit reliable proof of noncommission to have no legal consequence.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Continued criminal punishment is unconstitutional when, through an otherwise available habeas proceeding, the prisoner proves by clear and convincing evidence on the whole reliable record that he did not commit the offense. The burden rests on the prisoner and requires affirmative factual innocence, not simply evidence likely to prevent proof beyond reasonable doubt at a new trial. A court making that determination must end custody under the invalid conviction, subject only to independently lawful custody. House's present submission satisfies the distinct Schlup gateway but does not establish affirmative noncommission to that higher degree, so no freestanding writ issues now.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The protection concerns factual innocence of the punished offense, rather than disagreement with sentence selection or proof of trial error alone. It operates through an available habeas channel and supplies no new successive-application authorization or original jurisdiction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Reliable scientific evidence may establish noncommission alone or with other proof. A confession by the actual perpetrator is not required. The substantive rule neither supplies successive-petition authorization nor dispenses with an independently applicable habeas limitation.
* **Transition:** The proposed restriction concerns persons substantively beyond the State's lawful punishment power, not a new trial procedure. Stone would apply the entering substantive-rule retroactivity principle, without treating retroactivity as satisfaction of §2254(d) or authorization to reopen every final case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Herrera's narrow record ruling cannot be enlarged into either a categorical denial or a fixed standard. Finality protects lawful adjudication, but cannot itself authorize punishment after reliable proof that the prisoner did not commit the crime. Clear-and-convincing affirmative innocence marks that substantive finding and keeps it distinct from Schlup's probability of reasonable acquittal. Here the prosecution's proof is seriously undermined, but the unresolved reliability of the alternative-perpetrator accounts and surviving circumstantial evidence prevent the separate affirmative conclusion. The proper immediate consequence is constitutional-merits review through the gateway, not an innocence-based release order.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Resolve Herrera v. Collins's reservation without overruling its record-specific denial; preserve Carriger v. Stewart's distinction between gateway and writ. Apply Schriro v. Summerlin's substantive/procedural distinction to the new proposal rather than borrow its separate Ring watershed holding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent statutory obstacles in differently situated, successive or state-merits-adjudicated applications lie outside this first-application question and lack the necessary case-specific predicates here. No general exemption from those obstacles is adopted.

# 78. Hill v. McDonough

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hill v. McDonough
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-8794; judgment below, 437 F.3d 1084.
* **October Term:** 2005.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of dismissal of a civil-rights challenge to an execution protocol as an unauthorized successive habeas application.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-06-12. Historical calendar placement.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a condemned prisoner's challenge to Florida's particular lethal-injection protocol may proceed under §1983, and whether identifying a specific alternative protocol is a prerequisite to that procedural route.
* **Question Granted:** The merits question stated above; no broader question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed dismissal for lack of jurisdiction after treating Hill's complaint as the functional equivalent of a successive habeas petition filed without appellate authorization.
* **Relief Requested:** Hill seeks reinstatement of his civil action and consideration of injunctive protection against the challenged protocol. Florida seeks affirmance; the United States supports it and additionally invokes untimeliness and administrative exhaustion.
* **Material Record:** After earlier federal habeas proceedings, Hill sued Florida officials under §1983 four days before the January 24, 2006 scheduled execution. He alleges that the three-drug sequence risks gratuitous severe pain through inadequate anesthesia followed by paralysis and cardiac arrest. He challenges that manner of execution, not the murder conviction or the State's authority to execute him by a constitutionally valid procedure. Florida law requires lethal injection absent the permitted election but does not prescribe this precise chemical protocol. The constitutional merits of the medical allegations have not been established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The route depends on the legal and practical consequences of success, not the label on the complaint or the fact that execution might be delayed. Reclassification would activate independent successive-application restrictions. Classification, PLRA exhaustion, the Eighth Amendment merits and stay equities are distinct questions; none is established by obtaining certiorari.
* **Relevant Dates:** Initial conviction: 1983, followed by resentencing and collateral review. Florida adopted lethal injection as the default in 2000. Death warrant: November 29, 2005. Federal civil complaint: January 20, 2006. Appellate judgment: January 24. Argument: April 26.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion requires an additional brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-06-12; precedent through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None established as necessary to decide the stated question under entering law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 supplies the asserted civil-rights vehicle; §§2254 and 2244 govern an actual habeas challenge. The PLRA's actually available administrative-remedy requirement and restrictions on prospective relief apply according to their terms. A stay requires an independent equitable showing.

### Issue 1: Execution-procedure classification and separately justified relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Nelson v. Campbell permits a §1983 challenge to an allegedly unnecessary execution procedure where success leaves a practicable, lawful way to carry out the sentence. Its reasoning does not turn temporary delay into necessary invalidation, but preserves reconsideration if the challenged step is actually indispensable. Nelson separately retains PLRA requirements and denies an automatic stay, with a strong contextual presumption against delay that could reasonably have been avoided.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First identify the relief and whether it necessarily defeats execution under governing law. Then assess the properly classified claim's merits and statutory conditions, and separately any requested stay. These are distinct predicates, not a single execution-delay exception to §1983.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §§1983 and 1997e(a); 18 U.S.C. §3626; 28 U.S.C. §§2254 and 2244(b); Florida Statutes §922.105. The Federal Rules govern pleading. Neither the fact of a death sentence nor the existence of earlier habeas review alone converts every procedure claim into a successive application.
* **Material Other Authority:** Preiser v. Rodriguez and Heck v. Humphrey separate claims necessarily undermining custody or sentence validity from other civil-rights claims. Nelson already applies that separation to execution procedures. No later lethal-injection merits test or alternative-method element is date-eligible entering law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Nelson's stated principle extends beyond the particular surgical cutdown procedure, while leaving the factual necessity of a challenged method and the requirements for ultimate relief open to actual adjudication.
* **Open Point:** Whether challenging the chemical sequence changes classification, whether a prisoner must plead a particular replacement protocol, and how the lower courts should handle remaining exhaustion, timing and merits questions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Hill relies on Nelson and distinguishes the validity of his sentence from an allegedly avoidable painful implementation. Florida argues that enjoining its operative protocol frustrates execution in practice and belongs in habeas, particularly after prior collateral review. The United States proposes a specific-alternative requirement and alternatively seeks affirmance based on late filing and failure to exhaust. Its brief emphasizes opportunities to challenge injection after Florida's 2000 change. Hill's timing must nonetheless be assessed against the actual information and administrative process available to him. None of these alternative defenses establishes the protocol's constitutionality merely by being asserted.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reverse the habeas classification and remand for the civil action's remaining prerequisites and merits. It could affirm if success necessarily bars all practicable authorized execution or if a preserved alternative ground is fully established, but cannot infer that from the date of filing alone. An order allowing the suit need not grant a stay. A final injunction, if warranted, must satisfy the PLRA's need, narrowness and intrusiveness limits; a preliminary order must satisfy its separate statutory duration and findings rules.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A uniform actual-consequence test resolves the procedural issue. The case does not require an advisory protocol, a constitutional guarantee of painless execution, or a final judgment on disputed medical evidence.

**Material Uncertainty:** The lower courts did not decide all exhaustion and equitable facts. No factual conclusion about anesthetic failure, practicable substitutes, administrative obstruction or deliberate delay is manufactured. Nelson remains controlling even though later historical lethal-injection doctrine is unavailable at this date. No oral-argument concession is attributed without a verified transcript.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: apply one route test to execution procedures, whether preparatory surgery or the drugs themselves. A broader rule placing every method claim in §1983 would disregard procedures genuinely necessary to carry out the sentence. A challenge that necessarily forecloses every practicable, legally authorized execution route has different consequences; mere delay or officials' preference for the current protocol does not prove that necessity.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Classification turns on the relief’s actual effect on lawful execution. A complaint challenging a replaceable implementation step need not specify a complete replacement protocol to enter §1983; the prisoner must separately establish the constitutional and equitable grounds for relief.
* **Transition:** Not applicable; no departure from the entering transition rules is proposed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Nelson permits relief because an unnecessary procedure can be removed while leaving the sentence enforceable. Nothing in that rationale makes surgical access special or makes the inmate responsible for choosing every operational detail. Florida retains legal choices concerning the injection sequence. The specific-alternative demand would impose a new pleading barrier even when officials can comply with both an injunction and the judgment. Equally, permission to sue does not establish a serious unconstitutional risk or excuse strategically delayed litigation. Nelson's contextual equitable rule protects the State's enforcement interest while requiring attention to when the claim and necessary information were actually available. Exhaustion likewise turns on an actually available administrative route, not a generic assertion that a prison grievance process exists.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Nelson v. Campbell's classification principle beyond venous access while preserving its three bounded rules for channel, PLRA restrictions and interim relief as separate predicates. Do not consolidate them into a categorical last-minute-litigation test. Preserve Preiser v. Rodriguez and Heck v. Humphrey's necessary-invalidation distinction; no precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The protocol's medical constitutionality, actual administrative availability, reasons for delay and the necessary terms of an injunction require findings absent from the threshold dismissal. The legality of every possible execution method lies outside the question presented.

# 79. United States v. Betancourt +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Betancourt
* **Citation or Docket:** 422 F.3d 240 (5th Cir. 2005); no separate Supreme Court docket for this proceeding is established.
* **October Term:** 2005.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Merits review of the forfeiture component of a final federal criminal judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-06-12. Assigned merits placement; no historical Supreme Court decision on this date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether §853(a)(1) reaches all lottery winnings traceable to a ticket interest bought with drug proceeds, and whether that criminal forfeiture categorically escapes the Excessive Fines Clause because the property derives from proceeds.
* **Question Granted:** The merits question stated above; no broader question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed the criminal judgment and the forfeiture of Betancourt's $5,481,462.91 interest. It read §853 to reach property derived from drug proceeds and treated that proceeds forfeiture as outside the Eighth Amendment's punishment category.
* **Relief Requested:** Betancourt seeks limitation or invalidation of the forfeiture. The United States seeks its preservation as forfeiture of property traceable to the convicted drug conspiracy. The grant does not include the separate sentencing issues considered below.
* **Material Record:** Betancourt was convicted of a cocaine conspiracy and two possession-with-intent-to-distribute counts. A forfeiture jury found that his interest in the winning December 2002 Texas lottery ticket was bought with proceeds of the convicted conspiracy and that no likely other source existed. He and a neighbor shared the ticket arrangement. The resulting forfeiture covers his interest, not an adjudicated forfeiture of the neighbor's independent share. The criminal record describes an extensive distribution operation. Betancourt argued below for a $152,000 ceiling, twice the $76,000 in gross proceeds found by the jury, invoking §853(a)'s separate alternative-fine language.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is criminal forfeiture attached to conviction, not a civil seizure case. Statutory tracing and constitutional proportionality are distinct. The presented question accepts the jury's proceeds-source finding for purposes of review; no new tracing evidence, third-party claim or forfeiture-jury constitutional issue is assumed. The 292-month sentence and drug-quantity challenges lie outside the grant.
* **Relevant Dates:** Winning ticket: December 11, 2002. Preliminary forfeiture order: June 25, 2003. Sentencing: November 4, 2003. Fifth Circuit decision: August 17, 2005. June 12, 2006 is the assigned merits placement, not a historical Supreme Court adjudication.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion requires an additional brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-06-12; precedent through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None established as necessary to decide the stated question under entering law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 21 U.S.C. §853(a) directs forfeiture upon qualifying conviction of property constituting or derived, directly or indirectly, from proceeds. Its separate permission for a fine up to twice gross profits does not, by its own terms, cap the forfeitable property. A statutory finding of nexus remains distinct from the Eighth Amendment inquiry.

### Issue 1: Traceable winnings and the statutory forfeiture measure

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision fixes §853(a)(1)'s application to lottery returns. Austin v. United States and United States v. Bajakajian preserve the distinction between statutory forfeiture authorization and constitutional limitations; neither narrows the word derived to the original cash amount.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the defendant's property and the legally proved causal chain from the convicted offense. A change in form or an increase in value is not necessarily a break in derivation. Separate an alternative fine from forfeiture of identified property before assigning either a statutory ceiling.

### Issue 2: Punitive criminal forfeiture and gross disproportionality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Austin v. United States treats a forfeiture as a fine when it is at least partly punitive; civil or in rem labels are not decisive. United States v. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First determine whether the sovereign's exaction is at least partly punitive in function. If so, assess gross disproportionality using the actual offense, culpability, harm, authorized sanctions and property relationship as relevant considerations, without a fixed ratio or mandatory mechanical checklist. Constitutional proportionality receives independent appellate review; underlying facts receive the governing factual review.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment's Excessive Fines Clause; §853's conviction-based criminal forfeiture scheme. The statutory double-profits fine is a potentially relevant legislative measure, not the constitutional maximum for every form of property forfeiture.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bajakajian involved lawful currency and a reporting offense, not cocaine-derived winnings. That distinction matters to gravity and property relation, but its reservation of other forfeitures does not establish a categorical proceeds exception.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing functional-punishment and gross-disproportionality rules govern once their predicates exist. They neither make all civil recovery punitive nor make every traceable criminal gain automatically excessive.
* **Open Point:** Whether this conviction-based forfeiture can be excluded categorically from the Clause and, if not, whether an adequate proportionality determination exists on the lower court's reasoning.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Betancourt contends that the amount bears an impermissible relation to the offense proceeds and invokes the twice-profits provision as a limit. The United States relies on the jury's traceability finding and the breadth of direct or indirect derivation. The Fifth Circuit regarded the statutory cap argument as a confusion of fine and forfeiture, then treated proceeds forfeiture as nonpunitive for Eighth Amendment purposes. Those are independent steps: rejecting the proposed statutory ceiling does not itself establish constitutional immunity, while rejecting immunity does not prove that this substantial drug-trafficking forfeiture is excessive.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Protection for St. Cyr’s eligible plea is established; it is not a universal immunity for every preenactment immigration event. The relevant event here could be the prior reentry, continued unlawful presence, or a completed application or entitlement if one existed.
* **Open Point:** Whether the new consequences operate prospectively on continued unlawful presence in this record, or retrospectively on completed reentry despite the absence of a prechange adjustment transaction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The new provision could apply to Fernandez-Vargas notwithstanding his pre-enactment reentry. **Petitioner:** Reentry is the statutory trigger and occurred under a regime permitting discretionary relief; removing that eligibility adds a consequence to a completed event. Congress’s omission of explicit language covering earlier reentries supports protection under Landgraf. **Government:** The provision governs the ongoing unlawful situation after its effective date. Fernandez-Vargas neither sought adjustment nor completed a protected exchange before the change, and his later marriage cannot generate a former-law entitlement. **Material limit:** The Government’s approach need not deny St. Cyr’s protection for a completed eligible plea. The petitioner’s claim concerns eligibility for consideration, not a guaranteed favorable immigration decision. Neither eventual family hardship nor abstract reliance alone resolves statutory reach.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm on this record if the statute governs continued unlawful presence after April 1, 1997 and does not withdraw a consequence attached to a completed protected transaction. Reverse and remand for application of the former regime if the legally relevant event is completed reentry and Congress gave insufficient contrary direction. A tailored remand could be necessary if an actual prechange application or exchange were supported but not evaluated; one cannot be assumed merely from the long residence.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific temporal holding can govern prechange unlawful reentry followed by continued unlawful residence and a postchange adjustment request. A universal rule covering every prior reentry would also decide people with prechange pending applications or protected exchanges not shown here. Relief under the former regime, if required, would provide lawful eligibility consideration rather than automatic adjustment.

**Record, Source, and Date Uncertainty:** The official record does not establish a prechange adjustment application or transaction. No later statute or immigration decision may be used to decide this June 2006 event. The family facts are relevant to the timing claim but do not themselves establish eligibility under the former law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Continuing unlawful presence and completed legal consequences

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved legal challenge is reviewable and the material dates are established. No prechange filing is established.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether applying reinstatement here imposes an impermissible new consequence on a protected completed event.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Landgraf and St. Cyr, while resolving their application to this continuing-status record.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule follows the actual statutory trigger and the absence of a protected completed transaction. One step broader would erase prechange applications or exchanges not before the Court. One step narrower would provide no usable answer to this recurring chronology.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 1231(a)(5) governs a person who unlawfully reentered before April 1, 1997, continued that unlawful presence afterward, and first sought adjustment on a postchange basis, absent a protected completed transaction that the new rule would burden retroactively. Fernandez-Vargas’s later marriage and application do not create former-law eligibility. A person invoking retroactivity must identify the relevant completed event and changed legal consequence; no proof of subjective reliance is required where such a protected exchange exists.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Continued unlawful presence and a later adjustment request on this record. The rule does not convert every earlier reentry, without more, into a vested right to former discretionary relief.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the completed event and its new legal consequence rather than demanding subjective reliance. St. Cyr protects its qualifying prechange exchange; postchange marriage and application alone do not establish that exchange.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Landgraf demands attention to what the law changes, not merely whether some fact occurred before enactment. St. Cyr protected an eligible guilty plea that surrendered trial rights while a specified opportunity existed; that consequence cannot be erased by calling later removal procedure prospective. Fernandez-Vargas shows no comparable completed transaction. His continuing unlawful presence extended well beyond the effective date, and the claimed adjustment basis arose later. The distinction is the legal consequence of an actual exchange, not a demand that he prove he read immigration statutes or would have acted differently.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products and Lindh v. Murphy applied; INS v. St. Cyr reaffirmed and distinguished on its completed-exchange predicate.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Prechange adjustment applications, completed eligible pleas, and independent challenges to the original order are outside this record.
