## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Ickes
* **Citation or Docket:** 393 F.3d 501 (4th Cir. 2005). No separate Supreme Court docket identified.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Simulated merits review of a final federal criminal judgment and preserved suppression claim.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-12-07; assigned calendar date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether 19 U.S.C. §1581(a) authorizes examination of electronic files on computers and disks carried in an entering vehicle, and whether the First and Fourth Amendments require a warrant despite the border-search exception on this record.
* **Question Granted:** The issue stated above; not a verbatim quotation of the grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit affirmed the conviction and denial of suppression, sustaining statutory authority and rejecting a categorical First Amendment exception.
* **Relief Requested:** Ickes seeks suppression and relief from the dependent conviction; the United States seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** At actual entry from Canada in August 2000, officers inspected Ickes’s van. Before examining the computer they found physical photographic material and other items generating concrete suspicion of prohibited material. Officers then examined a computer and about seventy-five disks and obtained incriminating files. Statements were made after the search began, so those statements cannot supply the earlier search’s justification. The record is not one of wholly suspicionless digital examination.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Statutory coverage and constitutional reasonableness are separate. The record does not establish remote-account access, a forensic extraction method, or an indefinite data-retention practice. Those facts cannot be inferred from the word computer.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2005-12-07; completed Court decisions through June 27, 2005, with event-specific legislation and procedure stated below.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established for this review.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material.

### Issue 1: Statutory authority and the actual border inspection

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Flores-Montano permits reversible vehicle-compartment inspection at an actual border without individualized suspicion, with the Government bearing the border and inspection predicates and execution remaining independently reasonable. It did not decide digital contents. Kyllo v. United States requires a warrant or a recognized exception for a protected informational search.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An exception must justify the intrusion actually performed. Technology is not categorically outside general statutory language, and statutory coverage does not itself settle the Fourth Amendment question.
* **Other Governing Law:** 19 U.S.C. §1581(a); First and Fourth Amendments; the governing prohibition on transporting child pornography. Cargo and contents can include electronic media carried across the border.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Ramsey supplies actual-border inspection authority. United States v. Montoya de Hernandez preserves separate limits for bodily intrusion and detention; neither a physical-container analogy nor expressive content alone conclusively resolves every digital search.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering decisions establish substantial actual-border authority and independently reasonable execution. They leave the materially different digital-content application open.
* **Open Point:** Whether inspection of carried digital contents requires particularized suspicion and a purpose-limited search, and whether this record satisfies that standard without a warrant.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The statute broadly includes carried media, and a First Amendment exclusion would obstruct legitimate border inspection. **Ickes:** Congress did not expressly identify electronic files, and inspecting expressive contents exposes private thought beyond ordinary cargo. **United States:** General cargo and document language encompasses digital storage; the actual entry and discovered material support the search. The strongest privacy concern concerns scope and method, not a categorical exemption for every expressive file.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the supported inspection falls within statutory authority and a reasonably executed border exception. If the record instead establishes an unsupported additional intrusion material to the conviction, vacate the suppression ruling for its independent analysis. A violation would not itself settle causation, an applicable exclusion exception, or the proper criminal remedy.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Section 1581(a) can cover electronic media without resolving constitutional limits. The Court could apply ordinary border authority to the supported search, or distinguish digital contents from routine physical inspection by requiring particularized suspicion and a search confined to imported contraband. A general rule governing remote accounts or comprehensive forensic retention would exceed this record.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** The record establishes local media carried at actual entry and antecedent suspicion. It does not establish remote access, comprehensive forensic extraction, or indefinite retention.

Montoya de Hernandez demonstrates that the border exception can require individualized suspicion when the intrusion materially exceeds routine inspection. Digital contents warrant that safeguard because the search can expose extensive lawful private expression, while imported contraband remains subject to border control. Kyllo’s warrant-or-exception principle requires justification for the actual intrusion. Here physical material discovered before digital access provided particularized suspicion of the same prohibited contents. The documented inspection concerns carried media, and no separate more intrusive method is established.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Flores-Montano retained for physical vehicle inspections and distinguished as to digital contents; Montoya de Hernandez extended by analogy to the reasonable-suspicion threshold for this nonroutine informational intrusion; Kyllo reaffirmed. The digital threshold is a proposed holding, not attributed to those earlier cases.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The absent facts concerning comprehensive forensic methods, remote accounts, suspicionless examination, and prolonged retention prevent deciding those different intrusions.

### Issue 1: Congressional authority to enforce Title II against a State

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey establishes prison coverage but reserves immunity. Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett sustains expressly authorized enforcement of valid Article I employment duties and rejects a categorical Article I immunity barrier; its adverse Section 5 determination is confined to Title I’s full employment scope. Tennessee v. Lane sustains appropriately fitted preventive Title II protection for judicial access without requiring an individual constitutional tort in every case.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the valid substantive obligation, the relevant enumerated enforcement power, unmistakable authorization, and appropriate remedy independently. Section 5 unquestionably supports remedies for actual Fourteenth Amendment violations and may also support fitted prevention under City of Boerne v. Flores.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §§12131–12133 and 12202; Fourteenth Amendment §§1 and 5; incorporated Eighth Amendment. Prison coverage does not eliminate statutory qualification, disability causation, modification limits, or lawful damages conditions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Farmer v. Brennan supplies the constitutional deliberate-indifference framework for a qualifying prison-conditions claim. The statutory duty may extend beyond an independently proved constitutional violation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Current law precludes treating either Garrett or Lane as a universal denial or approval of every Title II prison application. Garrett’s employment commerce predicate does not itself prove a commerce predicate for every prison service.
* **Open Point:** The validity of enforcing the overlapping constitutional and statutory duties, and the proper disposition of residual statutory-only claims.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The appellate court separated viable constitutional allegations from statutory claims but retained categorical immunity for the latter. **United States and Goodman:** Congress may enforce incorporated constitutional protections and may reasonably prevent unconstitutional disability mistreatment in custody. **Georgia:** The ADA’s broader accommodation duties cannot be equated with constitutional requirements; a class-wide abrogation conclusion needs an adequate power and fit analysis. The United States appears as a party defending congressional authority, not merely as an amicus. No unverified argument concession is treated as dispositive.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the immunity ruling for overlapping violations and remand the remainder for claim-specific power analysis. A broader preventive holding requires identifying the actual protected class of conduct and fit. Affirmance could be appropriate for a particular unsupported application only after addressing all properly invoked powers; no blanket historical Article I prohibition is available. Any award requires liability and remedy findings.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** An actual-constitutional-violation rule, with distinct analysis of preventive Title II duties and any supported Article I basis.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** Amended pleading, claim-specific facts, statutory-only applications, and compensation prerequisites remain unadjudicated.

# 17. Volvo Trucks North America, Inc. v. Reeder-Simco GMC, Inc.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Volvo Trucks North America, Inc. v. Reeder-Simco GMC, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 546 U.S. 164; No. 04-905.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a jury judgment under the Robinson-Patman Act.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-01-10; calendar date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the proof establishes discriminatory sales and competitive injury among purchasers when a truck manufacturer gives different concessions for customer-specific bids, usually involving different retail customers.
* **Question Granted:** The issue stated above; not a verbatim quotation of the grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, 374 F.3d 701: affirmed the federal damages judgment, treating common functional level and geographic territory, reduced profits, and dealer-reduction evidence as sufficient.
* **Relief Requested:** Volvo seeks reversal of the federal judgment; Reeder seeks preservation of the jury award. The separately adjudicated state franchise claim is not the granted federal issue.
* **Material Record:** Dealers requested manufacturer concessions before bidding on specially configured trucks and bought trucks only after winning. Reeder compared its concessions with discounts on other dealers’ unrelated successful bids. Two identified head-to-head episodes involved a concession equalized before both dealers lost, and equal initial concessions followed by a larger concession to the selected dealer. Reeder alleged that Volvo intended to reduce its dealer network.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The relevant purchases, actual competition, statutory injury, causation, and damages must be distinguished. Evidence is reviewed with proper respect for the jury’s reasonable inferences, but an intent to eliminate a dealer cannot replace a missing statutory element. Preserve the state-law judgment’s independent status.
* **Relevant Dates:** Dealer relationship began in 1995; suit filed in 2000; appellate judgment in 2004; historical argument October 31, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-01-10; completed Court decisions through June 27, 2005, with event-specific legislation and procedure stated below.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established for this review.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material.

### Issue 1: Purchaser competition and discriminatory-price injury

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Brooke Group v. Brown & Williamson requires competitive injury and holds that harm to a rival or intent alone cannot establish its primary-line predation claim. Spectrum Sports v. McQuillan likewise preserves independent competitive-danger elements. Neither decision supplies the distinct secondary-line elements here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the actual statutory offense and its evidentiary inferences. Do not transplant primary-line below-cost and recoupment requirements into a secondary-line claim or infer liability from ordinary commercial unfairness alone.
* **Other Governing Law:** 15 U.S.C. §13(a), requiring covered discriminatory sales of like-grade commodities and the specified competitive effect; §15 requires injury caused by the violation for private damages.
* **Material Other Authority:** FTC v. Morton Salt Co. permits a supported inference from substantial sustained price discrimination among competing purchasers. Falls City Industries v. Vanco Beverage and FTC v. Sun Oil address actual competition and injury; they do not dispense with those predicates.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The governing decisions preserve competitive-element proof but do not decide whether special-order bidding is categorically excluded or whether this selected comparison proves a relevant purchaser rivalry.
* **Open Point:** Whether the evidence connects the concessions to actual competitive disadvantage, and how narrowly to frame any failure of proof.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below and Reeder:** Dealers competed in the same territory before invitations issued; discriminatory concessions and a planned network reduction could impair that ongoing rivalry. The jury could infer harm without identical customers in every comparison. **Volvo:** The selected transactions did not involve competing purchasers, the head-to-head examples did not prove an actionable disadvantage, and the award protected a dealer rather than statutory competition. **United States as amicus:** Supported reversal based on the purchaser and competitive-injury requirements.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if a reasonable jury could find all statutory predicates from relevant comparisons. Reverse if the proof cannot establish purchaser competition and injury, while leaving independent state relief intact. A legal-instruction defect could warrant a properly confined retrial; a completed record’s failure of proof does not automatically require one. A categorical bidding exemption is a broader alternative requiring separate justification.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Proof-specific reversal with a reusable actual-competition requirement and express preservation of supported market-wide evidence.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** The completed comparison evidence must support the verdict; neither a missing sustained price pattern nor a different competitive market may be assumed.

### Issue 1: Silent orders, reasonable time, and statutory pendency

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Carey v. Saffold covers timely continuation through California’s ordinary successive-level review and requires an actual state-timeliness inquiry; merits discussion does not establish timeliness. Pace v. DiGuglielmo excludes an application rejected under an unsatisfied filing deadline and preserves separate, properly supported equitable protection.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A prisoner must establish the dates and qualifying pendency supporting an exclusion. State-law timeliness controls the ordinary-review interval; neither a nationwide deadline nor a conclusive merits-label presumption replaces that inquiry.
