# 52. Salinas v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Salinas v. United States.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-8400; 547 U.S. 188.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits correction of a sentencing-predicate error in appellate plain-error analysis. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-04-24 (calendar date for merits review).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a simple-possession conviction qualifies as a controlled-substance offense under the career-offender guideline and can eliminate prejudice from the disputed separate counting of earlier robberies.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 142 F. App’x 830, affirming the prison sentence while modifying an overbroad supervised-release condition. Its prejudice analysis counted simple possession as an additional qualifying predicate.
* **Relief Requested:** Salinas seeks correction of the career-offender analysis and lawful sentencing review; the Government acknowledges the simple-possession classification error but retains any lawful independent grounds for the sentence.
* **Material Record:** After guilty pleas to two bank-robbery counts, Salinas received 120 months’ imprisonment and three years of supervised release. He challenged treating two prior robberies as unrelated, among other sentencing issues. Trial counsel had not objected. The Fifth Circuit reasoned that any relatedness error was immaterial because a separate felony simple-possession conviction supplied another predicate. The actual relationship of the robberies therefore was not resolved by that rationale. The appellate court separately narrowed a condition prohibiting any addictive substance, including tobacco.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The classification error is legal, but relief still requires the applicable preservation and remand analysis. A guilty plea to present bank robbery does not automatically admit every recidivist enhancement fact. Booker retains binding Guidelines and Gatewood rejects a categorical recidivism exception; the appellate analysis must respect both rules. The Court must not infer a mandatory release, a specific replacement range, or authority for new enhancement proceedings from this limited correction.
* **Relevant Dates:** Fifth Circuit judgment August 8, 2005; the relevant guideline text is the 2003 Manual; scheduled event April 24, 2006. Later guideline amendments and later remand outcomes are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The earlier Salinas v. United States concerns bribery and RICO, not this guideline definition. Booker, Gatewood and Shepard govern the sentencing issues here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

### Issue 1: The actual controlled-substance definition

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Stinson v. United States permits consistent interpretive commentary but forbids commentary from amending contrary guideline text. United States v. LaBonte requires the Commission and sentencing courts to respect governing statutory requirements. These authorities demand the actual elements of the applicable provision rather than a generalized career-criminal label.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Match the prior conviction’s established elements to the applicable guideline definition; neither a felony label nor commentary can supply a missing qualifying element.
* **Other Governing Law:** Guidelines §§4B1.1(a) and 4B1.2(b) in the applicable Manual. The controlled-substance definition covers the specified manufacture, import, export, distribution or dispensing offenses, and possession with the specified intent. Simple possession without that additional intent is not the defined offense merely because the jurisdiction calls it a felony.
* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the governing guideline text and the Stinson and LaBonte rules identified above.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Conviction status and qualifying offense elements are separate. A different federal definition used for another purpose does not silently amend this guideline.
* **Open Point:** The remaining qualifying predicates, including the properly analyzed relationship of the robbery convictions; the simple-possession conviction cannot answer that question.

### Issue 2: Binding Guidelines, recidivist facts and prejudice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Booker preserves binding Guidelines ranges based on constitutionally established facts and retains enacted appellate review. Gatewood and Shepard require appropriate charging and jury proof beyond reasonable doubt for necessary disputed recidivist enhancement facts, absent valid admissions or lawful waivers; no categorical Almendarez-Torres exception remains. Legal classification of established elements is judicial. Williams v. United States preserves statutory sentencing-error and remand analysis.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A forfeited claim requires error, plainness, an actual effect on substantial rights and a separate fairness, integrity or public-reputation determination under Olano. An invalid fallback predicate cannot establish harmlessness or lack of prejudice. Preserved charging omissions and forfeited claims must not be merged.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth and Sixth Amendments; the applicable Guidelines and 18 U.S.C. §§3553 and 3742 as preserved by Booker; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(b) for a forfeited claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Olano requires separate findings of error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights and the warranted exercise of corrective discretion; presumed prejudice from a sentencing label cannot replace that inquiry.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Correction applies in the pending direct review under governing temporal rules. Binding Guidelines remain lawful when the range rests on constitutionally established facts.
* **Open Point:** The lawful range, validly established predicates, preservation and effect of the corrected calculation on this actual sentence. The remand itself authorizes no new indictment or enhancement jury.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Salinas argues that the earlier robbery cases should count together and that the resulting career-offender treatment is unsupported. He also raised a recidivist charging-and-proof objection, ineffective assistance and a Booker argument. The Fifth Circuit used the possession conviction to defeat prejudice and counsel-related prejudice without deciding the robbery relationship on its merits. The Solicitor General concedes that simple possession is not a qualifying controlled-substance offense. That concession is supported by the text, but it does not establish the remaining facts. The unrelated modification of supervised release does not depend on the disputed drug predicate. The remand must assess each sentencing claim under Booker’s binding-range rule and Gatewood’s element protections.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacatur and remand may remove the invalid fallback and require proper review of the preserved sentencing claims under current law. Immediate resentencing at a chosen number would require findings about the actual lawful range and procedural entitlements not yet established. Affirmance could rest only on a genuinely sufficient independent ground, not the erroneous drug classification or a presumed identical advisory sentence. The Court can preserve the unchallenged convictions and independent supervised-release correction while directing reconsideration of reasoning dependent on the invalid predicate. It need not decide every contested feature of related-case counting in the first instance.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The definition supports an intermediate rule for the applicable career-offender provision, with a narrow remand applying established sentencing and preservation rules. It does not decide other statutes’ drug definitions or a replacement sentence.

# 53. Seegars v. Gonzales +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Seegars v. Gonzales.
* **Citation or Docket:** 396 F.3d 1248; D.C. Circuit Nos. 04-5016 and 04-5081; historical petition No. 05-365.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of dismissal for lack of pre-enforcement standing. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-04-24 (assigned merits-review date; no corresponding historical Supreme Court merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether residents who allege that they wish to possess handguns prohibited by District of Columbia law must demonstrate a specific threat of prosecution to establish Article III standing for a preenforcement Second Amendment challenge? Whether a registered firearm owner’s allegation that she wishes to remove a legally required trigger lock when necessary for self-defense establishes sufficiently imminent injury for preenforcement review?
* **Question Granted:** The two preenforcement standing questions stated above; no firearm-merits question is added.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** D.C. Circuit, 396 F.3d 1248, affirming the pistol-claim standing dismissal and reversing the district court’s recognition of standing for Hailes’s trigger-lock challenge, leaving all claims dismissed on standing grounds.
* **Relief Requested:** The residents seek a judicial declaration and appropriate prospective relief; federal and District officials seek to retain dismissal. A final merits injunction requires additional adjudication beyond correcting standing.
* **Material Record:** Five residents alleged that they would promptly acquire and register pistols for home protection but for the District’s bar on registration of new pistols. Jordan already owned a pistol stored outside the District. Hailes owned a registered shotgun kept locked at home and sought to remove the lock when necessary for self-defense. Criminal penalties govern unregistered possession and the challenged storage requirements. No plaintiff had been individually threatened or prosecuted, and none applied to register a pistol. The statutory registration restriction is categorical for the described new acquisitions; separate licensing and prosecution responsibilities require attention.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The dismissal posture requires proper treatment of concrete allegations, not ultimate proof of constitutional entitlement. Standing must be shown against officials with responsibility for the challenged constraint; federal and local enforcement roles are distinct. Registration review offers a separate route but does not automatically exhaust or eliminate federal jurisdiction. The trigger-lock claim concerns present disabled storage as well as a future defensive occasion. A ruling on standing cannot assume the merits are already decided.
* **Relevant Dates:** Appellate decision February 8, 2005; rehearing denied June 21, 2005; petition filed September 16, 2005. April 24, 2006 is organizationally assigned.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Love v. Pepersack and United States v. Wright already establish an individual arms guarantee and its governing review. No later historical handgun decision is date-eligible. The listed case remains MERITS even though the reviewed ruling is jurisdictional.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, together with applicable enactments effective before this event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the question presented.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply the jurisdiction, standard of review, preservation rules and allocation of proof specified below.

### Issue 1: Handgun acquisition and a specific prosecution threat

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno permits pre-enforcement review when a criminal law directly restrains concrete intended activity and serious penalties create a sufficiently real risk; an actual prosecution is unnecessary. Maine Right to Life rejects mandatory use of an optional advisory route on its actual statutory design. These rules supplement Lujan’s concrete injury, traceability and redressability requirements rather than abolish them.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the plaintiff’s actual intended conduct, coverage, present restraint and credible enforcement risk. A generalized objection to a law is insufficient, but a personally addressed warning is not invariably required. Lack of disavowal alone is not enough in every case.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; Declaratory Judgment Act; Babbitt v. United Farm Workers. District registration, possession, licensing and storage provisions and their criminal penalties. The existence of an administrative route is distinct from an enacted exhaustion requirement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Babbitt v. United Farm Workers supplies the pre-enforcement inquiry into concrete intended conduct, statutory coverage and a credible enforcement threat.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing pre-enforcement framework is not confined to speech. The panel’s special-threat formulation must be evaluated against the governing injury, traceability and redressability requirements.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pleaded handgun plans, registration barrier and enforcement practices establish injury and a redressable connection to the named officials without a specific prosecution threat.

### Issue 2: Trigger-lock compliance and imminent injury

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lee v. Harcleroad recognizes present injury from an applicable legal duty even without an express criminal penalty. Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno permits review of concrete intended conduct restrained by a credible enforcement risk. Lujan requires an actual or imminent injury, causation and redressability; Babbitt supplies the corresponding pre-enforcement framework.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish the present effect of a mandatory storage requirement from uncertainty about when a defensive emergency will arise. Alleged intended conduct, statutory coverage and the enforcement connection remain necessary.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; the District’s storage requirement and criminal penalties, including any applicable statutory exceptions or defenses. The Second Amendment supplies the asserted interest; standing does not establish its ultimate violation.
* **Material Other Authority:** Babbitt v. United Farm Workers; no later historical handgun decision supplies entering law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A present legal restraint and a contingent future event raise different injury questions. The existence of a duty does not dispense with its actual application to the plaintiff or the defendant’s ability to redress it.
* **Open Point:** Whether Hailes’s ownership, ongoing locked storage and stated intention to unlock when necessary for defense establish sufficiently imminent injury despite uncertainty about a future emergency.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The residents contend that criminal enforcement presently deters the very conduct they identify and that waiting for a personal prosecution warning would prevent lawful anticipatory review. They treat Hailes’s disability requirement as a current constraint on her owned shotgun. The Government emphasizes the lack of an individualized threat, the unattempted registration route and uncertainty about a future need for defense. The panel felt bound by Navegar’s unusually demanding enforcement standard; Judge Sentelle argued that Babbitt required no special personal warning. Officials also offered a collective-right merits defense; Love and Wright supply the governing individual-right framework if jurisdiction permits later merits review. An available registration appeal may sharpen the record but is not itself proof that these concrete constitutional claims are advisory.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can recognize sufficiently pleaded pre-enforcement injury and remand for proper-defendant and merits adjudication. A claim-specific remand may be needed if an official lacks enforcement responsibility or a particular intended activity remains genuinely indefinite. Affirmance requires an actual defect in injury, causation or redressability, not a universal demand for a prior threat unique to the claimant. A final handgun or storage injunction would require adjudication of the actual scheme beyond the two standing questions. Any subsequent merits proceedings remain subject to Love and Wright; no separate question about their governing force is added.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An intermediate pre-enforcement rule may reject personalized-warning requirements while retaining concrete injury, credible enforcement and redressability. A merits remand may apply Love and Wright without deciding final validity of the entire District scheme.

# 54. Northern Insurance Co. of New York v. Chatham County

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Northern Insurance Co. of New York v. Chatham County.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1618; 547 U.S. 189.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of summary judgment asserting a county’s residual sovereign immunity in admiralty. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-04-25 (calendar date for merits review).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a county that is not an arm of the State may nevertheless claim a separate residual or admiralty-specific sovereign immunity because it operates a drawbridge under delegated governmental authority.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 129 F. App’x 602, affirming summary judgment for Chatham County under circuit precedent recognizing residual immunity despite the absence of arm-of-State status.
* **Relief Requested:** Northern Insurance seeks reversal of the immunity dismissal and adjudication of its subrogated maritime claim; the County seeks immunity from the suit.
* **Material Record:** A portion of the County-operated Causton Bluff drawbridge malfunctioned and struck the insured vessel as it passed on the Wilmington River. The insurer paid its insureds and brought an admiralty action to recover. The County conceded the absence of ordinary Eleventh Amendment immunity, understood below and in the review question as the absence of arm-of-State status. The lower courts nonetheless treated delegated state power as supplying residual immunity. Negligence, causation and the recoverable amount have not been resolved by that immunity judgment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The granted question assumes the County is not a State arm for the relevant operation. The Court should not reopen that premise through an expanded label after granting review on it. Admiralty subject-matter jurisdiction and the insurer’s subrogation claim are distinct from the immunity defense. State-law immunities do not automatically defeat an otherwise applicable federal maritime cause. The present case does not require a universal statement about all counties in every function.
* **Relevant Dates:** Collision October 6, 2002; Eleventh Circuit judgment January 28, 2005; argument March 1, 2006.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Hess and Jinks address the State–local entity distinction. Katz concerns bankruptcy, a different statutory setting; no intervening judgment changes the county-status question here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, together with applicable enactments effective before this event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the question presented.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply the jurisdiction, standard of review, preservation rules and allocation of proof specified below.

### Issue 1: The County’s status and asserted residual immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. requires examination of the entity’s actual legal, financial and institutional relationship to the State; exercising governmental power alone is insufficient. Treasury exposure is highly probative, not invariably indispensable. Jinks v. Richland County preserves the municipality–State distinction and refuses to convert a county’s limitations defense into State immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual defendant and its relation to the sovereign before deciding waiver or legislative displacement. A public function does not itself transform a separate local entity into the State. An asserted immunity must have a lawful source, not merely a familiar label.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III admiralty jurisdiction, 28 U.S.C. §1333 and the applicable federal maritime liability law; Georgia’s entity structure supplies the County’s status predicates.
* **Material Other Authority:** Workman v. New York City recognizes municipal amenability in admiralty and the application of federal maritime principles. Mt. Healthy and Lake Country Estates preserve the separate-entity inquiry. Ex parte New York concerned an actual State, not a conceded nonarm county.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The County’s nonarm premise resolves the asserted structural status. Different facts supporting actual arm status would require the governing entity inquiry, not a broader residual-immunity shortcut.
* **Open Point:** Whether admiralty or delegated bridge operation supplies an additional county immunity independent of that status.

### Issue 2: Admiralty and genuine State defendants

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Florida Prepaid permits unmistakably authorized State exposure within valid Article I power. Alden v.
