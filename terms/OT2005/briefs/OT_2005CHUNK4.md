# 44. Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith Inc. v. Dabit

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith Inc. v. Dabit
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1371; 547 U.S. 71.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of dismissal under the Securities Litigation Uniform Standards Act.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-03-21 (historical Supreme Court calendar date used for scheduling).
* **Question Presented:** Whether SLUSA bars a covered state-law securities class action alleging that fraud caused investors to retain securities, although those holders could not bring an equivalent private federal purchaser-or-seller claim.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 395 F.3d 25, allowing the holder portion of the state-law class action to proceed after the district court had dismissed under SLUSA.
* **Relief Requested:** Merrill Lynch seeks dismissal of the covered action; Dabit seeks to proceed with claims based on retention of securities and related business losses.
* **Material Record:** A former Merrill Lynch broker alleges that misleading research manipulated stock prices and caused brokers and their clients to retain overvalued securities. The pleaded class includes brokers claiming losses from these holdings and from clients’ reactions. The complaint describes a market-manipulation scheme involving transactions by others; the plaintiff’s own lack of a purchase or sale is the asserted exemption from SLUSA. These are allegations at the dismissal stage, not findings that fraud occurred.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns the statutory class-action bar, not adjudication of fraud liability or creation of a private federal holder claim. The covered-class, state-law and covered-security predicates must be satisfied. Any claim-specific amendment or genuinely independent allegation must be evaluated under ordinary procedure rather than by a label. Legal statutory coverage is reviewed independently.
* **Relevant Dates:** Second Circuit decision January 11, 2005; argument January 18, 2006.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Dura Pharmaceuticals concerns loss causation in a private federal action; it does not decide SLUSA coverage. No prior effective same-term ruling resolving this statute is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The applicable jurisdiction, review, preservation and proof requirements are stated below.

### Issue 1: The transaction connection and the private-action limit

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** SEC v. Zandford requires a real connection between the deception and securities transactions, not mere temporal proximity or any fiduciary wrong. Wharf v. United International Holdings applies federal fraud law to an actual option purchase. Dura Pharmaceuticals requires actual causally related loss in a private damages action. None equates those private-action elements with the scope of a separate statutory class-action restriction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted cause or restriction before transferring an element from another setting. A transaction can be integral to a deceptive scheme even if the particular claimant does not execute it. Causation, reliance and injury remain independent where the governing action requires them.
* **Other Governing Law:** SLUSA, 15 U.S.C. §78bb(f), bars specified covered class actions under state law alleging material misrepresentation or omission in connection with purchase or sale of covered securities. Its express conditions, exceptions and preservation clauses control. Blue Chip Stamps v. Manor Drug Stores limits the implied private federal action to purchasers and sellers; that limitation does not automatically define the enacted bar.
* **Material Law Difference:** Central Bank preserves a bounded private aiding-and-abetting claim. SLUSA’s enacted class-action restriction remains distinct from that private liability rule and from Blue Chip Stamps’ purchaser-or-seller limitation.

* **Material Other Authority:** Blue Chip Stamps v. Manor Drug Stores limits the implied private federal action to purchasers and sellers; that cause-of-action limit does not itself determine the reach of SLUSA’s separate enacted restriction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Transaction connection and private cause-of-action eligibility are distinct inquiries.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory bar reaches this holder class where the alleged deception is connected to covered market purchases or sales by others.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Merrill Lynch argues that Congress used a transaction-linked phrase broader than the purchaser-or-seller limitation on a particular plaintiff’s implied remedy and sought a uniform rule for covered securities class litigation. Dabit argues that claims unavailable in federal court should not disappear from state class proceedings and that retention is not a purchase or sale. The Second Circuit accepted that distinction for holder allegations. The defense answers that the complaint attacks a market scheme operating through purchases and sales, and that SLUSA limits the class device without necessarily extinguishing every individual state-law claim. The litigants’ competing policy accounts cannot replace the statute’s actual coverage conditions or exceptions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal may hold that the claimant’s personal purchase or sale is unnecessary when the alleged fraud has the required connection to covered transactions. Affirmance would construe the bar as incorporating the private federal standing limit, despite the different statutory function. A narrower remand could distinguish genuinely nontransactional misconduct from the pleaded market scheme, but it should not treat retention language as conclusive. Dismissal of a covered class action leaves questions about individual actions and statutory exceptions to the governing text. The Court need not decide the alleged fraud’s truth, each broker’s actual loss or whether a different federal cause of action exists.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: SLUSA does not borrow the plaintiff-specific purchase requirement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Statutory coverage is ripe on the alleged scheme; its truth is assumed only for dismissal. Remaining statutory predicates and exceptions must be identified, not presumed away.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a holder’s lack of a personal purchase or sale exempts this covered class action.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Give the statutory transaction language its own function while requiring a genuine connection between the alleged fraud and covered securities transactions.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers holder class allegations integral to a purchase-or-sale scheme. It does not bar every dispute involving an issuer, broker or security. A narrower plaintiff-specific rule would import an implied-remedy limitation into a different enacted restriction; a broader securities-related rule would erase the required transaction nexus.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For SLUSA’s covered-class-action bar, the alleged deception must genuinely connect with a purchase or sale of covered securities, but the named plaintiff need not personally be that purchaser or seller. A class alleging retention losses from the pleaded market-trading fraud therefore is not exempt solely because its members are holders. Other statutory conditions and exceptions remain necessary.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Covered state-law class actions alleging transaction-connected fraud, including this alleged manipulated-market scheme; not ordinary professional negligence independent of a securities transaction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the covered purchase or sale and the deception’s integral role in it. A broker or security mentioned incidentally is insufficient; every statutory coverage condition and express exception retains its own force.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** SEC v. Zandford distinguishes deception integral to transactions from a coincidental fiduciary wrong. The alleged manipulation here works through the market’s purchases and sales even though Dabit’s asserted response was retention. Blue Chip Stamps defines who may invoke an implied private federal remedy, while SLUSA expressly restricts a described class-action category. Treating those as identical would ignore the different legal tasks. The absence of an equivalent federal holder suit does not rewrite Congress’s class-action rule.
* **Precedent Treatment:** SEC v. Zandford applied at its actual transaction-connection scope. Blue Chip Stamps preserved as a private federal cause-of-action limit. Dura Pharmaceuticals and Central Bank retain their governing liability rules and are not expanded or displaced.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Missing pleaded basis: no particular independent claim or express statutory exception is established for adjudication in this record.

# 45. United States v. Grubbs

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Grubbs
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1414; 547 U.S. 90.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of suppression following a conditional guilty plea.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-03-21 (historical Supreme Court calendar date used for scheduling).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourth Amendment permits an anticipatory warrant supported by probable cause of a future delivery and requires the warrant itself to recite its triggering condition.
* **Question Granted:** Whether suppression is required after the anticipatory condition occurred when that condition appeared neither in the warrant itself nor in an affidavit both incorporated into the warrant and shown to the person searched. The validity of anticipatory authorization is a necessary antecedent.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 377 F.3d 1072, amended at 389 F.3d 1306, reversing denial of suppression because the affidavit containing the trigger was not presented to the residents. The district court had found incorporation and possession of the affidavit by the officers; the appellate court treated nonpresentation as independently dispositive.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of suppression; Grubbs seeks exclusion of the evidence seized under the anticipatory warrant.
* **Material Record:** The magistrate approved an anticipatory home search based on an affidavit conditioning execution on delivery of the videotape inside the residence. The warrant referred to attached descriptions of the premises and objects, but did not recite the trigger. The district court regarded the affidavit as incorporated; the scope of that incorporation must be distinguished from whether it was shown to the residents. Grubbs’s wife brought the package inside before the search. Officers possessed the affidavit but gave the residents the warrant without it. No premature execution is alleged.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The conditional plea preserved the warrant question. Legal sufficiency is reviewed independently, with appropriate respect for the issuing magistrate’s probable-cause assessment. The government must identify an authorized, sufficiently particular warrant and a factually supported trigger. Suppression and any officer-immunity question are separate; this criminal case does not adjudicate damages.
* **Relevant Dates:** Warrant and controlled delivery in 2002; appellate amendment in 2004; argument January 18, 2006.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Groh v. Ramirez governs omitted particularity but did not adopt a categorical advance-service rule. The earlier listed United States v. Ickes raises a different search category and supplies no effective same-term holding.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The applicable jurisdiction, review, preservation and proof requirements are stated below.

### Issue 1: Conditional probable cause

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The Fourth Amendment cases preserve warrant, probable-cause and actual-scope requirements. Illinois v. McArthur permits a tailored temporary measure justified by its actual evidence and safeguards, not an unrestricted reasonableness substitute. Wilson v. Layne requires execution to remain within the warrant’s lawful purpose.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The magistrate assesses the actual forecast and ensuing evidentiary connection; police may execute only within the resulting authorization.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; Illinois v. Gates requires a practical, evidence-supported fair probability. An anticipatory warrant must connect both the expected event and the resulting presence of evidence to the place to be searched. Judicial authorization precedes the search even though the described evidentiary condition matures later.
* **Material Other Authority:** Illinois v. Gates supplies the practical, evidence-supported fair-probability inquiry for both the expected trigger and the resulting presence of evidence.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Probable cause may assess predictions, but speculation supplies no fair probability.
* **Open Point:** Whether adequately conditioned judicial authorization must await physical arrival of the evidence.


### Issue 2: Particularity, trigger and execution notice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Groh v. Ramirez invalidates a warrant that identifies no things to be seized and incorporates no document supplying that limit. An officer’s personal knowledge cannot replace the judge’s missing authorization. Groh expressly leaves advance presentation and the universal sufficiency of incorporation-and-accompaniment unresolved.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The warrant must identify the place and objects with constitutionally sufficient particularity, and execution must satisfy the actual judicial conditions. What the warrant must describe and when documents must be shown are distinct questions.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment’s warrant text specifies place and persons or things. Rule 41 and other valid execution requirements remain separately enforceable; a constitutional ruling does not erase them.
* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond Groh and the separately applicable warrant and execution provisions identified above.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Groh addresses absent seizure authorization, not every omitted operational fact.
* **Open Point:** Whether the operative warrant must state or incorporate the condition defining when entry becomes authorized; whether nonpresentation of an otherwise incorporated affidavit independently invalidates the search.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Grubbs argues that the operative authorization must communicate the condition limiting entry and that the residents must receive the curative document. The Government argues that place-and-object particularity suffices and that the approved affidavit and fulfilled delivery establish authority. Alternatively, it proposes severing any impermissible pre-delivery authorization: because the actual search occurred after delivery, it says no seized evidence derives from that temporal defect. The district court accepted incorporation and declined to require presentation; the Ninth Circuit made presentation dispositive. Those positions require separating judicial adoption of the trigger, service of the document and the remedy for any constitutional defect.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may uphold anticipatory authorization while requiring the operative warrant to state the trigger or incorporate a document stating it. That path requires assessment of the actual incorporation and any preserved suppression defense, without automatically adopting the Ninth Circuit’s presentation rule. It may instead find the approved supporting affidavit sufficient regardless of incorporation and reverse the stated suppression ground. A categorical presentation requirement would need an independent constitutional basis and treatment of justified delayed disclosure.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule requiring objectively identifiable judicial conditions governs anticipatory warrants generally without placing all probable-cause evidence on their face. A narrower ruling could address only this warrant’s incorporation. Suppression requires its own analysis; a fulfilled condition does not itself settle whether the operative authorization was valid.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The appellate judgment did not resolve every incorporation and remedial question under this separation of issues. No missing adverse factual finding or unpreserved government defense is assumed.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Anticipatory authorization requires supported conditional probable cause

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The condition occurred before entry. Review concerns the authority conferred when the warrant issued, not an unsupported finding that officers searched prematurely.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a magistrate may authorize a search conditioned on a particular future delivery.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gates’s practical probable-cause inquiry to the predicted event and the resulting presence of evidence.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule reaches evidence-supported, event-conditioned warrants. A broader standing license to search whenever evidence later appears would surrender judicial control. A narrower mail-only exception would miss the relevant safeguard: an identified, judicially assessed condition.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An anticipatory warrant may issue when the magistrate has a substantial basis to find a fair probability that the stated event will occur and that evidence will then be at the described place, with execution limited to that condition. The controlled delivery supplies those probabilities here; issuance need not await physical arrival.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Judicially assessed future events and searches within their authorized scope; no unrestricted delegation to police to develop probable cause later.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Police must establish that the actual condition occurred before entry. Staleness, a materially different delivery or an unfulfilled condition is assessed under ordinary probable cause and the authorization’s actual terms.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gates already evaluates probabilities rather than certainty. A magistrate may assess a controlled delivery before it happens, while withholding authority to enter until it occurs. Wilson v. Layne requires execution to remain within the authority granted. The completed delivery resolves the timing objection to this execution but does not decide what the operative warrant incorporated.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Gates applied to supported prediction; Wilson v. Layne reaffirmed as to authorized scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A premature, stale or materially different execution is outside the established facts.

### Issue 2: The operative authorization must identify the condition limiting entry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The warrant describes the place and objects. The preserved dispute concerns the additional event that makes a home search authorized and whether the warrant incorporated it.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an unincorporated application can alone supply an essential execution condition, and whether failure to present an incorporated affidavit independently invalidates this warrant.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Groh’s distinction between the judicial authorization and the officer’s supporting application to the essential trigger. This resolves a question Groh left open; it is not described as an existing trigger-on-the-face rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. An essential condition must be ascertainable from the warrant or a document it expressly incorporates. A broader demand to reproduce every probable-cause fact or serve all supporting papers before entry lacks support here. A narrower reliance on the officer’s private understanding would fail to identify the judicial limit that authorizes entry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An anticipatory warrant must state its essential execution condition or expressly incorporate a document stating it; an officer’s knowledge of an unincorporated application cannot supply that judicial limit. The incorporated document must actually define and accompany the executing authority. Failure to show it to the occupant, standing alone, does not establish that the magistrate failed to impose the condition. This warrant’s actual incorporation must therefore be resolved before its validity and the resulting suppression remedy are determined.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Conditions defining when judicial authority to enter arises. Supporting evidence and operational details that do not limit that authority need not all appear on the warrant’s face.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Examine the warrant’s actual adoption of the limiting document rather than inferring incorporation from police possession or knowledge alone. Apply independently governing service requirements to any preserved notice claim and existing exclusion rules to any established constitutional defect.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. Ordinary direct-review rules govern this pending case; the holding does not reopen final judgments.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Groh holds that knowledge of an application cannot replace a missing judicial limitation. Although the Fourth Amendment expressly names place and objects, a warrant remains a judicial authorization whose essential condition must be identifiable; a prediction in an application and a limit in the authorization perform different functions. Incorporation supplies a practical means of stating the limit without duplicating the affidavit. The district court found incorporation, while the appellate judgment treated presentation as decisive. That disputed document question cannot be resolved by assuming either wholesale incorporation or none. The Government’s temporal-severance theory also requires an independently valid authorized portion; occurrence of the anticipated event does not itself supply omitted judicial language. Leon’s objective-reliance rule, if procedurally available, retains its facial-deficiency and officer-responsibility limits rather than merging validity with suppression.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Groh extended to an essential anticipatory condition, without converting its reserved service question into an advance-presentation requirement. Leon and Arizona v. Evans preserved for their separate, bounded remedial rules; no automatic reasonable-reliance finding follows.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The incorporation application and any remaining preserved remedial or service ground require review of the actual documents and procedural record. A universal advance-service entitlement is unnecessary to the presented disposition.

# 46. Sarhank Group v. Oracle Corp. +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sarhank Group v. Oracle Corp.
* **Citation or Docket:** 404 F.3d 657.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of an appellate vacatur and remand in a Convention award-enforcement proceeding.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-03-21 (assigned scheduling date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the absence of an arbitration agreement signed by the party against whom a foreign award is asserted defeats federal subject-matter jurisdiction under the New York Convention, or instead concerns the merits of enforcement? Whether a United States court may enforce a foreign arbitral award against an American parent corporation based on the arbitrators’ application of foreign law without independently determining whether the parent agreed to arbitrate under applicable American contract or agency principles?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions; no expanded question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 404 F.3d 657, vacating the Southern District of New York judgment confirming an Egyptian arbitral award against Oracle and remanding for independent contract-and-agency findings.
* **Relief Requested:** Sarhank seeks enforcement against Oracle; Oracle resists enforcement on the ground that it never agreed to arbitrate and also challenges federal subject-matter jurisdiction.
* **Material Record:** Sarhank contracted with Oracle Systems, a foreign subsidiary, under a distributorship agreement containing arbitration. Oracle, the American parent, did not sign. The Egyptian panel treated the parent as bound and entered an award against both entities. Oracle objected to arbitrability. Egyptian proceedings did not unsettle the award. The district court confirmed against the parent; the appellate court required findings on whether contract, agency or other recognized principles actually bound it. The existence of a corporate relationship is established; whether the existing findings establish Oracle’s consent or another basis for binding it is disputed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A federal appellate remand remains reviewable under the governing federal certiorari jurisdiction; the state-court final-judgment rule is not imported. The parties dispute whether absence of an agreement defeats Convention jurisdiction or enforcement on the merits. Its objection to arbitrability, the applicable agreement law and the effect of the foreign proceedings bear on the asserted consent and delegation theories.
* **Relevant Dates:** Distributorship agreement in 1991; termination dispute in 1997; arbitral award March 11, 1999; district confirmation in 2004; appellate ruling April 14, 2005. The March 21, 2006 event is organizationally assigned.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Buckeye Check Cashing concerns allocation of a whole-contract invalidity challenge after agreement exists. It supplies no effective ruling and does not eliminate the antecedent consent inquiry. Karaha Bodas governs the Convention’s recognition framework, not a universal parent-company consent rule.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 27, 2005, applied immediately before this event; no intervening decision is established.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any actual earlier effective Buckeye Check Cashing or Arbaugh decision might clarify separability or jurisdictional classification. Neither is identified as effective; the current consent and jurisdiction authorities independently govern.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The applicable jurisdiction, review, preservation and proof requirements are stated below.

### Issue 1: Jurisdiction and judicial determination of agreement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Steel Co. distinguishes judicial power from failure of a substantive claim. First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan requires courts independently to determine agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation; presenting an objection while denying panel jurisdiction does not itself establish delegation. Specht v. Netscape Communications requires actual or reasonably communicated assent and a supported contractual or equitable basis before binding a nonsignatory; an incidental or speculative commercial advantage does not supply it.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The party invoking arbitration must establish the actual contractual predicate, and courts determine disputed assent absent clear and unmistakable delegation; First Options leaves independent nonsignatory-binding theories unresolved.
* **Other Governing Law:** Convention Articles II and V; FAA §§202, 203, 207 and 208. Section 203 grants jurisdiction over actions or proceedings falling under the Convention; the parties dispute how a nonsignatory’s agreement objection affects that grant and the Convention’s enforcement defenses. Bell v. Hood distinguishes substantial federal claims from ultimate entitlement to relief.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bell v. Hood distinguishes jurisdiction over a substantial federal claim from the claimant’s ultimate entitlement to prevail.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** First Options and Specht require a supported basis for agreement or attribution and recognize actual delegation; whether the Convention enforcement posture alters their application to a foreign award is disputed.
* **Open Point:** Whether the disputed agreement affects statutory jurisdiction or enforceability, and what agreement, delegation or attribution findings the proper inquiry requires.

### Issue 2: Foreign award and remand scope

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Karaha Bodas respects the Convention’s primary and secondary recognition roles and confines refusal to authorized grounds; governing substantive contract law does not itself designate a different primary arbitral jurisdiction. First Options and Specht govern the antecedent agreement inquiry; no controlling decision settles how that inquiry applies when a foreign panel has held a nonsignatory bound under foreign law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Convention recognition confines refusal to authorized grounds, and courts determine disputed assent absent clear and unmistakable delegation; how those principles interact when a foreign award is asserted against a nonsignatory is disputed.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Convention’s specific agreement and scope defenses, ordinary contract-and-agency principles, and the distinction between validity of an agreement and identification of who made it. Whether foreign adjudication of the award establishes an American parent’s consent beyond its corporate status is disputed.
* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the identified Convention recognition and agreement authorities.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Recognition favors actual arbitration agreements and authorized awards, subject to the Convention’s defenses.
* **Open Point:** Whether the court must independently determine Oracle’s agreement notwithstanding the panel’s foreign-law ruling, which law governs any such determination, and whether the existing findings bind Oracle.


## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Sarhank emphasizes the written subsidiary agreement, the foreign award, the Egyptian proceedings and the Convention’s enforcement policy. Oracle responds that it signed no agreement and that the panel’s enterprise theory cannot replace American contract or agency grounds for attributing consent. It also characterizes the absent agreement as a jurisdictional defect. The appellate court separated jurisdiction from enforceability and required independent findings. Sarhank relies on the foreign adjudication as a basis for enforcement; Oracle maintains that its preserved objection requires an independent inquiry into who became bound. The parties dispute the legal significance of the existing findings and whether additional findings are necessary.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court must classify the agreement objection under the Convention and FAA jurisdictional grant before reaching disputed enforcement merits. If jurisdiction exists and the existing findings do not establish a binding agreement, delegation or attribution, affirmance can preserve a focused factual remand. Reinstatement of confirmation depends on whether the applicable law and existing record already establish a sufficient enforcement basis. A jurisdictional dismissal requires a statutory ground consistent with the distinction between federal judicial power and failure of a substantive claim. A final denial of enforcement requires a record sufficient to resolve the asserted binding theories. Any review of the commercial merits remains limited by the Convention’s authorized defenses.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Georgia argues that sharing a home entails the risk that a co-resident will exercise independent authority to admit police, and that an objector should not acquire unilateral control over another resident’s choices. Randolph answers that consent is an exception to the warrant requirement, not a power to waive an equally situated present resident’s rights over an explicit objection; police can seek a warrant or act on a genuine emergency. The Georgia court distinguished absent-objector authority. The independent-evidence alternative deserves precision: the wife’s authority to tell police what she knows does not itself authorize police entry into the protected home.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance may adopt a rule confined to a present resident’s clear contemporaneous refusal, while preserving Matlock, reasonable apparent-authority reliance and genuine exigency. Reversal may treat each common occupant’s authority as independently sufficient despite an actual refusal; that requires addressing why the State may choose one resident’s permission as a waiver of another’s personal privacy. A broader rule requiring police to locate every potential objector would exceed the case. A rule approving entry whenever domestic disagreement exists would equally erase the required emergency predicate. The practical consequence is a need for a warrant or a genuinely independent exception for this search, not a right to prevent the co-resident from cooperating with police.

These imports belong under heading 3306 rather than the residual food-preparation heading.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The meaning of oral-hygiene preparation and the established functions of the reviewed goods, not clinical claims or the treatment of every candy.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Classify different goods by their actual characteristics and the governing tariff text; absence of an antimicrobial ingredient does not itself exclude an oral-hygiene preparation.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The heading and informative notes recognize nontherapeutic oral preparations, including oral perfumes. The monograph distinguishes several mechanisms of controlling malodor and does not make bacterial inhibition the sole hygienic function. The undisputed cleansing and neutralization characteristics therefore matter. A residual category cannot prevail merely because the goods are consumed orally when a specific heading describes their established preparation and use.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Mead Corp. applied to independent classification with actual persuasive consideration. Haggar and Christensen preserve their different delegation settings; none supplies the missing antimicrobial limitation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. Classification of different goods is an ordinary application of the tariff rule to different facts.

### Issue 2: Persuasive consideration does not require accepting a defective reason

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Customs mechanism and its actual explanation are available; no binding legislative rule establishing the proposed restriction is identified.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What weight the classification letter receives and what remedy follows.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Mead’s genuine persuasive inquiry without fixed deference or automatic agency remand.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow to the ruling mechanism, explanation and established record. A broader rule discounting all Customs expertise would contradict Mead; mandatory acceptance would erase independent statutory judgment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A transaction-specific Customs classification receives the weight warranted by its actual reasoning, expertise and fit with the statute and established goods. The letter’s failure to account for nonantimicrobial oral hygiene and the relevant product functions leaves its contrary classification unpersuasive here. The court may determine the proper heading on these undisputed facts and leave only actual entry calculations for implementation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This ruling letter and classification record, not delegated regulations issued through a materially different statutory mechanism.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the proper heading to the actual entries and tariff version when calculating duties or refunds. A classification ruling supplies no unproved monetary amount.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mead forbids both exclusion of useful agency reasoning and automatic obedience to an institutional label. Customs’ analysis must address the evidence it purports to classify. Its restrictive reading is inconsistent with the heading’s examples and explanatory context; its generalized food rationale overlooks established functions. No unresolved fact requires repeating the same administrative analysis, while the actual duty amount remains an implementation question.
