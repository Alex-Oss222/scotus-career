# 64. DaimlerChrysler Corp. v. Cuno

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** DaimlerChrysler Corp. v. Cuno
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 04-1704 and 04-1724; judgment below, 386 F.3d 738.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a final Sixth Circuit judgment invalidating Ohio's investment tax credit under the Commerce Clause.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-05-15. Calendar date for merits review.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the plaintiffs have Article III standing to challenge Ohio's investment tax credit and, if so, whether the credit impermissibly discriminates against interstate commerce.
* **Question Granted:** The investment-credit Commerce Clause question and the expressly added question whether respondents have standing to challenge Ohio Revised Code §5733.33. The separate municipal property-tax judgment is not reopened.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit upheld a local property-tax exemption but invalidated the state franchise-tax investment credit. It did not address standing; the district court had relied on municipal taxpayer standing.
* **Relief Requested:** The company and state officials seek reversal of the invalidation; taxpayers seek to preserve it. Absence of federal jurisdiction would require removing the merits adjudication without deciding the credit's validity.
* **Material Record:** Ohio offered a franchise-tax credit tied to qualifying in-state investment, alongside Toledo's property-tax exemption, to support DaimlerChrysler's expansion. Taxpayer plaintiffs alleged depletion of government revenue and consequent fiscal injury. They filed in state court; defendants removed, and plaintiffs initially sought remand. The current standing defense relies on state taxpayer status, alleged effects on local revenue distributions, and the relation between the state and municipal incentives. These theories do not identify an assessment imposed on a plaintiff because of the challenged credit.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Standing must exist for the particular state-credit challenge. The disputed tax incentive is not converted into municipal action by including it in one development package. Claims of displacement or Michigan investment injury were not developed as current merits-brief grounds; this brief does not revive them. Removal raises a distinct question about the disposition required by §1447(c).
* **Relevant Dates:** The appealed opinion was entered October 19, 2004; rehearing was denied January 18, 2005. Argument occurred March 1, 2006. The applicable credit is the version governing the investment, notwithstanding later phaseout measures.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Wilkins, Tax Commissioner for Ohio v. Cuno is the consolidated state-official docket within this single listed matter. Steele v. Industrial Development Board involved supported local fiscal injury from an affirmative municipal financing decision, not generalized state revenue depletion.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-05-15; governing decisions through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the question presented.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article III injury, causation and redressability are independently required. Standing precedes a federal merits judgment. Section 1367 does not itself remove constitutional limitations; §1447(c) governs jurisdictionally defective removals.

### Issue 1: Fiscal injury, related claims, and the permissible judgment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Steele v. Industrial Development Board permits municipal taxpayer standing on supported probable local fiscal effects caused by the challenged local financing decision and redressable through control of that authority. It rejects policy disagreement and speculative foregone transactions. Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife and Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw require concrete injury and supported causal and remedial connections. None makes taxpayer status alone an interest in every state treasury decision.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable municipal-standing rule requires a supported probable revenue connection, not an exact loss calculation or municipal repayment guarantee. A claimant must connect the particular challenged act and requested remedy to the qualifying injury; an adequately supported different claim supplies no missing injury.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; 28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1367, 1441 and 1447(c); the Commerce Clause; Ohio's operative investment-credit provisions. The Commerce Clause prohibits specified discriminatory burdens, but it does not eliminate Article III's plaintiff-specific requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lapides v. Board of Regents requires remand under §1447(c) when original jurisdiction is absent, distinguishing discretionary treatment of supplemental claims after a valid federal claim ends. Frothingham v. Mellon and Doremus v. Board of Education reject generalized federal or state taxpayer grievances. Flast v. Cohen supplies a bounded Establishment Clause exception, not a general economic-policy exception. Those preexisting authorities do not decide whether a separately proved local injury exists here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law permits an actually supported local fiscal claim and rejects abstract objections. It does not establish that every state tax credit reduces a particular plaintiff's resources or that supplemental jurisdiction cures an Article III deficiency.
* **Open Point:** Whether the current distribution and related-claim theories supply a sufficiently concrete injury, and the correct disposition of the removed credit challenge if they do not.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The taxpayers defend the Sixth Circuit's discrimination analysis and argue that reduced receipts burden both state and municipal taxpayers. They also contend that an existing municipal challenge supports related review of the state incentive. The company and state officials contest those fiscal assumptions and insist on a concrete injury from the state credit itself; on the merits they defend investment incentives as permissible tax policy. The lower court's municipal-standing premise did not establish the separate state-credit injury. Neither side's position on the credit's economic desirability resolves jurisdiction. No distinct Solicitor General merits proposal is established by the materials used here.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find the identified standing theories inadequate, vacate the state-credit merits judgment, and direct an appropriate jurisdictional disposition. Because this action was removed, the mandate should address remand to state court rather than assume that failure of federal standing extinguishes the claim everywhere. If concrete standing is established, the Court could reach the Commerce Clause issue and sustain or reject the investment credit; that route would require resolving discrimination and permissible tax distinctions, not assuming the merits from the asserted revenue loss. The local property-tax adjudication is outside this review.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A jurisdictional rule may distinguish genuine local fiscal injury from generalized state revenue claims and reject standing by association. It need not announce a universal rule about all tax expenditures, the merits of development incentives, or every alternative basis for municipal standing.

**Material Uncertainty:** The federal record supports the presented fiscal theories, not an adjudicated quantified personal loss. The removed-case posture makes the form of jurisdictional relief material.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Steele relied on concrete evidence of probable taxable alternative financing and defendants' control of the resulting local decision. Here the state credit's projected fiscal consequences and later legislative spending choices supply no comparable personal connection. Supplemental jurisdiction concerns the statutory reach of an already valid federal case, not a license to adjudicate an injury the plaintiffs have not suffered. Preserving that distinction avoids treating ordinary economic uncertainty as a categorical bar while enforcing the actual constitutional predicate.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Steele v. Industrial Development Board at its evidentiary scope; apply Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife and Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw without adding an exact-dollar requirement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The credit’s Commerce Clause validity and any state-court standing question are outside a federal adjudication lacking Article III jurisdiction.

# 65. Sereboff v. Mid Atlantic Medical Services, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sereboff v. Mid Atlantic Medical Services, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-260; judgment below, 407 F.3d 212.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a judgment enforcing a health plan's reimbursement provision against preserved settlement funds.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-05-15. Calendar date for merits review.
* **Question Presented:** Whether ERISA §502(a)(3) authorizes a fiduciary to enforce a reimbursement provision through an equitable lien on identifiable third-party recovery held by the beneficiaries.
* **Question Granted:** The merits question stated above; no broader question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed the district court's recovery from the identified settlement fund in relevant part.
* **Relief Requested:** The Sereboffs seek reversal of the reimbursement award as unauthorized legal relief. Mid Atlantic seeks enforcement of its claimed share of the fund, subject to the plan's allocation terms.
* **Material Record:** The employer plan paid $74,869.37 for injuries from an automobile accident. Its third-party-recovery clause required reimbursement from recoveries, net of prorated reasonable fees and costs, and rejected an automatic reduction merely because full claimed damages were not recovered. The beneficiaries settled for $750,000, held the proceeds, and agreed during litigation to preserve the disputed sum in an investment account. The fiduciary seeks that particular fund rather than payment from unspecified general assets.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The actual remedy and the defendants' connection to the property matter more than the complaint's equitable label. The issue granted concerns the equitable category; distinct arguments about whether otherwise equitable relief is appropriate must be preserved to receive review. The record is not a dissipated-fund or remote transferee case.
* **Relevant Dates:** The accident occurred June 22, 2000. The Fourth Circuit decided May 4, 2005, and denied rehearing June 1. Supreme Court argument occurred March 28, 2006.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion requires an additional brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-05-15; governing decisions through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the question presented.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 502(a)(3), 29 U.S.C. §1132(a)(3), authorizes a fiduciary to obtain appropriate equitable relief enforcing plan terms. The claimant must establish an authorized equitable category and its actual predicates; contractual entitlement alone does not supply statutory remedial authority.

### Issue 1: An agreed lien on an acquired, identifiable recovery

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Mertens v. Hewitt Associates limits §502(a)(3) to traditionally equitable categories while preserving genuine monetary equity. Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson rejects personal collection of an accrued contractual debt dressed as equity, requiring the relevant property connection for a trust or lien. Its express limits reject a universal tracing formula for every equitable remedy. Harris Trust and Savings Bank v. Salomon Smith Barney preserves authentic equitable recipient claims and defenses.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the remedy, the particular property, the equitable entitlement, and the proper holder. A demand against general assets differs from enforcement against a specifically obligated recovery. Varity Corp. v. Howe preserves the additional requirement that equitable relief be appropriate where adequate ERISA relief otherwise exists.
* **Other Governing Law:** ERISA §502(a)(3) and the plan's actual reimbursement language. The source of a lien may be an agreement sufficiently identifying a future fund and the share promised; statutory equitable limits remain independent of plan drafting.
* **Material Other Authority:** Barnes v. Alexander recognizes an equitable lien on a specifically promised share of a future contingent fee once the identified fund is obtained. It supplies a historical equitable category, not a rule allowing any payment obligation to become a lien by nomenclature.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Great-West already excludes unsupported personal debt collection and requires the relevant property-holder connection. It does not hold that the plaintiff's original expenditures must themselves have become the assets against which every agreement-based lien is enforced.
* **Open Point:** Whether this agreement and retained recovery satisfy an equitable lien's predicates without tracing the plan's medical payments into the settlement dollars.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Sereboffs contend that reimbursement is contractual money collection and that equitable restitution requires a tracing connection to funds originally transferred by the plaintiff. They also invoke equitable limits on subrogation. Mid Atlantic answers that a specific reimbursement agreement charges the actual settlement fund and that the lien is different from generic restitution based on unjust enrichment. The Fourth Circuit treated the property in the beneficiaries' possession as decisive. The United States supports the plan, relying on agreement-based equitable liens and tracing to the settlement, without requiring tracing to the plan's earlier payments. That proposal supports enforcement against this fund, not unrestricted personal liability.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could recognize an agreement-based lien and affirm recovery from the identified fund, leaving any properly preserved allocation matter to the lower court. It could instead classify this demand as legal debt and reverse the §502(a)(3) award. A narrower remand would be warranted if the agreement failed to identify the recovery or the record failed to establish possession; neither gap is apparent in the described record. A decision on general-assets liability is unnecessary.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The lawful distinction turns on a specifically charged fund, the agreed share, and the holder connection. A categorical rule that every reimbursement provision is equitable would exceed the preserved category and contradict the entering debt limitation. A universal original-payment tracing rule would exceed what Great-West adopted.

**Material Uncertainty:** The exact award must respect reasonable prorated fees and costs under the agreement. Questions about additional appropriateness defenses have preservation limits and should not be silently treated as resolved on the merits.

# 66. S. D. Warren Co. v. Maine Board of Environmental Protection

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** S. D. Warren Co. v. Maine Board of Environmental Protection
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-1527; judgment below, 2005 ME 27, 868 A.2d 210.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of Maine's final judgment sustaining Clean Water Act certification requirements for federally licensed hydroelectric facilities.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-05-15. Calendar date for merits review.
* **Question Presented:** Whether releases of water from hydroelectric dams may constitute a discharge under Clean Water Act §401 even when no pollutant or water from a distinct source is added.
* **Question Granted:** The merits question stated above; no broader question is assumed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Maine Supreme Judicial Court rejected Warren's argument that its facilities lacked the discharge necessary for §401 certification.
* **Relief Requested:** Warren seeks reversal of the requirement and resulting certification conditions. Maine seeks affirmance of the certification trigger and preservation of the lawful conditions incorporated into the federal licenses.
* **Material Record:** Warren operates five hydroelectric dams on Maine's Presumpscot River. Water is impounded, passed through turbines, and released back into the river, bypassing portions of its channel. In connection with renewed federal licenses, Maine required water-quality certifications with minimum-flow and fish-and-eel-passage conditions. Warren sought the certifications under protest and exhausted state review while disputing the statutory trigger. The challenge does not turn on proof that an outside pollutant was introduced.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The preserved question concerns §401's discharge trigger, not whether every possible flow condition satisfies the statute. The facilities require federal licenses, and releases enter the river. The record establishes no certification waiver, defective notice or unrelated-condition finding.
* **Relevant Dates:** Maine's final decision was issued in 2005. Argument on the federal statutory question occurred February 21, 2006. Only the Clean Water Act and licensing regime in force for these proceedings are used.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion requires an additional brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-05-15; governing decisions through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the question presented.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A qualifying federal license requires certification, waiver within the statutory period, or the specified alternative certification authority. Judicial review of the state certificate and review of the federal license remain distinct proceedings.

### Issue 1: Discharge without pollutant addition and the resulting certification authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology requires an actual possible discharge before §401 applies. Once triggered, certification may impose conditions on the licensed activity needed to meet incorporated federal requirements and other appropriate state water-quality requirements. Its conceded discharges did not decide whether every dam release meets the trigger. South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe concerns the different addition requirement in §402.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable certification rule preserves both the trigger and the activity-wide scope of necessary conditions. Minimum flow may protect water quality, and water-allocation savings do not create a blanket exemption. Unrelated project policy remains outside §401.
* **Other Governing Law:** 33 U.S.C. §1341 uses discharge without the pollutant qualification found in §1342. Section 1362(16) includes discharge of pollutants within discharge; §1362(12) separately defines discharge of a pollutant by reference to addition from a point source. The distinct statutory formulations must retain operative meaning.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory text and settled administration of §401 by the relevant federal agencies support separating an unqualified outflow from §402's pollutant-addition inquiry. Agency practice is interpretive evidence, not an independent power to impose conditions Congress withheld.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering PUD law settles the consequence of a sufficient discharge and the legal limits of conditions, but its conceded trigger leaves this definitional question open. A §402 conclusion does not automatically decide §401.
* **Open Point:** Whether channeling and releasing the same river water through these generating facilities is a discharge for certification purposes.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Warren argues that the statutory association between discharge and pollutant discharge requires an addition; returning the same water therefore cannot trigger certification. It invokes §402 decisions and concerns about state interference with federal hydropower licensing. Maine responds that Congress used the unqualified term deliberately and that flow, temperature, oxygen and passage effects can implicate water quality without an added pollutant. The United States supports Maine on the ordinary meaning and statutory distinctions, preserving the separate §402 regime. The state court accepted the broader trigger; its judgment does not turn §401 into general project approval authority.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reading discharge as outflow supports affirmance and leaves necessary certification conditions effective. Importing an addition requirement supports reversal because the asserted trigger would be missing, without deciding that every hydropower condition is unlawful under other authority. A remand for a particular condition's compliance nexus would be appropriate only if such a preserved issue actually required findings; the granted question does not establish that need.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A trigger holding may govern same-river releases through licensed works while retaining the actual discharge requirement. It need not collapse §401 into §402, expand federal licensing jurisdiction, or decide a general unitary-waters theory under a different provision.

**Material Uncertainty:** The record description establishes release through the generating works. It does not supply a basis to resolve independent challenges to each fish-passage or flow specification. Modern amendments or agency rules are not imported into the 2006 event.

Here water actually leaves the licensed generating works and enters the river. Statutory context confirms the ordinary outflow reading without depending on a free-floating environmental purpose.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Clarify PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology at its reserved trigger and reaffirm its condition limits. Distinguish South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe's §402 inquiry. No overruling is proposed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of unchallenged individual certification conditions, activities without releases, and different jurisdictional-water disputes are outside the presented record.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Zedner v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-5992; judgment below, 401 F.3d 36.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Direct review of a federal conviction after denial of a timely Speedy Trial Act dismissal motion.

### Issue 1: Statutory exclusions, waiver, estoppel, and dismissal

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision authorizes unlimited prospective waiver of this statutory deadline. New Hampshire v. It does not create authority to replace an express statutory procedure with a private agreement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted exclusion and satisfy its predicates. General procedural equity operates within the governing statute. Estoppel requires attention to the actual positions and judicial acceptance, not the proposition that any defendant who asks for time loses the statute's protection.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§3161(c)(1), 3161(h)(8), and 3162(a)(2). Trial ordinarily must begin within 70 countable days; specified periods are excluded. An ends-of-justice continuance requires the judge's actual balancing of the public and defendant interests and reasons in the record. Section 3162 preserves the deliberate distinction between dismissal with and without prejudice.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Act protects public as well as individual interests. Ordinary harmless-error principles cannot simply eliminate the dismissal consequence Congress attached to an expired clock and a timely motion. The defendant's separate incompetency-period challenge implicates another express exclusion and need not be decided if the earlier violation requires relief.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The enacted scheme authorizes defined delay without making the defendant's consent a general exclusion. New Hampshire supplies a bounded integrity doctrine, not an automatic waiver substitute.
* **Open Point:** Whether the blanket waiver or asserted litigation inconsistency excuses the 91 days, whether findings can be supplied only after the relevant motion, and whether the statutory dismissal consequence can be treated as harmless.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Zedner argues that the public interest makes the prospective waiver ineffective, that the relevant continuance lacked statutory findings, and that a timely motion triggers dismissal. The United States does not defend unlimited waiver as a general rule; it emphasizes judicial estoppel where the defendant requested and benefited from an otherwise justifiable continuance. It argues that the court effectively considered appropriate factors and that the defendant should not obtain reversal for a defect he induced. It separately defends the incompetency exclusion and harmlessness. The Second Circuit accepted versions of those alternatives. The statutory findings and remedy, rather than the desirability of delay in the abstract, decide their legal sufficiency.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reject waiver and estoppel, count the 91 days and reverse for dismissal with a prejudice determination on remand. It may find an actually supported contemporaneous ends-of-justice decision or a valid narrow estoppel and affirm on that interval, then address the independently challenged competency period. It may adopt a harmless-error limitation, but must explain its compatibility with mandatory dismissal after a timely motion. Reversal does not automatically require dismissal with prejudice or foreclose reprosecution.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The case supports a clear rule about prospective waiver, judicial responsibility for exclusions and the statutory remedy. It does not require treating every delayed articulation of contemporaneous reasons as invalid, nor calculating every day of the entire prosecution when one interval is dispositive.

**Material Uncertainty:** The choice of dismissal with prejudice requires findings on remand. A factual assertion that the trial judge would have granted a proper continuance is not the missing contemporaneous finding itself.

# 75. Mohawk Industries, Inc. v. Williams

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Mohawk Industries, Inc. v. Williams
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-465; judgment below, 411 F.3d 1252.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Merits review of an interlocutory appellate judgment sustaining federal RICO allegations after partial denial of a motion to dismiss.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-06-05. Calendar date for merits review.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a corporation and outside recruiting businesses alleged to work together in a continuing unlawful recruitment arrangement can form a RICO enterprise distinct from the corporate defendant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit allowed the federal and state RICO theories to proceed at the pleading stage. It separately rejected the remaining wage-based unjust-enrichment claim; those state-law components are not automatically reopened by the limited federal enterprise question.

* **Material Record:** The complaint alleges that Mohawk and outside recruiters coordinated recruitment, transportation, placement and harboring of unauthorized workers, including concealment and assistance with false documentation. Recruiters allegedly received fees and Mohawk allegedly obtained a labor supply that depressed lawful employees' wages. These allegations identify an asserted continuing arrangement; neither immigration offenses nor wage effects are adjudicated facts. Corporate personnel and genuinely independent recruiters must not be conflated when assessing distinctness.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Pleading sufficiency is not proof of enterprise structure, operation, predicates or injury. The limited grant does not supply jurisdiction to decide every state-law component. Any immigration predicate must meet its complete statutory terms, not merely an allegation that some worker lacked authorization.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Eleventh Circuit decided June 9, 2005. Argument occurred April 26, 2006. The case follows Anza on the June 5 calendar.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Anza v. Ideal Steel Supply Corp. concerns tax fraud, pricing and competitor loss. This case concerns a recruitment association and employee wages; both require their own causal chain under §1964(c).
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-06-05; governing decisions through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.

The then-applicable pleading standard governs; later association-in-fact or pleading decisions are not imported.

### Issue 1: Corporate associations, distinctness, common purpose, and direction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Cedric Kushner Promotions v. King holds a natural person distinct from a separately incorporated enterprise even when the person owns it, while expressly reserving corporate defendants associated with entities including themselves. Reves v. Ernst & Young requires some role in directing the enterprise's affairs, not merely ordinary service or one's own affairs. Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp. separately requires factual and proximate causation for civil damages.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Do not merge enterprise identity, common activity, operation or management, predicates and injury into one generalized wrongdoing allegation. Economic benefit or a commercial relationship alone does not establish every element. Distinct returns to participants do not necessarily disprove a shared course of conduct.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1961(1), 1961(4), 1962(c), and 1964(c); the charged immigration predicates under 8 U.S.C. §1324 and their financial-gain condition for RICO inclusion. The enterprise definition uses includes and covers legal entities as well as informal associations; its application to mixed associations is the open construction.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Turkette recognizes an ongoing organization and associates functioning as a continuing unit for a common purpose, with enterprise and pattern separately established even where proof overlaps. It does not require an independently lawful purpose or formal charter.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Cedric Kushner resolves natural-person/corporation distinctness, not this corporate-association configuration. Reves requires direction of the association itself. A liability rule cannot be inferred simply by citing the distinct identities of the corporation and a vendor.
* **Open Point:** Whether legal entities can participate in an association-in-fact and whether this continuing recruitment arrangement supplies a distinct enterprise rather than the corporation conducting ordinary affairs through agents.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Mohawk argues that the statutory reference to individuals does not reach an informal association of corporations and that ordinary recruitment relationships do not create a distinct enterprise. It also emphasizes divergent incentives and the need for direction of enterprise affairs. The employees allege a continuing coordinated arrangement, not simply wage payments or independent vendors. The United States supports them on the enterprise question, construing includes as illustrative and distinguishing a sustained criminal collaboration from a commercial contract alone. The Eleventh Circuit found the allegations sufficient, with proof deferred. No party's economic prediction supplies the missing civil causation findings.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reject categorical exclusion of corporate associations and affirm the enterprise ruling at the pleading stage, preserving remaining defenses. It could require a more concrete allegation of an association distinct from the corporation and remand for that standard, or dismiss if the pleaded configuration necessarily describes only corporate affairs. Any effective intervening Anza ruling must be applied to the distinct wage-injury allegations; absent one, Holmes remains controlling.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The record supports a rule about mixed corporate associations, common purpose and independent enterprise direction. It does not support treating every commercial supply chain as racketeering, nor requiring every participant to receive identical benefits. The separate causal inquiry must remain explicit.

**Material Uncertainty:** Whether the recruiters were independent, whether the arrangement functioned as a unit, and whether the alleged immigration crimes caused wage injury remain unproved. No intervening causation ruling is established.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening OT2005 judgment is established.

### Issue 1: Governing contract law and an enforceable beneficiary interest

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Orff v. United States distinguishes sovereign consent from asserted contractual benefit and leaves beneficiary status open. O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC and Atherton v. Then distinguish a contract intended to confer enforceable third-party rights from a public project expected to benefit many people. * **Existing Rule's Reach:** No entering Supreme Court holding declares these landowners intended beneficiaries. Their direct service agreements may support claims against irrigation districts, but do not automatically bind nonsignatories to perpetual upstream deliveries.

### Issue 2: Related state litigation and exceptional abstention

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Colorado River Water Conservation District v. United States preserves the ordinarily obligatory exercise of federal jurisdiction and recognizes a narrow exceptional concurrent-proceeding doctrine. Moses H. Cone Memorial Hospital v. Mercury Construction requires confidence that the state proceeding can adequately resolve the relevant controversy. Quackenbush v. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Related subject matter alone is insufficient. * **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1441 and 1447; the actual state pleadings and requested declarations. No general state-court exclusivity over federal reclamation-contract interpretation is established.
* **Material Other Authority:** The state action's focus on obligations of irrigation districts under landowner service agreements differs from this claim against the Conservation District under the project instruments. The legal ability to adjudicate a question is distinct from whether the pending action will resolve it.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The ordinary rule requires adjudication absent the relevant exceptional circumstances. The record permits a concrete comparison of proceedings without deciding the whole field of water-rights abstention.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pending state case supplies a sufficiently complete substitute for this federal-contract dispute.

The landowners rely on water-delivery purposes, state-law appurtenance, their payments and contract-validation proceedings to establish intended benefit. They also seek abstention in favor of their related state action. The Conservation District answers that duties run to the contracting districts and that its federal obligations cannot be enlarged by separate agreements it never signed; it alternatively argues that the United States is indispensable. The Ninth Circuit rejected beneficiary status and abstention without reaching that alternative. The actual instrument excerpts support an absence-of-enforceable-intent analysis, not an invented express blanket disclaimer.

The Court may affirm retained jurisdiction and reject the asserted beneficiary right on the contract terms.
