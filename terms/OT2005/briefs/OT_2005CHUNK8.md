# 92. Burlington Northern & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. White

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Burlington Northern & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. White
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-259; 548 U.S. 53 (2006).
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the identified lower-court judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-06-22; historical calendar placement.
* **Question Presented:** Whether Title VII retaliation requires an adverse change in the terms or conditions of employment, and what degree of adversity is necessary; whether the reassignment and unpaid suspension established on this record satisfy the governing standard.
* **Question Granted:** The question stated above; no broader question is presented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirming a jury verdict for White under its materially adverse employment-action framework.
* **Relief Requested:** The railroad seeks reversal of the retaliation judgment. White seeks affirmance based on the reassignment and suspension.
* **Material Record:** White, the only woman in her department, performed forklift work after being hired as a track laborer. After she complained of sex-based treatment, the railroad reassigned her from forklift duty to more arduous track work. A later disciplinary suspension kept her without pay for 37 days before the railroad reinstated her with backpay through its grievance process. The jury found retaliation and awarded compensatory damages. The claims concern the comparative duties and the interim loss and uncertainty, not merely job titles or the final restoration of wages.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review follows a completed civil trial. The preserved standard-of-adversity question must be separated from causation, evidentiary sufficiency, and any independently unchallenged verdict determinations. Later reimbursement does not erase the need to identify what legally cognizable harm occurred while pay was withheld.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date above fixes the legal cutoff; later decisions and enactments supply no entering rule.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Bennett v. Hendrix concerns constitutional retaliation against private citizens and qualified immunity. Hartman v. Moore concerns retaliatory prosecution. Similar deterrence vocabulary does not make their distinct sources, causation requirements, and defenses interchangeable.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-06-22; precedent through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Bennett and Hartman concern constitutional retaliation in different settings; neither establishes Title VII’s statutory reach here.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Court must establish the applicable review jurisdiction and decide only the preserved questions on the existing record. Legal conclusions receive independent review; disputed facts remain subject to the governing trial and appellate rules.

### Issue 1: Actionable retaliation under Title VII

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Robinson v. Shell Oil Co. includes former employees within §704(a), without deciding the actionable-harm or employment-nexus standard. Jackson v. Birmingham Board of Education recognizes retaliation’s relationship to enforcement in a different statutory setting.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the actual prohibition and preserve proof of protected activity, retaliatory causation, and a legally sufficient adverse act. Robinson resolves protected status, not all elements. No current reusable rule supplies the precise adversity threshold asked here.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §2000e-3(a) prohibits discrimination because of opposition or participation; its wording differs from §2000e-2(a)’s express employment-terms language. The applicable remedial provisions govern compensation for proved harm.
* **Material Other Authority:** Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth addresses tangible employment actions for vicarious liability in harassment, not the complete scope of the separate retaliation prohibition. The EEOC’s interpretations may inform meaning according to their reasoning and consistency.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Former-employee coverage is settled. The distinct retaliation provision’s workplace boundary and degree of necessary adversity remain open under entering law.
* **Open Point:** Whether material deterrence of a reasonable worker supplies the standard, and whether workplace terms delimit all prohibited retaliation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Reassignment to less desirable work and the suspension supported liability under its materially adverse employment approach. **Railroad:** Title VII regulates employment relationships; retaliation should require a materially adverse employment action, and restored pay removes actionable suspension harm. **White:** The separate statutory wording protects access to enforcement from materially deterrent employer action, whether or not that action alters formal job terms; arduous reassignment and more than a month without income qualify. **Solicitor General:** The United States likewise urged a link to compensation, terms, conditions, or employment status. **EEOC:** Its then-current retaliation guidance described a broader deterrence principle. That difference must be considered rather than presenting every federal participant as proposing the same interpretation. Ordinary workplace slights and lack of retaliatory causation remain limiting issues under the broader account.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm under a materially deterrent reasonable-worker standard if the evidence supports both challenged acts. Affirm under a narrower employment-action standard if these particular acts satisfy it, while recognizing that this leaves other applications unresolved. Reverse or remand if the instructions permitted liability for immaterial acts and the error could have affected the judgment. Backpay can reduce a wage remedy without logically extinguishing all interim injury or distress.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A statutory material-deterrence rule can reach retaliatory actions outside formal employment terms, while excluding petty slights and ordinary minor annoyances. A terms-and-conditions rule would exclude serious external pressure lacking the required employment connection. The fact-specific application must evaluate the actual comparative jobs and suspension circumstances rather than classify every transfer or later-corrected suspension identically.

**Record, Source, and Date Uncertainty:** The official opinion supplies the material trial evidence and arguments, but the entire trial record is not separately supplied. No finding that every reassignment, every discipline decision, or every administrative correction is retaliatory follows. The standards in later retaliation cases are not effective entering law.

### Issue 1: Material interference with access to statutory protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** A final trial judgment presents both the legal standard and supported concrete applications.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** The statutory reach and minimum adversity of employer retaliation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open statutory question using the text and Robinson’s enforcement context.

One step broader would turn routine unpleasantness into a federal tort. One step narrower would import an employment-terms condition Congress omitted.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Title VII’s retaliation provision reaches employer action that, in context, would materially deter a reasonable worker from protected opposition or participation, whether or not it changes formal employment terms. * **Next-Case Guidance:** Evaluate the actual burden on a reasonable worker in the claimant’s circumstances. Harm outside formal job terms can qualify, while trivial friction cannot. Reimbursement may reduce compensation without erasing the retaliatory adversity already imposed.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Robinson holds that retaliation protection survives the employment relationship because the relevant text and enforcement operation require it; it expressly left harm standards open. That reasoning supports reading the retaliation prohibition according to its own words. Ellerth’s category serves attribution of supervisory harassment and cannot silently rewrite another provision. The railroad’s narrow view would permit serious coercion outside job terms while forbidding a smaller formal employment change. Materiality avoids that anomaly without federalizing ordinary friction. On the verdict record, arduous work and prolonged loss of income are concrete burdens; later restoration bears on relief but does not make the interval disappear.

# 93. Woodford v. Ngo

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Woodford v. Ngo
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 05-416; 548 U.S. 81 (2006).
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the identified lower-court judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2006-06-22; historical calendar placement.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a prisoner satisfies 42 U.S.C. §1997e(a) by presenting a grievance rejected as untimely, once no further administrative remedy remains, or must comply with the available grievance process’s critical procedural requirements.
* **Question Granted:** The question stated above; no broader question is presented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing the district court’s exhaustion dismissal because no administrative remedy remained available after the late grievance was rejected.
* **Relief Requested:** California officials seek reversal and enforcement of procedural compliance. Ngo seeks continued federal adjudication notwithstanding the grievance rejection.
* **Material Record:** Ngo, a California prisoner, challenged restrictions imposed after release from segregation. His administrative grievance was rejected as untimely under the prison system’s deadline, and further administrative efforts did not obtain merits consideration. The district court dismissed his federal civil-rights action for nonexhaustion. The appellate dispute addresses the legal consequence of the procedural rejection, not a finding that the underlying prison restrictions were constitutional.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1997e(a) requires exhaustion before a covered action is brought. This case does not decide the separate merits, damages entitlement, or ultimate facts concerning actual access to the grievance process. A missed deadline and a genuinely unavailable process are different conditions; identifying the applicable deadline and whether the process could actually be used matters.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date above fixes the legal cutoff; later decisions and enactments supply no entering rule.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Beard v. Banks concerns the substantive justification for prison restrictions; its later scheduled review does not resolve exhaustion.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2006-06-22; precedent through June 27, 2005 and applicable enacted law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established as effective.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that changes the present issue under entering law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Court must establish the applicable review jurisdiction and decide only the preserved questions on the existing record. Legal conclusions receive independent review; disputed facts remain subject to the governing trial and appellate rules.

### Issue 1: Required use of actually available administrative procedures

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Booth v. Churner requires exhaustion of an actually accessible responsive process even when it cannot award requested damages. Porter v. Nussle extends that obligation to all prison-life episodes, including individual excessive-force claims. Both expressly leave a comprehensive procedural-default rule open.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Availability is actual, not presumed from a written manual. The statute is mandatory for responsive available remedies; a damages-only prayer does not excuse it.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1997e(a), as amended by the PLRA, and the applicable California grievance procedures; §1997e(c)(2)’s distinct permission to dismiss certain defective merits claims without exhaustion.
* **Material Other Authority:** McCarthy v. Madigan’s nonstatutory discretion does not override the later statutory command. McNeil v. United States enforces its own statutory sequence; habeas procedural-default doctrine arises in a separate framework.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Covered claims must be submitted through available procedures. Booth and Porter do not settle whether final procedural rejection after completed administrative review also forfeits an independent federal action.
* **Open Point:** Whether the enacted exhaustion requirement incorporates a separate procedural-default sanction after a prisoner has pursued the grievance process through its available conclusion.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Ngo had no remaining administrative remedy and therefore met the exhaustion requirement when he sued. **Officials:** A prisoner cannot exhaust by withholding a timely grievance until the process closes; Congress required the institution’s fair opportunity to address the complaint. **Ngo:** Congress required exhaustion of available remedies, not an unwritten permanent forfeiture rule; short or poorly administered prison deadlines can foreclose judicial rights regardless of claim strength. **United States:** As amicus urging reversal, it supports proper exhaustion rather than deliberate or accidental bypass through procedural rejection. **Material qualification:** The statute says available. The case does not establish that every procedural rejection is legally correct or that obstruction, inability to obtain forms, or misleading directions must be ignored. These predicates require evidence if genuinely disputed.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may construe exhaustion to require compliance with critical available procedures, reverse, and remand for application of that rule. Alternatively, it may distinguish completed administrative pursuit from federal procedural default, affirm the restoration of Ngo’s action, and leave the independent federal merits for adjudication. Both paths preserve Booth’s and Porter’s duty to use a responsive available process and reject a damages-based bypass.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The procedural-rejection record supports a rule addressing completed grievance review. It need not decide every form of inaction, obstruction, pleading, or administrative reopening.

**Material Uncertainty:** The underlying prison restrictions and ultimate remedies remain unadjudicated. Neither an untimely grievance nor successful exhaustion establishes a constitutional violation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Exhaustion requires a real opportunity for administrative response

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory interpretation is preserved; factual access must not be decided by appellate assumption.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether final procedural rejection after completed available grievance review also forfeits the prisoner’s independent federal action.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the question Booth and Porter reserved by separating required administrative pursuit from an additional forfeiture of federal claims. Preserve mandatory exhaustion of remedies actually available to the prisoner.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers a grievance actually submitted and carried through available administrative review, whether rejected procedurally or on the merits. A broader declaration that mere expiration always exhausts remedies would disregard the duty to pursue a responsive process. A narrower requirement excusing only official obstruction would add a federal forfeiture sanction the statute does not state.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prisoner who submits the grievance and pursues all actually available administrative review exhausts §1997e(a) even when officials finally reject it as untimely; the statute does not separately forfeit the federal claim because of that procedural rejection. The prisoner must complete the review or reopening steps actually prescribed and available under the governing grievance rules, and administrative deadlines remain enforceable within that process. Ngo completed the available review, so lateness alone cannot bar this action.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Federal prison-life actions after completed administrative pursuit, including constitutional and statutory claims. A damages-only request still does not excuse exhaustion. This holding does not recognize exhaustion from mere inaction while a responsive process remains available.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts distinguish a prisoner who abandons an available grievance or appeal from one who obtains a final administrative rejection through the available process. An actually prescribed avenue for reviewing a late grievance must be used; hypothetical or indefinite discretionary reconsideration requests are not additional exhaustion requirements.
* **Transition:** Apply this construction to pending covered actions. It establishes no independent basis to reopen a final federal judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Booth requires use of a responsive process despite its inability to award damages; Porter applies that sequence to individual prison episodes. Neither decides that a procedural rejection extinguishes the later federal action. Section 1997e(a)’s words impose exhaustion before suit, whereas a permanent default sanction answers a different question. Prison officials may enforce their deadlines without receiving authority to determine, by those deadlines alone, whether a federal right remains judicially enforceable. Ngo sought a grievance decision and pursued review; officials chose a procedural disposition. Unlike an appeal reviewing that administrative decision, this action independently alleges a federal violation. The analogy to habeas default therefore cannot supply the omitted sanction. Administrative opportunity and screening explain the required sequence but do not authorize rewriting it as permanent forfeiture. No discretionary futility exception or exemption for constitutional claims is created.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Booth v. Churner and Porter v. Nussle reaffirmed at their mandatory-exhaustion holdings; their reserved procedural-default question is resolved without importing habeas default into an independent civil-rights action.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits of the prison restrictions, recovery, and a comprehensive pleading allocation are outside this exhaustion question. A prisoner who never invokes the process presents a different record.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Laboratory Corp. of America Holdings v. Metabolite Laboratories, Inc.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** State Street Bank & Trust Co. v. **Below:** The Federal Circuit sustained the claim and infringement consequences under the issues it addressed, without directly resolving the present §101 formulation. **LabCorp:** The claim makes the natural relationship itself the decisive act of infringement; generic testing followed by recognition cannot supply an eligible invention. Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted if the preservation defect prevents sound merits review; that threshold path leaves the lower judgment intact without approving its substantive reach.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A merits holding can address this claim’s generic measurement followed by inference while preserving patents on concrete diagnostic inventions with additional qualifying limitations. A broad rule excluding all medical diagnosis, or approving all useful test results, would extend beyond the claim and the existing principle/application distinction. A threshold dismissal would establish no eligibility rule.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The properly presented facial challenge squarely reaches the statutory tie command; no factual finding of equilibrium is needed to evaluate the rule it imposes.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** A State may define lawful mitigation procedures and avoid numerical weighting, but may not command death merely because mitigation fails to outweigh aggravation. Eligibility proof remains distinct from the affirmative individualized selection of death.
* **Transition:** The rule applies to Marsh and nonfinal cases under ordinary direct-review principles. No special reopening of final judgments is proposed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Buchanan upheld a charge because it left an effective life option after aggravation and permitted the independent culpability force of mitigation. Tuilaepa and Harris reject compulsory numerical machinery, but do not make a State’s most severe punishment a permissible default whenever mitigation fails to prevail. Ring supplies jury proof for eligibility, not an answer to ultimate selection. ### Issue 1: The detained person may invoke the promised notification right

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The claims arise in existing criminal and collateral proceedings and seek effect for a treaty command directed to the detained individual.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Article 36’s individual terms are judicially invocable.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the question Breard expressly reserved through the treaty text.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Recognition follows the specific personal notification command. One step broader would infer a universal treaty damages action. One step narrower would render the individually promised advice judicially meaningless.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Article 36 gives a detained foreign national a right to timely advice of the opportunity for consular notification that the person may invoke in a properly presented criminal or collateral proceeding. The claimant must establish coverage and violation by the ordinary factual standard of that proceeding; the right does not itself prescribe suppression, compensation, or release.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The notification and access rights actually stated in Article 36, within proceedings otherwise lawfully before a court.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A qualifying detainee may invoke Article 36’s personal notification command through an available criminal or collateral proceeding. Enforceability of that command does not itself create a damages action or a particular exclusionary remedy.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text identifies the individual’s rights and requires that the individual be informed. The interstate character of the treaty does not erase that chosen beneficiary. Breard left enforceability open, so recognizing it here is a new merits holding rather than reversal of an existing categorical bar. LaGrand and Avena provide substantial interpretive assistance consistent with the text, while a judgment’s binding effect remains governed by its own parties, scope, and domestic legal channel.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Breard v. Greene applied at its interim scope; no final Breard holding is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A freestanding damages action and direct enforcement of another case’s ICJ judgment are outside the presented claims.

### Issue 2: Full effect requires a real corrective opportunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The separate preservation and remedial records are established, but the causal facts necessary for final relief are not.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether nonnotification automatically excludes statements and when default defeats treaty effect.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the Convention’s domestic-procedure permission together with its full-effect qualification and resolve their previously open relationship.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The source requires an effective opportunity, not automatic reversal. One step broader would excuse every treaty default and suppress unrelated evidence. One step narrower would let a State cause ignorance and rely on that same ignorance to extinguish review.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An Article 36 violation does not automatically require suppression, but domestic procedure must permit meaningful review of a supported claim that the violation caused loss of timely assertion or material consular assistance. A claimant must identify the causal loss with evidence, not speculation; when official nonnotification caused the absence of a realistic earlier opportunity, default cannot alone bar that review. Final relief must correct the demonstrated harm through an authorized remedy proportionate to its effect.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Notification claims in which preservation, actual knowledge, lost assistance, and remedy are genuinely at issue. A timely informed detainee or an unrelated trial error presents a different causal case.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply ordinary preservation and external-cause principles together with Article 36(2)’s full-effect condition. Require a supported causal account of the lost opportunity; neither nonnotification alone nor speculative consular assistance establishes relief.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Coleman and Carrier preserve actual procedural defaults and external-cause analysis. They do not require ignoring a treaty term qualifying domestic procedure. Breard refused interim relief on an insufficient submission rather than resolving every later claimant’s cause and prejudice. Notification can matter through legal advice, language support, investigation, or access, but none is guaranteed and none may be invented. Sanchez-Llamas therefore receives a causal remedial inquiry rather than automatic exclusion. Bustillo receives an inquiry into the actual lost opportunity, not a judicial finding that the alleged alternative perpetrator was guilty.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Breard v. Greene preserved; Coleman v. Thompson and Murray v. Carrier applied consistently with the treaty’s specific full-effect condition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The missing causal assistance facts prevent a present suppression or new-trial determination for either petitioner.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A ruling that the Constitution imposes no categorical mid-decade prohibition would leave each preserved challenge to the particular assignments for adjudication under the applicable standards. ### Issue 2: Evidence negating the State’s retained knowledge requirement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Montana v. Admission for another limited purpose does not cure an express prohibition on element use.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The prosecution proves actual elements beyond a reasonable doubt. * **Open Point:** Whether the specific mental-disease exclusion has sufficient justification and whether otherwise admissible evidence was prevented from rebutting the retained mental state here.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Arizona could define insanity through wrongfulness and channel mental-disorder evidence to that defense. **Clark:** Removing cognitive incapacity from insanity and excluding mental-disease evidence on mens rea together deny a meaningful opportunity to show he did not knowingly kill an officer; accepting diagnosis does not settle what he understood. **Arizona:** Traditional insanity formulations vary; psychiatric diagnoses and ultimate capacity opinions can confuse legal and medical categories, and ordinary observations remain usable on intent. **United States:** As amicus urging affirmance, it supports state latitude and the distinction between offense proof and affirmative insanity. **Material limitation:** Neither side can treat schizophrenia as automatic innocence or insanity. The actual prosecution elements remain legally fixed, and a defendant’s failure to prove an excuse does not establish those elements beyond a reasonable doubt.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Sustain the wrongfulness formulation while reversing the categorical evidentiary exclusion as applied to otherwise reliable mental-state rebuttal. Sustain both if the exclusion has sufficient justification and preserves meaningful element contest. Invalidate the insanity formulation only if a constitutional minimum requires the omitted component, a broader conclusion not compelled by existing precedent. If the trial judge’s scope of consideration is genuinely unclear, remand for a record-based determination; no appellate finding of sanity, knowledge, or lack of intent can replace it.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can preserve substantial state authority over insanity while enforcing the existing retained-element rule. It can protect reliable explanatory evidence without compelling ultimate legal opinions or accepting diagnosis as conclusive. A categorical constitutional insanity test would reach further than correcting this evidentiary barrier. Any new trial follows from the unlawful restriction, not a finding that the State lacked enough admissible evidence to prosecute.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Wrongfulness may define the separate excuse

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statute’s definition is preserved and can be assessed independently from the evidence restriction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether due process invariably requires both traditional insanity alternatives.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the distinction between a genuine affirmative defense and proof of the actual offense; decline a new universal insanity formula.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The answer preserves legislative choice over this excuse without weakening the offense burden. One step broader would authorize eliminating all meaningful mental-state contest. One step narrower would constitutionalize a historical formulation despite the distinct protected element right.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Due process does not require a State to include both traditional M’Naghten formulations in its insanity defense merely because it recognizes wrongfulness as the governing excuse, provided the State still proves its actual offense elements and preserves a meaningful opportunity to contest them. Clark has not established that the wrongfulness formulation alone is unconstitutional.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The definition of this affirmative insanity defense. Competence to stand trial and the admissibility of element-rebuttal evidence remain separate.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A lawful insanity definition does not alter the prosecution’s burden on retained offense elements. Failure to prove the excuse does not establish knowledge or purpose.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Patterson permits genuine offense and defense definition, while Egelhoff forbids using those labels to evade retained elements. Medina and Cooper protect trial competence and do not decide what responsibility excuse Arizona must enact. A person may fail to establish the chosen excuse yet still defeat the prosecution’s knowledge proof, which is why upholding the defense definition cannot decide the next issue.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Montana v. Egelhoff reaffirmed; Patterson v. New York applied; Medina v. California and Cooper v. Oklahoma distinguished.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: An insanity channel cannot block reliable element rebuttal

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The reported rule expressly prevented mental-disease evidence from performing its otherwise relevant element-rebuttal function, and the trial is on direct review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Arizona may exclude that use because it separately recognizes insanity.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Egelhoff to mental-disease evidence and reject the categorical channel; preserve ordinary reliability controls.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within retained-element evidence. The State must prove the offense it enacted against a meaningful evidentiary contest. One step broader would compel every speculative expert opinion. One step narrower would let the same forbidden exclusion survive merely because illness replaces intoxication.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State that retains an intentional-or-knowing offense element may not categorically exclude otherwise admissible reliable mental-disease evidence rebutting that element merely because the evidence also supports an insanity defense. The prosecution retains proof beyond a reasonable doubt; the defendant must satisfy ordinary relevance and admissibility requirements, and the State must justify any categorical restriction. Arizona’s channel to insanity does not provide sufficient justification on this record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Reliable observations and explanations bearing on the actual mental state. Unsupported diagnosis-to-innocence inferences, irrelevant symptoms, and properly excluded ultimate legal opinions gain no immunity from ordinary evidence rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Assess psychiatric evidence for relevance and reliability in its proposed element use. A general concern about experts does not justify excluding otherwise admissible evidence merely because it also supports a separately burdened insanity defense.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Egelhoff held that allowing intoxication evidence for memory or physical capacity did not cure a ban on its mental-state use. Arizona’s insanity-only channel repeats that defect, with a separate defense burden that cannot replace Winship’s prosecution burden. General concerns about psychiatric disagreement justify careful reliability decisions, not exclusion of all otherwise admissible explanatory evidence, especially where the disease itself is undisputed. Chambers and Rock preserve meaningful defense proof while permitting justified limits. The judge’s role as factfinder does not permit an appellate assumption that the explicit restriction was ignored. Winship, Egelhoff, Chambers, Rock, and Martin perform distinct functions within the disputed evidence question. Stone would state the retained-element principle as one administrable rule, preserving ordinary reliability limits and the independent affirmative-defense burden rather than merging every evidentiary objection into a new unrestricted admission test.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Montana v. Egelhoff applied and extended to mental disease; In re Winship, Chambers v. Mississippi, Rock v. Arkansas, and Martin v. Ohio reaffirmed at their respective functions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular expert-method disputes and a genuinely different statutory offense definition are not presented. Final harmlessness requires the actual trial record and cannot be supplied by inference here.
