# 1. Dye v. Hofbauer

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dye v. Hofbauer
* **Citation or Docket:** 546 U.S. 1; No. 04-8384.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the federal appellate judgment denying habeas relief.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-10-11; calendar date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal prosecutorial-misconduct claim was fairly presented when the state appellate brief identified its federal basis, and whether a federal habeas petition sufficiently pleaded that claim through an expressly incorporated supporting brief.
* **Question Granted:** The issue stated above; not a verbatim quotation of the grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 111 F. App’x 363: on panel rehearing, vacated an earlier grant of habeas relief and affirmed the district court’s denial.
* **Relief Requested:** Dye requests reversal of the presentation and pleading barriers and consideration of his federal claim; the warden requests affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Dye was convicted at his third trial of two murders and a firearm offense. The challenged prosecution conduct concerned his defense that a prosecution witness committed the crimes. The reconstituted appellate panel treated Dye’s state appellate brief as absent from the record, although the district-court record contained it. That brief described specific misconduct, identified federal due process, and cited federal authorities. His federal petition repeatedly referred to an attached supporting brief. The state appellate opinion addressed state law without discussing the federal claim.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Exhaustion is claim-specific. Whether an opinion mentions a claim differs from whether the litigant presented it. Removing these procedural barriers does not establish prosecutorial misconduct, satisfy every applicable habeas limitation, or resolve other claims.
* **Relevant Dates:** Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed November 28, 1995; state supreme court review ended in 1996. The conflicting federal appellate decisions issued in 2002 and 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2005-10-11; completed Court decisions through June 27, 2005, with event-specific legislation and procedure stated below.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established for this review.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2254(b); Habeas Rule 2(c), and incorporation under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 10(c) as applicable through Habeas Rule 12.

### Issue 1: Federal notice and incorporated factual pleading

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Baldwin v. Reese requires the submitted petition, brief, or validly incorporated material to identify the particular claim’s federal nature. It does not require courts to search unrelated lower-court opinions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Meaningful claim-specific notice may come from constitutional language, pertinent federal authority, or an understandable substantive description. Exhaustion, procedural default, merits review, and entitlement to a writ remain separate.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 2254 requires exhaustion of available state remedies, subject to its stated exceptions. Habeas Rule 2(c) requires grounds and supporting facts; proper incorporation can supply those facts.
* **Material Other Authority:** Smith v. Digmon makes presentation depend on the papers submitted rather than a state opinion’s silence. The actual state brief and incorporated federal memorandum are the controlling record materials.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Baldwin v. Reese already protects valid incorporation and adequate federal notice. Whether these particular documents supplied that notice is principally record application.
* **Open Point:** Whether the particular cross-references incorporated sufficiently specific allegations; the merits of misconduct remain outside this presentation question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The panel inferred inadequate federal presentation from an asserted missing brief and the state opinion’s silence; alternatively, it found the federal petition too general. **Dye:** The filed state brief expressly federalized the claim, and the appended memorandum supplied particular facts. **Warden:** The reviewing court must require a genuinely presented federal claim and a sufficiently pleaded petition, rather than equating any prosecutorial complaint with due process. That general concern does not answer the actual documents.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse both procedural barriers and remand for unresolved claim adjudication if the documents establish notice and incorporation. Affirm a genuinely independent barrier only if supported by the actual record. A direct unconditional writ would require additional merits and remedial findings not established by these presentation questions.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific application may establish that express incorporation satisfies pleading requirements without creating a duty to search collateral materials. A broader rule excusing all federal-nature notice lacks support.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** The documentary record resolves presentation and incorporation; prosecutorial misconduct and any remaining habeas defenses remain for adjudication.

# 2. Schriro v. Smith

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Schriro v. Smith
* **Citation or Docket:** 546 U.S. 6; No. 04-1475.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a federal habeas order requiring a state jury determination.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-10-17; calendar date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal habeas court may require Arizona to determine intellectual disability by jury before Arizona has applied its own procedure, absent a demonstrated constitutional defect in that procedure.
* **Question Granted:** The issue stated above; not a verbatim quotation of the grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit order suspended federal habeas proceedings and directed a state proceeding in which disability must be decided by a jury unless the parties waived a jury.
* **Relief Requested:** Arizona requests removal of the mandatory jury condition. Smith seeks enforcement of an effective procedure protecting against execution if intellectually disabled.
* **Material Record:** An Arizona jury convicted Smith of murder, kidnapping, and sexual assault in 1982; the court imposed death. Earlier proceedings included evidence of low intelligence as mitigation, but no categorical intellectual-disability claim. Following Atkins v. Virginia, Smith raised that claim during federal appellate litigation. The Ninth Circuit required initial state adjudication and prescribed a jury without an adjudicated failure of Arizona’s own procedure.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The constitutional prohibition and a particular factfinding mechanism are distinct. The state claim had not yet been adjudicated under Arizona’s disability process. Low intelligence is not itself a finding of the complete disability criteria.
* **Relevant Dates:** Conviction and sentence in 1982; district-court habeas denial in 1996; Atkins v. Virginia effective June 20, 2002. Stewart v. Smith concerned an earlier, different procedural ruling in this litigation.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2005-10-17; completed Court decisions through June 27, 2005, with event-specific legislation and procedure stated below.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established for this review.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material.

### Issue 1: Reliable disability adjudication and limits on a prescribed jury

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Atkins v. Virginia prohibits execution after established intellectual disability and requires a reliable determination addressing impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations, and developmental onset. It reserved proof allocation and a universal hearing design.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The categorical execution restriction is mandatory; the State retains procedural choice subject to reliability and other applicable constitutional guarantees. A score or label alone does not establish the protected status.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; federal habeas authority and exhaustion requirements. The power to enforce a federal restriction does not confer general supervisory authority over state-court procedure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ford v. Wainwright supports effective implementation of a substantive execution restriction. It does not itself dictate a jury for every disability inquiry. Any separate jury-right theory requires its own preserved legal basis.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law settles the substantive protection and the duty of reliable process, but does not settle every permissible procedural design.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual Arizona procedure, once identified and applied, fails a constitutional requirement; this record does not establish that failure.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** A state determination was needed, and the court prescribed a jury absent waiver. **Arizona:** Atkins v. Virginia leaves appropriate procedure to the States, subject to constitutional review; the appellate order anticipates a defect. **Smith:** The execution prohibition requires dependable factfinding and meaningful opportunity to establish disability. That protection supports review of actual procedural defects but does not, without more, establish a universal jury entitlement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacate the jury condition while maintaining a lawful state-adjudication and federal-review route. Sustain a specific jury requirement only if an independently applicable, preserved constitutional rule requires it on established facts. The Court cannot resolve disability from the limited mitigation evidence alone.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow federal-authority holding preserves state procedural choice and subsequent constitutional review; it neither endorses all Arizona rules nor denies reliable factfinding.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** No final state disability adjudication or established defect in Arizona’s chosen procedure is before the Court.

# 3. Kane v. Garcia Espitia

* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of federal appellate habeas relief.

### Issue 1: Clearly established self-representation law

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Williams v. Taylor identifies clearly established law as this Court’s holdings at the relevant state merits decision, with an objective legal inquiry under §2254(d)(1). Lewis v. Casey protects effective access through workable arrangements and requires supported actual or imminent obstruction of a qualifying claim; it does not establish a preferred library specification.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A general holding may govern without factual identity, but a distinct unresolved entitlement cannot be supplied by lower-court agreement or constitutional intuition. Statutory review eligibility and an actual constitutional violation remain separate.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth Amendment; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1). Faretta v. California protects a competent, voluntary choice of self-representation; the legal scope of material assistance is a further question.
* **Material Other Authority:** Faretta v. California; Bounds v. Smith as limited and applied in the entering Lewis v. Casey. An access-to-courts claim and the asserted Sixth Amendment library entitlement require separate analysis.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law protects self-representation and meaningful court access, but supplies no specific freestanding pretrial library guarantee from Faretta v. California alone.
* **Open Point:** Whether an adequately preserved practical-obstruction claim on these facts satisfies a distinct governing rule. The presented Faretta-only basis must be assessed under the temporal habeas constraint.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Self-representation would be impaired without legal books, so Faretta v. California itself required access. **Warden:** No Supreme Court holding established the specific entitlement necessary for habeas relief. **Garcia Espitia:** A right to conduct one’s defense must include a practical means of preparing it; repeated denied requests and ignored orders show a concrete obstacle. The latter is a substantial factual concern, but its precise constitutional source and preservation must be demonstrated.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the unsupported §2254(d)(1) ground and remand for any preserved unresolved basis. Sustain relief only by identifying a temporally available governing holding and independently establishing its violation under applicable statutory limits. A new direct-review rule cannot alone justify the existing habeas judgment.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The available narrow rule is about the holding on which relief rests, not a universal denial of court access to self-represented prisoners.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** The relevant state merits date and preservation of a distinct access-to-courts claim must be established before either can support relief.

# 4. Eberhart v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Eberhart v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 546 U.S. 12; No. 04-9949.
* **October Term:** 2005
* **Matter Type:** MERITS
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of reversal of a federal new-trial order.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-10-31; calendar date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the then-operative Rules 33 and 45 impose a jurisdictional limitation, and whether the Government forfeits untimeliness by litigating the motion through a merits ruling before objecting.
* **Question Granted:** The issue stated above; not a verbatim quotation of the grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, 388 F.3d 1043: reversed a new-trial order because additional grounds were filed outside the rule’s seven-day period, treating the limit as jurisdictional.
* **Relief Requested:** Eberhart seeks removal of the jurisdictional barrier and restoration of review of the new-trial order; the Government seeks reversal of that order on available grounds.
* **Material Record:** After a cocaine-conspiracy conviction, Eberhart timely sought acquittal or a new trial on a transcript-related ground. Almost six months later he added hearsay and buyer-seller-instruction grounds. The Government opposed them on the merits without raising timing. The district judge granted a new trial based on the combined grounds, then the Government first invoked untimeliness on appeal.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish forfeiture of a procedural defense from authority to enlarge a deadline over a timely objection. The substantive propriety of granting a new trial is a separate review question.
* **Relevant Dates:** The operative conduct occurred under the seven-day Rule 33 deadline and the restrictive enlargement language then in force. Later amendments do not govern that completed filing merely because they exist later.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified for this proceeding.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 2005-10-31; completed Court decisions through June 27, 2005, with event-specific legislation and procedure stated below.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None established for this review.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No effective same-day decision is established. Subsequent Arbaugh v. Y & H Corp. concerns a statutory coverage threshold rather than this rules-based forfeiture problem.

### Issue 1: Mandatory procedural deadlines and forfeiture

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Carlisle v. United States already classifies the timely invoked Rule 29/45 restriction as a remedy limitation rather than subject-matter jurisdiction, while reserving Government forfeiture. Kontrick v. Ryan holds that a rules-based filing defense is forfeited when raised only after adverse merits adjudication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Source, placement, and function determine whether a command is jurisdictional. A rule may be mandatory when timely invoked yet susceptible to forfeiture; forfeiture is not an unauthorized extension.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §3231 supplies federal criminal jurisdiction. Rules 33 and 45 govern the new-trial filing and enlargement conditions applicable to these proceedings.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Robinson and United States v. Smith must be read at their actual procedural holdings; general jurisdictional language does not replace examination of the source of judicial authority.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The nonjurisdictional classification and postmerits-forfeiture principle are already established in closely related rules. Their application to Rule 33 supplies the remaining issue.
* **Open Point:** The precise latest premerits point for raising a timing objection is not required to decide an objection first raised on appeal after a merits ruling.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Existing descriptions of the deadline as jurisdictional compelled enforcement despite the late objection. **Eberhart:** Kontrick v. Ryan forecloses that classification; the Government forfeited the defense by litigating the merits. **Government:** The rule is inflexible and the late-added grounds were outside its period; independent objections to the new-trial order may remain. A rule’s strictness alone does not answer forfeiture.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the jurisdictional ruling and remand for preserved merits issues. If the timing defense had been timely invoked, enforce it under the applicable rules. Upholding the order’s procedural availability does not compel a finding that the district court properly exercised its new-trial discretion.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A general postmerits-forfeiture rule for the identified criminal procedural defense, with statutory jurisdiction and merits review left distinct.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** Independent objections to the new-trial order remain for appellate review; forfeiture of the deadline does not decide those objections.

The general ten-year offset limit does not bar that covered collection, and retention of the general limit does not repeal the loan-specific exception.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Only debts and benefit payments covered by the enacted provisions; all applicable notice, review, percentage, protected-payment, and other collection limits remain operative.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify both a source authorizing the particular benefit offset and a source governing the debt’s collection period. Neither alone supplies the other.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Union Bank v. Wolas rejects adding a maturity restriction absent from operative statutory language. Here the statute expressly removes limitations on offsets for the covered loans, and a later statute expressly opens the benefit source. Reading them together gives each provision effect without requiring Congress in 1991 to enumerate a collection method authorized in 1996. Failed later legislation supplies no contrary enacted command.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Union Bank v. Wolas applied by statutory method. No controlling decision is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A conflict lacking the required express reference is not presented because Congress supplied that reference here. Individual offset calculations, hardship procedures, and unrelated debts are outside the claim.
