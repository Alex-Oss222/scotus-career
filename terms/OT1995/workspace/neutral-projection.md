### Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: opening Standing State §3, including its October 2, 1995 revision.

### Additional published separate positions

### Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, No. 94-1530; 516 U.S. 124; October Term 1995.

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, concurring.** They join the Court's opinion with the express understanding that the present covered-ground holding does not decide reviewability of a remand under Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill. Thermtron held both that a particular unauthorized remand was improper and that the ordinary review bar did not cover remands outside §1447(c). Cohill later recognized discretion to remand remaining state-law claims after elimination of the sole federal claim, despite the absence of express statutory authorization. Cohill did not decide whether §1447(d) bars review of that distinct remand. Neither today's affirmance nor these two Justices' reservation resolves that issue in either direction.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurring.** They also find an independent review bar in §1452(b). Equity there means fairness or appropriateness, not the former division between legal and equitable pleading. Failure to remove promptly fits that meaning. Making a statutory time bar reviewable while equivalent delay labeled laches is immune would make appellate jurisdiction depend on a label. Hernandez v. Brakegate supplies the modern sense of equity; Sykes v. Texas Air explains the incoherence of label-dependent review. The chapter's structure and the 1977 House report's parallel treatment of remand review support a supplementary bankruptcy-remand regime, while Timbers directs attention to the coherent statutory scheme.

They give an alternative explanation as well: even if a procedural or jurisdictional impediment were called legal rather than equitable, equity may describe the choice to remand rather than dismiss. Section 1452(b) permits that fair disposition without requiring the impediment itself to fit an old equitable category. Their position concerns remands of claims related to bankruptcy. It does not insulate lack of subject-matter jurisdiction in a retained case: a decision not to remand may lack a separate immediate appeal while jurisdiction remains open to district-court reconsideration and eventual review through an otherwise proper channel. Their additional ground neither answers the separate Cohill question outside its statutory coverage nor commands a majority of the Court.

Authority: [Record](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Things_Remembered_Inc_v_Petrarca_merits_1995-12-05.md).

### Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492; 516 U.S. 137; October Term 1995.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the active-employment rule, its preservation of Smith's barter holding, the separate relation requirement, and reversal of both use-based appellate affirmances. Because the present convictions rest on the storage-and-accessibility use theory that the Court rejects, he would also vacate the firearm convictions now. He would then remand for ordinary proceedings, including any legally available charge based on carrying or possession that was properly preserved. That alternative does not convert possession into an offense under the applicable §924(c), disturb separate possession judgments, or decide an unpresented charging or retrial question. He requires neither automatic acquittal nor automatic retrial. No other Justice joins this different remedial position.

### Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue; 28 F.3d 1501; Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** All three join Parts I and III, including the actual-forum viewpoint rule, the concrete conclusion that the establishment defense fails on these private-use facts, and the remedial limits. They withhold assent from Part II's comprehensive contextual Lemon explanation. Their ground is the distinction between genuinely private religious advocacy and governmental religious action, reflected in Kennedy's Mergens opinion and in the access reasoning of Rosenberger and Chabad. Voluntary attendance, parental choice and private leadership support access here; a religious message or possible subjective impression of endorsement does not alone permit suppressing private expression. They propose no unlimited forum, general financing entitlement or alteration of existing establishment law through this concurrence.

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the Eighth Circuit and leave the judgment for the school district in place. Perry and Cornelius permit a genuinely restricted forum, and his Mergens analysis treats the activity the school actually admitted as decisive. The Scout and athletic programs are secular activities related to the school's extracurricular program; the fact that both those programs and the adult-led Club concern moral development does not make them the same admitted activity. He acknowledges the policy's religious restriction and Lamb's Chapel's prohibition on viewpoint exclusion within an admitted subject, but finds the activity distinction sufficient for this Club's denial. The younger audience and continued extracurricular day reinforce the restricted character of the forum. His disagreement concerns that comparison, not a general exemption allowing schools to suppress religious viewpoints, a necessary finding that every Club meeting establishes religion, or rejection of Lamb's Chapel's governing rule.

### Love v. Pepersack; Fourth Circuit No. 94-1582, 47 F.3d 120; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas — plurality ground supporting due-process affirmance.** The four assume the state interest and accept the alleged deliberate, knowing and malicious arrest-only practice. They nevertheless find this eligibility-screening restriction insufficient to establish the separate substantive wrong. Unresolved dispositions concern the program's eligibility subject even when officials knowingly exceed statutory authority. Collins separates state duty from constitutional arbitrariness; Romein and Beach supply limited economic-rights analogies. The four adopt no benign-motive finding or legislative-classification burden for this executive act. They reject categorical cure through state review, an absolute-discretion entitlement rule and a finding that deadline pressure compelled the policy. Their bounded conclusion does not exempt every affirmative licensing act. Stone-Zsela does not join this application; it is not the Court's controlling rationale.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions and the personal-right, incorporation and necessary-acquisition holdings. He agrees with the due-process dismissal: unauthorized state administration does not by itself create a federal substantive wrong, state review corrected the state-law deprivation, and the incorporated Second Amendment supplies the specific protection for the asserted arms liberty. Substantive due process should not duplicate it. No separate entitlement to every lawful private transaction follows.

He would reverse the arms dismissal with constitutional infringement resolved, rather than vacate for an open application. In his view the guarantee centrally protects a law-abiding person's acquisition and keeping of a common firearm for lawful self-defense in the home. Nunn and Chandler support personal holders and defensive use while preserving regulation; Andrews supplies the acquisition incident, with its state-law and weapon-specific limits. Incorporation does not require reopening the Privileges or Immunities Clause.

His disqualification rule requires adjudicated conduct or incapacity genuinely connected to danger: conviction for a serious violent offense, a judicial dangerousness determination with adequate process, a domestic-violence protective order after notice and hearing, or adjudicated functional incapacity to possess safely. A bare diagnosis, accusation or uncontestable administrative label is insufficient. Convictions for murder, serious battery, armed robbery, violent domestic abuse, terrorism, planned unlawful force against government or comparable violence can support dispossession; arrests alone cannot. He does not settle every valid disqualifying conviction or restoration and duration rule.

On the pleaded record, Love's arrests were accusations; her identified obscene-show misdemeanor conviction established neither violence, dangerousness nor relevant incapacity. The Chief would hold that she could not constitutionally be denied an ordinary handgun solely because those arrests appeared in the files. The State may promptly investigate genuinely unresolved potentially disqualifying conduct under authority its law gives it. Missing dispositions do not become adjudications of guilt, and time pressure authorizes no judicially invented extra waiting period. Public safety does not supply a missing factual predicate.

His remand would resolve infringement while leaving personal responsibility, lawful relief and properly available defenses for ordinary proceedings. He decides no general public or concealed carrying rule, sensitive-place restriction, prohibited-weapon or magazine category, ordinary dealer licensing, recordkeeping, commercial regulation or storage rule. His affirmative application and adjudication-based disqualification framework receive no other Justice's join and are not the Court's law.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, and by Souter as to Part A only, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** All four join the procedural nonreach of immunity and the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions.

In **Part A**, all four would vacate due process and remand for assessment of the actual affirmative-policy claim. Zinermon and Stevens's Daniels concurrence distinguish substantive wrongdoing, complete when committed, from adequacy of procedure. Later correction alone cannot cure the former. The knowing-barrier allegations cannot be replaced by findings of necessary haste or official caution. Collins retains the need for a federal interest and substantive arbitrariness without completing this different application. An independent sufficient ground could permit affirmance despite erroneous reasoning, but these framing defects are material. The dissenters would return entitlement, constitutional sufficiency, attribution, defenses and relief for decision. They establish no violation, automatic discovery or trial, or damages entitlement. Malice and correction expense do not supply every element.

In **Part B**, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm arms dismissal under the specific state-nonapplication precedents. They acknowledge affirmative original evidence of individual keeping, acquisition and personal security, and the power to reconsider under Benton. Differing state clauses, military-suitable weapon boundaries, regulatory authority, the Reconstruction statute's equality and restoration setting, and Howard's privileges-or-immunities framing nevertheless fail, in their view, to justify this right-specific displacement of Cruikshank and Presser. Lewis's extant-felony setting is not a complete answer to Love. They adopt neither a collective-only rule, formal-enrollment condition nor state-correction cure. Souter does not join Part B; he joins the Court's arms holdings and bounded vacatur.

Authority: [Record](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Love_v_Pepersack_merits_1995-12-11.md).

### Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions; 56 F.3d 1281; source petition identifier No. 95-647; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Kennedy.** They would affirm on the independently sufficient failure-of-proof ground under the governing Section 2 results inquiry. Houston Lawyers permits judicial-election coverage while retaining legitimate institutional considerations, and De Grandy requires proof of ultimate unequal opportunity rather than automatic liability from polarization or a feasible additional district. The trial court evaluated both experts, several kinds of elections, actual candidate preferences, party affiliation and incumbency. In their view the Court of Appeals upheld permissible contextual factfinding rather than applying a racial-intent requirement or numerical safe harbor. They agree that legal error would defeat factual deference, but find no error warranting reconsideration of this independent ground. They do not defend presumed racial partiality, categorical structural immunity, or the proposition that all proposed remedies are unconstitutional. Kennedy's earlier broader objection to judicial vote-dilution coverage is not the ground he selects here. Neither joins Thomas's noncoverage rationale.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** They would affirm on their separate statutory view that Section 2 does not reach this judicial vote-dilution claim. Their preserved Holder and De Grandy positions reject dilution coverage; Scalia's Chisom and Houston Lawyers dissent supplies earlier judicial-election-specific support. They acknowledge that the Court's contrary coverage holdings govern unless changed, but continue to urge their construction rather than join the majority's comparison and totality framework. They do not join O'Connor's trial-record application merely because they seek the same decree. Their opinion supplies no additional votes for an evidentiary proposition, categorical remedial rule or present change in the Court's coverage law.

### Lawrence v. Chater, No. 94-9323; 516 U.S. 163

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court's opinion and adds that certiorari administration properly permits a case-specific assessment of the totality of the circumstances. The value of obtaining the lower court's judgment about a potentially consequential development need not depend on first proving that the claimant will win. That discretion remains judicial and supplies no automatic rule accepting Government requests. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice would use §2106 when a material intervening development creates a reasonable possibility of a different result and reconsideration would conserve judicial resources. He does not adopt the Court's distinct reasonable-probability formulation. Henry separates this procedural return from a benefits award. The agency's changed position matters because it may unsettle the existing judgment, not because administrative deference supplies power to vacate. A strategic litigation reversal, an immaterial development, or a position outside lawful agency authority would not suffice, and remand may not predetermine the merits. Thomas Jefferson University and MCI supply cautions for the receiving court's independent examination of statutory authority, procedure, consistency, fair notice, and the position's lawful weight; they do not make the new position controlling. No other Justice joins this opinion.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny the petition. An intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment can justify vacatur without a merits determination. An established defect can also support a justified error-correcting disposition; plenary review is not foreclosed. Conditional executive advocacy of uncertain relevance supplies no sufficient ground here. The agency's qualified acknowledgment establishes neither its legal force nor its application to this claimant. A clearly cognizable agency change could qualify, but a desire to reassess an arguably valid decision or obtain better reasoning does not. The unproved predicates of the state amendment do not cure that defect. The dissent concerns judicial justification for this remedy, not a ruling that the agency can never examine constitutional validity.

Authority: [Record](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Lawrence_v_Chater_GVR_1996-01-08.md).

### Stutson v. United States, No. 94-8988; 516 U.S. 193

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court and adds that case-specific, totality-of-the-circumstances discretion is a proper part of certiorari administration. The combination of an uncertain review premise and the potential loss of appellate consideration supports obtaining a fresh lower-court judgment without deciding excusability. This position creates no entitlement for every short delay and no exception to attribution of counsel's conduct. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny. Pioneer was available when the lower courts acted, and an unexplained affirmance may have applied its approach and still rejected the excuse. The Government concedes a point of law concerning relevance, not error in the judgment. Its changed argument therefore establishes neither new intervening law nor a defective judicial premise. The dissent accepts vacatur without a merits determination for an intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment. It does not foreclose plenary review or a justified error-correcting disposition. None of those grounds is established here. Uncertainty about which ground sustained a judgment, or a desire for better reasoning, does not itself justify vacatur; the proper exercise of §2106 requires more than the possibility that reconsideration could change the result.

Authority: [Record](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Stutson_v_United_States_GVR_1996-01-08.md).

The unintroduced medical evidence and separate chilling contention require their own lawful basis. No other Justice joins this proposal or opinion.

### North Carolina paternity amendment effective October 1, 1995

The amendment permits a posthumous paternity judgment when the action began before the putative father's death; when it begins within one year after death and no estate-administration proceeding began within that year; or when an estate-administration proceeding began within that year and the action begins within the claims period specified by G.S. 28A-19-3(a). A judgment establishing paternity under this subsection must take effect as of the day before the father's death. In a contested paternity action brought more than three years after the child's birth or after the putative father's death, evidence from a blood or genetic marker test is required. The act applies only to actions commenced on or after October 1, 1995, but before October 1, 1998, without regard to the father's date of death, and expires October 1, 1998. No particular claimant's satisfaction of those requirements is established.

### Published separate positions

### Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc., No. 94-2003; 516 U.S. 233. — 1996-01-16

**O'Connor's plurality, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg.** Section 102(b) leaves functions, procedures, and methods free, but an original selection and arrangement does not necessarily lose all protection merely because users employ it to operate software. Feist protects original arrangement while withholding ownership of the underlying material. Baker permits use of a system without establishing that every particular presentation of it is excluded. The plurality would reject the First Circuit's categorical treatment and distinguish any original expression from the unowned operation.

That view covers both implementations without declaring every command or all 469 choices protected. Compatibility matters to purpose and defenses, but does not automatically answer classification or fair use. Campbell requires the actual defense inquiry; Kennedy's caution against categorical borrowing permission remains consistent with the plurality's limits. The plurality supports remand, not automatic restoration of liability or an injunction, and does not reach code or screen claims outside the appeal.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** He would protect the command compatibility necessary to operate users' existing macros through Campbell's full §107 inquiry. Such use has a concrete functional purpose beyond copying expression for its own sake; the commands' utilitarian character matters. The amount copied must be connected to actual interoperability rather than assumed necessary. Legally cognizable substitution and licensing markets count, while loss from lawful competition alone is not protected market harm. Commercial purpose is relevant but not decisive, and user investment creates no general property right in a learned interface.

The First Circuit did not resolve the fact-sensitive defenses. He would have it address them and obtain necessary findings, while allowing an alternative statutory ground within the grant without demanding an advisory copyrightability decision. He finds neither all compatibility copying fair nor all software structure unprotectable, and reserves source code, screen expression, and unnecessary copying. No other Justice joins this rationale.

**Souter, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Breyer.** The particular hierarchy is the method by which users issue executable instructions and existing macros operate. Section 102(b) excludes a method in whatever expressive form it is embodied; the availability of different original terms or arrangements does not remove that exclusion. Baker protects use of the system, and Feist distinguishes a copyright in the overall work from rights in excluded constituent material.

These are user commands, not the independent implementing code or separable visual presentation. That boundary answers the objection that treating all software as operational would swallow program protection. Because the copied subject matter is excluded, no fair-use decision is needed. The dissent would affirm both components without adopting a general interoperability license, Boudin's possible new privilege, or a rule denying protection to all interfaces.
