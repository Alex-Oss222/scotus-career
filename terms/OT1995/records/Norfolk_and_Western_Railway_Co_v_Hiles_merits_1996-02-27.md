### Holdings

**Common authority:** Thomas's opinion and all eight complete joins identified above directly support each following holding, its application and limits. No combination of separate rationales is required.

**Ordinary preparation is distinct from failure of required performance.** Former 45 U.S.C. §2 imposes an absolute duty that coupling equipment perform its statutory function when properly set; ordinary, nondefective lateral displacement requiring drawbar alignment does not alone violate that duty. The Act does not categorically prohibit every preparatory task between cars or separately require automatic-realignment equipment.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing text required couplers coupling automatically by impact and capable of uncoupling without workers going between the car ends. Affolder recognizes that automatic coupling presupposes proper setting; Carter preserves that qualification while rejecting an ordinary-care defense. The same distinction applies to preparing a nondefective drawbar as to opening a knuckle. O'Donnell's absolute-performance holding remains effective when an engaged coupler fails.

Hiles correctly emphasizes that the Act protects workers from manual-coupling dangers and that mechanically sound parts can still fail to perform. But that principle does not make every injury during preparatory work a statutory violation. Lateral play accommodates curves, and the enacted duty does not itself require a mechanism eliminating every manual alignment task. Neither customary practice nor a general appeal to safety decides whether this appliance was awaiting ordinary preparation or had failed its required function. The 1994 recodification at 49 U.S.C. §20302(a)(1)(A) adds no substantive requirement governing this 1990 injury.

**Performance-based proof and ordinary-setting proof remain distinct.** An employee may establish a violation by failure to perform the required function without proving negligence or identifying a particular mechanical defect. The railroad may establish that the complained-of condition instead resulted from ordinary nondefective misalignment before proper setting; once a statutory violation contributing to injury is proved, due care, inspection, later successful operation, contributory negligence or assumption of risk does not erase it.

**Controlling explanation:** Affolder and Carter make actual performance decisive within the proper-setting qualification. The railroad's permissible explanation concerns whether the statutory duty was breached; it is not a safe harbor for careful inspection after a breach. Nor can “preparation” excuse a malfunction simply by relabeling it. A statutory violation must have contributed to the employee's injury within the applicable FELA remedial framework, but causation does not supply a missing violation. No attempted impact is invariably required: the Act does not demand a futile or damaging collision before an otherwise provable failure may be shown. Conversely, an attempted coupling would not by itself establish Hiles's right to a directed liability ruling on this record.

**The per se liability direction requires reversal without an appellate finding for the railroad.** Injury during realignment does not establish statutory liability as a matter of law. The reason, extent and legal classification of this displacement remain for lawful consideration under the correct distinction, together with any properly preserved performance and causation issues.

**Controlling explanation:** The general need for lateral movement does not prove that this particular condition was ordinary or nondefective. The inspection account and asserted AAR compliance were excluded affidavit and proffer evidence, not findings. The difficulty moving the bar, the uncoupling evidence and the disputed curve inference also cannot be resolved merely from the trial court's erroneous rule. Wagner and Parker preserve assessment of the actual displacement and performance evidence. The absence of a prior impact and the absence of an identified broken component do not justify this Court's entering final judgment for the railroad. Reversal removes the directed-liability foundation of the damages judgment while leaving only procedurally available issues for further proceedings. It neither manufactures the unpleaded negligence claim nor guarantees another trial.

### Precedent Treatment

- Affolder v. New York, Chicago & St. Louis Railroad Co., 339 U.S. 96: applies its proper-setting qualification to ordinary nondefective drawbar alignment, retaining absolute performance once the appliance is properly set.
- Carter v. Atlanta & St. Andrews Bay Railway Co., 338 U.S. 430: preserves performance-based proof without negligence or identification of a particular defect and the separate causal requirement; ordinary FELA comparative-negligence principles do not excuse a proven statutory violation.
- O'Donnell v. Elgin, Joliet & Eastern Railway Co., 338 U.S. 384: leaves intact the absolute duty when an engaged coupler breaks; preparation supplies no defense to such an established failure.
- San Antonio & Aransas Pass Railway Co. v. Wagner, 241 U.S. 476, and Atlantic City Railroad Co. v. Parker, 242 U.S. 56: preserve consideration of the actual evidence bearing on drawbar displacement and failure, rather than making every realignment injury conclusive.
- Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall, 512 U.S. 532: its distinct negligent-emotional-injury rule remains unchanged; its negligence and zone-of-danger requirements do not govern this statutory-performance claim.

### Law After Decision

The proper-setting qualification extends to ordinary nondefective drawbar alignment. An injury during that preparation is insufficient by itself; a proven failure of required performance remains actionable without negligence or identification of a particular mechanical defect. Violation and causal contribution remain separate. The decision establishes no general exemption for misaligned drawbars, inspection safe harbor, universal impact prerequisite, new technology mandate or broader operating prohibition.
