## Holdings

### The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Authority:** Part I-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The review bar expressly addresses the circuit's authorization determination. Section 2241 separately names this Court and its Justices as habeas decisionmakers, and AEDPA leaves that grant intact. Section 2244(b)(3)(A)'s advance-authorization mechanism expressly precedes filing in a district court; it does not direct applicants to obtain circuit permission before filing an original petition here. The amended circuit-transfer rule likewise does not name this Court.

McCardle requires respect for withdrawal of a specified appellate route. Yerger preserves a separate habeas source against an unwarranted implied repeal, and Bollman explains its statutory source and constitutional appellate character. An application filed directly here is called original as a matter of procedure; it is not an expansion of Article III's enumerated original jurisdiction. Section 1651 authorizes writs in aid of jurisdiction independently possessed, not a new freestanding source. The Court does not decide every possible certified-question, interlocutory or other supervisory route not presented here.

### Temporal operation of the channel rule

**Controlling proposition:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Authority:** All nine adopt this channel result. Part I-B's explanation is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy concur through the distinct relevant-activity method.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf requires attention first to Congress's temporal direction and otherwise to whether applying the particular provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events. A procedural or jurisdictional label alone supplies no answer. This provision governs the new May authorization determination and the ensuing request for its review. Its application to that post-enactment proceeding does not depend on retroactively changing the lawfulness of trial conduct. The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 does not automatically govern every different amendment; Georgia's qualification under that chapter is not established or assumed here.

### Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion

**Controlling proposition:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative distinctions:** For the second or successive §2254 applications described by the statute, paragraph (1) requires dismissal of a claim already presented in a prior application. Paragraph (2) requires dismissal of a new claim unless either it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral cases by the Supreme Court, or both of these conditions are met: the factual predicate could not previously have been discovered through due diligence; and the facts, if proved and viewed with the evidence as a whole, establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense.

Paragraph (3) requires permission from the appropriate court of appeals before district-court filing; a three-judge panel decides whether there is a prima facie showing and must act within thirty days. Its grant or denial is not appealable and is not subject to a rehearing or certiorari petition. Under paragraph (4), authorization does not finally establish entitlement: the district court must dismiss an authorized claim unless the applicant establishes the statutory requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** McCleskey recognizes lawful restrictions on abusive successive requests; it does not collapse these distinct statutory conditions. A request that this Court announce a new rule is not reliance on an already announced, previously unavailable rule made retroactive by this Court. New medical disagreement, an assertion of innocence or satisfaction of some other gateway does not automatically establish paragraph (2)(B)'s cumulative diligence, constitutional-error and clear-and-convincing conditions. The present submission does not establish those conditions; the Court creates no new default, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure finding to reach that assessment.

Rule 20 separately requires the petition to explain why relief was not sought in district court, state how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, demonstrate exceptional circumstances warranting this Court's discretionary exercise, and show that adequate relief cannot be obtained in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1)'s alternatives are exhaustion of available state-court remedies, absence of available state corrective process, or circumstances rendering that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Paragraph (2) separately permits, but does not require, merits denial despite nonexhaustion. Under paragraph (3), a State is not deemed to waive exhaustion or estopped from invoking it unless the State through counsel expressly waives it. No exhaustion, process-availability, effectiveness or waiver question is decided here. The denial below explains the direct request, but does not prove its substantive sufficiency. The Court denies this petition for want of the exceptional original showing; it does not find an otherwise disputed state remedy conclusively available or adequate. Section 2241 permits a lawful transfer, but none is ordered.

### The constitutional objection in this case

**Controlling proposition:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Authority:** Part II's nine-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Suspension Clause protects the writ's constitutional function; it does not exempt every repeated application from source-bound procedural and substantive regulation. The retained §2241 route is used here, not merely named as an inaccessible alternative. The Court independently considers the actual claims and finds no entitlement under the traditional extraordinary standard. Thus no otherwise sufficient claim is denied solely because all meaningful review has been eliminated.

Yerger supports preserving the separate route, while Klein's distinction between changing governing law and prescribing an outcome prevents treating every jurisdictional amendment as an unlawful command. The provisions applied here establish channels and successive-claim standards rather than direct the Court to falsify a fact or decide under an unchanged rule in a prescribed way. The Court does not approve elimination of every meaningful forum, resolve a case of established innocence excluded from all relief, or decide the constitutionality and temporal operation of §§2254(d)–(e), all of Title I, or every possible supervisory-review restriction.

### The reasonable-doubt claim

**Controlling proposition:** The challenged voir-dire statements and complete final charge do not create a reasonable likelihood that the jury convicted without proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every essential element. The presented constitutional instruction ground therefore supplies no habeas relief.

**Authority:** Part III-A, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone concurs in denial on successive-application and extraordinary-writ grounds without deciding this constitutional question.

**Controlling explanation:** Victor and Sandoval require examination of the complete instruction, not automatic approval or invalidation of the words moral certainty. This charge kept the burden on the State from beginning to end, distinguished criminal proof from civil preponderance, and defined doubt through evidence, lack of evidence or conflicting evidence. It required acquittal when jurors remained wavering, unsettled and unsatisfied. When the proof was entirely circumstantial, it required exclusion of every other reasonable theory; it forbade conviction on suspicion, conjecture or possibility and again directed acquittal for reasonable doubt.

That connected explanation, together with the court's direction concerning counsel's statements, defeats the proposed understanding that a lesser moral impression could replace evidentiary proof. Correct boilerplate alone would not cure a contradictory charge; the instructions' actual operation is what matters here. Sullivan would require reversal without harmless-error reweighing if the proof standard were constitutionally vitiated, but that predicate is absent. The Court does not use the strength of the prosecution evidence to excuse a defective instruction or decide a new instruction claim, waiver or retroactivity issue.

### The medical-testimony trial-process claim

**Controlling proposition:** On this record, admission of Tillman's testimony and the submitted forensic criticisms do not establish constitutionally fundamental unfairness or the requested constitutional rule that only a physician may provide such testimony. This is a determination of the presented trial-process claim, not a finding that each challenged medical conclusion was correct.

**Authority:** Part III-B, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone rests on the separate extraordinary-review ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lisenba asks whether the evidentiary treatment denied fundamental fairness. Ake, Ford and Eddings do not establish the proposed automatic physician-only rule. Tillman's credentials were exposed through examination, contrary medical testimony was offered, Whitaker supplied independent qualified testimony, and jurors were instructed to determine expert weight and reject an opinion whose factual assumptions they did not find true.

Spitz and Arden do more than criticize credentials: they attack the forensic basis of the earlier-death estimate, observations, samples and other interpretations. The Court considers that substance. But expert disagreement and criticism of the work do not on this showing prove knowing use of false evidence, concealed qualifications, deprivation of a constitutionally required expert or another established trial-process violation. Neither an altered estimate nor the experts' asserted certainty proves perjury. The ruling neither certifies every forensic statement nor invents additional claims from supporting observations concerning injury or binding.

### Innocence as a gateway

**Controlling proposition:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Authority:** Part IV-A, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept insufficiency under the governing standard while retaining their stricter view; Stone does not join this additional application.

**Controlling explanation:** The experts' three-to-five-day estimate would support the alibi and is supported by detailed criticism of the forensic chronology. Their review included Whitaker's testimony; the Court does not disregard their opinions merely because they leave his professional qualifications unchallenged. Whitaker nevertheless independently favored death near disappearance while acknowledging the later interval as possible. The disappearance, abandoned car, call from Felker's home and unchanged clothing remain additional evidence bearing on chronology.

Considering those grounds together, the new opinions do not remove reasonable adverse assessment to the probability required by Schlup. The Court does not find the affidavits false or use other-offense evidence beyond its stated motive, intent and state-of-mind purpose. The legal conclusion is about this gateway showing. Even a successful gateway would permit examination of an underlying constitutional claim; it would not itself prove that claim or establish paragraph (2)(B)'s distinct requirements.

### The separate constitutional innocence claim and original relief

**Controlling proposition:** Even assuming a possible constitutional claim based on actual innocence, this considered submission does not establish exceptional circumstances warranting the Court's original habeas intervention. Original relief is denied without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence burden, rejecting every such constitutional claim, or requiring an additional return merely because new expert opinions are offered.

**Authority:** Part IV-B, joined by all nine Justices; this is an independently sufficient original-writ ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Herrera resolved the insufficiency of its particular submission while leaving the Justices divided on a general innocence right and its threshold. It supplies no single general burden to import here. Rule 20 requires an exceptional original showing even when jurisdiction exists and a constitutional theory is assumed.

The later opinions and their detailed physiological criticism receive consideration alongside Whitaker's estimate, his acknowledgment of the later interval and the circumstantial chronology. The criticism raises a substantive medical dispute. It does not, on the submission as a whole, demonstrate the extraordinary basis for this Court to intervene directly. The Court does not substitute the mere existence of a conviction or the circuit's denial for that examination, declare the experts dishonest, or assume that an unexamined Georgia remedy adequately resolves the claim. No new general innocence standard, factual hearing, transfer or further return is necessary to decide this original request. This ground remains distinct from both the trial-process holdings and Schlup's procedural gateway.

### Pending application A-890

**Controlling proposition:** Execution's irreversibility does not alone establish entitlement to a stay. Because the independently considered claims show no sufficiently substantial basis for relief through the available proceedings and no identified unfinished legally required process warrants protection, application A-890 is denied.

**Authority:** Part V, joined and expressly adopted as application reasons by all nine Justices, with the signed explanations and qualifications identified below.

**Controlling explanation:** Bowersox requires attention to actual substantial relief grounds in a successive-petition request; the existence of an application or retained jurisdiction is insufficient. Lonchar does not authorize a separate equitable dismissal sanction, but it preserves the distinction between lawful petition consideration and stay discretion. The identified required-process condition recognized by five Justices there supplies no further proceeding once lawful claim-specific consideration is complete here. McFarland's counsel/preparation protection creates no newly established preparation entitlement on this record.

The Court denies the pending request after that independent consideration. It does not rely solely on the circuit's refusal of authorization, infer an automatic late-filing penalty, or adjudicate the legality of an execution on an invented date. No existing Supreme Court stay is altered.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte McCardle:** Applied to the specified withdrawn appellate route; no conclusion follows that every separate habeas source is repealed.
- **Ex parte Yerger and Ex parte Bollman:** Applied to the retained statutory habeas channel and its constitutional appellate character. They do not make issuance of an original writ automatic.
- **Landgraf and Rivers:** Their controlling provision-specific temporal inquiry remains in force; the application here concerns post-enactment authorization/review proceedings, not all pending habeas litigation.
- **McCleskey v. Zant:** Its recognition of lawful successive/abuse restrictions supports consideration of the new standards; it does not eliminate their separate textual requirements or settle their mandatory reach here.
- **United States v. Klein:** Its jurisdiction/rule-of-decision distinction is preserved; the present channel and standards do not direct a factual result under unchanged law.
- **Victor and Sandoval:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood test is applied by the eight Associates, with its existing limits on isolated terminology and automatic cures intact.
- **Sullivan:** Its structural-error rule is preserved; the Court finds no constitutional vitiation of the proof standard and conducts no harmless-error substitute.
- **Lisenba:** Its fundamental-fairness inquiry governs the presented medical-testimony trial claim. **Ake, Ford and Eddings** do not supply the proposed automatic physician-only testimony requirement.
- **Schlup:** Its gateway remains distinct from a substantive innocence claim and the new statutory conditions; the eight Associates find the present showing insufficient under its governing probability standard.
- **Herrera:** Its particular-submission holding is preserved without converting any separate theory into a general Court rule. The present original denial likewise assumes the possible constitutional claim without deciding its universal merits threshold.
- **Bowersox, Lonchar and McFarland:** Their distinct substantial-ground, required-process and counsel/preparation rules retain their actual triggers. None guarantees additional proceedings or a stay from filing alone.

## Law After Decision

Ordinary review of the specified circuit authorization ruling is barred, while §2241 original habeas remains a separate, extraordinary channel. The district-court authorization process does not govern filing here. The substantive successive standards may inform original consideration; their mandatory direct force remains unresolved.

The decision rejects the present constitutional objection without approving every AEDPA provision or every future exclusion of meaningful review. It denies these trial grounds, the asserted gateway and the extraordinary original showing at their stated authority levels. It creates no uniform constitutional innocence burden, physician-only evidence rule, mandatory further hearing, presumed adequate state remedy or general entitlement to temporary protection.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela's concurrence.** Stone joins the channel, limited temporal, statutory-reservation, constitutional, independent original-denial and stay grounds. He does not decide whether the reasonable-doubt charge was constitutional. Its trial basis was available earlier, and the claim does not identify the required newly established retroactive rule or newly discovered predicate warranting extraordinary successive relief. He treats the medical evidence seriously against the full record, including Whitaker's independent support for the earlier chronology, but finds no extraordinary showing for original relief. He keeps that conclusion distinct from the Court's additional trial-process and exact gateway holdings. His signed A-890 position is denial: execution is irreparable, but neither claim demonstrates a sufficiently substantial prospect of relief through the available proceedings. The circuit's authorization refusal alone is not his reason.

**Stevens's concurrence, joined by Souter.** They retain their Herrera position that probable actual innocence can support a constitutional claim and warrant relief in an appropriate case. Their Schlup and Lonchar positions require serious consideration of the complete probative evidence and protection of actually required process. Neither creates an automatic additional return whenever expert affidavits dispute chronology. The present affidavits are substantive and have been examined with the contrary medical and circumstantial grounds; this submission does not require exceptional original intervention. They join the Court's denial without adopting a categorical no-right rule, a uniform Herrera burden or a presumption that a Georgia remedy is adequate.

**Thomas's concurrence A, joined by Scalia and Kennedy.** They reach the same temporal result by identifying the activity regulated: a post-enactment authorization determination and the ensuing request for review. They do not join Part I-B's general sequential explanation. Their method does not decide the temporal operation of unrelated Title I provisions or require a ruling that §§2244(b)(1)–(2) bind every original application.

**Thomas's concurrence B, joined by Scalia.** They preserve their Herrera position that new evidence of innocence alone supplies no freestanding constitutional right to reopen the conviction, and retain their stricter gateway view. Those are additional positions, not the Court's rule. They independently accept the common assumed-claim, particular-submission and extraordinary-writ denial, so the judgment requires no adoption of their categorical theory.

**Thomas's Circuit Justice statement C.** Thomas presents A-890 and personally denies the requested stay. He adopts Parts III, IV and V: the particular trial grounds and original submission fail, retained jurisdiction creates no substantial relief ground, and no identified required proceeding remains unfinished. His concurrence-B constitutional view is additional; denial does not depend solely on it or on the circuit's ruling. This is his own signed explanation of the referred request, not an order continuing or dissolving any previous stay.
