## Source, authority and operative text

Congress enacted [Public Law 104-132](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ132/pdf/PLAW-104publ132.pdf) on April 24, 1996. A [preserved official PDF](../sources/chunk6_control/aedpa.pdf) retains the exact source. Title I, sections 101–108, appears at 110 Stat. 1217–1226. The linked official enacted text, including every qualification, controls; the following descriptions are an index to that text and do not replace it. The statute supplies noncase law, not a Supreme Court holding.

Section 101 adds 28 U.S.C. §2244(d): a one-year limitation for the specified state-custody habeas applications, measured from the latest of the four stated triggering events, together with the specified exclusion for time during properly filed state collateral review. Section 102 replaces §2253, including the certificate-of-appealability requirement for the stated state-process and §2255 appeals, the substantial-showing requirement and identification of the qualifying issues. Section 103 amends Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 22, preserving its specific district-court application, transfer and certificate procedures.

Section 104 amends §2254: exhaustion and its exceptions; permission to deny on the merits notwithstanding nonexhaustion; express state-counsel waiver; limits on relief for claims adjudicated on their merits in state court; the state-fact presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden; conditional limits on evidentiary hearings; appointed-counsel provisions; and the specified treatment of collateral counsel's ineffectiveness. Each component retains its enacted trigger and exceptions. This admission supplies no interpretation of the contrary-to, unreasonable-application or factual-determination language.

Section 105 amends §2255, including its one-year limitation measured from the latest of four stated events, counsel provision, and court-of-appeals certification for a second or successive motion under either stated ground. Section 106 amends §2244(a) and (b), including repeated and newly presented claim treatment, court-of-appeals authorization and district-court conditions. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) states the particular appeal, rehearing and certiorari restriction for the authorization decision; this admission does not enlarge it or decide its relationship to an original habeas request.

Section 107 creates chapter 154, §§2261–2266. Its special procedures depend on the enacted state-counsel mechanisms, competence standards, offers and orders, including the separate unitary-review provision. The chapter states its stay, filing, review-scope, adjudication-time and enforcement rules with their exceptions. Section 107(c) expressly makes chapter 154 applicable to cases pending on or after enactment. This explicit provision is distinct from the other Title I amendments, which do not contain that same general pending-case clause. No State is found to qualify merely because the chapter was enacted. No new stay, filing deadline in a particular case, waiver, termination or missed deadline is inferred.

Section 108 changes the identified provision of 21 U.S.C. §848(q)(9) governing investigative, expert and other representation services and the conditions for confidential requests. Its actual terms are preserved by the official source. This Record admits Title I only; it does not purport to inventory the Act's unrelated titles.
