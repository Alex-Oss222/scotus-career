The Act enacts Utah Code section 76-7-310.5, amends sections 76-7-314 and 76-7-315, and supplies an uncodified section 4. Section 310.5 defines the specified dilation-and-extraction and saline procedures, excludes the separately named dismemberment-before-removal, suction-curettage and suction-aspiration procedures from the former definition, and prohibits knowing performance after the section's physician-determined viability unless all other available procedures would pose a risk to the woman's life or health. The physician uses best clinical judgment, particular known pregnancy facts, and reasonably available medical technology and information to determine whether there is a realistic possibility of maintaining and nourishing life outside the womb with or without temporary artificial support. Intentional, knowing and willful violation is a third-degree felony. Those fault terms and the separate general penalty provisions are not collapsed.

Section 314(1)(a) retains third-degree felony treatment for intentionally performing an abortion otherwise than as authorized by the part; (1)(b) excludes the woman seeking or obtaining her own abortion from criminal liability. Subsection (2) makes willful violation of sections 307, 308, 310, 310.5, 311 or 312 a third-degree felony; (3) treats other violations of the part as class A misdemeanors. Section 315 provides that sections 302, 304(2), 305(2) and 310.5 do not apply when a serious medical emergency leaves insufficient time for compliance. Section 4 confines the new viability-determination method to section 310.5 and its purposes and states that it must not affect or relate to other statutory provisions or litigation concerning them.

The Act's meaning, constitutionality, relation to the pending injunction, and effect on the separate petition questions are not adjudicated. Respondents' implied-repeal and mootness contentions remain disputed litigation positions. ## Holdings

The Act creates no holding of the Court. Its text prohibits knowing performance of the defined procedures after viability is determined under section 310.5, unless all other available abortion procedures would risk the woman's life or health. The section uses a physician's best clinical judgment based on the known particular pregnancy facts and reasonably available medical technology and information, assessing a realistic possibility of life outside the womb with or without temporary artificial support. Its definition of dilation and extraction excludes dismemberment before removal, suction curettage and suction aspiration. Its own third-degree-felony clause requires intentional, knowing and willful violation.

Amended section 314 separately retains intentional unauthorized-abortion liability, the pregnant woman's exemption from criminal liability for her own abortion, willful-violation penalties for the listed sections including sections 307, 308 and new 310.5, and misdemeanor treatment of other violations. Amended section 315 makes sections 302, 304(2), 305(2) and 310.5 inapplicable when a serious medical emergency leaves no time to comply. Section 4 confines the new viability method to section 310.5 and its purposes and disclaims effects on other statutory provisions and their litigation.
