## Authority and operative text

The North Carolina General Assembly enacted [Session Law 1995-424](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/SessionLaws/HTML/1995-1996/SL1995-424.html), ratified July 12, 1995. The official complete session law was read, including both amendments and the effective-date provision. The following is the resulting amended text, with legislative deletion/underline markup removed but words preserved:

Section 1, G.S. 49-14(c):

> (c) No such action shall be commenced nor judgment entered after the death of the putative father, unless the action is commenced either:
> (1) Prior to the death of the putative father;
> (2) Within one year after the date of death of the putative father, if a proceeding for administration of the estate of the putative father has not been commenced within one year of his death; or
> (3) Within the period specified in G.S. 28A-19-3(a) for presentation of claims against an estate, if a proceeding for administration of the estate of the putative father has been commenced within one year of his death.
> Any judgment under this subsection establishing a decedent to be the father of a child shall be entered nunc pro tunc to the day preceding the date of death of the father.

Section 2, G.S. 49-14(d):

> (d) If the action to establish paternity is brought more than three years after birth of a child or is brought after the death of the putative father, paternity shall not be established in a contested case without evidence from a blood or genetic marker test.

Section 3:

> This act becomes effective October 1, 1995, and applies to actions commenced on or after that date, but before October 1, 1998, without regard to the date of death of the putative father. This act expires on October 1, 1998.

The pre-amendment subsection (c) prohibited commencing the action or entering judgment after the putative father's death. The amendment adds the three stated alternatives and the direction concerning the date assigned to a paternity judgment. Subsection (d) independently adds actions brought after the putative father's death to the contested-case test-evidence requirement. These provisions and the transition remain distinct. The cross-reference to G.S. 28A-19-3(a) is preserved as enacted; this record does not determine a particular estate's claim period or whether any claimant satisfies it.

## Law After Decision

The amendment permits a posthumous paternity judgment when the action began before the putative father's death; when it begins within one year after death and no estate-administration proceeding began within that year; or when an estate-administration proceeding began within that year and the action begins within the claims period specified by G.S. 28A-19-3(a). A judgment establishing paternity under this subsection must take effect as of the day before the father's death. In a contested paternity action brought more than three years after the child's birth or after the putative father's death, evidence from a blood or genetic marker test is required. The act applies only to actions commenced on or after October 1, 1995, but before October 1, 1998, without regard to the father's date of death, and expires October 1, 1998. No particular claimant's satisfaction of those requirements is established.
