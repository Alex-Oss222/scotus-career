## Controlling holdings

### Similarity must concern the challenged opportunity

**Operative rule:** In an equal-protection challenge to prison programs, whether prisoners are similarly situated must be assessed in relation to the treatment challenged. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resource choices may materially distinguish a particular program; general differences between entire institutions cannot categorically defeat every comparison of the benefits provided.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices named in Opinion Topology, controls this and the following two holdings unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Equality does not require identical institutions before any particular deprivation may be examined. The women's institution is smaller than the Nebraska State Penitentiary and houses prisoners with different sentence lengths and security characteristics. Those circumstances may explain particular choices; they do not establish that sex is irrelevant to every challenged opportunity. Hogan requires scrutiny of the actual sex distinction rather than a broad institutional label. Turner preserves attention to legitimate prison administration and resources; it does not exempt an actual sex classification from the governing equality rule.

The State correctly rejects a demand to duplicate the larger prison's entire program mix. But dismissal based on whole-institution dissimilarity is broader than that premise supports. The prisoners likewise cannot prove a constitutional injury merely by showing that another institution spends more or offers more programs. The Court vacates categorical dismissal while leaving the significance of concrete operational differences for the individual claims.

**Precedent treatment:** Hogan's real-justification inquiry and Turner's attention to actual operational constraints are applied together; neither becomes a rule of identical services or a prison-program immunity.

### Actual sex classifications and neutral decisions require distinct inquiries

**Operative rule:** If a challenged program decision actually classifies by sex, in its terms or administration, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the sex distinction. If the choice is genuinely sex neutral, plaintiffs must prove that it was made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and awareness of disparity alone do not establish that discriminatory purpose, though circumstantial evidence may do so.

**Authority:** The unanimous whole-opinion coalition identified above directly adopts both branches and their qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Craig and Hogan supply the scrutiny of actual sex classifications. Davis and Arlington Heights distinguish disparate effects from purposeful discrimination while permitting proof from circumstances. Feeney distinguishes a decision made because of its adverse consequences for a sex from one made despite them. Segregated housing does not itself decide which description fits each program choice; nor must plaintiffs produce a written admission before they may establish discriminatory administration.

Kelley confirms that a sex-conscious allocation remains subject to an actual important objective and substantial connection even when real program constraints exist. Its Title IX athletic routes are not prison rules. J.E.B. similarly rejects sex as a proxy for individual opportunity, but its jury-specific sequence of proof is not a new prison-program procedure. The Court preserves ordinary plaintiff burdens for neutral decisions and the State's justification burden for established classifications. It announces no impact presumption, least-restrictive-means requirement or right to a selected program.

**Precedent treatment:** Craig, Hogan, Davis, Arlington Heights and Feeney supply their distinct established functions. Kelley and J.E.B. inform sex-classification review without transferring their setting-specific statutory or procedural rules.

### The legal error requires further adjudication, not restored liability

**Operative rule:** The existing liability determination cannot stand merely on sex-separated housing and unequal programs without the required classification or purpose findings, but the absence of adequate findings under that mistaken approach does not justify dismissal through an overbroad comparator rule. The liability reversal is affirmed and the dismissal direction vacated so the preserved claims can be adjudicated under the proper standards.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices directly adopt this mixed disposition and its bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate majority and dissent differ over what the trial findings establish. The Court does not turn that disagreement into its own finding either of discrimination or of its universal absence. It instead separates whether a particular comparison is meaningful, whether the State used sex or acted with discriminatory purpose, whether the applicable justification is sufficient, and what relief follows. The district court's disparity premise is insufficient for immediate restoration; the appellate court's categorical dissimilarity premise is insufficient for complete dismissal.

The reviewed controversy remains limited to the pleaded program comparisons. Title IX, independent access-to-courts claims, other male institutions excluded by the denied amendment, unpleaded discriminatory funding, and any later final judgment are outside it. Gunter's individual responsibility and immunity, Clarke's official-capacity responsibility, damages and equitable relief remain separate and undecided. Judgments concerning other defendants are not reopened. Legally adequate existing findings may be used; additional proceedings are neither prohibited nor automatically required.

**Precedent treatment:** The ordinary classification and purpose authorities govern further adjudication; no new substantive liability or remedial test is substituted.
