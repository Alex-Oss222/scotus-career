## Source, authority and operative text

[Public Law 104-134](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ134/pdf/PLAW-104publ134.pdf) was enacted April 26, 1996. A [preserved official PDF](../sources/chunk6_control/plra.pdf) retains the original enacted text. The relevant provisions are Title VIII, sections 802–804, 110 Stat. 1321-66–1321-75. Section 801 names the title the Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995; that title does not change the 1996 enactment date. The official source identifies itself as the typeset text of the signed hand enrollment, printed without corrections. Its exact enacted language, qualifications and cross-references control. This admission does not substitute later amended Code wording for that text.

Section 802 replaces 18 U.S.C. §3626. It separately addresses prospective and preliminary relief, prisoner-release orders, termination and its limitation, consent decrees and private settlements, state-law remedies, motions and automatic-stay periods, special masters, and defined terms. Prospective relief requires the specified findings concerning the federal violation, reach and intrusiveness; public-safety and criminal-justice effects receive the stated weight. Preliminary relief has its own ninety-day condition. Release orders require the particular antecedent relief, time and factfinding predicates, and the specified three-judge procedure in federal court. No statutory requirement is treated as satisfied by admission alone.

The original termination limitation in §3626(b)(3) uses the enacted current-or-ongoing formulation. Section 3626(e)'s original automatic-stay provisions run from a pending motion under the stated timing rules; this Record admits no such motion or elapsed period. The definitions distinguish prospective relief from compensatory damages, consent decrees from private settlements, and prison-conditions proceedings from habeas proceedings challenging the fact or duration of confinement. The state-law-remedy provision retains its own scope.

Section 802(b)(1) expressly applies the amended §3626 to all prospective relief whether originally granted or approved before, on or after enactment. That provision is retained separately from the particular grounds and procedures for termination. It supplies no automatic finding that existing relief lacks the required basis and no determination of a constitutional objection.

Section 803 changes the identified Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act provisions, including personal-signature requirements, and replaces 42 U.S.C. §1997e. The latter supplies the stated available-remedies exhaustion condition; dismissal provisions and their exception to prior exhaustion; attorney-fee conditions and limits; the stated physical-injury limitation; hearing arrangements; waiver-of-reply provisions; and the enacted prisoner definition. Their text does not establish any litigant's exhaustion, fee entitlement, injury or dismissal in an existing case. The original fee language and thresholds must be read as enacted, without a later judicial gloss.

Section 804 amends 28 U.S.C. §1915, including assets and account documentation, full-fee and installment rules for covered prisoner filings, the no-assets initial-payment safeguard, dismissal and counsel-request provisions, costs, and the three-prior-dismissals provision with its imminent-danger exception. It also adds the identified bankruptcy nondischargeability text. These are separate statutory mechanisms, not a universal ban on indigent filings. No prior action is deemed a qualifying dismissal without a source-supported determination under applicable law. This admission's express prospective-relief transition clause does not settle every other component's temporal application to pending matters.
