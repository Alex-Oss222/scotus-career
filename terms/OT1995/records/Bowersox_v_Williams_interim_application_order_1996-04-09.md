## Precedent Treatment

- **Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, and Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320:** The successive-petition stay standard requires substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Actual procedural availability, prior dispositions and any independent merits ground matter. Neither petition count nor appellate silence substitutes for that inquiry. Barefoot's meaningful-review and necessary-stay rules for an initial certified appeal retain that setting; a certificate in this successive proceeding is not an automatic stay entitlement. Its certiorari-stage probability and irreparable-harm test is separate. Stokes's separate All Writs and heightened-deference reasoning is not promoted into a Court holding.
- **Delo v. Blair:** The equal division supplies no Court precedent or majority rationale.
- **Gomez and McFarland:** Independent stay equity and the State's enforcement interest survive; serious claims and irreversibility do not alone compel late relief. McFarland protects its actual statutory preparation opportunity while preserving denial for proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction. No new preparation entitlement is established here.
- **Schlup and Sawyer:** Crime innocence and death-eligibility innocence retain different thresholds. Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record, showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict; mere possibility or a reviewing judge's doubt is insufficient. Sawyer requires clear and convincing proof that absent constitutional error no reasonable juror would find death eligibility under state law; added discretionary mitigation alone does not suffice. Neither gateway establishes an underlying violation or automatically grants relief or a stay. No gateway is found satisfied.
- **Lonchar:** Its first-personal-petition dismissal correction and conditional necessary-process protection do not displace the successive-petition rule. Any necessary-process application requires identifying the obligation still due and inability to complete it lawfully in time; those predicates are not established here. Its restoration of a displaced district stay supplies no automatic remedy in this case.

The pre-AEDPA statutes and Rules remain applicable. Rule 9(a) requires delay-caused prejudice to the State's ability to respond and preserves its reasonable-diligence qualification; there is no enacted five-year presumption. Rule 9(b) requires the actual prior-merits/repeated-ground or abuse predicates. The then-effective §2244(b) subsequent-application permission follows a federal denial after an evidentiary hearing on a material factual merits issue or a hearing on a legal merits issue. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; the State's clear, particular abuse pleading precedes the prisoner's resulting burden. New evidence is not automatically a new ground. Exhaustion and its absence/ineffectiveness exceptions, default and independent merits grounds remain distinct. No current predicate is adjudicated.

The Court adds no new construction of §2251. No precedent is overruled and no final substantial-grounds standard is changed.
