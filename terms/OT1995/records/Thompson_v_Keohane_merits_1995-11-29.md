## Record, competing requests and scope

During the September 15, 1986 station interview, two unarmed plainclothes troopers questioned Thompson for about two hours in a small room. He drove there in response to a request to identify belongings. ## Controlling holdings

### Ultimate Miranda custody requires independent federal review

**Operative rule:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would cause a reasonable person to understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question requiring independent federal habeas review. Section 2254(d)'s presumption for factual determinations does not attach to that ultimate custody conclusion; historical events and supported credibility findings remain subject to the statute's own conditions.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt the classification, statutory limits and vacatur/remand. Thomas dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller's functional approach asks what the respective courts must decide, not whether a conclusion can be described as an inference. State courts determine what happened: the setting, words, conduct and credible accounts. The further question applies a national constitutional boundary to those established events. Independent application gives the objective arrest-equivalence rule in Berkemer and Stansbury consistent legal content without retrying the events.

Patton and Witt show that some assessments under legal standards remain factual because their subject matter and observational advantages support that allocation. They do not make Miranda custody presumptively factual. Nor does Miller turn every ultimate inference or mixed issue into a question reviewed anew. Custody's objective legal function supplies the distinction here. Wright established no general rule requiring acceptance of a reasonable state legal decision; Withrow allows this Miranda claim while retaining its ordinary habeas limits.

The federal courts applied the factual presumption to the ultimate noncustody conclusion. The judgment must therefore be vacated and the case remanded for independent application to the established circumstances. Voluntary arrival, accusations, duration, setting, assurances of freedom to leave and actual departure must receive their proper significance together. Private police intentions or later surveillance allegations do not become communicated custody facts by assertion. The Court decides neither ultimate custody nor suppression, harmlessness, an independent coercion claim, retroactivity or entitlement to release; no abandoned procedural default is restored.

**Precedent treatment:** Miller's functional fact/law distinction is applied to Miranda custody; Berkemer and Stansbury's objective standard is preserved; Patton and Witt retain their distinct factual classifications; Wright and Withrow retain their actual limits.

### The statutory protection of historical factfinding remains unchanged

**Operative rule:** Under the governing §2254(d), a competent state court's determination of a factual issue after a merits hearing in a proceeding involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by a written finding, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia, is presumed correct subject to the statute's exceptions. Excluding ultimate custody from that presumption neither establishes an exception for any historical finding here nor permits disregard of supported credibility determinations.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts this boundary on independent review; it makes no case-specific finding that a statutory exception applies.

**Controlling explanation:** The presumption yields when the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears, or the respondent admits that: (1) the factual dispute's merits were unresolved; (2) the factfinding procedure did not afford a full and fair hearing; (3) material facts were inadequately developed; (4) the state court lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter or applicant; (5) an indigent applicant was unconstitutionally denied appointed counsel; (6) the applicant lacked a full, fair and adequate hearing; or (7) the proceeding otherwise denied due process. The eighth exception applies when the pertinent state record is produced as the statute provides and, considered as a whole, does not fairly support the factual determination.

At a federal evidentiary hearing, once due proof of the state factual determination is made, the applicant bears the burden to show its error by convincing evidence unless a circumstance in(1)–(7) is shown, otherwise appears or is admitted, or the federal court concludes under(8) that the pertinent state record as a whole lacks fair support. Those conditions and allocations remain intact. A ruling on papers does not itself prove failure of the statutory hearing requirement. The remand corrects classification of the ultimate legal application, not any established historical fact, and creates no categorical right to a new evidentiary hearing or writ.

**Precedent treatment:** Section 2254(d)'s existing factual framework and Miller's respect for historical facts are preserved; no replacement general deference rule is adopted.
