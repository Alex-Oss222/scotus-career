## Event

Dalton v. Little Rock Family Planning Services, No. 95-1025. Summary review on certiorari petition, March 18, 1996; October Term 1995. No oral argument was held. The Court reviews the Eighth Circuit's July 25, 1995 judgment, 60 F.3d 497, affirming the Eastern District of Arkansas, 860 F. Supp. 609. Rehearing below was denied September 22, 1995.

The first petition question concerns whether a Medicaid-participating State must fund medically necessary abortions for eligible women whose pregnancies result from rape or incest despite its contrary constitution. The second challenges the scope of the injunction and declaration against Arkansas Amendment 68. Review is limited to the second question.

Amendment 68 §1 prohibits public abortion funding except to save the mother's life. The published July 25, 1994 decree enjoined the amendment in its entirety for as long as Arkansas accepts Medicaid funds and separately enjoined inconsistent abortion-funding provisions of its Medicaid plan. An unpublished July 27, 1994 stay-denial order declared the amendment null, void and of no effect. The court of appeals sustained the broad relief on Arkansas inseverability grounds. ## Holdings

### Relief must follow the established federal conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** When the established Medicaid conflict concerns particular applications of a state funding restriction, federal declaratory and injunctive relief must protect those applications without invalidating or suspending severable applications for which no conflict has been established. The July 25 injunction and July 27 declaration therefore cannot reach Amendment 68 in its entirety. The funding obligation underlying that conclusion is accepted without deciding the first petition question.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine participating Justices identified in Opinion Topology. The rule has nine direct joins and requires no combination of separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Supremacy Clause makes federal law controlling where the state rule conflicts with it. It does not eliminate every other operation of that rule. The lower courts' Medicaid construction, left unreviewed, supports protecting the specified eligible patients against contrary administration. It supplies no finding that every non-Medicaid public-funding application violates federal law. Harris v. McRae leaves the precise statutory funding issue open; denial of review supplies no answer to it.

The lower courts relied on Arkansas inseverability law and declined to write a new exception into the amendment. That objection does not justify this decree. Confining federal relief to conflicting applications leaves the enacted text in place; it neither creates a new state appropriation nor inserts an exception. Once §1 retains lawful applications, the premise that §§2–3 must lose every function because §1 has disappeared cannot support their blanket invalidation. Any genuinely remaining state-law severability question remains for the proper court. The State may not use another section to evade the Medicaid protection left standing.

NTEU permits sufficiently broad relief when the proved defect and an indivisible prohibition require it, while excluding unrelated claims from the decree. Here the Medicaid conflict does not establish a defect in every application. Madsen's remedial holdings likewise preserve lawful independent operation without imposing its speech-specific test on this case. The Court adopts no general rule against broad relief and no new facial-review formula. The crime-victim program illustrates §1's operation outside Medicaid; its funding source and independent constitutional validity are not decided.

### Prospective protection follows the applicable obligation

**Holding and operative rule:** The confined decree must preserve the adjudicated Medicaid protection while Arkansas participates and controlling federal law requires the coverage. It may not suspend unrelated applications merely because the State continues participating, regardless of a later change in the governing conditions. An actual lawful change may support appropriate modification; this decision declares no current lapse or automatic dissolution.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by the same nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The decree's continued reach must correspond to the federal obligation it enforces. Pub. L. 104-91 continues the relevant conditions under the account-specific qualifications stated above; locating that continuation establishes no intervening repeal or expiration. The present remand accordingly implements a decided scope correction while retaining the funding protection left unreviewed. A possible future change is not an event the Court can declare now. The receiving courts must express the actual obligation and its limits, without adopting a replacement funding policy or predicting a later legislative choice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither the underlying Title XIX/Hyde construction nor a constitutional entitlement to public abortion funding. It does not adjudicate the constitutionality of a direct abortion prohibition or foreclose a properly preserved constitutional challenge to a remaining application supported by a developed record. Casey's complete standard and Fargo's application remain unchanged; their access protections are not transformed into a funding entitlement. No independent standing, enforcement or jurisdictional issue drawn from the separate Nebraska litigation is decided.
