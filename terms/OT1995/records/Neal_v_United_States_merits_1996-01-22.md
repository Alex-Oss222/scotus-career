## Holdings

### The statute and Guidelines retain their separate LSD measures

**Operative rule:** For LSD absorbed into the blotter-paper carrier at issue, §841(b)(1)'s statutory mixture-or-substance weight includes the actual carrier weight under Chapman. Amendment 488's 0.4-milligram-per-dose calculation governs the Guidelines Drug Quantity Table within its lawful scope and does not replace that statutory measure; when the statutory minimum exceeds the maximum of the applicable Guidelines range, §5G1.1(b) makes the statutory minimum the Guidelines sentence.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices. The rule and its explanation command nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman v. United States construed the statute to count the LSD and its carrier together. The Commission's later amendment selects a constructive weight to reduce disparities among carrier media. Its commentary expressly preserves statutory mandatory minima. Courts must give both rules their proper operation rather than turn the Guidelines change into a statutory amendment.

Stinson v. United States makes valid interpretive commentary authoritative within the Guidelines system, while excluding commentary that conflicts with a federal statute. It does not empower the Commission to overrule a judicial construction of §841. Patterson v. McLean Credit Union gives particular force to statutory stare decisis because Congress can amend the enacted rule. The amendment's policy criticism of carrier disparities is not itself an adequate ground to overrule Chapman here. The Court does not hold that statutory precedent can never be reconsidered, or that every later agency interpretation is foreclosed by every earlier judicial statement.

The amendment's qualifications remain within their own field. For unmarked blotter paper, a quarter-inch by quarter-inch section is presumed one dose. For liquid LSD not yet placed on a carrier, pure-drug weight may inadequately reflect seriousness and may warrant an upward departure. Neither provision creates an exception to §841's minimum or requires a departure in this case.

The Court leaves lawful Commission policymaking intact. It decides no unpresented sentencing exemption, Guidelines temporal question, or distinct constitutional challenge involving an unusually heavy carrier with very little LSD.

**Precedent treatment:** Chapman remains the controlling statutory construction. Stinson's authority for lawful Guidelines commentary is preserved within its statutory limit. Patterson supports adherence without making statutory precedent categorically irreversible.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453:** Followed for actual LSD/carrier weight under §841; the Commission's constructive Guidelines measure does not displace it.
- **Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36:** Applied to preserve valid commentary within the Guidelines system and its limitation against conflict with a federal statute.
- **Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164:** Applied for the special force of statutory stare decisis while Congress retains authority to amend the rule.
