### The arms guarantee is personally held and does not require formal militia enrollment

**Operative rule:** The Second Amendment protects an individual's keeping and bearing of arms within the guarantee's constitutional scope. Formal enrollment or current service in an organized militia is not a prerequisite to invoking that protection; the Court does not thereby decide every protected weapon, defensive purpose or manner of carrying.

**Authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six direct votes. No aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Amendment identifies the people as the holders of the right. Its militia purpose does not convert those holders into governments or organized military units. United States v. Miller asked whether the particular weapon, on the record there, bore a relationship to militia preservation or efficiency. Its account described citizens supplying their own arms. That inquiry retains force but establishes no formal-enrollment prerequisite.

Earlier state decisions supply evidence of personal protection with real regulatory limits. Nunn v. State rejected a militia-members-only account while sustaining concealed-carry restrictions. State v. Chandler associated open carrying with defense of oneself and the country while upholding its concealed-weapons prohibition. Andrews v. State expressly described private keeping and its ordinary incidents, although its controlling analysis tied protected arms to citizen military preparation, permitted substantial regulation and rested on Tennessee's constitution after rejecting direct federal application.

These sources distinguish the holder of the right from its purposes and boundaries. They establish no unrestricted right to every weapon or use. Miller is neither converted into a general self-defense code nor read as withholding all protection outside an organized militia. United States v. Johnson's collective-only premise cannot sustain this dismissal. Constitutional coverage and restriction remain distinct questions.

**Precedent treatment:** Miller's weapon-and-record inquiry is preserved; a formal-membership prerequisite is rejected. Nunn, Andrews and Chandler supply historical evidence at their actual scopes, not binding federal incorporation holdings. Johnson's incompatible collective-only premise is rejected.

### The Fourteenth Amendment applies the personal arms guarantee to the States

**Operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause applies the Second Amendment's personal guarantee to state action with the same constitutional standards that govern federal action. Cruikshank and Presser are overruled only insofar as they foreclose that application; no total incorporation of every Bill of Rights provision or new Privileges or Immunities holding follows.

**Authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six direct votes for this rule and limited treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** Duncan v. Louisiana asks whether the particular guarantee is fundamental to the American scheme of justice. Malloy v. Hogan applies an incorporated guarantee by the same standards against States. Benton v. Maryland undertakes a right-specific historical inquiry and expressly displaces contrary nonincorporation precedent. Those cases do not already incorporate arms; they supply the method applied here.

The personal keeping and acquisition evidence, together with Reconstruction protection of arms as a civil right concerning liberty and security, establishes the guarantee's fundamental character. Section 14 of the 1866 Freedmen's Bureau Act expressly included the constitutional arms right among citizens' equal protections, without regard to race, color or previous slavery. It was a temporary measure covering places with rebellion-interrupted judicial proceedings until their restoration, or discontinued constitutional relations until restoration and representation in Congress. It was not a perpetual nationwide licensing code. Howard's explanation of the proposed Fourteenth Amendment expressly included personal arms rights and state restraint. His discussion ran through privileges or immunities; one speaker's explanation does not establish every ratifier's understanding or prescribe today's doctrinal vehicle.

The differing state clauses and regulatory boundaries do not negate the personal guarantee those sources recognize. They require careful treatment of scope, which incorporation itself does not exhaust. Cruikshank and Presser correctly described the 1791 Amendment's original federal operation, but their categorical barrier to state application cannot stand after this particular guarantee is examined under the later incorporation method. That intervening method and the right-specific historical showing justify this limited change; disagreement with an old result alone would not.

Presser's disposition concerning unauthorized armed military organization remains undisturbed. The Court invalidates no entire state firearms regulatory regime.

**Precedent treatment:** Duncan, Malloy and Benton supply the incorporation method and equal standards. Cruikshank and Presser lose only their barrier to Fourteenth Amendment application of the arms guarantee; unaffected propositions retain their force.

### Necessary acquisition cannot be excluded merely because the State obstructed purchase

**Operative rule:** Acquisition necessary to constitutionally protected keeping falls within the protection of that keeping; a State cannot categorically exclude the claim merely because it obstructed purchase instead of seizing an existing arm. This principle does not establish that every handgun is protected, guarantee purchase from every seller, prohibit investigation or licensing, or make violation of state authorization alone a constitutional infringement.

**Authority:** Part III-C of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six direct votes for the acquisition principle and these general boundaries. Stone-Zsela's affirmative application to Love is not adopted by this portion.

**Controlling explanation:** Protected keeping would lack practical effect if government could exclude the necessary act of acquiring an arm solely because it precedes possession. Andrews expressly identified purchase, ammunition, repair and related transport as incidents of private keeping. Its state-law basis, military-suitability boundary and permitted regulation qualify that historical evidence; they do not erase acquisition's relationship to possession.

This argument does not rest on unqualified freedom to contract. Its constitutional work comes from the protected arms interest. The weapon and conduct must fall within that guarantee, the restriction must be assessed under it, and responsibility and relief require their own predicates. No comprehensive weapon classification, scrutiny tier or closed regulatory list is adopted. Recognition of the necessary acquisition incident defeats a categorical purchase exclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Miller's actual scope inquiry remains; Andrews supplies historical acquisition evidence without adoption of every proposition in its majority or its broader separate opinion.

## Published noncontrolling positions and continuity

**O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas — plurality ground supporting due-process affirmance.** The four assume the state interest and accept the alleged deliberate, knowing and malicious arrest-only practice. They nevertheless find this eligibility-screening restriction insufficient to establish the separate substantive wrong. Unresolved dispositions concern the program's eligibility subject even when officials knowingly exceed statutory authority. Collins separates state duty from constitutional arbitrariness; Romein and Beach supply limited economic-rights analogies. The four adopt no benign-motive finding or legislative-classification burden for this executive act. They reject categorical cure through state review, an absolute-discretion entitlement rule and a finding that deadline pressure compelled the policy. Their bounded conclusion does not exempt every affirmative licensing act. Stone-Zsela does not join this application; it is not the Court's controlling rationale.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions and the personal-right, incorporation and necessary-acquisition holdings. He agrees with the due-process dismissal: unauthorized state administration does not by itself create a federal substantive wrong, state review corrected the state-law deprivation, and the incorporated Second Amendment supplies the specific protection for the asserted arms liberty. Substantive due process should not duplicate it. No separate entitlement to every lawful private transaction follows.

He would reverse the arms dismissal with constitutional infringement resolved, rather than vacate for an open application. In his view the guarantee centrally protects a law-abiding person's acquisition and keeping of a common firearm for lawful self-defense in the home. Nunn and Chandler support personal holders and defensive use while preserving regulation; Andrews supplies the acquisition incident, with its state-law and weapon-specific limits. Incorporation does not require reopening the Privileges or Immunities Clause.

His disqualification rule requires adjudicated conduct or incapacity genuinely connected to danger: conviction for a serious violent offense, a judicial dangerousness determination with adequate process, a domestic-violence protective order after notice and hearing, or adjudicated functional incapacity to possess safely. A bare diagnosis, accusation or uncontestable administrative label is insufficient. Convictions for murder, serious battery, armed robbery, violent domestic abuse, terrorism, planned unlawful force against government or comparable violence can support dispossession; arrests alone cannot. He does not settle every valid disqualifying conviction or restoration and duration rule.

On the pleaded record, Love's arrests were accusations; her identified obscene-show misdemeanor conviction established neither violence, dangerousness nor relevant incapacity. The Chief would hold that she could not constitutionally be denied an ordinary handgun solely because those arrests appeared in the files. The State may promptly investigate genuinely unresolved potentially disqualifying conduct under authority its law gives it. Missing dispositions do not become adjudications of guilt, and time pressure authorizes no judicially invented extra waiting period. Public safety does not supply a missing factual predicate.

His remand would resolve infringement while leaving personal responsibility, lawful relief and properly available defenses for ordinary proceedings. He decides no general public or concealed carrying rule, sensitive-place restriction, prohibited-weapon or magazine category, ordinary dealer licensing, recordkeeping, commercial regulation or storage rule. His affirmative application and adjudication-based disqualification framework receive no other Justice's join and are not the Court's law.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, and by Souter as to Part A only, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** All four join the procedural nonreach of immunity and the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions.

In **Part A**, all four would vacate due process and remand for assessment of the actual affirmative-policy claim. Zinermon and Stevens's Daniels concurrence distinguish substantive wrongdoing, complete when committed, from adequacy of procedure. Later correction alone cannot cure the former. The knowing-barrier allegations cannot be replaced by findings of necessary haste or official caution. Collins retains the need for a federal interest and substantive arbitrariness without completing this different application. An independent sufficient ground could permit affirmance despite erroneous reasoning, but these framing defects are material. The dissenters would return entitlement, constitutional sufficiency, attribution, defenses and relief for decision. They establish no violation, automatic discovery or trial, or damages entitlement. Malice and correction expense do not supply every element.

In **Part B**, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm arms dismissal under the specific state-nonapplication precedents. ### The arms guarantee is personally held and does not require formal militia enrollment

**Operative rule:** The Second Amendment protects an individual's keeping and bearing of arms within the guarantee's constitutional scope. Formal enrollment or current service in an organized militia is not a prerequisite to invoking that protection; the Court does not thereby decide every protected weapon, defensive purpose or manner of carrying.

**Authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six direct votes. No aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Amendment identifies the people as the holders of the right. Its militia purpose does not convert those holders into governments or organized military units. United States v. Miller asked whether the particular weapon, on the record there, bore a relationship to militia preservation or efficiency. Its account described citizens supplying their own arms. That inquiry retains force but establishes no formal-enrollment prerequisite.

Earlier state decisions supply evidence of personal protection with real regulatory limits. Nunn v. State rejected a militia-members-only account while sustaining concealed-carry restrictions. State v. Chandler associated open carrying with defense of oneself and the country while upholding its concealed-weapons prohibition. Andrews v. State expressly described private keeping and its ordinary incidents, although its controlling analysis tied protected arms to citizen military preparation, permitted substantial regulation and rested on Tennessee's constitution after rejecting direct federal application.

These sources distinguish the holder of the right from its purposes and boundaries. They establish no unrestricted right to every weapon or use. Miller is neither converted into a general self-defense code nor read as withholding all protection outside an organized militia. United States v. Johnson's collective-only premise cannot sustain this dismissal. Constitutional coverage and restriction remain distinct questions.

**Precedent treatment:** Miller's weapon-and-record inquiry is preserved; a formal-membership prerequisite is rejected. Nunn, Andrews and Chandler supply historical evidence at their actual scopes, not binding federal incorporation holdings. Johnson's incompatible collective-only premise is rejected.

### The Fourteenth Amendment applies the personal arms guarantee to the States

**Operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause applies the Second Amendment's personal guarantee to state action with the same constitutional standards that govern federal action. Cruikshank and Presser are overruled only insofar as they foreclose that application; no total incorporation of every Bill of Rights provision or new Privileges or Immunities holding follows.

**Authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six direct votes for this rule and limited treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** Duncan v. Louisiana asks whether the particular guarantee is fundamental to the American scheme of justice. Malloy v. Hogan applies an incorporated guarantee by the same standards against States. Benton v. Maryland undertakes a right-specific historical inquiry and expressly displaces contrary nonincorporation precedent. Those cases do not already incorporate arms; they supply the method applied here.

The personal keeping and acquisition evidence, together with Reconstruction protection of arms as a civil right concerning liberty and security, establishes the guarantee's fundamental character. Section 14 of the 1866 Freedmen's Bureau Act expressly included the constitutional arms right among citizens' equal protections, without regard to race, color or previous slavery. It was a temporary measure covering places with rebellion-interrupted judicial proceedings until their restoration, or discontinued constitutional relations until restoration and representation in Congress. It was not a perpetual nationwide licensing code. Howard's explanation of the proposed Fourteenth Amendment expressly included personal arms rights and state restraint. His discussion ran through privileges or immunities; one speaker's explanation does not establish every ratifier's understanding or prescribe today's doctrinal vehicle.

The differing state clauses and regulatory boundaries do not negate the personal guarantee those sources recognize. They require careful treatment of scope, which incorporation itself does not exhaust. Cruikshank and Presser correctly described the 1791 Amendment's original federal operation, but their categorical barrier to state application cannot stand after this particular guarantee is examined under the later incorporation method. That intervening method and the right-specific historical showing justify this limited change; disagreement with an old result alone would not.

Presser's disposition concerning unauthorized armed military organization remains undisturbed. The Court invalidates no entire state firearms regulatory regime.

**Precedent treatment:** Duncan, Malloy and Benton supply the incorporation method and equal standards. Cruikshank and Presser lose only their barrier to Fourteenth Amendment application of the arms guarantee; unaffected propositions retain their force.

### Necessary acquisition cannot be excluded merely because the State obstructed purchase

**Operative rule:** Acquisition necessary to constitutionally protected keeping falls within the protection of that keeping; a State cannot categorically exclude the claim merely because it obstructed purchase instead of seizing an existing arm. This principle does not establish that every handgun is protected, guarantee purchase from every seller, prohibit investigation or licensing, or make violation of state authorization alone a constitutional infringement.

**Authority:** Part III-C of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six direct votes for the acquisition principle and these general boundaries. Stone-Zsela's affirmative application to Love is not adopted by this portion.

**Controlling explanation:** Protected keeping would lack practical effect if government could exclude the necessary act of acquiring an arm solely because it precedes possession. Andrews expressly identified purchase, ammunition, repair and related transport as incidents of private keeping. Its state-law basis, military-suitability boundary and permitted regulation qualify that historical evidence; they do not erase acquisition's relationship to possession.

This argument does not rest on unqualified freedom to contract. Its constitutional work comes from the protected arms interest. The weapon and conduct must fall within that guarantee, the restriction must be assessed under it, and responsibility and relief require their own predicates. No comprehensive weapon classification, scrutiny tier or closed regulatory list is adopted. Recognition of the necessary acquisition incident defeats a categorical purchase exclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Miller's actual scope inquiry remains; Andrews supplies historical acquisition evidence without adoption of every proposition in its majority or its broader separate opinion.

**Operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as it affirms dismissal of the individual-capacity Second Amendment claim, and that component is remanded under the recognized personal guarantee, state application and acquisition principle. Neither nonapplication to the States nor lack of formal militia affiliation sustains dismissal; the actual weapon and conduct, constitutional restriction, lawful regulation, personal responsibility, properly presented defenses and available relief remain for determination.

State illegality and constitutional infringement remain separate, although the knowing-policy allegations cannot be replaced by findings of good faith or unavoidable time pressure.

Hafer requires each officer's own federal wrong. Pletcher's alleged recommendation, Pepersack's disapproval, Messick's notification and knowing supervision, and Tippett's alleged knowing consent and failure to stop the practice require their own treatment. An allegation that a supervisor should have known is not a finding of actual knowledge. Office alone supplies no liability.

## Law After Decision

Effective December 11, 1995, the personal Second Amendment guarantee applies to States through the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause on the same constitutional terms as federal action. Formal militia enrollment is unnecessary, and necessary acquisition cannot be excluded merely because purchase precedes keeping. Cruikshank and Presser's contrary state-application barrier no longer controls. Miller's genuine scope inquiry and permissible regulation remain. The Court supplies no comprehensive firearms test or present determination that Love's handgun and the alleged denial satisfy the full constitutional inquiry.

The due-process dismissal stands without a common sufficient application rationale. State illegality alone and a supposed entitlement to every lawful contract remain insufficient, but no general due-process licensing test, automatic state-remedy cure or specific-source displacement rule becomes law. The separate rationales do not alter Albright's authority limits.

Qualified immunity remains available for proper assertion under existing law. This decision neither resolves that defense nor replaces its clearly-established-law framework. Conduct-date notice remains separate from this merits holding; a current constitutional development supplies no retrospective notice by itself. Unappealed and abandoned dispositions remain outside the judgment.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas — plurality ground supporting due-process affirmance.** The four assume the state interest and accept the alleged deliberate, knowing and malicious arrest-only practice. They nevertheless find this eligibility-screening restriction insufficient to establish the separate substantive wrong. Unresolved dispositions concern the program's eligibility subject even when officials knowingly exceed statutory authority. Collins separates state duty from constitutional arbitrariness; Romein and Beach supply limited economic-rights analogies. The four adopt no benign-motive finding or legislative-classification burden for this executive act. They reject categorical cure through state review, an absolute-discretion entitlement rule and a finding that deadline pressure compelled the policy. Their bounded conclusion does not exempt every affirmative licensing act. Stone-Zsela does not join this application; it is not the Court's controlling rationale.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions and the personal-right, incorporation and necessary-acquisition holdings. He agrees with the due-process dismissal: unauthorized state administration does not by itself create a federal substantive wrong, state review corrected the state-law deprivation, and the incorporated Second Amendment supplies the specific protection for the asserted arms liberty. Substantive due process should not duplicate it. No separate entitlement to every lawful private transaction follows.

He would reverse the arms dismissal with constitutional infringement resolved, rather than vacate for an open application. In his view the guarantee centrally protects a law-abiding person's acquisition and keeping of a common firearm for lawful self-defense in the home. Nunn and Chandler support personal holders and defensive use while preserving regulation; Andrews supplies the acquisition incident, with its state-law and weapon-specific limits. Incorporation does not require reopening the Privileges or Immunities Clause.

His disqualification rule requires adjudicated conduct or incapacity genuinely connected to danger: conviction for a serious violent offense, a judicial dangerousness determination with adequate process, a domestic-violence protective order after notice and hearing, or adjudicated functional incapacity to possess safely. A bare diagnosis, accusation or uncontestable administrative label is insufficient. Convictions for murder, serious battery, armed robbery, violent domestic abuse, terrorism, planned unlawful force against government or comparable violence can support dispossession; arrests alone cannot. He does not settle every valid disqualifying conviction or restoration and duration rule.

On the pleaded record, Love's arrests were accusations; her identified obscene-show misdemeanor conviction established neither violence, dangerousness nor relevant incapacity. The Chief would hold that she could not constitutionally be denied an ordinary handgun solely because those arrests appeared in the files. The State may promptly investigate genuinely unresolved potentially disqualifying conduct under authority its law gives it. Missing dispositions do not become adjudications of guilt, and time pressure authorizes no judicially invented extra waiting period. Public safety does not supply a missing factual predicate.

His remand would resolve infringement while leaving personal responsibility, lawful relief and properly available defenses for ordinary proceedings. He decides no general public or concealed carrying rule, sensitive-place restriction, prohibited-weapon or magazine category, ordinary dealer licensing, recordkeeping, commercial regulation or storage rule. His affirmative application and adjudication-based disqualification framework receive no other Justice's join and are not the Court's law.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, and by Souter as to Part A only, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** All four join the procedural nonreach of immunity and the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions.

In **Part A**, all four would vacate due process and remand for assessment of the actual affirmative-policy claim. Zinermon and Stevens's Daniels concurrence distinguish substantive wrongdoing, complete when committed, from adequacy of procedure. Later correction alone cannot cure the former. The knowing-barrier allegations cannot be replaced by findings of necessary haste or official caution. Collins retains the need for a federal interest and substantive arbitrariness without completing this different application. An independent sufficient ground could permit affirmance despite erroneous reasoning, but these framing defects are material. The dissenters would return entitlement, constitutional sufficiency, attribution, defenses and relief for decision. They establish no violation, automatic discovery or trial, or damages entitlement. Malice and correction expense do not supply every element.

In **Part B**, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm arms dismissal under the specific state-nonapplication precedents. They acknowledge affirmative original evidence of individual keeping, acquisition and personal security, and the power to reconsider under Benton. Differing state clauses, military-suitable weapon boundaries, regulatory authority, the Reconstruction statute's equality and restoration setting, and Howard's privileges-or-immunities framing nevertheless fail, in their view, to justify this right-specific displacement of Cruikshank and Presser. Lewis's extant-felony setting is not a complete answer to Love. They adopt neither a collective-only rule, formal-enrollment condition nor state-correction cure. Souter does not join Part B; he joins the Court's arms holdings and bounded vacatur.

## Procedure After Action

The due-process dismissal stands. The Fourth Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as it affirms dismissal of the individual-capacity arms claim, and that component returns through the Court of Appeals for proceedings consistent with the personal guarantee, state application and necessary-acquisition holdings. The lower courts must assess the actual constitutional restriction, weapon and conduct within the guarantee, lawful regulation, each officer's responsibility, properly asserted defenses and legally available relief. No actual infringement, damages amount, punitive entitlement, fee award or immunity grant/denial is established. No automatic trial, discovery order, injunction or continuing-denial finding follows. Unreviewed dispositions remain undisturbed.
