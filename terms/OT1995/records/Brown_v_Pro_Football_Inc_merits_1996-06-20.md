## Holdings

### Joint implementation within an ongoing bargaining process

**Holding and operative rule.** The nonstatutory labor exemption protects members of an existing consensual multiemployer bargaining group when they jointly implement their last lawful wage proposal during or immediately after bona fide bargaining reaches impasse, the conduct directly arises from that bargaining, and it concerns mandatory employment terms and the bargaining participants. Contract expiration, impasse and the union's rejection of the particular proposed wage do not by themselves end that protection; it does not cover product-market collusion, agreements with nonlabor parties, sham bargaining, or restraints remote in time or purpose from the labor relationship.

**Authority.** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology, controls this proposition and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation.** Federal labor law permits joint bargaining by an employer group and leaves lawful bargaining responses available when negotiations reach impasse. Connell recognizes the need to accommodate that process with antitrust law while denying protection to restraints that reach beyond it. Jewel Tea supplies the collective-bargaining foundation of the exemption; its different opinions do not furnish one undifferentiated majority explanation. Bonanno establishes that impasse alone does not dissolve the bargaining relationship, and Buffalo Linen recognizes the legitimate role of coordinated employer bargaining responses.

The clubs' implementation concerns the represented players' wages, follows genuine bargaining, and directly continues that process. Applying antitrust liability at this point would subject its lawful operation to treble-damages judgments about the permissible bargaining response. The players correctly observe that an agreement fixing compensation would ordinarily raise antitrust concerns. But requiring union acceptance of this particular term before recognizing the exemption would let the rejection of a proposal transform an ongoing labor dispute into antitrust liability even though the bargaining relationship persists. The exemption protects the bounded process, not the desirability of the wage or the identity of the employer. The Court leaves open the precise point at which later conduct becomes sufficiently detached from bargaining to lose protection. It decides neither the legality of unrelated restraints nor any labor-law violation outside the submitted premises.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Jewel Tea, 381 U.S. 676:** Applied for the exemption's collective-bargaining foundation, preserving the distinction between White's and Goldberg's different explanations rather than treating either as a six-Justice rationale.
- **Connell, 421 U.S. 616:** Applied for the accommodation of labor and antitrust law; its refusal to exempt exclusion of unrelated subcontractors from the product market remains in force and marks the boundary against restraints on outsiders.
- **Bonanno, 454 U.S. 404:** Applied for the proposition that impasse alone does not dissolve consensual multiemployer bargaining; the precise later endpoint of this exemption remains undecided.
- **Buffalo Linen, 353 U.S. 87:** Applied for the recognized legitimacy of coordinated employer bargaining responses within the statutory labor relationship; it does not create immunity for every employer agreement.

## Law After Decision

The Sherman Act remains applicable outside the protected bargaining conduct. Within the continuing consensual multiemployer relationship, immediate implementation of the last lawful mandatory-term proposal after genuine impasse can retain the established exemption even without agreement on that term. The decision does not create a sports exception, a comprehensive labor-market immunity, or protection after conduct becomes remote from bargaining. Labor-law obligations and remedies retain their independent force.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens distinguishes the protection of collective employee demands and consensual group negotiation from immunity for an employer-only agreement imposing a wage the union never accepted. Jewel Tea's negotiated working condition does not establish that the employer restraint here receives the same protection. Connell shows why lawfulness under labor law cannot alone eliminate an otherwise independent antitrust constraint. Stevens accepts that impasse need not end the bargaining relationship under Bonanno; continued bargaining duties nevertheless do not establish an affirmative license to suppress wage competition through concerted enforcement of the rejected wage. His position preserves lawful multiemployer negotiation and permits ordinary antitrust defenses to be considered. He would reverse the exemption ground and remand for the unresolved appellate issues, without automatically restoring the entire monetary and injunctive judgment.
