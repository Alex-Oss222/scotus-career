## Holdings

### 1. A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding and operative rule:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Seven directly adopt the rule and reversal of the categorical dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the character of the later order, not a numerical limit, determines whether §1291 permits immediate review. Cohen requires a conclusive, sufficiently important issue separate from the merits that would be effectively unreviewable after final judgment. Mitchell recognizes the qualified-immunity category for a legal question about immunity from suit. A complaint and a developed summary-judgment record can present different legal questions; a legal defense to a reinstated claim may not have been available on the first appeal. Here the first appellate decision did not adjudicate the job-termination claim because it then stood dismissed. That circumstance exposes the defect in automatic exhaustion without establishing Behrens's immunity. Concerns about duplication and delay are substantial, but they do not justify withholding review solely because an earlier appeal occurred when the later issue independently meets the existing category. Digital Equipment requires disciplined examination of the category and every collateral-order condition; it does not equate public immunity with any asserted right to avoid litigation. Johnson preserves the factual boundary, while the first mandate and ordinary restrictions continue to govern repetitive arguments. The Court therefore rejects the categorical rule and remands for examination of the actual issues. It does not hold that every second denial is appealable or determine that every issue Behrens presents is legal.

### 2. An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding and operative rule:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this conditional legal/factual rule. Stevens and Breyer do not regard the condition of authorized successive review as satisfied here.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson distinguishes a legal question about the consequences of assumed conduct from a dispute about what the evidence establishes. Its boundary governs the second appeal just as it governs the first. The District Court's statement that material factual disputes remain does not itself demonstrate that every legal formulation is unavailable; conversely, calling a contention legal does not establish jurisdiction if it depends on selecting the official's disputed version. The Ninth Circuit must identify the proper assumptions and the actual legal issue without deciding the underlying employment conditions or regulatory facts. Kimberlin preserves review of a separable legal rule while excluding factual selection. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date precedent on an authorized immunity question, but supplies no independent jurisdiction or later notice. Swint forbids enlarging appellate authority for convenience; a legal issue does not carry unrelated factual disputes with it. The Court leaves actual issue sorting to the Court of Appeals. Its instructions do not resolve the factual merits, establish any property interest or constitutional violation, or award immunity.

### 3. A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Eight directly adopt the conditional principle; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this part. Stevens and Breyer continue to reject a second ordinary appeal as of right.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell's protection concerns immunity from defending the claim to which immunity applies. Having to litigate a different claim does not erase that interest. Respondent's proposed categorical objection would deny legal-immunity review whenever any other claim survives, regardless of the separate defense and the distinct litigation burden. The Court rejects that inference while preserving every other jurisdictional requirement. Swint confirms the complementary limit: one proper appeal cannot justify review of a separate nonappealable matter merely because deciding both would save litigation. The present rule therefore gives no jurisdiction over the United States negligence claim, the earlier substitution ruling or unrelated issues concerning continuing interference. It also does not decide whether the actual claims and assumed facts here produce an appealable legal question; that inquiry remains for the Ninth Circuit under the Court's other holdings.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp.:** Applied as the source of the cumulative collateral-order conditions; rejection of numerical exhaustion does not remove importance, conclusiveness, separateness or effective-unreviewability requirements.
- **Mitchell v. Forsyth:** Applied to legal qualified-immunity review and immunity from suit. The decision makes clear that the category is not exhausted merely by an earlier pleading-stage appeal, while leaving each later issue to satisfy its requirements.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Applied without weakening its factual-sufficiency exclusion or its rules for properly assumed facts; it does not impose a numerical one-appeal ceiling.
- **Kimberlin v. Quinlan:** Its distinction between a separable legal rule and factual selection is retained; no general mixed-order jurisdiction is created.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its independent consideration of relevant conduct-date law governs an authorized legal issue; it creates no jurisdiction and permits no retrospective notice from later law.
- **Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct:** Its category and cumulative-condition discipline remains controlling; the public qualified-immunity category remains distinct from ordinary private settlement rights.
- **Swint v. Chambers County Commission:** Its prohibition on convenience-based expansion is applied. Review of a separate claim or factual dispute does not follow from one proper immunity appeal; the reserved connected-review question remains reserved.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District:** Its exclusion of purely factual-sufficiency appeals remains unchanged; the present decision concerns successive-stage legal review, not the supervisory-liability merits decided there.

## Law After Decision

The existing legal-immunity appeal category cannot be restricted by an automatic rule allowing only one pretrial appeal. A later order must receive the ordinary jurisdictional analysis, on properly assumed facts and without evidentiary reweighing. Surviving different claims neither destroy otherwise qualifying claim-specific review nor become reviewable through it. The final-judgment rule, independent collateral-order conditions, prior mandate and ordinary preservation limits remain in force. No new substantive immunity or general interlocutory-appeal entitlement results.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Stevens.** They would affirm the jurisdictional dismissal. They join Parts II and III's conditional legal/factual and claim-specific rules but find no authorization for a second ordinary pretrial appeal as of right. Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require attention to importance and practical finality, not merely identification of another separable legal question. One pretrial immunity appeal adequately protects the sufficiently important interest in avoiding trial; repeated appeals can duplicate legal adjudication and delay resolution, while avoidance of additional discovery does not necessarily warrant another interruption. The reinstated termination claim presents a substantial argument for additional review, but does not justify expanding judge-made collateral review in this case. Mitchell's immunity-from-suit rationale does not require an appeal at each successive stage.

They would leave an exceptional later legal question to a separately authorized route. Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written statement that the order involves a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that immediate appeal may materially advance the litigation's termination. Application must be made within ten days, and the Court of Appeals may permit review in its discretion; applying alone creates no stay. No such certification or permission is established here. Sections 1292(e) and 2072(c) authorize rulemaking within §2072's substantive-rights limit; they are not themselves an operative rule permitting this appeal. The dissent favors that institutional route over another appeal as of right. It neither determines that every issue Behrens raises is factual nor decides his immunity.
