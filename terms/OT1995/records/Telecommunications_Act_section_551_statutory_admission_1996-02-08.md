## Source, authority and operative text

Congress enacted Public Law 104-104 on February 8, 1996. Section 551, 110 Stat. 139–142, is preserved in full in [the official-text reading copy](../sources/chunk4_c/telecom1996_section551.txt), derived from [the enacted public law](https://www.congress.gov/104/plaws/publ104/PLAW-104publ104.htm). The retained [complete official HTML](../sources/chunk4_c/telecom1996.html) and [complete text extraction](../sources/chunk4_c/telecom1996.txt) preserve context. The enacted text, including its conditions, controls over this admission's description.

Section 551(a) states congressional findings about children's television exposure, parental concerns, and the interest in empowering parents. Paragraphs (6)–(9) address sexual material, parental blocking technology, and Congress's view that information and blocking tools offer a nonintrusive and narrowly tailored means. These are legislative findings, not new judicial findings about the record in Action for Children's Television or a Court holding that any challenged restriction satisfies constitutional review.

Section 551(b) adds proposed Communications Act §303(w): guidelines and recommended procedures for identifying and rating sexual, violent or other indecent programming, and rules for transmitting ratings of programming that has been rated so that parents can block it. Its proviso excludes authorization to rate programming on the basis of political or religious content. The advisory committee must have the balanced representation, resources and reporting deadline specified in subsection (b)(2).

The applicability condition is explicit:

> The amendment made by subsection (b) of this section shall take effect 1 year after the date of enactment of this Act, but only if the Commission determines, in consultation with appropriate public interest groups and interested individuals from the private sector, that distributors of video programming have not, by such date—(A) established voluntary rules for rating video programming that contains sexual, violent, or other indecent material about which parents should be informed before it is displayed to children, and such rules are acceptable to the Commission; and (B) agreed voluntarily to broadcast signals that contain ratings of such programming.

Section 551(c) adds §303(x), directing the FCC to require a blocking feature in covered television receiving apparatus shipped in interstate commerce or manufactured in the United States, with screens at least 13 inches diagonally, subject to the alternative-technology provision. Section 551(d) adds §330(c): covered shipment and manufacture must conform to FCC rules; carriers merely transporting apparatus without trading in it are excepted. Industry standards are subject to FCC oversight and include receipt of the specified line-21 rating signals. The alternative-technology provision requires the FCC to amend its rules when the alternative meets all three statutory conditions: blocking without program ratings, comparable consumer cost, and comparable effectiveness and ease across a broad range of programs on a multichannel system. The conforming amendment is also retained in the complete source.

Section 551(e)(2) provides:

> In prescribing regulations to implement the amendment made by subsection (c), the Federal Communications Commission shall, after consultation with the television manufacturing industry, specify the effective date for the applicability of the requirement to the apparatus covered by such amendment, which date shall not be less than two years after the date of enactment of this Act.

Thus enactment and the statutory program are known on February 8, 1996; the conditional rating amendment cannot take effect before February 8, 1997, and the covered manufacturing requirement cannot become applicable before February 8, 1998. No later FCC determination, rule, industry agreement, receiver penetration, compliance event or technological availability is admitted by this Record. These limits do not decide whether the statute or any proposed alternative is legally relevant to a pending challenge; that is for the relevant Court event on its record.

## Law After Decision

Section 551 establishes a statutory program for parental information and blocking technology, including the 13-inch receiver threshold, the carrier exception and the conditions for alternative technology. Its conditional television-rating amendment takes effect one year after enactment only upon the specified FCC determination about the absence of acceptable voluntary rating rules and voluntary rating signals. The FCC must set applicability of the covered manufacturing requirement no earlier than two years after enactment. The statute does not establish that a nationwide rating or receiver-blocking system was operational in March 1996. Congressional findings are not judicial findings about a pending case.
