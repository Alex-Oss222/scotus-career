## Controlling holdings

### Use requires active employment of the firearm

**Operative rule:** Under the applicable §924(c)(1), a defendant uses a firearm only by actually and actively employing it as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment does not by itself satisfy use; firing or employment as a weapon is not required, and Smith's actual firearm barter remains use.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices named in Opinion Topology, supplies nine direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal of the use-based appellate affirmances.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress used a conduct word and separately referred to carrying. Reading use as every way a gun's availability may support a criminal enterprise would erase the difference between actual employment and possession. Smith holds that offering a firearm in exchange for drugs employs it in the transaction even though as merchandise. That example broadens the purposes for which a gun may be actively used; it does not eliminate the act of using it.

The Government's protective-availability and emboldening account addresses a real danger, but that danger cannot replace the conduct Congress specified. Bailey's weapon was stored in a locked automobile trunk; Robinson's was kept in her bedroom near drug proceeds. The trial theories and instructions permitted conviction through possession and availability rather than active employment. Those grounds cannot sustain the appellate judgments. Staples and Ratzlaf reinforce respect for the distinct enacted elements; they do not create an extra knowledge-of-illegality requirement or a universal presumption that every criminal ambiguity must be resolved before ordinary interpretation.

The Court provides no closed list of qualifying conduct and no comprehensive definition of carrying. It decides no retroactivity question and leaves distinct possession offenses untouched.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith's barter holding is applied and its unaddressed possession boundary resolved; Staples and Ratzlaf retain their offense-specific knowledge holdings without extension to a new mental element here.

### Connection to the offense cannot substitute for using or carrying

**Operative rule:** The Government must establish the use or carrying conduct independently of the requirement that it occur during and in relation to the predicate offense. A firearm's actual or potential facilitation may satisfy the required relationship under Smith, but it does not establish active employment under the use prong merely because the firearm is present or available.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Part I coalition directly adopts this distinct-elements proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith gives the relation requirement work by excluding an accidental or coincidental connection and requiring a purpose or effect concerning trafficking. Its actual exchange also supplies the separate act of employment. The two showings therefore coexist; one does not do the other's work. Harris likewise requires independent proof of §924(c)'s use or carrying even where the qualifying predicate itself includes a firearm. The Court applies that separation here without enlarging Harris's carjacking holding or deciding the outer boundary of sufficient facilitation.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith's relation rule and Harris's independent-conduct requirement remain in force; neither makes storage into use.

### The carrying alternative remains for proper consideration on remand

**Operative rule:** Both use-based appellate affirmances are reversed, and the D.C. Circuit may consider whether a legally sufficient and properly available carrying basis supports either reviewed firearm conviction. The presence of carrying language in the charges and instructions establishes no finding that every carrying element was lawfully determined, that the error was harmless, or that any particular remedial course is permissible.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight votes. Stone-Zsela supports remand but dissents from leaving the trial convictions available for that consideration without first vacating them.

**Controlling explanation:** The verified instructions submitted carrying in both cases, but they also treated possession and intended availability as sufficient without preserving the necessary conduct distinction. The Court decides the presented use issue; it does not decide an unexamined carrying theory from a charge label. The lower court must address the independent lawful requirements and procedural availability before relying on that alternative. Robinson's aiding-and-abetting submission likewise proves neither a substantive firearm offense nor intentional assistance by itself. Harris preserves the distinct requirement of intentional assistance; mere presence, association or awareness is insufficient.

The remand accordingly directs no carrying conviction, immediate acquittal or retrial. It neither determines waiver nor resolves sufficiency or harmlessness from instruction excerpts. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments remain undisturbed. The Court's refusal to order immediate trial-conviction vacatur is not a holding that either conviction is valid on carrying grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's independent-conduct and intentional-assistance requirements remain separate from the possibility of remand review; no new carrying or remedial rule supplants ordinary requirements.
