#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Tax or penalty for bankruptcy priority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the priority question for the allowed 1989 §4971(a) assessment only. Decide statutory classification without adjudicating a constitutional punishment claim.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the inherited bankruptcy construction rather than transfer the Internal Revenue Code label or Kurth Ranch’s constitutional consequence.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The §4971(a) assessment is not an excise tax entitled to priority under the applicable §507(a)(7)(E). Its operation penalizes failure to meet an independent pension-funding obligation, and no enacted cross-reference makes its tax label dispositive for bankruptcy priority.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Bankruptcy Code expressly borrows external definitions where it intends to do so; the provision at issue contains no such direction. The assessment arises from an unlawful funding omission and operates to sanction and deter that omission. Feiring, New York, and Sotelo require classification by the operation relevant to bankruptcy rather than by a word selected in another statutory setting. That resolves priority, not dischargeability, proportionality, or constitutional punishment. [P1, statutory provisions and record; S1, Kurth Ranch for the limited distinction]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress may expressly accord a specified assessment priority. Ordinary revenue taxes are not penalties merely because they encourage compliance. No other disallowed assessment is revived.

##### Issue 2: Equitable subordination and the operative judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the categorical-subordination question. Do not resolve whether creditor misconduct is indispensable to every possible §510(c) application or decide a new case-specific ground not found below.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Confine equity to a supported application within the enacted priority structure. Retain the denial of tax priority while vacating the unsupported subordination.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 510(c) does not authorize automatic subordination of all claims in a noncompensatory-penalty category merely because payment competes with compensatory creditors. The categorical subordination of the Government’s allowed claim must be set aside, without conferring the tax priority rejected under §507.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Every distribution affects other creditors. Treating that ordinary consequence as sufficient would authorize courts to create a new statutory ranking for an entire class Congress did not so rank. The plan already provides an alternative ordinary-unsecured classification if subordination fails. The lower courts must implement the judgment or consider only a properly preserved, legally sufficient, case-specific equitable ground. No such ground is supplied as an established fact here.
