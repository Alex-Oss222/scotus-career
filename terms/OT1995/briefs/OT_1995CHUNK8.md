**Shaw v. Reno**

**Current rule:** Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial district design, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the design-only equal-protection claim without racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or another independently cognizable constitutional deprivation. This substantive boundary is not heightened pleading, a universal downstream-loss requirement or an additional injury element for independent statutory claims. Other claims retain their own elements; no predominance scrutiny rule or general replacement for Croson or Metro Broadcasting follows. This remains so when race actually predominates and traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status or participation injury can be complete in government’s own unequal treatment without a second governmental act, monetary loss or later electoral defeat; shared-electorate composition alone does not establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race. Absence of a statutory command or prior adjudication requiring a particular district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. Shape is evidence, not a necessary gateway or sufficient proof. Purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal consequences remain separate.

**Lujan**

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1991/records/Lujan_v_Defenders_of_Wildlife_merits_1992-06-12.md), June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).

**Northeastern Florida**

**Current rule:** A governmental barrier preventing persons able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms supplies injury without proof that they would have won a contract. Accepted allegations suffice only at the appropriate stage; genuinely disputed elements require stage-appropriate proof. Replacing an ordinance does not moot the dispute when the successor continues the challenged exclusion from particular competitions; a replacement actually ending that injury may have a different effect.

**Holder v. Hall**

**Current rule:** A local governing body’s size is within Voting Rights Act §2 coverage. A vote-dilution challenge must supply a reasonable comparison with independent legal and practical roots, not a number chosen solely to produce desired racial winners. No uniquely ideal size is required. Accepting the comparison does not establish liability: the claimant must still satisfy the applicable Gingles predicates and the statutory totality inquiry into unequal electoral opportunity. A remedy for an established violation must address the violation while considering genuine governmental interests; the decision supplies no automatic judicial entitlement to a particular number of offices.

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** The primary position is VER-1: governmental consideration of race in drawing a shared territorial constituency does not, by itself, establish concrete personal injury, including when the objection is phrased as an objection to the voter’s own assignment. For claims within the fixed question’s premise of no concrete personal inequality, direct jurisdictional dismissal without prejudice and vacate merits relief dependent on those claims. The Miller-compatible approach below is the fallback; it does not displace the primary position. The operative reconstructed judgment determines the precise appellate instruction.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Drawing a territorial constituency joins residents in a common political unit. Considering racial population data in making that decision does not necessarily confer different rights, opportunities or civic standing on any person within it. Residence establishes a relationship to the district; it does not by itself establish injury. The plaintiff must identify the personal treatment or impairment being challenged beyond disagreement with the government’s consideration of race. Stone accepts well-pleaded concrete facts at the appropriate litigation stage and applies ordinary proof requirements where facts must be established. Standing does not require proving the underlying violation.

The allegation that the State subjected the plaintiff to a racial assignment requires an answer. Stone’s proposed distinction is that a shared constituency does not, merely through its design, legally assign constituents different political identities or make the representative responsible only to one race. An inference of inferior status or unequal representation needs the alleged governmental operation that supports it; racial composition alone does not establish that the representative will neglect other constituents. This reasoning rejects neither nonmaterial equality injuries nor the possibility that geographical arrangements can impose personal unequal treatment. A plaintiff suffering actual unequal treatment need not identify a separate financial loss, electoral defeat or second governmental act. Unequal opportunity itself may be injury without proof of losing the ultimate benefit.

**Limited change to Miller:** Part I-A of the completed simulated Miller record recognizes standing for its established personal-assignment allegations notwithstanding the failure of the design-only merits theory. Stone proposes to reconsider and overrule that threshold holding only to the extent it treats exposure to the racial design of a shared territorial constituency, without another concrete personal injury, as sufficient. This is an express change, not an unresolved question or an application already required by Miller. The completed Miller record remains unchanged.

Stone’s asserted legal reason is that Miller drew the wrong inference from personal exposure: exposure identifies the person affected by the governmental design, but does not automatically establish the concrete injury Article III separately requires. That argument must stand on its account of injury. Miller is not internally contradictory merely because it recognizes standing and rejects the merits, and Stone does not assert intervening law, changed facts or demonstrated unworkability that the record does not supply. Miller’s substantive equal-membership and voluntary-inclusion holdings remain intact.

The prior access to a judicial forum carries weight under Stone’s precedent principles. Closing that route is a real cost, not an interest erased because the claim may lose. Stone concludes that the asserted Article III error justifies the limited correction despite that cost; fewer lawsuits or a preferred policy result would not suffice. Ordinary opportunities to identify an independently sufficient injury and the dismissal’s nonmerits character remain, but neither pretends that forum-access reliance is unaffected.

**Preserved claims and sources:** Unequal electoral treatment, exclusion, dilution, impaired competitive opportunity, inferior civic status or another concrete personal injury may establish standing under the applicable source without additional financial loss or electoral defeat. These examples are not exhaustive. Add no new constitutional prerequisite to statutory Voting Rights Act claims and preserve their existing elements, enforcement routes and ordinary standing requirements. Apply the same injury principle regardless of race. Allen’s rejection of abstract stigma does not itself settle a personally asserted assignment injury. Warth preserves the distinction between standing and success; Bell’s narrow insubstantial-federal-question doctrine is not a substitute ground for answering this Article III question.

**Fallback under unrevised Miller:** If Miller’s threshold holding remains controlling, reject a genuinely abstract objection lacking personal exposure and recognize standing for allegations within its personal-assignment holding. Reject unsupported predictions of discriminatory representation where they are the only asserted injury, but do not eliminate an independent sufficient basis. Apply claimant-specific exposure and ordinary evidentiary rules only where the record warrants them; invent no expert-report, alternative-map or electoral-loss prerequisite. The fixed review concerns standing. Resolve that question and return remaining issues for the proceedings the actual judgment requires; decide no ungranted merits controversy.

**Casey**

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Kyles**

**Holding:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently satisfy that standard, and the defendant need not show acquittal more likely than conviction or that the remaining evidence is legally insufficient; the established suppression here meets the standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collective inquiry includes legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. It does not excuse lack of diligence about evidence already known to the defense, require open-file discovery or make every nondisclosure structural error.

**Holding:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Holding:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

The permission is discretionary, not a mandatory waiver rule. A consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review presents the distinct fair-inclusion setting addressed in Caspari; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate.

**Simmons**

**Current rule:** When the State puts future dangerousness in issue and the only lawful alternative to death is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. No single verbal formula is required. Speculative clemency does not make ordinary parole available. On direct review, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that denying the explanation did not contribute to the jury’s selection of death; where it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it continues to seek death. The error is not classified as structural.
