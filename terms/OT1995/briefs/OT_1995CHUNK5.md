**Lujan**

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the current-threat language without converting imminence into a demand for completed present injury.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 6972(a)(1)(B) requires an endangerment that may presently exist when suit is filed. ### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Supplied individual pre-vote position retained. The packet’s label does not independently document approval; no new approval, choice, or Court outcome is created by this reorganization. Apply any actual approval only to its documented scope.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Preserve the source-bound equitable relief awarded to restore the affected beneficiaries to the position they would have occupied absent the fiduciary breach, while excluding legal damages or duplicative relief available through another adequate ERISA channel.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Decide first whether Varity acted in a fiduciary capacity when making the challenged representations and then whether the requested relief is appropriate equitable relief under §502(a)(3).
- **Supporting Authorities:** ERISA §§3(21)(A), 404, and 502(a)(1)(B)–(3); *Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell*, 473 U.S. 134; *Mertens v. Hewitt Associates*, 508 U.S. 248; *Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen*, 514 U.S. 73.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** A person acts as an ERISA fiduciary to the extent it exercises discretionary plan administration or communicates plan information in that capacity, and §502(a)(3) authorizes appropriate individual equitable relief for a fiduciary breach when no other ERISA provision provides an adequate remedy for that injury.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Varity did more than announce a corporate restructuring: it used its plan-administration authority to explain existing benefit security and deliberately induced beneficiaries to surrender protected plan interests through materially false assurances. ERISA defines fiduciary status by the function performed, so the same employer may act as a settlor in one act and a fiduciary in another. Section 502(a)(3) operates as a source-bounded equitable safety net where the statute supplies no other adequate remedy, but *Mertens* prevents conversion of that language into general legal damages.

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

**Gomez**

**Holding:** A court considering an execution stay may weigh the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and an applicant's unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim independently of any habeas procedural bar. On this record, the unexplained postponement of the cyanide-gas challenge through four prior federal habeas proceedings leaves the April 20 stays without sufficient equitable support; those stays must be vacated even assuming the claim is cognizable under §1983 and not barred by abuse-of-writ doctrine.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Six of nine participating Justices adopt this independent equitable ground. Stone, Blackmun and Stevens dissent. No other ground is necessary to the judgment, and no fractured-opinion rule is invoked.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1983 channel, the final Eighth Amendment method claim, the application of every habeas-default element, Younger abstention and any universal alternative-method requirement remain undecided. The decision creates no permanent injunction or direction barring another federal stay.

**Herrera**

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Herrera's delayed and materially inconsistent affidavits, considered with the identification, physical and documentary evidence supporting his convictions, do not establish entitlement to the requested federal habeas relief or justify the district court's innocence-based stay, even assuming an exceptionally persuasive post-trial showing could constitutionally bar execution. This record-specific rejection establishes no uniform freestanding-innocence burden and does not decide whether such a constitutional claim exists.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, and White's express agreement with its reasons for finding this submission insufficient in his concurrence in the judgment: five Justices adopt this limited record conclusion. White does not join the plurality's broader discussion or an unspecified extraordinarily high threshold. No whole opinion commands five votes; no Marks synthesis establishes a common general test.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling opinion recognizes or rejects a freestanding constitutional innocence claim or fixes its general burden. The undefined extraordinary threshold and White’s rational-factfinder minimum have no established logical-subset relationship under Marks.
