**Oregon Waste**

**Current rule:** The State must identify the actual intrastate burden for which it compensates, show that the interstate charge roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and establish that the taxed events are substantially equivalent, reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies for one another. General income taxation and waste disposal are not such equivalent events. For a use tax said to equalize local sales taxes, the comparison must use the sales tax actually imposed in the affected locality. Statewide averages and burdens elsewhere cannot cure an excess there. Equal or lower use-tax burdens in other localities are not invalidated merely because an excess exists elsewhere; relief addresses the established inequality through lawful remedial procedures.

**West Lynn**

**Current rule:** A measure discriminates when it favors local economic interests by burdening interstate competitors, including a waste charge expressly keyed to out-of-state origin, compulsory routing that reserves a processing service to a designated local private operator, or an integrated assessment-and-distribution scheme that collects from the whole market but returns the proceeds only to local producers. The entire linked operation matters; formally equal collection does not cleanse its discriminatory distribution. The government must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Ordinary incidental-burden balancing does not validate discrimination. Conservation, sanitation, cost recovery and financing a favored private facility do not themselves meet that burden; contracting with a private operator does not make compulsory market regulation governmental market participation.

**Reich**

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Holding:** When a State holds out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, due process forbids withdrawing that remedy after payment for the constitutional invalidity asserted and then relying on the taxpayer's failure to pursue a different prepayment route. Georgia must afford Reich a clear and certain opportunity for the refund or constitutionally adequate equivalent relief due under federal law, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount; it may prospectively establish an adequate prepayment system with fair notice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The substantive tax invalidity is already accepted. The Court neither declares every Georgia prepayment avenue inadequate nor converts discretionary stays into guaranteed stays. It creates no general government-estoppel doctrine, exemption from clearly announced reasonable requirements, fixed refund, interest award, or reopening of final judgments. Prospective procedural changes remain permissible with adequate review and fair notice.

**National Private Truck**

**Holding:** Because the invoked §1983 equitable action affords no relief on this record, §1988 supplies no fee entitlement through that action. Success on state law does not categorically defeat fees where an independently viable fee-eligible federal claim exists.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Maher continues to permit fees through an available fee-eligible action without an express federal merits decision. No independently viable alternative fee-eligible federal claim is established here; reasonable amount, an unpresented damages action and the independent state fee ground remain outside review.

**Holding:** Section 1983 does not authorize injunctive or declaratory interference with state tax administration where an adequate legal remedy actually protects the claimant. The limitation arises from federal equitable principles informing §1983, not direct application of 28 U.S.C. §1341 to state courts, and does not restrict otherwise lawful state-law equitable relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this remedial rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Extraordinary relief remains possible for irreparable injury, cloud on title or a genuine risk of numerous suits between the same parties involving the same law or facts. Mere parallel claims by many taxpayers do not suffice. An inadequate remedy, including continued unconstitutional taxation after a refund judgment, may change the equitable inquiry. No general exhaustion rule or withdrawal of a promised refund is authorized; the general state-court duty to entertain section 1983 is assumed, not decided.
