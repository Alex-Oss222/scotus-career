## Source-supported sentencing record and enacted rules

Source: [United States v. Koon, 34 F.3d 1416](https://openjurist.org/34/f3d/1416), sentencing section J and its material notes; it quotes the sentencing memorandum at 833 F. Supp. 769, 786-790. The Ninth Circuit sustained the convictions and the district court's two-level bodily-injury adjustment, but vacated the sentences for resentencing after rejecting both downward departures. Those separate conviction and injury-adjustment rulings are not automatically reopened by the submitted departure questions.

The district court used the 1992 Guidelines. This is independently identified as an objective sentencing fact in the official published record recitation at 518 U.S. 88-89; no later resolution is incorporated. The [Commission's archived 1992 Manual](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/archive/1992-federal-sentencing-guidelines-manual) supplies the actual text. Chapter 2H1.4 used the greater of 10 or six plus the underlying-offense level. The lower court used aggravated assault: 15 plus six, four for dangerous-weapon use and two for bodily injury, producing level 27, category I, range 70-87 months. The separate five-level and three-level downward departures produced level 19 and 30-month sentences. The five levels were for victim misconduct; the three combined other factors. The three-level combination was not supported by a finding that each factor independently justified the full three levels.

The sentencing judge found that King's resistance and danger had ended by the criminal portion of the force. He also relied on earlier misconduct, the initial lawful force and the rapid transition during the encounter. The appellate opinion disagreed with the legal adequacy of that rationale and with its fit to the applicable guideline, while quoting the actual findings. A conclusion that King was still posing a danger during the criminal force would contradict the identified sentencing finding. A conclusion that the judge relied exclusively on mere chronological but-for causation would omit the judge's quoted dynamic-arrest and heartland reasoning; that is the court of appeals' criticism, not a substitute for the sentencing explanation.

The other departure grounds require separate treatment: unusual prison vulnerability tied to police status and extraordinary publicity; administrative/professional consequences including loss of law-enforcement employment; very low likelihood of recidivism; and the burden of successive state and federal prosecutions. The appellate court treated these as impermissible grounds in this case. It acknowledged unusual publicity but rejected its legal relevance, and noted that the record did not establish unavoidable continuous solitary confinement. The district court recognized that dual-sovereignty doctrine permitted the second prosecution; its departure did not rest on a double-jeopardy violation.

The selected complete [statutory and policy texts](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK8_B_STATUTE_EXTRACTS.md), together with the source receipt's separately identified §5H summaries, preserve these distinctions:

- Section 3553(b) requires an applicable-range sentence unless a circumstance of a kind or degree not adequately considered by the Commission should result in a different sentence. The adequate-consideration inquiry looks only to Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Section 3553(a) separately supplies sentencing purposes, offender/offense circumstances, available sentences, disparities and restitution.
- Section 3742(e) separately prescribes review of illegality, incorrect application, unreasonable outside-range sentences, and plainly unreasonable sentences without a guideline. Its final sentence requires due regard for credibility, clear-error protection for factual findings, and due deference to application of the Guidelines to facts. Section 3742(f) states the distinct remand/affirmance consequences. It does not say every legal interpretation receives deference.
- Chapter 1, Part A, 4(b) explains the typical range of cases addressed by a guideline, permits unusual cases to be considered for departure, identifies excluded grounds, and anticipates infrequent departures. It does not make every unmentioned factor forbidden.
- Section 5K2.0 recognizes incompletely considered circumstances and unusual degree; a factor already reflected in the applicable guideline may justify departure only on the specified unusual-degree basis. It does not erase a prohibition elsewhere.
- Section 5K2.10 permits reduction when the victim's wrongful conduct significantly contributed to provoking offense behavior. Its five listed considerations govern the extent: comparative size/strength; persistence and the defendant's efforts to avoid confrontation; reasonably perceived danger including reputation; actual danger; and other victim conduct substantially contributing to danger. Its criminal-sexual-abuse and nonviolent-offense qualifications, including unusual nonviolent retaliation after extended provocation, remain in force. The later sixth factor is not part of the 1992 text.
- Section 4A1.3 expressly locates category I's lower bound at the first offender with the lowest risk of recidivism and disallows a below-range departure on adequacy of criminal history. Sections 5H1.5 and 5H1.11 describe employment and public-service/prior-good-works considerations as not ordinarily relevant, while §5H1.10 excludes race, sex, national origin, creed, religion and socioeconomic status. Discouragement, full consideration and prohibition are not synonyms.

Actual Williams, including its burden on the sentence defender after established invalid-factor reliance, and actual Stinson govern. Williams requires both the same-sentence showing without invalid factors and independently reasonable extent grounded in the sentencing court's actual valid reasons. It does not place the causal burden on the challenger, treat possible lawful reimposition as enough, or authorize appellate invention of replacement reasons. A disposition affecting the three-level combination need not automatically determine the independent five-level departure.

## Supported contrary paths and separate remedies

One review path combines independent correction of legal permission and interpretation with substantial deference to a sentencing court's supported judgment about the atypical case and degree. The counterargument favors closer independent control where departure classification develops a uniform national range. The competing allocation must confront §3742(e)'s due-deference sentence, protected factual findings and the institutional task actually at issue. A chosen label does not excuse legal error or transfer every component to a single standard.

For the five-level departure, the significant-provocation threshold and actual §5K2.10 considerations must be applied to the judge's complete findings: danger and resistance had ended during the criminal force, yet the judge relied on the preceding misconduct, lawful force and rapid transition. One path finds that complete dynamic sufficient for legally permitted mitigation; the contrary path finds that the required connection or permissible degree is absent once force became criminal. Do not replace either with a new finding of continuing danger, or reduce the judge's rationale to chronology alone.

For the separate three levels, test prison vulnerability, employment/professional consequences, recidivism and successive prosecutions distinctly under the Manual. Prohibited, discouraged, already considered and unmentioned are different categories. The low-recidivism point must confront category I's express lower bound; extraordinary-publicity vulnerability must confront both its record support and the absence of inevitable solitary-confinement findings. Loss of position cannot simply be treated as a free socioeconomic privilege. Lawful successive prosecutions need not be constitutional error to be offered as a sentencing circumstance, but legality alone does not establish mitigation. No finding makes each factor independently sufficient for all three levels.

If an invalid factor affected a departure, actual Williams requires the sentence defender's whole-record same-sentence showing and independently reasonable extent grounded in actual valid reasons. A possibility of lawful reimposition is insufficient. The five-level departure does not stand or fall merely because the other combination contains an error. The final disposition must identify which review, factor and remedy questions it resolves for each petitioner without reopening unaffected convictions.
