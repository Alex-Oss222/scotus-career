## Operative provisions independently confirmed

Section **623(a)(1)**, following “It shall be unlawful for an employer—”, states:

> (1) to fail or refuse to hire or to discharge any individual or otherwise discriminate against any individual with respect to his compensation, terms, conditions, or privileges of employment, because of such individual's age;

Section **631(a)** states:

> The prohibitions in this chapter shall be limited to individuals who are at least 40 years of age.

The frozen packet's ellipsis joins the subsection's introductory phrase to paragraph (1) without changing its operative words. “At least 40” includes Finnell at age 40. Neither provision expressly makes the replacement's membership in the protected group a prerequisite to an evidentiary inference. That textual verification supports the existing presentation of the parties' dispute; it does not itself decide the legal question or establish discrimination.

The notes record removal of §631(a)'s former upper age limit effective January 1, 1987, subject to the stated collective-bargaining transition ending no later than January 1, 1990. That transition cannot restore a general upper limit for this August 1990 event. The later removal of the parenthetical reference to former §623(g) concerned the separate group-health-coverage provision, not the minimum protected age or a replacement-age requirement.

## Material qualifications and their actual role

**Section 623(f)(1)** permits otherwise prohibited action under subsections (a), (b), (c), or (e):

> where age is a bona fide occupational qualification reasonably necessary to the normal operation of the particular business, or where the differentiation is based on reasonable factors other than age, or where such practices involve an employee in a workplace in a foreign country, and compliance with such subsections would cause such employer, or a corporation controlled by such employer, to violate the laws of the country in which such workplace is located;

**Section 623(f)(3)** separately permits an employer, employment agency, or labor organization “to discharge or otherwise discipline an individual for good cause.” The notes disclose no change to either of those operative clauses between the August 1990 discharge and this 1994 edition. The employer's asserted performance and reorganization reasons remain potentially nondiscriminatory grounds; the statutory permissions do not turn disputed reasons into findings, establish good cause merely upon assertion, shift an unstated burden, or eliminate the age-motivation inquiry. No record premise establishes a bona fide occupational qualification or a foreign-workplace conflict. Those alternatives are not new issues requiring decision.

**Section 631(c)** supplies no generic exception for managers. It permits compulsory retirement only for an employee who has attained **65**, was employed in a bona fide executive or high policymaking position for the **two-year period immediately before retirement**, and is entitled to an **immediate nonforfeitable annual retirement benefit of at least $44,000 in the aggregate** from the employer's specified pension, profit-sharing, savings, deferred-compensation plans, or combination. Paragraph (2) requires adjustment under EEOC regulations, after Treasury consultation, when the benefit is not a straight life annuity without ancillary benefits or when employee/rollover contributions exist, to the equivalent noncontributory straight-life-annuity measure. O'Connor was 56. His managerial title cannot satisfy the age condition, and this exception neither authorizes the challenged discharge nor changes the prima facie question.

The **1994 version of §623(f)(2)** contains distinct seniority and employee-benefit permissions. The seniority system must be bona fide, not intended to evade the chapter, and may not require or permit involuntary retirement because of age of a protected individual. The benefit-plan permission requires either the specified per-benefit or benefit-package equal actual payment/cost rule, as permissible under the identified June 22, 1989 regulation, or a voluntary early retirement incentive plan consistent with the chapter's relevant purposes. Neither benefit alternative excuses failure to hire, and a benefit plan may not require or permit the prohibited involuntary retirement. The provision expressly puts proof of lawfulness on the employer, employment agency, or labor organization acting under those identified seniority/benefit alternatives. This is an **express, confined statutory burden**; it is not a new allocation for every ADEA case or for the submitted replacement-age question.

**Conduct-date qualification:** The notes state that Public Law 101-433, enacted **October 16, 1990**, replaced §623(f)(2) and added provisions including (k) and (l). Its general applicability reaches employee benefits established or modified on or after enactment and other conduct occurring **more than 180 days after enactment**, subject to the detailed collective-bargaining, state-plan and continuing-benefit-payment rules. The discharge here occurred in August 1990, before enactment. The 1994 wording is therefore not automatically the conduct-date rule for that discharge. The amendment note preserves the former §623(f)(2), which allowed observance of a bona fide seniority system or bona fide employee benefit plan not a subterfuge to evade the chapter, while preserving the stated failure-to-hire and age-based involuntary-retirement prohibitions. No seniority, pension, benefit-plan, or retirement defense is established in the supplied case record, so resolving those transition details further would not change the present neutral frame.
