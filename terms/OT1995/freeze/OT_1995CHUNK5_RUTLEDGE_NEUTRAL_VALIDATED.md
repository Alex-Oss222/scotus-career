## Record, judgment, and question

The Seventh Circuit's November 10, 1994 opinion, 40 F.3d 879, covers Rutledge, Henson, and Hagemaster's consolidated appeals. Bauer writes for Bauer, Flaum, and visiting Judge Foreman; there is no separate writing. The entire reported opinion, both footnotes, and panel note were read. The relevant ruling accepts the conspiracy charge as a lesser included offense of CCE yet sustains separate convictions and concurrent sentences under Bond/Bafia so long as punishment does not exceed the CCE maximum. The unrelated defendants' claims and Rutledge's other rejected trial/sentencing claims are outside the presented question.

The second superseding indictment, JA 2–7, charges Count 1's enterprise from early 1988 through late 1990, a continuing series of §§841(a)(1)/846 violations, concert with at least five persons whom Rutledge organized/supervised/managed, and substantial income/resources. Count 2 alleges the same-period knowing cocaine-distribution agreement. The parties agree that the same agreement supplies Count 2 and the concert element here. The Government preserves a legal contention that §848 might, in another case, reach concert involving an unwitting participant; it does not insist upon resolving that categorical question to sustain this judgment. Do not silently turn an assumption/concession about this prosecution into a universal element holding.

The December 29, 1992 judgment follows guilty verdicts on six counts. JA 8–12 confirms a life term on Count 1 and a concurrent life-without-possible-release term on Count 2, plus an independent concurrent life term on Count 3, ten years on Count 4, consecutive firearm terms, and a total $300 special assessment covering six counts. No fine, incarceration costs, or restitution was ordered. The two challenged convictions therefore remain distinct judgments even though these two prison terms run concurrently. Relief concerning Counts 1–2 does not itself release Rutledge, erase the other convictions, or reopen their sentences.

The petition's actual question asks whether entry of both judgments and concurrent sentences violates the Fifth Amendment when identical conduct proves the conspiracy and a necessary CCE element. Petitioner requests vacatur of either count and its corresponding sentence, leaving the choice initially to the sentencing court; the Government's narrower formulation requesting preservation of CCE is not a concession by petitioner that only conspiracy may be vacated.

## Operative provisions

- Fifth Amendment: no person shall be subject for the same offense to be twice put in jeopardy of life or limb. This is a single-prosecution, cumulative-conviction/punishment case; successive-prosecution doctrine does not automatically decide it.
- Section 846's received text: any person who attempts or conspires to commit a subchapter offense is subject to the same penalties prescribed for the offense that was the object. Shabani supplies unlawful agreement and knowing participation, beyond reasonable doubt, without an overt-act element. The indictment's listed overt acts do not create a new statutory element.
- Section 848(c) requires a felony violation of subchapter I or II that is part of a continuing series, undertaken in concert with five or more other persons as to whom the defendant occupies an organizer, supervisory, or other management position, from which he obtains substantial income or resources. It adds requirements to the agreement; the dispute is what Congress intended for the overlapping agreement offense.
- Section 848(a): twenty years to life, specified fines and §853 forfeiture; a final prior §848 conviction triggers thirty years to life and enhanced fines. Subsection (b)'s mandatory-life route requires principal leadership plus either the stated 300-times drug quantity or $10 million gross receipts within twelve months. Subsection (d) excludes suspension/probation and the identified D.C. parole regime. Neither subsection supplies an express direction to impose an additional §846 conviction. Section 848(e)'s intentional-killing/death provisions are distinct and not charged here; do not borrow their additional-penalty language for these counts.
- Offense conduct straddles the 1988 amendments. The briefs print their then-current statutory versions and petitioner expressly says intervening changes are immaterial to this appeal. Their matching core §846/§848(c) text supports the element inquiry, but this review did not independently reconstruct every offense-date penalty amendment. No new sentencing calculation is authorized by that omission.
- Official 1994 18 U.S.C. §3013(a)(2)(A) imposes $50 on an individual convicted of a federal felony; (b) collects it like a criminal fine; (c) then terminates the obligation five years after judgment, applicable irrespective of imposition date; (d) includes assimilated §13 offenses. Do not import the later $100 assessment. These assessments are not concurrent prison time.

## Entering law and public positions that bear on the forks

**“Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment” — Present operation effective April 27, 1995, United States v. Harris.** All nine adopted the bounded Hunter application: express statutory authorization permits cumulative convictions/punishment in one prosecution even assuming Blockburger overlap. The actual February 5, 1996 PUBLIC Bishop/Stokes decision again applies this rule unanimously and preserves Whalen's contrary-intent presumption when no clear authorization appears. Stevens joins the governing rule while expressly retaining his constitutional objection to Hunter's foundation. This is material current law omitted by a simple Jeffers/Ball-only account; it does not establish congressional authorization for §§846/848.

**“Knowing narcotics agreement without an overt-act requirement” — Present operation effective November 1, 1994, Shabani.** O'Connor's opinion is joined by all other Justices. Mere association or awareness is insufficient. The precise agreement requirement must be used in any Blockburger comparison.

**“Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt” — Present operation effective Dixon, June 28, 1993, with Felix's March 25, 1992 components continuing.** The simulation retains Grady. Souter's retained-Grady proposition had Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter; Felix's agreement/substantive distinction had White, Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. These public positions concern successive prosecutions and the distinction between an agreement and its substantive objects; they do not declare conspiracy distinct from an enterprise containing the same agreement. Historical briefs' post-divergence descriptions cannot overwrite these rules.

**“Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution” — Present operation effective June 14, 1995, Witte.** O'Connor, Stone, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer hold that relevant-conduct use within the authorized sentencing range is not separate punishment/prosecution; Scalia and Thomas concur only on the narrower distinct-prosecution ground; Stevens dissents. **“Conditional coordination of undischarged sentences,” same effective authority:** O'Connor, Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer adopt the received §5G1.3(a)/(b)/(c) sequence. No automatic cap or concurrency cure follows. Neither Witte component decides whether a second judgment is itself unauthorized punishment.

**“Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review” — Present operation effective Lebron, February 21, 1995, for bounded disavowal; X-Citement Video, November 29, 1994, for defenses of an existing judgment; Izumi/Caspari/Madsen retain separate components.** The assessment question requires an actual fair-inclusion/preservation determination. Respondent can defend the existing judgment by a supported alternative without enlarging it; neither useful briefing nor abstract logical priority authorizes a new claim.

Pre-divergence primary reported decisions were read in full, including their separate writings and footnotes: **Jeffers, 432 U.S. 137; Ball, 470 U.S. 856; Garrett, 471 U.S. 773.** Jeffers assumes without deciding the lesser-included premise, rejects cumulative fines under §§846/848, and addresses the defendant's insistence on separate trials. Its retained convictions are not a fully adjudicated approval of the present dual-judgment issue; the earlier conspiracy judgment was not before the Court. Its relevant agreement is not unanimous: White dissented on cumulative punishment, a point petitioner's reply corrects. Ball distinguishes permissible multiple charging/verdicts from unauthorized dual judgments and treats the second conviction, collateral consequences, and stigma as material despite concurrency; it remands for vacatur of one conviction. Stevens's concurrence objects to allowing multiple charging to encourage compromise. Garrett permits punishment for substantive predicates plus CCE, explicitly distinguishes conspiracy's shared concerted-activity danger, and addresses continuing enterprise conduct after an earlier prosecution. O'Connor separately stresses successive-prosecution finality/continued conduct; Stevens dissents. Garrett's substantive-offense holding cannot simply be transferred to the same agreement.

## Strongest competing routes

**Petitioner:** Jeffers's legislative-intent holding, reaffirmed in Garrett's conspiracy discussion, supplies no cumulative punishment for this same agreement; Ball makes a second conviction and concurrent sentence punishment, not merely a bookkeeping entry. Blockburger reinforces that §846 adds no element to the particular CCE agreement. Reenactments left that interpretation undisturbed. Section 3013 shows a present monetary consequence, and no prediction of negligible future consequences or harmlessness can erase an unauthorized judgment. Congress knows how to direct cumulation, as §924(c) shows. A district-court choice to vacate one count implements the limitation without altering the verdicts on independent offenses.

**United States:** Congress created separately defined statutes for an agreement and a more serious hybrid enterprise containing substantive violations; Jeffers limited total authorized penalties but left convictions/concurrent terms in place. Ball's nearly coextensive firearm offenses and legislative structure can be distinguished. Preserving both judgments protects a valid conspiracy verdict if the additional CCE elements later fail, without increasing present imprisonment or fine. Modern no-parole/related-cases rules reduce identified collateral effects and the additional stigma may be negligible. This is an argument for congressional intent and lawful judgment structure, not an assertion that concurrency invariably defeats double jeopardy; state collateral consequences are not categorically denied. The Government reserves the general concert/agreement distinction while permitting decision on legislative-intent grounds here.

## Reach and remedy gates

1. Decide whether the common agreement is assumed or affirmatively construed as lesser included; distinguish elements, overlapping evidence, and actual congressional intent. Do not resolve all conceivable enterprises involving unwitting persons without need.
2. Decide whether legislative intent forbids only aggregate imprisonment/fines or also dual convictions. Explain Ball/Jeffers rather than inferring a holding from silence or treating conspiracy like Garrett's substantive predicate.
3. Address the Government's assessment-preservation objection: it says no lower challenge and a question framed around concurrency; reply says assessment is an automatic subsidiary consequence of the challenged dual judgments. The JA and official statute establish the amount, not a waiver conclusion. A disposition on other grounds may leave this disagreement unnecessary.
4. If a defect is found, identify the count, judgment, sentence, and associated assessment requiring correction. Petitioner's requested remedy permits either count; do not silently promise release or disturb unpresented counts.
5. Keep competing alternatives visible: outright vacatur; one combined judgment/sentence retaining both verdicts; staying entry on one verdict; and possible reinstatement after a later successful challenge to CCE. The Government favors a combined disposition as fallback and notes finality/piecemeal-appeal costs of staying entry; petitioner says a combined label still records two convictions, supports vacatur, and asks to leave contingent reinstatement questions to concrete later cases. Neither a future acquittal, retrial, reinstatement, nor categorical bar should be assumed. The distinct reasons for a future reversal would matter.
