# Bowersox — proposed bounded second-degree reconstruction

**Status: proposed, not adopted.** Preparation only; no Court action or vote. Authority for express selection is Engine §14(5). The published reports identify a second-degree felony-murder due-process and equal-protection claim but do not establish its precise theory or complete prior-presentation history. The following minimum premises permit substantive evaluation without fabricating historical filings or resolving a procedural bar by silence.

For this follow-up only, treat the second-degree felony-murder claim's due-process and equal-protection theories as resting on alleged inconsistent application of Missouri's then-applicable lesser-offense rules to Williams, using the reported trial evidence. Assume for legal assessment that the omitted instruction was available under those state rules and that its refusal differed from treatment of materially similar cases. No additional protected-class allegation, discriminatory-purpose evidence beyond that alleged inconsistency, new trial evidence, or separate defect in the second-degree murder and manslaughter instructions actually given is posited.

For this limited stay assessment, assume the second-degree ground is procedurally available. This is not a finding about the actual earlier filings and changes no other claim's procedural barriers.

If expressly selected, identify these as authorized simulation premises, not recovered historical facts. They neither merge the first- and second-degree claims nor establish federal invalidity, substantiality, a stay, ultimate prejudice, or relief. Each Justice must independently decide whether the bounded theory supplies a substantial available federal ground under the effective law. No final result or coalition is selected.

The reconstructed claim is confined to this application assessment. The earlier interim order and every completed adjudication remain intact. No service, deadline, party compliance, execution, new evidence, or lower-court finding is supplied.
