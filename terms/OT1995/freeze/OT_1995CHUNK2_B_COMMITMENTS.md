## 1. Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue — December 11, 1995

**Non-Stone disposition:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would **affirm the Eighth Circuit's reversal and remedial remand**. Stevens would **reverse the Eighth Circuit and leave the school-favoring district-court judgment in place**. No Justice proposes damages, compulsory worship, reopening instructional hours, or an unconditional right for every community group.

### Law, reach and record

The speech question is preserved: the lower opinion identifies the posttrial argument and the district court's decision on viewpoint discrimination. The case seeks declaratory and injunctive access relief. The actual 3–6 p.m. arrangements, not the possibility of access after 6, define the dispute. The district court found both the Scouts and the Club concerned with moral development while distinguishing secular skill activities from Christian faith instruction. The policy allows Scout speech only with an express exclusion of religious speech and activity. The legal question is whether that is a neutral activity definition or exclusion of a perspective from an admitted subject.

**Applicable register:** “Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits,” present operation through Rosenberger and Chabad, June 29, 1995, preserving Lamb's Chapel, June 7, 1993. Lamb's Chapel forbids viewpoint exclusion even in a nonpublic school forum but does not abolish genuine subject limits. Chabad requires actual private attribution and rejects a categorical location defense; Pinette creates neither universal forum immunity nor a mandatory disclaimer formula. Rosenberger's printing program does not itself decide this facilities case. Lemon remains governing establishment law; no later school-club decision is used.

The Club is parent-led, open without denominational exclusion, attended with parental permission, and advertised as privately sponsored. Prayer and Bible study are part of its program. Students are 11–15, and meetings follow instruction while other school activities continue. Those features receive separate consideration: private sponsorship is not inferred merely from the word “club,” and age does not by itself prove official worship. The late bus creates convenience, not compulsory participation. A former board member's past involvement does not establish current governmental control.

### Supported alternative paths

**Access path:** The admitted Scout program includes instruction and expression about character and moral development. Excluding the Club because its approach to that subject is Christian, reinforced by the explicit no-religion proviso, is viewpoint discrimination under Lamb's Chapel. A sincere desire to avoid establishment does not turn the exclusion into a neutral rule. Voluntary attendance, parental choice, distinct adult private leadership and express nonsponsorship keep this use private. The strongest contrary argument is that a religious instructional meeting is a different activity and that younger students encounter it during the school's broader extracurricular day. That argument does not overcome the actual admitted subject or establish school direction on this record. The access conclusion need not treat every devotional practice as eligible in every forum.

**School-discretion path:** The district retained a nonpublic forum for established Scouts and athletics, activities tied to its secular educational program; broad moral-development similarities do not make every adult program comparable. Cornelius and Perry permit reasonable speaker/activity distinctions. Lamb's Chapel involved a plainly admitted adult presentation subject, and Mergens construed a statute rather than requiring all schools to invite adult-led religious instruction. The strongest contrary argument is the policy's explicit religious exclusion even within Scouts, combined with the district court's finding of common moral-development purposes. Stevens nevertheless regards the actual activity and relationship distinctions as sufficient for this Club's denial; he does not adopt an unlimited power to suppress religious perspectives in activities actually admitted.

## 2. Love v. Pepersack — December 11, 1995

**Non-Stone disposition:** All eight would **affirm dismissal of the presented federal damages action**. The common sufficient grounds are qualified immunity for the individual officials on the pleaded substantive and firearms theories, and Will's separate bar to §1983 damages against the State. All eight preserve the live nature of past damages despite approval and decline to manufacture a procedural-due-process claim.

### Law, reach and alternative paths

The conduct date is September 1990, not the date of this judgment. The state court's correction of the application denial remains in effect. No issue here asks the Court to reconsider that correction. The case does not present a continuing denial requiring a new prospective injunction.

**Immunity path, adopted:** The alleged violation of Maryland's approval conditions does not by itself show a clearly established federal substantive duty or destroy qualified immunity. Roth permits state-created interests, but does not make every erroneous deprivation a substantive federal wrong. Harlow and Anderson require particularized federal notice; Davis v. Scherer preserves the distinction between violation of state law and the right furnishing the federal damages action, including its qualification for rules that themselves establish the relevant federal right. The inherited Cruikshank and Presser decisions foreclose any claim that a federal Second Amendment right against this state licensing practice was clearly established in September 1990. Miller supplies no contrary state-law licensing holding. This resolves damages without a new incorporation ruling or a collective-right holding.

The current Hodge decision, October 23, 1995, was joined on individual immunity by all eight non-Stone Justices. It preserves the distinction between a disputed state-created interest and clearly established federal protection, and permits that specific immunity ground without mandatory constitutional adjudication. Hafer preserves individual-capacity personhood and personal responsibility while retaining individual defenses; Will separately governs the State. Albright supplies no unified substantive-due-process test, state-remedy bar or tangible-loss requirement.

**Competing merits paths:** One could assume entitlement and hold this safety-related administrative denial does not establish substantive constitutional arbitrariness, or find a deliberately unauthorized deprivation potentially sufficient and permit further merits proceedings. The latter's strongest point is the alleged standard practice of using arrests that state law does not make disqualifying; the former's strongest point is that constitutional substantive protection is not appellate enforcement of a state licensing code. Neither merits path eliminates the independent conduct-date immunity issue. The claimed liberty to complete a lawful purchase does not identify clearly established federal protection at the requisite level in 1990. The statute's approval conditions are not treated as irrelevant to entitlement or as mere procedure; they simply do not establish the missing federal notice by themselves.

**Why not adopt the lower court's broader rationale:** A damages claim can survive eventual state correction; an injury need not be incapable of any later remedy to be constitutional. No general proposition that successful state litigation cures every substantive federal violation is approved. Nor is immunity based solely on a lack of a case with identical facts. The inherited nonincorporation decisions and the absence of established substantive protection for this licensing refusal provide affirmative legal context. The preserved theories do not allege a separate clearly established discrimination, retaliation or seizure claim.

## 3. Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions — December 18, 1995

**Non-Stone disposition:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would **vacate and remand for jurisdiction-specific application of the correct §2 standards**. O'Connor and Kennedy would **affirm on the tried record's failure of proof**, without adopting categorical structural immunity or racial-partiality assumptions. Scalia and Thomas would **affirm on their separately preserved statutory noncoverage position as to vote dilution**. The two affirmance rationales are not one opinion coalition. No Stone vote is supplied and no final Court majority is declared.

### Law entering and scope

The challenge covers ten circuit-election systems and district elections in four named counties, as the neutral addendum specifies; a statewide class does not expand that scope. Section 2 applies to judicial elections under Chisom and Houston Lawyers. Ordinary fact review is clear error; whether an incorrect legal criterion shaped the findings is a legal question. The plaintiffs challenge the ultimate opportunity determination and the separate remedy holding, not each historical subsidiary finding.

**Applicable registers:** “Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims” (present operation June 30, 1994, De Grandy, with Growe/Voinovich preserved); “Section 2 challenges to governing-body size” (June 30, 1994, Holder); and “Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury” (June 29, 1995, Miller, preserving Shaw). Their actual rules, not historical cases bearing the same names, govern.

A comparison must have legal and practical roots independent of a desired racial tally. The applicable Gingles conditions remain necessary and not sufficient; the statutory totality inquiry concerns equal opportunity to elect preferred candidates, without requiring discriminatory purpose, proportional officeholding or maximization. The race of a candidate does not conclusively identify voter preference. Appointment and unopposed incumbency can affect the significance of success. Genuine linkage, candidate-pool and court-administration interests remain relevant, but neither a facially lawful structure nor linkage categorically exempts an election from §2. The simulated Holder rule accepts workable comparisons without demanding a uniquely ideal design. It does not automatically validate this proposed recircuiting or require creation of offices.

The lower opinion's inference that racial awareness necessarily makes judges racially partial cannot substitute for proof under the actual Shaw/Miller law. Even the Justices who opposed those holdings must identify an independently supported ground rather than silently import displaced doctrine. Conversely, correction of that premise alone does not automatically overturn an independently sufficient no-dilution finding.

### Enhanced comparison of lawful paths

**Vacatur path:** The opportunity and remedy inquiries contain interconnected legal errors: potential success by preferred candidates must be analyzed within each challenged electoral jurisdiction, success explained by appointment or unopposed incumbency cannot mechanically defeat a claim, and the possibility of a preferred white candidate is relevant without making raw statewide success an automatic answer. The remedy discussion repeatedly conflates opportunity with guaranteed election of Black judges and treats race-aware districts as inherently damaging to impartial adjudication. A remand can preserve historical facts and legitimate governmental interests while correcting those rules. Its strongest obstacle is the lower court's express independent holding, after weighing both experts and multiple kinds of elections, that the third predicate was not established. A general objection to remedial reasoning cannot displace that holding. The vacating Justices find the intertwined legal framing sufficiently material to require reconsideration, but do not find dilution in all challenged jurisdictions themselves.

**Record-affirmance path:** De Grandy preserves the plaintiff's ultimate burden and rejects automatic liability from polarized-voting evidence or another feasible plan. The trial court considered both experts, judicial and other races, actual candidate preferences, party affiliation and incumbency, and found the necessary unequal opportunity unproved. Clear-error review does not authorize choosing the dissent's factual account. Affirmance can therefore rest on the failure of proof without the appellate remedial alternative. Its strongest obstacle is that deference does not protect a finding reached through mistaken definitions of opportunity or improper statewide offsets. O'Connor and Kennedy read the primary ground as a permissible contextual inquiry, not a numerical safe harbor, and do not endorse an intent requirement or replace minority preferences with officeholder race.

**Noncoverage path:** Scalia and Thomas retain the statutory objections they expressed in the simulated Holder and De Grandy matters; Scalia's Chisom/Houston Lawyers dissent is additional earlier judicial-election-specific support. They would reject judicial vote-dilution claims under their construction of §2. The decisive barrier to broader joins is contrary binding coverage precedent, reinforced by the simulated Holder majority. Their position is a proposed separate rationale, not current law and not a reason the other six may avoid the governing results inquiry.

**Rejected unqualified paths:** No Justice here finds a record basis for an immediate statewide liability judgment, an order selecting a particular map, guaranteed election of Black candidates, creation of judgeships, or a categorical ban on all race-aware remedies. Cumulative voting was not a proposed remedy in the reported case; the dissent's suggestion supplies no automatic entitlement to have it ordered. No constitutional purposeful-discrimination claim is revived.
