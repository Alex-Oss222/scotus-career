The packet's enacted Amendment68 text has three components: §1 forbids public abortion funding except to save the mother's life; §2 states policy protecting unborn life to the extent permitted by the Federal Constitution; §3 excludes contraceptives and disclaims requiring an appropriation. A grant would require ordinary merits development of TitleXIX categories, state discretion, the rider, the federal directive and the actual enforceable duty; it would not itself reverse the funding holding. Summary reversal of Q1 is not justified merely by quoting the appropriations prohibition. The strongest **deny** case is the absence of an identified material split, the developed lower consensus, and a separable remedial issue that can be corrected without deciding that difficult construction. No holding from a denial can settle either side's theory.

### Q2: complete denial, plenary grant or summary correction

The strongest **denial/affirmance** argument rests on the lower courts' actual Arkansas-law inseverability finding and their refusal to rewrite an exception into the amendment. Respondents may also emphasize that the published injunction is temporally conditional, not a permanent repeal, and that state officials must not evade the funding obligation through §§2–3. Those points are substantial, especially for Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer given their *NTEU* class-remedy joins. They do not establish that every non-Medicaid application conflicts with federal law. The unpublished July27 declaration also cannot be ignored.

The selected **summary correction** addresses a federal scope error before deciding how words are severed under Arkansas law. Limiting an injunction to applications displaced by federal Medicaid law does not insert new words into the state constitution or remove any enacted language. The whole text can remain on the books subject to federal priority where an actual conflict arises. If implementation raises a genuine remaining state-law severability question, that question remains for the proper court; the Supreme Court need not announce a new Arkansas-law construction. An anti-evasion clause can preserve the adjudicated Medicaid duty, but cannot declare §§2–3 universally invalid on an unproved theory.

The strongest **plenary-review** alternative is that the state-severability question and potentially different published/unpublished orders require fuller examination before relief changes. The verified petition appendices resolve the operative scope; both questions were squarely presented and opposed. Each Associate therefore accepts summary correction at the federal-remedy level, without resolving an additional constitutional claim or contested fact. A new source-dependent jurisdictional premise, state-construction issue or substantive funding ground would require further process rather than expanding this summary judgment.

## Exact permissible remedy and join boundaries

Grant review limited to Q2. Vacate the judgment **only insofar as it affirms invalidation or suspension of Amendment68 beyond applications conflicting with the Medicaid obligation that remains unreviewed**; remand for the lower courts to enter corresponding declaratory and injunctive relief. The July27 “null, void and of no effect” declaration must be corrected to the same limited extent. Deny review of Q1 expressly, without adjudicating its statutory merits.

The remand preserves the funding-specific prohibition against relying on Amendment68 to refuse medically necessary abortions for Medicaid-eligible women in the adjudicated rape/incest categories while the applicable federal obligation controls. It does not award damages, decide past reimbursements, order payment for every abortion, command Arkansas to remain in Medicaid, decide the separate Nebraska judgment, or adjudicate the crime-victim program's constitutional validity. A later lawful change in funding conditions may justify appropriate modification; no hypothetical amendment, withdrawal or automatic expiration is declared now. The current continuation does not itself alter the decree.

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's *NTEU* broad-remedy joins are not contradicted by this bounded correction: there the existing indivisible class prohibition's proved defect justified class protection, whereas here the established Medicaid conflict does not reach every application of a state funding amendment. Scalia and Thomas's *Stutson* objections are not evaded: this is a reasoned decision identifying actual remedial error, not a return because the lower court might have used an unknown premise. O'Connor's application-specific remedial preference is directly consistent. All may join the federal-scope rule at this level without joining one another's broader access, spending or facial-review theories.
