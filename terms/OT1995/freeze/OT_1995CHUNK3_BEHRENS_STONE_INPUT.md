## OT1995-029 — Behrens v. Pelletier

**Event:** 1996-02-21 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 299

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1244

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Permit the second interlocutory appeal only to decide the legal immunity question on the facts the district court treated as sufficiently supported; dismiss any attempt to contest evidentiary sufficiency.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Appellate jurisdiction extends only to the separable legal immunity issue. The court of appeals may not decide which facts the record supports or which witnesses a jury should believe.
- **Supporting Authorities:** Simulated *Johnson v. Jones*, 515 U.S. 304; simulated *Elder v. Holloway*, 510 U.S. 510; *Mitchell v. Forsyth*, 472 U.S. 511; 28 U.S.C. §1291.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not bar a later summary-judgment appeal, but the later appeal reaches only whether the facts assumed or deemed adequately supported violate clearly established law and does not permit appellate reweighing of the record.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Qualified immunity protects against litigation burdens at distinct procedural stages, so a categorical one-appeal rule would make a later legal immunity error effectively unreviewable. At the same time, *Johnson* preserves the final-judgment rule by excluding disputes over what the evidence proves. The collateral-order category therefore permits successive-stage legal review while retaining the factual boundary.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not authorize repeated appeals from an unchanged legal ruling, appellate review of evidentiary sufficiency, or interlocutory resolution of which factual account is true.
