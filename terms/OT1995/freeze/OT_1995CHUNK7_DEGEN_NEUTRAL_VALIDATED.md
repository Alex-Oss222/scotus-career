## Supported facts and independent proceedings

Brian Degen is a dual United States/Swiss citizen. He left before indictment and disputes an intent to evade prosecution when he departed. The Government and lower court rely instead on his knowing refusal to return afterward. The extradition treaty does not require a party to surrender its own nationals. Neither the date of departure nor citizenship alone answers every fugitive-status or necessity question.

The Government's complaint invokes 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(6) and (7) against real and personal property in California, Nevada, and Hawaii, alleged to be proceeds or facilitating property. The court controls the res and can enforce the ultimate property judgment. Brian timely filed verified claims and answers through counsel, denying substantive forfeiture allegations and raising defenses including limitations, retroactivity, and probable cause. His indictment and refusal to return establish neither guilt nor the merits of those property defenses.

The District of Nevada, 755 F.Supp.308, struck Brian's claim in December 1990 on disentitlement grounds. The Ninth Circuit, 47 F.3d1511, approved the defense bar. It identified the district court's mistaken view that disentitlement was mandatory rather than discretionary, but held Brian's appellate objection to that mistake waived. That subsidiary preservation ruling is distinct from the preserved challenge to the existence/scope of the asserted power. Do not reframe the case as a proved ordinary discovery default or treat an acknowledged discretion error as automatically preserved here.

Karyn Degen's claims initially survived. Her later failure to oppose a supported renewed summary-judgment motion, after extensions and reopened discovery, produced a separate judgment and affirmance. She did not seek this review. Her failure is neither Brian's litigation misconduct nor a basis to reopen her judgment upon his success.

## Governing law and the actual tools it supplies

**1994 forfeiture statutes.** Section 881(a)(6) covers specified drug-exchange proceeds and facilitating money/property, subject to its innocent-owner knowledge/consent qualification. Section 881(a)(7) covers real property used or intended to commit or facilitate a covered offense punishable by more than one year, again with its stated innocent-owner qualification. Section 881(d) incorporates applicable customs forfeiture procedure only insofar as consistent. Under 19 U.S.C. §1615, the claimant's burden follows the Government's antecedent showing of probable cause for institution of the action. No subsequent forfeiture reform is imported.

The identified official `21usc881_1994.txt` gives §881(i): a qualifying indictment/information concerning a related civil forfeiture “shall, upon motion of the United States and for good cause shown, stay the civil forfeiture proceeding.” A qualifying charge, relationship, Government motion, and good cause are distinct conditions. The existence of this tool is not proof that a stay was sought, required on the existing record, or adequate for every indefinite overseas absence. Protective orders and Rule 37 sanctions likewise require their own authority, predicates, notice/process, and proportionate scope; hypothetical future disobedience is not present misconduct.

**Actual Ortega-Rodriguez, March 8, 1993:** Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter among six Justices, requires a sufficient connection to the appellate process where flight and recapture precede a timely appeal. It preserves rules for actual disruption, inability to conduct meaningful review, and prejudice to a legally available retrial. It does not establish universal least-restrictive sanctions or decide this separate civil defense bar. Its concern with the relationship between sanction and proceeding is relevant by reasoned analogy.

**Actual Good, December 13, 1993:** Kennedy's pre-seizure opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg among six Justices, ordinarily requires prior notice and meaningful hearing for real-property forfeiture seizure; an exigency requires a showing that less restrictive measures would not protect the Government. The exact Standards and Tests entry is **Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property**, present operation effective December 13, 1993. Its movable-property, emergency, title, compensation, and criminal-forfeiture limits survive. The separate statutory-timing holding rejects an unstated dismissal sanction for breach of §§1602–1604 where the action satisfies §1621, while preserving independently supported relief. Good does not require automatic final forfeiture dismissal, return, or damages here.

**Actual Kokkonen, May 16, 1994:** Scalia's unanimous opinion demands an independent jurisdictional basis for postdismissal enforcement of a separate settlement unless a lawful order incorporates obligations or retains enforcement jurisdiction. The Standards and Tests entry is **Jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal**, present operation effective May 16, 1994. Its source-and-function limitation informs the inherent-power question, but Degen's court already has jurisdiction over this pending forfeiture. Kokkonen is not a categorical denial of inherent power.

**Actual Carlisle, April 29, 1996:** Scalia's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer among seven Justices, forbids general rules and inherent supervision from supplying the same late acquittal foreclosed by specific Rules. It preserves inherent authority generally. Stevens, joined by Kennedy, dissents on a distinct retained pre-sentence duty, not unrestricted equity or a power to ignore an applicable rule. Here the inquiry must identify whether a specific forfeiture mechanism conflicts with or leaves room for the asserted sanction; the analogy does not answer that question automatically.

**Surviving pre-divergence sources.** Chambers, 501 U.S.32, distinguishes inherent litigation-sanction power from unbounded punishment; Hovey, Hammond, and Societe distinguish deprivation of a hearing as punishment from a justified litigation-related sanction or evidentiary inference. The safe handoff identifies official `chambers_usreports.txt`, `hovey_usreports.txt`, `hammond_usreports.txt`, and `societe_usreports.txt`. They require analysis of the connection and asserted necessity, not a label-driven decision that every defense bar is either valid or invalid.
