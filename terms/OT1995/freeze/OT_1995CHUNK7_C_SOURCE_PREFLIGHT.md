**Primary record and verified qualifications.** The existing Alabama opinions, `sources/A_Richards_lower_AlaSC_662So2d1127.txt` and `sources/A_Richards_prior_AlaSC_527So2d1270_Bedingfield.txt`, support the current neutral's posture and competing preclusion accounts. The judgment under review is the permissive interlocutory appeal from denial of summary judgment on federal claims, 662 So.2d 1127; federal employee/Buck Act claims are not before this Court. No federal-tax merits judgment is available to affirm or reverse here.

The petitioners' merits brief, printed pp.1–3 and 16–20 (`richards_07...`, lines 301–414, 977–1175), states that Bedingfield was not a class action, that neither side sought additional joinder or class treatment, and that petitioners received no notice their claims could be impaired. It distinguishes nonparty identity from sameness of cause, invokes Hansberry/Martin/Zenith, and asks reversal and remand for a hearing. The no-notice assertion is an identified litigant position supported by cited record material, not a new appellate finding. The existing neutral carefully does not convert the source record's lack of findings about notice/participation/control into an affirmative finding on every one of those subjects.

The respondents' merits brief, printed pp.29–36 (`richards_08...`, lines 1764–2090), contains the important concessions and alternative: public-right virtual representation is an exception requiring caution; broad claim preclusion “arguably goes too far” where the only privity is identity of interest in a public question; narrower **issue preclusion** is urged if the exemption issue was actually and necessarily decided. Respondents rely on the earlier complaints, Grubb's treatment of an issue necessarily resolved but unmentioned in an opinion, allegedly identical interests, the City's resources, and later assessment of adequacy. The Alabama majority says federal theories “could have been,” and arguably were, adjudicated; Maddox contests same-cause/actual-adjudication reasoning. Do not rewrite this disagreement as an uncontested finding of actual federal adjudication.

The public/private-interest distinction must be confronted rather than supplied by a label. The current complaint seeks relief from each taxpayer's own liability, including refunds; respondents contend all taxpayers share the identical public issue. Their reliance and municipal-bond interests are supported advocacy and lower-court reasoning, not substitutes for a constitutional binding predicate. Ordinary state-law finality remains relevant, but deciding that someone was constitutionally bound is distinct from retrying a correctly binding judgment's state-law errors.

**Supported paths, without a disposition choice.** A supported adequacy/representation basis could sustain binding within its constitutional scope; a constitutionally inadequate nonparty-binding basis could require reversal or vacatur and remand without adjudicating the tax merits. The respondents' issue-preclusion alternative still requires both a lawful basis to bind these persons and actual/necessary determination; relabeling claim preclusion does not independently supply representation. No automatic invalidation, refund award or federal-employee relief follows from failure of preclusion. No categorical rule against every representative taxpayer judgment should be inferred from the absence of Rule 23 treatment in this case alone.

## Whren — objective justification, actual restraint, and limited remedy

**Record and procedural corrections.** The original JA is only 21 printed pages and includes the suppression ruling, complete D.C. Circuit opinion, docket chronology and January 5, 1996 grant. `whren_05._Joint_Appendix_djvu.txt`, pp.4–5, records the trial judge's acknowledgment of discrepancies and acceptance of Officer Soto's testimony. Pages 8–10 describe the prolonged stop, no signal, unreasonable speed, approach to a vehicle already stopped at another intersection, order to park, and ensuing plain-view observation. The preserved traffic justification is based on credited testimony, not a finding that the officers had antecedent drug probable cause.

JA pp.17–18 sustains probable cause based on failure to give full attention, failure to signal, and unreasonable speed (18 D.C.M.R. §§2213.4, 2204.3, 2200.3). Petitioners challenge **the initial stop**, not the subsequent search (JA18). A merits handoff should not turn review into unrestricted reconsideration of the later drug search or every use of force. The means of effectuating the initial restraint remain part of petitioners' reasonableness argument; the existence of traffic probable cause does not itself authorize any duration, scope or manner of detention.

The neutral's statement that the D.C. Circuit “affirmed the convictions” needs the existing sentencing qualification: it rejected the Fourth Amendment claim but remanded by agreement for correction of the lesser-included §841 convictions and resentencing relative to §860. JA2, 18–19 and both merits briefs (`whren_06...`, p.11 n.14, lines 1394–1402; `whren_07...`, p.7 n.1, lines 560–569) confirm that the lesser convictions were vacated on remand; original prison terms were generally reimposed, Whren's fine was not, and the new sentencing appeals were held pending this case. Review does not undo or re-order that completed remand without a distinct basis.

**Strongest competing tests and exceptions.** Petitioners expressly advocate an **objective would-have test**, not invalidation whenever an officer also has a drug-investigation motive (`whren_06...`, pp.11–14, especially lines 1454–1488). Departmental practice and the danger/arbitrariness of the particular stop are their evidence of objective unreasonableness. They rely on General Order 303.1, which generally confines traffic enforcement to uniformed officers in marked cars or the designated unit, but contains an express exception for a violation **so grave as to pose an immediate threat to others' safety**. Their text also identifies written-warning requirements and a limited extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception (`whren_02...`, pp.3–4; `whren_06...`, pp.5–7). Do not reduce that regulation to an unconditional prohibition.

The government argues known objective probable cause suffices for an ordinary stop, independent of another investigative aim; varied departmental priorities do not rewrite constitutional authority. It distinguishes inventory/administrative searches in which standardized limits substitute for individualized suspicion and preserves separate limits on subsequent scope and duration. It also argues the regulation allocates duties rather than removes statutory police power. See `whren_07...`, pp.7–10, 15–17, 28 and 31–33. Petitioners dispute that the inventory, pretext and arbitrariness authorities are confined in that way. This is a real fork, not simply “objective facts versus subjective motive.”

Selective enforcement based on race or another constitutionally forbidden criterion remains a distinct constitutional concern. The briefs make that concern material, but Soto denied racial motivation and the trial court credited his testimony; no finding that these stops were racially selected is newly established here. The government's equal-protection alternative argument supplies neither automatic proof nor a predetermined remedy. Actual Armstrong governs its own **federal charging/discovery** setting; it must not silently be extended to every police-stop claim.

The original petition's conflict description includes the Tenth Circuit, but petitioners' merits brief pp.10–11 updates it: Botero-Ospina, 71 F.3d783 (1995) (en banc), moved the Tenth Circuit to the could-have side; Ninth/Eleventh decisions remain the stated contrary line. The neutral's “no material conflict identified” is therefore underinclusive.

**Supported paths and scope.** The Court can determine whether probable cause for these observed violations suffices despite the asserted pretext, or whether the proposed objective reasonableness restriction calls for relief or a remand under the accepted record. Any ruling adopting a broader test must say whether credited findings decide its application or further lawful factfinding is required. No remedy is supported merely by hypothesizing that all traffic stops are discriminatory; no universal permission for unusually intrusive seizures follows from sustaining an ordinary probable-cause stop.

**Date-eligible Justice-specific authority.** Official sources: `scott_usreports.txt`, 436 U.S.128, Rehnquist's opinion joined by Stevens, at 136–139 on objective assessment; `prouse_usreports.txt`, 440 U.S.648, White's opinion joined by Stevens, at 653–663 on suspicionless discretionary vehicle stops and permissible constrained alternatives. `bertine_usreports.txt`, 479 U.S.367: Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia join the Court; O'Connor also joins Blackmun's insistence on standardized opening-of-container procedures. `wells_usreports.txt`, 495 U.S.1: O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy join the Court; Stevens concurs in the judgment and objects to unnecessary permission for discretionary container opening. These inventory cases have conditions that must be stated, not transplanted wholesale to a probable-cause stop.

The actual Wilson v. Arkansas opening-slice entry gives Thomas's nine-Justice announcement/reasonableness holding, including supported danger/futility/escape/destruction qualifications and unresolved remedial/quantum questions. It does not decide pretext. Ornelas is **not yet effective for this preparation**; the parent must supply its completed, date-effective rule, if any, in the June 10 refresh. No historical Ornelas outcome is imported.

## Degen — inherent power and genuinely distinct alternatives

**Identity and record.** `degen_02._Petition_for_Writ_of_Certiorari_djvu.txt`, pp.i, 1 and App.1a–39a, supplies the actual question and judgments: Ninth Circuit, 47 F.3d1511 (February 10, 1995, amended May 5); District of Nevada, 755 F.Supp.308. The question concerns inherent/supervisory power to deny any defense to this property owner because he has not returned to face a separate criminal indictment. It does not present a statutory authorization enacted after this event.

Degen is a dual United States/Swiss citizen, left before indictment, and disputes intent to evade prosecution at departure; the government and lower court rely on later knowing refusal to return. The Swiss treaty does not obligate extradition of a party's own nationals. The civil complaint invokes §881(a)(6)/(7) against real and personal property in California, Nevada and Hawaii, alleged to be proceeds or facilitating property; the court has control over the res. Brian timely filed verified claims/answers denying the substantive forfeiture allegations and asserting defenses, including limitations/retroactivity/probable cause. Neither indictment nor disentitlement establishes the merits of those defenses or guilt. Sources: petition App.1a–8a; `degen_06...`, pp.2–5, 40–43; `degen_07...`, pp.2–5, 43–46; JA29–41, 53–87.

**Separate wife judgment and preservation.** Brian's claim was struck in December 1990. Karyn's claims initially survived; she later failed to oppose a supported renewed summary-judgment motion despite extensions/reopened discovery, and her judgment was separately affirmed. She did not seek this review. Do not describe her default as Brian's litigation default or reopen her final claim automatically. The Ninth Circuit also identified the trial court's mistaken view that disentitlement was mandatory rather than discretionary, but held the corresponding claim waived because Brian did not argue it on appeal. These are express lower-court and government positions, not a reason to abandon review of the preserved power question. See petition App.7a–14a; government's brief pp.5–9 and n.4.

**Swiss custody/prosecution is a disputed alternative with admitted facts and contested consequences.** Swiss authorities arrested Brian in November 1992. The Ninth Circuit refused record supplementation with DOJ letters and rejected an inadequately developed claim that this ended fugitivity. In this Court the government acknowledges both authenticity and its request that Switzerland prosecute substantially the same conduct, correcting misleading statements below (`degen_07...`, pp.44–47 and nn.25–26; `degen_06...`, pp.42–49 and App.1a–4a). Government arguments: issue not properly presented in petition/lower court, no motion for reconsideration, no good-faith return attempt, and no causal connection between its misstatements and the procedural default. Petitioner's reply, pp.17–20, answers that misinformation caused the record deficiency, the admitted facts permit resolution, and unclean hands is fairly included and became clear upon the opposition concession. Preserve those disputes; neither invent a valid waiver determination by this Court nor certify a wholly voluntary present ability to return from Swiss custody.

**Competing power/due-process arguments.** The government invokes closely related criminal/civil litigation, court dignity, inability reliably to supervise an overseas claimant, and potential use of civil discovery to bypass criminal limits. It acknowledges control over the res and ability to enforce the ultimate property judgment, distinguishing record-development/enforcement-of-discovery concerns (`degen_07...`, pp.16–18). It argues a stay pending an indefinitely postponed criminal case is practically inadequate (pp.22–24). Petitioner invokes Hovey/McVeigh/Societe, the distinction between refusing affirmative appellate assistance and extinguishing a property defense, lack of demonstrated abuse in this civil action, and ordinary sanctions rather than conclusory punishment for other-case conduct. The source record supports competing arguments, not a finding that every discovery threat already occurred.

Official 1994 Code sources were recovered as whole-title PDFs after individual-granule URLs returned error HTML. `21usc881_1994.txt` is a verbatim extraction from `USCODE1994_title21.pdf`: subsections (a)(6)/(7) include their innocent-owner qualifications; subsection (d) incorporates applicable, consistent customs procedure; subsection (i) requires a **United States motion and good cause** to stay a related civil forfeiture following the prescribed indictment/information. `19usc1615_1994.txt`, extracted from `USCODE1994_title19.pdf`, states the claimant burden with the antecedent requirement that government probable cause first be shown. That older regime must not be confused with subsequent forfeiture legislation. A stay, protective order or Rule 37 response requires its own actual predicate and scope; a hypothetical sanction is not an already existing failure to obey an order.

**Supported paths and remedy.** The preserved inherent-power question permits assessment whether this outright defense bar is justified, or whether denial/remand should rest on a narrower power limitation while reserving constitutional or fugitive-definition questions. If a lawful disentitlement theory remains, actual necessity, relation to the proceeding and preserved disputes matter; present knowledge of overseas residence alone does not prove a discovery violation. Constitutional due process, definition/status of a fugitive, Swiss developments and unclean hands are distinct alternatives with the preservation limitations above. A successful power challenge would permit lawful consideration of Brian's defenses, not determine ownership, discharge the criminal indictment, reopen Karyn's claim, or guarantee damages/return of all property. The lower court's Good-related treatment also does not convert Good into an automatic final forfeiture dismissal or compensation remedy.

**Date-eligible Justice-specific authority.** Actual Ortega-Rodriguez: Stevens's six-Justice appellate-connection rule includes Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; it retains sanctions for actual disruption and does not establish a universal least-restrictive-sanction rule. Actual Good: Kennedy's pre-seizure opinion includes Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg and demands justified process exceptions, with the title/compensation limits retained. Actual Kokkonen: Scalia's unanimous opinion limits ancillary/inherent jurisdiction to its proper basis; it is not a no-inherent-power rule. Actual Carlisle: Scalia, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the seven-Justice rule against using general authority to evade specific restrictions; Stevens and Kennedy dissent on a distinct retained pre-sentence power and expressly do not defend unrestricted equity. Sources are the opening slice and `entering-law/PUBLIC_Carlisle_v_United_States_merits_1996-04-29.md`/authorized neutral projection.

Pre-divergence `chambers_usreports.txt`, 501 U.S.32, pp.43–51, 58–60 and 60–76: Stevens and O'Connor join the majority's restrained inherent litigation-sanction authority; Scalia's dissent preserves powers necessary to protect proceedings and disagrees with sanctioning unrelated primary breach; Kennedy's dissent, joined by Souter, insists on necessity and use of applicable textual mechanisms. These are distinct positions, not proof that any Justice must accept or reject this particular civil forfeiture defense bar. `hovey_usreports.txt`, `hammond_usreports.txt`, and `societe_usreports.txt` provide the distinction between punishment by denying a hearing and justified litigation-related sanctions/inferences. No unverified present-case opinion or argument exchange is used.

## Exxon — causation and the independent warranty question

**Actual questions and scope.** The petition and merits brief both state two questions: tort recovery after Reliable Transfer when defendants' assumed breaches are causes in fact and the captain is grossly negligent; and warranty recovery after Italia Societa on comparable assumptions (`exxon_02...`, p.i; `exxon_05...`, p.i). `exxon_pre_june_docket.txt` contains the actual Archive docket OCR, ends at March 19 argument, and records an unqualified petition grant; it contains no separate grant of bifurcation. The particular bifurcation challenge remains outside the accepted submitted scope. The primary review authority is the actual questions plus 1995 Supreme Court Rules 14.1(a) and 24.1(a), available in `sources/chunk4_a/SCOTUS_Rules1995_515US1195_1254.txt`, pp.1208–1209 and 1226–1227, rather than a current-case historical merits opinion.

Preserve the parties' scope dispute honestly: HIRI says bifurcation/due process are not fairly included (`exxon_08...`, p.8); Exxon answers that exclusion of pre-breakout proof is part of the causation error, not solely an appeal of the bifurcation order (`exxon_09...`, pp.15–17). Rule 24's option to consider plain error is permissive, not a new grant or routine basis to reopen the excluded issue. The factual/legal adequacy of deciding the submitted causation and warranty questions remains distinguishable from a free-standing challenge to trial management.

**Record and findings.** The JA contains the pleadings, bifurcation orders, contractual rulings, bench findings and Ninth Circuit opinion. Findings at JA143–160, 167–176 and appellate review at JA217–233 support the neutral's safe-position/subsequent-navigation account. The breakout began in severe conditions; a hose remained connected and posed concern, but the trial court found that by 1830 the ship had reached a safe position and could continue backing out. The master failed to plot fixes for an extended interval, failed to use available personnel, and commenced the final shoreward turn without knowing the ship's position. The court found this grossly/extraordinarily negligent, independent and unforeseeable conduct the sole proximate cause. Exxon contests the legal consequences and argues the grounding risk remained within the risk created by the mooring failures; its characterization is not a replacement for those findings. Both lower courts' treatment and the respondents' Rule 52 objection must be stated. HIRI asserts the limited factual challenge made below has been abandoned here (`exxon_08...`, pp.38–42).

The parties assumed/conceded collective cause-in-fact for the limited first phase; that does not establish which respondent individually breached which duty. JA73–74 and the merits briefs distinguish a possible later phase allocating responsibility for the breakout. The source record does not establish that all respondents admitted personal negligence or every asserted warranty. The lower judgment concerns **stranding-related damages** under Rule 54(b); claims concerning oil spilled at breakout remain to be tried (`exxon_08...`, p.8 n.12). Avoid a decree that disposes of every breakout claim.

**Distinct arguments and qualifications.** Exxon argues comparative fault eliminates an all-or-nothing superseding-cause bar; alternatively, even if the doctrine survives, foreseeable grounding within the created risk falls under Restatement §442B/Kinsman rather than an independent extraordinary cause. It separately invokes strict liability, express safe-berth and implied workmanlike-service warranties, Italia's allocation to the party able to prevent the defect, and mitigation as a damages reduction rather than elimination of contractual liability (`exxon_05...`, pp.19–22, 29–37). Respondents distinguish factual contribution from legal cause, normal intervening negligence from extraordinary independent superseding conduct, and legal-cause analysis from comparative allocation. Grossness alone should not silently replace every independence/foreseeability component.

The HIRI respondents independently invoke contract **avoidable consequences** and **foreseeability of damages**, saying the supported findings defeat stranding-related recovery even if duty/breach are assumed (`exxon_08...`, pp.32–38). These are distinct alternative grounds, not merely tort labels. Contract content also matters: the safe-berth warranty included the right to proceed to/depart and lie safely afloat; the district court held it could remain operative after breakout and later found no post-breakout breach. See JA110–113 and 162–166; `exxon_07...`, pp.3, 11 n.7. Do not announce that superseding tort cause necessarily defeats every possible express risk allocation, or that mere nonnegligence necessarily satisfies every warranty. The case can be addressed at the actual contract and damage scope.

**Supported paths and remedy.** Comparative allocation applies to legally contributing faults if the applicable doctrine and findings leave multiple legal causes. If superseding cause remains available and is validly established here, earlier factual contribution need not by itself require stranding recovery. The contract question must be addressed or left open with an explicit supported reason; tort analysis alone should not silently dispose of a differently allocated contractual risk. If further proceedings are required, distinguish corrected legal causation, any permissible reconsideration of facts, warranty scope, and allocation. No source supports an immediate Supreme Court percentage allocation or award.

**Date-eligible authority and individual continuity.** `reliable_transfer_usreports.txt`, 421 U.S.397, supplies the unanimous pre-divergence proportional-fault rule, including equal allocation when fault is equal or cannot fairly be measured. It is a damages-allocation authority whose relationship to superseding cause is now disputed. `italia_usreports.txt`, 376 U.S.315, concerns implied maritime workmanlike service and nonnegligently defective stevedore equipment, with its own causal/warranty setting; its deterrence explanation is not a universal contract-insurance rule.

The actual McDermott/Boca Grande rules in the opening slice are Stevens opinions joined by the then-full Court, including O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, on reliable proportionate settlement credit/contribution and settlement-risk limits. Actual Milwaukee is Stevens's eight-participant opinion joined by those same active Justices; it concerns interest on an otherwise compensable loss, not erasure of liability predicates. Actual Yamaha and Zicherman are in their bounded `entering-law/PUBLIC_...` files: O'Connor's opinions joined by the other non-Stone Justices respect the relevant statutory/remedial fields and reserve liability/causation. Yamaha's §1292(b) certified-order rule does not enlarge questions in a Supreme Court certiorari petition. Those authorities provide Justice-specific continuity, not automatic resolution of the submitted maritime-causation dispute.
