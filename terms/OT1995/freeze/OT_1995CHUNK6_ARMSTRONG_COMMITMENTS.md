## Posture and record controlling these commitments

Review lies under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) from the en banc Ninth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal following the Government's refusal to obey selective-prosecution discovery. The lower appeal was from dismissal under §3731, not an independently appealable discovery order. All nine event-date Justices participated at argument and remain seated; no nonparticipation is established. The commitments below concern only the eight Associates.

This is a pretrial discovery case. Neither court found racial discrimination on the merits, acquitted respondents or adjudicated guilt. State charges were never filed against these defendants. The challenge concerns selection for federal rather than state prosecution, not transfer following dismissal of an actual state prosecution. The Government attacks the discovery predicate, not independently the selected sanction's appropriateness if that predicate was valid. No authenticated explicit “with prejudice” term is supplied.

The material evidence remains differentiated:

- Beller identified **24 Black defendants** in cocaine-base matters handled by the federal public defender and **closed during 1991**, excluding out-of-district cases and specified early retained-counsel matters. This is not all federal crack cases, all cases filed in 1991 or the eligible charging population. It identifies neither that population's racial distribution nor similarly situated persons passed over.
- Reed's professional experience includes other-race state crack-sale defendants and repeat offenders. O'Connor relays a treatment professional's observations about users and dealers. Their different foundations and relevance remain distinct. Neither submission identifies the complete quantity, firearm, evidentiary and criminal-history circumstances of matched federal-selection comparators. Missing particulars do not prove that no comparator exists.
- On reconsideration, Scheper supplied specific reasons involving quantities, repeated transactions, multiple participants, joint federal investigation, firearms, recordings, threats and criminal histories, and denied racial selection. Investigators described the small subset referred federally and Black defendants prosecuted by the State. These are developed sworn proffers, not appellate findings of truth or neutrality. Non-Black federal defendants identified by the Government qualify a district-wide claim without disproving the precisely bounded closed-1991 sample.
- The judge expressly said she considered all parties' evidence. The parties dispute her reliance on the supplemental declarations. The commitments assess them rather than treating them as absent. The newspaper article was discounted. The Berk–Campbell study was outside the original district-court record and does not supply a missing denominator.

The adjudicated discovery order concerns cases with **both cocaine-base and federal firearm charges**, defendants' race, investigative agencies and federal-selection criteria over its stated period. The September 8 hearing specifies “1989 through 1992.” The subsequent crack-only exchange is ambiguous; neither disposition below depends on resolving it as an expansion. The published lower-court description controls the scope assessed here.

## Entering law and supported decisional alternatives

**Constitutional elements and discovery.** Wayte, 470 U.S. 598, 607–610 and n.5, preserves broad prosecutorial choice subject to discriminatory-effect and discriminatory-purpose requirements, but leaves discovery unresolved. McCleskey, 481 U.S. 279, 292–299, cautions against inferring purpose from an inadequately connected statistical pattern in its capital setting; it establishes no categorical exclusion of statistics or discovery-stage demand for completed merits proof. Yick Wo permits a sufficiently supported discriminatory-administration inference; it does not establish this sample's missing comparison facts.

Actual Wade, May 18, 1992, Part A permits constitutional review despite prosecutorial discretion. Part B requires a substantial threshold showing of improper motive for substantial-assistance discovery or a hearing; useful assistance plus generalized accusations does not suffice. Its discovery rule is binding at that sentencing stage and provides a reasoned analogy here, not a previously adjudicated charging-comparator rule. Actual Kimberlin, June 12, 1995, unanimously rejects categorical direct-evidence requirements while preserving constitutional elements, specific nonconclusory support and lawful discovery limits. It does not import ordinary civil discovery into criminal charging.

Actual Klinger, December 4, 1995, unanimously ties comparison to the challenged treatment and permits circumstantial purpose evidence without equating disparity with purpose. Its program-specific comparison illustrates why legitimate charging differences matter without demanding complete identity. J.E.B.'s staged jury-selection procedure keeps initial inquiry distinct from ultimate persuasion but is not automatically the procedure for prosecutor discovery. Cooper, April 16, 1996, addresses ultimate competence persuasion and supplies no discovery quantum here.

Actual Williams, May 4, 1992, preserves a bounded grand-jury disclosure duty and a distinct sufficient, nonexclusive supervisory-dismissal class with independent prejudice and a without-prejudice remedy. Neither component automatically governs this discovery sanction. Actual Carlisle, April 29, 1996, preserves rule-consistent supervision while forbidding an asserted general power to accomplish the same act specific rules foreclose. It supplies neither categorical charging immunity nor an independent discovery entitlement. Sheets, also April 29, permits circumstantial proof of its supported disclosure facts; its ordinary civil verdict review and confined confidentiality right establish no charging-discovery rule. AEDPA and PLRA, within the admitted provisions' conditions, govern no presented pretrial selective-prosecution request or this §3731 appeal.

**Rule 16 is a separate question.** The applicable (a)(1)(C) permits inspection/copying of enumerated materials in Government possession, custody or control that are material to preparation of the defense, intended for its trial case in chief, or obtained from or belonging to the defendant. These are alternative triggers; the two latter triggers are not established for the requested selection material. Paragraph (a)(2), except as provided in (a)(1)(A), (B), (D) and (E), excludes the specified internal attorney/agent investigation or prosecution documents and separately limits witness statements by §3500. Paragraph (a)(3) preserves Rules 6, 12(i), 26.2 and (a)(1)(A)'s grand-jury exceptions. Aggregate information, selection criteria and internal deliberative documents do not necessarily fall in the same category.

Paragraph (d)(1) permits protection on sufficient showing, including an ex parte written showing whose full text must be sealed and preserved if relief is granted. Paragraph (d)(2) authorizes discovery, continuance, exclusion and other just responses to noncompliance; it does not establish the original duty, and the actual sanction is not newly attributed to that paragraph. The relevant provisions were not created by the 1993 expert or 1994 organizational-statement amendments. No later restyling applies.

Martin invokes Rule 16 for a genuine selective-prosecution defense. The United States replies that materiality incorporates a supported preliminary showing and expressly disclaims inventing a fourth categorical exemption; it also identifies Martin's rule objection as newly raised. These commitments do not turn that presentation dispute into a factual waiver finding. They can resolve the existing threshold question without deciding categorically that “defense” excludes selective prosecution. Rule 17(c), Brady and compulsory process have independent conditions; no established alternative entitlement cures a deficient showing. Carlisle prevents using supervision to evade an applicable rule restriction, but does not decide that Rule 16 is such a restriction here.

**Path R — reverse.** Require a substantial preliminary showing consisting of specific, credible evidence tending to support discriminatory federal selection, including effect and purpose, while requiring less than a prima facie merits case. For the statistical/state-comparison claim here, require some reliable basis for inferring that different-race offenders relevantly similar for federal selection were passed over. No universally mandatory named, identical comparator or direct admission is imposed; suitably grounded statistics, circumstantial proof or direct evidence connected to the selection can establish the necessary inference. The closed-office sample and general state observations, assessed together and with the response, do not supply that connection here. General assumptions about who commits crimes cannot fill it. Requiring this predicate does not find the Government's declarations true or decide ultimate discrimination.

The strongest objection is that defendants seek the very information that would make comparison possible and presented more than a naked accusation. Path R answers that discovery may complete a supported inference but its availability cannot depend solely on the hope that discovery will supply the connection absent from the existing showing. Relative access informs permissible scope after a sufficient predicate; it does not itself establish race-based selection. No finding that particular state records were easily available or unconditionally subpoenaable is needed.

**Path A — affirm.** Require concrete facts supporting a reasonable inference of discriminatory effect and purpose, appreciably short of establishing the defense, and assess the cumulative evidence and Government response under legally bounded discretion. The complete racial concentration in the defined defender sample, actual professional state/federal observations and limited investigative purpose can be enough to test the asserted explanations through the ordered information. Government explanations are relevant but need not be accepted as conclusive before examination. The strongest objection is that the evidence concerns general crack offending rather than the federal armed-crack selection at issue. This path must treat that mismatch as a limitation on inference, not erase it or require prosecutors to disprove discrimination whenever a selected sample is uneven.

**Bounded remand alternative.** A genuinely different governing rule or legally necessary separation of the requests could require reassessment. Neither an operator's research gap nor a preference for criteria-only disclosure supplies that need. On the respective commitments below, the developed record permits resolving the challenged order; no Justice presently selects remand merely to obtain more evidence. A later proposed separation requires its own lawful basis and renewed compatibility review.

## Independent commitments

### Stevens

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Affirm the judgment sustaining discovery and the consequent dismissal sanction, on Path A. His Wayte majority join preserves constitutional constraints without having fixed the discovery standard. His dissent in McCleskey resists treating evidentiary difficulty as dispositive constitutional insulation. His actual Williams join recognizes confined judicial protection, and his Carlisle dissent distinguishes a genuine residual judicial function from circumvention of an express restriction. Those sources support allowing this supported, pretrial inquiry; they do not alone determine its threshold.

The decisive application is cumulative: the limited but uniformly Black sample and Reed's concrete professional state/federal contrast give the district judge a basis to inquire beyond allegations. The judge considered the developed response. Unproved differences in every comparator characteristic should not become a demand for the complete defense before obtaining selection information. Under Rule 16, information material to a genuine selective-prosecution defense is not categorically outside “defense”; the present inquiry remains subject to the enumerated exclusions and protection. The submitted order is sustained without a new entitlement to protected internal memoranda.

**Counterpath and barriers:** Wade's threshold and the firearm/quantity mismatch are substantial objections. Wade's assistance-only showing lacked the racial-pattern and state-practice material present here; the mismatch diminishes rather than necessarily destroys its preliminary significance. Stevens would not join an inference that every disparity proves discriminatory effect or purpose, the extra-record study, a presumption shifting ultimate persuasion, or unqualified access to charging files. He would refuse Path R's conclusion that this cumulative showing cannot support the existing inquiry; narrowing its phrasing alone does not remove that disagreement.

**Remedy:** Affirm the reviewed dismissal sanction without adding a prejudice characterization or merits discrimination finding. Do not grant a new unconditional opportunity to comply after the Government deliberately obtained review through refusal. A separately supported change to the order or sanction would require reconsidering this disposition.

### O'Connor

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R. Her Wayte and McCleskey majority joins support connecting the evidence to the actual discretionary decision. Her authorship of actual Klinger makes treatment-specific comparison, rather than whole-population generalization or complete identity, particularly decisive. Her Wade Part B join supports meaningful screening below completed proof; her Kimberlin join forecloses a direct-evidence-only rule.

The sample has a real but insufficiently connected numerator. Reed's state observations do not establish similarity to the federally selected combination of scale, firearm allegations and available evidence. The developed response exposes those relevant differences without requiring a finding that its assertions are true. Assuming Rule 16 covers the asserted defense, its materiality requirement does not dispense with this predicate.

**Counterpath and barriers:** Deference to the district judge and access disparity favor Path A, but cannot replace a legally adequate connection between the proffer and the challenged selection. Her Klinger rule bars treating every difference as automatically decisive; she would refuse a perfect-identity rule, categorical statistical exclusion, immunity from discovery or a conclusion that the Government proved neutrality. Cooper's different ultimate-burden problem does not eliminate preliminary screening.

**Remedy:** Reverse the affirmance; remand for removal of the invalid discovery order and dependent dismissal, followed by ordinary pretrial proceedings. No new trial, conviction or compelled new evidentiary submission.

### Scalia

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R. His McCleskey majority and Wade Part B joins support a concrete predicate before probing discretionary prosecution. His actual Carlisle opinion requires attention to the particular rule's authorization and limits; actual Kimberlin, which he joined, preserves circumstantial proof while allowing specificity requirements.

Rule 16 supplies no textual exemption from materiality for an asserted constitutional defense. Assume its availability and reject this showing rather than decide an unnecessary categorical construction not adopted below and disclaimed by the Government's reply. The evidence does not connect the selected sample to relevantly similar non-Black persons omitted from federal prosecution. Neither Rule 2 nor supervisory terminology independently supplies that missing entitlement.

**Counterpath and barriers:** Respondents' claim that discovery is needed to identify the comparison warrants a lower threshold than merits proof, not abolition of a threshold tied to the constitutional elements. His Carlisle reasoning cannot be converted into a holding that Rule 16 expressly prohibits all selective-prosecution discovery. He would refuse an unrestricted balancing power to order inquiry despite the missing predicate, but also a direct-admission condition inconsistent with Kimberlin.

**Remedy:** The order and refusal-based dismissal lose their predicate; reverse and remand for ordinary prosecution proceedings. No merits neutrality finding or general abolition of supervisory authority.

### Kennedy

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R. His Wade Part B and Kimberlin joins support both a substantial preliminary showing and legitimate circumstantial proof. His Klinger join requires comparison directed to the actual treatment. His authorship of Cooper separates a process-triggering showing from ultimate persuasion; it does not support importing competence's allocation into prosecutor discovery.

His Carlisle dissent recognizes a distinct retained judicial function only where, on his construction, no specific rule forecloses it. That position does not make every requested exercise of supervision justified. Here he would recognize judicial authority to protect against racial selection but find the existing comparison evidence inadequate, assuming Rule 16's availability rather than declaring the constitutional claim outside it.

**Counterpath and barriers:** The practical obstacle to proving selection before discovery favors Path A. Nevertheless, some supported relation to comparable federal-selection decisions remains necessary, and the sworn explanations identify material gaps not answered by broad state-practice observations. He would refuse blanket immunity, a requirement to prove ultimate discrimination first, or reliance on Carlisle as eliminating all residual power. He would also refuse converting his concern for effective protection into an automatic criteria-disclosure duty.

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand to remove the unsupported discovery order and dependent dismissal. Preserve ordinary lawful pretrial adjudication; direct no new investigation or automatic evidentiary reopening.

### Souter

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R. His authorship of actual Wade supplies the most direct separation between reviewable unconstitutional prosecutorial purpose and a substantial threshold for discovery. His Kimberlin and Klinger joins require a circumstantial-proof pathway and meaningful, issue-specific comparison. His Carlisle join preserves rule-consistent implementation.

These respondents offer more than Wade's generalized accusation, but “more” does not itself satisfy the necessary predicate. The closed-1991 sample reports who was prosecuted within one office's selected files; it does not reliably describe who faced equivalent federal selection but escaped it. The supplemental observations do not cure that gap, even before crediting the Government's truth claims. Rule 16 may be assumed applicable; materiality and the discovery threshold remain to be satisfied.

**Counterpath and barriers:** Access difficulty and professional observations make Path A substantial. His distinction is between completing an already grounded inference and discovering whether the missing selection contrast exists at all. He would not join language requiring a prima facie merits case, making named comparators universally indispensable, dismissing statistics categorically, or treating the Government's explanations as findings.

**Remedy:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the order and sanction; return for ordinary proceedings without that predicate. A future properly supported request retains its ordinary legal conditions, without an ordered new opportunity or merits ruling.

### Thomas

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R. His Wade Part B join supplies a threshold inquiry; his Kimberlin and Klinger joins preserve circumstantial proof and treatment-specific comparison. His actual Mezzanatto opinion uses rule text and established background principles at the particular waiver's scope, not an unlimited prosecutorial-regularity rule. His Carlisle join rejects a general-power substitute for unmet rule conditions.

Assuming Rule 16 reaches the proposed defense, the enumerated materiality trigger does real work. Neither the all-Black defender sample nor general other-race state prosecutions connects the comparison to the charged federal firearm/drug setting. His actual Williams join demonstrates that supervised prosecutorial conduct is not categorically immune, while leaving this independent evidentiary predicate necessary.

**Counterpath and barriers:** The pattern is concrete and discovery is preliminary, which favors Path A. But concreteness about the selected numerator does not establish relevance of the missing comparison. He would refuse both an inference of entitlement from disparity alone and a categorical direct-evidence rule incompatible with his Kimberlin join. No universal extension of Mezzanatto or transplantation of Williams's grand-jury remedy is permissible.

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand for removal of the order and dependent dismissal, without declaring discrimination disproved, ordering conviction or specifying dismissal prejudice.

### Ginsburg

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R with an express evidentiary-form reservation. Her actual Kimberlin opinion rejects categorical direct-proof restrictions but expressly preserves constitutional elements, nonconclusory support and lawful discovery limits. Her Klinger join likewise rejects overbroad comparator exclusions without making disparity sufficient. Her Carlisle join confirms that protective adjudication must use an authorized procedural vehicle.

The difficulty is the connection to federal selection, not that respondents' proof is circumstantial or lacks a prosecutor's admission. The defined defender sample is not a district-wide census, and the supplemental state material does not relate the relevant prosecution characteristics. The result can rest on the existing showing even assuming Rule 16 covers the defense; a categorical “not a defense” construction is unnecessary.

**Counterpath and barriers:** Path A properly emphasizes information imbalance and the danger of requiring inaccessible proof. She would permit proof through an adequately grounded comparison pattern and would not require complete identification or discovery-quality particulars before discovery. This record nevertheless lacks enough grounding for the actual selection inference. She would refuse language equating “substantial” with ultimate merits proof, requiring identical named offenders, categorically disregarding circumstantial evidence or calling the Government's assertions conclusive. Such qualifications are conditions of her reasoning join, not merely stylistic preferences.

**Remedy:** Reverse the present discovery/sanction judgment and remand for ordinary proceedings. No affirmative directive to reopen evidence or deny a distinct future supported request; no guaranteed criteria-only alternative.

### Breyer

**Disposition and sufficient ground:** Reverse on Path R, preserving proportionate discovery once a grounded predicate exists. His actual Kimberlin join permits circumstantial evidence with procedural screening; his Klinger join ties comparison to the operative treatment. His Cooper join attends to a burden's practical operation without prescribing a universally defendant-favorable allocation. His Carlisle join accepts the limits of the authorized vehicle rather than creating an equitable substitute.

A low-cost or staged request may lessen intrusion, but does not make this sample representative or connect general state crack cases to the materially different federal-selection circumstances. Assessing the entire submission, the inference remains too dependent on an unproved comparison population. Rule 16's materiality, exclusions and protective provisions should retain distinct work; the case can be resolved assuming the claimed defense falls within the Rule.

**Counterpath and barriers:** Path A's strongest point is that selecting criteria first may make protection effective without demanding inaccessible comparator detail. That consideration can govern the form of authorized discovery but does not independently establish its predicate. He would refuse a categorical prohibition of statistical proof, a complete-merits burden, invented compliance-cost findings, an assumption of easy alternative access or automatic disclosure merely because criteria are useful.

**Remedy:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the order and dependent sanction; remand for ordinary pretrial proceedings. Neither an automatic second chance for the existing request nor an unconditional bar to later supported discovery follows.
