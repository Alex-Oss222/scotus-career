## Two supported paths

**A — Affirm on the actual request's limited scope.** Statutory-minimum relief requires a Government motion authorizing that form of relief. A request expressly confined to the Guidelines does not activate it. No separate paper or particular citation is indispensable: the request's substance controls. Section994(n) directs Commission policy within section3553(e)'s statutory condition; section5K1.1's permission to depart from the Guidelines and its commentary do not silently remove that condition. The strongest counterargument is that the statute says only upon Government motion and the assistance is conceded, so requiring separate assent to the statutory consequence adds an unwritten veto over the degree of departure. The response is that Congress created distinct permissions and a motion must seek the relief for which it serves as trigger. Judicial control over the amount of an authorized statutory departure remains, but is not activated here.

**B — Reverse and remand for exercise of statutory sentencing authority.** A Government request affirmatively identifying the qualifying assistance satisfies Congress's initiation condition; section3553(e)'s second sentence and section994(n) then leave assistance-related extent to the court under Commission guidance. The same motion can activate the coordinated scheme despite its Guidelines label. The strongest objection is the actual letter's limitation, reinforced by the statutory/Guidelines distinction in Neal. This path distinguishes Neal: Congress there supplied a statutory quantity already authoritatively construed; here Congress expressly created assistance departure authority and did not prescribe separate prosecutorial control of each ceiling. The Commission is not overriding the statute on this construction. A remand permits but does not require a sentence below120months and fixes no amount.
