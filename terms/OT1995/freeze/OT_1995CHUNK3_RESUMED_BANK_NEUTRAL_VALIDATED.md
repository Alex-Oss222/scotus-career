## Corrected question, record and posture

The question is whether the Act permits a bank's federal-court action seeking to enforce interbank liability established by Regulation CC under §4010(f), given the customer-remedy exclusion in §4010(a) and the jurisdiction language in §4010(d). The supplied event caption, argument date, docket and granted-merits stage remain the authorized event premises. They were not researched through the matter's later Supreme Court opinion or docket treatment.

The full amended Seventh Circuit opinion, **First Illinois Bank & Trust v. Midwest Bank & Trust Co., 30 F.3d 64 (1994)**, establishes the following:

- The April 1992 amended complaint alleged that Midwest returned a **$64,294.27** check unpaid and negligently failed to tell First Illinois that the reason was insufficient funds. The asserted duties were **12 C.F.R. §§229.30(d) and 229.33(b)(8)**, with recovery sought under **§229.38(a)**. The material allegation is an omitted reason for nonpayment, not a finding that notice was late.
- The district court granted First Illinois summary judgment, denied Midwest summary judgment, and entered **$43,912.06**, the claimed loss. It found that returning the check without the insufficient-funds advice breached Regulation CC's standard of care. These are the district court's determinations, not determinations made by the Seventh Circuit or by this Court.
- The Seventh Circuit itself raised jurisdiction at argument, required memoranda, and held that federal jurisdiction was absent. Both parties conceded that they were depository institutions under the Act. The panel treated §4010(a)'s exclusion of another depository institution as limiting §4010(d). It vacated the district judgment and remanded with instructions to dismiss for want of jurisdiction. It did not resolve the appeal's underlying regulatory-liability merits.
- The panel suggested administrative resolution under §4009(c)(1), or perhaps state court. It adhered to its interpretation after the Board reported that there was no available administrative mechanism for resolving such disputes. The Board, New York Clearing House Association and Standard Bank and Trust Company supported rehearing. The panel denied rehearing; no active judge requested an en banc vote. These are lower-court proceedings and public positions, not any Supreme Court petition treatment.
- The lower caption is First Illinois. The authorized event caption is Bank One Chicago. No finding about a corporate succession mechanism or date is needed or supplied. No identity dispute is established.

## Statutory text and material qualifications

The following text is from the official **1994 United States Code**. Section 4010's source note identifies enactment on August 10, 1987 and effectiveness on September 1, 1988. Its separate liability, defense and forum provisions must remain separate.

### 12 U.S.C. §4010 — Civil liability

**(a) Civil liability**

> Except as otherwise provided in this section, any depository institution which fails to comply with any requirement imposed under this chapter or any regulation prescribed under this chapter with respect to any person other than another depository institution is liable to such person in an amount equal to the sum of—
>
> (1) any actual damage sustained by such person as a result of the failure;
>
> (2)(A) in the case of an individual action, such additional amount as the court may allow, except that the liability under this subparagraph shall not be less than $100 nor greater than $1,000; or
>
> (B) in the case of a class action, such amount as the court may allow, except that—
>
> (i) as to each member of the class, no minimum recovery shall be applicable; and
>
> (ii) the total recovery under this subparagraph in any class action or series of class actions arising out of the same failure to comply by the same depository institution shall not be more than the lesser of $500,000 or 1 percent of the net worth of the depository institution involved; and
>
> (3) in the case of any successful action to enforce the foregoing liability, the costs of the action, together with a reasonable attorney's fee as determined by the court.

**(b) Class action awards**

> In determining the amount of any award in any class action, the court shall consider, among other relevant factors—
>
> (1) the amount of any actual damages awarded;
>
> (2) the frequency and persistence of failures of compliance;
>
> (3) the resources of the depository institution;
>
> (4) the number of persons adversely affected; and
>
> (5) the extent to which the failure of compliance was intentional.

**(c) Bona fide errors**

> (1) General rule
>
> A depository institution may not be held liable in any action brought under this section for a violation of this chapter if the depository institution demonstrates by a preponderance of the evidence that the violation was not intentional and resulted from a bona fide error, notwithstanding the maintenance of procedures reasonably adapted to avoid any such error.
>
> (2) Examples
>
> Examples of a bona fide error include clerical, calculation, computer malfunction and programming, and printing errors, except that an error of legal judgment with respect to a depository institution's obligation under this chapter is not a bona fide error.

**(d) Jurisdiction**

> Any action under this section may be brought in any United States district court, or in any other court of competent jurisdiction, within one year after the date of the occurrence of the violation involved.

**(e) Reliance on Board rulings**

> No provision of this section imposing any liability shall apply to any act done or omitted in good faith in conformity with any rule, regulation, or interpretation thereof by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, notwithstanding the fact that after such act or omission has occurred, such rule, regulation, or interpretation is amended, rescinded, or determined by judicial or other authority to be invalid for any reason.

**(f) Authority to establish rules regarding losses and liability among depository institutions**

> The Board is authorized to impose on or allocate among depository institutions the risks of loss and liability in connection with any aspect of the payment system, including the receipt, payment, collection, or clearing of checks, and any related function of the payment system with respect to checks. Liability under this subsection shall not exceed the amount of the check giving rise to the loss or liability, and, where there is bad faith, other damages, if any, suffered as a proximate consequence of any act or omission giving rise to the loss or liability.

The subsection (a) individual and class additions, and costs and reasonable attorney fees for successfully enforcing its foregoing liability, are not automatically part of a subsection (f) action. Subsection (f)'s ordinary ceiling is the check amount; its additional-damage language requires bad faith and proximate consequence. Subsections (c) and (e) protect different conduct on different stated conditions. No source supplied here establishes a timely-filing controversy, an applicable defense, bad faith, or an entitlement to any new damages or fees in this case. Recognizing a forum does not decide any of those questions.

### 12 U.S.C. §4008 — regulatory authority

**Subsection (a)** requires the Board, after notice and opportunity to comment under 5 U.S.C. §553(c), to prescribe regulations to carry out the chapter, prevent its circumvention or evasion, and facilitate compliance.

**Subsection (c)** provides:

> (1) Responsibility for payment system
>
> In order to carry out the provisions of this chapter, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System shall have the responsibility to regulate—
>
> (A) any aspect of the payment system, including the receipt, payment, collection, or clearing of checks; and
>
> (B) any related function of the payment system with respect to checks.
>
> (2) Regulations
>
> The Board shall prescribe such regulations as it may determine to be appropriate to carry out its responsibility under paragraph (1).

Section 4008(b) says the Board **shall consider**, among other proposals, requiring specified processing improvements by regulation. Its subsection (b)(6) proposal concerns a payment determination within one business day for a check above a Board-prescribed minimum and direct notice to the receiving institution if it will not be paid. That proposal is not itself a finding that this bank violated a statutory one-day deadline, nor is it the omitted-reason allegation actually described below. No expired disaster exception in the Code's historical notes is used as law affecting this claim.

### 12 U.S.C. §4009 — administrative enforcement

The lower court specifically invoked subsection (c)(1):

> Except to the extent that enforcement of the requirements imposed under this chapter is specifically committed to some other Government agency under subsection (a) of this section, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System shall enforce such requirements.

Subsection (a) allocates compliance enforcement to the named banking regulators; subsection (c)(2) separately permits the Board, for its specified noncomplying institutions or persons, to prohibit regulated institutions and persons from transactions involving them. Neither the quoted residual-enforcement command nor the separately described prohibition is an express adjudicatory mechanism for awarding one bank its damages against another. The Board's reported lack of a mechanism is a source fact. Whether the statutory arrangement leaves judicial enforcement available remains a legal question. The lower court's possible state-court route is not a verified determination that a particular state cause of action exists.

### Surviving pre-divergence primary authorities

- **Touche Ross & Co. v. Redington, 442 U.S. 560, 568–579 (1979):** Congressional intent, beginning with enacted language and structure, governs whether a private remedy exists. A reporting requirement and general jurisdiction clause did not create the claimed substantive damages action. The jurisdiction provision itself imposed no liability; regulations and a remedial purpose could not supply authority missing from the statute. The decision preserved the received §10(b) action's distinct history. Stevens joined the Court's opinion. This is a constraint and possible distinction: the model must determine what legal work §4010(f)'s express loss-and-liability delegation does, rather than assuming §4010(d) alone creates a remedy.
- **Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11, 15–25 (1979):** Congressional intent can appear in statutory language or structure without an express private-action sentence. The Act's void-contract provision carried specific limited rescission and restitution relief, but the general anti-fraud provision did not authorize additional private damages. The precise textual consequence and remedial limit matter. The majority declined to infer that a federal right had been relegated exclusively to state courts without an indication of congressional intent. Stevens joined White's dissent favoring a broader damages remedy; that dissent does not displace the majority rule. The majority's footnote 4 also records the distinction between failure to state a claim and subject-matter jurisdiction under §1331. A negative private-remedy conclusion must therefore not be mechanically relabeled a lack of all federal jurisdiction.

## Neutral alternative paths and remedy boundaries

**Integrated subsection (d)/(f) route.** The words “any action under this section” can be read to include an action enforcing Board-established interbank liability under subsection (f). On that reading, subsection (a)'s exclusion still performs work by withholding its distinct customer remedy from banks; subsection (f) supplies the interbank liability regime and limits. The statutory text, not agency preference alone, must supply the authorization. The strongest counterargument is that subsection (f) delegates rulemaking and loss allocation without expressly saying a bank may sue, while subsection (a) contains an explicit interbank exclusion. The model must decide whether the combination of provisions answers that objection.

**Restricted private-remedy route.** The opposing reading treats subsection (d)'s actionable liability as tied to subsection (a) and regards subsection (f) as a regulatory allocation provision implemented through the administrative scheme or otherwise available state remedies. Its strongest support is the explicit interbank exclusion and the caution in Touche Ross against creating a private action from regulation or jurisdiction language alone. Its strongest counterarguments are subsection (d)'s reference to the entire section, subsection (f)'s express treatment of interbank liability in that same section, and the absence of an express administrative damages tribunal or exclusive state forum. Administrative convenience or a perceived remedial gap cannot by itself resolve those textual objections in either direction.

**Agency-construction alternatives.** A Justice may find the text sufficient to resolve the forum question without deciding the weight owed the Board's legal position, or may treat the Board's construction as permissible within an independently established delegation. Neither route may give the Board power to create federal jurisdiction Congress withheld. Neither requires a general holding about deference on every jurisdictional question.

**Disposition if the interbank route is recognized:** reverse the Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment and remand for its consideration of properly remaining challenges to the district judgment. This removes the forum barrier; it does not newly find negligence, causation, loss, bad faith, timeliness or a defense, and does not automatically reinstate a final damages award or command an injunction.

**Disposition if the asserted route is rejected:** the district judgment cannot be sustained on a liability route the Court holds unavailable. The exact decree still must match the ground actually adopted. Preserving the Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal requires a jurisdictional conclusion, not merely rejection of a cause of action. An alternative §1331 or failure-to-state-a-claim analysis needs its own date-eligible support and preserved-posture inquiry if it becomes outcome-determinative. Nothing here orders nonexistent administrative damages proceedings or establishes an unexamined state cause of action.

The prior neutral addendum's comparative statement ranking one reading above the other is not a commitment and is not adopted as a neutral factual premise. The independent modeling context must evaluate both readings on the actual statutory terms and entering law.
