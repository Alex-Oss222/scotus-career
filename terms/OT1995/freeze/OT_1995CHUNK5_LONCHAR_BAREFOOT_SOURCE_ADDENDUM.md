## The qualifications supplied by Barefoot

At 888–894, the Court requires a meaningful opportunity to address the merits and an appellate merits disposition after a certificate of probable cause issues. The specific stay formulation at 893–894 says that the court of appeals, where necessary to prevent execution from mooting the case, should grant a stay when a condemned prisoner obtains a certificate **on his initial habeas appeal**. With adequate notice and opportunity to address the merits, expedited consideration and simultaneous decision of the appeal and stay are permissible. Where time precludes a considered merits decision in that setting, the stay must be granted. The certificate requires a substantial showing of denial of a federal right, not proof of ultimate success; footnote 4 at 893 identifies reasonably debatable issues or questions deserving further consideration. Capital punishment's severity alone does not compel certification.

This is a protection of an identified review opportunity under specified conditions. It is not a holding that the mere filing of every first district-court petition creates an automatic execution stay. The validated Lonchar posture is a still-pending first personal federal petition whose district stay the court of appeals vacated on the State's motion. It is not an established final district denial followed by Lonchar's certified habeas appeal. No missing certificate, final denial, or completed appellate merits review may be invented to satisfy *Barefoot*'s precise trigger. Nor do unsuccessful next-friend proceedings, merely by existing, turn that first personal petition into the successive-petition setting addressed separately at 895.

At 895 the Court states that second and successive petitions “present a different issue” and that a stay should reflect “substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted.” *Delo v. Stokes*, 495 U.S. 320, 321–322 (1990), applies that requirement to an actual successive petition and an established abuse ground. Those authorities remain relevant within their scope; they do not by themselves prescribe the screening rule for Lonchar's first pending personal petition. Any use of their reasoning by analogy must identify the analogy rather than silently require Lonchar to overcome a successive-petition barrier that has not been established.

At 895–896 *Barefoot* separately describes stays pending certiorari after appellate denial of habeas relief: probable support for review, significant possibility of reversal, and likely irreparable harm. Those requirements address interim protection while seeking this Court's review. They are not the substantive test for deciding the legal merits of Lonchar's already-argued challenge to the stay-vacatur judgment. The same paragraph's considerable-weight language about appellate stay decisions must retain its certiorari-stage setting.

## Effect on the neutral alternatives

The first-petition statutory argument has a further source-supported distinction to address: **absence of automatic suspension upon filing does not establish freedom to defeat every legally required review opportunity by withholding interim protection**. Section 2251's “may” and its no-stay consequence, as accurately supplied in the statutory addendum, describe statutory authority and the effect of pendency alone. *Barefoot* demonstrates that governing review obligations can nevertheless require a stay in a qualifying procedural setting. Accordingly, the statutory text alone does not answer whether necessary protection follows from the ordinary first-petition process in Lonchar's different posture.

A party may draw on *Barefoot*'s protection of meaningful review to argue that execution cannot be allowed to substitute for the examination the first-petition statute and Rules require. That is an argument from *Barefoot*'s principle to the identified district-court process, **not a direct application of its initial-appeal/CPC holding**. It must still identify the required process, address Rule 4's lawful screening and dismissal authority, explain any necessary connection to the challenged stay vacatur, and confront the asserted independent equitable ground. It supplies no finding that any undisclosed claim is substantial, no right to an evidentiary hearing, and no ultimate habeas entitlement.

The competing equitable argument retains source support and must confront the same distinction. *Barefoot* at 887–888 describes federal habeas as secondary and limited after direct review, rejects indefinite delay as an entitlement, and permits expedition consistent with careful treatment of nonfrivolous claims. Its actual appellate holding accepts prompt procedures when meaningful consideration has occurred. Those passages support assessing lawful expedition and actual abuse; they do not themselves create an independent first-petition dismissal sanction or prove that the necessary examination occurred here. A reliance on public *Gomez* or *McFarland* law must preserve the scope and qualifications already stated in the validated packet, including the reasonable opportunity protected by the latter. Neither the word “equity” nor first-petition status supplies the missing analysis.

These qualifications preserve the packet's narrower alternative of resolving the legal standard and returning for a reasoned application to the actual petition and stay judgment. They neither require nor exclude that course. A merits dismissal, an execution-stay decision, and a determination of what statutory process remains due continue to be distinct questions. Any operative protection must rest on its actual legal purpose and support; this source addendum selects no protection, expiration, or timetable.
