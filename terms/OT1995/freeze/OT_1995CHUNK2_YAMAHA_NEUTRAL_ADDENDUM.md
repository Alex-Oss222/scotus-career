# Yamaha: actual interlocutory review channel

Neutral procedural supplement; no same-matter Supreme Court opinion, additional grant question, Stone position or historical lineup. Source: the authentic Third Circuit opinion, 40 F.3d 622, 624–626 (1994), [CAP judicial text](https://static.case.law/f3d/40/html/0622-01.html), cross-checked with [OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/40/f3d/622/calhoun-v-yamaha-motor-corp). Local complete text: `../sources/CHUNK2_control/Yamaha_lower.txt`.

The Eastern District of Pennsylvania granted Yamaha partial summary judgment against future earnings and punitive damages, but denied summary judgment against loss of society and support/services, reasoning that federal maritime law displaced state wrongful-death and survival remedies. Both parties sought certification; the district court certified whether a federal maritime cause of action permits loss of society, future earnings and punitive damages. Both sides petitioned under Appellate Rule 5(a); the Third Circuit granted both petitions and consolidated Nos.93-1736 and 93-1737 under §1292(b). Both parties also briefed whether federal maritime law displaced state remedies. The Third Circuit considered that anterior issue part of the appealed order and necessary to decide it; that jurisdictional explanation is a lower-court reason open for review, not already controlling Supreme Court doctrine.

The Third Circuit affirmed denial of Yamaha's partial-summary-judgment motion, reversed its grant, and remanded. It left the district court to determine Pennsylvania versus Puerto Rico law and the consequences of the applicable wrongful-death and survival laws. The complaint invoked diversity and admiralty and alleged negligence, strict liability and implied warranties; no final liability or damages award is under review.
