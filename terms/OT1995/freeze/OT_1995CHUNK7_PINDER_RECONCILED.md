## Corrections that govern the final commitments

**1. Replace the unqualified descriptions “categorical immunity” or “categorical exclusion” with the actual disputed application.** The en banc judgment granted Johnson immunity on this due-process claim. Wilkinson's opinion classified the alleged conduct as nonprotection and found insufficient notice, but expressly recognized a different affirmative-danger paradigm in which custody may not be dispositive, 54 F.3d at 1177, and separately discussed direct causation at 1179 n.*. It did not simply overlook the assurance/reliance theory; it rejected that theory's classification as affirmative governmental injury on this account.

The nine judges supporting judgment did not adopt one universal custody requirement. Wilkinson, Hall, Wilkins, Niemeyer and Williams joined the opinion fully. Widener excepted the three sentences recognizing the separate paradigm. Motz joined Parts I–IV, called immunity the sole holding and rejected Part V's broader policy dicta. Hamilton, joined by Luttig, concurred only in the judgment on notice. Russell's dissent, joined by Ervin, Murnaghan and Michael, took the opposite view of affirmative causation and preconduct notice; it expressly accepted DeShaney's rejection of knowledge-and-promise special relationships while retaining Jensen's distinct danger discussion.

Accordingly, the four vacaters disagree with **the en banc application of omission and notice principles to the alleged culpable displacement of private protection**. They do not vacate merely because the lower court supposedly required custody for every possible affirmative injury, failed to mention danger creation, or demanded an identical precedent. The lower majority itself recited Anderson's nonidentical-facts rule. Their disagreement is with how it applied the rule. The four affirmers adopt their own limited notice ground, not all of the en banc reasoning or Part V.

**2. Culpability must qualify the deprivation, not merely the voluntary act of speaking.** The independent phrase “potentially intentional or conscience-shocking” remains a favorable pleading possibility, expressly recognized but not decided in district footnote 8. It is not a finding of knowing falsity, desire to injure, favoritism or deliberate exposure. Intentionally giving an assurance is not, by itself, an intentional constitutional deprivation. Nor does moral outrage at the deaths establish culpability or historical notice.

Archie is particularly important: it separately rejected constitutional culpability on its facts and found no state creation of danger or hindrance of private aid. Its discussion distinguishes intentional deprivation and extreme indifference to a known mortal risk from poor judgment or tort negligence; its incremental-risk/private-aid language cannot be detached from those limits. Bowers likewise rejected liability for release of the dangerous person while recognizing the active-tortfeasor distinction. Neither case awarded relief for Pinder's precise mechanism. DeShaney preserves the significance of no state-created or increased vulnerability without announcing a complete affirmative-danger test. Jensen's knowledge/promise relationship branch cannot be restored after DeShaney.

The four vacaters conclude that the sufficiently culpable, causally effective form of the alleged displacement falls within a sufficiently particular preconduct deprivation principle. They do not hold that any nonnegligent reassurance, reliance or failure to warn does so. Their narrow notice conclusion must be stated now; a remand cannot discover a mental state and then manufacture earlier legal notice from that finding. Conversely, uncertain proof of the qualifying conduct does not authorize an adverse appellate factual finding. Lesser or materially different conduct retains its own constitutional and immunity analysis under existing procedure.

## Remedy and assembly boundaries

For the four affirmers, Johnson retains individual immunity on the reviewed due-process claim. Constitutional sufficiency of the distinct affirmative-danger theory remains open; no new merits or damages proceeding is ordered on that claim.

For the four vacaters, remand permits proper adjudication of the already asserted theory within P2, not amendment by judicial invention. It does not unconditionally restore every part of the district order or the vacated panel opinion. The district's Rule 12(b)(6) treatment and the appellate summary-judgment descriptions remain discrepant; resolving the separable legal question neither settles that discrepancy adversely to Pinder nor commands a new evidentiary process. Any inquiry into culpability, reliance or causation must follow the actual procedural posture and ordinary preservation, pleading and proof rules. No special hearing, unrestricted discovery or trial is mandatory.

District footnote 13 prevents a finding that greater charges would have ensured overnight detention. No commitment depends on that counterfactual, the disputed commissioner representation, the disputed probation account, knowing falsity or favoritism. A warning remains a suggested correction of allegedly induced vulnerability, not a freestanding federal warning entitlement. Equal-protection allegations do not establish the due-process mental state. Kimberlin permits appropriate circumstantial proof without supplying a missing substantive culpability standard.

Official-capacity/municipal liability, equal protection, §1985, state tort counts and an unpresented procedural claim remain outside the reviewed disposition. There is no compensation, formal mandate date or additional Supreme Court event. The two necessary merits-writing functions are P3's notice-only affirmance and P2/P4's contrary bounded vacatur; their shared threshold conclusions can be stated consistently without pretending that their sufficient grounds coincide.
