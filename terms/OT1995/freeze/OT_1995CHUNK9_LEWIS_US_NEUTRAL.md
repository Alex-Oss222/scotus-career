#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Aggregate statutory exposure and the jury right

* **Controlling Reasoning Below / Institutional Posture:** The Second Circuit treated Congress’s maximum for each count as decisive and rejected aggregation even though the counts permitted more than six months in total.
* **Petitioner or Applicant’s Position:** The same prosecution exposes one defendant to a year of imprisonment; splitting the conduct into petty counts does not remove the need for the jury’s protection.
* **Respondent or Competing Position:** Each offense remains legislatively classified as petty, and aggregation changes neither offense’s character. Contempt cases lack the statutory maximum that supplies the rule here.

##### Issue 2: Judicial sentencing cap and the judgment consequence

* **Controlling Reasoning Below / Institutional Posture:** The magistrate judge relied on a personal pretrial sentencing commitment. The Second Circuit affirmed on nonaggregation and did not need that commitment to sustain its judgment.
* **Petitioner or Applicant’s Position:** A judge cannot select the factfinder by promising not to use all the sentencing authority Congress supplied; the requested jury protection precedes conviction.
* **Respondent or Competing Position:** The advance cap made imprisonment above six months unavailable in practice and therefore kept this particular proceeding petty.

* **Alternative or Unreached Grounds and Limits:** The jury request was preserved. The formal convictions remain under review despite the absence of an actual custodial sentence. Aggregation and the effect of the judge’s cap are separately presented questions; an answer rejecting aggregation could leave the cap question unnecessary. No knowing jury waiver is established by proceeding to trial after the request was denied.
* **Federal Participation:** The federal party’s position is identified above where applicable. No separate Solicitor General or amicus contention is invented beyond the cited record.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Lawful Paths and Consequences:** Rejecting aggregation permits affirmance without the cap issue. Recognizing aggregate jury entitlement requires deciding whether this cap legally removed the exposure; if not, the convictions require a jury-trial remedy. #### Legal effect of the announced cap

The magistrate announced before trial that imprisonment would not exceed six months in any circumstances. That announcement is a record fact; whether it legally removed the aggregate authorized exposure is a legal question. The Second Circuit’s nonaggregation holding made its discussion of a cap unnecessary to the judgment. No separate enforceable waiver, sentencing order or statutory limitation is inferred merely from the word promise. The effect of an asserted binding cap must be determined from the actual announcement and its legal authority, rather than treating the adjective binding as an established fact. [65 F.3d 252](https://openjurist.org/65/f3d/252/united-states-v-lewis).

**Bagwell**

**Current rule:** A fixed, noncompensatory public fine for completed out-of-court disobedience is criminal when the contemnor has no effective opportunity to purge the imposed obligation through compliance. Announcing a schedule prospectively does not by itself make later irrevocable assessments civil. Compensation measured by actual loss and genuinely conditional coercion, including a daily fine that can be avoided through compliance, remain distinct; ability to comply must be real. Serious criminal contempt requires criminal procedure, including a jury, even if a judge found disobedience beyond a reasonable doubt. The need to enforce an injunction does not replace those protections.
