## Exact source and scope

The complete operative text of sections 504–506 and Act section 561 is preserved in [the exact enactment reading](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK10_TELECOM_504_506_561_EXACT_TEXT.md), copied from the retained [official enactment](../entering-law/CHUNK10_RESEARCH_A_SOURCES/DENVER_1996.html), [Public Law 104-104, 110 Stat.136–137, 142–143](https://www.congress.gov/104/plaws/publ104/PLAW-104publ104.htm). This admission is confined to these provisions; it does not purport to admit every unrelated amendment in the entire Act.

Section 504 adds Communications Act section 640, 47 U.S.C. §560. Upon a cable-service subscriber's request, a cable operator must, without charge, fully scramble or otherwise fully block the audio and video programming of each channel carrying such programming so that one not a subscriber does not receive it. Scrambling means rearranging the signal content so that the programming cannot be viewed or heard understandably. The enacted section contains no independent indecency or sexually-oriented-channel trigger. Its words must not be replaced with section 505's narrower program category. The source establishes the command, not compliance, technical feasibility in every household, a new judicial construction of subscription scope, or nationwide availability of a particular device.

Section 505 adds Communications Act section 641, 47 U.S.C. §561. A multichannel video programming distributor providing sexually explicit adult programming or other indecent programming on a channel primarily dedicated to sexually-oriented programming must fully scramble or otherwise fully block that channel's video and audio so a nonsubscriber to that channel or programming does not receive it. Until complying, the distributor must limit children's access by not providing that programming during hours the FCC determines a significant number of children are likely to view it. The section supplies the same understandable-viewing-or-hearing scrambling definition. Its amendment takes effect 30 days after February 8, 1996—March 9, 1996. This source supplies neither an FCC-selected hour nor a finding of compliance, signal leakage, effective enforcement, or a judicial order concerning that separate provision.

Section 506(a) inserts an exception into Communications Act section 611(e), 47 U.S.C. §531(e): a cable operator may refuse to transmit any public-access program, or portion, containing obscenity, indecency or nudity. Section 506(b) separately amends section 612(c)(2), 47 U.S.C. §532(c)(2), to permit refusal of any leased-access program, or portion, containing those categories. The two permissions affect distinct access categories. They do not command exclusion, adopt the 1992 section 10(a) written-policy formulation, or impose section 10(b)'s segregate-and-written-request regime on public-access programming. No separate delayed effective date is supplied for sections 504 and 506; their enacted changes date from February 8. No particular exercise, contractual consequence or constitutionality follows from admission.

Act section 561 is distinct from the codified 47 U.S.C. §561 created by Act section 505. It requires a district court of three judges convened under 28 U.S.C. §2284 for a civil action facially challenging Title V, an amendment made by that title, or a provision thereof. An interlocutory or final judgment, decree or order of that court holding such a provision unconstitutional is reviewable as of right by direct Supreme Court appeal filed within 20 days of entry. The enacted trigger is not every communication case, every as-applied challenge, or a judgment upholding a provision. This source creates no petition, appeal, order or date of entry in a particular case and does not reroute review of a challenge confined to a 1992 provision.

## Law After Decision

Section 504 requires free, complete audio and video scrambling or blocking upon a cable-service subscriber's request under its enacted subscription terms; it contains no independent indecency or sexually-oriented-channel condition. Section 505 separately requires full audio and video scrambling or blocking of sexually explicit adult or other indecent programming on channels primarily dedicated to sexually-oriented programming, and until compliance requires withholding that programming during FCC-determined hours when a significant number of children are likely to view it. That amendment takes effect March 9, 1996. Both sections define scrambling as rearranging the signal so its programming cannot be viewed or heard understandably. No actual hours, compliance or technical-feasibility finding is established.

Section 506(a) permits a cable operator to refuse public-access programs or portions containing obscenity, indecency or nudity; section 506(b) supplies the separate leased-access permission. They permit refusal rather than requiring it and remain distinct from the 1992 provisions. Sections 504 and 506 operate from enactment. Their constitutionality and any particular exercise are not adjudicated here.

Act section 561 requires a three-judge district court under 28 U.S.C. §2284 for a civil facial challenge to Title V, its amendments or provisions. An interlocutory or final judgment, decree or order holding a covered provision unconstitutional is directly appealable as of right to the Supreme Court within 20 days of entry. It does not prescribe that route for every communications dispute or an action confined to a 1992 provision.
