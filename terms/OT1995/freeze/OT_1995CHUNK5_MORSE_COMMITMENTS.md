## Common threshold and record conclusions

All eight reach the presented statutory issues through the direct appeal from the three-judge court's dismissal with prejudice of Counts III and IV and denial of statutory injunctive relief. Sections 5 and 10(c), the three-judge posture and the actual judgment furnish the review channel. This is not a certiorari grant whose scope can be reconstructed from excluded metadata. Miller's apportionment ruling supplies no universal direct-appeal rule or cause of action.

All eight reject dismissal of the entire appeal as moot merely because the June 1994 convention has ended. Morse paid $45, repaid the campaign reimbursement, and expressly seeks return of that identified payment from RPV or the Albemarle committee. His asserted statutory restitution theory is sufficiently substantial to require a decision about its legal foundation; the absence of an ultimate entitlement cannot be assumed to make the controversy disappear. The payment, asserted retention and requested return supply his personal injury, asserted causal link and potential redress. This conclusion neither awards restitution nor establishes which defendant must repay it. The coverage and private-action objections remain grounds for rejecting the statutory claim on their merits, as the affirmance commitments do, rather than reasons to omit the monetary controversy.

The recognized private §5 coverage action under Allen and the asserted limited §10 action must be considered with their own remedial authority. A request for return of the precise exaction alleged to have been imposed through an unprecleared voting prerequisite presents a substantial restoration theory; it is not equivalent to a demand for consequential or punitive damages. Franklin distinguishes relief within an existing action from creation of that action. Meghrig requires attention to the particular statute's limitations and forbids treating the word restitution as sufficient authority, but it does not hold every monetary restoration unavailable. The present record and judgment do not conclusively eliminate this pleaded form of relief before deciding the statutory questions. No general §5 damages holding is adopted here.

No Associate relies on an unqualified rule that every election dispute is capable of repetition yet evades review. RPV's objections concerning these plaintiffs' future participation, their delay, and the duration of litigation remain distinct from the continuing monetary dispute. The contrary evidence of recurring fees and election cycles is material but does not itself prove every individual's future exposure. The commitments need not resolve the full prospective standing or recurrence question to review the fee controversy; any still-requested prospective relief requires its own live basis on remand. No additional standing ruling for Bartholomew or Enderson is necessary to the presently shared statutory question. Enderson's local Hampton committee is not made a defendant by inference.

All eight keep the challenged fee change separate from a claimed change from primary to convention. The reply's asserted lower oral-argument proffer and complaint language are preserved as advocacy, not as an independently verified transcript. RPV disputes preservation and emphasizes the different Senate seats and unheld 1990 primary. No present commitment grants relief against a change of nomination method or finds that change established. The fee issue can be decided without resolving whether this additional claim was preserved or instead is a supporting argument. Lebron permits some included legal arguments despite disavowal; it does not authorize new factual findings or require consideration of every adjacent claim. ## Law entering the distinct issues

**Section 5.** The statute protects against administration of changed voting qualifications, prerequisites, standards, practices or procedures in a covered jurisdiction without the prescribed preclearance. Section 14(c)(1) includes action necessary to make a vote effective in primary, special or general elections, including votes concerning public or party office; its examples are nonexclusive, while its election connection still matters. Under 28 C.F.R. §51.7 a party change must affect voting, relate to a public electoral function, and occur under explicitly or implicitly granted covered-jurisdiction authority. Recruitment, campaigning and platform drafting are excluded examples; primary elections choosing nominees, delegates or party officials are covered examples. A party's label alone satisfies neither side of that boundary. Section 51.23(b)'s appropriate-party-official submission route answers the asserted absence of a submission mechanism without answering substantive coverage.

The operative Standard **“Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority”**, present operation effective Presley, January 27, 1992, requires a direct voting connection, recognizes nonexclusive electoral categories, and gives substantial consideration to the Attorney General within the statute. It does not make electoral effect sufficient to establish delegated party authority. The Standard **“The designated channel for preclearance review”**, effective Shaw, June 28, 1993, confines actual preclearance merits to the designated process; the local court may enforce the submission requirement without granting or denying preclearance. Actual Holder concerns §2 coverage, benchmarks, proof and remedy, not automatic §5 coverage of every party action.

Smith, Classic and Terry supply the surviving constitutional account of nominally private actors performing an electoral function. Sheffield and Allen supply statutory functional and coverage guidance. They must be applied to this nomination process, not treated as a finding that all party activity is governmental. Virginia's recognition of a qualifying party, statutory choice of nomination method, certification and preferential general-ballot access support the delegation argument; §24.2-508's preservation of inherent party functions, independent candidacy and the absence of a convention-loser bar support the response. No omitted Virginia-code provision is supplied by assumption.

**Association.** The operative Standard **“Private parade composition and compelled messages”**, effective Hurley, June 19, 1995, protects actual compelled expression, without a universal exemption for every activity of an expressive association. Eu, Tashjian and La Follette protect party control on their own terms. Requiring preclearance of this prerequisite is not automatically compelled adoption of a message; conversely, the administrative burden and interaction with convention procedures cannot be dismissed simply because nomination has a public consequence. Burdick's **“Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system”**, effective June 8, 1992, supplies a different actual-burden application of Anderson, not the merits disposition of this party-control dispute.

**Section 10.** Subsection (a)'s findings and declaration, subsection (b)'s authorization and direction to the Attorney General, and subsection (c)'s jurisdiction over “such actions” do different work. Section 10 contains no express private-plaintiff clause. Section 12(a) and (c) identify rights secured by §10; §12(d) separately supplies Attorney General enforcement; §12(f) refers to persons asserting statutory rights without exhaustion. Section 3's 1975 “Attorney General or an aggrieved person” language and §14(e)'s fee provision strengthen, but do not by themselves answer, the private-enforcement inference. Former §10(d) was repealed and supplies no current exception. A fees or jurisdiction provision is not treated as a freestanding action, and no unpresented §1983 route is substituted.

The operative Standard **“Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses”**, effective Suter, March 25, 1992, requires provision-specific congressional intent and keeps direct implication distinct from §1983. **“Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination”**, effective Franklin, February 26, 1992, addresses relief within an established action. **“Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud”**, effective actual Central Bank, April 19, 1994, preserves a confined received private action: Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg joined its rationale, while Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas rejected the added secondary liability. Neither historical Central Bank's different outcome nor a universal implication rule is imported. Musick, Peeler's contribution holding develops an incident of already shared liability; it does not by itself create a new defendant, claimant or statutory action.

**Intervening law.** All eight have joined Meghrig's full construction of RCRA's abatement remedy, including its rejection of general earlier-cost recovery even when a current danger remains and its two exact reserved later-cost categories. That statute-specific ruling requires scrutiny of the asserted $45 remedy but does not decide it. Varity's individual relief rests on an express residual equitable grant, and its adequacy rule is qualified rather than absolute. It supplies no implied §10 action; its existence-of-another-remedy reasoning likewise supplies no automatic Attorney-General exclusivity rule. Wisconsin confirms that personal injury and likely redress are distinct from ultimate merits and does not establish future recurrence here. Barnett requires the actual federal entitlement and unresolved conditions before relief, without a new party-delegation, voting-coverage, association, or private-action holding.

## Individual commitments and join limits

### Stevens

**Count III:** Reverse. A qualifying party's exercise of the state-recognized authority that places its selected candidate on the general-election ballot is part of the electoral process for this purpose. Requiring a payment to participate in that selection is directly related to voting under Allen and Presley. The State cannot avoid §5's voting protections simply by allowing that nominating step to be conducted through a convention instead of a primary. Section 51.7's public-function and delegated-authority conditions are both met at this limited functional level. The primary example is not an exclusive list.

His Presley dissent's functional treatment of electoral authority supports this application; his actual Holder joins support reading voting protection with attention to established statutory operation and reenactment. The official-nomination link, not a bare benefit or subsidy, supplies the necessary public relationship. Lebron's distinct governmental-corporation test is not substituted. He would join the common official-nomination holding, and also its explanation that both the actual-practice allegation and the fee's effect on eligibility for selected delegate participation defeat the lower court's categorical convention exemption. He would not turn the alleged practical primary into a final factual finding.

**Count IV:** Reverse. Read together, the individual voting-right declaration, §12's recognition of §10-secured rights, the Act's received dual-enforcement structure and Congress's 1975 recognition of aggrieved-person proceedings show authorization for this limited private statutory challenge. Allen is an important interpretive foundation, not an earlier holding already deciding §10. Actual Central Bank supports giving received statutory private enforcement and later congressional treatment real interpretive force without equating silence with legislation. Stevens's Suter position concerned an enacted §1983 route and is not treated as an earlier vote on direct §10 implication.

**Remedy / joins:** Reverse the two dismissal grounds and remand. No automatic ruling that the fee is a poll tax or substitute, no dollar award, and no substantive preclearance ruling. Hurley protects expressive selection, but submission of this monetary voting prerequisite does not compel adoption of a platform, candidate or unwanted expressive contingent. A broader assertion that all convention rules are covered is a join barrier, as is a universal private action under every VRA provision.

## Adversarial alternative-path checks

**1. §5 functional coverage versus statutory actor limits.** The strongest coverage path is not simply that nomination matters to elections: Virginia gives this qualifying party's selection a prescribed official ballot consequence, and the challenged payment controls participation in that selection. That supplies both a public electoral function and implicit public authority, while Allen and §14's inclusive terms resist a convention-label exemption. The strongest answer is that ballot recognition accommodates independently held associational freedom and demonstrated popular support; Virginia neither creates this party nor necessarily delegates the particular fee decision. The government therefore must establish more than political importance or a benefit.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find the necessary additional official-nomination relationship here, without extending it to ordinary endorsements or all internal affairs. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas find the supplied statutory relationship insufficient to establish governmental delegation. They do not deny that the fee affects the party's choice or that genuine delegated nomination can be covered. The disagreement is preserved at that operative level; Presley's direct-voting requirement is neither disregarded by the reversers nor treated as the affirmers' complete answer. The practical-primary and delegate-eligibility paths cannot independently bypass the delegated-authority issue. Williams's unverified complete rationale does not decide either side.

**2. Association and avoidance.** RPV's strongest objection is that selecting delegates and candidates is expressive association and preclearance may delay or constrain convention procedure. The strongest answer confines this case to a financial voting prerequisite within the official nomination function, leaving platform, recruitment, campaigns and ordinary debate outside the regulation. The five coverage reversers find no compelled message or demonstrated constitutional obstacle to that confined obligation. The three affirmers use the substantial association concern to support their statutory limit; they do not announce that even proved public electoral functions are immune. No side relies on an imagined federal veto of every convention discussion or treats every monetary participation condition as constitutionally benign.

**3. §10 private enforcement versus a distinct specified officer action.** The affirmative path relies on the statute's identified individual right, §12's explicit recognition of §10-secured rights, Allen's private-enforcement setting and the 1975 aggrieved-person provisions as evidence of congressional intent. Its strongest objection is that these materials recognize some existing private actions without naming §10, whose officer direction and “such actions” language are unusually specific. The negative path preserves those distinctions; its strongest objection is that the Act already pairs private rights with Attorney General enforcement, so an express public route is not necessarily exclusive.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find the combined textual and legislative context sufficient for this limited §10 action. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas require a more specific indication and affirm the dismissal. Souter's Suter join is directly confronted rather than omitted: he finds the specific right and statutory context that were inadequate there. O'Connor and Kennedy's willingness to recognize some existing-action incidents or express equitable relief does not become acceptance of this new statutory claim. Actual Central Bank strengthens the received-enforcement argument but does not itself create §10 liability; its dissenters remain free to distinguish that controlling holding. Neither Varity nor Meghrig supplies a universal affirmative or negative answer.

**4. No private action, substantive fee validity, and relief are different judgment paths.** A Justice who rejects the private §10 action may affirm Count IV without deciding whether the fee is a poll tax. A Justice who recognizes the action must still consider RPV's alternative non-poll-tax defense; the dismissal record does not establish the disputed functional and factual predicates needed to sustain the judgment on that ground. The latter four therefore remand rather than order repayment. The prospect that §5 relief may affect the necessity or timing of further §10 adjudication is preserved, not treated as an automatic jurisdictional bar or mandatory disposition before the lower court identifies surviving relief.

**5. Expired temporary relief versus a live statutory controversy.** RPV's expired-convention and future-participation arguments have force against particular prospective requests. They do not dispose of the identified payment still in controversy. The voters' answer likewise cannot convert that payment into an already established refund entitlement. The commitments decide the contested statutory predicates and leave source-supported restoration and any independent prospective basis to proper treatment. If a later development actually eliminates every live remedy, the lower courts must address jurisdiction and the appropriate judgment disposition; Bancorp does not authorize calling genuine mootness a merits affirmance. No such later development is found here.

## Remedy and proposed proposition compatibility

For Count III, the five reversing Associates can directly join this proposition: **a changed monetary prerequisite to participation in this qualifying party's state-recognized selection of its general-election ballot nominee is subject to §5; the convention form does not exempt that function, and the district court erred in dismissing it categorically.** This is confined to the function and statutory relationship identified above. No logical-subset or Marks aggregation is needed for those five Associates' shared proposition. The alternative practical-primary and delegate-election explanations must not be assigned five joins at a broader level without preserving O'Connor's and Breyer's limits.

The resulting remand would enforce the preclearance requirement only against changes from the legally operative baseline that require submission and have not been precleared. The district court must not treat the pleading's 1964 premise as a conclusive historical finding. Purpose-and-effect preclearance merits remain with the designated channel. Section 5 permits the statutory D.D.C. or Attorney General route, including the enacted sixty-day and good-cause provisions; local relief does not choose their merits. No fresh convention, election, candidate selection, platform order or immediate permanent invalidation of the fee follows. The now-expired temporary convention requests are not revived.

For Count IV, the four reversing Associates directly share recognition of a private action for the alleged §10 voting injury and rejection of dismissal solely because only the Attorney General may sue. They do not join a finding that this fee is a poll tax or substitute. The four affirming Associates share rejection of that asserted private action while preserving Allen's §5 route and the separate constitutional proceedings. No final Court majority on §10 is presumed before the Chief's authorized position enters assembly.

Counts I and II remain in their existing single-judge posture; Count V's reported voluntary dismissal is not undone. No damages, classwide refund, new defendant, attorney-fee amount, special mandate date, compulsory new evidentiary hearing or continuing Supreme Court supervision is added. Reversal of a dismissal does not itself resolve every preserved factual, statutory or remedial issue.
