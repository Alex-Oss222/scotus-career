## Review scope, lower judgment, and vehicle

The petitioner's merits question page, preserved in the filtered document labeled `13. Petitioners Brief`, presents three questions: whether §92 preempts a state prohibition on the federally authorized bank insurance activity; whether a state law prohibiting banks from selling insurance was enacted for the purpose of regulating the business of insurance; and whether §92 specifically relates to that business. These implement the runtime's submitted preemption/McCarran scope. No additional question, earlier Supreme Court action, or broader grant is inferred.

The Eleventh Circuit, 43 F.3d 631 (January 30, 1995), affirmed the Middle District of Florida's judgment following a bench trial denying permanent declaratory and injunctive relief. Its operative reasoning is that Florida §626.988 was enacted for the purpose of regulating the business of insurance and that §92 does not specifically relate to that business, so McCarran-Ferguson preserves Florida's restriction. Ordinary preemption is a separately contested possible ground for preserving the judgment; the opinion did not separately settle it against Barnett. The runtime's description of the lower holding as merely finding federal permission insufficient to displace state law is therefore incomplete.

The lower court expressly left two cross-appeal issues undecided: whether this bank's Belleview branch makes the association “located and doing business” in a qualifying place under §92, and whether a qualifying bank may sell insurance nationwide or only within its small locality. The district court had resolved these questions favorably to Barnett, and respondents challenged them. Their preservation prevents treating the runtime's “qualifying small-town location” as a conclusively established entitlement or converting a preemption ruling into unrestricted nationwide sales authority.

The complaint sought prospective relief against enforcement of allegedly preempted state law. The Eleventh Circuit sustained federal-question jurisdiction under §1331, Shaw v. Delta Air Lines, 463 U.S. 85, 96 n.14 (1983), and Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908), and appellate jurisdiction under §1291. Review here proceeds through §1254(1). The merits dispute is statutory; neither an independent damages claim nor an adjudicated entitlement against the State treasury is presented. The lower court reviewed statutory construction de novo and bench findings for clear error, observing that the parties did not allege error in the trial court's factfinding. The dispute over what those facts establish about statutory purpose remains a legal controversy, not permission to find new facts.

## Material record and disputes

Barnett Marion is a national bank subsidiary of Barnett Banks, Inc. Its principal office is in Ocala; it has a branch in Belleview, whose population was fewer than 5,000 under the last decennial census. It purchased Linda Clifford's insurance agency. Clifford, a Florida-licensed agent, became a bank employee operating inside the bank. On the purchase date Barnett sued for authority to market insurance to existing and potential customers regardless of their location. Four days later the Department issued an Immediate Final Order prohibiting the agency activity except credit life and credit disability sales.

The lower court accepted the State's account that the restriction protects prospective and present policyholders against coercion, concentration of resources, and pressures capable of undermining insurers' independent risk decisions and solvency. It relied on Florida decisions, statutory placement and purposes, and testimony by Department officials. Barnett disputes whether this establishes the relevant federal statutory purpose: it points to protection of independent agents against bank competition, the proposal's banking concerns, the breadth of the affiliation ban, other laws directly targeting coercion and insurer solvency, and the distinction between post hoc explanations and the law's purpose. These are competing legal and record arguments; neither a finding of bank misconduct nor a finding that the State's protective concerns are fabricated is supplied.

The proposed 1974 bill's preamble is legislative-history material. It is not itself the operative prohibition. Respondents contest the significance of the preamble's removal and insist that consumer protection can be effected by an exclusion. Barnett contests the use of rational-basis decisions upholding the law as conclusive proof of McCarran coverage. State-law validity and federal preemption are distinct inquiries.

## Enacted provisions and exact limits

Official 1994 **12 U.S.C. §92** provides:

> In addition to the powers now vested by law in national banking associations organized under the laws of the United States any such association located and doing business in any place the population of which does not exceed five thousand inhabitants, as shown by the last preceding decennial census, may, under such rules and regulations as may be prescribed by the Comptroller of the Currency, act as the agent for any fire, life, or other insurance company authorized by the authorities of the State in which said bank is located to do business in said State, by soliciting and selling insurance and collecting premiums on policies issued by such company; and may receive for services so rendered such fees or commissions as may be agreed upon between the said association and the insurance company for which it may act as agent: Provided, however, That no such bank shall in any case assume or guarantee the payment of any premium on insurance policies issued through its agency by its principal: And provided further, That the bank shall not guarantee the truth of any statement made by an assured in filing his application for insurance.

The federal numerical condition is **no more than 5,000**, under the last preceding **decennial** census; “under 5,000” is not an exact substitute. The statute authorizes agency activity and agreed fees or commissions, not insurance underwriting or a guarantee of insurer obligations. State authorization of the insurer and the Comptroller's rules remain express conditions. State authorization of the insurer is distinct from the disputed claim that the State may veto the national bank's agency authority. The earlier moratorium described in the Code notes ended March 1, 1988; it creates no current moratorium.

Official 1994 **15 U.S.C. §1012(a)** subjects the business of insurance and every person engaged in it to state laws relating to its regulation or taxation. Section **1012(b)** states:

> No Act of Congress shall be construed to invalidate, impair, or supersede any law enacted by any State for the purpose of regulating the business of insurance, or which imposes a fee or tax upon such business, unless such Act specifically relates to the business of insurance: Provided, That after June 30, 1948, the Act of July 2, 1890, as amended, known as the Sherman Act, and the Act of October 15, 1914, as amended, known as the Clayton Act, and the Act of September 26, 1914, known as the Federal Trade Commission Act, as amended, shall be applicable to the business of insurance to the extent that such business is not regulated by State Law.

The official text's June 30, 1948 date controls over the January 1, 1948 date printed in the merits statutory appendix. Section 1013(a)'s suspension of the listed antitrust laws, including Robinson-Patman, likewise ended June 30, 1948. Section **1013(b)** provides: “Nothing contained in this chapter shall render the said Sherman Act inapplicable to any agreement to boycott, coerce, or intimidate, or act of boycott, coercion, or intimidation.” That is a distinct Sherman Act qualification, not a general exception defeating every state law directed at coercion. No antitrust claim is adjudicated here.

The lower opinion and predecision statutory appendices reproduce the relevant **Florida §626.988(1)(a), (1)(b), and (2)**:

- “Financial institution” includes banks, bank holding companies, savings and loan associations, their identified holding/service companies, and subsidiaries, affiliates, and foundations. Its definition excludes an institution granted a Federal Reserve Board exemption under §4(d) of the Bank Holding Company Act; an institution that neither owns more than 10 percent of the capital stock nor exercises effective control of the specified bank, savings and loan, or chapter 494 entity licensed or authorized in Florida; and a bank that is **not a subsidiary or affiliate of a bank holding company** and is in a city with **less than 5,000** inhabitants under the last preceding census. Barnett's affiliation defeats the last exclusion. The other exclusions are not found applicable.
- “Insurance agency activities” means procuring applications for, soliciting, negotiating, selling, effectuating, or servicing insurance policies or contracts, **other than credit life and credit disability insurance**. These are exclusions from the defined activity; they are not the independent-bank exclusion.
- Section (2) states: “No insurance agent or solicitor licensed by the Department of Insurance under the provisions of this chapter who is associated with, under contract with, retained by, owned or controlled by, to any degree, directly or indirectly, or employed by, a financial institution shall engage in insurance agency activities as an employee, officer, director, agent, or associate of a financial institution agency.”

**State-text limit:** The cleared current-law appendix expressly prints only “pertinent part,” not all of §626.988. The separately reproduced 1974 committee substitute includes a financial-institution-agency definition, licensing enforcement, continuation of agencies existing April 2, 1974 under the same ownership and control with restricted successor licensing, an independent officer/director exception requiring freedom from financial-institution ownership/control and no participation in insurance earnings, and an exception for continuously engaged April 2, 1974 agents limited to the same license type and institution. Those are verified provisions of that historical proposal, **not independently verified complete operative 1993 statutory text**. They may not be silently represented as current qualifications or found to cover Barnett. A holding limited to the challenged affiliation prohibition need not decide any such unasserted exception. A decree addressing other subsections or a claim under one of those provisions would require the effective statute and relevant record.

## Strongest supported positions and lawful alternatives

**Barnett's ordinary-preemption position:** Section 92 expressly authorizes a defined national bank to sell insurance, subject to specified federal and insurer-authorization conditions. Florida forbids that very activity solely because of the bank affiliation. Franklin and de la Cuesta demonstrate that an authorized option can be protected against prohibition although the bank could physically comply with both laws by not selling. The branching cases are distinguishable because Congress there expressly incorporated state restrictions. The State's strongest response is that §92 only removed a federal corporate-capacity disability so small-town national banks could compete where state banks could sell; the text's reference to state-authorized insurers and the settled state-regulatory setting allegedly preserve evenhanded restrictions. That construction must be tested against the particular authorization, not accepted merely because permission is less than a command.

**Respondents' ordinary-preemption position:** The law treats similarly situated state and national banks alike and does not obstruct §92's claimed competitive-equality purpose. Federal permission can coexist with state prohibition in such settings as Pacific Gas & Electric and Exxon; insurance is traditionally state regulated. Respondents distinguish Franklin's essential deposit-taking activity and de la Cuesta's affirmative federal objective. Barnett answers that Congress's added source-of-revenue authority is concrete, that its grant contains no such parity condition, and that a nondiscriminatory ban can still defeat the granted authority. Neither “banking” nor “insurance” as a label resolves this conflict.

**First McCarran clause:** Barnett argues the affiliation ban regulates competition and banking, not performance or reliability of insurance contracts; a general anti-coercion law would address the claimed harm without excluding otherwise qualified agents. Respondents argue that the sale and licensing of insurance agents are integral to the protected policy relationship, including prospective purchasers; exclusions may regulate, and Fabe does not confine protection to narrow antitrust activity. The lower findings and arguments concerning purpose must be addressed if this ground is decided. A legislature's failure to choose a less restrictive policy is not by itself an enacted McCarran disqualification. Nor does calling the ban an insurance licensing provision automatically establish coverage.

**Specific federal relationship:** Barnett argues §92 specifically identifies fire, life, and other insurance agency sales, premiums, commissions, and insurance-related prohibitions. A statute can concern banking and specifically relate to insurance at once. The pre-1944 view of the Commerce Clause did not negate Congress's power to create and authorize national banks; McCarran's enacted exception is not limited to later statutes. Respondents insist that the authorization's purpose and effect concern bank income, not the transfer of insurance risks, and that Fabe's clear-statement language, “specifically,” and the phrase “Act of Congress” require a more express intrusion into insurance regulation. They distinguish John Hancock's express ERISA duties. This is a genuine statutory-construction dispute; neither a mere incidental mention of insurance nor a claim that the entire Federal Reserve Act must principally concern insurance can be assumed decisive.

**Narrower route:** The Court could assume, without deciding, that Florida regulates the business of insurance and decide whether the particular §92 authorization specifically relates to that business and actually conflicts with the ban. That route preserves the first-clause purpose dispute and ordinary licensing questions. Conversely, absence of ordinary conflict would sustain the judgment without resolving McCarran; McCarran preservation requires both the protected character of the state law and absence of a qualifying federal enactment. A finding that §92 qualifies removes the McCarran obstacle but does not dispense with ordinary conflict analysis.

Party briefs describe the Comptroller's support for Barnett and an approach recognizing that genuine education, experience, financial-responsibility rules and policyholder-protective exclusions can regulate insurance. Those descriptions do not establish a complete independent reading of the government brief or an agency rule resolving every state requirement. Existing public NationsBank law does not delegate the meaning of McCarran to the Comptroller.

## Remedy and limits

Barnett requests reversal and remand for final declaratory and injunctive relief on whether §92 preempts §626.988. Respondents request affirmance. A ruling for Barnett on the disputed preemption premise must identify its effect on the judgment and preserve the unresolved qualifying-location and customer-geography cross-appeals unless separately and lawfully decided. It cannot order unrestricted nationwide insurance activity simply from Belleview's population. A remand for those preserved matters remains available.

Any preemption holding must identify the challenged prohibition and the federal activity protected. It would not automatically invalidate all Florida insurance regulation, remove licensing and conduct rules compatible with federal authority, excuse §92's conditions, authorize underwriting, decide every bank's location, or establish damages. Barnett's reply expressly disavows wholesale displacement of evenhanded regulation and distinguishes the cited OCC letter concerning §24 banking functions from §92 insurance-agent licensing. The Court need not issue an advisory test for every degree of state interference to resolve a categorical affiliation ban.
