## Express reservations and historical qualifications

**Statutory and congressional authority.** Inherent powers can in many instances be controlled or overridden by statute or rule; recognizing inherent authority does not empower a court to disregard an applicable limit. Conversely, this ruling does not require fresh express enactment for every inherited judicial power. No then-applicable statute is found to authorize this categorical sanction, and no subsequent forfeiture-disentitlement statute enters the case. The decision does **not** decide Congress's constitutional power to authorize a different disentitlement rule, or whether enforcement of a rule supplied under proper authority would violate due process. The latter is the comparator's express reservation. Neither reservation recognizes an existing authorization or approves a hypothetical future rule.

**Constitutional concern versus holding.** *McVeigh*, *Hovey*, *Hammond*, *Societe* and actual *Good* identify the significance of an opportunity to defend and the distinction between punitive exclusion and a proper litigation sanction. The sufficient holding remains the scope of the inherent power claimed here. No independently sufficient constitutional invalidation of every disentitlement rule is adopted. *Good* remains binding within its real-property/pre-seizure scope; O'Connor and Thomas receive no invented join in its majority rationale, and its exigency inquiry does not become the rule for every defense sanction.

**Specific management limits.** P3's preserved options include Rule 26(c) protective orders and, where a supported problem warrants them, controlling the form of proof or, in an extreme case, permissible theories to prevent improper exploitation of the civil/criminal asymmetry. These are conditional case-management possibilities, not immediate orders to eliminate any identified defense. If absence produces noncompliance with a legitimate pleading, discovery or evidence order, ordinary sanctions—including dismissal when appropriate under Rules 37 or 41(b), or properly bounded inherent authority—remain possible. No noncompliance, sanction entitlement or preventive-management predicate is presumed. Details remain for the district court on the actual problem and applicable law. Some defenses may require little discovery; that observation establishes neither their merit nor an evidentiary finding. The existence of these tools supports the lack of justification for categorical exclusion without guaranteeing that every possible future problem has an easy remedy.

**Probable cause and secure jurisdiction.** Historical Degen recites that the Government had shown probable cause. That comparator recital is not inserted as a new finding in this source-qualified neutral record. The present decision neither newly finds probable cause nor declares that an otherwise established showing below is absent. Section 1615's Government probable-cause predicate and subsequent claimant burden keep their actual operation. The decisive supplied fact is control of the res and enforceability of the eventual judgment; resolution of the presented power issue does not depend on a new merits finding. This is a record-use boundary, not a contrary substantive forfeiture rule.

**Fugitive characterization and Swiss facts.** Even assuming the Government's characterization for this power inquiry, no holding determines that Brian is a fugitive in every disputed sense. His Swiss arrest, the United States' request for Swiss prosecution and the Government's admitted correction remain qualified as in the neutral. They do not establish present freedom to return, impossibility of return, the merits of unclean hands, or preservation of every alternative. The Ninth Circuit's adverse preservation treatment of the mandatory/discretionary objection is not reversed by relabeling the issue. Missing words on Government brief p.47 perform no decisional work. No new source fact or correction is required for the confined holding.
