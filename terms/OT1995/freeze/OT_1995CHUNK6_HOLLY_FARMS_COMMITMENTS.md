### H — Primary-agriculture harvesting objection

All eight decline to consider the new harvesting theory under §160(e). The documented omission before the Board, also identified in the appellate and petition presentation, is not an affirmative substantive concession. No extraordinary circumstance excusing it is supplied. The preserved secondary-agriculture objection is decided. No commitment holds that poultry catching can never be primary agriculture in another properly presented case. The strongest alternative—treating harvesting as a new label for the general agricultural objection—does not overcome the source-supported distinction: the earlier presentation invoked secondary agriculture, whereas the new route would avoid its actor/place and relationship conditions entirely.

### D — Live-haul drivers

All eight sustain coverage on the presented record. These drivers' transportation is substantially off-farm and is performed for Holly's separate processing operation. Ownership, reacquisition of possession and agricultural activities elsewhere in the company do not make that hauling work an act of a farmer. Bayside strongly supports the rejection of that attribution; its rule is applied to these drivers rather than extended to decide the catchers' on-farm claim. Beginning a haul at a farm does not make the off-farm delivery itself an on-farm practice. The statutory reference to delivery does not eliminate the gateway and farming-relationship conditions. No categorical rule for every driver, mixed-duty employee or processor is adopted.

The strongest contrary route is that Holly resumes its farmer role on collecting its birds and then delivers them to market. The independent growers actually performed the raising; removal for processing does not itself establish renewed husbandry. The supplied operations provide no separately sufficient raising function for these drivers. This conclusion does not depend on deciding that a processing plant can never be a market.

### C and F — Catchers and forklift operators

The two on-farm occupations share a material dispute, while retaining their actual functions. Five Associates sustain the Board's classification of each; three would exclude both as secondary agriculture. The difference concerns the relationship to the growers' farming, not whether either activity occurs on a farm.

## Individual commitments and alternative-path tests

### Stevens

**Commitment:** H: decline the new primary theory. D, C and F: sustain coverage and affirm the judgment within the granted classification issue. Join a permissible-construction holding based on both the statutory relationship requirement and the actual functional-integration finding.

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Bayside opinion recognizes that fact-specific agricultural classification can reasonably go one way even where another reading is plausible. His Health Care join and MCI dissent support considered functional treatment within the particular statutory delegation; Town & Country retains the express exclusion. Here the growers finish raising marketable birds, while a separately managed crew removes them on a processing schedule. The statutory on-farm gateway is satisfied for C and F, but the Board can reasonably find the separate relationship condition unsatisfied.

**Alternative and objection:** Cooping and loading are familiar preparation for market and can complete a farm operation; the on-farm language protects contractors as well as a farmer's own workforce. Stevens nevertheless accepts the agency's distinction here because the actual organization, timing and onward handling support processing-related removal, and the statutory examples remain conditional. The affirmance must explain this relationship rather than treat Bayside as controlling an on-farm claim it reserved.

**Joins and remedy:** Will join the common affirmance ground set out below, including category-specific explanation and §160(e). Will not join a rule excluding all processor-employed farm workers from agriculture, a holding that farm location never matters, or an invented agency rationale. No separate writing is necessary. No remand follows merely because another lawful interpretation exists.

### O'Connor

**Commitment:** H: decline the new primary theory. D: sustain coverage. C and F: reverse their inclusion because their on-farm catching, cooping and loading are secondary agriculture; vacate dependent enforcement of the unit-based bargaining order and remand for the consequences specified below.

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** Her Health Care dissent join insists that one statutory predicate not be narrowed to do work assigned to another. Her Town & Country and NationsBank joins recognize deference only inside the enacted boundary. Here the on-farm route independently reaches nonfarmer employees, and the actual preparation of these growers' mature birds for removal remains incident to their farming even though it also supplies Holly's plant. The same-farm relationship gives effect to “such”; a processing customer's schedule does not eliminate that relationship.

**Alternative and objection:** The Board has an actual integration ground and substantial operational evidence, not merely a firm-wide label. O'Connor accepts that those facts may distinguish independently organized processing from farm work, but finds them insufficient to turn these particular acts of poultry preparation into a separate operation under the statute. Deference cannot make the actor alternative effectively indispensable to a nonfarmer's on-farm crew. Her position does not make every on-farm activity agricultural or treat every regulatory example as unconditional.

**Joins and remedy:** Will join a partial-reversal opinion preserving both requirements, identifying why C and F satisfy them, and affirming D. Will not join a compulsory firm-wide exemption or an unpreserved primary-agriculture ruling. A remand confined to applying the classification and reconsidering affected unit/remedy consequences is appropriate; a remand leaving the C/F statutory answer entirely open does not adopt her conclusion. No MCI position is imputed to her.

### Scalia

**Commitment:** H: decline the new theory. D: sustain coverage. C and F: reverse inclusion under the preserved secondary branch, with consequential limited vacatur and remand.

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His MCI Court join and separate special-conditions ground enforce separate textual limitations; his Health Care dissent join rejects deletion of a statutory term through functional terminology. Town & Country and NationsBank prevent treating that approach as categorical hostility to agency interpretation. Catching, cooping and loading these birds on the farm prepare that farm's agricultural product for market; the text permits that conjunction even if the purchaser benefits and controls timing. An agency may distinguish a true separate operation, but cannot require the practice to serve only farming or require that an independent contractor be employed by the grower.

**Alternative and objection:** The affirmance accurately points to “such” and the conditional statutory examples. Scalia gives “such” a real limiting role by requiring the preparation to concern the farming operation on that farm, rather than unrelated products or the processor's other activities. On this supported record that condition is met. The actual integration evidence does not show slaughter, dressing or comparable processing at the farm and does not negate the preparation function. He does not invent a factual finding that the grower employs or directs the crew.

**Joins and remedy:** Will join the C/F direct statutory classification, separate D ruling and preservation disposition. Will not join an agency-best-reading requirement for every ambiguity, a rule erasing the relationship condition, or a primary-harvesting merits holding. The MCI concurrence supplies an individual text-sensitive position, not a controlling general test. The limited remedial position below is sufficient; no additional opinion is needed if the common partial-reversal writing preserves these terms.

### Kennedy

**Commitment:** H: decline the new theory. D, C and F: sustain coverage and affirm within the granted issue. Join the bounded permissibility ground, not a categorical rule that processing employment defeats agriculture.

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Health Care dissent insists that the Board respect what the enacted conditions actually say. His MCI joins likewise preserve statutory bounds, while his NationsBank and Town & Country joins accept a reasonable classification after the boundaries are independently identified. In this statute the relation to “such farming operations” is itself an express condition, not an agency-added exception. The Board can assess that relation through the actual separation between growers' raising and Holly's removal system. Its supported processing-integration ground gives legal work to that condition while leaving the independent on-farm gateway intact.

**Alternative and objection:** Farm preparation and processing supply can coexist; a rule requiring exclusive service to the grower would add words. Kennedy therefore rejects such a rule. He nevertheless accepts that the present removal operation can reasonably be classified as distinct: raising to marketable size is performed by independent growers, and the challenged crews are organized, timed and managed for the subsequent plant operation. This differs from excluding patient care from the ordinary employer-interest phrase in Health Care; the present statute expressly asks which farming operation the practice accompanies. The distinction is a case-specific inference from his public authorities, not a claim that he previously decided poultry status.

**Joins and remedy:** Will join an opinion that expressly preserves simultaneous purposes as possible, rejects employer identity as conclusive, addresses the on-farm work, and sustains this actual application as permissible. Will not join a rule that an independently employed crew can never serve a farm operation or that all activities after birds reach a particular age are processing. He needs no separate writing if those limits are in the shared opinion. A genuine statutory-gateway error would require remand, but the actual integration ground prevents treating the agency decision as resting exclusively on farmer status.

### Breyer

**Commitment:** H: decline the new theory. D, C and F: sustain coverage and affirm within the granted issue.
