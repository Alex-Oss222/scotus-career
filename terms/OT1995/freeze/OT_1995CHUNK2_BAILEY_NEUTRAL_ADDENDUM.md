# Bailey and Robinson: charged alternatives and instructions

Neutral positive-source verification; no Supreme Court disposition, lineup or Stone position. The Internet Archive collection's own docket search returned items `micro_IA40385013_0617` (94-7448) and `micro_IA40385013_0618` (94-7492). Petition, Joint Appendix and both merits briefs were downloaded as OCR text for each. No claim is made that an unexamined part lacks a fact. This supplement rests on the positively located trial instruction excerpts in the consolidated Joint Appendix.

[Joint Appendix](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385013_0617/micro_IA40385013_0617%2005.%20Joint%20Appendix.pdf), pp.15–16, reproduces Bailey's February 28, 1990 charge on count two: using or carrying a firearm during and in relation to the count-one drug offense. It requires the predicate drug crime and knowing use or carriage during and in relation to it, but permits that element to be found from possession and intended availability. It distinguishes mere presence and says display or discharge is not necessary. The original language, rather than this digest, controls exact quotation.

The same Appendix, pp.35–37, reproduces Robinson's October 31, 1991 count-five charge. It includes use or carriage of the .22 caliber Derringer during and in relation to the count-three/four possession offenses; it permits constructive possession and availability for protection or assistance without affirmative brandishing or firing. It also gives an alternative aiding-and-abetting charge by reference to the earlier instruction. The district court's December 18, 1991 Rule 29 decision at pp.38 onward identifies her renewed challenge to that conviction. These positive references establish that carriage was within both charged counts; they do not establish its legal definition, sufficiency, harmlessness, separate waiver, or entitlement to retrial.

The limited use question can therefore be decided while leaving an independently lawful carriage issue for appropriate consideration on remand, without ordering a carry conviction or automatically disturbing the distinct drug and felon-possession judgments. The neutral context must assess its own proper remedy from these facts and the approved question.
