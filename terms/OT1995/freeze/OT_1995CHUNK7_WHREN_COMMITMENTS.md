## Judgment and shared sufficient rationale

**Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the judgment under review insofar as it rejects the initial-stop Fourth Amendment suppression challenge, and remand; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would affirm on that issue.** Neither disposition repeats or undoes the completed vacatur of the lesser-included §841 convictions and resentencing concerning §860. The later sentencing appeals remain subject to their own lawful disposition. No new trial, acquittal, release or new sentencing proceeding is ordered, and no evidentiary hearing is ordered by this Court.

A, C, D and E are common rationale commitments of all eight, not merely agreement in a judgment; B divides them four to four and determines each Associate's disposition. None of the eight requires a categorical rule that every probable-cause seizure is reasonable for every purpose, that police policy can never supply relevant evidence, or that motives are irrelevant to every constitutional claim. The four supporting B would not join a motive-only test, an automatic policy-violation rule or open-ended discovery. Broader formulations require renewed consideration of the join limits below.

Scott's objective assessment supplies the principal legal approach. Prouse supplies the important qualification: discretionary vehicle restraint without an adequate individualized basis is constitutionally different from intervention founded on observed traffic violations. Robinson reinforces the distinction between an objectively justified intrusion and the officer's additional investigative interest, within its own lawful-arrest/search setting. Neither case itself eliminates execution limits.

Petitioners offer an objective would-have test, not a rule invalidating every mixed-motive stop. Their strongest argument concerns selection where ubiquitous minor offenses permit officers to choose nearly anyone. All eight treat that concern as substantial, and all eight reject making constitutional permission depend on customary enforcement propensity, which would substitute variable priorities for the established individualized justification. They divide on whether objective evidence that traffic authority served as an instrument for an otherwise unsupported investigation can rebut the presumption that probable cause creates. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer answer that it can, because such evidence shows misuse of authority rather than mere deviation from custom, and because the Government's justification is measured by an objectively reasonable traffic-enforcement decision rather than by custom. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas answer that the counterfactual, however framed, still requires identifying the relevant practice and deciding how departures, assignments and exceptional danger alter it, even though the observed conduct supplies probable cause.

Bertine and Wells do not already answer that question. Standardized inventory criteria and purpose-related limits perform legal work where the inventory justification, rather than individualized probable cause of the offense, supplies the authority, and an inventory conducted as an investigatory ruse cannot borrow that exception. The four supporting B draw from that anti-ruse principle, not from inventory standardization, the conclusion that objectively available traffic authority may not be used as an instrument for an unrelated investigation lacking its own grounds; the four rejecting B confine the principle to exceptions whose authority depends on purpose. All eight agree that inventory conditions are not transferred wholesale to this observed-violation stop and that Prouse constrains suspicionless discretion without making typical enforcement practice the measure of every individualized traffic seizure.

General Order 303.1 is therefore addressed without adjudicating compliance. Its ordinary assignment of traffic enforcement to uniformed officers in marked vehicles or the designated unit is qualified by violations so grave as to pose an immediate threat to others' safety; its warning provisions also have their actual qualifications. The order is not an unqualified prohibition. Neither departure from the ordinary assignment nor satisfaction of the safety exception is found here. The accepted unreasonable-speed observation does not automatically establish that exception. For the four supporting B, departure from the order is evidence bearing on instrumental use, not proof, and its unresolved application, with the surrounding circumstances, presents a substantial issue for the lower courts; for the four rejecting B, policy compliance is not a constitutional prerequisite and its unresolved application requires no remand.

Wilson remains fully effective. Lawful authority to act and constitutional execution are different questions. The accepted account describes officers approaching an already stopped vehicle, directing it to park and then observing drugs. The packet identifies no independently sufficient, preserved unusual-manner ground on which to invalidate that initial restraint. This conclusion does not turn an omitted duration or force detail into an affirmative factual finding. The later search remains outside the independently challenged question.

Ornelas does not itself settle pretext. Its historical-fact protection and genuine contextual due weight are real, while the ultimate constitutional judgment remains independent. The neutral already treats the credited traffic observations as supplying probable cause. Neither independent review nor police experience authorizes inventing another violation, antecedent drug probable cause, a safety-exception finding or a conflict requiring a hearing. Resolving the legal choice here under the controlling allocation supplies no reason to return the case merely to restate that allocation; the remand that the four supporting B would order applies the new standard, not the review allocation.

## Justice-specific commitments and join limits

### Stevens

**Result and sufficient ground:** Vacate and remand on A–E. His joins in Scott and Prouse support objective assessment while requiring a genuine individualized basis for a traffic restraint; the accepted violations supply that basis and make the stop presumptively reasonable. His Bertine join and Wells concurrence in the judgment, 495 U.S. 12–13, treat constraints on police discretion as part of reasonableness even within an established exception, and the anti-ruse principle those decisions share supports B's objective rebuttal. His Bertine join does not make inventory standardization a universal traffic-stop condition.

**Material competing position:** Affirmance on objective probable cause alone is administrable, and Prouse's missing individualized justification and Wells's inventory authority distinguish their operative restrictions. His Armstrong concurrence, joining the Court's Rule 16 reading, preserves the district judge's bounded discretion within the staged inquiry to call for a response to a disturbing selection pattern; that discretion supports a threshold-first inquiry, not a Fourth Amendment entitlement to suppression whenever an objectively justified stop departs from enforcement custom. He therefore rejects both petitioners' broader would-have condition and conclusive probable cause.

**Join barrier and remedy:** He would not join an assertion that probable cause resolves every execution question, a general approval of inventory ruses, or an assertion that equal protection supplies a proved and adequate remedy for these petitioners. His actual Wilson join supports the execution reservation. He would vacate and remand without finding a General Order violation, ordering suppression or directing a discretionary discrimination inquiry in a suppression review that does not present its necessary predicates. No separate writing is required if the opinion states B at this bounded scope.

### O'Connor

**Result and sufficient ground:** Vacate and remand on A–E. Her Bertine joins, including Blackmun's standardized-container concurrence, and her Wells Court-opinion join make objective constraints on police discretion part of an exception's reasonableness and forbid using objectively available authority as an investigatory ruse. They do not require petitioners' would-have condition after observed traffic probable cause, but they support B's narrower rebuttal on specific objective evidence of instrumental use. Wilson supplies the independent execution boundary.

**Material competing position:** The known offense provides the individualized basis missing from inventory and suspicionless-selection rationales, which is the strongest reason to treat probable cause as conclusive. She accepts that basis as a presumption and rejects petitioners' extension of practice evidence into a constitutional measure, while holding that objective evidence of instrumental use, judged on factors none of which is conclusive, can rebut it; motive, ordinary discretion or a policy violation alone cannot. Actual manner review remains available.

**Join barrier and remedy:** As author of Ornelas's controlling allocation, she requires independent ultimate constitutional review together with protected facts and genuine contextual due weight; the lower courts apply B to the credited record first. Her Armstrong writing, concurring in setting aside the order but dissenting from the Rule 16 reading and the staged remand, concerns judicially supervised discovery into federal charging; she supports B because its threshold uses evidence presented on the suppression motion and opens no discovery. No credibility reopening, policy-violation finding, suppression or automatic remedy is part of her vote.

### Ginsburg

**Result and sufficient ground:** Vacate and remand on A–E. **Material competing position:** Her Kimberlin opinion preserves competent circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive when motive is an element of the underlying claim, while requiring specific, nonconclusory support and lawful limits on discovery. ### Breyer

**Result and sufficient ground:** Vacate and remand on A–E. His actual Wilson and Ornelas joins support a practical objective rule with genuine execution limits and contextual consideration. A rebuttal confined to specific objective evidence, with a threshold that precedes further inquiry and a Government justification judged independently of custom, answers that practical objection; it is not a new free-floating balancing test.

**Material competing position:** Petitioners' focus on objectively demonstrable selection practice and routine minor violations deserves an answer beyond an objection to subjective motive. **Join barrier and remedy:** His Armstrong opinion reads Rule 16 to reach a properly presented constitutional defense capable of defeating the prosecution and confines discovery into charging selection to a threshold showing, focused disclosure and the merits. It supports neither automatic police-stop discovery nor a guaranteed discrimination remedy here, and he would not join transfer of its Rule 16 rationale into this distinct setting. He would vacate and remand without a traffic-policy hearing as of course, an empirical enforcement requirement or a sentencing consequence.

## Adversarial alternative-path assessment

### Path 1 — conclusive ordinary-stop probable cause with independent limits: selected by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas

**Strongest opposition:** Because minor traffic violations are pervasive, an ordinary probable-cause rule can leave selection heavily discretionary. Petitioners' objective would-have proposal seeks an administrable external limit using practice, assignments and safety, rather than invalidating every mixed motive. Prouse and the inventory cases show that objectively lawful-looking conduct can require limits on discretion. Wilson shows that authority alone is not the whole reasonableness inquiry.

**Resolution:** Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas accept those descriptions but distinguish the legal work performed by the proposed limits. Prouse addresses restraint without the individualized basis present here. Inventory standards define use of a purpose-limited exception; they are preserved in that field. Wilson preserves actual execution review, which the selected rule also preserves. For them none makes typical enforcement propensity, or evidence of instrumental use measured against it, an additional prerequisite to the ordinary seizure on these established violations. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer accept the same distinctions but find that they leave the anti-ruse principle unanswered when objective evidence shows traffic authority used as an instrument for an otherwise unsupported investigation.

**Remedy and coalition:** For the four selecting this path, the supplied probable-cause predicate and factual protections permit affirmance without a new safety-exception or motive finding, and each directly supports the same sufficient rule and remedy. The result does not depend on aggregating an equal-protection rationale with a Fourth Amendment rationale.

### Path 2 — presumption with objective rebuttal and Government justification: selected by Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Strongest affirmative case:** Objective circumstances, including enforcement norms, can show that a traffic infraction is being used as an otherwise arbitrary entry point into a criminal investigation. A presumption that yields only to specific objective evidence of that instrumental use, followed by a Government justification measured by an objectively reasonable traffic-enforcement decision rather than custom, extends the anti-ruse concerns of Prouse, Bertine and Wells while avoiding an inquiry that turns on an officer's denial of improper intent. Stevens's Wells position supplies the strongest individual reason for the extension; O'Connor's regulated-inventory joins give it a concrete analogue; Ginsburg's Kimberlin opinion supplies the threshold design; Breyer's Armstrong opinion shows how a threshold confines an inquiry into official selection.

**Strongest opposition:** The test must explain why this additional condition governs an observed-violation stop and how constitutional authority is distinguished from local assignments and priorities. A practice-based standard cannot treat every departure as unconstitutional, because the General Order is qualified and enforcement circumstances differ. Constitutional concern about arbitrary selection does not itself establish the test's operative trigger, content or remedial result.

**Justice-specific resolution:** Stevens retains Scott and Prouse's individualized-basis distinction and applies his Wells objection only through specific objective evidence of instrumental use. O'Connor confines standardized inventory conditions to their justification and accepts the rebuttal because motive, ordinary discretion or a policy violation alone is insufficient and no discovery is opened. Ginsburg's Kimberlin design answers the trigger and content objection with a specific, nonconclusory threshold. Breyer accepts the rebuttal because the threshold precedes any further inquiry and the Government's justification is judged independently of custom, not by a free-floating balance. Scalia and Kennedy preserve the authority distinction and independent execution limits and reject the path. Souter insists on addressing the selection concern but finds that evidence of instrumental use measured against enforcement practice still makes practice dispositive. Thomas's Wilson rationale concerns actual entry execution rather than this additional prerequisite. Their rejection rests on those legal distinctions, not merely a claim that petitioners are asking for a subjective test.

**Remedy:** The present packet does not establish whether the credited conduct satisfies the General Order's immediate-safety exception or whether the rebuttal threshold is met. The four selecting this path therefore support a bounded remand for the lower courts to apply it, with the Government able to establish independent lawful grounds or that an objectively reasonable traffic-enforcement decision would have produced materially the same stop. It does not justify assuming a policy violation, ordering automatic suppression, vacating every charge, reopening the later search, or repeating the completed sentencing vacatur. No such factual premise is silently supplied.

### Path 3 — distinct initial-manner ground: preserved in principle, unsupported as an alternative disposition here

**Strongest affirmative case:** Wilson defeats a categorical contention that probable cause alone settles every aspect of a seizure. A properly preserved initial-restraint objection based on supported unusual force, duration or manner would require a separate reasonableness assessment even if the traffic basis were sound.

**Strongest opposition and resolution:** The final neutral supplies no distinct, sufficiently established execution objection capable of changing this judgment. The officers' plainclothes assignment and investigative aim do not themselves establish unreasonable force, duration or manner. All eight preserve the legal possibility without inventing the decisive facts. Reliance on an exact disputed execution detail would require a bounded source extract before it could support a different commitment. This is not an automatic reopening of factfinding or an independently available favorable disposition on the present packet.

### Separate equal-protection route: reserved, not an alternative Fourth Amendment judgment

Purposeful racial enforcement remains constitutionally objectionable on its own terms. Soto denied racial motivation, and the district judge credited him; this handoff preserves that procedural status without making a new discrimination or neutrality finding. Kimberlin protects legally competent circumstantial proof where motive is an element. Armstrong remains an actual federal charging-discovery decision: Rule 16 reaches a properly presented constitutional defense capable of defeating the prosecution, a staged inquiry runs from a threshold showing through focused disclosure to the merits, its record justified a limited inquiry, though not the original order, and the remedy for proved discrimination is unresolved. Its conditions do not automatically govern police-stop litigation.

All eight refuse to treat availability of a distinct constitutional theory as either proof of its elements or proof that suppression, discovery, damages or another remedy will follow. No separate equal-protection claim is adjudicated here, no burden is invented, and no supposed adequacy of another remedy is necessary to the selected Fourth Amendment ground. Stevens's Armstrong discretion position and Breyer's Armstrong opinion remain accurately separate; neither is converted into a controlling Whren procedure, and B's threshold is a Fourth Amendment rebuttal, not discovery.
