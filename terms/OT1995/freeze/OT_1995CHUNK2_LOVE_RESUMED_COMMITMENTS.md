### Competing paths tested on the complete primary material

**Retention:** Cruikshank and Presser specifically withhold state application, not merely announce an abstract rule about other guarantees. Presser also protects the federal military resource independently of the Amendment. Andrews accepts federal nonapplication and construes a distinct Tennessee clause; its majority protects military-suitable weapons, sustains substantial restrictions and requires evidence to classify a revolver. Nunn and Chandler sustain concealed-carry restrictions. Section 14 operates in a bounded Reconstruction and racial-equality setting, and Howard's argument is most directly about privileges or immunities. Duncan, Malloy and Benton require a particular fundamental-right inquiry rather than automatic total incorporation. This is a substantial argument for preserving the specific federal precedent and distinguishing recognition of a personal historical interest from incorporation of the asserted acquisition claim.

**Incorporation and bounded remand:** The core historical interest is held by persons, with keeping and necessary acquisition distinct from formal military service. Miller's citizen-arms history and weapon-focused treatment do not establish a formal-enrollment threshold. Nunn and Chandler connect arms with personal as well as common defense; Andrews, despite its narrower weapon rule and state-law basis, directly recognizes an individual's keeping, purchase, ammunition, practice and repair. Congress's enacted §14 locates arms within citizens' liberty, security and civil protections, without an enrollment condition. Howard explicitly places arms in the personal-rights list and explains protection against state restraint. The variety of lawful regulation in these sources does not negate that core, any more than qualifications on other guarantees alone defeat their incorporation. Benton demonstrates that intervening selective-incorporation doctrine may justify overruling an earlier adverse state-application precedent after the right-specific inquiry. The constitutional basis is the received Due Process incorporation doctrine; Howard is historical evidence, not a judicial due-process holding or a substitute Privileges or Immunities route.

### Selected five-Justice ground and its exact scope

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would vacate the Second Amendment dismissal and remand on these jointly supportable propositions:

1. **Personal invocation:** The arms guarantee protects an individual's keeping and bearing of arms within its constitutional scope. Formal enrollment or present service in an organized militia is not a prerequisite to invoking it. Miller's militia-related treatment of the particular weapon remains relevant; it is not converted into a standing rule restricting the right to a state organization. The decision does not decide every weapon, purpose or carrying practice protected by the Amendment.
2. **State application:** The Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause makes that personal guarantee applicable to state action under the received selective-incorporation method. The same constitutional standards govern state and federal action; a reduced state version is not adopted. Cruikshank and Presser are overruled only insofar as they foreclose that state application. Their other actual holdings, including lawful regulation of unauthorized military organization in Presser, are not overruled by this proposition. The Court does not revive a universal direct application of the Bill of Rights to States or decide a new Privileges or Immunities theory.
3. **Acquisition incident:** Acquisition necessary to keeping arms within the guarantee's protection cannot categorically be placed outside the right merely because the challenged act concerns permission to purchase rather than seizure of an existing weapon. This does not create a right to purchase every firearm, from every seller, without investigation or regulation. The scope of the protected arms and the constitutional validity of the actual restriction remain distinct from whether Maryland law authorized it.
4. **Bounded disposition:** Neither inherited nonincorporation nor the absence of a pleaded formal militia affiliation sustains dismissal. The cause returns for assessment of the pleaded purchase restriction under the personal guarantee, with the actual weapon and claim within the constitutional scope, permissible regulation, personal responsibility, properly presented defenses and remedy all treated separately. The Court does not find that the alleged arrest-only policy necessarily violates the Second Amendment, find the respondents liable, award damages, restore an unpleaded procedural claim or direct an injunction or trial.

The five do not adopt a categorical rule that every handgun is protected or every licensing error is an infringement. Nor do they preserve the Fourth Circuit's demand that an individual explain how her own possession improves an organized militia as a threshold to invoking the right. Miller's actual weapon-and-record inquiry survives; the Court neither turns it into an exhaustive military-weapons-only rule nor discards its militia-related rationale. No modern scrutiny tier, closed list of exceptions, universal self-defense test or evidentiary burden is invented. Application of the received arms guarantee to this licensing record is left for proper adjudication rather than resolved through a description of Maryland's public-safety interest alone.

This is an actual five-Associate proposal, not a hypothetical successful-adoption scenario. Whether and how it becomes the Court's opinion depends on reconciliation and assembly, including Stone's separate position. The five directly support the same stated incorporation, personal-invocation and acquisition propositions; their votes are not pooled from incompatible rationales through Marks.

## 4. Substantive due process and contractual liberty

### Competing lawful dispositions

**Affirmance formulation, supported by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Assume, without deciding, that Maryland's disapproval limits gave Love the asserted protected interest. Accept the allegations of deliberate, knowing and malicious use of the arrest-only policy. The complaint still identifies an unlawful way of administering a screening program, rather than an independently established substantive constitutional prohibition on the described economic transaction restriction. The stated conduct concerns unresolved arrest dispositions potentially bearing on statutory eligibility; that objective subject remains connected to the screening function even if the officials knowingly exceeded the actual authority Maryland granted. Intentional violation of that authority and the expense of correcting it do not alone establish the distinct constitutional arbitrariness required by Collins. Ordinary contract liberty does not transform compliance with every state licensing rule into a fundamental federal right.

This formulation does not find actual good faith, unavoidable time pressure, staffing necessity, actual disqualification or a valid conviction. It does not sustain dismissal because the State later corrected the denial or because economic loss is required for every constitutional claim. It does not treat all deliberate executive invasions of a state-created interest as immune from substantive review. Its sufficient ground is the constitutional insufficiency of this alleged eligibility-screening policy even on the assumed entitlement and culpability, not an absence of jurisdiction, absence of any state interest, hypothetical immunity or a categorical state-remedy bar.

**Vacatur/remand formulation, supported by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** A substantive violation, if otherwise established, is not erased because state judicial review later ends the restriction. The court of appeals also may not replace the alleged knowing, malicious affirmative policy with a finding that necessity compelled cautious corner cutting. Collins distinguishes a state interest from a federal wrong, but its actual protective-omission holding does not establish that this affirmative restriction is constitutionally insufficient after those errors are removed. The Court should vacate the due-process dismissal and require assessment of the actual claim, including the entitlement and distinct substantive-arbitrariness questions, under correct legal and pleading premises. Contractual liberty remains part of that inquiry without heightened protection merely because a contract is possible.

This formulation neither declares the complaint ultimately sufficient nor holds every intentional state-law violation actionable. It identifies legal and pleading errors material to the existing judgment and declines to decide the remaining constitutional-insufficiency question on a benign or necessary-policy inference. Neither automatic damages nor automatic discovery or trial follows. The lower court may resolve a properly presented remaining dispositive ground, including a properly raised personal immunity defense. The court need not adopt Gardner as a universal Supreme Court entitlement test.

**Strongest challenge to affirmance:** It risks replacing the complaint's alleged malice with an invented public-safety justification, or treating a general screening purpose as conclusive despite intentional use of an unauthorized barrier. **Answer within the four-Justice formulation:** The alleged policy's stated trigger itself concerns unresolved potentially disqualifying arrests; the conclusion is limited to the constitutional sufficiency of those concrete alleged acts, accepts their unlawfulness and culpability, and makes no factual finding about necessity or good motive. Those four would refuse a draft that relied on supposed unavoidable time pressure or later cure.

**Strongest challenge to remand:** Correction of an overbroad explanation does not require vacatur if the accepted allegations still lack a substantive federal wrong; Collins itself distinguishes deliberate state breaches from constitutional arbitrariness. **Answer within the four-Justice formulation:** That principle remains binding, but it does not itself supply the missing application to an alleged affirmative, knowingly unauthorized continuing barrier. On this pleading record, the disputed characterization does substantive work, and the Court should not choose the benign factual account to complete it. These four would refuse a draft that treats their remand as recognition of an absolute purchase right or a finding that malice alone establishes liability.
