## Operative proposition commitments

### R1 — statutory applicability and representative authority

All eight Associates support the following limited propositions. A state judicial judgment incorporating a class settlement is within §1738; class form, consent disposition and release of an exclusively federal claim do not themselves exempt it. A federal court must start with the rendering State's law, rather than substitute its own preclusion rule. Marrese's competency limitation on adjudicated claims does not automatically establish an identical limitation on a judicially approved release; its footnote 2 expressly reserves settlement-law treatment.

Nor does §27's allocation of federal adjudicatory jurisdiction, standing alone, establish that an authorized class representative can never compromise a related Exchange Act claim. Individual settlement capacity and representative authority nevertheless remain different questions. Silence after notice is not a substitute for lawful representation. Section 1738 cannot bind a person through a constitutionally inadequate representative proceeding merely because the judgment uses expansive words.

### R2 — Delaware-law application

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer would determine that Delaware gives ordinary preclusive effect to this affirmed settlement judgment, including its express release of the identified federal actions. The judgment incorporates the release rather than merely noting a separate private agreement. Nottingham establishes that inability to adjudicate the federal claim does not itself prevent an otherwise authorized settlement release. The state judgment's release, final approval and state affirmance cannot be stripped of ordinary effect by relitigating asserted state-law error as though the federal court were hearing a direct appeal.

This conclusion does not create a universal rule that any claim arising from the same acquisition may be released. Nottingham's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits remain part of Delaware law; Prezant requires actual Rule 23 conformity and an express adequacy determination. The six do not make new findings about who tendered shares, counsel's incentives or whether the constitutional adequacy question was fully and fairly litigated. Those matters retain the R4 treatment. An alleged error in applying state settlement law and a legally available objection that the claimant was never constitutionally bound are not interchangeable.

Stevens and Ginsburg would remit the **content and application of Delaware preclusion law to this judgment**. They accept that the document expressly incorporates the release; their objection does not depend on denying its words. Their concern is that the Ninth Circuit has not applied the proper state-law inquiry and that the relationship among Nottingham's factual-predicate limit, Prezant's representative requirement, finality and this judgment should not receive its first dispositive federal determination here. They would not replace the Ninth Circuit's overbroad federal rule with a Supreme Court state-law application before that work occurs below.

### R3 — §27 and §1738

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support the conclusion that §27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for the judgment category presented. Exclusive jurisdiction over adjudicating Exchange Act liability does not forbid a state court with jurisdiction over the underlying state suit from approving an authorized representative release. Uniform federal adjudication is not defeated simply because a settlement court assesses claim value without adjudicating liability. A proper settlement is not a state judgment on the merits of the federal securities claim.

Ginsburg supports R1's rejection of an automatic jurisdictional inference and the §1738 inquiry, but would reserve the additional statutory-exception determination until the state-effect predicate is established below. Do not count her as joining the complete R3 holding. This reservation does not establish that she would find an implied repeal.

The actual state order also contains injunctive language. Recognition of an incorporated release and direct coercive interference with a pending federal case remain distinct. Nottingham's refusal to enjoin the federal action does not invalidate every release; conversely, incorporation does not validate extraterritorial injunction or contempt authority. R2–R3 do not decide an independently preserved objection to that operation, or guarantee that such an objection defeats recognition. No federal jurisdiction is removed merely by the state order's wording.

### R4 — due process, finality and remaining issues

All eight retain the following remand boundary. Hansberry, Shutts and the governing preclusion principles require constitutionally adequate representation; notice, an opportunity to object and opt-out rights do not independently cure inadequate representation. The Ninth Circuit did not decide the fact-specific constitutional alternative. The lower court may consider properly presented unresolved objections and the antecedent extent to which they remain available collaterally. Neither a finding of constitutional inadequacy nor an unconditional entitlement to relitigate a question fully and fairly determined is made here. The existing findings are part of that inquiry, not erased evidence and not self-proving answers to its disputed constitutional dimensions.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg additionally support express explanation that adequate representation must exist **at all times**, and that a final class judgment remains susceptible to a legally proper collateral attack for failure of that constitutional prerequisite. They preserve petitioners' contention that adequacy was actually resolved through full and fair litigation as well as respondents' challenge to that contention. This is the function of the historical Ginsburg II–B join, and it remains supported by Hansberry, Shutts and the full Prezant opinion. The other five support the stated constitutional minimum and reservation but are not committed to every affirmative discussion or formulation in that fuller explanation. Assembly may test a common narrower text; it may not infer those additional joins from silence.

All eight preserve valid opt-outs, including the identified individual opt-out litigants outside this review. They decide no tender-offer liability, private right under §§14(d)(6)–(7), renewed federal class certification, damages or unpresented securities count. Traditional contract consent as an independent dispositive theory remains unadjudicated. A rendering State's legally applicable contract approach would still govern through §1738; no federal fiction of individually negotiated assent is adopted. Dismissal of covered claims may follow only after the remaining legally available grounds are resolved.
