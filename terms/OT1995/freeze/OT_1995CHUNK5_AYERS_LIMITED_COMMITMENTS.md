## Actual threshold and the strongest competing path

**Each Associate would dismiss the reconstructed writ as improvidently granted.** Statutory authority exists on the adopted pending-appeal and presentation premises. The defect is the exceptional-review justification, not jurisdiction, waiver, absence of a filing, or a failure attributed to the parties because the researcher lacks documents. No new petition-stage grant-or-deny poll is modeled.

Rule 11 requires a case of such imperative public importance as to justify departure from ordinary appellate practice and require immediate determination in this Court. It does **not** require a separately defined emergency, an imminent expenditure, or any particular factual category. Absence of a library emergency is therefore not an automatic DIG rule.

**Strongest path to proceeding:** The litigation concerns an unfinished statewide dismantlement obligation, and the challenged ruling may impose an enrollment-based limitation inconsistent with the Court's own Fordice mandate and intervening Jenkins holding. Correcting that discrete legal restriction could influence how remedial work proceeds. The reported ASU and JSU projects make the question practical rather than abstract. The reconstructed prior-review premise permits reconsideration of whether to proceed; it is not necessary to invent another grant. Immediate disposition of an isolated legal issue may sometimes justify bypassing appellate judgment without an emergency.

**Why that path is insufficient on this packet:** The State's continuing duty and the availability of violation-linked educational repair are already established. The disputed task is reading and applying those rules to this denial and its particular findings. The pending appeal can perform that work. The record does not establish that awaiting its judgment prevents effective library relief, that an impending decision depends on immediate Supreme Court clarification, or another concrete reason why determination in this Court is imperative now. The library projects were reported in March 1995; they are not verified March 1996 commitments whose progress necessarily forecloses correction. The importance of the statewide litigation remains substantial, but the authorized question adjudicates neither its overall sufficiency nor its other live components. A potentially consequential error is not by itself a reason for review before appellate judgment.

The earlier grant premise does not settle this inquiry or compel a merits disposition. These commitments do not assume that the grant originally concerned a broader question, that a new defect emerged after a particular argument, or that the Court previously made a factual urgency finding. They assess the authorized reconstructed review as supplied.

## Conditional library merits: positions if the Court reaches the question

These conclusions preserve the material merits alternatives for reconciliation and final compatibility. **They do not override the threshold commitments above, enter a conditional Court judgment, or establish a merits coalition in a DIG disposition.** A decision to reach the merits over a threshold objection does not itself answer the Justice's substantive join objections.

### Common limits of every conditional position

The review concerns only the denial at 1458. Historical inadequacy, a present difference in collections, and mission-related traceability do not by themselves establish every element of a present library injury or entitlement to a particular addition. The allocation formula's relation to missions must be examined with the finding that mission policies continue fostering separation; the latter does not automatically establish a complete current-effect finding for each allocation. Accreditation does not prove dismantlement. Findings that volume counts do not significantly influence student choice and that institutions have different missions remain findings, subject to ordinary review. Physical-space quality is distinct from collection quality. Existing JSU and ASU improvements are not judicial awards made in this event or proof of their completion.

Neither disposition creates a funding-parity entitlement, identical missions, a fixed volume target, a new appropriation or an educational-improvement guarantee. No Associate supports a new Supreme Court finding of current injury, necessity, cost or feasibility on this record. None supports changing admissions, reporting, endowment, maintenance-control or other decree provisions. None treats denying further library relief as a formal Freeman release, declares systemwide compliance or orders a new supervisor. Missing transcripts do not prove that the litigants failed to present evidence.

## Proposition-level compatibility of the conditional merits paths

This section records compatibility for later assembly; it does not combine conditional views into an actual merits judgment.

1. **No automatic library parity or award:** All eight Associates accept that the supplied record does not authorize equal collections, identical institutional missions, a specified additional project or an expenditure. This shared limit is compatible with either sustaining or remanding this denial.
2. **No universal white-enrollment condition:** All eight reject an opinion holding that necessary repair of an established segregation-caused educational injury is categorically unavailable whenever additional white enrollment is unproved. Scalia's conditional affirmance rests on his confined reading of this particular denial, not adoption of that categorical rule. His agreement with the legal limit is not a vote to vacate the denial.
3. **Application requiring limited reconsideration:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that the denial's express rationale requires correction and does not establish a sufficient independent application of the proper educational-repair inquiry. They would vacate only that denial and remand. Their common ground does not determine present library injury, necessity or the amount of lawful relief. Scalia withholds this application and supports limited affirmance on the reading stated above.
4. **Remand limits:** The seven conditional remand positions preserve actual findings unless properly challenged under ordinary review; leave all other provisions and challenges undisturbed without merits affirmance; and permit further relief or renewed denial under the correct governing rule. They do not require a new trial, hearing, deadline, supervisor or funding procedure. Ordinary lawful proceedings remain available as needed. Neither the State's continuing dismantlement burden nor the limits on remedy-specific findings is rewritten.

No publication, author, opinion assignment or final joinder is selected here. A conditional alignment does not establish authority under Marks and requires no fractured-decision synthesis.
