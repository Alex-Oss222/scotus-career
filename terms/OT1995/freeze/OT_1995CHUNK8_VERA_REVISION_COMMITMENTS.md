## Entering law and the genuinely competing merits paths

The operative Standards are **Personal districting exposure and direct apportionment review**, effective Miller, June 29, 1995; **Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury**, Shaw, June 28, 1993, retained and clarified by Miller, June 29, 1995; **Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing**, Lujan, June 12, 1992; and **Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing**, Northeastern Florida, June 14, 1993. Their complete qualifications in the slice govern. The earlier SCLC decision applies the territorial-design boundary without adding a constitutional-injury gate to §2 or presuming that minority-preferred officials serve only one race.

**Adherence path.** Under Shaw and Miller II–III, predominant racial consideration in a common territorial constituency, even with ordinary criteria subordinated, does not alone establish the asserted deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. Actual racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership and other independently cognizable wrongs retain their own elements and remedies. Such a wrong can be complete in government's own treatment; no second governmental act, monetary loss or electoral defeat is invariably necessary. Here no such additional operative treatment is pleaded or found for the plaintiffs. This path reverses the sole legal ground for the three-district decree without erasing supported purpose findings or declaring the whole plan lawful.

Its strongest objection is that an official predominantly racial assignment is itself the unequal treatment, so that demanding an independently cognizable deprivation wrongly exempts a racial classification. That is a substantial constitutional disagreement, but Miller decided it on an accepted predominance finding and rejected the same sorting, status and representation inferences. Greater detail, a trial rather than pleading posture, racial targets or unusual shapes do not distinguish that rule. The adherence path rests on the shared-electorate distinction and the actual absence of an operative participation or status restriction, not on an assumption of benevolent motives or a universal downstream-loss requirement.

**Express reconsideration path.** The alternative supported by the published Shaw and Miller dissents would replace the territorial-design-only boundary insofar as it excludes official racial assignment in which race predominates and ordinary districting criteria are subordinated. That proved personal classification would trigger strict scrutiny: a compelling interest and a sufficiently fitted use of race. Mere racial awareness, minority opportunity, a population percentage or an unusual shape alone would not establish predominance or automatic invalidity. It is a proposed change to Shaw and Miller II–III, not a factual exception to them. Miller's standing, direct-review, permission-versus-compulsion, evidence and clear-error propositions can remain intact.

Its strongest objection is stare decisis: two recent decisions directly adopted the contrary boundary, Miller did so on proved predominance, and SCLC has since applied it in a remedial setting. The proponents below expressly confront that obstacle. Their basis is their maintained individual-equality objection to exempting geographical allocation by race, not a claim that intervening cases or new factual labels silently overruled Shaw. The challenged 1991 enactment preceded Shaw and therefore could not have been enacted in reliance on Shaw, although subsequent governmental reliance on the announced rule remains a real institutional consideration. Their proposed change addresses only this constitutional trigger; it neither abolishes statutory dilution coverage nor makes all race-aware voluntary districting unlawful. Their conclusions about which consideration prevails are separately stated below.

**Independent-deprivation alternative.** Within existing law, an actual exclusion, participation disability or imposed inferior status could support a different constitutional claim. The packet expressly supplies none. Confusion, misdirected-ballot and campaign testimony is configuration evidence, not a plaintiff-specific pleaded injury or finding. Nor is there a candidate-eligibility condition, racial ballot label, discriminatory constituent service or legal rule making a representative responsible only to one race. A writer treating assignment itself as the missing deprivation must acknowledge that this changes Miller's treatment of precisely that inference. Neither a new-injury remand nor revival of dismissed §2, Fifteenth Amendment or state-law counts is supported.

## Purpose findings, statutory justification and district-specific application

### Supported purpose findings and competing explanations

Miller V-A and V-B supply the common evidentiary baseline: shape is evidence rather than a necessary gateway or sufficient violation; purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal significance is independently determined. The present record supports accepting the trial court's findings of racial predominance and subordination for the three districts. This acceptance does not adopt every rhetorical statement in the lower opinion or turn the presence of race data into proof about every line. In particular, the lower court's phrase that the districts were explainable only by race is read with its express acknowledgment of political, incumbent and community considerations; predominance does not require the absence of every other motive.

| District | Supported application | Strongest competing explanation and reason it does not require rejection of the finding |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | The trial court found the racial population objective central to the district's construction, supported by testimony, the preclearance narrative, block and neighborhood choices and departures from ordinary criteria. It found race primary despite the competition for voters among the proposed district and neighboring incumbents. | Frost and Bryant wanted Democratic constituents; geography, transport, land use and common interests were offered. The lower court considered those explanations and found the racial allocation integral to the accommodation. Its evaluation of the later demographic study and direct purpose evidence supplies a supported factual basis. This does not establish a universal equivalence between Democratic politics and race. |
| 18 | The effort to preserve Black electoral opportunity was implemented through block-level inclusion and exclusion and a substantial change in racial composition, intertwined with District 29. The lower court found the chosen racial population objective predominant. | Preserving an existing opportunity district, incumbency and the dispersed residential pattern matter. They explain why a State might consider race, but do not compel a finding that race remained subordinate in these particular choices. The court's supported purpose finding is distinct from whether preservation is constitutionally justified. |
| 29 | The new Hispanic-opportunity objective, racial target, interwoven boundaries and precinct splitting support the finding that the racial population goal controlled the construction despite accommodations to candidate and neighboring-incumbent interests. | Martinez, Green and Andrews had competing political constituencies; connected communities and partisan balance were advanced. The lower court found that maintaining the racial target remained decisive through those adjustments. Total population, voting-age population and citizen voting-age population remain distinct; no legal percentage requirement is inferred. |

The strongest alternative is to vacate the predominance findings as infected by a rule that any purposeful majority-minority district is suspect, or to treat incumbent protection as necessarily predominant. The published findings go beyond racial awareness, identify the actual district-by-district construction, and consider the competing explanations. Miller does not authorize replacing those findings with a newly imagined political compromise. Conversely, accepting them does not endorse the lower court's constitutional conclusion under entering law. The individual commitments below preserve that distinction.

### Conditional and operative fit inquiry

For a Justice adhering to Shaw/Miller, the lack of a constitutional trigger is dispositive. No actual holding that Texas established or failed strict scrutiny is necessary, and no new alternative invalidity holding is selected for those Justices. They may acknowledge the following legally available alternatives without joining an operative strict-scrutiny decision.

For a Justice proposing the new trigger, justification must be examined rather than treating predominance as automatic liability. The following bounded fit conclusion is selected by the individual proponents below:

1. Assume that compliance with a valid Voting Rights Act obligation can supply a compelling interest; this case does not require a categorical holding that every asserted statutory concern is compelling, or that no such concern can be. A State may reasonably act to avoid an actual statutory violation without waiting for a judgment compelling one exact map. Miller IV and its conditional discussion, Voinovich and the prior dissent preserve that room for lawful choice.
2. Section 5 nonretrogression and §2 unequal-opportunity proof remain distinct. Beer does not require creating new opportunity districts 29 and 30 merely because population grew. Preserving District 18 presents the stronger nonretrogression concern, but the duty does not itself require increasing a chosen racial share to a fixed target regardless of available alternatives. Pleasant Grove's separate purpose rule is not reduced to retrogression. Section 2 retains applicable Gingles conditions and a totality inquiry, including actual preference, cohesion and ordinarily defeating majority bloc voting; no Black–Hispanic coalition or statutory liability is established merely by combining populations or describing polarization. Growe, Holder and De Grandy retain their coverage, comparison, proof and no-maximization rules.
3. Texas's discrimination history and the competing polarization evidence make the compliance claim serious. They do not alone show that the particular predominant racial construction was reasonably fitted to the asserted duty. The lower opinion's rejection of adequate §2 justification is evaluated as a legal conclusion, not treated as an independently established fact that discrimination or unequal opportunity never existed. Its citizenship observations supply no new population-denominator rule.
4. On this record the required fit is not established for the three districts. For District 30, the lower findings concerning C500 and C606 and the testimony about preserving neighborhoods supply concrete evidence of a more ordinary districting route to a minority opportunity district; the selected departures were substantially tied to accommodating incumbents. For Districts 18 and 29, the lower findings concerning C606 and C676 and witness acknowledgments likewise support the availability of approaches giving greater respect to ordinary criteria. C676 is not treated as independently proving an equivalent Hispanic-majority opportunity district: its Houston proposal included a joint influence district, and C606 and the remaining findings do separate work. No precise alternative is declared legally sufficient, required or adopted. The conclusion is that the demonstrated allocation and departures are not adequately connected to the asserted statutory need, even allowing reasonable latitude, rather than that the State must select the prettiest map.
5. The State's political and incumbent interests remain relevant explanations, but cannot independently supply a compelling justification for a proved predominant racial classification. The United States' separate fit defense is considered; the State's reported lack of a serious precise-shape defense is neither a default nor a waiver binding that intervenor. Its assertion that the alternatives reduce opportunity is weighed against the trial court's contrary findings and testimony rather than erased. On those findings, the defense does not establish that the challenged racial allocation was reasonably necessary for the compliance interest, as opposed to serving the additional political accommodations.
6. The lower court's demand for the least possible irregularity is not adopted. A State need not use a universal compactness metric, preserve every boundary, disregard practical geography or prove that a federal court would order its exact district. The affirmance position sustains the result on the supported failure of connection and fit, with these qualifications, rather than incorporating that overbroad geometric formula. No collateral preclearance review or setting aside of the November 1991 preclearance occurs.

The strongest narrower alternative for the proponents is a fit-only remand because the lower court used too strict a shape formulation and the alternatives' electoral effectiveness was disputed. That path is legally available if the surviving findings do not resolve reasonable necessity. The proponents below instead find the record sufficient: the district-specific construction findings, actual alternative-plan discussion and testimony about achievable minority districts establish the deficient connection without requiring the least-irregular-map formula or independent appellate map measurements. They do not resolve unknown statistical details, certify an alternative's entire legality or infer a litigant default from unavailable originals. Were a proposed opinion to rely on one alternative's exact voting-age/citizenship composition or equal electoral performance as an indispensable fact, that narrower factual issue would require return to neutral research rather than a fabricated finding.
