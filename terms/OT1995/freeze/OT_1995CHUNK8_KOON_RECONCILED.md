## Controlling rule support and scope for assembly

All eight support the following review and category framework. Section3553(b) requires the applicable range unless a kind or degree of circumstance inadequately considered by the Commission should yield a different sentence. Adequate consideration is assessed through Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. A forbidden factor cannot support departure. An encouraged factor permits departure when not already taken into account. A discouraged factor, or an encouraged factor already reflected in the applicable guideline, requires exceptional degree or another supported feature making the case different from the ordinary instance; the specific §5K2.0 provision preserves the substantially-excessive-degree requirement where the applicable guideline and adjustments already account for the factor. Unmentioned factors require examination of the structure and theory of the relevant guideline and the Guidelines as a whole, with the expectation that unguided departures will be highly infrequent. These conditions preserve the statutory requirement that the circumstance should result in a different sentence; rarity alone is not sufficient.

The sentencing court's institutional experience supports deference on the concrete comparison. It receives no deference for an incorrect legal interpretation, categorical permission error or mathematical mistake. Stinson makes valid interpretive commentary authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to statute, inconsistent with the guideline or plainly erroneous; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Neither Rivera's suggested analytical sequence nor the label abuse of discretion eliminates those legal limits. Section3553(a)(2)'s sentencing purposes remain relevant to the particular sentence; they do not authorize courts to invent categorical factor bans beyond the Commission's scheme.

The five-level holding is supported by all eight at **both application and extent**. Section2H1.4 incorporates aggravated assault and adds the color-of-law component. Its relevant comparison includes unprovoked official assaults; it is not limited to excessive-force arrests. The six-level official-conduct addition applies to provoked and unprovoked conduct alike. The sentencing judge found significant provocation, initially lawful force and a transition to unlawful force within seconds; the reasoning was more than but-for chronology. Those findings permit the five-level reduction despite the finding that resistance, actual danger and reasonable perception of danger had ended when force became criminal. The decision does not excuse the assault or reopen guilt.

The full §5K2.10 limits remain: wrongful victim conduct must significantly contribute to provoking the offense. Extent takes account of comparative physical characteristics, persistence and efforts to avoid confrontation, reasonably perceived danger including reputation, actual danger, and other victim conduct substantially contributing to danger. Victim misconduct ordinarily does not justify the provision in criminal sexual-abuse offenses; it usually does not apply to nonviolent offenses, although unusual extended provocation and harassment leading to property retaliation may qualify. These are neither a new finding of continuing danger nor a requirement that provocation end at the same instant as the crime. No later sixth factor is imported.

For the separate three levels, the five affirmative Associates sustain the actual vulnerability and successive-prosecution **grounds**, not the amount of reduction or an independent entitlement to three levels. The unusual vulnerability finding rests on the combination of police status and extraordinary notoriety, not occupational prestige or guaranteed solitary confinement. The successive-prosecution finding concerns the significant burden of these proceedings; lawful dual sovereignty does not erase that factual burden, and the ruling finds no double-jeopardy violation. The three dissenting Associates' record-specific objections remain as set out above; none rejects every hypothetical vulnerability or successive-prosecution case.

Seven reject the employment consequences because losing a public position and future work after willfully abusing that authority is adequately contemplated here. That is distinct from treating job loss as prohibited socioeconomic status. Sections5H1.5 and 5H1.11 discourage ordinary employment/public-service matters; the former's second paragraph permits attention to employment when setting probation or supervised-release conditions, not an automatic prison-range departure. Section5H1.10 excludes its listed classifications, including socioeconomic status. Stevens's contrary view concerns the unusual combination of collateral consequences on this record, not an exemption for police officers.

## Actual Williams mandate

The starting calculation was level27, categoryI, range70–87 months. The separate five-level and combined three-level departures led to level19 and 30 months. Upholding the five-level component does not reinstate either sentence or validate the additional three levels.

Invalid reliance is established: all eight reject low recidivism; seven also reject employment, and Souter/Ginsburg/Breyer additionally reject the vulnerability and successive-prosecution grounds. Petitioners, as defenders of the original sentences, have not established from the whole record that the sentencing judge would have imposed the same sentences without the invalid reliance. The district court grouped the four considerations and expressly stated that none alone justified the three levels. No finding establishes that the remaining valid combination would yield the same sentence. Possibility of lawful reimposition is not the actual Williams test.

The cases therefore return through the Court of Appeals to the District Court for further sentencing consistent with the valid-ground holdings. Any departure extent must be independently reasonable on valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court. A reviewing court may not invent substitute reasons; an aggregation of valid circumstances is not a device for retaining invalid ones. The mandate does not set a replacement term, presume error harmless, promise reimposition of 30 months or reopen the convictions or bodily-injury adjustment. The upheld five-level component remains settled within this reviewed record.
