## OT1995-003 — Citizens Bank of Maryland v. Strumpf

**Event:** 1995-10-31 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1340

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as revised on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Remand for proceedings on the validity of the asserted setoff and any remaining Code requirements.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Reach the preserved merits question and leave unpresented claims and remedies to their ordinary channels.
- **Supporting Authorities:** 11 U.S.C. §§ 362(a), 542(b), and 553; ordinary setoff law; *United States v. Whiting Pools, Inc.*, 462 U.S. 198.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** A creditor's temporary refusal to pay, imposed while it promptly asks the bankruptcy court for stay relief, is not a setoff, because it does not permanently settle the mutual debts.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Setoff requires a decision to apply mutual debts against one another, not every temporary refusal to pay. Reading the stay to require immediate payment would destroy the preservation function Congress retained in §553 before the court could decide entitlement. The hold must remain temporary and tied to prompt judicial process. Good faith is not a general exception to the automatic stay; what matters is that the hold did not permanently settle the accounts. Whether the hold was otherwise wrongful, including whether it covered more than the claimed setoff, remains a separate question that this holding does not decide.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** An indefinite freeze, a secret debit, or a hold untethered to a colorable setoff and prompt stay motion may constitute prohibited control or setoff.

**Stone’s disposition:** Reject Libretti’s challenge to his informed waiver of a forfeiture jury. Affirm forfeiture supported by the governing statute and record; vacate and remand any identified unsupported portion of the property order. Leave the constitutional source of the forfeiture jury right unresolved. Preserve the guilty plea and conviction.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** A defendant may knowingly relinquish a jury right and admit facts establishing forfeiture. Libretti’s informed waiver therefore permits resolution without deciding whether the jury protection rests on the Constitution or former Rule 31(e). The sentencing label alone cannot resolve that constitutional question, and there is no need to do so where the waiver is valid. Simulated Gaudin is completed law concerning jury decision of enacted criminal elements; this disposition neither expands nor displaces its holding.

Consent does not enlarge the court’s statutory power to punish. Section 853 controls which property may be taken. The plea’s actual admissions and the existing evidence may establish the required basis without a separate hearing for every asset, but an agreement to forfeit cannot substitute for a statutory authorization that is absent. Lawful acquisition does not by itself immunize property: property may be an authorized instrumentality or enterprise interest, and substitute forfeiture remains available when the statute’s conditions are satisfied. Apply those provisions to the identified property and record rather than presume that all assets are forfeitable or that every asset lacks a basis.

**Remedy and limits:** Preserve each supported portion of the forfeiture order. Vacatur concerns an identified unsupported portion, with remand only to resolve its lawful treatment or separation from the supported order. No blanket new asset-by-asset hearing requirement follows. Ordinary preservation, the actual admissions, statutory limits and third-party interests retain their force. Meaningful waiver and a firm limit on judicial punishment can operate together without a new procedural test.
