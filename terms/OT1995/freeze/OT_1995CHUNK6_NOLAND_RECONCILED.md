## Comparator and record reconciliation

The verified historical comparator records reversal and remand with every participating Justice joining the same rationale and no separate writing. Thus each of these eight Associates historically supported the statutory-class ground and the express reservation whether misconduct must always precede equitable subordination. History is corroboration, not the source of these commitments. No Associate departure in judgment, governing ground, reservation or remedy is supported after testing the actual changed law below.

The actual Sixth Circuit disclaimer is preserved. Petition Appendix A, 20a, says its decision does not mean every tax penalty will be subordinated and requires weighing the equities. Pages 19a–20a nevertheless justify the result by noncompensatory character, payment of actual-loss creditors, the failed enterprise's insufficient assets and retained tax/interest priority. The Government challenges the legal sufficiency of those reasons, not a nonexistent express rule automatically subordinating every penalty. The original freeze correctly resolves that challenge.

The statutory distinction also survives verification. Section 726(a)(4) addresses nonpecuniary penalties arising **before the earlier of the order for relief or appointment of a trustee**. It does not assign all penalties fourth priority. These §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition administrative expenses retain case-era §507(a)(1) priority; §348(d) excepts §503(b) claims from conversion's ordinary treatment. Section 726's express subjection to §510 and §510(c)'s permitted subordination of allowed claims remain operative. Susceptibility to that authority does not establish that the lower courts supplied a lawful reason for using it.

The reconciled ground rejects judicial replacement of Congress's category ordering by a general preference for compensation. It preserves statutory equitable exceptions and judicial development rather than freezing doctrine in 1978. The historical qualification concerning a need to reconcile conflicting congressional choices is consistent with the freeze: no such conflict is established here, and the coexistence of priority and §510(c) does not itself authorize a replacement ranking. “Claim-specific” distinguishes an equitable application from class reordering; it establishes neither a new mandatory checklist nor a universal creditor-misconduct requirement. No hypothetical lawful no-misconduct application is adjudicated.
