## Validated source framing

- **Case Name:** Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.
- **Citation / Docket:** 517 U.S. 706; No. 95-244
- **October Term:** 1995
- **Entry Stage / Event Type:** Inventory-authorized merits review; decision pending in this simulation.
- **Question Presented:** (1) Whether the court of appeals erred in holding that an order remanding a removed action to state court on abstention grounds is reviewable by appeal under the collateral-order doctrine of *Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp.*, 337 U.S. 541, rather than only by mandamus. (2) Whether the court of appeals erred in holding that the abstention powers of federal courts are limited to actions in equity or, alternatively, in limiting the exercise of *Burford* abstention solely to actions in equity.
- **Authorized Review Scope:** The stated questions and the parties’ scope and preservation contentions govern the submitted merits review. Allstate’s merits brief restates the questions as whether an order remanding a case on *Burford* grounds is reviewable by appeal and not merely by mandamus, and whether an action at law against a private party seeking money damages can present the exceptional circumstances necessary for a federal court to decline to exercise its jurisdiction under *Burford*; the Commissioner’s reply objects under Rule 14.1(a) that this restatement changes the substance of the second question.
- **Legal Area:** Federal courts — abstention, remand, and appellate finality
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, *Garamendi v. Allstate Insurance Co.*, 47 F.3d 350 (No. 91-55855, decided February 2, 1995; petition for rehearing denied and suggestion for rehearing en banc rejected May 19, 1995; mandate issued May 31, 1995), reviewed the District Court’s July 1, 1991 order remanding the removed action to state court on *Burford* abstention grounds. It held that 28 U.S.C. §1447(d) did not bar review because the remand rested on no ground specified in §1447(c); that the abstention-based remand was a final collateral order reviewable on appeal, so it treated Allstate’s filing as an appeal rather than as the mandamus petition Allstate had requested in the alternative; and that a district court may not abstain under *Burford* when the plaintiff seeks only legal relief. It did not reach Allstate’s further argument that *Burford*’s requirements were not met. Its judgment reads: “The district court’s remand order is VACATED and the case is REMANDED for proceedings consistent with this opinion.” Neither the opinion nor the order denying rehearing rules on Allstate’s motion to compel arbitration or directs arbitration or a stay.
- **Requested Supreme Court Disposition / Relief:** The Commissioner (petitioner) asks that the Ninth Circuit’s judgment be reversed and the case remanded with appropriate directions, contending that under *Thermtron* a remand order is not a final judgment, that this order is not an appealable *Cohen* collateral order, and that it was reviewable only by mandamus (or by §1292(b) certification if both courts concur); and that federal courts’ abstention authority, including *Burford* abstention, is not confined to actions in equity under the merged federal system. Allstate (respondent) asks that the judgment be affirmed, contending that the remand was a final decision appealable under §1291 because, like an abstention-based dismissal, it surrendered federal jurisdiction, and alternatively was an appealable *Cohen* collateral order; that *Burford* permits a federal court to withhold only equitable relief that would restrain or interfere with state administrative proceedings or decisions and disrupt state policymaking on matters of distinctively local concern, and so affords no discretion to decline jurisdiction over this removed action at law for contract damages; and that, at a minimum, the District Court could not abstain without deciding Allstate’s pending Federal Arbitration Act motion. If review by appeal was unavailable, Allstate asks that the judgment be affirmed on the alternative ground that mandamus should issue, or that the case be remanded for the Ninth Circuit to address its mandamus request. Any decision must stay within the presented issues and source-authorized remedy.
- **Material Facts and Record:** California’s Insurance Commissioner, as liquidator and trustee of the insolvent Mission Insurance companies (ordered into liquidation by the Los Angeles Superior Court in 1987), sued Allstate and other reinsurers in that court in 1990 on the same causes of action as an earlier suit against Mission’s reinsurers (*Gillespie I*) that had been consolidated with the liquidation proceeding. The liquidation court’s orders assert sole and exclusive jurisdiction over the Mission companies’ assets and enjoin actions against those companies or the liquidator without that court’s leave; its conservation and liquidation orders also authorize the liquidator to bring legal or equitable actions in that or other states. Allstate filed claims in the liquidation proceeding in 1987; the Commissioner says they present the same setoff contentions as Allstate’s defenses here, while Allstate says the proofs of claim reproduced in the Joint Appendix were not before the District Court and have since been superseded. Against all defendants, the complaint seeks a declaration of the parties’ rights under the reinsurance agreements and of the amounts the defendants are obligated to pay, and, on a contract count, the sums due plus special damages; its conspiracy and tort counts, seeking damages according to proof and punitive damages, are pleaded against Doe defendants. Allstate removed the action on diversity grounds in August 1990 and moved under the Federal Arbitration Act to compel arbitration under arbitration clauses in the reinsurance agreements and to stay the litigation pending arbitration; the District Court deferred that motion until it decided the Commissioner’s motion to remand. On July 1, 1991, the District Court (C.D. Cal., No. CV 90-4713-WMB) held that it had jurisdiction, rejecting the Commissioner’s argument that the liquidation court’s in rem control of Mission’s assets excluded federal jurisdiction and noting that the liquidation court had authorized the Commissioner to seek redress in other forums; held that *Colorado River* abstention did not apply because there was no concurrent state litigation to which Allstate was a party; but abstained under *Burford*. It treated as critical Allstate’s anticipated defense that it may set off balances Mission owes it under other reinsurance contracts (Cal. Ins. Code §1031), a contested state-law question it understood to be before the California Supreme Court, and concluded that federal adjudication could undermine California’s interest in uniform and orderly insurance liquidations through inconsistent rulings. It rejected Allstate’s contentions, based on *New Orleans Public Service, Inc. v. Council of City of New Orleans*, 491 U.S. 350 (*NOPSI*), that *Burford* applies only to federal courts acting in equity and only to interference with state administrative proceedings or orders, reading *NOPSI* as a caution against extending the doctrine rather than as such a limit. It declined Allstate’s alternative request to stay the action pending a California Supreme Court setoff ruling, reasoning that it was unclear what would remain to adjudicate afterward, and remanded the entire action to state court without ruling on the arbitration motion. In 1992 the California Supreme Court decided *Prudential Reinsurance Co. v. Superior Court*, 3 Cal. 4th 1118, permitting some reinsurer setoffs and barring others. The Commissioner states that disputes over the application of that decision and of §1031 remain open. One such dispute, an appeal from a March 8, 1994 liquidation-court setoff ruling, was decided for the Commissioner in December 1995 (*Quackenbush v. Imperial Casualty & Indemnity Co.*, 41 Cal. App. 4th 828): the Court of Appeal held that *Prudential* barred the setoff at issue and that the parties’ settlement agreement foreclosed the reinsurer’s alternative single-entity theory; the Commissioner describes the decision as resting primarily on that settlement agreement. Allstate, which has not answered, states that it will dispute the claims and assert setoff, and that a federal court can apply §1031 and *Prudential* as ordinary state law.
- **Preservation / Threshold / Vehicle Issues:** Issue 1 — appealability of the remand: whether review lies by appeal under §1291 (as a final decision or a *Cohen* collateral order) or only by mandamus. Issue 2 — whether federal abstention authority, or *Burford* abstention specifically, is limited to actions in equity, and whether this action is one at law. The order reviewed below is the District Court’s abstention-based remand. Below, the Commissioner argued that §1447(d) barred review by appeal or mandamus; a Ninth Circuit motions panel denied his motion to dismiss the appeal, holding under *Thermtron* that §1447(d) does not apply to a remand not based on jurisdictional grounds, and referred to the merits panel whether the appeal should instead be treated as a mandamus petition; the merits panel likewise held §1447(d) inapplicable. The petition’s questions do not present the §1447(d) point. Allstate treats it as uncontested; the Commissioner’s reply states that he does not concede it and would view all remand orders as within §1447(d)’s bar, acknowledging that *Thermtron* holds otherwise on its facts but citing the Kennedy concurrence in *Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca* as leaving the question open in other contexts. No federal court has ruled on Allstate’s motion to compel arbitration and to stay the litigation pending arbitration. The Ninth Circuit did not reach whether *Burford*’s requirements were otherwise met; the Commissioner says that whether the District Court properly exercised any discretion to abstain is not before this Court, while Allstate argues that no *Burford* basis exists here in any event. Allstate states that the Commissioner has not challenged the District Court’s *Colorado River* ruling, and objects that abstention grounds based on *Louisiana Power & Light Co. v. City of Thibodaux* or *Fair Assessment in Real Estate Ass’n v. McNary*, as urged by the Commissioner’s amici, were not raised below; the Commissioner cites those decisions in support of his position that abstention is not confined to equity. On rehearing the Ninth Circuit declined to consider, as waived because first raised in the rehearing petition, the Commissioner’s argument that the declaratory-relief count seeks more than legal relief. In this Court the Commissioner argues that the declaratory claim and Allstate’s setoff claims are equitable under California law, that Allstate’s own requested stay pending arbitration and setoff are equitable relief, and that this suit to marshal an insolvent insurer’s assets is part of quasi in rem receivership proceedings rather than an ordinary in personam action between private parties; Allstate answers that the declaratory request merely recasts the damages claim, that the argument was waived, and that the liquidator sues as trustee for the Mission estate on the companies’ contract claims. Allstate also argues that abstention was inappropriate in the face of its pending Federal Arbitration Act motion, whose enforceability is governed by federal law; the Commissioner responds that Allstate’s asserted post-liquidation right to arbitrate is an unsettled question under state and federal law that remains open for initial resolution in state court.
- **Relevant Dates:** Petition filed August 11, 1995; argument February 20, 1996; decision June 3, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.

#### Additional public case information

- **Conflict / Percolation:** The petition asserted conflicts on both questions. On appealability, it cited *Corcoran v. Ardra Insurance Co.*, 842 F.2d 31 (2d Cir. 1988) (abstention-based remand reviewable by mandamus, not appeal), and *Doughty v. Underwriters at Lloyd’s, London*, 6 F.3d 856 (1st Cir. 1993) (*Burford*-based remand not immediately appealable under *Cohen*); Allstate’s opposition responded that *Minot v. Eckardt-Minot*, 13 F.3d 590 (2d Cir. 1994), permitted a direct appeal from an abstention-based remand and appears to limit *Corcoran* to its facts, and that *Doughty* adopted no categorical rule. On the law–equity question, it cited *Wolfson v. Mutual Benefit Life Insurance Co.*, 51 F.3d 141 (8th Cir. 1995), which declined to draw rigid law–equity lines for abstention purposes, and *General Glass Industries Corp. v. Monsour Medical Foundation*, 973 F.2d 197 (3d Cir. 1992); Allstate responded that *Wolfson* affirmed a stay of a claim against an insolvent insurer’s estate, distinguished receivers’ suits to enlarge the estate and does not conflict in result, and that post-*NOPSI* appellate decisions on which the Ninth Circuit relied, *University of Maryland v. Peat Marwick Main & Co.*, 923 F.2d 265 (3d Cir. 1991), and *Fragoso v. Lopez*, 991 F.2d 878 (1st Cir. 1993), accord with the decision below.

## Law-entering snapshot — validated with the completed-May28 refresh

The June 3 merits channel is review of the Ninth Circuit judgment under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Whether the Ninth Circuit itself had appeal jurisdiction is a presented antecedent issue. Diversity removal jurisdiction is not a finding that abstention is lawful. Section 1291 final decisions, *Cohen* collateral orders, §1292(b) certified interlocutory appeals and mandamus remain separate channels. Section 1292(b) requires written district certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion and possible material advancement of termination, a timely application within ten days and appellate permission; the application alone effects no stay. No certification is established here. Mandamus cannot be assumed merely because direct appeal fails.

Earlier current-term law adds *Things Remembered* (December 5, 1995): §§1447(c) and (d) together bar appellate review of a remand for a timely raised removal-procedure defect or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, including a case also removed under §1452. Classification of the remand ground is permissible; review of a covered ground's correctness is not. It does not cover every remand by its own force. Kennedy's concurrence, joined by Ginsburg, reserves discretionary *Cohill* remands; Ginsburg's §1452(b) equity construction, joined by Stevens, is noncontrolling and limited to that bankruptcy statute. Neither writing resolves the present *Burford* appeal. *Behrens* (February 21, 1996) rejects an automatic one-pretrial-appeal restriction within legal immunity review while retaining ordinary finality, collateral-order and preservation limits; it supplies no general right to appeal every judicial refusal to act.

Surviving pre-divergence authority includes *Thermtron*, 423 U.S. 336 (statutory review bar and mandamus), *Cohen*, 337 U.S. 541 (collateral category), *Moses H. Cone*, 460 U.S. 1 (effectively conclusive abstention stay, federal arbitration context), *Burford*, 319 U.S. 315, *Colorado River*, 424 U.S. 800, and *NOPSI*, 491 U.S. 350 (exceptional abstention rather than an automatic response to state regulation). *NOPSI* states the *Burford* branches subject to timely and adequate state-court review: difficult state-law questions of substantial public importance transcending the immediate result, or federal review disruptive of state efforts to establish coherent policy on a matter of substantial public concern. These are alternatives, not cumulative requirements. The formulation expressly addresses a federal court sitting in equity declining interference with state administrative proceedings or orders; what that phrasing implies for this action is the presented dispute, not a premise to omit. Its opinion cautions that a complex regulatory structure alone does not establish abstention. Their general propositions must retain their actual setting: a retained-jurisdiction stay is not identical to dismissal or remand; a regulatory program's importance alone does not demonstrate the particular interference required for *Burford*. The equity premise and scope of those authorities are contested here, not answered by the preparer's characterization.

Relevant enacted provisions are 28 U.S.C. §§1291, 1292(b), 1332, 1441, 1447(c)–(d), 2201; FAA §§2–4; McCarran-Ferguson, 15 U.S.C. §§1011–1015; and California insurance-liquidation provisions including §§1020, 1031 and 1058. No inference that the FAA motion must succeed follows from its existence. The state court's asset orders and its permission to sue elsewhere must be read together. The District Court rejected exclusive in-rem jurisdiction and found no concurrent Allstate action supporting *Colorado River*.
