## Actual published positions and their limits

**O'Connor:** She joined Breyer's entire Court opinion, including retention of *Southland*, actual-commerce coverage, generally applicable contract defenses and the bounded remand. Her separate concurrence preserves her view that *Southland* originally misconstrued the Act, referring to her *Southland* and *Perry* dissents. She nevertheless gives strong weight to statutory stare decisis after a decade of operation without demonstrated unadministrability. Once state-court application is retained, she supports broad, uniform actual-commerce coverage; narrowing commerce is not a substitute for overruling *Southland*. Her original-construction reservation does not diminish her actual join or mandate. The source does not state an unconditional promise never to reconsider statutory precedent.

**Thomas:** He authored the state-court dissent, joined by Scalia. He reads the Act's structure and federal-court judicial directions as failing to establish *Southland*'s substantive state-court command, would overrule that aspect of *Southland*, and would affirm on nonapplication to the state proceeding. He does not reach the alternative commerce questions or find a general contract defense successful. The published dissent expressly supplies **no independently sufficient specific-performance holding under §§3–4**. It cannot be expanded into a decision on those provisions' full state-court reach.

**Scalia:** He joined Thomas's dissent and wrote separately. His actual published explanation is that earlier adherence to *Southland* **when overruling was not requested** did not resolve the **express request** presented in *Allied-Bruce*. He rejects reliance on the statutory interpretation as independently sufficient to preserve a state-court command the statute does not establish. He would affirm on the state-court ground without deciding commerce coverage.

That request-sensitive explanation is material to later analysis. The bounded public source contains no express prospective undertaking that Scalia will adhere to *Southland* in all future cases, and none that he will repeat his dissent regardless of whether overruling is requested. Absence of one undertaking does not establish the opposite undertaking. The source records his actual earlier position and rationale; the present question, posture, stare decisis and later recorded joins still require independent assessment.
