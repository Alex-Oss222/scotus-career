### Available merits paths and their limits

**Limited process path.** Recognize a child's independent liberty interest in protection against serious, avoidable injury from the manner in which the State abruptly severs an established caregiving relationship to implement a lawful custody transfer. On this record, the State cannot reject as legally irrelevant the child's request to present evidence directed specifically to such harm and practicable implementation measures. The interest constrains implementation; it does not supply a right to choose the permanent custodian, preserve the adoption or require a best-interests contest against a fit father.

The required initial protection is a meaningful, prompt opportunity for the child's representative to present concrete evidence about serious transition harm and for the father to respond before compulsory implementation. The state court must consider that issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. No full trial, court-appointed clinician, automatic evidentiary hearing, particular proof burden, number of visits or fixed period follows. The court may use procedures sufficient to the actual proffer and disputes; it need not prolong proceedings for unsupported assertions. Genuine emergency protection retains its own lawful operation, but no emergency finding is supplied here. A child's representative may not turn a manner-of-transfer inquiry into indefinite custody litigation.

This is an expressly proposed doctrinal development. Its best support is the child's own interest, direct state compulsion and separation of implementation from ultimate parental entitlement. Its strongest objection is that Smith, Parham and Flores concern different protected interests and do not constitutionalize emotional consequences of a lawful parental placement. Its proponents answer that a categorical refusal even to consider serious avoidable injury from the State's chosen implementation is distinct from guaranteeing happiness, ideal placement or private protection. They do not infer actual serious harm merely from duration of residence.

**Affirmance path.** Reject the claimed federal entitlement to additional transition process on the established record. Lawful restoration to a fit parent is not institutional confinement, and the child's bond with prospective adopters does not itself establish a constitutionally protected right against that restoration or an independent right to a preferred transition procedure. Mathews does not create the antecedent liberty interest. Serious concern about the consequences can support state-law safeguards without establishing a federal procedural right from the facts supplied.

Its strongest objection is that it allows the State to order immediate, affirmative disruption without considering a child's distinct vulnerability, although a confined process need not defeat the father. Its proponents distinguish recognition of a concrete constitutional deprivation from treating every possible harmful consequence of a lawful custody order as a new liberty interest. They reserve a properly supported claim of arbitrary affirmative bodily injury or a materially different, independently established constitutional wrong; they do not decide such a claim against the child by inventing findings or treating Pinder's immunity holding as its merits rejection.

## Entering law and supported alternatives

**A — exemption for this implementation; affirm.** The established nonstatutory exemption accommodates the NLRA's collective-bargaining regime with federal antitrust law. It protects this agreement among members of an existing, consensual multiemployer bargaining group to implement their last wage offer after good-faith impasse, where the conduct concerns a mandatory bargaining subject, arises directly from that bargaining and concerns its participants. Impasse and contract expiration alone do not terminate that protection. The conclusion does not exempt every employer agreement, every restraint described as a labor-market restraint, or conduct indefinitely after bargaining has ended.

Jewel Tea, 381 U.S. 676, supplies protection for genuine bargaining over working conditions: White's three-Justice opinion and Goldberg's three-Justice opinion give different explanations and must not be recast as a single six-Justice rationale. Connell, 421 U.S. 616, supplies the later Court's controlling recognition and limits of the nonstatutory exemption. Its denial of protection to exclusion of unrelated subcontractors from the product market does not decide the present restraint. Bonanno, 454 U.S. 404, treats impasse as insufficient by itself to dissolve a multiemployer bargaining relationship; Buffalo Linen's recognition of legitimate group bargaining and bargaining weapons is part of that explanation. Section 8(d) does not compel an agreement or concession. Auciello preserves continuing postexpiration representation subject to its actual presumptions and safeguards; it does not itself decide antitrust immunity.

The strongest objection is that collective bargaining can require concerted negotiation without authorizing employers to suppress wage competition after the union rejects a new term. A general labor-policy justification cannot make an employer cartel lawful. Path A answers only for conduct immediately rooted in genuine bargaining, on the mandatory subject of the represented employees' wages, during the continuing relationship. Having antitrust tribunals prescribe the permissible wage response at impasse would regulate the very bargaining process federal labor law leaves in operation. Neither a prior union-approved version of this particular salary term nor renewed union assent to its implementation is an indispensable condition of this limited exemption.

**B — no exemption for this unassented employer wage restraint; reverse and remand.** The exemption protects employee collective action and negotiated accommodations; it need not immunize an employer-only agreement fixing a wage never accepted by the union. Lawfulness under the NLRA does not invariably establish federal antitrust immunity, as Connell's separate federal-remedy treatment demonstrates. The particular restraint is joint enforcement of the salary ceiling through league discipline, not simply each employer's independent decision to implement an offer. Jewel Tea involved a bargained-for term serving immediate employee interests; the present term was imposed over union opposition.

The strongest objection is that this distinction exposes coordinated employer bargaining to antitrust liability at precisely the stage when the bargaining relationship and labor-law obligations persist. Bonanno establishes that impasse alone is no rupture. Path B accepts those premises but distinguishes continuing bargaining duties from an affirmative antitrust license for joint wage suppression. It does not invalidate multiemployer negotiation, collective union action, or every postexpiration term. Rejection of the exemption alone requires remand for the defendants' unresolved appellate challenges; it does not automatically reinstate the entire damages, injunction and fee package.

Neither a categorical exemption for all conduct affecting this labor market nor a rule terminating every exemption at contract expiration is adopted by any row below. No new unfair-labor-practice finding, hypothetical decertification, or finding that the NFL is a single antitrust actor supplies either path.

**A — sustain denial of excise-tax priority.** The bankruptcy meaning of tax depends on the exaction's operation, rather than the Internal Revenue Code's label alone. Feiring establishes the federal characterization inquiry and examines the statutory incidents. United States v. New York applies that method to federal as well as state exactions: an ordinary wage-base tax supporting unemployment programs remains a tax despite incentives to contribute to a state system. Sotelo likewise looks beyond a penalty label when the liability represents taxes already withheld and not remitted. None makes every regulatory exaction a penalty or every IRC assessment a bankruptcy tax.

This assessment is imposed because the employer failed its independent pension-funding obligation; the amount is a percentage of that deficiency. The payment does not discharge the funding obligation or supply the benefits represented by the separate pension/PBGC liability. Its operation is a sanction for that default, not collection of a previously existing tax or an ordinary revenue measure whose credit provisions incidentally influence behavior. Thus the disputed assessment is a noncompensatory penalty for bankruptcy priority. Section 507(a)(7)(G)'s express priority for qualifying tax-related penalties compensating actual pecuniary loss does not cover this noncompensatory assessment. This characterization alone neither disallows the claim nor subordinates it.

The strongest contrary argument is that Congress expressly called §4971(a) a tax, placed it within federal excise-tax legislation, and provided a payable federal exaction; deterrent effect does not erase a tax. ### A. State compensation standard and Rule59

## Enhanced issue comparison

### A. One proceeding with aggregate authorized imprisonment above six months

**Path A — offense-specific seriousness.** Affirm because each legislatively defined offense is petty under the full Blanton rule and joining two such offenses does not itself change either offense's legislative character. Congress's authorization of separate punishments does not without more classify the offenses as serious. The possibility of more than six months from consecutive sentences is the aggregation issue, not an additional penalty attached to either offense. The supported fine, assessment and probation package supplies no independent basis overcoming the petty presumption here. This conclusion does not foreclose a different additional-penalty package.

**Strongest contrary case.** A defendant subjected to one prosecution carrying a year's imprisonment faces the same practical loss as a defendant charged with one serious offense. Codispoti rejects evasion by dividing a serious aggregate punishment into six-month contempts. The jury's protection against arbitrary punishment might seem no less necessary here.

**Resolution.** Codispoti's actual-sentence measure answers contempt without legislative maximums; it does not erase the statutory seriousness measure reaffirmed in Blanton and actual Nachtigal. Taylor likewise addresses contempt without legislative authorization of serious punishment. Both show why their sentences matter in that setting. The present holding concerns statutory petty offenses; it does not weaken serious-contempt safeguards, authorize circumvention of an independently serious offense or decide every possible multiple-prosecution arrangement. Congress's concurrent-sentence default is supportive context, not a finding that consecutive sentences are unavailable and not an independent categorical constitutional test.

**Path B — aggregate seriousness.** Reverse if combined authorized imprisonment in one prosecution triggers the jury right and the announcement cannot lawfully remove it. This path treats the single proceeding's aggregate loss as the relevant seriousness measure, extending Codispoti's protection beyond its contempt facts. Its strongest obstacle is that Blanton and Nachtigal identify the legislature's offense-specific punishment scheme, not the prosecutor's joinder choice, as the ordinary measure. No earlier actual holding has displaced that distinction. The individual conclusions below adopt Path A; they do not treat Path B as frivolous or already prohibited by a square holding.

### B. The pretrial cap

Under Path A the cap does no necessary legal work; its efficacy is reserved. Under Path B one must separately choose between an effective advance surrender of power to exceed six months and an unenforceable judicial attempt to alter the statutory jury trigger. The exact promise supports the former argument; the absence of an identified statutory reduction or waiver, and Baldwin/Blanton's authorized-penalty rule, support the latter. Taylor's acceptance of a reduced contempt sentence cannot by itself settle the statutory-offense question. The selected commitments do not resolve this branch merely to supply a second ground for affirmance.

## Statutory and regulatory qualifications integral to the commitments

1. **The enacted comparison.** Section 360k(a) requires a state requirement respecting a human-use device, an applicable federal requirement, a difference or addition, and the requisite relation to safety, effectiveness or another federally included matter. Common-law liability can impose a requirement; it is neither categorically excluded nor automatically displaced. The actual duty is compared, rather than the negligence or strict-liability label. The Justices divide over the sufficiency and operation of the asserted federal manufacturing and warning requirements, as stated below.

2. **Separate exemption mechanisms.** Section 360k(b) permits, on State or local application and after notice and opportunity for an oral hearing, an exemption by regulation under prescribed conditions if the state requirement is more stringent, **or** if compelling local conditions require it and compliance would not violate an applicable federal requirement. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second alternative; it is not silently added to the first. Section 808.1(c) preserves federal requirements despite an exemption. No pertinent granted exemption is established. Nonpreempted equal duties do not first need an exemption. Section 360j(f)(2)'s manufacturer/device GMP exemption or variance is different: exemption requires that compliance be unnecessary to assure safety and effectiveness; a variance requires adequate proposed methods, facilities and controls, subject to necessary conditions and the statute's petition, decision and hearing provisions. No such exemption or variance is found here, and no agency-exhaustion requirement for the Lohrs is invented.

3. **Section 808.1 in full.** Subsection (b) includes force-of-law court decisions. Subsection (d) identifies specific counterpart regulations or other specific requirements applicable to a particular device before divergent state requirements are displaced. Its examples preserve general requirements whose purpose reaches other products as well as devices, including the stated commercial-law example, and equal or substantially identical requirements. Those provisions are not read without (d)(6)(ii): general adulteration/misbranding prohibitions may survive, but their application as a divergent substantive requirement for a specific device can be preempted; actual interpretation and enforcement, rather than identical wording alone, matters. The further exceptions remain intact: professional-practice licensing subject to conflicting federal §520(e)/(g) restrictions; state procurement contracts; Medicaid or similar payment criteria; general enforcement, inspection, registration and licensing subject to conflicting federal records, reporting and GMP requirements; administrative delegations; revenues and fees; and the identified atomic-energy/radiation measures until applicable specific FDA requirements intervene. None is factually applied to create an exemption here. Subsection (e)'s FDA responsibility, subject to judicial review, to determine equality or difference through the prescribed procedures does not establish an unpresented exhaustion bar. Subsection (f) preserves applicable federal duties whether or not a state rule is preempted or exempted.

4. **Manufacturing.** Original Part 820 imposes real obligations, not advice: quality systems, personnel and facilities, component acceptance, production controls and records, inspection, failures, complaints, labels, packaging and distribution have operative terms. Its scope concerns finished human-use devices; an accessory suitable for use can be a finished device, while the provision does not generally apply to component/part manufacturers and blood products have the separate Part 606 treatment. The everyday word component does not decide the lead's regulatory status. Additional critical-device duties require their own applicability basis; class III is not silently equated with every critical-device condition. Where compliance with two regulations is impossible the specific device regulation supersedes the general rule under §820.1; the exemption/variance route remains. No specific command, granted exception, violation, critical-device factual classification or substantive design approval is supplied by inference. Section 820.100's conformance requirement and device-master records do not themselves select the safe design for this lead.

5. **Warnings.** Section 801.109 conditionally exempts qualifying prescription devices from the consumer adequate-directions requirement, preserving its lawful possession and prescription/order conditions. Subsection (b)'s prescription caution and method-of-use label requirements have the surgical-instrument exception. Subsection (c)'s practitioner-use information includes indications, effects, routes, methods, frequency/duration and relevant hazards, contraindications, side effects and precautions; information **may** be omitted only if commonly known to licensed practitioners, with the written-request/Commissioner-opinion route retained. No qualifying omission is found for this lead. Subsection (d) separately governs labeling purporting to give use information, with its name-only reminder-piece exception. Subsection (e)'s issuance/revision-date requirement excludes labels and cartons. These are different exceptions, and permission is not obligation. Submission under §807.87(e) does not establish substantive FDA approval of particular warnings. No later amendment is treated as an earlier clearance condition.

6. **Savings and agency authority.** Section 360h(d) preserves liability despite compliance with an order **under that section**; it is not an unrestricted savings clause for every device suit. Section 371(a)'s efficient-enforcement rulemaking grant, together with the agency's device-regulation responsibilities and express §360k(b) task, supplies relevant authority for interpreting and administering this scheme, not an unlimited power to create or withdraw preemption. The formal §808.1 regulation receives consideration on its actual terms. Actual MCI requires statutory boundaries first; Sweet Home's six-Justice Chevron ground remains controlling; Smiley applies that sequence and assumes without deciding that courts always independently determine preemption's scope. Smiley therefore does not itself command deference to every direct preemption pronouncement. No separate litigating interpretation is automatically controlling, and neither its litigation setting nor age alone conclusively disqualifies a reasoned interpretation.

But the record's evidence that single-sex education can help both sexes, coupled with some women's capacity and interest, leaves the reservation of this distinctive public opportunity to men unjustified. The important-objective test cannot stop with the abstract desirability of diversity. The connection must account for the particular disadvantage, as his Romer reasoning illustrates at a different scrutiny level. He accepts predicted changes without treating preservation of every feature as an independently constitutional objective overriding equal access.

**Remedy comparison.** Affirmance with monitoring has genuine force: VWIL is a real program, and constitutional remedies need not make institutions identical. Yet equal support promises and a shared leadership objective do not remedy the combined loss of training, affordable degree opportunities and institutional benefits. Jenkins rejects treating installation alone as completed cure. On this approved design, further monitoring cannot by itself supply the missing substantive opportunity; that is why reversal rather than a finding of bad implementation is required. Sweatt's tangible/intangible inquiry identifies the relevant deficit without extending racial doctrine wholesale.

**Scope and joins.** Join the existing intermediate standard, present liability, and reversal of the plan assuming faithful implementation. Join an enforceable remand requiring the unequal exclusion to end, ordinarily through consideration of qualified women at a continuing public VMI unless an actually adequate lawful alternative is established. The lower court retains the work of practicable implementation and necessary safeguards, with no indefinite postponement justified by mere promises. He refuses an automatic institutional-merger rule, a permanent resource-equality decree, an inference of malicious intent, or rejection of every genuine sex-sensitive educational adjustment. No new factual hearing is required merely to reweigh settled findings; proceedings are for lawful implementation and any genuinely material remedial proposal.

## Ginsburg

**Independent result and grounds.** Affirm liability in No. 94-2107; reverse the VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 and remand for effective relief. The State's showing does not justify making sex an absolute bar to the distinctive public opportunity, and the alternative does not correct that concrete disadvantage even when all pledged assistance is credited.

**Own eligible positions.** Her authored actual Harris concurrence focuses on whether sex creates disadvantage in the conditions of the actual opportunity, rather than requiring an extraneous tangible-loss measure; that statutory workplace analysis is an individual-position anchor, not the constitutional rule here. Her actual J.E.B., Kelley and Klinger joins establish the governing sex-classification and comparator rules. Her Jenkins join, Romer join, Settle join and Lewis v. Casey join in Souter's separate writing preserve supported substantive opportunity and effective repair without automatic liability from every disparity. No later opinion or advocacy has been substituted for those date-eligible judicial positions.

**Liability comparison.** The strongest State path treats single-sex composition as a real teaching method rather than a judgment of female inferiority. She accepts the distinction and the findings that the method benefits its students. But describing the interests of women as a group does not justify excluding those willing and able to undertake this public training. The State must answer the denied opportunity, rather than direct all women to the curriculum judged suitable for most. Hogan's burden and Klinger’s benefit-specific comparison require that answer. She does not need to prove that every founder or current official acted from hostility, or that women and men have identical physical characteristics.

**Remedy comparison.** A professionally designed women's leadership program can confer substantial benefits and could be a legitimate educational project. The strongest affirmance path credits precisely that value, the per-student subsidy, ROTC, and the promised VMI network access. Its difficulty is remedial: it leaves the excluded women with a different, materially less complete substitute for the State's distinctive opportunity, including engineering access, facilities and institutional credential. Her Harris explanation supports assessing real disadvantage rather than allowing a nominal resource or benefit to end the inquiry. Sweatt confirms that educational opportunity includes material intangible advantages. The result does not require denying the alumni pledge or predicting that its members will violate it.

**Scope and joins.** Join a Hogan-based holding with its existing burden; “exceedingly persuasive” supplies no new strict-scrutiny rule. Join relief requiring otherwise qualified women to receive nondiscriminatory consideration if Virginia continues this public program without an adequate lawful substitute. Permit necessary practical adjustments; reject preserving the absolute exclusion merely to avoid them. Reserve legitimate programs directed at supported unequal opportunity and the unpresented question of private single-sex education. She will not join language judging women's interest or capacity by the typical member, excusing the present exclusion through general access to other colleges, or allowing an indefinite period of promised equality. A separate writing is unnecessary if the shared opinion states those limits and the actual injury plainly.

## Breyer

**Independent result and grounds.** Affirm the liability judgment in No. 94-2107; reverse approval in No. 94-1941 and remand for practical, effective correction. Give the supported pedagogical findings weight while applying the existing heightened scrutiny to the opportunity actually denied. The proposed alternative fails that remedial task at the level of the complete educational opportunity, not because every institutional difference independently violates the Constitution.

**Own eligible positions.** His actual Kelley and Klinger joins are direct adopted judicial positions concerning sex and educational/programmatic differences. His Jenkins join adopts effective, violation-linked educational repair and meaningful institutional discretion. He joined Souter's Romer and Settle opinions and Souter's Lewis v. Casey separate writing, including concrete opportunity, proper common proof, State remedial choice and limits on unsupported scope. These are expressly joined writings; this handoff does not invent a personally authored pre-1996 sex-admissions opinion for him.

**Liability comparison.** The strongest sustaining path credits VMI's tested success and the institutional costs of changing an integrated method. Courts need not choose their preferred teaching method or insist that educational experts are infallible. But deference is not an answer to the State's reservation of the whole unique opportunity to one sex. The accepted capable-women evidence and the absence of a corresponding opportunity make the categorical bar broader than the demonstrated educational justification supports. Kelley allows actual-fit judgments rather than categorical indifference to sex; it does not establish that resource variety alone justifies this exclusion. Settle reinforces the distinction between respecting pedagogical choices and abandoning constitutional review, without transferring its causation test.

**Remedy comparison.** The strongest alternative is conditional approval with serious monitoring, permitting different methods aimed at comparable leadership formation. He credits the actual ROTC, subsidy and alumni commitments and does not presume future failure. But this approved design's combined differences in training, available degrees, financial burden and resources leave more than harmless variation. Adequacy cannot be inferred from a shared goal or equal annual state payment. Jenkins and Casey permit necessary implementation measures once the wrong is established; they do not require a freestanding constitutional right to each facility, yet forbid general educational improvement unrelated to the denial.

**Scope and joins.** Join the shared standard, present-record liability and plan inadequacy. Join a remand with enforceable correction and an opportunity for lawful operational design; if the public VMI continues without an adequate alternative, sex cannot remain the categorical admissions barrier. Do not force the lower court to recreate identical campuses, equalize every budget figure, invent new physical standards, or guarantee equal career outcomes. He will not join a categorical repudiation of educational expertise, strict scrutiny, a universal least-intrusive-remedy formula drawn from the PLRA, or a judgment against all single-sex programs. A separate writing is unnecessary if the opinion preserves these practical and doctrinal bounds.
