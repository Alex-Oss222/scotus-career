## Event, reach and common legal ground

No. 95-6; argued January 8, 1996; proposed February 27 decision. Review of the final Illinois appellate judgment through 28 U.S.C. §1257(a). All nine rostered Justices participate; this artifact models the eight Associates only. The trial court directed statutory liability and excluded nondefect evidence under a per se rule; damages were $492,500. The remedy supported below is reversal and remand for proceedings under the correct liability rule. No ordinary FELA negligence count is presented, and no new one is adjudicated.

**H1 — duty and preparation.** The automatic-coupler requirement is an absolute equipment-performance duty, not a general prohibition of every task that places a worker between cars. Its required coupling performance presupposes proper setting for coupling. Ordinary, nondefective lateral displacement requiring preparation does not by itself establish a violation. The distinction applies to drawbar alignment as well as opening the knuckle, subject to proof of what occurred. The Act does not impose a separate duty to install automatic realigning equipment merely because a drawbar can move laterally in normal operation.

**H2 — proof and defense.** A claimant can prove a violation through failure to perform the statutory function on the occasion; identification of a particular mechanical defect is unnecessary. Once that failure is established, due care, ordinarily adequate equipment, inspection or later successful operation does not excuse it. The railroad may establish that the complained-of condition was ordinary nondefective misalignment before the appliance had been properly set. That is a distinction concerning the statutory duty and its application, not comparative negligence, assumption of risk or an inspection safe harbor. Neither party's case is decided merely by the absence of an attempted impact; no futile or damaging collision is required to preserve an otherwise provable failure claim.

**H3 — application and remedy.** The per se direction that injury during realignment establishes liability cannot stand. The drawbar's necessary lateral play is undisputed in general, but the reason, extent and legal classification of this displacement are not conclusively established. The excluded inspection affidavit/proffer, the difficulty moving the bar, the uncoupling evidence and the disputed curve inference require consideration under the correct distinction. Reverse the Illinois appellate judgment and remand; do not direct judgment for the railroad or declare that this particular drawbar was proven nondefective. Preserve any properly presented statutory-performance and causal issue. A statutory violation must have contributed to the injury within the employee's applicable FELA remedial framework; the causal rule does not eliminate the antecedent violation requirement. The reversal necessarily removes the directed-liability foundation of the damages judgment without deciding a new damages measure or ordering a fixed recovery.
