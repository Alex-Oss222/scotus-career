## Changed-premise test and correction

The provisional trial-fact reservation materially withheld part of the historical holding. A shared affirmance did not make that departure immaterial. Neither the existence of a narrower alternative nor a preference for avoiding a broad rule supplies the required changed premise.

The record, presented question, infringement concession, lower concurrences and respondents' sufficiency alternative are not materially changed from the historical matter. Tull and Terry predate the divergence. The patent authorities checked during modeling remain available at their original scopes. Actual Wooddell protects a legal action's jury entitlement, not every issue within it. Actual Gaudin reserves judicial legal meaning while requiring jury determination of a criminal element; it does not constitutionalize jury resolution of patent construction. Actual [Thompson](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Thompson_v_Keohane_merits_1995-11-29.md) distinguishes ultimate legal application from historical findings in federal habeas review, rather than prescribing a patent jury allocation. Its factual safeguards are not displaced by allowing a trial judge to determine evidentiary matters in performing an assigned interpretive function.

The completed [Cooper decision](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Cooper_v_Oklahoma_merits_1996-04-16.md), frozen at `9f59d16`, is a changed current-term authority, but its competence burden and conditional retrospective remedy do not alter the patent allocation. Its prohibition on treating invalid old findings as self-proving supports actually assessing P3; it creates no general Seventh Amendment rule. The [April 15 Miller decision](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Planned_Parenthood_Sioux_Falls_Clinic_v_Miller_merits_1996-04-15.md) likewise supplies no patent rule. No Justice-specific simulated position changes these premises sufficiently to sustain the provisional trial-allocation departure.

This conclusion does not rest on history as a command. The existing authorities support judicial interpretation of the public grant while protecting distinct factual work. Silsby's identification of components producing a combination's result concerns application rather than construction strictly understood. Bischoff preserves jury determination of identity or diversity of external inventions while expressly reserving documentary construction. Heald's and Singer's qualifications about extrinsic explanation in comparing inventions do not conclusively assign every dispute used to construe a claim to a jury. Winans separates judicial construction from factual novelty and permits expert explanation without making legal construction an expert's opinion. These qualifications defeat the claim that every technical patent question belongs to the judge, but do not establish the broader jury reservation modeled here.

A damages action's jury status is only the first inquiry. The narrower issue is whether construing the claim, including its technical meaning, must go to the jury to preserve the historical right's substance. Neither the supplied historical qualifications nor Tull/Terry establishes an indispensable jury allocation for that whole interpretive task. Documentary-construction precedent, evaluation of technical meaning against the instrument's internal structure, and consistent scope of the public grant support judicial responsibility. Uniformity and technical complexity alone would be insufficient; here they do not override established contrary historical allocation. The historical unanimous conclusion is strong additional evidence for each Associate where those premises remain intact.

## Reconciled common propositions

**P1 — Jury protection and its boundary.** A patent infringement action seeking legal damages carries Seventh Amendment protection. The jury's role in disputed infringement facts, external product identity and other properly factual issues survives. An action's jury status does not make every subsidiary issue a jury task, and technical subject matter alone does not remove otherwise protected factual issues.

**P2R — Claim construction is the court's task, including its technical evidentiary content.** The court determines the legally operative meaning of the patent claim, including terms of art informed by extrinsic technical evidence. The Seventh Amendment does not require jury resolution of a technical-usage or expert-credibility dispute embedded in that construction merely because it is genuinely disputed or has factual underpinnings. The judge must evaluate the evidence in relation to the claim, specification and prosecution history and the coherence of the whole instrument. This assignment does not declare every evidentiary proposition intrinsically legal, exclude technical evidence categorically, or decide the appellate standard for all subsidiary findings. It does not transfer disputed infringement facts to the court.

**P3 — Independent record-specific sufficiency.** Retain the original JMOL alternative. Even giving petitioners' supported technical proffer its favorable significance and assuming the jury-allocation theory they advance, no reasonable cash-only reading of this instrument performs its article-related inventory/detection function. The claim, specification and article-based prosecution explanation supply that conclusion without disbelieving the inventor, patent lawyer or president, treating all extrinsic evidence as irrelevant, or simply accepting the lower court's label. Generic cash/invoice usage, advertising, one-scanner wording and batch processing do not erase the operative article requirement. Given the parties' concession that the accused system does not infringe if inventory includes clothing, noninfringement follows. The fuller source-specific analysis under “Record application supporting P3” in the independent freeze remains adopted.

P3 remains an independently supported alternative, not a reason to retain the rejected constitutional reservation. Nor does this case become a rule that any intrinsic evidence defeats any contrary expert account. There is no need for a new jury trial or sufficiency remand on this record; a material factual dispute outside construction would require its proper treatment in another case.
