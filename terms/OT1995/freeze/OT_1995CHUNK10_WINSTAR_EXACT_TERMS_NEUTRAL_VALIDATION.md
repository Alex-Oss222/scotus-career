## Competing textual considerations preserved

**Accounting hierarchy and change language.** Winstar section 10 is not an unqualified command to use GAAP. It begins with an exception, gives conflicting agreement/regulatory/approval terms their stated priority, directs interpretation of ambiguity, and gives the Board's resolution or action priority over conflicting regulations. It also expressly refers to subsequent clarification, interpretation or amendment of governing regulations and accounting principles. Statesman section 17 similarly has exceptions and priority rules but materially different change language, the staff/AICPA references, specified proposed-rule references and a separate real-estate-valuation sentence. These clauses must not be reduced either to a complete freeze or to an unlimited cancellation power. Their relationship to incorporated transaction-specific promises and the particular alleged later-law risk remains a construction question.

**Winstar's letter.** The net-worth credit, purchase-accounting amortization of no more than thirty-five years, discount treatment and dividend waiver are distinct numbered terms. The amortization wording permits a period within the maximum; it does not require use of the maximum. Regulatory reporting and GAAP financial statements remain distinct. The letter retains ongoing records/reporting duties and reserves statutory rights and powers, but the reservation expressly excepts the described enforcement of waived requirements, insofar as waived. An account omitting either the reservation or its exception would be incomplete. The letter alone decides neither incorporation, authorized assumption of future-law risk nor every meaning of regulatory capital. Winstar's direct-credit term must not be substituted for Statesman's separately stated $26 million credit.

**Statesman's compliance clause.** Section 20 imposes material compliance and extends the obligation to the specified related entities. Its good-faith contest proviso limits what this contractual section requires while applicability is contested. It cannot be omitted when the compliance clause is invoked. Nor does the proviso establish that a contest occurred, was in good faith, satisfied a procedure, excused statutory obedience generally, or won relief. Whether this covenant allocates the particular regulatory or legislative risk remains distinct from its text.

**Duration and termination.** Winstar's ordinary two-year term and Statesman's ordinary five-year term are qualified by specifically provided exceptions, written agreement on another date and the stated liquidation-receiver/loss-of-insurance triggers. The wording does not by itself determine the duration of every incorporated undertaking or liability. Those triggers are conditions, not newly found events. Winstar section 13(b)'s indemnity and litigation conditions must be considered on their own terms; its reference to termination under section 13(a) should not be silently paraphrased as a judicial resolution of every interaction with the receiver proviso. Statesman section 19(b) addresses the reserve balance; 19(c) has litigation, continuing-covenant and recovery qualifications and expressly retains 19(a)'s terminating proviso; 19(d) addresses specified submissions actually received by termination. None establishes an applicable indemnity or pending submission in this litigation merely by existing.

**Additional section 16 verification.** The complete Statesman section 16 has more than the five-year termination sentence. It addresses defined Sales during seven years, prior written approval and a percentage of net proceeds to be agreed at the time, capped at fifty percent, with a proportional investment-return adjustment. Its Sale definition aggregates sales consummated or agreed during a ninety-day period having an aggregate sales price of $20 million. A defined Sale during five years without approval triggers the stated immediate termination notwithstanding section 19; approval may be granted or withheld in the Corporation's sole discretion. Its exclusion concerns transfers to an affiliate of the Acquirer of stock or assets and liabilities of the specifically named Federated Financial, Combined Mortgage Corporation, Homestead Mortgage Corporation or Perpetual Financial Corporation. It is not a general exception for every affiliate transaction. These verified qualifications provide context if the termination scheme becomes material; no such Sale, approval, transfer or termination is found here.
