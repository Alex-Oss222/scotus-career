## Exact common disposition and sufficient rationale

**Disposition:** Dismiss the existing review as improvidently undertaken. Leave the Illinois judgment undisturbed without affirming its federal merits. No adoption is reinstated; no new custody, turnover, transition, stay, or remand direction issues in this matter. The child's separate question and every remedy in its distinct proceeding remain outside this disposition. The Court supplies no assurance that another proceeding is procedurally available.

**Sufficient prudential rationale:** The recovered original Does petition presents whether Stanley and Lehr constitutionally required Illinois to protect this father's opportunity under its thirty-day termination rule. The assigned question instead concerns the adults' own affirmative constitutional entitlement to preserve adoption or permanent custody against Illinois' consent and unfitness requirements. Those are different questions: the Constitution's permitting a less protective state rule would not itself establish the prospective adults' right to override the rule Illinois actually applies. The published adoption majority resolves the state statutory and evidentiary predicates, and the supplied separate writings do not analyze that independent adult entitlement. Given this affirmative mismatch between the petition's questions, the published judgment's analysis, and the broader question selected for plenary consideration, the Court should decline to decide the broader constitutional question in this vehicle.

This is a discretionary conclusion, not a rule that every mismatch compels a DIG, that an omitted question heading invariably defeats review, or that a federal claim is unreviewable whenever the state opinion is silent. The authority to review a sufficiently related or exceptional question remains. Here, using the recovered Stanley/Lehr challenge as the vehicle for an independent adult-right decision would require a materially different constitutional inquiry from the one those questions frame, without an identified published state adjudication of that inquiry. The Court need not undertake that inquiry merely because the existing plenary-review premise permits examining it.

**Independence from the unknown historical fact:** The prudential ground remains available even if an unrecovered state filing eventually establishes presentation of the adults' own right. Such a filing could answer the jurisdictional question but would not change what the original petition asks or what the published opinions actually analyze. No Justice treats unrecovered papers as absent from the Court's file, infers litigant noncompliance, or applies a party's burden against the adults on that basis. The reason for declining this vehicle is the demonstrated question and decisional mismatch, not exhaustion of the operator's research.

**Jurisdiction and independent state grounds reserved:** Cardinale and Webb require attention to the same federal claim in the state record; they do not permit inventing an admission, record deficiency, or waiver. The original majority's positive state-law ground may defeat an advisory correction of the narrower federal-minimum argument. It does not defeat a properly presented independent federal attack on the validity of the state rule itself. The DIG therefore does not adopt that ground as a jurisdictional disposition of the adults' assigned claim. No family-rights merits proposition changes current law.
