## Event and proposed disposition

United States v. Chesapeake & Potomac Telephone Co. of Virginia, No. 94-1893, and National Cable Television Assn. v. Bell Atlantic Corp., No. 94-1900. Argued December 6, 1995; February 27, 1996 postargument disposition through §1254(1). Nine Justices participate; all eight Associates independently support the following disposition in both dockets:

**Vacate the Fourth Circuit judgment and remand with instructions to return the cases to the District Court to consider the effect of the Telecommunications Act of 1996, including whether a live controversy and meaningful lawful relief remain.** The Court does not now direct dismissal as moot, decide the First Amendment merits, or adjudicate the successor statute. It does not itself vacate the District Court's judgment or extend that court's old injunction to the new statute. The receiving court must determine the continuing force or proper disposition of its own judgment and any proposed amendment or relief under the applicable jurisdictional, procedural and equitable rules.

The appellate vacatur is a present §2106 disposition justified by a material intervening enactment and unresolved threshold application. It is not an automatic award of complete Munsingwear vacatur based on a mootness finding that this Court has not made. Retention of a District Court order pending the proper lower-court disposition is not a Supreme Court affirmance of its merits or an entitlement to enforce it against new provisions.

## Components and sufficient ground

**C1 — intervening law and Article III.** Section 302(b)(1), effective February 8 and already admitted, repealed former §533(b). The former restriction cannot be reviewed on the assumption it continues unchanged. Yet repeal alone does not resolve this record: respondents identify their proposed Alexandria open-video service as a continuing injury, while the Government says ordinary cable service is now immediately available over telephone facilities and any open-video dispute is materially different. Northeastern Florida's replacement-injury holding requires examining the actual continuing burden, and Church of Scientology requires examining meaningful partial relief. Neither holds that a similar subject or desired business result makes all successor disputes the same case.

**C2 — statutory and record limits.** Section 653(a)(1)'s first sentence permits local exchange carriers to provide cable service through a complying open video system; its second, addressing a cable operator or other person, contains the regulations qualification. The separate certification provision addresses reduced regulatory burdens. Section 651(a)(3) makes ordinary Title VI treatment the default absent approved certification, while §653 supplies a distinct regime. These distinctions prevent adopting respondents' assertion of an automatic six-month blanket speech prohibition as an established statutory conclusion. They likewise prevent treating permission in the statute as proof that this proposed system presently complies or that the complained-of injury is entirely eliminated. The six-month rulemaking requirement, ten-day certification decision provision and §302(b)(3)'s protection against compulsory termination of an already approved system retain their distinct conditions. No new FCC rule, approval, actual system operation, damages claim or Rule 60(b) clarification is found.

**C3 — limited remand rather than superseded merits.** The lower courts have not assessed these changes. Their resolution may eliminate the controversy, identify continuing relief within it, or show that a new or amended challenge is required. Those alternatives should be assessed before a constitutional decision. The actual new-law effect on the named plaintiffs, permissible claim scope, ripeness and remedy remain for the lower court. The remand permits lawful consideration; it guarantees neither amendment nor relief. It does not affirm the old merits judgment, invalidate the new law or import Turner's intermediate-scrutiny application to a different restriction.

**C4 — judgment treatment.** Bancorp separates §2106 power from equitable entitlement to vacatur. The Government's legislative-change argument and respondents' voluntary-surrender argument do not justify either automatic complete vacatur or automatic preservation. Congress's restructuring is neither simply a private settlement nor self-proving happenstance; the eventual treatment of a judgment after a mootness determination must apply the actual equities. For today's appellate vacatur, the enacted alteration of the very regime adjudicated, the genuine threshold dispute, and the value of first-instance assessment supply the independent judicial justification. No finding of strategic manipulation is made. No further constitutional review of repealed or successor provisions is authorized by mere investment in the original litigation.
