## Lawrence v. Chater — No. 94-9323 — January 8, 1996

### Reach, competing paths, and operative commitments

Six non-Stone Justices support granting certiorari, vacating the benefits judgment, and remanding for consideration of the agency's material intervening position. Scalia and Thomas would deny the petition on the supplied record. No Justice here determines parentage, state intestacy rights, constitutional validity, benefit eligibility, retroactive entitlement, or the validity of the agency's proposed interpretation.

The supporting ground is procedural: §2106 permits reconsideration when a genuinely material intervening agency position bears on the judgment and the lower court has not considered it, with a lawful opportunity to consider it on remand. The Court must assess that connection itself. Government preference alone does not require vacatur. The packet supplies an actual agency reconsideration or clarification potentially affecting entitlement, not merely a request for another chance to litigate. Henry distinguishes a remand for reconsideration from a final merits ruling; Alvarado shows that even a surviving alternative argument need not be adjudicated here before a warranted remand. The present order neither endorses the new interpretation nor creates an exception to exhaustion, preservation, or statutory review requirements.

**Alternative paths.** A bounded GVR permits the reviewing court to examine the new position in the first instance; its strongest objection is that a favorable judgment should not be erased merely because an agency changes its advocacy. That objection requires an independently identified material relationship and genuine reconsideration, not automatic deference. Denial preserves finality where no error has been established; its strongest objection is that it leaves a benefits judgment operating on an agency premise the agency itself has materially reconsidered, when remand can examine that change without ordering benefits. A merits reversal or award is unavailable on the supplied packet. A plenary grant would require further development of the merits questions and is not these Justices' preferred procedural disposition.

## Stutson v. United States — No. 94-8988 — January 8, 1996

### Reach, alternatives, and operative commitments

Six non-Stone Justices support a GVR of the appeal-timeliness judgment; Scalia and Thomas would deny. The supporting disposition permits reconsideration of Rule 4(b) excusable neglect in light of actual Pioneer and the Government's changed position. It does not find the late filing excusable, make the notice timely, reverse the conviction, order a merits appeal, or treat one working day's delay as categorically harmless.

Pioneer makes carelessness capable of being neglect and requires all relevant circumstances in the Chapter 11 rule it construed, including prejudice, delay and administrative effects, reason and control, and good faith. It attributes counsel's conduct to the client. Its stated bankruptcy exceptions and limits are not silently erased or transferred into Rule 4(b). The Government now accepts its relevance to the criminal rule; the unexplained affirmance leaves it unclear whether that approach was considered. These combined circumstances provide a concrete reason for reconsideration without a present merits extension resolving every Rule 4(b) question.

**Two paths.** Reconsideration is supported by the potentially important construction, changed Government position, brief delay, and unexplained treatment together. Its strongest objection is that Pioneer already existed, silence does not prove legal error, and a party's concession cannot decide the legal question. The supporting disposition therefore makes none of those inferences singly and finds no excusability. Denial respects finality and the possibility that the lower courts applied the correct rule; its strongest objection is that the uncertain legal premise can determine whether any appeal is heard, while limited reconsideration leaves the same denial available on lawful grounds. Direct reversal of the timeliness decision is unsupported because the full relevant circumstances and findings are not established. Lawrence is not used on either path.

### Component commitments

All eight non-Stone Justices would **affirm the rejection of the parole-information claim, vacate the judgment insofar as it rejects the admitted-evidence mitigation claim under the uniquely-severe-disability/crime-attribution restriction, and remand that component for lawful consideration under the actual Graham standard**. All eight reject an independent entitlement to an instruction founded only on medical evidence that was not offered. The latter conclusion neither transforms unoffered material into trial evidence nor decides a new ineffective-assistance or exclusion claim. Unreviewed Brady, lesser-offense, and other grounds remain untouched, not newly affirmed on their merits.

**P — Parole information.** Simmons's legal-ineligibility predicate is absent: Texas permitted ordinary parole. A forecast that release is unlikely is not legal ineligibility, and a jury's prediction of future dangerousness is not confined to danger after release. The claim for the Simmons ineligibility instruction therefore fails without a harmlessness inquiry. An asserted constitutional duty to inform this jury of predicted parole improbability is a materially different proposition, not supplied by Simmons or by a showing of future danger alone. The packet identifies no basis for treating that proposed new entitlement as dictated at the 1989 finality date or for a Teague exception. Rejection here establishes neither a universal ban on truthful parole evidence nor approval of every potentially misleading statement about sentencing alternatives.

**M — Admitted mitigation.** The lower court required a crime attributable to a uniquely severe disability and treated the father's nonexpert hearsay account as insufficient to prove that linkage. Under actual Graham, neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to future danger is a prerequisite to an effective sentencing route for record-supported mitigation with material independent culpability significance. Johnson confirms that admission and partial future-danger relevance are insufficient when truthful special-issue answers prevent that use. The lower court's categorical restriction is therefore not a sufficient ground for disposing of this claim. The father's lack of diagnostic expertise permits appropriate evaluation of what his evidence supports; it does not establish that no abuse occurred or that no mitigation was admitted. No diagnosis, causal medical finding, credibility determination, or actual instructional violation is made here.

**D — Unoffered medical evidence and claimed deterrence.** The evidence that could have been presented is not part of the submitted sentencing proof. Delo permits an evidentiary predicate for a requested mitigating circumstance; an instruction cannot be required on the premise that an unproved diagnosis is established. The possibility that truthful mitigating evidence may also support future danger does not itself entitle a defendant to present no evidence and obtain an instruction on its assumed content. The independent demand for that instruction on unoffered medical evidence fails. This does not establish that a defendant must present evidence formally excluded by an unconstitutional rule or that every supported challenge to an actual legal impediment is barred; those propositions are not established on this packet. A lawful inquiry into what the charge permitted jurors to do with the admitted evidence remains part of M, without adding a new ineffective-assistance claim.

**R — Review and eventual remedy.** Vacatur corrects the reviewing framework; it does not order a writ, resentencing, a life sentence, release, a stay, or any deadline. On remand, the courts must determine what mitigation the actual admitted testimony supports and whether, considering the charge, evidence, and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that jurors lacked a lawful means to give its material independent culpability significance effect. Mere possibility does not suffice; the jury remains free to give evidence unfavorable or little weight. Actual Graham's prefinality analysis forecloses treating its functional rule as newly created simply because the present case differs, but a materially new requested extension must still pass claim-specific Teague analysis. Exhaustion, default, applicable factual presumptions, and other genuinely available defenses remain; no forfeited defense is revived or settled ruling reopened. Any error-effect inquiry and eventual relief must be lawfully established. Brecht, O'Neal, and Tuggle do not already select a universal Chapman or historical substantial-injury test for this category.
