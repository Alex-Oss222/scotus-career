## Petition scope, lower orders and current funding conditions

The petition presents both questions, and the January 5 opposition requests denial of both. Q1 concerns the obligation of a participating State to fund medically necessary abortions for Medicaid-eligible women in the adjudicated rape/incest categories despite contrary state law. It is not a petition about a constitutional right to public payment for every abortion. Q2 separately challenges the blanket injunction and declaration. A grant on Q2 neither grants Q1 nor decides its statutory construction. Rule-of-Four petition action remains distinct from a participating-majority summary merits action; this handoff supplies only the eight Associate choices.

The full [District Court report, 860 F. Supp. 609](../sources/A_Dalton_lower_DC_860FSupp609.txt), and [Eighth Circuit opinion, 60 F.3d 497](../sources/A_Dalton_lower_CA8_60F3d497.txt), confirm the actual breadth and the substantial opposing rationale. The published **July 25, 1994** decree enjoins Amendment 68 in its entirety for as long as Arkansas accepts Medicaid funds and separately enjoins inconsistent abortion-funding provisions of the state plan. The **July 27, 1994** stay-denial order in petition Appendix D-1–D-2 declares the amendment “null, void and of no effect.” These are distinct orders. Bowman's footnote describing the conditional published injunction does not eliminate the separate declaration. All appellate judges accept the funding result; Bowman's partial dissent concerns remedy. His stated inclination on an unconstrained statutory question is not a holding or a Supreme Court vote.

The lower courts genuinely relied on Arkansas inseverability law and judicial reluctance to rewrite an exception. That reasoning cannot be dismissed as nonexistent. But the selected federal correction leaves the enacted words in place, subject to federal priority in the adjudicated applications; it does not compose a new Arkansas appropriation or textual exception. The inference that §§2–3 must fall because §1 has been removed loses its premise when lawful §1 applications remain. This does not resolve every independent Arkansas severability issue or give §§2–3 permission to defeat the surviving federal protection.

The Nebraska consolidation creates no second Supreme Court docket and does not import its §1983 objection into Arkansas's preserved questions. Suter and Bank One require attention to the actual statutory duty, action and relief; they do not establish a new jurisdictional defect here. Harris leaves the precise funding question open. The parties' competing readings of Title XIX, the Hyde rider, state discretion, clear notice and HCFA's interpretation remain merits arguments, not holdings produced by a denial.

## Exact reconciled remedy available for assembly

1. **Deny certiorari on Q1**, without deciding the statutory funding merits or affirming them as Supreme Court law.
2. **Grant certiorari limited to Q2.** By reasoned summary merits disposition, **vacate the judgment only insofar as it affirms invalidation or suspension of Amendment 68 beyond applications conflicting with the Medicaid obligation left unreviewed**, and remand through the Eighth Circuit for corresponding declaratory and injunctive relief. Correct the July 27 blanket declaration as well as the July 25 injunction.
3. Preserve the funding-specific prohibition against using Amendment 68 to refuse the adjudicated medically necessary abortions for Medicaid-eligible rape/incest patients while Arkansas participates in Medicaid and the applicable federal obligation requires that coverage. The federal duty cannot be evaded through another section of the amendment. Preserve §1's otherwise lawful applications and the remaining operation of §§2–3 without adjudicating an independent constitutional challenge to them or the crime-victim program.
4. Tie prospective relief to the applicable federal conflict, rather than Medicaid participation alone regardless of future conditions. An actual later legal change may support appropriate modification; no present lapse, withdrawal, automatic expiration or dissolution is declared. The court need not rewrite the Arkansas text or select a replacement funding policy.
