## Historical positions and the actual changed-premise test

Historically, the judgment was affirmed 7–2. Thomas's full opinion was joined by Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor and Breyer joined Parts I–II but dissented from the disposition through Breyer's separate writing; that formal partial join must not be converted into an affirmance vote. Stevens concurred in the judgment alone. Souter retained a full formal join while separately disputing the sentence-by-sentence characterization of §5K1.1.

All historical paths preserved Congress's Government-motion condition and the Commission's inability to override it. The majority required authorization of statutory-minimum relief in substance, not a second paper or magic words. Its Part III distinguished a different unitary system: the Commission might condition a Guidelines departure on the kind of motion authorizing a statutory departure. It did not decide whether the Commission could adopt that system, because it concluded the existing provisions had not done so and the Government had not authorized below-floor relief here.

Stevens believed the Commission intended a unitary system, but Congress did not authorize conversion of a Guidelines-only request into permission to disregard its floor. His concerns about mandatory minima did not overcome Congress's placement of that exception with the prosecutor. Breyer and O'Connor instead read the Commission's own Guidelines as conditioning a Guidelines departure on the same kind of motion needed for a statutory departure. Their remedy was remand to determine whether the actual motion was sufficient for both or insufficient for either; they did not hold that this letter necessarily authorized a below-floor sentence.

The actual Wade motion condition and reserved independent-operation question materially match the premises of these historical positions. ## Final propositions, joins and remedies

**Congress's condition:** All eight preserve the requirement of a Government motion and the Commission's inability to alter Congress's command. Useful assistance alone is not judicial initiation. Substance governs; no separate application, prescribed formula or citation of §3553(e) is universally necessary. Wade's unconstitutional-purpose and lack-of-legitimate-end review, substantial threshold for discovery/hearing, and unresolved particular remedies remain. The motion condition is not declared Article III jurisdictional.

**Affirming statutory application — Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg:** The actual request for a Guidelines departure does not authorize relief below the 120-month statutory minimum. Section 3553(e)'s Government-motion requirement concerns that statutory relief; §994(n) and the Commission's provisions cannot deem a request for different relief to be such authorization. The sentencing judge determines an appropriate assistance-related sentence only within authority actually activated. Affirm the Third Circuit's judgment and the 120-month sentence; no resentencing, particular lower term or supervised-release modification is ordered.

The strongest contrary argument is that Congress has received the Government's substantial-assistance initiation and left reduction to the judge. These six find that argument insufficient because it separates the motion from the relief Congress makes available upon that motion. Medical, remedial or sentencing-policy preferences supplied by unrelated current decisions do not change what the actual letter requested. The result does not authorize the Government to prescribe the precise extent of a statutory departure once properly activated.

**Existing Guidelines construction — Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg:** Section 5K1.1 permits departure from the Guidelines on the Government's appropriate request. Its reference to statutory sentencing under the circumstances of §§3553(e) and 994(n) preserves the statutory condition rather than dispensing with it. The current provisions do not condition every Guidelines assistance departure on authorization to go below an applicable statutory minimum. Reserve whether the Commission could lawfully create that second, conditioning form of unitary system; no holding requires its rejection as beyond delegated power.

The nonexclusive §5K1.1(a) considerations concern significance and usefulness with attention to the Government's evaluation; truthfulness, completeness and reliability; nature and extent; injury or danger to the defendant or family; and timeliness. They do not require all five. Assistance is distinct from acceptance of responsibility. The Government's assessment receives substantial weight, especially when value is difficult to ascertain, without replacing judicial judgment within authorized bounds.

**Souter's interpretive qualification:** His agreement with the disposition and operative statutory/Guidelines conclusions does not depend on treating §5K1.1's two sentences as having different direct referents. He reads both as addressing Guidelines departures, with the Application Notes, given policy-statement force by §1B1.7, extending the standards to authorized statutory departures and satisfying §994(n). The common explanation may state that those standards guide a lawful statutory reduction, while leaving the direct-text-versus-commentary route unessential. A mandatory assertion that subsection (a) itself directly governs both categories would exceed his supported agreement.

**Stevens's judgment agreement and distinct ground:** Stevens does not join the five-Justice reading of the Commission's intent. He accepts that the Commission intended a unitary system, but agrees that Congress did not permit a Guidelines-only request to authorize a below-floor sentence. His statutory judgment ground is sufficient. Do not count him for the proposition that the Commission's actual provisions intentionally create separate authorizations, or merge his view of an impermissible conversion with Breyer's different conditioning theory as a common three-Justice rule.

**Conditional remand — O'Connor and Breyer:** These Justices accept Congress's control but read the Commission's Guidelines as requiring the same kind of substantial-assistance motion for a Guidelines departure as would authorize a statutory departure. Identical assistance language, cross-referencing commentary, §994(n)'s delegated authority and the analogous Rule 35(b) structure support this reading. In that system, the lower courts cannot accept the same motion as sufficient to depart from 135 months to the statutory floor while conclusively treating it as insufficient for statutory relief without considering the coordinated requirement.

Their strongest objection is the letter's expressly limited request and §5K1.1's failure expressly to condition a Guidelines departure on below-floor authorization. They accept the letter's actual terms and Congress's supremacy; the disagreement is the Commission's authority and the proper construction of its own system, not power to ignore the statute. They would remand for consideration whether this motion sufficed for both departures or for neither. They do not establish that the letter necessarily authorizes a sub-120-month sentence, direct any amount, or adjudicate an unpresented prosecution request to increase the existing term. Any ensuing relief must respect actual preserved claims and applicable procedural limits; no new cross-appeal is invented.
