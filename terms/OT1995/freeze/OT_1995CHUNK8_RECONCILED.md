## Final component commitments

### A. Section 406 prerequisite and sponsor adoption — all eight Associates

A §406(a)(1) violation requires a fiduciary to cause the plan to engage in a specified transaction with the statutory actual-or-constructive-knowledge predicate; party-in-interest status alone does not establish that prerequisite. Section 408's exceptions remain separate, and none is found applicable here. This holding does not resolve every form of relief against a nonfiduciary after an actual predicate violation.

Lockheed and its board act in the sponsor's capacity when designing and adopting these pension-plan terms, including the release condition; that act does not become fiduciary administration or asset disposition merely because it changes prospective benefit expenditures. The statutory fiduciary definition makes no pension/welfare distinction at that level. Different funding, vesting, amendment-procedure, disclosure and other substantive restrictions remain effective. The Court does not infer actual authorized adoption merely from retained amendment power.

Implementation by the Retirement Committee remains a separate alleged act. The statutory asset-disposition branch is not narrowed by inventing a discretion requirement. All eight reject the lower court's unqualified liability conclusion without the requisite fiduciary analysis. Six then decide the benefit-payment issue on the assumption favorable to Spink that implementation is fiduciary; Souter and Breyer would leave that merits issue open. Neither approach finds every Committee act nonfiduciary.

### B. Implementation / genuine benefit payments — six decide; two reserve

**Six-Associate ground:** Paying the described genuine enhanced retirement benefits to eligible participants under an otherwise lawful amended plan does not constitute the §406(a)(1)(D) transaction alleged merely because the required employment-related release benefits the employer. In statutory context, the prohibited dealings address uses of assets with parties in interest of a different character; benefits paid under the described plan condition do not become such dealings through the employer advantage alone. Recruitment, retention and early-retirement advantages show why employer benefit from compensation is not by itself decisive. The release has value, but value alone does not make this distribution the exchange Keystone addressed: property transferred to discharge an enforceable employer funding duty. No §408 exemption, net-benefit defense or employer ownership of surplus is created.

**Required qualifications:** This is not immunity for every transaction called a benefit. Independent participation, funding, vesting and other ERISA requirements remain. A sham distribution disguising an unlawful asset transfer or a kickback is not the pleaded issue. A particular release's enforceability under the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act or other applicable law is distinct and undecided; no enforceability ruling follows merely from rejecting the §406 characterization. Section 406(b)'s different prohibitions are not merged into this question. The Court decides no loss, amount, deception or hidden diversion.

**Souter/Breyer reservation:** They agree that sponsor status cannot substitute for examining implementation and that the Ninth Circuit's reasoning omitted a required inquiry. They would not now determine whether the release-conditioned payment falls within §406(a)(1)(D), even in the bounded form proposed. Lower-court consideration and submissions addressing this technical question are preferable. They do not hold the payment prohibited, deny the statutory prerequisite, invent a missing filing, or require a new evidentiary finding as the only possible reason for remand. They refuse assent to the entire affirmative benefit-payment classification, not merely to an overbroad sentence without its qualifications.

### C. Preserved Count III alternatives — all eight

The sponsor-capacity ruling defeats §404 fiduciary liability **insofar as it rests solely on design/adoption itself**. It is not an instruction to reconsider that settled capacity question on remand. The unaddressed §403(c)(1) anti-inurement alternative and any actually pleaded and preserved fiduciary implementation theory with distinct §404 elements remain for ordinary lower-court consideration consistent with the holdings. This preserves existing alternatives; it does not create a new administrative-deception claim, infer additional conduct, revive an unpresented claim or guarantee relief.

The six deciding component B foreclose only the reviewed §406 per se release-payment theory, leaving those independent alternatives. Souter and Breyer additionally leave the implementation-based §406 classification open on their proposed remand. For any preserved actionable wrong, actual Varity, Mertens and Peacock govern the distinct cause, equitable-category and predicate requirements. Sections 409/502(a)(2), §502(a)(3) and any properly available benefits remedy are not interchangeable. None of the eight orders unconditional dismissal of all of Count III on sponsor status alone.

### D. OBRA / Counts I and II — all eight

Section 9204(a)(1) directs §§9201 and 9202 to operate only for plan years beginning on or after January 1, 1988, and only for employees with an hour of service in a covered year. Those provisions respectively amend ADEA and ERISA accrual protection. They do not require credit for the earlier period of Spink's lawful exclusion from participation. Congress's specific temporal direction controls over an inference drawn from the general prohibition of age-based accrual reduction. A smaller total benefit resulting from the earlier uncredited period does not itself establish a reduced rate of accrual in a covered period. Neither an anti-discrimination purpose nor a later calculation date eliminates that distinction.

Section 9204(b) separately limits §9203's participation/normal-retirement-age amendments to covered plan years **and service performed on or after** January 1, 1988. Subsection (a) is not rewritten to contain subsection (b)'s extra words. The supplied first applicable plan-year date is December 25, 1988; admitting Spink on that date is not a new finding of liability for the preceding portion of calendar 1988. No independent unlawful covered-period accrual claim is adjudicated.

Section 9204(a)(2)'s qualifying pre-March 1, 1986 collective-bargaining transition remains: the first plan year on or after the earlier of January 1, 1990, or the later of January 1, 1988 and termination of the last qualifying agreement, without post-February 28, 1986 extensions. No supplied allegation invokes it. Section 9204(c) permits delayed formal amendment until the first plan year beginning on or after January 1, 1989 only with compliant interim operation and an amendment retroactively covering that interval. It does not postpone substantive compliance. No unadopted Treasury proposal becomes controlling law.

The express date rule resolves the case without a default presumption against retroactivity. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg retain their actual general Landgraf/Rivers framework; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their relevant-activity approach; Breyer applies current law without an invented pre-appointment join. Agreement on this enacted temporal direction does not erase those earlier explanatory differences.

## Statute, proof use and component boundaries

The operative provision is the unnumbered **Mont. Code Ann. §45-2-203** paragraph:

> A person who is in an intoxicated condition is criminally responsible for his conduct and an intoxicated condition is not a defense to any offense and may not be taken into consideration in determining the existence of a mental state which is an element of the offense unless the defendant proves that he did not know that it was an intoxicating substance when he consumed, smoked, sniffed, injected, or otherwise ingested the substance causing the condition.

The exception requires the defendant to prove lack of knowledge that the substance was intoxicating when ingested through one of the stated means. It is not an exception for every involuntary intoxication, unexpectedly severe impairment, inability to recall events, or inability to control conduct. No separate constitutional ruling on the exception's persuasion allocation is selected, and no finding establishes that Egelhoff satisfied it. O'Connor's historical description of an involuntary-intoxication exception must be rendered in the actual enacted terms: the continued permission for unknown-intoxicant evidence undermines an assertion that intoxication evidence is inherently incapable of illuminating mental state, without enlarging the statutory exception.

The jury heard intoxication evidence and could consider it concerning memory and physical ability to commit the acts; the contested instruction prohibited using it in determining purpose or knowledge. Admission for the first uses does not establish permission for the disputed use. Conversely, exclusion for the mental-state inquiry is not wholesale suppression of all intoxication evidence. A blackout does not establish absence of purpose, and physical functioning does not establish its presence. No reconciliation finding substitutes for the jury's application to the actual evidence.

## Controlling rule support and scope for assembly
