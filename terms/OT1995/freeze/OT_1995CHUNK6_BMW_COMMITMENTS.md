## Reachability, record and entering law

The case arrives on certiorari to a final Alabama Supreme Court judgment under 28 U.S.C. §1257. The petition separately presents whether the acknowledged use of foreign transactions received an adequate correction and whether the remitted award remains grossly excessive. The final certificate, following Gore's accepted remittitur, sustains **$2,004,000: $4,000 compensatory damages and $2 million punitive damages**, with the certificate's interest and costs. The original jury punitive award was $4 million. The rejected $4 million award is not the judgment now defended.

All nine event-date Justices participated at argument and remain seated at decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal is established. A quorum exists and five votes would control a Court judgment. This stage models only the eight Associates; it supplies no Stone vote.

The adjudicated wrong is BMW NA's intentional material nondisclosure in selling a repaired car as new. Compensation and punitive entitlement are not reopened. The $601 repair, $4,000 diminution, absence of an apparent finish problem during nine months' use, and absence of established physical injury bear on the magnitude and character of the wrong; they do not negate the state-law finding of deliberate suppression. BMW AG's separate dismissal is outside the granted challenge.

The lower court found substantial jury reliance on the roughly 1,000-sales multiplier. It prohibited using the foreign transactions as that multiplier because their wrongfulness had not been established, while permitting similar-acts evidence to establish pattern and intent. It expressly said its own amount determination excluded foreign acts, considered the Green Oil factors and comparable awards, and remitted by half. Its trial court had already held a postverdict evidentiary hearing and found no bias, passion or prejudice. These are actual review and correction, not Oregon's prohibition on amount review in Oberg. Houston's broader characterization of punishment for the whole pattern is his concurrence, not an admission by the Alabama majority that it secretly retained the excluded multiplier.

The estimated 11–14 Alabama transactions and the repeated policy bear on culpability and deterrence. They are not adjudicated compensatory claims totaling an automatically available punishment base. Conversely, this case does not establish a general prohibition against considering harm to others. The nationwide evidence can help establish knowing local misconduct without authorizing independent punishment of every transaction elsewhere.

The 1993 amendment to Alabama §8-19-5 does not retroactively change the 1990 sale's adjudicated duty. Its repair threshold and other States' disclosure laws may inform the notice and culpability arguments, but establish neither immunity for this transaction nor illegality of all foreign transactions. The petition's $2,000 statutory-sanction comparison is a comparison, not an exclusive cap. BMW's proposed $56,000 calculation likewise is not a constitutional entitlement.

**Governing law:** Haslip sustains common-law punitive adjudication subject to reasonable constraints, actual guidance and review, and a rational relationship to punishment and deterrence; it supplies no universal mathematical boundary. Simulated TXO supplies only its five-Justice, record-specific procedural sufficiency proposition. Stevens/Blackmun's potential-harm reasoning, Kennedy's rational-punishment ground and Scalia/Thomas's rejection of substantive numerical review do not combine into a substantive Marks rule. Oberg requires meaningful amount review or an adequate substitute when that traditional safeguard is removed; it imposes no ratio, compulsory remittitur, mandatory written opinion, prescribed review standard or fixed number of reviewing courts. The present substantive application remains a question to decide under surviving Haslip rather than a result mechanically supplied by either later simulated case.

Shutts and Allstate v. Hague support attention to a State's connection with transactions whose rights it determines. They do not alone establish a punitive-damages ceiling or make relevant pattern evidence inadmissible. The model does not turn their choice-of-law holdings into universal immunity for all out-of-state conduct.

**Current-term additions:** Cooper requires attention to an actually invalid decision rule and a remedy responsive to that defect, but here Alabama expressly undertook a corrected amount assessment. Its burden-dependent competency findings are not a description of BMW's appellate review. Bennis's qualified defense to forfeiture transfers no claimant burden or punitive rule. Noland's examination of operative grounds rather than labels is a permissible methodological analogy, not an independent constitutional duty to give a fuller punitive opinion. Armstrong supplies neither a punitive proof threshold nor automatic further proceedings. Brown Group supplies no punitive aggregation rule. 44 Liquormart neither protects fraudulent nondisclosure nor creates independent immunity for foreign transactions. None changes Haslip/TXO/Oberg, requires a fresh jury, or resolves BMW.

## Independent judgment commitments

`Reverse/remand` below means reverse the Alabama judgment **insofar as it sustains the $2 million punitive award**, and remand for a lawful punitive disposition. It leaves the $4,000 compensatory judgment, underlying suppression liability, availability of a lawful punitive award, and BMW AG's dismissal undisturbed. It fixes no replacement amount. A new punitive trial is permitted if required for a lawful redetermination under applicable law, not commanded merely because the original jury used an impermissible measure. On the present record, simply supplying more words for the same $2 million amount does not satisfy a substantive holding that this amount is excessive.

`Affirm` means leave the operative $2,004,000 judgment in force against the presented federal challenge, with no new punitive proceeding or alteration of the certificate's ordinary consequences.

## Enhanced alternatives, individual support and join boundaries

“Hard objection” identifies a proposition incompatible with the commitment as supported here. “Formulation reservation” identifies wording or scope the Justice can tolerate or avoid without changing the recorded judgment; it is not permission to reverse a vote for coalition convenience.

### Stevens

**Selected path and authority.** His Haslip Court join accepts meaningful substantive reasonableness as well as procedure. His TXO plurality looked beyond realized damages because the proven scheme threatened valuable royalties. That rationale does not turn every repetitive commercial wrong into an unbounded fund of potential loss. Here the $4,000 injury was economic, the supported repair was modest, and no much larger injury to Gore or equivalent threatened loss is established. Repetition and concealment justify punishment beyond compensation, but the corrected $2 million remains disproportionate to the supported wrong. Foreign transactions can illuminate intent without supplying an independently punishable nationwide aggregate.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** Affirmance has substantial support: the state court found deliberate recurring suppression, invoked profitability and deterrence, and actually reduced the verdict under a system he approved in Haslip. The adverse point to reversal is that no constitutional multiplier identifies precisely where permissible punishment ends. His answer is an as-applied judgment from the whole lawful record, not a new ratio or a demand to identify the maximum in order to reject this extreme amount. TXO's threatened royalty loss cannot be supplied here by supposition.

**Boundaries and remedy.** He can join the shared substantive invalidation and a reasoned discussion of harm, culpability and relevant sanctions without making an exhaustive test. Hard objections: a mandatory compensation multiplier, automatic exclusion of pattern evidence, retroactive application of the 1993 law, or the assertion that Oberg itself determines this sum. He does not require a new jury where lawful appellate correction is possible. He could join a territorial limitation confined to the unsupported independent punishment actually proposed; a universal foreign-conduct rule is unnecessary.

### O'Connor

**Selected path and authority.** Her Haslip dissent identified the risk of punitive choices unbounded by usable legal standards; her TXO dissent also compared the magnitude to legitimate punishment and addressed prejudice and ineffective review. Those positions, together with her Oberg join, support relief when actual judicial attention still leaves the exceptional exaction insufficiently justified. The state court's recognized multiplier error is objective; a charge or personal bias need not be fabricated. The remaining $2 million is far beyond the loss, the degree of established injury, and the ordinary sanctions or comparable cases identified in this record. Deliberate suppression remains culpable, but the defendant's wealth does not fill that gap.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** Haslip's controlling approval of Alabama procedures and the present halving support affirmance or merely another state review. The strongest objection to her broader procedural position is that the full charge is unavailable and the state court already exercised corrective power. She therefore takes the independently sufficient substantive path here rather than announcing facial invalidity of Alabama's scheme or relying on a missing instruction. Her TXO preference for a new trial on its supported bias ground does not establish such a predicate in BMW.

**Boundaries and remedy.** She supports reverse/remand for a new lawful punitive disposition, allowing adequate appellate redetermination. Hard objections: treating any remittitur as conclusive, upholding this sum solely because BMW can pay it, or making TXO's ratio a safe harbor. She favors explaining the comparative evidence and meaningful constraint, but does not require a fixed dollar ceiling or ratio to join. A mandatory new jury would require an additional supported finding that correction on the existing record cannot suffice; this handoff does not make it.

### Scalia

**Selected path and authority.** His Haslip concurrence defends traditional punitive adjudication; his TXO concurrence with Thomas rejects a federal substantive right to the constitutionally correct number. His full Oberg join and separate procedural explanation require meaningful review enforcing state-law limits. BMW received a postverdict hearing and a reasoned appellate examination; the latter rejected the improper multiplier and changed the judgment. Federal review of whether a safeguard exists does not permit ordinary disagreement over the dollar amount to become a finding that it was absent.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** Reversal confined to uncorrected foreign punishment is a lawful alternative independent of a federal numerical ceiling. The strongest objection to affirmance is that an unexplained halving may preserve the original forbidden starting point, and Houston understood the award as punishment for the whole pattern. The majority, however, expressly excluded foreign acts from its amount determination and supplied state-law reasons. Houston's account does not replace that statement. Neither a further proportional reduction nor a constitutional formula is required by his due-process ground.

**Boundaries and remedy.** Hard objections: a substantive federal reasonableness ceiling, treating “500:1” as constitutional proof, or calling the actual state review nonexistent. He could support a procedural remand upon an established remaining refusal to apply the governing limitation, but that is not established here. He need not decide the broad federal territorial rule to affirm the corrected judgment. His result does not validate the original $4 million or make every appellate reduction sufficient.

### Kennedy

**Selected path and authority.** His Haslip concurrence permits investigation of arbitrariness or bias while warning that disparate verdicts and general standards are not inherently unconstitutional. His simulated TXO rationale sustained rational retribution for a demonstrated malicious scheme and rejected appellate reconstruction of an unproved potential-harm calculation. Here the state court's own findings expose the original national measure. Once that measure is removed, the extraordinary remaining punishment is not rationally accounted for by the modest economic injury and the misconduct actually established. Qualitative deterrence deserves weight but cannot justify any sum against a wealthy repeated actor.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** His respect for traditional adjudication supports affirmance: intentional suppression was found, recurrence was shown, the trial judge rejected bias, and the appellate court corrected the amount. The strongest challenge to reversal is that his Haslip approach demanded more than a large ratio. The combination here supplies more: an established impermissible original measure, a modest lawful injury, no TXO-like demonstrated larger threatened loss, and a remitted amount still far beyond a supported rational punishment. This is not a finding that the trial judge lied about bias or that all nondisclosure was lawful.

**Boundaries and remedy.** He joins the case-specific substantive conclusion and reverse/remand. Hard objections: invented nationwide potential harm; a fixed ratio; or a rule that inconsistent jury results establish prejudice. He can accept comparisons as evidence, not mandatory arithmetic. A formulation concentrating on rational punishment for the proven wrong can carry his join; an exhaustive newly mandated checklist or an independent requirement for a written calculation cannot. No automatic retrial follows.

### Souter

**Selected path and authority.** His TXO join was confined to O'Connor's jury-guidance, prejudice, review and relief portions; it did not adopt her whole comparative-proportionality discussion. His Oberg join reinforces meaningful constraints while preserving the separate amount question. Applying Haslip now, he concludes that the State's legitimate punitive judgment does not extend to the $2 million sustained on this record. The territorial limitation removes the principal demonstrated scale of the original award; the remaining evidence of repeated local fraud supports punishment but does not supply a reasonable relationship for this extraordinary magnitude. This is a present substantive application, not an assertion that his earlier limited join already resolved it.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** A solely procedural view would affirm because actual trial and appellate amount review exists, or remand without deciding excessiveness. That is the strongest objection: adequacy of review cannot be judged simply by whether one likes the result. He accepts it as to Oberg and rejects a freestanding absence-of-review ground. The preserved substantive question nevertheless remains, and he answers it under Haslip rather than disguising an amount judgment as an omitted procedure.

**Boundaries and remedy.** Hard objections: treating his TXO position as assent to an entire numerical test, importing an exclusive set of factors, or compelling more hearings merely to make review look fuller. He can join an as-applied opinion explaining why this lawful record cannot sustain $2 million, with comparisons as corroboration. A fair-notice discussion may inform the same conclusion without denying the adjudicated disclosure duty. The remedy is reverse/remand for lawful disposition, not a selected sum or mandatory jury retrial.

### Thomas

**Selected path and authority.** His own full join in Scalia's TXO concurrence rejects federal substantive numerical review and sustains traditional procedures. His full Oberg join independently accepts the right to meaningful amount review or an adequate substitute. Those positions are compatible: the State must provide the safeguard, but its exercise does not create a federal entitlement to the amount a reviewing Justice would choose. Alabama supplied and used that safeguard here.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** The plausible reversal path would identify continued foreign punishment or an ineffective correction of that established error. The defect in the original jury measure is serious, and a token reduction would not automatically cure it. But the appellate court expressly removed the foreign transactions from its calculation, examined the state factors and comparative cases, and reduced the award. No record finding establishes that it continued independently punishing the excluded transactions. Requiring a reduction in proportion to the number of Alabama sales would substitute a new federal numerical rule for actual state review.

**Boundaries and remedy.** Hard objections: substantive comparative excessiveness as an independently enforceable federal dollar limit, mandatory ratios, and importing Scalia's unjoined Oberg concurrence as Thomas's own writing. He can accept the controlling Oberg procedural distinction without that attribution. He assumes the corrective obligation for purposes of resolving it and reserves an unnecessary universal rule about foreign conduct. He affirms this corrected judgment without approving the abandoned multiplier.

### Ginsburg

**Selected path and authority.** Her Oberg dissent assesses actual safeguards in combination and preserves a properly presented federal gross-excessiveness claim. This case presents that claim, so she addresses it rather than relying only on her earlier procedural disagreement. The clear-and-convincing culpability determination, actual postverdict hearing, appellate criteria, comparison and substantial reduction collectively support the State's disposition. Recurrent intentional concealment can warrant punishment well above one consumer's loss. Qualitative state judgment need not collapse into a constitutional ratio or an exact estimate of the defendant's gain.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** The strongest reversal argument combines the modest injury, competing disclosure rules, small comparable awards and the original national multiplier. Her reserved substantive inquiry permits relief if that combination establishes grossly arbitrary punishment; it is not foreclosed by her Oberg dissent. She finds the present showing insufficient because the operative state decision expressly excludes the multiplier and rests on deliberate recurring wrongdoing, with judicial correction under substantive state limits. Houston's contrary description does not establish that the majority ignored its announced restriction. The 1993 enactment likewise does not erase the adjudicated earlier duty.

**Boundaries and remedy.** She affirms both the adequacy of correction and the remaining amount against this federal challenge. Hard objections: inferring no review from the amount alone, imposing a mandatory new tier or trial, or portraying her as rejecting every substantive excessiveness claim. Her Bennis separate dissent's reservation of substantive limits reinforces that last distinction, without importing forfeiture doctrine. She could recognize a territorial limit while concluding that Alabama complied; agreement on that limit would not move her into the reversal coalition.

### Breyer

**Selected path and authority.** No prior Supreme Court punitive opinion by Breyer is supplied. His current Bennis join supports a qualified constitutional property protection with a remedy tied to the established defect; his Cooper join examines what legal safeguards actually do while refusing to infer the result of an unperformed lawful adjudication. Neither supplies the BMW answer. Under controlling Haslip, he independently concludes that the reasons used here do not adequately constrain a $2 million punishment for the established economic wrong after the nationwide measure has been rejected. Repetition, wealth and litigation expense are relevant but do not each authorize an otherwise unsupported magnitude.

**Alternative and strongest counterargument.** Affirmance gives real force to state judicial correction and the latitude Haslip permits. A process-only remand might also avoid selecting a substantive boundary. The strongest objection to reversal is that Alabama already exercised the very review Oberg protects and need not explain an exact mathematical derivation. He accepts that objection to a pure process ruling. The sufficient ground instead is a present, case-specific substantive judgment under Haslip that the sustained amount is arbitrary in relation to the lawfully punishable wrong. No claim of a missing hearing, continued invalid standard or unperformed appellate review is necessary.

**Boundaries and remedy.** Hard objections: deriving a punitive ratio from Cooper, transferring Bennis's claimant burden, imposing mathematical precision or treating all states' different disclosure laws as a defense to Alabama liability. He can join the shared substantive conclusion and reverse/remand without an exclusive checklist or newly mandatory proceeding. His recent Armstrong and 44 Liquormart positions support attending to the actual legal predicate and justified fit; they supply no punitive standard and are not counted as earlier punitive votes. A distinctive historical BMW formulation is not used to fill the source gap.
