## 1. Citizens Bank of Maryland v. Strumpf

**No. 94-1340. Argued October 3; proposed event October 31, 1995.** The Fourth Circuit reversed the district court and reinstated the bankruptcy sanctions other than the unappealed contempt component. The challenged predicate is its treatment of the bank's temporary administrative hold as an impermissible setoff. The bank later obtained a setoff order, after which the account had been emptied. That later order is not itself under review here.

### Common operative conclusions and judgment scope

All eight Associates **vacate the Fourth Circuit's judgment insofar as it restores the sanctions on the categorical temporary-hold theory, and remand for proceedings confined to legally live, preserved matters under the following rules**. They reject both a per se stay violation from this kind of hold and an unrestricted bank power to freeze estate claims. This is a common rationale commitment, with the individual qualifications below, not eight votes for unconditional factual exoneration.

**S1 — Section 362(a)(7).** A limited, genuinely temporary refusal to pay a deposit debt while the creditor seeks a judicial determination of claimed setoff rights is not itself the setoff prohibited by §362(a)(7), where the creditor has not extinguished the mutual obligations or finally applied the deposit against its own claim. Whether a setoff occurred turns on the substance of the creditor's act and treatment of the debts, not the label “hold” or the mere absence of a bookkeeping entry. Section 553 preserves qualifying prepetition mutual setoff rights; it creates none and leaves the actual exercise of setoff subject to the stay. A completed unilateral application remains prohibited absent relief.

**S2 — Section 362(a)(3).** The deposited money is not a segregated stock of the debtor's cash in the bank's custody: the estate owns the debtor's claim against the bank. Section 542(b) expressly excepts payment of a matured or demand debt to the extent it may be offset under §553. Construing §362(a)(3) to compel immediate payment of that very amount whenever the bank temporarily withholds it to obtain the court's decision would nullify the specific payment qualification. On those conditions, the temporary nonpayment is not forbidden control over estate property. This holding does not exclude the claim from the estate, exempt tangible property under §542(a), authorize payment refusal beyond the potentially offsettable amount, or establish that every postpetition retention falls outside §362(a)(3).

**S3 — Section 362(a)(6).** A limited hold used to preserve a claimed setoff for judicial resolution, without conditioning release on payment or using the hold to pressure the debtor into satisfying the bank's claim, is not itself an act to collect, assess, or recover the prepetition claim. A coercive demand, a final application, or other collection conduct presents a different question. The present digest supplies no such independent demand; it supplies no finding about every possible bank-debtor communication either.

**S4 — Practical and statutory limits.** The creditor must seek judicial relief with appropriate promptness and retain only the amount that may qualify for offset; it may not secure an indefinite private substitute for relief under §362(d), or disregard the emergency route in §362(f). The Code's express §542(b) qualification supports preservation while the judicial process operates; the existence of §362(d)/(f) does not make prior permission an unexpressed condition of every momentary refusal to pay. No fixed five-day safe harbor, categorical deadline, or bank-created adequate-protection order is adopted. Section 363's cash-collateral and adequate-protection rules remain distinct. Plan confirmation under §1327 may matter to an otherwise valid setoff right, but its effect and the procedural availability of that contention cannot be decided from plan silence alone.

**S5 — Sanctions and mandate.** Section 362(h), as then numbered, requires a violation, injury and willfulness before its actual-damages/fees command; punitive damages additionally require appropriate circumstances. The reinstated award cannot stand on the rejected categorical theory. Vacatur does not decide that any surviving violation was harmless, that an erroneous legal view automatically defeats willfulness, that nominal damages alone prove injury, or that punitive relief follows from knowledge of bankruptcy. On remand, the court may address only properly preserved and procedurally available contentions concerning the actual timing, extent, or independent character of the hold and any available confirmation defense. It must first identify the challenged act and valid legal duty, then any injury, willfulness and sanction consequence. It may not reopen the unappealed contempt disposition or undo the later setoff order through this mandate.

### Record application and competing paths

The ordinary rule is decidable without finding a new amount, date, or confirmation concession. The bank's obligations were retained rather than finally discharged by the challenged temporary hold, and the bank sought court relief. But the materials give an October 2 hold letter and an October 7 filing alongside lower-court/party descriptions of simultaneous action. They also give a $3,500 cap and differing debt figures, and conflicting descriptions concerning the proof of claim and confirmation. Those are not converted into a judicial finding of same-day filing, a harmless excess, or complete forfeiture of the confirmation argument.

**Competing stay path:** Affirm because any restriction on the estate's use of its account is present control and functional collection, while §362(a)(7) forbids self-help and §362(d)/(f) supplies judicial relief. Its strongest point is that a creditor can obtain the practical advantage of a setoff without canceling the debt, while depriving a Chapter 13 debtor of usable money. The recorded eight-Justice conclusion answers that point through the express §542(b) exception and limits S1–S4: the right at issue is conditional payment of a deposit debt, not private seizure of the estate's physical money; indefinite, excessive, coercive, or final deprivation is not protected by calling it temporary. The Code cannot coherently preserve a possibly valid offset from immediate turnover while making every short preservation act contemptuous.

**Competing bank path:** Reverse outright with a declaration that this entire hold and every sanction were unlawful to condemn, or hold that preservation of setoff always controls after confirmation. Its strongest point is that the court later authorized offset and that the appellate dispute centrally concerns the temporary-hold rule. The commitment does reject that rule, but the later authorization does not retrospectively resolve every timing/amount question, and the present review does not establish all procedural predicates for a §1327 ruling. The limited remand supplies the bank the legal correction without deciding factual alternatives from conflicting descriptions. No new damages trial is commanded if no live preserved alternative remains; ordinary implementation may then terminate the sanctions dispute.

## 2. Libretti v. United States

**No. 94-7427. Argued October 3; proposed event November 7, 1995.** The review concerns the December 23, 1992 criminal-forfeiture sentence and the Tenth Circuit's affirmance, 38 F.3d 523. Libretti objected at sentencing to the factual basis for the forfeiture as a whole. The record is not treated as an entirely unpreserved challenge. The unpressed excessive-fines claim, Rule 32 objections, third-party proceedings and appeal-jurisdiction rulings are not silently substituted for the two procedural questions presented.

### Components, governing grounds, and formal positions

**L1 — Rule 11(f) does not require an independent factual-basis determination for an agreed criminal-forfeiture sentence. All eight Associates agree.** Rule 11(f)'s required inquiry establishes the factual basis for the guilty plea to the offense. Sections 848 and 853 make forfeiture a consequence imposed as part of sentence; the separate indictment notice and forfeiture-verdict provisions do not turn every forfeitable interest into a substantive offense element. The judge must establish the factual basis for CCE guilt, but this particular rule does not require a separate guilt-style inquiry for every property interest included in the sentencing agreement. “Punishment” is relevant to identifying the consequence but is not alone the argument: the offense/penalty structure and the distinct work assigned to Rules 7(c)(2) and 31(e) distinguish conviction from determination of forfeiture extent.

L1 neither permits an unlawful sentence nor requires the court to accept an unreliable stipulation. Section 853(a)'s proceeds, facilitation and CCE-control branches remain distinct; §853(p) requires its specified substitute-property conditions and value ceiling. The §853(d) presumption requires its two evidentiary predicates. An agreement limited to the defendant's right/title/interest does not dispose of another person's interest. The government acknowledges continuing sentencing responsibility and discretion to inquire. Those duties are not reclassified as Rule 11(f), and no statutory predicate is found merely because Libretti pleaded guilty.

**L2 — Source of the jury right.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that the property-extent determination here is a sentencing determination rather than a Sixth Amendment element of CCE guilt. Rule 31(e) provides a real statutory special-verdict protection; its existence does not make the matter a guilt element. Gaudin's jury determination of every element therefore remains intact, and Austin's punitive characterization does not import all criminal-procedure safeguards. Their holding is confined to forfeiture following the established CCE conviction under this statutory scheme, not a universal rule that every fact affecting any punishment is outside the jury guarantee. Stevens and Souter **reserve the constitutional jury question**, because their L3 statutory ground requires further proceedings without reaching it.

**L3 — Waiver of Rule 31(e).** All eight conclude that the statutory special-verdict right is waivable; none makes a constitutional right automatically disappear because a statute also protects it. Six — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer — conclude that the signed broad property agreement, represented plea, colloquy identifying forfeiture, and express ending of the trial sufficiently establish waiver of the statutory determination of agreed forfeiture. The court's failure to give a separate explanation of Rule 31(e) does not alone defeat the waiver, and Rule 23(a)'s trial-waiver requirements do not prescribe a new separate colloquy for this nonconstitutional sentencing verdict. The finding rests on assent to the identified forfeiture disposition in this record, not competence, silence, or a signature standing alone.

Those six preserve supported challenges to actual understanding, voluntariness, fraud, coercion and agreement scope. The colloquy's distinction between future lawful earnings/inheritance and concealed past enterprise proceeds addresses the reach of the transfer, and does not itself establish that Libretti expected a separate jury to decide the listed property after he accepted its forfeiture. They do not infer that counsel privately explained Rule 31(e). They determine that a separate judicial description of the right is not a prerequisite and that this affirmative agreement adequately relinquished the statutory determination of the agreed interests. Their conclusion does not authorize forfeiture beyond §853 or beyond the defendant's lawful power of disposition.

**Stevens and Souter disagree on L3.** They would vacate the forfeiture disposition and remand for an actual right-specific waiver inquiry. The source contains general guilt-trial advice and broad property language but no distinct forfeiture-verdict advice; it also contains judicial explanations narrowing the property's connection to drug transactions. That record is insufficient to treat the property agreement itself as conclusively establishing an informed surrender of the separately retained special-verdict right. They do not find counsel failed to advise, that Libretti was incompetent or coerced, or that Rule 31(e) cannot be waived. They require resolution of actual understanding rather than an inference from a general guilt waiver. They reject automatic application of plain-error prejudice to an intentional-waiver dispute and preserve the actual sentencing objection.

**L4 — Judgment and remedy.** The six affirm the Tenth Circuit's judgment **as to the Rule 11(f) and forfeiture-jury grounds presented here**. They add no independent asset-by-asset finding, no merits resolution of absent owners' claims, and no new excessive-fines ruling. Affirmance on the procedural questions leaves the existing sentence in place; it does not establish a new legal power to forfeit property outside the governing statute or agreement. Stevens and Souter would vacate the defendant's forfeiture disposition for the L3 inquiry; they otherwise reject the claimed categorical Rule 11(f) duty. If a valid informed waiver exists or is lawfully obtained on remand, ordinary forfeiture sentencing may proceed subject to §853. If it does not, the district court must determine the proper statutory jury procedure and agreement consequences. Their mandate would not automatically return all property or set aside CCE guilt, imprisonment, supervision, fine, assessment or community-service terms.

The petitioner's requested remand concerns factual basis and jury waiver; the reply leaves the consequence of inability to obtain waiver open. The government's acceptance of separate guilt and forfeiture determination is a litigating position, not a finding that this agreement is always severable. Then-Rule 11(e)(1)(C)'s withdrawal consequence applies if a plea was contingent on a specified sentence and that bargain is rejected; neither remanding Justice finds that condition established here. Third-party §853(n) proceedings are not the defendant's substitute forum, and the ineffective April 2 reconsideration does not already supply the required hearing.

### Adversarial alternatives at the material forks

**For L1, the strongest inquiry-required path** reads “judgment” and “plea” to include charged forfeiture, invokes Rule 7(c)(2)'s notice and Rule 31(e)'s separate verdict, and warns that a defendant can bargain away lawful property without any reliable nexus. That warning is substantial, especially for the expressly listed substitute distribution check and other disputed interests. But it does not make property extent a factual basis of the CCE offense. The separate rules show that forfeiture has procedural protections; they do not enlarge Rule 11(f) to every component of sentence. All eight preserve substantive authority and reliable sentencing obligations instead of deriving a new Rule 11(f) asset hearing. The contrary extreme — agreement alone authorizes any forfeiture — fails for every Justice because the statute still defines the permissible sentence and ownership reach.

**For L2/L3, the strongest full-affirmance path** treats an affirmative bargain surrendering the defendant's identified interests as a lawful stipulation eliminating the need to decide those interests by special verdict. The six regard the record as sufficient under the bounded waiver principles of Mezzanatto and Godinez, rather than a mere failure to demand a verdict. Its strongest objection is that a defendant may know he is surrendering guilt trial and agree to property consequences while misunderstanding a distinct statutory jury protection. The six answer that the actual property agreement and courtroom assent, not guilt alone, resolve that statutory determination; they will not join language making a general guilty plea automatically waive every independent issue.

**The competing limited-remand path** accepts L1 and waivability but requires a finding of informed relinquishment of the particular special-verdict right. Stevens and Souter regard Olano's distinction between intentional waiver and nonassertion, Godinez's separate actual-understanding requirement and the record's narrowing colloquy as sufficient reasons not to treat the broad writing as conclusive. Their strongest counterargument is that the right is statutory, no rule commands a separate advisement, and normal sentencing stipulations would become unstable if every procedural consequence had to be recited. They therefore require no universal ritual or proof that counsel discussed every rule: their remand is tied to this separately retained verdict protection and the actual dispute over what the agreed forfeiture encompassed. Neither finds a new statutory asset defect from unread evidence. The constitutional question can remain reserved on their path.

## 3. American Life League v. Reno

**Assigned merits event November 13, 1995.** The authorized vehicle is merits review of the Fourth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal with prejudice under Rule 12(b)(6), not a historical petition action. No grant, argument date, Supreme Court merits docket, private petition tally or additional order is invented. Historical-source petition No.94-1867 identifies research materials only. The lower appeal is No.94-1869, 47 F.3d 642; district No.94-700-A.

### Material text and pleading premises retained in every commitment

Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force, or physical obstruction and intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt. It separately reaches acts **because** someone is or has been obtaining/providing reproductive health services and acts **in order to intimidate** that person, another or a class from obtaining/providing them. Interference means restricted freedom of movement, intimidation reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to self or another, and obstruction impassable or unreasonably difficult/hazardous passage. Section (a)(3)'s challenged branch covers intentional reproductive-facility property damage/destruction or attempt because of the services. The defined services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services relating to reproduction, pregnancy and termination; they are not confined to abortions or a single viewpoint.

The parent/legal-guardian exception removes **any penalties under the section** for the described conduct directed **exclusively at that minor**. It is neither a general secular-obstruction exception nor a nonexistent qualification. The constitutional and RFRA analyses below explicitly retain it. The worship provisions exist but are outside the presented issues.

The criminal, government civil and private-remedy schedules remain distinct. The private eligible claimant **may elect** $5,000 per violation before final judgment **in lieu of actual compensatory damages**; this does not mandate that award in all cases or replace every other remedy. The amount's reasonableness, actual number of violations, particular liability, punitive damages and criminal sentence are not before the Court. Section 248(d) preserves constitutionally protected expressive conduct, including peaceful demonstrations/picketing; it is a construction limit, not a premise that every covered act is already unprotected. Severability extends to provisions and applications. The enactment covers conduct from May 26, 1994.

The pleaded future conduct includes prayer, counseling, demonstrations and peaceful physical obstruction. “Peaceful” does not establish unobstructed passage, and the plaintiffs are not convicted violent offenders. The circuit assumed a substantial religious burden; the government disputes that threshold. No Justice resolves sincerity, doctrinal necessity, an individual's exact physical method, or private enforcement history as a fact. The RFRA alternatives below expressly assume the pleaded substantial burden for purposes of evaluating the dismissal.

RFRA supplies a separate claim: after substantial burden, the government bears both production and persuasion that applying the burden **to the person** furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means. Later federal law is subject unless it expressly excludes RFRA by reference; FACE does not. A constitutional neutral-law conclusion and intermediate speech scrutiny do not satisfy RFRA by substitution. Ordinary standing, appropriate relief, belief protection and the Establishment Clause remain.

### Shared constitutional and statutory conclusions

**A1 — Commerce power: all eight reject the presented commerce invalidity claim.** The challenged provisions directly protect the provision and receipt of medical services and the facilities through which those services are supplied against targeted coercive obstruction, violence and destruction. Congress regulates interference with an actual interstate services market, supported here by the reported legislative evidence of interstate patients, personnel and supplies and actual disruption/closure of service. The direct relation to service transactions differs from the remote chain from stand-alone school firearm possession through education and productivity rejected in the controlling Lopez holding. The legal object is not converted into noneconomic activity merely because the obstructing person's purpose is ideological or his act is local.

The common sufficient ground is the substantial commercial relationship of this regulated class, within the bounded economic-class reasoning retained in Lopez and applied in Harris. No individual crossing or amount is required by an element that Congress did not enact. Harris's actual vehicle-history element remains essential to that statute; it is not made a universal condition for all class regulation. Robertson's direct-enterprise holding confirms the significance of actual commercial inputs, labor and output in its own setting, but does not itself classify every protester as an interstate enterprise. The present common ground neither adopts an exhaustive commerce formula nor validates every local offense with remote economic costs. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their broader Lopez dissenting substantial-effects view, but join this narrower sufficient ground.

The alternative persons/things-in-interstate-commerce theory is not needed to cover every statutory application. No assertion that some patients cross state lines is transformed into a universal individual nexus. Because the commerce ground suffices, all eight reserve the unadjudicated Fourteenth Amendment §5 alternative. Direct regulation of private conduct does not command state legislation or administration; all eight reject the independent Tenth Amendment objection on that bounded basis, without diminishing New York's anti-compulsion rule.

**A2 — Content/viewpoint classification and expressive obstruction: all eight reject the presented facial speech invalidity claim on a common conduct-based, intermediate-scrutiny ground.** The service-related motive elements identify the conduct's target and the access-related harm; they do not require an antiabortion message, religious belief, or hostility to a particular viewpoint. An actor can violate the relevant branch without communicating an idea. Political disagreement, criticism, prayer, counseling and persuasion alone do not meet the physical-act and harm definitions. A statute selecting the content of protected expression would not become neutral through a benign label; this statute's selection is tied to interference with medical access and service facilities.

Mitchell permits legally relevant motive to accompany independently proscribable conduct and protects abstract belief; it does not by itself establish that every peaceful obstruction is unprotected. For potentially expressive but physically obstructive conduct, the O'Brien/Ward inquiry therefore remains necessary. Protecting access to medical services and persons from physical restriction or apprehended bodily harm is substantial and unrelated to suppressing their opponents' message. The act directly advances that interest by addressing impassable or unreasonably difficult/hazardous access, force, threats and intentional destruction, rather than requiring listeners to accept counseling or banning advocacy throughout a broad area. Its definitions, intentional-act/motive predicates and protected-expression limitation confine the burden to that harm. Nonobstructive demonstration, prayer, signs, leafleting and willing conversation remain usable; no numerical buffer, advance invitation rule or compulsory silence is imposed here.

This is ordinary statute review, not automatic adoption of Madsen's heightened injunction standard or its distances. Turner requires review of actual selection and fit, with supported legislative predictions receiving deference rather than conclusiveness. Here the relevant connection between a physical blockade and denied access is direct; the constitutional conclusion does not depend on an unverified numerical legislative statistic or a forecast that all clinics will fail. The Act does not compel private speakers to include another message, as in Hurley. It preserves speech while regulating physical interference; the right to refuse another's message does not include physical dominion over that person's route to a medical service.

The comparison with labor or environmental protest does not establish a statutory exemption for those views. Their conduct falls within FACE only if the enacted service-related purpose and other predicates are established; a different motive or object may fall outside. Congress's selection of this physical service harm differs from selecting favored messages for scarce editorial space. The limited parent/minor exception is relational and confined to acts directed exclusively at the actor's own minor; it does not select abortion viewpoints or permit a general blockade against other service-seekers. These classifications answer the facial speech challenge, without deciding discriminatory enforcement or every mixed-motive application.

**A3 — Vagueness/overbreadth: all eight reject the presented facial claims.** The actual passage, movement and bodily-harm definitions, coupled with the required intentional conduct and service-related purpose, supply ascertainable conduct boundaries and do not criminalize mere offense, resentment or successful persuasion. That differs from the substantial protected coverage remaining in the controlling R.A.V. decision. Section 248(d)(1) reinforces a textually available limitation; it does not save any future application that actually punishes protected advocacy. True force threats remain distinct from harsh political hyperbole. The Court need not prescribe a new universal true-threat mental-state rule or decide an uncharged speaker's words. Speculative use of protected statements as motive evidence does not establish substantial overbreadth under Mitchell, while relevance and the bar on punishment of abstract belief remain.

**A4 — Private statutory damages: all eight reject the categorical objection.** Congress may provide elected violation-based statutory damages without requiring proof of the same amount as actual compensatory loss; Claiborne does not categorically forbid that remedy. Liability must still rest on this defendant's legally attributable violation, not shared views, membership, another person's acts, or protected persuasion that reduces demand. An injunction's actual-concert doctrine is not a substitute for the liability predicates of a damages claim. The holding neither decides whether $5,000 is reasonable nor imposes an award, counts violations, validates punitive damages, or measures damages from a mixed lawful/unlawful campaign. The statute's election is permissive and replaces actual compensatory damages, not automatically every form of relief.

**A5 — Constitutional religion, with a genuine scope difference.** All eight reject the facial claim that the statutory text establishes religious targeting: its predicates concern acts and service access, not the actor's religion, and religious prominence among opponents is not an objective targeting finding. The Act does not create a general exemption for comparable secular blockades while excluding religious ones. The parent/minor qualification must be considered; it concerns an exclusively parent/guardian-to-own-minor relation rather than collective outsiders' obstruction of others' access. That limited distinction does not itself establish the substantial secular underinclusion presented by the Lukumi scheme.

Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally reject the asserted constitutional exemption under the governing Smith/Lukumi neutral-law rule on the pleaded frame. O'Connor and Souter **reserve the broader constitutional substantial-burden exemption question** in light of their respective Lukumi positions and the statutory remand they support. They can join the rejection of demonstrated facial targeting and the speech holdings without endorsing Smith's full account. Neither adopts a new constitutional exemption or overrules Smith. No Justice's RFRA position is obtained by collapsing it into this constitutional component.

### A6 — RFRA: two supported dispositions

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would vacate the dismissal of the RFRA prospective-enforcement claim and remand for the separate statutory inquiry.** They assume the sufficiently pleaded substantial burden for purposes of the dismissal review, without establishing it at trial. Under their own Clearwater and Swanner commitments, a general invocation of access, health and safety, coupled with a successful intermediate-scrutiny speech analysis, does not by itself demonstrate least restrictive means for these persons. The present dismissal treats the intended physical obstruction at a general level; it does not resolve the plaintiffs' actual claimed religious conduct, the legally material application and accommodation possibilities, or the significance of the statute's own parent/minor qualification under RFRA's more demanding test.

Their remand does not grant a right to block a clinic, require an exemption that deprives another person of service, diminish the compelling weight of reliable medical access and bodily safety, or force patients to travel to another clinic. The government can show that a proposed accommodation would leave precisely the harm Congress may prevent. But it must carry the specified burdens for the persons and conduct at issue. The district court must identify which claimant and threatened application presents a live claim, determine substantial burden if genuinely contested, and if necessary require the government's claimant-specific compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means showing. It must consider concrete alternatives and any legally comparable exemptions without deciding religious truth or presuming that commerce strips protection. A mere desire to persuade rather than physically obstruct may fall outside the prohibition, but it may not be attributed to a claimant in place of the pleaded burden.

**Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the RFRA dismissal on the bounded assumed-burden application and thus affirm the entire judgment within the presented scope.** Their own Swanner dissent joins distinguish a general statutory label from an application that necessarily imposes the protected injury on an identified third party. Assuming the alleged religious obligation is to engage in the statutory physical obstruction, the requested immunity would leave other service-seekers facing impassable or unreasonably difficult/hazardous access because of the objector's religious decision. Allowing prayer, counseling, demonstrations and persuasion without that physical restriction preserves communication; permitting the very obstruction would remove the access protection in the affected encounter. Notice, referral to another facility, or reliance on a patient's ability to find another route does not preserve the same protected access. Those are reasons about this asserted exemption, not a declaration that entering commerce forfeits RFRA.

The three treat the parent/minor exception as materially different for this requested outsider obstruction: it is restricted to the parent's or guardian's own minor and cannot cover interference with other patients, providers or a general entrance. They do not decide all legal powers within a family, validate abuse, or declare a religious parent unprotected by RFRA. The limited relational exception does not supply a comparable secular entitlement to impose these plaintiffs' alleged blockade on others. Their conclusion applies to the assumed request to perform the defined obstructive conduct; it does not foreclose a materially different supported religious practice or an enforcement threat against nonobstructive expression. They find the direct statutory harm and the requested exemption sufficient on the pleading assumption, without finding each person's sincerity, exact acts, violence, interstate travel or historical guilt. They retain the government's production and persuasion burden and do not impose on claimants a universal duty to prove an alternative.

### Enhanced alternative-path tests

**Commerce.** The strongest invalidation path treats the regulated activity as local ideological protest or ordinary crime without an individual commerce element, and views aggregate medical-market consequences as Lopez's forbidden causal chain. It properly resists a federal police power and judicial addition of a missing element. The common eight-Justice answer identifies a direct target — service provision/receipt and the facilities providing it — rather than a remote general productivity effect. Harris preserves class regulation within an actual economic setting; its particular enacted nexus is not a freestanding rule for all statutes. The conclusion does not decide the §5 alternative, or use a reported finding as conclusive proof of every application. A broader rationale that any locally committed offense with medical costs is commercial would lose the Lopez-majority Justices' joins.

**Speech.** The strongest invalidation path compares the covered service-related motives with other motives producing identical obstruction and invokes Turner/R.A.V. against selective suppression of antiabortion expression. It is especially substantial for peaceful expressive obstruction: Mitchell's independently criminal battery does not answer that issue by label. The common answer evaluates the physical target and defined harm, both service-related motive branches and the availability of protected advocacy; it applies intermediate scrutiny rather than declaring all protest conduct outside the First Amendment. No protected message is required to violate the law, no disagreement is itself punishable, and the qualifying physical access harm supplies the fit. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas's R.A.V. separate theory would itself preserve genuine conduct and category-related injury distinctions; their present join does not make that theory controlling. A proposed rule that motive is never constitutionally significant, or that a savings clause alone defeats overbreadth, would not retain these joins.

**RFRA.** The strongest affirmance path is the three-Justice application above: a religious entitlement to cause the defined access deprivation leaves the protected person bearing the objector's choice, and suggested nonblocking alternatives preserve expression without sacrificing access. The five remanding Justices accept the force of that showing in a properly identified application but reject treating it as complete at the generalized dismissal stage. Their Swanner commitments preserve an application-specific inquiry even when the law protects important direct third-party interests. The strongest objection to remand is that it becomes a mandatory fact hearing whenever a claimant says religion requires the prohibited injury. The five adopt no such rule: a claim can fail on its actual pleadings and legally sufficient government showing, and a genuine substantial-burden failure ends RFRA without compelling-interest review. They conclude only that this dismissal record and reasoning do not complete the governing statutory task. No preliminary or permanent injunction follows automatically from their remand.

The three affirming Justices confront the contrary Swanner majority rather than substituting their dissent for its law. They apply its required person/conduct inquiry on an express assumption and find the requested immunity logically defeats the protected access in that encounter. They reserve materially different accommodations and enforcement threats. Their disagreement with the five concerns the sufficiency of this pleading-level application, not RFRA's force, burden sequence, or the existence of an obligation to consider material exceptions.

## Governing law and the two principal paths

**Original responsibility:** Mississippi v. Louisiana establishes this Court's exclusive jurisdiction over the interstate controversy and preserves distinct private-title litigation; it previously decided no geographic merits. Kansas supplies independent examination of a Master's work with appropriate weight for technical and witness assessment, not an appellate Rule 52(a) command or conclusive adoption of an earlier district judgment. It does not impose its Compact's factual elements or a universal proof quantum here. Nebraska likewise separates claims, findings and final relief; its enforcement/modification burdens are not the burden for Stack Island.

**Path R — accept the supported report within a stated legal rule:** A boundary established on one side of a continuing island does not switch to the other merely because the main navigation channel changes sides. Missouri supplies that island-side rule. Indiana confirms that altered river geography and eventual channel filling do not alone transfer an established island boundary, while its low-water cession remains distinct. The Arkansas decisions establish that an old boundary channel may continue to change by gradual erosion/accretion while it remains a running stream, even after another channel carries the main flow; when the old channel ceases running and becomes stagnant, its boundary fixes and subsequent filling does not continue accretion across that line. Applying that latter principle to a divided island channel preserves the original boundary side without falsely treating the navigation shift itself as a proven sudden new-bed avulsion. It is the governing channel's continued running and later cessation, not the word “frozen,” that distinguishes the two stages.

The strongest objection to Path R is Louisiana's combination of alleged breaks in island continuity, hydrographic/elevation objections and a proposed line far from the live channel. Those objections could matter: disappearance, a different origin of southern formations or an inaccurately located former boundary channel cannot be answered by a place name or a general preference for stable borders. R supplies a reasoned contrary determination for each, including a mean-high-water alternative and an explanation of the surveyor's changed line. The accepted result below rests on those reasons, with the coordinate inconsistency reserved; it is not an independent reading of unread exhibits.

**Path L — sustain an exception and return for a new hearing or different boundary:** A demonstrated legal requirement that the channel fixed immediately at the navigation shift, a supported finding of total island destruction, or a failure to locate the former boundary channel would defeat all or part of the recommended boundary. The strongest basis is the technical specificity of L's objections, including the asserted 1883 overlay, 1948 depths, farmland and the surveyor's earlier different line. The decisive objections to this path on the validated record are that it conflates the live navigation and boundary channels, does not overcome R's separately explained continuity/formation determinations merely by citing technical credentials, and does not identify an additional opportunity that was denied in the full supplemental process. Independent review permits rejection of a report; it does not require a new trial whenever expert evidence conflicts. No Justice treats the historical district judgment or the Master's prestige as dispositive.

**Acquiescence is a separate alternative, not a substitute finding:** M argues prescription and sovereign acquiescence, and L disputes the necessary official conduct and occupation. R makes no recommendation on that basis. Indiana demonstrates why genuine sovereign conduct and long acquiescence may matter; it does not establish those facts here. Because the selected island/channel ground suffices, all eight decline that independent alternative. No adverse-possession judgment among private persons is treated as binding either State's sovereignty.

## Component conclusions and bounded rule adopted

### Exception A — movement of the boundary channel

Overrule. The island-side boundary stays in the original boundary channel when the main navigation channel shifts to the other side, but erosion/accretion can move that still-running boundary channel. It fixes when that channel ceases running and becomes stagnant, rather than at the earlier change in the preferred navigation route. The Arkansas avulsion cases support the running/stagnant distinction; Missouri supplies the island-side premise. Their conjunction does not declare the navigation shift an avulsion or allow any former Mississippi land to migrate across an active boundary channel without legal consequence.

### Exception B — alleged disappearance

Overrule at the scope of R's supported continuity determination. The early-history alternatives lead to the same 1881 Mississippi-side origin under the expert recognition identified in R pp.14–15; no finding that the original pre-statehood island unquestionably survived every earlier event is necessary. For 1883, R pp.19–21 does not rest solely on the new exhibit's questioned provenance: it also evaluates the navigation light, published contemporary map, sworn 1885 accounts of residence/cultivation since 1882 and the Commission's 1883 work description. For 1948, R pp.21–23 identifies vegetation at the corresponding location and the 1949 elevation evidence, as well as the map sequence and disputed absence of precise elevations. The report's finding of continuity is accepted on that cumulative basis, not a newly measured depth or the false proposition that Louisiana offered no hydrographic evidence.

The disagreement between R's 1949 elevation reference and M's 1948 description remains unresolved. No holding relies on converting the 1949 point into a proved 1948 observation. The finding adopted is continuous identity under the report's cumulative reasoning, not an independent modern reconstruction or a rule that overlapping outlines invariably prove survival.

### Exception C — island definition

Overrule on the report's **assumed mean-high-water alternative**, without adopting a universal practical-permanence definition for every river landform. R pp.22–24 expressly assumes Louisiana's mean-high-water formulation in testing this record and finds no convincing significant failure under that assumption. The report also supplies other continuity evidence; labels alone would not suffice. Because the same result follows within that assumed framework, the Court need not decide whether public-land, tidal or surveying definitions mandate the rule for every interstate island dispute. This disposition does not hold that any wet shoal qualifies as an island, that mere recurrence at a former location is identity, or that a physical loss of all relevant land could never matter. “Convincing” in the report is not adopted as a new universal clear-and-convincing proof burden.

### Exception D — Smith line, south-end formation and location

Overrule the challenge to R's identified former-channel boundary recommendation, while reserving the exact implementing schedule. R pp.25–28 finds the southern sandbars on the Mississippi side of the boundary channel before they merged with the continuing island and before the old channel ceased carrying flow. Its explanation concerns the position of the confluence, not a general claim that every downstream deposit belongs to the island. It distinguishes Smith's use of another surveyor in the earlier litigation from his own later determination. The claimed distance from the present navigation channel does not, without more, locate the former boundary channel or disprove it. No new geometric measurement or ruling on the identity of a particular farmer's parcel is made.

The line merits are adopted as the old boundary channel identified by R, subject to verification of the inconsistent call before a precise decree. Neither Appendix A nor E is incorporated now as an operative coordinate schedule. This limitation is not a finding that the whole report lacks technical evidence.

### Exception E and new-hearing petition

Overrule E's legal/technical-treatment objection for the preceding reasons and separately deny the requested new trial of the supplemental hearing. R pp.9–11,17–28 describes independent consideration of the stipulated earlier record, extensive additional testimony and exhibits, cross-examination, argument and two site views at different stages. Louisiana's objections receive independent examination; a disagreement about what the technical proof establishes is not itself denial of an opportunity to present it. R's specific documentary and expert grounds, including its assumed-test analysis, answer the accusation that it simply reinstated a jurisdictionally defective district judgment or relied on names alone. No general exclusion of expert testimony or categorical preference for lay witnesses is adopted.

### Private-title cancellation

Deny Louisiana's prayer to cancel the Houston Group's claim of title, solely in the controversy Louisiana presents. M p.31 identifies the absence of an exception, and L's enumerated exceptions and prayer concern the boundary. R pp.28–32 rejects the disappearance premise and explains that Louisiana, a stranger to these Mississippi titles, cannot obtain cancellation by asserting unidentified other persons' supposedly superior claims. Accept that limited ground and disposition; do not independently decide the alternate federal-patent presumption, laches, deed-description merits or every southern riparian title. No affirmative decree quiets title against absent owners. Mississippi v. Louisiana preserves their otherwise lawful separate claims and distinguishes ownership from sovereign boundary jurisdiction.

### Exact decree implementation

Support an interlocutory disposition of the exceptions and cancellation prayer, retain original jurisdiction, and require an authenticated consistent proposed boundary schedule before the implementing decree. The official report prints Point 3 as **32°48′47″ N, 91°09′37″ W** in Appendix A and **32°49′47″ N, 91°09′37″ W** in Appendix E. The Court cannot enter both or silently choose one. The needed source is the cited survey exhibits P-32D, LA-1A, P-32E and/or Mississippi counterclaim paragraph 5, or an authenticated correction that resolves the discrepancy. The later December 4 event must await that verification. Arkansas (1970) and Indiana illustrate the lawful separation of a decided boundary principle from the survey requiring subsequent Court approval; they supply no substitute coordinate here.

This is an exact implementation blocker, not a reason to turn every independently reviewable exception into an open evidentiary trial. No coordinate, correction, injunction against an identified private parcel, allocation of costs or final closure is invented.

## Freeze and later-stage limits

These commitments permit a merits disposition of the October exceptions at the bounded scope above. The exact coordinate decree remains stopped for the named authentication. An expanded finding that an actual map is forged, an elevation is precisely established in a particular year, a specific claimant's property lies across the boundary, or an acquiescence element is independently proved would require the particular underlying exhibits and testimony; no such conclusion is supplied here. No later historical decree may repair this gap by importation.

The original independent bytes remain frozen when reconciliation begins. A new material record fact must return through sanitized validation/model refresh; limited research alone is not a changed in-world premise. Historical comparison and Stone compatibility remain later stages.
