## Exact common propositions and comparator qualifications

**I — Review and scope: all eight.** Retain the independent §1257(a)/Cox first-category analysis: the final preclusion ruling practically concludes Richards and Hill's remaining federal claims; implementation is preordained despite the state interlocutory label. The separate federal-employee Buck Act proceedings do not leave these petitioners a merits proceeding that could eliminate their objection. This is an express analysis supported by the frozen orders and Cox materials, not a finality holding attributed to the historical Richards opinion's silence. *Casarotto*'s fourth-category arbitration route and *Quackenbush*'s §1291 routes remain distinct. Review reaches nonparty due process, not tax constitutionality or Buck Act merits.

**II — Constitutional binding prerequisite: all eight.** Ordinary state preclusion autonomy remains, subject to the constitutional limit on an extreme application denying a real opportunity to be heard. Shared objectives, vigorous advocacy, resources and the public importance of the legal question do not alone establish authority to adjudicate absent persons' legal rights. Preserve legitimate privity such as guardian/ward or trustee/beneficiary relationships; genuine adequate representative litigation; control-based binding; and special remedial arrangements such as bankruptcy or probate that foreclose successive litigation consistently with due process. Neither finality policy nor the label “virtual representation” supplies the missing relationship.

The historical notice qualification is retained precisely: **assume without deciding** that in some class or representative suits adequate representation might cure absence of notice. Even on that assumption, the representative basis offered here is insufficient. No universal rule either requires individual notice in every representative proceeding or makes representation an automatic cure. Conversely, mere notice does not itself establish authority or impose a general duty on strangers to intervene. The independent freeze's refusal to find actual nonnotice or every conceivable absence of control remains unchanged.

**III — Application, genuine public rights and supporting property ground: all eight.** These petitioners were nonparties to a nonclass case. The supplied lower-court basis was essentially identical interests, reinforced by public fiscal finality; respondents add the City's resources and incentive. That rationale does not constitutionally establish representation of petitioners' personal tax obligations and claims for paid sums. The decision need not find that the earlier litigants argued badly, adjudicate every connection, or transform petitioners' assertions about what the prior pleadings purported to represent into additional appellate facts.

The historical discussion of the Birmingham finance director does not support a blanket rule about all public officials. Whether public officials always adequately represent constituents' personal rights remains reserved. Nor can supposed representation of city taxpayers, without more, be treated as established representation of these county taxpayers. The present record need not be supplemented to reject the rationale actually used, and no affirmative finding of an undisclosed authorization or control relationship is made.

Preserve the distinction between (a) a taxpayer's challenge to misuse of public funds or public action affecting that taxpayer only indirectly and (b) a challenge to the State's direct levy of the taxpayer's own funds. As to the former, assume wide state latitude over standing and limits on repeated proceedings. This case involves the latter. Genuine public-right representative judgments remain possible; absence of Rule 23 treatment or inclusion of refund relief is not independently a universal invalidating test. The Constitution does not import every federal class-action requirement into state procedure.

The historical opinion's additional property reasoning remains express support: when an individual claim is otherwise available, extinguishing that personal chose in action implicates its protected status under *Logan*, as well as the opportunity to challenge the direct levy described in *Brinkerhoff-Faris*. The identified Alabama rationale does not establish an exclusive public-action remedial scheme; shared interests and revenue reliance cannot be converted into one. This reinforces the same due-process ground, not a tax-merits holding, a new cause of action, or a finding about every feature of Alabama law. Reliance on precedent in rejecting a later claim is distinct from conclusively binding its owner to another person's judgment.

**IV — Issue-preclusion alternative and bounded remedy: all eight.** Calling the proposed bar issue preclusion does not cure the logically prior absence of a constitutionally sufficient basis to bind these nonparties. Retain the independent rejection of that narrower defense without deciding whether the exemption issue was actually and necessarily determined. A pleaded theory, an issue that could have been litigated, and an actually necessary determination remain different predicates. This express treatment follows the frozen alternative and shared constitutional ground; it is not a claim that historical Richards separately adjudicated the exact issue-preclusion predicate.

Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court's dispositive preclusion judgment as applied to the reviewed Richards-Hill federal claims and remand to that court for consistent further proceedings. Do not reduce the disposition to bare reversal, order immediate relief on the tax, or replace it with a mandatory new representative-adequacy hearing. Ordinary legally available defenses and remedy conditions remain, but the mandate guarantees neither discovery nor an opportunity to construct a new preclusion theory. It reopens no state-count ruling, earlier Tax Injunction Act dismissal, properly binding judgment, or separate Buck Act claim. No damages, refund, injunction, tax invalidation, special deadline, or entitlement to eventual recovery is decided.
