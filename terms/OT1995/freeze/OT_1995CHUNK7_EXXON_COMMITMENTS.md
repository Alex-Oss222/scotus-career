### I. Review, reachability and established premises

The reviewed Rule 54(b) judgment, affirmed by the Ninth Circuit, supplies the appellate controversy. The packet establishes no jurisdictional or participation defect. The submitted questions encompass the legal adequacy of the tort and warranty causation grounds, including whether the accepted facts and phase-one assumptions permit their resolution. They do not encompass a free-standing ruling that bifurcation was improper. Supreme Court Rules 14.1(a) and 24.1(a) preserve that distinction. Yamaha's certified-order rule and Quackenbush's complete-forum-surrender appealability grounds do not enlarge this review.

Historical events and genuinely factual findings receive Rule 52 clear-error protection. The model accepts the supported findings that the vessel reached a safe position by 1830 and could continue backing out; the master failed to plot fixes, failed to use available personnel, and initiated the shoreward turn without knowing the vessel's position. The connected hose and Exxon's continuing-risk argument are considered rather than erased. The lower courts' concurrence does not insulate an erroneous legal definition of superseding cause. Nor does independent examination of that definition permit replacement of the supported events with Exxon's competing account.

The decision does not resolve HIRI's asserted abandonment as an additional waiver holding: no material factual error is established on the admitted presentation in any event. It independently tests the challenged legal premises against the accepted facts. Even if the ultimate causal characterization were assessed independently, the disposition is the same. No broader rule allocating review of every maritime mixed causation question is necessary or adopted. Ornelas's nonwarrant Fourth Amendment allocation is expressly context-specific; neither its majority nor Scalia's separate position supplies a universal civil review standard.

The phase-one premise is collective cause in fact, together with the permitted assumption of fault or strict liability for the breakout. It is not a finding that every respondent breached a duty, an admission of every respondent's negligence, or an allocation of responsibility. The conclusion below therefore does not transform factual contribution into legal liability and then erase an adjudicated percentage.

### II. Reliable Transfer leaves genuine superseding causation available

Reliable Transfer replaces the unequal consequences of automatic equal division with proportional responsibility when multiple parties' faults legally contribute to maritime collision or stranding damage. Its rejection of the major-minor doctrine precludes excusing one operative wrongdoer merely because the other was much worse. That rule does not require treating every factual antecedent as a legally operative cause of every later loss.

A later actor's negligence can cut off the earlier legal causal chain when, in the actual circumstances, it constitutes an independent, extraordinary and unforeseeable intervention outside the risk that makes the earlier conduct legally responsible for the loss. The degree of fault is not an independent substitute for that inquiry. Ordinary negligent efforts to escape the danger the first wrongdoer created, or a later event within that danger's scope, cannot be excluded simply by assigning them a severe label.

The settlement rules adopted in McDermott and Boca Grande do not require a contrary holding. Their operative standard, **Maritime settlement credit and contribution protection**, effective **April 20, 1994**, demands meaningful litigation and reliable allocation when settlement credit and contribution protection are at issue. It does not establish every party's liability or eliminate contractual and causal limits. Milwaukee's **Compensatory maritime prejudgment interest**, effective **June 12, 1995**, presupposes an otherwise compensable amount. Its protection against a second reduction for fault already included in principal damages does not supply a missing liability predicate. Neither standard is amended.

### III. The stranding findings support that distinction here

The original mooring failure factually placed the vessel in the sequence that ended in grounding. The question is whether that sequence remained the operative legal cause when the master, after reaching a position from which safe departure remained available, failed to establish his position or use available assistance and then undertook the final shoreward turn.

The accepted findings identify more than an especially bad emergency response. Restoration of a safe position and the continuing ability to back out supply the material separation between the original danger and the later stranding. The master's subsequent unsupported navigation provides the independent intervention. The conclusion does not turn on gross negligence as a category, a numerical disparity in fault, elapsed time alone, or an assumption that a mooring failure can never foreseeably produce grounding.

Exxon's strongest argument, including its invocation of Restatement §442B and Kinsman, is that negligent navigation and grounding remained within the risk of the breakout. That argument would matter where the original peril continued materially to constrain navigation or the intervening conduct remained within the risk making the earlier breach responsible. Here, the accepted findings concerning restored safety, available assistance and safe departure sustain the conclusion that the final navigation was an independent intervention. The doctrine is applied to those findings, not to an abstract description of grounding as either always foreseeable or never foreseeable.

A comparative percentage becomes necessary only if multiple legal causes survive. The phase-one assumptions therefore permit the present decision without deciding which respondent initially failed, whether each owed the same duty, or what percentage each would otherwise bear. The claimed exclusion of earlier evidence establishes no specified legal error that makes those unresolved allocations necessary to the submitted causal question. This is not a separate approval of every aspect of bifurcation.

### IV. Contract recovery fails on a distinct damages ground

The contractual question is not disposed of merely by attaching the tort label to a warranty claim. Italia establishes that workmanlike-service responsibility can extend beyond negligence and that the allocation of contractual responsibility requires attention to the promised performance and the party able to prevent the relevant defect. A due-diligence finding therefore cannot answer a claim asserting an actual stricter promise.

For this component, assume the claimed strict safe-berth/workmanlike undertaking and an antecedent breach within its scope, without adjudicating which respondent is bound. The asserted post-breakout deficiencies likewise are not rejected merely because they satisfied due diligence. A claimant's ordinary obligation to avoid loss by reasonable measures remains distinct from whether the promised performance was strict or negligent. Although avoidable consequences can reduce rather than eliminate damages, the accepted findings address all the stranding loss before the Court: the vessel could leave safely, a competent mooring master and ship personnel were available, the master did not use those resources, and safe departure did not require the additional tug. The final turn without established position produced a loss avoidable by ordinary available navigation after safety had been attained.

Thus, even on the assumed stricter obligation, the particular stranding damages are not recoverable under these submitted warranty theories. This is a loss-limitation ground supported by findings distinct from the district court's selection of due diligence. It does not reason that reasonable terminal care necessarily satisfies every express warranty. It does not impose a universal rule that any negligence by a promisee extinguishes contractual recovery. Nor does it require a percentage deduction from a loss found wholly avoidable at the relevant stage. HIRI expressly presented avoidable consequences as an independent contract ground; applying it requires no new historical finding or new contractual term. The additional contract-foreseeability argument supports the same distinction between the promised performance and later loss, but is not a second independently necessary holding.

The contract-source qualifications are integral to this commitment:

- The quoted safe-berth provision promises a berth to which vessels may proceed or depart and where they may lie safely afloat. Its second sentence excludes warranties of the safety of **public channels, fairways, approaches thereto, anchorages and other publicly maintained areas, inside or outside the port**. The single-point-mooring amendment remains part of the supplied promise. The decision does not remove those exclusions or newly find that the reef or grounding location falls within one of them; no exclusion is applied as an independent ground.
- The quotation was introduced below as the agreement **“in relevant part.”** No complete executed agreement, complete original paragraph, or unquoted special allocation of later navigation losses is assumed. The disposition addresses the submitted safe-berth and workmanlike-service theories using the actual supplied terms and findings; it does not infer a litigant default from incomplete research files or adjudicate an additional promise not presented in the admitted record.
- **Joint Trial Exhibit 92** is the separate General Instructions, Discharging/Loading Orders and Indemnification instrument. Its servant/imputation and indemnity language, together with the master's retained legal responsibilities and final decisions, are not silently inserted into the safe-berth clause. The decision does not rest on a blanket ruling that all assisting personnel's conduct must be imputed to Exxon or that indemnity defeats an overlapping independent undertaking. The lower court's safe-berth qualification remains part of the record.
- The undertaking's continued operation after breakout is preserved. A pre-breakout superseding act does not by itself excuse a later breach. The post-breakout no-breach conclusions were reached under due diligence and are not silently converted into findings of compliance with strict warranty. The independent factual findings about safe departure and available assistance nevertheless retain their significance for the claimed loss.
- The Ninth Circuit reserved strict warranty versus due diligence, and Exxon conceded compliance only with due diligence. Both qualifications remain. The decision reserves that construction choice, the related corporations' individual contractual responsibility, the complete interaction of the two instruments, and every unprovided additional term. It makes no new finding of individual breach or contractual assent.

These reservations do not require a remand on a duty question whose resolution cannot change recovery of this separately avoidable stranding loss under either of the submitted standards. A materially different express assumption of that later loss, if newly supplied as a preserved theory, would require fresh neutral validation; it is not manufactured here in either party's favor.

### V. Remedy and authority limits

Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment sustaining the Rule 54(b) judgment against Exxon for stranding-related damages. No remand, new trial, new causation hearing, allocation percentage, damages award, contribution order or new contract-construction proceeding is ordered. The remaining breakout oil-spill claims retain their actual procedural posture. No determination of their cause, breach, damages, or responsible corporate defendants follows from this affirmance.

The judgment does not settle every safe-berth clause, eliminate strict maritime warranties, adopt due diligence as the governing construction here, impose wholesale imputation under Exhibit 92, or extend this loss limitation to damages arising while the original peril remains operative. Ordinary mandate procedures remain; no special timing is supplied.
