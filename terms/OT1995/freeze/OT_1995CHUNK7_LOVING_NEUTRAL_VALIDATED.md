## Validated source framing

**Identity and channel.** *Loving v. United States*, No.94-1966, argument January 9, 1996; review under 28 U.S.C. §1259(1) of CAAF's judgment, 41 M.J. 213, affirming convictions and sentence. The Army Court of Military Review had affirmed at 34 M.J. 956; reconsideration was denied at 34 M.J. 1065. CAAF rejected the delegation objection under *Curtis* and *Matthews*. The safe source supplies no separate lower dissent requiring additional treatment.

**Questions and scope.** The petition asks whether Congress may delegate its responsibility to establish military capital aggravators consistently with Article I and the Eighth Amendment, whether it implicitly did so, and whether constitutionally adequate standards were supplied. Petitioner's merits questions divide the authorization issue into the need for an express delegation and whether implicit authority exists. The government's question is whether the President had authority to promulgate RCM1004 where the UCMJ expressly authorizes death. The inquiry therefore includes statutory authority, any clarity requirement, guidance, constitutional delegability and the distinct Article II alternative. It is not merely whether an acknowledged sentencing-factor delegation is constitutional: the delegation itself is disputed. Individual-factor validity, unrelated conviction claims and wholesale incorporation of civilian capital doctrine are not silently added.

**Facts established by the safe sources.** Loving was an Army private at Fort Hood. He was convicted of premeditated murder of Bobby Sharbino, felony murder of Christopher Fay, attempted murder and four robbery specifications. Fay, an active-duty soldier driving a taxi for additional income, was killed on Fort Hood; Sharbino, a retired Army sergeant, was killed in Killeen. The eight-member panel unanimously imposed death, dishonorable discharge and forfeiture of all pay and allowances on April 3, 1989. Overlapping specifications dismissed as multiplicitous are not additional retained convictions. The panel unanimously found three aggravators: premeditated murder during robbery, RCM1004(c)(7)(B); another Article118 murder in the same case, (c)(7)(J); and actual perpetration of the felony-murder killing, (c)(8). These findings do not answer who constitutionally may prescribe the factors.

**Relief.** Petitioner seeks reversal of CAAF's judgment (merits p.50); the government seeks affirmance (p.47). A legal defect in capital authorization would require identification of its sentence-level consequence and the proper military remand. It would not automatically imply acquittal, release, a new guilt trial or invalidity of every punishment. Affirmance of this challenge would not be presidential approval of execution.

## Governing texts and applicable versions

The [complete bounded JA132–151 text](../sources/chunk7_b/LOVING_JA132_151_SAFE_EXTRACT.md) and [image PDF](../sources/chunk7_b/LOVING_JA132_151_SAFE_EXTRACT.pdf), committed 0fd6c5e, contain the actual reproduced earlier language. The 1994 Code files corroborate rather than replace it. The pre-1990 Article36(b) reporting requirement and pre-1991 RCM1004 are retained. Later felony-murder amendments and later omission of reporting do not enter this case by substitution.

- **Constitution.** Article I vests legislative powers in Congress and authorizes rules for the government and regulation of the land and naval forces; Article II's executive and Commander-in-Chief responsibilities are the government's separate asserted source/context. The Eighth Amendment supplies the asserted capital-narrowing concern. Invoking one provision does not settle the statutory or other constitutional question.
- **Article118, 10 U.S.C. §918 (JA145).** A person subject to the Code who unlawfully kills without justification or excuse commits murder if he (1) has a premeditated design to kill, (2) intends to kill or inflict great bodily harm, (3) engages in an act inherently dangerous to another and evincing wanton disregard of human life, or (4) perpetrates or attempts burglary, sodomy, rape, robbery or aggravated arson. The court-martial may direct punishment, **except that clauses (1) and (4) carry death or imprisonment for life**. Clauses (2) and (3) are not made capital by this text. The 1992 clause-(3) wording change does not alter either charged capital alternative.
- **Article18, §818 (JA139).** Subject to Article17, general courts-martial may try Code offenses and adjudge punishment not forbidden by the Code **under presidentially prescribed limitations**, including death only when specifically authorized by the Code. The separate law-of-war jurisdiction permits punishments allowed by the law of war. An Article16(1)(B) judge-alone general court-martial lacks jurisdiction over an offense for which death may be adjudged unless previously referred as noncapital. Loving's eight-member panel does not invoke that exception, and neither exception supplies an unlimited power to create capital offenses.
- **Article36, §836 (JA140, prior to the 1990 amendment).** Presidential regulations may prescribe pretrial, trial and post-trial procedures, including modes of proof, for courts-martial, military commissions and other military tribunals, and procedures for courts of inquiry. So far as the President considers practicable they must apply generally recognized principles of law and evidence in federal district-court criminal trials, and may not contradict or be inconsistent with the UCMJ. Subsection (b) states: **“All rules and regulations made under this article shall be uniform insofar as practicable and shall be reported to Congress.”** Whether capital-selection factors fall within procedures is contested; the text does not answer that by its label alone.
- **Article56, §856 (JA141).** “The punishment which a court-martial may direct for an offense may not exceed such limits as the President may prescribe for that offense.” Whether restricting which already-capital murders may receive death is such a punishment limit is the principal authorization fork, not an established concession.
- **Article106a, §906a (JA142–144).** Congress expressly addresses espionage. Its capital categories concern specified defense/national-security subjects, with death or other punishment as directed. Death requires a unanimously found statutory aggravator beyond reasonable doubt and unanimous substantial outweighing of mitigation by aggravation, permits guilt and/or sentencing evidence and broad mitigation latitude. The aggravators include another qualifying espionage/treason conviction, grave risk of substantial national-security harm, grave risk of another person's death, and **any other factor prescribed by the President under Article36**. This later, express treatment informs both sides' statutory arguments but is not Loving's murder provision or proof of the meaning of Article118 by itself.
- **Article55, §855.** Flogging, branding, marking, tattooing and other cruel or unusual punishment are prohibited; irons are prohibited except for safe custody. The official 1994 text identifies no intervening amendment to this prohibition. It is a statutory limit, not an adjudication here of all capital procedures.
- **Article71, §871.** The death part may not be executed until approved by the President. The President may commute or remit it; the death part may not be suspended. Subsection (c)(1) independently requires a final judgment as to legality through the applicable military and Supreme Court review routes where review has not been waived/withdrawn; it does not make this merits decision an execution order. The separate waiver/withdrawal route in (c)(2) concerns dismissal or punitive discharge, not a new authority to execute death. Subsection (d)'s suspension power also excludes death. The 1994 amendment changes court names in (c)(1), not these substantive safeguards; the 1983 provisions precede the 1989 adjudged sentence.

**RCM1004 before the 1991 amendment (JA132–138).** Death requires an expressly capital Manual offense (or separately authorized law-of-war offense), unanimous conviction by all members present when voted, and compliance with the applicable aggravator/procedure provisions. Written aggravator notice precedes arraignment, but failure of timely notice does **not** bar later notice/proof unless the accused demonstrates specific prejudice and that a continuance or recess is inadequate. The accused has broad mitigation/extenuation latitude. At least one listed aggravator must be proved beyond reasonable doubt and unanimously found with the required notice. All members must also agree that admissible aggravating circumstances, including the eligibility factor, substantially outweigh extenuation/mitigation. Broader sentencing aggravation is not limited to the listed eligibility factors and cannot replace the antecedent eligibility finding.

The military judge instructs on the applicable factors/procedures and consideration of all mitigation; the members vote separately by secret written ballot in closed session on each instructed factor before the sentence vote, and the court-martial president announces the aggravators found if death is adjudged. Evidence may come from before or after guilt findings, or both. These are distinct notice, proof, unanimity, weighing and announcement requirements, not one generalized balancing standard.

The complete factor list and offense-specific exclusions remain in the linked seven-page primary text. Material murder provisions are (c)(7), limited to Article118(1), and (c)(8), limited to Article118(4). The former includes robbery/other listed offenses or flight, and a second Article118 conviction in the same case; the latter requires actual perpetration of the killing. Other listed factors retain their own limits: the enemy-presence and actual national-security-damage factors exclude Articles118/120; endangerment of other lives excludes Articles104/106a/120; war/occupied-or-hostilities territory is limited to Articles118/120; rape and espionage provisions are offense-specific. No uncharged factor is substituted for the three actually found.

Paragraph (d) separately excepts Article106 spying from (a)(2), (b), (c) and RCM1006/1007, providing its distinct sentencing procedure and operation-of-law announcement. Paragraph (e), except for Article106, allows other RCM1003 punishments including life in lieu of death, subject to specific Manual limits. A death sentence includes dishonorable discharge or dismissal as appropriate; confinement is a necessary incident rather than a component of that sentence. Those exceptions prevent a universal description of all military capital cases but do not turn Loving's murders into spying.

**Executive Order12460 (JA146–151).** Issued January 24, 1984, it invokes constitutional and UCMJ authority and supplies the earlier capital procedure/factors. Section6 makes the amendments immediately effective for trials of capital offenses committed on or after that date. Section7 directs the Secretary of Defense to transmit the order to Congress under Article36. The directive establishes neither a fact of transmission nor a violation of reporting, neither of which is presented as a contested record fact. This express prospective provision must not be erased; this source does not require substituting the order's initial factor text for the later pre-1991 RCM version reproduced at JA132–138. ## Opposing grounds and assumptions

Petitioner argues that selecting the offenders who may die is substantive lawmaking that Congress cannot transfer consistently with Article I and the Eighth Amendment. Alternatively, only an express and clear authorization could suffice; Articles18/36/56 do not expressly authorize capital-selection factors and cannot bear that implication. Article106a's explicit scheme underscores the absence for murder. Even if a delegation exists, Congress supplied no adequate standard for capital selection. The President's concentration of legislative, prosecutorial, military-judicial and clemency functions intensifies the objection. These arguments challenge authority and guidance, not the panel's factual findings alone.

The government argues that Congress itself made the relevant murder alternatives punishable by death or life and that Articles18/36/56 authorize presidential limits and procedures within that choice. Capital factors restrict rather than create exposure, with civilian criminal principles, discipline, military practice, statutory safeguards and later legislative treatment giving context and boundaries. Article106a supports rather than negates the established presidential role. The government also argues both that overlapping executive/military responsibility supports a less demanding delegation inquiry and, separately, that independent Article II power sustains the rules. The latter is an alternative constitutional ground and cannot be silently substituted for statutory authority.

Government merits nn.5–7 does not dispute, for resolving this case, *Matthews*' limited additional-aggravator requirement while preserving whether all civilian capital doctrines apply throughout the military. It characterizes petitioner as not disputing adequate narrowing if Congress itself enacted these factors. This permits a bounded legal assumption, not a finding that every factor or every military capital system is constitutional. The Court may assess the challenged authority without deciding more of civilian military incorporation than necessary. Petitioner need not concede congressional delegation because he accepts hypothetical substantive adequacy.
