## Historical comparator and material premises

Fletcher, joined by Rymer, affirmed the district court's summary judgment; D. W. Nelson dissented on the Fourth Amendment question. The majority sustained the program even assuming that ordinary law enforcement was its only purpose, using reduced offender privacy, medically limited collection, identification utility and objective coverage. That sufficient rationale differs from the district court's special-needs/penal-administration reasoning. The majority also rejected the general hearing and ex post facto claims and affirmed the Milligan judgment on absence of evidence connecting Knox or Pearce personally to the mistake. Its final judgment does not convert the district court's negligence rationale into the appellate holding.

There is **no historical Supreme Court Rise merits vote or writing to attribute to any Associate**. The lower split is powerful evidence of the available legal paths and their objections, not a personal historical commitment for these eight Justices. The changes below therefore compare premises and outcomes with the lower comparator, without inventing a Supreme Court departure or treating agreement with a circuit dissenter as a historical Justice join.

The historical lower opinion's broad use of Vernonia cannot replace the actual Court's carefully confined athletic-program decision. Actual Vernonia preserves bodily-search status and no waiver, and upholds its documented safety program with confidential, nonpolice use and restricted consequences. This changes the precedent available to justify Rise's ordinary investigatory objective; it does not compel one answer to every different context. Actual Sandin likewise preserves direct constitutional liberty and a sufficient, nonexclusive genuine-state-constraint inquiry. Its historical alternative formula is not governing law. Finally, the enacted adequate-sample, repeat-sample, health, use and destruction terms qualify the lower court's shorthand. Those terms were already law: correcting an incomplete legal description is not a newly discovered factual injury or a new enactment.

## Common statutory and remedial limits

All eight carry forward the complete frozen statutory account. Section 137.076 covers the listed rape, sodomy, unlawful penetration, sexual-abuse, public-indecency, incest and child-display offenses; burglary with intent to commit a listed sexual offense; promoting or compelling prostitution; attempt or conspiracy to the listed felonies in paragraphs (a)–(c); and murder or aggravated murder. Attempted murder is outside the list. The program cannot be described as confined to violent sex crimes or extended to every person with a criminal history.

An appropriate-agency request triggers the individual's duty despite omission of a separate judgment order. The sentencing court must enter the order, require reimbursement unless the person lacks ability to pay, and impose the sample condition when probation applies. Collection is as soon as practicable and, for incarceration, before release. State Police notice of inadequacy requires an additional sample; its notice that an adequate sample was previously received excuses another draw. A court finding that collection would create a substantial and unreasonable health risk also excuses it. The September 29, 1991 incarcerated cohort receives the same subsections (1)–(4) limits. There is no absolute one-draw ceiling and no power to demand arbitrary repeats.

Collection must be medically acceptable and performed by the specified licensed or qualified personnel or under physician direction/control, with prescribed procedures. The civil-liability protection concerns authorized, medically acceptable collection; deviations that do not affect reliability do not alone make the test or opinion inadmissible. Section 181.085 permits retaining samples and derived physical material, identity/genetic-profile analysis, identification databases and anonymous population-frequency databases. It restricts disclosure to the specified investigation/proceeding recipients and purposes, independently required discovery, and lawful court/grand-jury process for otherwise unprivileged evidence; recipient public agencies remain bound. Record inspection is allowed on request subject to reasonable likelihood of prejudice to a pending investigation; access to test physical material need not be provided. Accuracy-challenge and preservation procedures remain required as stated.

A written destruction request with a certified reversal order ordinarily requires destruction, subject to a separate qualifying obligation and preservation of physical evidence relating to another covered person. A §137.225 set-aside does not itself affect the material. Release alone creates no destruction right. The lower court's genetic-predisposition description is judicial characterization, not a quoted freestanding statutory prohibition. Identity matching is not the only authorized activity because anonymous frequency use is expressly allowed. Neither unrestricted future use nor universal perpetual retention nor actual unauthorized analysis is established.

## Final component commitments and exact grounds

### A. Fourth Amendment

**Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg:** Reverse the Fourth Amendment ground sustaining summary judgment and remand the covered plaintiffs' claims for appropriate claim-specific relief. Forced extraction and the protected examination of the resulting material require justification. An ordinary future-crime identification objective, supported by prior qualifying conviction alone, does not justify this suspicionless bodily intrusion on the presented record. Schmerber's acceptable medical means do not dispense with lawful grounds to enter the body; Winston preserves the distinct bodily interest. Skinner, Von Raab and actual Vernonia do not supply a general ordinary-investigation exception, and Bell's institutional-security rationale cannot simply be renamed to cover the database. Sitz shows that ordinary enforcement is not an absolute bar to every suspicionless seizure, but its brief roadside intervention does not authorize extracting and retaining internal material.

Their strongest contrary path is the confined identification exception accepted by the other three: convicted status, legitimate identification, neutral selection, modest medical burden, demonstrated utility and regulated uses all matter. The five acknowledge those facts; they do not rest invalidation on predicted misuse, a nonexistent unconditional retention rule or a supposed need for suspicion in every conceivable setting. They find those features insufficient to justify this extension. They leave a different security/health program, consent, a supported warrant or exception, and materially different noninvasive identification methods undecided.

**Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer:** Affirm this component for the actual conviction-defined identification scheme. Their sufficient ground is a new application of contextual reasonableness: the qualifying convictions and lawful correctional relationship, objective selection, modest medically controlled intrusion, demonstrated identification utility and statutory use/sample/health/retention safeguards together justify it. It is not a no-search theory, an already established DNA exception, or an automatic application of special needs. The strongest objection is the new bodily acquisition for unrelated future prosecutions and the retained material's privacy significance. Their answer depends on this defined program; they refuse a general forfeiture of offender privacy, arrestee testing, arbitrary repeat sampling, unrestricted genetic investigation or materially harsher collection.

**Common remedial ceiling:** No direct damages, automatic destruction order, finding of liability by every defendant, or adjudication of hypothetical secondary misuse. Actual claims, capacities, immunities, participation and lawful remedial requirements govern on remand. Collection, analysis, disclosure and retention are not interchangeable remedial commands.

### B. General precollection process — verified claim and bounded remand

**Source and scope finding:** The published opinion's paragraph 3 identifies the general pre-draw hearing claim, and Part IV, paragraph 22, expressly decides whether prison officials must provide a hearing before requiring blood under Chapter 669. It does not merely decide a plaintiff-specific dispute about one already adjudicated exception. Its two stated premises are that medically acceptable extraction does not implicate due process and that predicate conviction is the only criterion, leaving little to contest. The same opinion acknowledges the health protection in its search discussion, while enacted §137.076(2)(c) and (4) also distinguish inadequate samples, adequate-prior-sample notice and court-determined health risk. Thus the process rationale legally omits operative conditions; it does not establish that those conditions are unavailable or were denied to these plaintiffs.

The statutory text is pertinent to the preserved general hearing question because the usefulness of an opportunity to object depends on what the collection law actually makes material. Considering that text does not create an individual health, prior-sample, testing-method or denial-of-access claim. No finding establishes that Rise, Durham or Rhodes qualified for a health or sample exception, that available court/agency procedures were inadequate, or that a particular official refused such a procedure. Milligan's proved coverage mistake illustrates why coverage and application are distinct from the existence of a conviction, but supplies neither another plaintiff's injury nor liability of Knox/Pearce.

**Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Vacate the general-process summary judgment and remand the preserved general hearing claim, only insofar as an independent process issue remains live after the Fourth Amendment disposition. The sufficient correction is rejection of the categorical premise that adjudicated conviction exhausts everything legally material and that safe medical means answer all procedural concerns. Bodily integrity supplies a direct liberty interest; actual Sandin also requires consideration of meaningful substantive constraints rather than treating every statutory instruction as automatically creating a constitutional procedure. Mathews supplies the private interest, error risk and value of added process, and governmental burden inquiry. A final conviction is not retried.

The lower court must address that existing claim under the correct legal framework, taking account of actual lawful opportunities to invoke material statutory conditions. The remand does **not** require a new evidentiary hearing, discovery, an oral hearing before every draw, a prescribed notice or response period, or a new proof burden. Ordinary record verification and existing court/agency access may suffice. It makes no adverse inference from unavailable detail and no finding of deficient process. It neither authorizes amendment to add new individual exception claims nor decides such claims. Existing preservation and remedial rules remain; if the independent question is no longer live, it need not be decided. A process mechanism cannot cure an unconstitutional search.

The strongest opposing path is to affirm the denial of a categorical additional-hearing entitlement now, since no individual exception dispute is established. These five agree there is no automatic formal-hearing entitlement, but decline to sustain the judgment through an assumed account of existing procedures or the false conviction-only premise. Their disposition corrects law within the claim actually decided; it does not grant substantive relief from an unproved procedural injury.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Affirm only the rejection of a categorical right to an additional hearing before every otherwise lawful collection. The final conviction is objective and already adjudicated, and the statute retains its actual court/agency exceptions. The strongest objection is that an inadequate process could prevent invocation of those exceptions, as a real coverage error shows. Their narrower ground leaves such a concrete, properly presented claim open rather than approving every procedure or declaring bodily integrity irrelevant. They do not join the lower court's unqualified conviction-only premise, find procedures adequate from silence, or nullify sample/health conditions. Scalia's A position independently prevents collection on the justification rejected there.

### C. Ex post facto challenge

**All eight affirm** rejection of the retrospective-punishment challenge as presented. The scheme's design and demonstrated operation serve regulated identification, not added punishment for the earlier offense. Past-conviction selection and burdensome blood collection, retention, probation conditions and possible deterrence warrant examination; a civil label is not dispositive. On this record, medically limited collection and reimbursement qualified by inability to pay do not establish additional penal severity. No enlarged sentence, reduced parole opportunity, newly criminalized conduct or eliminated defense is established.

Actual Morales and Collins preserve the punishment boundary. Morales's meaningful lost opportunity to obtain earlier release does not establish that every later burden is punishment, and its particular substantiation rule is not transferred to this program. The ruling approves no unspecified sanction, added incarceration or extension beyond an otherwise lawful release date. An unreasonable search is not necessarily punishment; nonpunitive classification does not establish search reasonableness. Milligan's unauthorized demand and discipline are separately addressed, not treated as a lawful statutory application establishing general punitive operation.

### D. Milligan's remaining claim against Knox and Pearce

**All eight affirm** summary judgment for the only two remaining defendants on the absence of evidence of their own causal involvement in the mistaken demand and ensuing discipline. Actual Hafer requires each officer's own federal violation; supervisory title or general implementation responsibility is insufficient. The uncontroverted affidavit identifies Tuttle's mistaken extension of the attempt language in (d) to murder in (e); Tuttle and the disciplinarians are not defendants. Milligan's attempted-murder and unlawful-weapon convictions were not covered, and no actual draw from him is established.

The strongest contrary path invokes repeated unauthorized demands, a fine and segregation as possible deprivations of protected liberty. Actual Sandin preserves that possibility, but cannot supply the missing link to Knox/Pearce. The ruling does not adopt the district court's at-most-negligence account as a universal resolution under Daniels, decide claims against unsued responsible actors, infer waiver from Milligan's failure to raise coverage at his two hearings, or invent segregation duration and conditions. The unappealed dismissals of DOC, State Police, Madsen and the John Doe defendants remain undisturbed. No damages, responsible-actor liability, immunity conclusion or new individual remand is supplied.
