## What the additional record establishes

**Identified California voters.** Amended-complaint paragraph 20, JA p.41, alleges that **John Mack** is a United States citizen and a voter and taxpayer in California and Los Angeles, residing in the 28th Congressional District, 30th State Senatorial District and 49th State Assembly District. Paragraph 21 alleges that **Olga Morales** is a United States citizen and a voter and taxpayer in California and Los Angeles, residing in the 25th Congressional District, 23rd State Senatorial District and 55th State Assembly District. These are specific pleaded identities and residences. The excerpts do not independently prove those allegations at trial or establish an unchanged residence through March 1996.

**Identified individual appellants.** The July 8, 1993 entry, JA pp.24–25, records the filing in the Second Circuit of a copy of district-court docket entries and a notice of appeal on behalf of listed appellants. The list expressly includes **“Appellant John Mack” and “Appellant Olga Morales.”** The July 16 entries for Forms C and D again list both and record the forms filed with proof of service and their respective deadlines satisfied, JA pp.25–27. July 8 is the date of this appellate docket entry; this excerpt does not independently establish the date the original notice was filed in the district court or decide timeliness.

**Confirmation in the lower opinion.** The Second Circuit opinion's caption, App.1, lists John Mack and Olga Morales among the **Plaintiffs-Appellants**. App.2 lists Arizona among the **Intervenors-Plaintiffs-Appellants**. The same caption separately identifies **“People of the State of California ex rel Daniel E. Lungren, Attorney General”** as **“Plaintiff,”** without the appellate designation. Thus the actual lower opinion corroborates the individual voters' appellate participation and distinguishes it from California's stated caption status.

**Agreement in the federal brief concerning individual appeals.** Footnote 16 states that California did not appeal the district judgment, citing C.A. App. A387–A390, and immediately acknowledges that **“Several municipalities and individual voters in California did appeal.”** It then raises legal questions about municipalities' rights, whether individuals or the State are the proper parties, and whether individuals may continue after their State forgoes appeal. Its willingness to assume individuals could have Article III standing is conditional argument, not a factual concession of every injury element or a ruling that standing exists. Conversely, its argument does not establish that no California voter appealed: it expressly says some did, now supported by the named docket and caption entries.

**Respondents' reply and the different adjustment figures.** Footnote 27 asserts California voters' own representational interests notwithstanding the State's nonappeal; it also invokes Arizona and argues that one proper plaintiff suffices for the same nationwide relief. Its opening characterization of the United States as suggesting that “none” of the plaintiffs has standing should not replace the federal brief's more qualified language. Footnote 26 acknowledges that the later revised adjusted figures would shift a seat from Wisconsin to California. The federal brief, pp.18–19, likewise distinguishes the original proposal's Wisconsin/Pennsylvania-to-California/Arizona two-seat change from the later corrected proposal's **Wisconsin-to-California single-seat change**. Arizona's participation is established; Arizona's receipt of an additional seat under the later correction is not. The specific California voter appellants therefore cannot be omitted from the factual account by relying solely on Arizona or the State of California's participation.

## California's own appellate status: discrepancy retained

The JA docket lists also include **“Appellant Daniel E. Lungren”** in the July 8 entry and subsequent Forms C/D entries. That docket label is in tension with the lower opinion's separate “Plaintiff” designation for the People of California ex rel Lungren and the federal brief's assertion that California did not appeal. Respondents' footnote 27 also proceeds from California's nonappeal rather than asserting that the State remained an appellant.

Do not silently erase the docket label, infer that California definitively appealed, assume the docket contained a clerical error, or invent a withdrawal/dismissal. These excerpts do not explain whether the docket list reflects the actual notice's designation, later changes, or some other distinction. The underlying notice cited at C.A. App. A387–A390 and any pertinent subsequent lower appellate filing/order would be needed to resolve the discrepancy if the State's own status becomes dispositive. The narrower, independently corroborated fact that **Mack and Morales were individual appellants** is not contradicted by these sources and does not depend on resolving the State's status.
