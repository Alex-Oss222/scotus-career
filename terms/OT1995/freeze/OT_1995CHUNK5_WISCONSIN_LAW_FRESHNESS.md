## Varity: actual effect and limits

Varity holds that the particular benefit presentation constituted ERISA fiduciary administration, that its deliberate disloyal deception breached §404, and that §502(a)(3) permits appropriate individual equitable redress within traditionally equitable categories. The controlling adequacy principle is qualified: adequate relief under another ERISA provision normally makes further equity unnecessary; the stricter condition in the separate concurrence is not the Court's rule. These are ERISA function, duty and remedy holdings. They neither establish a census accuracy duty nor change APA review, statistical deference, constitutional discriminatory-purpose requirements or census standing.

## Meghrig: actual effect and limits

Meghrig requires a qualifying RCRA endangerment when the citizen action begins, while allowing past contribution and delayed physical effects within that condition. It separately rejects the pleaded completed-cleanup reimbursement action. The full remedial majority also rejects general reimbursement of earlier expenses merely because danger remains, while reserving costs arising after a properly commenced citizen action **and** costs paid after invocation of RCRA's statutory process. Invocation is not equated with filing, and neither reserved category is affirmatively authorized. No earlier holding is overruled.

Those conclusions construe §§6972–6973 and their enforcement structure. They **do not create a general filing-time rule for census challenges**, transform completion of census reporting into Article III mootness, erase a continuing allocation injury, or establish that declaratory or corrective census relief is unavailable. The opinion explicitly leaves ordinary equitable principles across other statutes intact. Its discussion of Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools concerns remedial authority under that precedent; it does not revise the distinct census decision, Franklin v. Massachusetts.

Meghrig's refusal to infer a remedy from policy or an equitable label reinforces the existing obligation to identify the enacted authority and distinguish a statutory command from a preferred policy. It does not answer whether §195 prohibits or permits sampling, whether the Secretary's actual grounds were sufficient, or whether a preserved constitutional claim warrants reconsideration. Its independent statutory defect does not negate the plaintiff's alleged financial injury; it therefore supplies no basis for collapsing Wisconsin's injury, merits and remedy inquiries.
