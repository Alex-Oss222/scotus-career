## Record, scope and law entering the event

The state supreme court's **judgment has four votes; Riley's lead reasoning has three**. Griffin's result-only concurrence does not adopt every rationale. Cavanagh dissents with Levin and Brickley; Levin also writes separately. The federal Court reviews the judgment on the properly presented federal owner-interest questions. It has no general appellate power to decide whether Michigan courts applied the best reading of their nuisance statute or abused state-law equitable discretion.

John Bennis used the jointly owned car to pick up a woman and engage in a sexual act in an area described as having a continuing prostitution nuisance. Officers did not observe a money exchange; the lead state opinion relies on lewdness associated with prostitution and alternatively on circumstantial prostitution evidence. Earlier encounters do not establish repeated unlawful use of this particular car. The statute's application and the upheld nuisance judgment supply the federal posture without requiring a new Supreme Court finding on payment, frequency, or state-law offense elements.

The state lead opinion **assumes** Tina lacked knowledge of and did not consent to the illegal use. It also treats the car as voluntarily entrusted to her co-owner husband, not stolen or taken initially without her permission. These are different propositions. Neither assumption establishes that she did everything reasonably expected to prevent misuse, nor that a particular reasonable step was available and omitted. The existing record describes a contested hearing and equitable consideration of alternatives. The packet's sale-cost and other-car particulars remain packet-derived, not independently verified trial findings. No ownership fraction, price, personal contribution, asset value, sale expense, or practical substitute-car availability is supplied by this model.

MCL 600.3801 identifies the listed nuisance uses. Section 3815(2) says knowledge need not be proved; it is a statutory proof provision, distinct from the lower court's conclusion about the effect on Tina's interest and from a federal constitutional rule. The lead theory concerns this car's contribution to an existing neighborhood nuisance and is expressly bounded by that condition. It is not a state majority rationale establishing that every isolated act in every automobile anywhere demands forfeiture.

The operative Standards and Tests provisions are **Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review**, present operation effective Austin/Alexander, June 28, 1993; **Donee ownership and civil-forfeiture relation back**, present operation effective Buena Vista, February 24, 1993; and **Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property**, present operation effective James Daniel Good, December 13, 1993. Austin recognizes covered punitive forfeitures and reserves truly innocent ownership; it does not supply an adjudicated substantive due-process defense, a comprehensive instrumentality test, or state incorporation. Buena Vista applies enacted owner protection and preserves the opportunity to establish it before relation back; it leaves a constitutional guarantee unresolved. Good concerns prior process for real-property seizure and retains movable-property exigency distinctions; the existence of a hearing here makes its procedural holding insufficient by itself to resolve final title.

Libretti, November 7, 1995, supplies additional current law: consent cannot enlarge statutory forfeiture authority or dispose of interests beyond the defendant's lawful power. Its limited sentence/jury classification and actual waiver conclusions do not transfer a federal §853(n) defense into Michigan law. They also do not equate a husband's consent to his punishment with his wife's surrender of constitutional property rights. The five-Justice Part II classification remains controlling at its stated statutory scope; the published reservations about resolving constitutional treatment from a sentencing label do not displace it. Souter and Ginsburg separately rely on actual awareness and informed relinquishment while reserving the constitutional source. Stevens's dissent seeks statutory assurance, not automatic return or an established constitutional jury right. Section 853 may reach instrumentalities, enterprise interests and qualifying substitute property despite lawful acquisition; lawful source alone is not immunity. Conversely, an agreement cannot supply a missing statutory predicate. None of these distinct criminal-forfeiture propositions establishes or forecloses the present Michigan substantive owner defense.

Goldsmith-Grant, 254 U.S.505, and Van Oster, 272 U.S.465, sustain forfeiture in their actual unlawful-use and voluntary-entrustment settings despite titleholder ignorance. Neither decides theft or other taking without the owner's privity/consent. Goldsmith also leaves hypothetical incidental carriage on a sleeper or steamer unresolved; this reservation is not an existing constitutional connection test. Calero-Toledo, 416 U.S.663, sustains forfeiture against an uninvolved, unaware lessor that voluntarily entrusted the yacht and neither alleged nor proved all reasonable preventive measures. Its 689–690 discussion raises the owner-protection question for involuntary taking or an owner who proves lack of involvement/awareness and all reasonably expected preventive action. That is a reservation, not a previously adjudicated exception.

Calero-Toledo's Part III directly rejects the uncompensated-taking objection in its valid-forfeiture application. Fuller, 409 U.S.488, supplies only a qualified analogy about eminent-domain valuation where the claimed increment derives from revocable use of government grazing land. It is not a forfeiture holding and does not authorize an unrestricted principle that any governmental power eliminates compensation.

## Enhanced alternative-path analysis

**Entrustment application.** The strongest affirmance route applies Goldsmith, Van Oster and Calero-Toledo: the titleholder need not participate in the wrong when property voluntarily entrusted to another is actually put to a prohibited use. The strongest objection is that a genuinely blameless co-owner with no reasonable means to prevent a concealed act may serve no legitimate deterrent purpose, especially if the property is only an incidental location in an area-wide nuisance theory. The four affirming associates answer at the level this record establishes: the vehicle transported the participants and hosted the act, lawful shared use was voluntary, and lack of knowledge is the constitutional claim's established assumption. They do not find reasonable prevention affirmatively impossible or hold that the reserved situation could never matter. Equitable discretion is an additional feature of the application, not a substitute for every constitutional limitation.

**Qualified substantive owner protection.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer adopt the following rule for this case: due process protects the separately held interest of an owner who establishes that she neither participated in nor knew of the prohibited use and did all that reasonably could be expected to prevent it. Mere co-ownership and consent to ordinary shared use do not categorically defeat that protection. The claimant must establish the circumstances bringing her within the protected category; the rule creates no presumption from marriage, ownership or assumed ignorance. It requires reasonable preventive action, not prevention at any imaginable cost or omniscient supervision. They prescribe no new numerical or heightened proof standard; the ordinary state factfinding rules apply subject to a meaningful opportunity to establish this constitutional protection. They do not allocate to the State a new universal obligation to disprove every owner's innocence as part of every forfeiture case.

The strongest counterargument is that Calero-Toledo reserved this question while sustaining forfeiture on an unaware lessor's record, and that existing entrustment doctrine itself supplies a deterrent and anti-collusion justification. The four remanding associates accept that the reservation is not binding law and make the adoption explicit. They distinguish the class with both established blamelessness and reasonable prevention from ignorance alone; where no reasonable preventive act remained, treating permission for ordinary use as conclusive would empty the reserved protection of substance. Their remedy follows the missing findings, not an appellate conclusion that Tina automatically qualifies. This path narrows the ordinary entrustment rule for a newly defined protected category without overruling its applications to owners who do not establish that category.

**Broader protection or immediate return.** An alternative would deem every unaware co-owner constitutionally immune or treat the car as such an incidental location that forfeiture of Tina's interest is necessarily invalid. Austin's punitive discussion and the reserved Goldsmith hypotheticals furnish arguments, but no admitted holding establishes either categorical result. The actual car conveyed the participants and contained the act; repeated misuse of the same car is not established. All eight decline immediate unconditional restoration on the present assumptions. The remand group leaves the broader incidental-location issue open because its owner-specific rule supplies sufficient relief; the affirmance group decides only this vehicle-use setting. No blanket innocent-owner constitutional immunity or universal instrumentality formula is joined.

**Takings as an independent route.** Lawful forfeiture under the actual constitutional and statutory predicates does not automatically become eminent-domain acquisition requiring compensation. Calero-Toledo supplies direct support; Fuller illustrates only a bounded distinct-power/value principle. The owner's strongest response is that this proposition assumes precisely what she contests: lawful power to extinguish her separate interest. All eight preserve that sequence. The affirmance four find the reviewed due-process objection insufficient and reject the separate compensation demand. The remand four reject an automatic-taking theory but will not use the word forfeiture to pretermit the predicate inquiry. They seek protection of an interest not lawfully forfeitable if she proves the defense, not a present holding awarding just compensation after authorizing its taking.

**Procedural and statutory alternatives.** Good does not create an automatic return remedy after this contested hearing, and Libretti's §853(n) framework cannot be imported into state law. Michigan's equitable power may permit tailored relief; it is not itself a federal requirement to pay every owner or a basis for this Court to adopt the state dissent's preferred statutory construction. The federal question can be reached despite the lower court's divided rationale because its four-vote judgment sustains taking the whole vehicle, and no identified independent state ground avoids the federal challenge. The absence of complete Supreme Court briefs prevents additional waiver, concession or precise question-language findings; it does not authorize a new question under the Excessive Fines Clause.
