## Event and material record

Granted merits review, No.95-5207, of Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals No.F-92-533, 889P.2d293 (Jan10,1995), affirming first-degree murder conviction and death sentence. Assigned question: whether due process permits requiring a defendant to prove present trial incompetence by clear and convincing evidence. The conviction's other guilt/penalty issues and unrelated jury-waiver challenge are not added. Petitioner requests reversal/new trial; State requests preservation of its judgment. All nine event-date Justices participate; no source-established nonparticipation.

Oklahoma tit.22 §1175.4(B), as quoted in recovered lower/party materials, directs the court at the hearing to determine by clear and convincing evidence whether the person is incompetent and presumes competence for burden allocation. The lower court authoritatively treats persuasion as defendant-borne. Minor transcript variation in the presumption's wording does not change that operative interpretation. §1175.1 concerns present understanding of charges/proceedings and rational effective assistance; §§1175.2–.6 supply initial sufficient-doubt application, examination, postexamination hearing, treatment and restoration machinery. A defendant, counsel, prosecutor or court may initiate inquiry. Hearing opportunities include presence, cross-examination and a jury demand. A multiple-stage scheme does not establish that its ultimate proof quantum is valid or that every safeguard functioned adequately in this case.

Dr.King found incompetence in1990; later hearing evidence was divided between continuing concern and competence/malingering. Cooper was found competent following that hearing. In1992 counsel again raised communication/behavior concerns, despite having earlier entertained feigning. Dr.Murphy testified to a major affective disorder preventing rational assistance, while acknowledging manipulation/malingering could coexist. Inmates described marked abnormal behavior; jailers reported ordinary communication; State relied on apparent understanding and pro se civil filings. The judge expressly invoked failure to carry clear-and-convincing incompetence, although accepting that Cooper was not normal and recognizing Murphy's diagnosis. Source does not establish how the judge would find facts under preponderance, much less that evidence was exactly balanced. Later abnormal trial behavior prompted renewed objection and requests for mistrial, commitment or reevaluation; no later application of a different proof quantum is recovered.

The appellate court upheld both the higher burden as law and findings made under it. It found the second hearing's defense evidence stronger but State lay evidence sufficient to permit the adjudicated result under the State's standard. That is neither a finding under a lower burden nor a clinical resolution by this Court. A contention that the May hearing was merely preliminary was abandoned on appeal; do not conflate the sufficient-doubt trigger with persuasion at the completed postexamination inquiry. Diagnosis, malingering, legal competence, sanity at offense, factual guilt and legal innocence remain distinct.

## Competing positions and supported paths

**State's strongest supported position:** Medina permits defendant persuasion and recognizes counsel/expert safeguards despite difficulty assisting proof; Oklahoma offers repeated inquiry, examination and factfinding, may treat feigning as a serious reliability problem, and has legitimate timely-prosecution interests. Competence is not an offense element, and the constitutional prohibition does not mechanically choose a probability threshold. An erroneous incompetence determination also delays adjudication and can produce unwanted treatment/confinement under independently applicable law. These arguments do not establish that malingering occurred or that a higher defendant burden improves the relevant error allocation in this case.

**Petitioner's strongest supported position:** preponderance resolves equipoise, while clear-and-convincing persuasion permits trial when a factfinder considers incompetence more likely but not sufficiently certain. Capacity to help prove incapacity is the very problem; repeated hearings using the same heightened allocation do not cure it. Counsel and experts assist inquiry but cannot eliminate the allocation's consequence. Historical/common contemporary practice and the foundational right to participate give legal support for requiring a lower quantum. Petitioner need not prove actual incompetence under an unadministered standard to present the preserved facial/procedural rule issue; that does not entitle him to a factual declaration of incapacity.

1. **Invalidate the higher quantum and set aside the burden-dependent judgment.** Preserve Medina's defendant-preponderance holding, sufficient-doubt trigger and Dusky definition. The strongest objection is that due process permits reasonable procedural choices and not every improvement in accuracy is constitutionally compelled. Answer must identify why this particular allocation risks the protected prohibition, using a date-eligible method rather than declaring Medina overruled by implication.
2. **Sustain the higher quantum within Oklahoma's demonstrated hearing safeguards and affirm the reviewed legal ground.** The strongest objection is the class of defendants more likely than not incompetent whom the rule can send to trial; an assertion that genuinely incompetent defendants usually prove it clearly does not resolve that consequence. Support would require explaining why the complete procedure constitutionally tolerates that allocation, not simply equating competence with insanity or treating each hearing's repetition as a cure.
3. **Narrow application/remand route if sufficient independent legal error is properly within the submitted issue.** Judge's remarks about inability to help counsel can illuminate application and risk; they cannot silently create an ungranted Dusky claim or make burden review unnecessary without examining scope. Reversal solely for a new hearing-threshold or jury-waiver theory would exceed the prepared scope. No automatic equipoise finding or appellate substitution of experts for factfinder.

If the higher burden is invalidated, remedy is a distinct choice. The record contains contemporaneous reports and transcripts that might support retrospective evaluation, but no source adjudicates whether a reliable retrospective determination now can be made. Pate/Drope's concerns and Godinez's preserved inquiry require assessing feasibility rather than assuming it; a confined remand may direct that assessment, with lawful further proceedings upon competence if reliable retrospective review cannot support the conviction. An unconditional new trial is petitioner's requested remedy and must be tested against that possible narrower route. No acquittal, unconditional release, automatic commitment, finding that Cooper is now competent, or invented harmlessness calculation is supported. Existing C&C appellate sufficiency does not itself show the burden immaterial. This supplement establishes no general harmless-error or structural-error category.
