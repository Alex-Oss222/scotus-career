## Booking doctrine: exact source and join validation

### Received authorities and the two supportable paths

**Innis.** The Court's rule, 446 U.S. at 300–03 and nn.5–9, covers express questions and their functional equivalent. The reasonably foreseeable incriminating-response inquiry describes the latter; an innocuous asserted purpose is not an independent immunity for direct questions. Intent and known susceptibility may inform the objective inquiry. Stevens's dissent, id. at 309–18, would also treat conduct reasonably understood as calling for an answer as interrogation, without limiting protection to successful or predictably incriminating elicitation. It supplies particular support for his refusal to excuse evidentiary use merely because the substantive question served a protective purpose. It does not establish any unreported fact about these officers. [Primary text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/446/291).

**Muniz.** Parts I, II, III-A and IV were joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy among the present Associates. Part III-B's testimonial-content analysis was joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; Stevens instead joined Rehnquist's separate performance rationale. Part III-C's administrative-booking rationale had Brennan, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, four votes. It accepted that the direct questions were interrogation while allowing the described administrative questions; its footnote 14 expressly preserves the limit against using booking questions to extract incriminating admissions. Part IV addresses limited instructions and procedure-understanding questions, not all requests for substantive factual accounts. No majority medical-question exception already exists in those portions. [Primary text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/496/582), 496 U.S. at 588–608 and n.14.

The sustaining commitment therefore must be stated as a **bounded adoption and application of an administrative-booking ground**, supported by Muniz's rationale and procedural distinctions. It cannot be rendered as a finding that direct medical questions are categorically outside Innis or that every such answer is physical evidence. Its operative limits are substantive: the question must be reasonably related to assessing necessary medical attention for the observed condition; its content and setting must support that administrative function; an investigative demand or an objectively reasonably likely means of extracting incriminating offense information is outside the accepted ground. Medical intent alone, the booking location, and the existence of a camera do not suffice.

The reported visible limp, short condition-focused question, and accepted medical-intake finding provide affirmative support for this particular application. The source does not ask how the injury happened, identify the questioner as Rhodes, or establish that questioner's knowledge of the pursuit. Absence of those facts is not a finding of ignorance. The seven do not require that invented finding: they sustain the limited administrative question on its actual content and found setting. Its answer's later evidentiary usefulness does not by itself defeat the exception they adopt. They acknowledge the accident assertion's testimonial character rather than transform it into bodily performance.

Stevens's contrary position is also supported. He accepts the medical-purpose finding, recognizes the permissibility of attending to health, and rejects admission of this responsive factual account without the required protection. His Muniz join does not require acceptance of Part III-C: the joined separate opinion treated the responses there as demonstrations of mental or physical functioning rather than substantive historical assertions. An accident assertion has materially different content. His refusal to extend the booking rationale accordingly does not repudiate his physical-evidence or procedural-communication joins.

**Quarles.** O'Connor's separate opinion, 467 U.S. at 660–74, is material adverse evidence for an unqualified medical-purpose exception: it rejects evidentiary use of unwarned testimonial interrogation merely to advance a protective end. Her later express Muniz III-C join nevertheless supplies the narrower administrative distinction. Stevens's join in Marshall's dissent, id. at 674–90, supports separating police ability to ask protective questions from criminal use of answers. Neither position supplies an actual emergency or imports a public-safety or derivative-evidence ruling here. [Primary text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/467/649).

## Recording, identification and remedy safeguards

For the recording, all eight may join the proposition that mere capture of physical appearance or movement is not compelled testimonial disclosure under Muniz's Court portions. The video medium changes neither a factual answer's testimonial character nor the limits of a valid administrative ground. Suppression of one unlawful answer would not automatically suppress separable physical observations. Conversely, physical character alone is not an evidentiary ruling under Rules 401 or 403. The lower opinion reports an objection and rejection for lack of abuse, but does not reproduce the arguments or trial presentation. Rejection of the independent evidentiary objection is sustained only within that reported, bounded adjudication. No affirmative new balancing, particular trial use, blanket admission, or absence-of-review finding is supplied.

The identification commitments also remain bounded. Manson's reliability inquiry and Biggers's considerations apply to both the out-of-court and in-court challenges. The short interval and reported observation do not erase the suggestion; the actual independent-recollection and reliability findings, reviewed below, provide the additional record basis. Stevens's Manson concurrence particularly rejects substituting corroborating guilt evidence for reliability of the identification. Neither a new five-finding checklist nor imagined certainty, lighting, detailed description or testimony is required or supplied.

Stevens's remedy follows a legally identified admission error, not a request to discover whether error occurred. Chapman governs preserved constitutional admission error on this direct review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the affected conviction. Review must examine the actual presentation and each count actually affected. A count may stand if the Government carries that burden; otherwise the lawful new-trial consequence applies to the affected conviction. No count-specific harmlessness, automatic conviction-wide reversal, immediate new trial or acquittal is decided now. The answer's possible presence on the tape is part of that inquiry only if the actual record establishes it.

Muniz's separate opinion joined by Stevens expressly accepted remand for consideration of harmlessness once the Court found an answer inadmissible, 496 U.S. at 608. That source confirms the compatibility of his preserved evidentiary distinction and a limited remedy; it adds no new factual premise. Actual Tuggle and Allridge likewise distinguish correction of an adjudicative error from immediate final relief, but their collateral categories do not supply this direct appeal's harmlessness rule. Ordinary nonconstitutional evidentiary error retains Kotteakos's different whole-trial inquiry. None of the eight finds such an independent video error here.

The seven sustaining Associates identify no admission error on the claims they resolve and do not reach harmlessness. Their affirmance must not be described as a conclusion that the pursuit, arrest, guns or identifications make an error harmless. Nor does a missing digitally retrieved transcript count in the Government's favor as evidence. The source limit constrains broader adjudication and any fresh prejudice determination without invalidating the reported findings.
