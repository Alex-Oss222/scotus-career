## Common component conclusions and exact proposed action

**A — Review channel.** Exercise the supplied direct appellate jurisdiction in each of the three dockets. The required three-judge court granted an injunction on the constitutional merits. Miller Part I-B, joined by each Associate, preserves the distinction between that merits grant or denial and a nonmerits dismissal. The substantial constitutional claim does not become jurisdictionally frivolous merely because Shaw and Miller bear strongly on its ultimate success. No invented defect in panel designation, notice, intervention or appeal papers defeats the reconstructed channel. This conclusion does not decide whether a different, nonmerits judgment would follow the same route.

**B — Five personally assigned residents.** Reject the contention that an additional electoral or downstream loss is indispensable to Article III standing. Blum and Powers challenge their own assignment to District 18, Thomas and Vera their own assignment to District 29, and Orcutt his own assignment to District 30. Their claimed personal subjection to the State's race-based assignment is the relationship to governmental action recognized in Miller Part I-A. That holding was unanimous and did not depend on the claimant winning the asserted equality theory. It is not enough that someone resides somewhere in Texas or dislikes the plan. Here the specified residents challenge their own electoral assignment, not merely the racial composition of someone else's district.

The assignment governs where each plaintiff lives and votes; the challenged plan is the asserted source of that treatment. Relief against use of that assignment would remove the complained-of governmental treatment if the legal theory prevailed. Redressability does not require advance success on the merits or proof that a plaintiff would win an election, receive a preferred representative, or obtain a particular replacement district. Conversely, it does not permit a speculative chain of independent third-party decisions. These are the direct assignment and relief relationships supplied by the adopted record.

This conclusion preserves Lujan's litigation-stage support requirement. The Court accepts the reconstructed lower-court adjudication of the identified residents' relationship to their own districts and decides whether the absence of a further injury defeats standing as a matter of law. It neither finds a newly pleaded injury nor holds that unsupported allegations would always suffice after trial. No actual dispute about a plaintiff's residence, eligibility, exposure or the plan's operation is supplied that would justify a factfinding remand. Missing original research papers do not become a failure of the plaintiffs' proof.

**C — Chen and district-specific limits.** Chen supplies no independent Article III basis for the relief concerning Districts 18, 29 and 30: he is assigned to District 25 and alleges no personal connection to the challenged racial assignments in the three districts under review. His generalized concern cannot be pooled with the five residents' personal exposure to create his own injury. His deficiency does not remove the five residents or require dismissal of their claims. At least one personally affected plaintiff supports adjudication of each of the three district challenges; the Court need not decide every possible nonresident or cross-district claim to resolve the standing objection to the decree. Chen's unsuccessful District 25 merits claim and the rejection of the other district challenges are not reopened in these defendants' appeals. No plaintiff appeal is supplied. No new order dismissing his distinct, unappealed District 25 claim is warranted.

**D — What the standing holding does not decide.** Preserve the separate five-Justice Shaw/Miller substantive rule. Those decisions do not treat predominant territorial design alone as the asserted equal-protection deprivation, but that merits conclusion does not retract Miller's unanimous standing holding. Nor do they require a universal second governmental act, financial loss, changed election winner or later denial of service: genuinely unequal governmental status or treatment can itself be the completed substantive wrong in its proper setting. Nothing here recognizes such an additional wrong in this record, recasts testimony about precincts and ballots as an individual pleaded injury, revives the dismissed statutory or constitutional claims, applies strict scrutiny, finds adequate or inadequate fit, or approves a map. Recognition of standing is not approval of the declaration or injunction on the constitutional merits.

**E — Selected disposition of the three appeals.** Resolve the specified Article III question in favor of the five residents, reject the request to set aside the three-district decree for want of their standing, and retain Nos. 94-805, 94-806 and 94-988 for their remaining lawful disposition. Do not enter a terminal affirmance of the merits judgment, a merits reversal, or a vacatur of the declaration or injunction on this standing ruling. The remaining constitutional merits were expressly excluded from the reconstructed question; they are not silently decided by an affirmance label. This is a decision of the limited standing issue with retained appellate proceedings, not a claim that the full appeals have reached terminal disposition.

The proposed operative language is: **The Article III objection to adjudication of the claims of Blum, Powers, Thomas, Vera and Orcutt concerning their respective Districts 18, 29 and 30 is rejected. Chen furnishes no independent standing to obtain relief concerning those districts. The Court retains the three appeals for their remaining disposition. The existing declaration and injunction are neither affirmed on the merits nor vacated by this decision.** The actual final wording must preserve each of those consequences. ## Enhanced alternatives and strongest objections

### 1. Personal-assignment standing or a narrower/reconsidered Article III rule

The supported path applies Miller I-A to each personally exposed resident while enforcing Chen's different boundary. Its strongest objection is that the alleged injury is simply conscious territorial construction, which Shaw/Miller do not recognize as the asserted substantive deprivation. The answer is the controlling distinction Miller itself adopted: these actual plaintiffs challenge treatment governing their own electoral assignment, and their legal entitlement can fail without destroying the personal relationship and conditional redressability. No ultimate vote loss was required in Miller. Each Associate personally joined that distinction; four also joined the rejection on the merits. It cannot be discarded by relabeling the merits conclusion an absence of jurisdiction.

A narrower path would treat only an additional deprivation, or an assignment shown to impose an independently cognizable unequal status, as sufficient for Article III. As a rule requiring actual substantive success, that path conflicts with Miller I-A and Warth's distinction as incorporated there. It could be advanced only as an express reconsideration of Miller's standing proposition, or on a supported factual difference concerning actual personal exposure. This record supplies neither a different exposure relationship for the five residents nor a Justice-specific changed premise justifying reversal of any Associate's Miller standing join. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas's Lujan positions support enforcing exposure and proof, but not merging a legal merits deficiency with those requirements. The personal-classification objections of O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas point toward recognizing the residents' personal relationship, not denying it. The alternate rule therefore does not receive an independent commitment from any of the eight on the supplied record.

The other boundary is independently necessary: a generalized objection by Chen does not acquire standing from the five plaintiffs' valid personal connections. Nor is residence a universally sufficient substitute for exposure. Nothing in the chosen rule decides the standing of all nonresidents, a statewide action or a plaintiff asserting a different concrete injury.

### 2. Terminal affirmance, jurisdictional vacatur, limited remand or retained appeals

**Terminal affirmance:** its strongest argument is that the only question accepted for decision is standing, the lower court got that question right, and the Court ordinarily affirms when the asserted appellate error fails. The join barrier is the actual object of these direct appeals: a declaration of unconstitutionality and an injunction, not a separately appealable abstract standing declaration. A bare affirmance would leave it unclear whether the Court had adjudicated the excluded constitutional merits. A formula saying only that the standing ruling is sustained can accurately describe A–C, but must not be used to certify completion of the appeals or to announce that the lower merits judgment was reviewed and affirmed. All eight can support that descriptive formulation only with the retained-stage and relief qualifications in E–F. No independent commitment to a terminal merits affirmance is supplied.

**Jurisdictional vacatur or dismissal:** if the five supporting residents lacked standing, the declaration, injunction and dependent merits proceedings could not survive on Chen or the dismissed claims. That is a coherent consequence of the contrary Article III path, and it would require an express decree addressing all three appeals and the existing relief. It is not the selected result, because the factual predicate for applying that path contradicts the retained Miller standing rule as applied here. Chen's defect alone does not justify it.

**Vacatur/remand under Shaw or Miller:** the strongest argument is that Miller was decided after the lower court's 1994 orders and bears directly on their constitutional rationale. But that is a reason to address the merits through a properly authorized further disposition, not a ground to disguise an adjudication of the excluded merits as the consequence of standing. The Court has not been given a new merits grant, a merits submission or a separate disposition reconsidering the injunction under intervening law. A future properly supported order returning the merits for consideration is not foreclosed; it is not entered by this commitment. Nor does the current record establish a genuine unresolved residence or exposure question requiring a lower-court evidence remand. None of the eight directs the district court to search for a different injury, replead the case, revive dismissed claims or conduct a new trial.

**Retention:** its strongest objection is that it leaves the constitutional injunction and three appeals unresolved after answering the narrow question. That is a real procedural consequence, not a defect to conceal with an overbroad decree. It preserves a judicial controversy and the current orders while ensuring that the remaining disposition receives its proper scope and grounds. It changes no final merits rights, creates no new automatic stay and specifies no invented future event. The eight selected commitments therefore resolve standing now and retain the appeals. Their substantive disagreement about Shaw/Miller remains available if a later event lawfully presents it. This is a bounded disposition, not a refusal to decide the supplied standing question.
