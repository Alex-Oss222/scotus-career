**Common rule and explanation:** When LSD is absorbed into the blotter-paper carrier presented here, §841(b)(1)'s mixture-or-substance weight includes the actual carrier weight under Chapman. Amendment 488's 0.4-milligram-per-dose method changes the Drug Quantity Table calculation, not that statutory calculation. Its own commentary preserves mandatory minima; under §5G1.1(b), a statutory minimum above the maximum of the applicable Guidelines range supplies the Guidelines sentence. Stinson gives valid interpretive commentary authority within its domain and expressly excludes conflict with a federal statute. It does not authorize the Commission to amend a statute or displace Chapman's settled construction.

The strongest alternative treats the amendment as expert clarification of how to count some carrier weight or as a reason to overrule Chapman. The frozen rule rejects both: Chapman requires actual mixture weight, not an assigned proxy, and the Commission's different policy choice does not remove the statutory precedent's foundation. Patterson's statutory-stare-decisis principle supports retaining the construction absent an adequate justification for change; Congress remains able to change the statute. No holding declares statutory precedent irreconsiderable under all circumstances. The amendment's account of carrier disparities is accepted as its policy reason, not converted into a judicial finding that every application is constitutional or proportionate.

**Scope and remedy:** Affirmance leaves the reviewed statutory-minimum judgment in place; it is not a directive to compute or impose a new sentence. The neutral commitments deliberately do not adopt dose totals, carrier weights, a new statutory tier or an unpresented exemption. Historical source particulars corroborate posture but are not newly made outcome-material factual grounds. Hypothetical unusually heavy carriers with very little LSD and distinct constitutional challenges are not resolved. The Commission's general deference entitlement, beyond the source-of-law distinction sufficient here, is not decided. The historical opinion's post-divergence Lechmere citation is not imported as an additional simulated rule.

Amendment 488's actual qualifications remain separate: an unmarked quarter-inch by quarter-inch blotter section is presumed a dose for its calculation; liquid LSD not yet placed on a carrier may warrant an upward departure where pure-drug weight inadequately reflects seriousness. Neither qualification is an exception to the statutory minimum or an instruction to impose a departure here. No Guidelines temporal question or future congressional revision is adjudicated.
