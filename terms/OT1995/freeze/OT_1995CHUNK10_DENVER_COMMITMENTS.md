## Shared decisional premises and qualifications

### Reach, participation and operative text

All eight Associate Justices reach the challenged 1992 provisions and FCC rules through review of the June 6, 1995 en banc judgment, 56 F.3d 105, under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Each participates at argument and decision. No jurisdictional defect, litigant default, damages issue, or adjudicated removal of a particular program is supplied.

The provisions are distinct:

1. **Section 10(a):** An operator may prospectively enforce a written and published policy prohibiting programming it reasonably believes describes or depicts sexual or excretory activities or organs in a patently offensive manner under contemporary community standards. Permission is not a command to prohibit. The FCC's definition specifies standards for the cable medium. It is not Miller obscenity and carries no categorical serious-value exception; context still matters.
2. **Section 10(b):** When an operator has not voluntarily prohibited covered leased-access programming, the FCC must require provider identification, concentration on a single channel, blocking unless the subscriber requests access in writing, and programmer notification. The FCC's written request includes a statement that the requester is at least eighteen. Access must be supplied within thirty days, not after a mandatory thirty-day wait. The agency permits another blocked channel if the first lacks capacity. The described alternative for systems lacking premium/pay-per-view channels is an operator-controlled lockbox whose key or numeric code is retained until unblocking is requested. That party-reported accommodation is distinct from a parent-controlled lockbox; no numeric size threshold or universal feasibility finding is adopted. Neither accommodation eliminates the compulsory initial denial or written opt-in.
3. **Section 10(c):** Congress directs enabling regulations for operator prohibition of obscene material, sexually explicit conduct, or material soliciting/promoting unlawful conduct on PEG capacity. Its enacted categories differ from (a). The FCC construes sexually explicit conduct through its indecency definition and unlawful-conduct material as otherwise proscribed material, and permits operator certification requirements. Section 10(c) supplies neither (a)'s express prospective written-and-published-policy/reasonable-belief terms nor (b)'s mandatory blocking/request mechanism. Local selection of access speakers does not by itself classify every PEG channel as a constitutional public forum.
4. **Section 10(d):** The access-programming immunity exception concerns obscenity, not all protected indecency. It supplies no general threat of civil or criminal liability for transmitting protected indecent expression. No independent invalidation of (d) is proposed.

### Existing law actually applied

**Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions — present operation effective Turner, June 27, 1994:** Cable editorial selection and programmers' expression receive ordinary First Amendment protection; broadcast scarcity does not reduce cable review. Content classification examines selection and justification. The general content-based formulation has the eight joins identified in the supplied Turner entry, with Stevens's narrower position preserved. The five-Justice intermediate rule concerns the content-neutral full-power must-carry provisions. It is not the standard for (b)'s express indecency classification. The controlling limited predictive-review principle requires substantial consideration of supported legislative judgments together with independent constitutional review; neither a formal legislative trial record nor scientific certainty is indispensable. The four-Justice Turner conclusion that further evidence was needed is not an all-purpose remand requirement.

**ACT / Pacifica, March 4, 1996:** Protected nonobscene indecency, compelling child-protection and parental-supervision interests, and independent necessity/means review are governing law. The actual sixteen-hour broadcast schedule's invalidity does not decide any cable provision. Souter joined the protected-speech standard but proposed renewed review rather than final invalidity; Stevens, Scalia and Breyer would sustain that schedule on its actual contextual record. Those distinctions remain operative evidence of their positions.

**Private parade composition and compelled messages — present operation effective Hurley, June 19, 1995:** Souter's opinion for all nine protects the actual private expressive presentation against compelled insertion. It does not declare access-channel programs part of an operator's own presentation merely because the operator transmits them. Conversely, statutory access does not itself establish public ownership or a constitutional public forum. Rosenberger's actual independently edited publication program illustrates why attribution and the terms of an opened channel matter; its viewpoint holding is not a holding that every subject restriction is viewpoint discrimination. Lebron's special-creation/permanent-majority-appointment rule, in the supplied Speech Law slice, likewise does not make every regulated franchise governmental.

Predivergence Sable supplies the adult-access distinction and careful tailoring; Pacifica supplies its limited contextual broadcast principle. Blum/Jackson/Lugar and Reitman/Crawford frame the difference between review of legislation and attribution of a private editorial act. Perry/Cornelius require the actual forum arrangement. Alaska Airlines supplies independent operability and congressional-intent severability. These sources support the competing paths; no generic medium, ownership, or forum label resolves them all.

### Earlier June 26 law

Colorado adds no cable-specific test. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer's protection of a genuinely independent speaker cautions against equating institutional relationship with actual attribution. Stevens and Ginsburg's dissent permits a supported institution-specific contribution-channel judgment, not unexamined deference to any institutional label. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas's further party-expression position distinguishes a speaker's own message from another person's resources. Applying those distinctions here requires deciding whose editorial presentation and which governmental burden is involved. None of Colorado's positions supplies a new finding about PEG governance, cable technology or coercion. Reading the addendum therefore changes no statutory premise or operative cable standard; its Justice-specific implications are incorporated below.

### Alternatives, intervening law and shared remedial limits

The competing assessment of (b) considers the affirmative alternatives supplied: subscriber-requested devices under §544(d)(2), premium-preview notice and requested blocking under the separate §544(d)(3), program notice/schedules and parental control, and the 1996 section 504 no-charge requested full audio/video blocking duty. Section 504 has no sexual-content trigger in its text; section 505 separately concerns channels primarily dedicated to sexually oriented programming and carries its own blocking/time-rule conditions and thirty-day effective date. Neither enactment proves universal technical implementation. Future section 551 ratings/equipment is not an operating national alternative, and no future V-chip fact enters a commitment.

All eight reserve any independent facial challenge to **1996 section 506**. Its subsection (a) permits refusal of a public-access program or portion containing obscenity, indecency or nudity; public access is not silently expanded to every educational and governmental category. Subsection (b) supplies corresponding leased-access authority. The words and qualifications differ from the 1992 provisions. Those amendments do not repeal the challenged rules or automatically eliminate every continuing legal interest; they also prevent a judgment about §10 from guaranteeing carriage or erasing an independent source of authority. The 1996 Act's **section 561** requires the specified facial Title V challenges to proceed before a three-judge district court under §2284; direct appeal is supplied for the specified interlocutory or final holding of unconstitutionality and must be filed within twenty days. This is not the different provision codified at **47 U.S.C. §561**, and not an appeal from every district-court ruling.

The present review can decide the challenged 1992/FCC authority and order relief against it. Any remand must preserve the independent 1996 text, decide genuinely remaining remedial interactions within the issues properly presented, and avoid ordering a named program carried by force of invalidating §10 alone. No result below imposes a new FCC hearing, a universal franchise inquiry, a damages award, a broadcast timetable, a fixed compliance date, or an automatic interim injunction.

## Individual commitments and enhanced comparison

Each entry identifies a formal component position, sufficient grounds, competing path and strongest objection, permissible narrower reach, and the corresponding remedy. ### Stevens

**Own eligible anchors:** His Pacifica Part IV, especially the Court's contextual IV-C; his Sable Part IV join; his Turner concurrence accepting independent review but finding the existing structural record sufficient; his ACT dissent, joined by Scalia and Breyer; his full Hurley join. His Colorado dissent with Ginsburg supplies an institution-specific justification principle, not categorical power to restrict cable speech.

**Section 10(a) — affirm facial validity, independently of (b).** The limited restoration of an operator's own choice, constrained by a prospective written published policy and reasonable belief, may be sustained without treating all indecency as inferior speech or all cable as broadcasting. Hurley protects an actual private editor, and the compulsory-access setting does not erase that interest. The alternative is to treat selective repeal of access protection as government censorship regardless of the operator's independent choice. Its strongest point is Congress's express selection of sexual/excretory content and the integrated ban-or-block structure. Removing invalid (b) answers the compulsory-structure objection; a remaining prospective private-choice permission remains independently operable. The strongest objection to affirmance is that regulation cannot be insulated simply by using a private decision-maker. Stevens accepts that objection for genuine governmental inducement or control, but does not infer it from permission alone. He will not join a universal no-state-action rule or validate a particular franchise's ban.

**Section 10(b) — reverse approval and require removal of the compulsory segregation/blocking scheme.** Protected programs are transferred to a common restricted outlet, every household is initially denied them, and written subscriber action is required before adult access. These combined burdens materially differ from the actual broadcast timing structure he accepted in ACT. Here established cable channel-control mechanisms and requested blocking allow direct parental assistance without making every adult ask to restore access to the whole category. The strongest defense is that unpredictable mixed access schedules defeat ordinary parental scheduling and that written opt-in is less restrictive than a ban. Stevens credits both points but concludes they do not justify the cumulative mandatory restructuring, especially the all-or-nothing channel choice for mixed programming. This is a constitutional assessment of affirmative statutory features and supplied alternatives, not a finding that the government supplied no evidence or that every device is foolproof. He would not invalidate a narrowly drawn subscriber-control measure merely because some parental action is required.

**Section 10(c) — reverse the approval of the protected-indecency authorization and implementing rule at that scope.** Locally selected PEG programming is not established as an operator's own editorial presentation. Congress's broad permission to displace that selection, without (a)'s express prospective written-policy and reasonable-belief constraints, is not justified by merely invoking the operator's freedom. The competing private-owner approach has genuine force where an operator actually composes expression, but transmission ownership alone cannot answer this broader permission over access programming selected through local arrangements. The child-protection defense also lacks (b)'s promised adult restoration path; this is permission for complete denial, although no operator is commanded to ban. His conclusion does not classify all PEG as public forums and does not rest on the falsity of every conceivable operator interest. He would preserve ordinary obscenity and otherwise unlawful-material rules, reserve genuinely private expressive applications under their own law, and give no final §506 ruling.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join a contextual opinion sustaining the independent limited permission in (a) and rejecting the actual burdens in (b)/(c). Cannot join a speech-value hierarchy, universal public-forum theory, or categorical equation of all regulation with state action. Sever (b) from (a) under Alaska Airlines: the private permission functions without the rejected blocking command. For (c), remove the challenged authorization for protected indecency while preserving independently lawful unprotected-material authority and 1996-law questions.

### O'Connor

**Own eligible anchors:** Her Turner separate Parts I and III protect cable editorial autonomy and reject content-selected compulsory carriage; Part II separately rejects excessive burdens even assuming neutrality. Her Sable Part IV join, ACT Parts I–III join, Rosenberger concurrence on actual safeguards, Hurley join, and Colorado Part I and Parts II–III distinguish actual independent expression from categorical attribution.

**Section 10(a) — affirm on independent private-choice grounds.** The First Amendment ordinarily protects the operator's editorial refusal; Congress's limited relaxation of a statutory carriage constraint does not, without more, make the resulting private choice federal censorship. The strongest contrary path treats the content-defined alteration itself as a burden on the programmers' statutory access. She rejects the premise that every statutory entitlement to private editorial space becomes constitutionally irreversible as to its subject limits. Her Turner and Hurley positions supply the distinct operator interest. The counterargument that (b) effectively coerces prohibitions is answered by separately invalidating (b), not by presuming inevitable financial coercion the packet does not establish. She would reach an actual coercion or governmental-control application if supported, and would not sustain an operator's violation of an independently binding forum or access obligation simply by citing §10(a).

**Section 10(b) — reverse and set aside the compulsory scheme.** This is government content regulation, not protected operator choice. Her ACT join requires a real connection between child protection and the actual adult burden; her Turner alternative explains why broad statutory design may fail without a demand for endless factual development. The combined default block, written age request and forced regrouping of all covered programs burden willing adults beyond justified parental assistance. The strongest sustaining path relies on Sable's consideration of age screening and unpredictable programs. She distinguishes a tailored access-control method from compulsory segregation and continuing denial to every adult who has not affirmatively restored an entire channel. Existing parental controls and the new request-blocking obligation are affirmative, legally available alternatives, not assumed proof of flawless household compliance. She does not treat thirty days as a mandatory wait, declare all subscriber identification unconstitutional, or import Turner's intermediate standard.

**Section 10(c) — affirm against the presented facial challenge on private-choice grounds.** The statute enables, rather than requires, a private operator's refusal. There is no (b) compulsion on PEG. Neither a franchise nor a PEG label alone establishes that every refusal is governmental, and the facial theory cannot convert all private transmission capacity into a public forum. Her Rosenberger concurrence requires the actual institutional safeguards and attribution rather than a generalized analogy. The strongest invalidity argument is that locally selected access expression is not the operator's message and that (c) lacks (a)'s safeguards. She agrees those differences can matter to a properly established channel-specific governmental or contractual claim, but they do not establish universal constitutional attribution of an independent private refusal. The lack of a prescribed written policy does not make the private editor a governmental licensor. Her affirmance does not determine every PEG facility's status or decide §506.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join the same private-choice ground for (a)/(c), with actual state involvement and local obligations reserved. Can join a strict-tailoring invalidation of (b), including its integral identification/notification machinery, without an independent generic-vagueness holding. Cannot join final facial invalidation of (c), a blanket exemption from government-action review, or a compulsory-carriage order. Sever (b); affirm the distinct private permissions within these limits.

### Scalia

**Own eligible anchors:** His Sable concurrence at 131–133, including the distinction between a government ban and a utility's duty to carry; his joins in O'Connor's complete Turner separate opinion and Kennedy's protected-expression/content portions; his ACT dissent join; his full Hurley join; his Lebron constitutional-status opinion; and his Colorado joins in O'Connor's Part I and Kennedy's separate party-expression ground.

**Section 10(a) — affirm.** His Sable reservation and Turner/Hurley positions support recognizing an independent private refusal rather than creating a constitutional carriage entitlement from regulation. The content-based-restoration challenge correctly identifies governmental legislation but fails to establish that every resulting editorial refusal is governmental conduct. Lebron's actual governmental-creation/control conditions cannot be replaced with regulation and a franchise monopoly. The strongest objection is practical compulsion through (b). Scalia does not rely on a finding of no possible incentive, but the supported lower finding does not establish inevitable censoring and the facial statutory permission itself does not command refusal. If (b) falls, (a) remains independently operable. He would not approve a governmental official's use of an operator as a censor or immunize government-controlled channels under this private-choice rationale.

**Section 10(b) — affirm under the protected-indecency standard, separately from the private-choice ground.** The command is governmental and content selected; he does not deny either feature. Sable distinguished a wholesale ban from technological adult access controls, and his concurrence recognizes that the adequacy of protection must be judged in the actual setting without demanding a committee report. Here programmers may transmit, willing adult subscribers may obtain the segregated channel, and the default screen addresses the risk of unexpected child access to mixed leased programming. His ACT dissent accepts practical channeling where adults retain meaningful access; this cable measure uses household choice rather than making the speech unavailable throughout sixteen hours. The strongest contrary case combines written requests, regrouping, possible activation delay and less restrictive parent-operated controls. He regards the request as a confined age/access step and segregation as what makes the adult choice administrable; schedules and a parent-activated lockbox do not equally address unexpected access by children in households that have not activated a block. This conclusion accepts practical predictions, not guaranteed prevention, and does not assume the statutory section 504 duty has been universally implemented. He rejects neither protected status nor independent review.

**Section 10(c) — affirm.** The private refusal principle applies with no mandatory (b) mechanism. The strongest contrary argument is that PEG selection may be entrusted to other bodies and that Congress has selected a category for exclusion. In his view, neither circumstance proves governmental status for every private operator decision or confers an immutable constitutional entitlement to use private facilities. His Lebron rule preserves a different answer where the controlling institution is genuinely governmental; it does not establish such a status for all PEG. The FCC's construction of unlawful-conduct material as otherwise proscribed remains essential. He rejects a public-forum conclusion derived solely from Congress's channel designation, while reserving supported channel-specific governmental control and access claims.

**Joins/remedy:** Would affirm all three distinct components for their separately stated reasons, not a single no-state-action rationale. Will join rejection of the facial generic-vagueness claim under the context-sensitive definition and reasonable-belief terms where applicable: (a)'s reasonableness condition does not govern (c). Programmer notification/certification implementing the lawful screen is not automatically a governmental licensing board's prior restraint. No categorical serious-value exclusion or immunity for arbitrary governmental censorship is adopted. If another component is invalidated, he accepts independent severability of (a)/(c) and no expansion of relief to §506.

### Kennedy

**Own eligible anchors:** His Turner protected-expression/content-classification writing and its distinction between neutral source preservation and preference for messages; his Sable Part IV join; his ACT Parts I–III join; his Rosenberger Court opinion concerning independent expression in an opened channel; his Hurley join; and his Colorado concurrence protecting an institution's own speech while distinguishing actual transfers and conduits.

**Section 10(a) — reverse approval of the content-selected access exclusion.** Congress itself has selected protected sexual/excretory expression for removal from the otherwise established access protection. His ground addresses that legislative burden, not a declaration that every later private refusal is a state act. Turner protects programmers as well as operators and permits structural source regulation without authorizing government to select disfavored messages. The strongest sustaining path invokes the operator's private right not to speak, supported by Hurley and his own Colorado opinion. He distinguishes the program of an actual private speaker from a broad access obligation whose independently supplied messages are now singled out by Congress; a proven constitutional editorial claim remains available, but cannot justify the entire subject-selected withdrawal. Rosenberger informs that opened-channel distinction without converting this subject classification into viewpoint discrimination or every cable channel into a public forum. The child-protection purpose remains compelling, but allowing complete denial to adults is not carefully confined to that purpose. He can invalidate (a) independently of (b), without an unproved coercive-cost theory.

**Section 10(b) — reverse and remove the compulsory scheme.** It expressly burdens protected expression by content. The written-request condition is imposed on every adult subscriber, rather than on a demonstrated need to prevent a particular child's access; segregation then forces a single collective choice over otherwise different programs. Sable and his ACT join demand preservation of practical adult access through adequately tailored means. The strongest defense is that only initial screening is required and that mixed schedules impair parental supervision. He accepts the relevant risks but considers the actual combination unnecessarily restrictive given affirmative household blocking and notice alternatives. Sable's discussion of access codes supplies an example of a narrower adult-access mechanism, not approval of every written-request system in another medium. He does not rest on actual disclosure of subscriber lists, universal immediate blocking technology, or the absence of any legislative evidence. A more confined parental-control requirement remains open.

**Section 10(c) — reverse approval insofar as it authorizes exclusion of protected indecent PEG speech.** The case against content-selected legislative displacement is especially strong where speakers and purposes are selected through local access arrangements. Mere ownership of the cable plant does not establish that those messages constitute the operator's own editorial presentation. The contrary private-owner argument is substantial under Hurley, but its necessary expressive premise cannot be assumed for the statutory class. Neither the broad statutory category nor the agency's contextual definition supplies a child/adult distinction when the permission is exercised as complete exclusion. His ground reaches the governmental authorization for protected-content discrimination while preserving the potential of an actual private expressive defense. He does not announce that every PEG facility is a public forum or invalidate properly limited obscenity and otherwise-unlawful-material controls.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join the protected-content/tailoring grounds for all three provisions, with the distinct legislative-action and private-attribution questions expressly separated. Cannot join a no-state-action affirmance of (a)/(c), a broadcast-scarcity test, or relief requiring transmission notwithstanding independent rights and §506. Each challenged authorization must receive its own set-aside scope; no whole-Act judgment or forced program carriage follows.

### Souter

**Own eligible anchors:** His full Turner join, including its four-Justice record application; his ACT concurrence in part and dissent in part; his Hurley Court opinion; his Rosenberger dissent and the supplied contextual Chabad holding; and his Colorado joins in Part I and the separate narrower Parts II–III.

**Section 10(a) — affirm the independent limited permission.** Hurley gives concrete weight to the operator's private editorial interest. The written, published, prospective policy and reasonable-belief conditions confine the permission; neither regulation nor statutory access alone makes every choice governmental. The alternative legislative-discrimination theory correctly demands scrutiny of Congress's own acts, but does not eliminate the First Amendment interest in withdrawing a compulsory private carriage duty. He is willing to sustain this particular permission without deciding that every delegation or encouraged refusal escapes review. The strongest contrary point is that the ban and blocking branches were enacted together. Separating and invalidating the compulsory branch removes that structural objection while leaving an independently intelligible operator choice. He joins no finding of universally absent coercion or settled status for every access channel.

**Section 10(b) — reverse and require removal of the compulsory structure.** His ACT reservation was tied to renewed examination of an actual timetable's boundary and evidence; it does not make every unconstitutional design curable by additional research. This provision affirmatively groups diverse protected programs, blocks all households, and requires a written adult election for the whole channel. Even accepting the lower account of unpredictability and the feasibility of the prescribed blocking mechanism, those combined burdens exceed a properly tailored parental-assistance measure when requested household controls are available. The strongest alternative is a Turner-style remand for further comparative evidence, or affirmance because the adult can eventually opt in. He rejects remand here because the dispositive burden is the enacted combination, not an unresolved numerical uptake rate or disputed numeric exception. He does not find every alternative technically equivalent, infer actual privacy disclosure, or require an evidentiary hearing. A remand to implement removal differs from a remand leaving this structure's validity open.

**Section 10(c) — vacate approval and remand for the distinct constitutional inquiry; no final facial invalidity commitment.** The lower blanket private-choice explanation does not sufficiently distinguish PEG arrangements in which editorial selection belongs to local access bodies from a private expressive presentation. But the packet does not establish one constitutional actor/forum classification for the whole class. Hurley requires an actual private expressive burden; Rosenberger and Chabad preserve attention to the actual institution and attribution. The strongest invalidity path treats the enactment's content selection as enough for final relief. The strongest affirmance path treats ownership and discretion as enough to reject every facial theory. He accepts neither categorical shortcut. The court should address the governmental authorization and asserted operator expression in the actual regulatory/access arrangements, apply the protected-content standard where government burdens protected speech, and preserve the distinction between facial review and any established channel-specific claim. This is a necessary legal reexamination of the judgment's ground, not a finding that sources or litigant evidence are missing.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join (a)'s bounded private-choice affirmance and (b)'s final invalidity/removal, with no low-value speech premise. On (c), joins only vacatur for renewed review with no presumption that all PEG is a governmental forum, that all is private editorial expression, or that the provision is finally invalid. He prescribes no new universal franchise investigation, FCC hearing, automatic interim restraint or final carry order. The remand must address §506's actual independent text and legally available relief without adjudicating its constitutionality through this case.

### Thomas

**Own eligible anchors:** His joins in Kennedy's Turner protected-expression/content sections and O'Connor's Turner Parts I and III, with his nonjoin in her alternative intermediate ground preserved; his ACT Parts I–III join; his Hurley join; his Rosenberger concurrence and Court join; his Lebron join; and his Colorado joins in Part I and Kennedy's separate party-expression ground.

**Section 10(a) — affirm.** Turner and Hurley protect an actual private editor against compelled inclusion. A government choice to restore bounded discretion to that private speaker does not itself convert each exercise into a government suppression order. The strongest challenge emphasizes content selection and the statutory access entitlement. Thomas does not regard the entitlement as a constitutional transfer of all editorial ownership to access programmers; government regulation and franchise benefit alone are insufficient attribution under the supplied state-action authorities and Lebron. He reserves actual governmental control or a supported coercive application. The ban-or-block argument cannot establish universal compelled censorship merely because an operator has reasons to prefer one lawful route; if (b) is independently invalid, the permission survives without it.

**Section 10(b) — affirm under demanding content-based review.** He accepts ACT's protection of nonobscene indecency and independent review, and does not classify this command as private choice. The mechanism preserves an adult's ability to elect receipt rather than imposing ACT's broad hours of complete unavailability. Channel segregation lets households and operators identify the material that is screened; a written age declaration is connected to adult access. The lower explanation concerning unpredictable leased schedules supplies a concrete reason why ordinary parental timing controls differ. The strongest contrary path relies on default denial, the administrative request burden and household blocking alternatives. Thomas concludes those burdens remain sufficiently limited for the child-protection function: the statute requires an available adult access route, and the thirty-day provision is a compliance ceiling, not a mandatory delay. The possible adequacy of a different parent-choice scheme does not by itself establish that this arrangement violates the demanding standard. He does not adopt an intermediate test, an automatic child-protection defense, or a requirement that alternatives be foolproof; nor does he treat all serious programming as outside indecency.

**Section 10(c) — affirm the challenged private permission, reserving actual governmental facilities.** His Rosenberger position protects equality within the government's actual private-publication program, but establishes no universal constitutional right to use a privately owned cable system. His Lebron join requires genuine governmental institutional conditions rather than a label. The strongest invalidity path stresses local control of PEG selection and the lack of (a)'s safeguards. For Thomas, those matters can establish a different claim in a particular governmental arrangement; they do not convert all permissible private refusals into government censorship. The FCC's limited construction of unlawful-conduct material is preserved, and the statutory categories are not rewritten as (a)'s wording. He would not sustain a proven governmental actor's content restriction merely by calling its facility PEG or invoking an operator intermediary.

**Joins/remedy:** Would affirm (a), (b) and (c) on these separate grounds. Can join a context-sensitive rejection of facial vagueness and prior-licensing analogies while preserving legal constraints on actually governmental decisions. His prior strict ground does not authorize counting him for O'Connor's Turner intermediate-tailoring alternative. If another component falls, he accepts independent operability and severability of the remaining private permission; no independent §506 merits ruling or universal carriage entitlement follows.

### Ginsburg

**Own eligible anchors:** Her full O'Connor Turner separate-opinion join and her additional dissent concerning subject preference and inadequate justification; her ACT Court opinion; her Hurley and Lebron joins; her Rosenberger dissent join; her Chabad contextual-rule join; and her Colorado dissent join with Stevens.

**Section 10(a) — reverse approval of the governmental protected-content discrimination.** Her Turner position treats interwoven neutral and content-related purposes as content selection, not as neutralized by a benign justification. Congress has retained access protection while singling out protected sexual/excretory expression for loss of that protection. The strongest affirmance ground is an actual operator's editorial autonomy under Turner/Hurley and the difference between legislation and attribution. She preserves that difference: this disposition reviews Congress's subject-selected access rule, not every private refusal as a governmental act. A genuine independent editorial claim must be respected, but it does not justify the whole statutory permission merely from ownership of the transmission route. Her Colorado acceptance of institution-specific regulation requires the asserted institutional mechanism and fit; it does not establish a general deference rule or a cable-program attribution finding. The statutory child-protection rationale is too broad when the permitted result is complete denial to willing adults, and invalidity does not depend on proving coerced costs under (b).

**Section 10(b) — reverse and remove the compulsory scheme.** Her ACT opinion supplies the operative distinction between a compelling interest and adequate means. Here the direct burden consists of segregating programs, initial denial to every household and a written restoration request for an entire content-defined channel. Sable protects meaningful adult access, not merely theoretical availability on government-selected terms. The strongest defense is the unpredictability of programming and the practical effectiveness of a default screen. She accepts those considerations but finds the cumulative burden insufficiently confined when affirmative subscriber blocking and other parental controls address the asserted harm without presumptively withholding protected expression from all adults. The section 504 duty is relevant on its actual requested/no-charge terms, not as proof of universal efficacy. Her conclusion does not depend on actual subscriber-list disclosure, an invented serious-value safe harbor, or an assertion of no evidence in unreviewed materials.

**Section 10(c) — reverse the protected-indecency authorization.** Locally selected PEG expression and operator-authored programming are constitutionally distinct; the statutory grant of content-selected exclusion cannot be defended merely by invoking an owner's ordinary editorial choices elsewhere. Her Chabad join requires actual selection, control and attribution rather than a forum label, while her Hurley join protects an established private expressive presentation. The strongest contrary ground is that no operator is commanded to ban and that some access arrangements remain private. She concludes Congress's challenged governmental rule is nonetheless subject selected and overbroad in authorizing complete exclusion from independently selected PEG programming without a sufficient adult/child fit. She does not hold every PEG channel publicly owned, declare every private ban state action, or defeat a separately proved private expressive right. Lawful obscenity and otherwise-proscribed-material controls remain distinct.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join Kennedy at the stated legislative-content/tailoring level, but only if the opinion preserves real institutional distinctions, private editorial claims, and the actual scope of prior forum cases. Cannot join universal public-forum classification, automatic censorship attribution, or a broader §506 declaration. Relief removes the challenged protected-content rules rather than ordering publication. Her final invalidity grounds do not depend on another evidentiary search; a remand implements them and addresses independent surviving authority.

### Breyer

**Own eligible anchors:** His ACT dissent join with Stevens; his full Hurley and Lebron joins; his Rosenberger dissent join and Chabad contextual-rule join; and his Colorado joins in O'Connor's Part I and narrower Parts II–III. He was not a participant in Turner; its controlling rules bind him, but no historical or simulated Turner vote is assigned to him.

**Section 10(a) — affirm the limited independent permission.** The choice between an operator's expression and a programmer's access cannot be resolved by pretending that only one side speaks. A prospective written published policy governed by reasonable belief permits private editorial control within defined bounds. The competing path treats Congress's subject selection as dispositive. Breyer accepts that it requires constitutional attention but concludes this confined accommodation of the private operator's interest is permissible when detached from compulsory (b). Hurley's actual private-composition rule supports that interest; his Colorado position rejects categorical attribution based merely on institutional relationship. He will not join the claim that every statutory delegation is immune from review or that all access programming is necessarily the operator's own expression. A genuine governmental-control or coercive application remains open.

**Section 10(b) — reverse and require removal of the combined mandatory structure.** His ACT dissent accepted a practical contextual accommodation for broadcasting, not indiscriminate approval of every mechanism invoked to protect children. Cable's channel controls make parental choice more directly available, while the actual scheme restructures diverse programming and requires every adult to restore an entire segregated channel through a written request. The strongest defense is that default blocking addresses unpredictable encounters more reliably than parents' voluntary use of controls. He credits that advantage but finds the added practical benefit insufficient to justify the combined speech burden under the governing demanding review, given requested blocking and the other affirmative alternatives. This is no universal least-cost calculus and no newly invented multifactor test. His conclusion remains the same even if the operator-controlled lockbox accommodation works perfectly, because it implements rather than eliminates the default exclusion and request requirement. No technical finding about the accommodation's disputed eligibility is needed.

**Section 10(c) — reverse approval for protected indecent PEG expression.** The breadth of authority matters: unlike (a), the statute does not limit discretion through an express prospective published policy and reasonable-belief provision, and the affected speakers and uses come from local access arrangements rather than an established operator-composed presentation. Invoking the operator's property does not establish the necessary expressive interest at that breadth. The strongest affirmance path insists that private discretion cannot be equated with governmental censorship and that the record establishes no universal public forum. Breyer accepts both propositions but still finds the federal permission for complete content-selected exclusion inadequately justified in this statutory setting. The FCC's contextual definition is relevant but supplies no adult access path or missing statutory safeguards. He would decide the present protected-content authorization without classifying every PEG facility or adopting a comprehensive new cable test.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join Stevens's contextual (a)/(b)/(c) conclusions at the stated level and the controlling protected-speech/independent-review premises. Cannot join a speech-value hierarchy, a categorical no-state-action rule, or a universal PEG forum holding. Section (b) is independently removable while (a)'s limited choice remains operable. Section (c)'s protected-content authority is set aside at that scope; obscenity/otherwise-unlawful material, specific private expressive rights and 1996 §506 retain their separate status. No final transmission order follows.

## Proposition compatibility for reconciliation, without assembly

These are barriers and scopes, not a proposed Court opinion or an aggregate vote:

- An affirmance of (a) based on independent private choice is materially different from an affirmance assuming sufficient governmental involvement but finding a confined accommodation justified. Stevens, Souter and Breyer accept the latter bounded formulation; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas require preservation of their private-right/attribution ground. No join should be inferred merely from the common judgment position.
- Kennedy and Ginsburg's invalidity positions on (a)/(c) concern the content-selected legislative access rule. They do not rest on a finding that every operator decision is governmental or every channel a public forum. An opinion using either unsupported universal claim would lose the specified join.
- On (b), the invalidity commitments reach the combined compulsory segregation, blocking, written access and integral identification/notification structure. They do not separately invalidate every indecency definition, every age-screening mechanism or every provider notice requirement. Scalia and Thomas's affirmance requires genuinely available adult access and the actual regulatory qualifications; it is not a no-state-action disposition or a rejection of ACT's governing standard.
- On (c), Souter's vacatur is not assent to final facial invalidity. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas's facial affirmance reserves genuinely governmental and channel-specific claims. Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer's protected-content invalidity preserves ordinary obscenity and otherwise-unlawful-material controls and no adjudication of independent §506 authority.
- No Marks proposition or controlling coalition is asserted. No Chief Justice position, final public tally, author, assignment, circulation event or new join is supplied. Historical reconciliation must test these individual commitments against the frozen neutral premises and current public law before any assembly.
