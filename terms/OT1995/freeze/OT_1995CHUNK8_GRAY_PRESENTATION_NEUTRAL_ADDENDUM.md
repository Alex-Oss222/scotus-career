## 1. State Claim IX: shared quoted content and disputed breadth

Petitioner's merits brief at 13 quotes state habeas petition page 39: the Commonwealth disclosed its intention to use the Sorrell murders too late for reasonable preparation. It reports an evidentiary-hearing request at petition page 63. Respondent at 7 independently identifies Claim IX and C.A.A. 2826–2827, describing the same late-disclosure allegation and an objection to Tucker's reliability; it additionally quotes the statement that no amount of preparation time would have been adequate. That latter proposition also appears in the original state appeal excerpt at JA 124.

The original Claim IX text is thus positively represented by both sides as containing a preparation/late-notice complaint within a broader attack on uncharged-crime evidence and reliability. Respondent asserts that neither the petition nor its two amendments alleged suppressed favorable evidence or deliberate misleading about the issues, and characterizes the request at C.A.A. 2850 as a general prayer for a hearing. These broader absence assertions remain the respondent's account. This extraction has not independently read the complete petition and both amendments and does not certify those omissions.

Petitioner at 13–14 reports the Commonwealth's earlier position that Claim IX had been raised at trial and on direct appeal, quoting its dismissal brief and hearing. That concession's object is the Sorrell issue as then framed; it is not recorded here as a concession that every later formulation of deception or Brady was exhausted.

## 2. Actual state hearing and appeal excerpts

The entire reproduced state hearing excerpt at JA 110–116 first characterizes a group as legal claims requiring no further evidence, then discusses Smallwood and the Sorrell evidence. At JA 115–116 counsel also asks that those claims not be denied because further evidence could establish the likelihood of their effect on the jury. Both passages are present. Neither a categorical statement that counsel requested no evidence nor a claim that he specifically proffered all the later federal evidence follows from one passage alone.

The final order at JA 117–119 denies a plenary hearing and dismisses the petition. Claim IX is among the claims classified as decided adversely at trial and on direct appeal under Brooks/Hawks; other numbered claims receive Slayton or different treatment. The order is signed May 14, 1990 and expressly refers to an April 12 written opinion for its reasons. Thus the April 12 reference is to an earlier written opinion; the final order's signed date is separate. The appendix contents' May 23 description remains a distinct catalog discrepancy.

In the fully read **reproduced excerpt**, the state appeal's sections IV–VI explicitly contest lack of a hearing for Smallwood and ineffective-assistance claims. Section VIII, JA 122–124, asks the Virginia Supreme Court to reexamine its direct ruling concerning Claim IX and the Sorrell murders. It challenges reliability, delayed notice and the proof standard, including the allegation that late disclosure prevented reasonable preparation and the contention that no preparation time could have cured the larger asserted problem. It invokes due process and fundamental unfairness. Section VIII contains no separate express assignment that the circuit court denied a Sorrell evidentiary hearing. This is a description of the complete reproduced section, not a finding that the unexamined remainder of the state appeal contained no relevant assignment or that state procedural law made this presentation insufficient.

The January 11, 1991 order, JA 126–127, grants the Commonwealth's rehearing request, sets aside the November 1 refusal of appeal, and substitutes claim-numbered Hawks, Slayton and Penn dispositions, with remaining questions refused on the merits. The order's date and grouping control over a contrary month recited in later briefing. This source does not decide the present fair-presentation or adequate-independent-ground question.

## 3. Federal Ground A is positively quoted, not merely asserted to exist

Petitioner's merits brief at 15 identifies **federal habeas Ground A, pages 21–22**, and quotes allegations that late disclosure of the Sorrell evidence prevented reasonable preparation; that the late notice and refusal of investigator funds impaired defense of Gray's innocence of those murders; that Chesapeake police were investigating Timothy Sorrell at trial; and that favorable information suggesting another person's responsibility was not disclosed.

The quote thus supplies specific positive content: both the preparation complaint and an allegation of suppression were presented in the federal petition. It does not itself establish prior state presentation, actual suppression, deliberate deception, or the independent legal ground on which the federal court ultimately granted relief. Respondent at 9–10 points to **C.A.A. 32–35**, characterizing the same federal pleading as a broad factual recitation with general legal suggestions. The two locators identify a recoverable original if its full wording becomes dispositive. Neither party's characterization is substituted for an exhaustive reading of that original.

## 4. April 1 disposition, reconsideration, and the hearing

Both merits briefs identify an initial April 1, 1993 dismissal. Respondent at 9–10 and 42, citing **C.A.A. 194**, states that it rejected an advance-notice entitlement and separately rejected suppression as defaulted and unsubstantiated or without merit. That is an attributed party description of the April 1 order, which has not independently been recovered here. It is not a finding that every later Sorrell issue remained dismissed after reconsideration.

Petitioner's merits brief at 19 quotes the amendment, identifying its opinion pages 3–4: the court had not addressed the claim that admission of Sorrell evidence during the penalty phase violated due process, and would amend its judgment by finding that this Sorrell claim was consistently raised in state courts and was not procedurally defaulted. Respondent at 10 also acknowledges reconsideration, a preserved Sorrell claim and an ordered hearing, reproducing **C.A.A. 251–252**. Its quotation recounts the statements-only announcement, later notice of photographs/autopsy/circumstantial evidence, and Tucker as the connection to Gray. The existence and broad subject of the amendment are therefore supported by both sides, although they dispute its proper reach.

The reproduced August 25, 1994 District Court opinion, JA 335–336 and Appendix B, positively identifies **May 26, 1993** as the date of the opinion granting in part and denying in part the motion to alter or amend, and says the ordered hearing concerned whether introduction of the Sorrell evidence at sentencing violated Fourteenth Amendment due process. It reports the April 21–22, 1994 hearing. These are authenticated judicial recitations of the amendment and hearing; possession of a separate May 26 original is unnecessary to establish those limited facts.

Respondent at 11 additionally quotes a later hearing-definition order at **C.A.A. 260** concerning reliability and the opening hearing statement at **C.A.A. 265** concerning notice, cross-examination, and any undisclosed exculpatory information. It reports its own factual/legal-default objection. Petitioner at 20 quotes the same broad hearing statement. This establishes the competing recorded descriptions of the hearing's scope, without deciding that the amendment revived an independently rejected Brady ground or that later consideration implicitly did so.

The exact April 1 and May 26 texts remain needed if a proposed decision depends on the complete grounds left standing, a precise amendment of a particular claim, or what findings accompanied a cause-and-prejudice determination. ## 6. Newly recovered exact Virginia enactment

Official sources: [1995 Chapter 503](https://legacylis.virginia.gov/cgi-bin/legp604.exe?951+ful+CHAP0503) and [SB969 enacted-history page](https://legacylis.virginia.gov/cgi-bin/legp604.exe?951+sum+SB969). The full chapter and history were downloaded with a browser User-Agent and read. Preserved sources are [chapter HTML](../sources/CHUNK8_C/VA_1995_CHAP0503.html), [text extraction](../sources/CHUNK8_C/VA_1995_CHAP0503.txt), [history HTML](../sources/CHUNK8_C/VA_1995_SB969_history.html), and [history text](../sources/CHUNK8_C/VA_1995_SB969_history.txt). The official chapter states approval March 23, 1995; the official history expressly states **effective July 1, 1995**. The text-only extraction retains display markup's separate fragments, including deleted text elsewhere in the Act; the HTML controls. No deletion affects the two provisions quoted below.

Section 8.01-654(B)(2) reads:

> Such petition shall contain all allegations the facts of which are known to petitioner at the time of filing and such petition shall enumerate all previous applications and their disposition. No writ shall be granted on the basis of any allegation the facts of which petitioner had knowledge at the time of filing any previous petition.

New §8.01-654.1 reads:

> No petition for a writ of habeas corpus filed by a prisoner held under a sentence of death shall be considered unless it is filed within sixty days after the earliest of: (i) denial by the United States Supreme Court of a petition for a writ of certiorari to the judgment of the Supreme Court of Virginia on direct appeal, (ii) a decision by the United States Supreme Court affirming imposition of the sentence of death when such decision is in a case resulting from a granted writ of certiorari to the judgment of the Supreme Court of Virginia on direct appeal, or (iii) the period for filing a timely petition for certiorari has expired without such a petition being filed.

Chapter 503 also adds §654(C)'s special state-capital-habeas jurisdiction/hearing arrangement and makes other institutional amendments. Those provisions must be read if used; citation of B(2) alone does not import every part. The enacted chapter has no separate express transition clause adjudicating the operation of new §654.1 against a prisoner whose direct review ended before July 1, 1995. Source recovery establishes enacted text and its effective date, not the conclusion that it validly forecloses this particular claim. It is not an Admitted Source Record and makes no timeline insertion or current-law change. Later amended code text is not substituted for this enactment.
