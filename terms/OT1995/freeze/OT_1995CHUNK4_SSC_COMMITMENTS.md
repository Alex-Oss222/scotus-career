## Entering law

* **“Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce,” present operation effective West Lynn Creamery, June17,1994**, carries *Oregon Waste*'s April4 origin-discrimination component and *Carbone*'s May16 private-processing-monopoly component. A compulsory route reserving private processing business to a local private operator requires a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Financial guarantees and sanitation do not by themselves satisfy that burden. *Carbone* does not invalidate ordinary government purchasing or decide every genuinely public system.
* **“Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption,” present operation effective Boston Harbor, March8,1993**, protects genuine project purchasing within that decision's NLRA setting and limits. It supports attention to the actual purchased service, not automatic dormant-Commerce immunity for every government contract or sovereign command.
* *White*,460 U.S.204, permits conditions within city-funded projects and rejects an inflexible privity line. *Reeves*,447 U.S.429, protects participation in the sale of the State's own product within its scope. *South-Central Timber*,467 U.S.82, PartsIII–IV, is the four-Justice plurality's downstream-processing analysis, not a majority rule. Powell/Burger would remand participation; Rehnquist/O'Connor would sustain the sale condition. The requirement of unmistakable congressional authorization has distinct majority support. No congressional authorization for Smithtown's ordinance is established.
* **“Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden,” present operation effective Lohman, May23,1994**, as applied by *Fulton*, February21,1996, supplies no general financing exception. Smithtown cannot transform a contractual funding commitment into an identified equivalent tax burden. This case presents no actual compensatory-tax claim requiring a separate tax holding.

## Enhanced alternative-path test

The principal contract fork is **genuine integrated procurement** versus **an impermissible restraint in a separate downstream disposal market**. The strongest argument against the selected contract ground is practical: SSC remains a private hauler, the incinerator is privately owned, collection and disposal have distinct prices and suppliers, and the destination term helps finance a local private processor. The *South-Central Timber* plurality forbids treating participation in one market as unlimited authority over another. The strongest answer is equally concrete: Smithtown pays for disposal itself and the contracted service is not complete until that disposal occurs. It has not merely sold an item and claimed power over its purchaser's subsequent business. *White* permits inquiry beyond strict privity. No row holds that a government's contract label decides its constitutional capacity.

**Ordinance defense and alternatives.** Genuine public purchasing does not require opening every procurement to every supplier. That defense fails for the ordinance because covered independent haulers and customers are commanded to buy Ogden's service regardless of a Town purchase. Criminal penalties identify the coercive mechanism but are not a freestanding test: lawful nondiscriminatory health regulation may carry penalties. The Town's sanitary and financing interests are legitimate; no record-supported showing establishes why reasonable neutral regulation, lawful purchase and nondiscriminatory financing cannot adequately serve them. The decision directs none of those substitute programs and prescribes no tax or price.

**Further-proceedings alternative.** Return under R permits ordinary consideration of properly remaining breach and payment questions; it does not rest on an independently verified earlier remand command and guarantees no new hearing, trial or money judgment. A constitutional remand premised on an invented uncertainty about whether disposal was purchased is unsupported. The constitutional holding cannot resolve the Town's counterclaim, award the withheld sum, rescind the entire contract, establish CERCLA liability or decide antitrust immunity. No Associate commits to those additional results.

**Joinder consequences.** The eight commitments support the same component judgments, but O'Connor and Souter's precedent-following application must not be described as unqualified personal adoption of the broader original *Carbone* theory. They can join a severable passage that the existing rule controls this private ordinance. Their earlier separate views may be preserved without a new separate writing if the actual opinion's scope makes the reservation clear. No Marks synthesis or automatic conversion of judgment agreement into complete theoretical agreement is appropriate.
