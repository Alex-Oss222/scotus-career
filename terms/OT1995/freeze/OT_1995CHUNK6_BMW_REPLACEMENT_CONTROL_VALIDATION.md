## Actual entering law

The controlling texts are the public/legal portions of [TXO, June 25, 1993](../../OT1992/records/TXO_Production_Corp_v_Alliance_Resources_merits_1993-06-25.md), [Oberg, June 24, 1994](../../OT1993/records/Honda_Motor_Co_v_Oberg_merits_1994-06-24.md#public-projection), and the current Standards entry “Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts,” present operation effective Oberg. The [chunk C entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK6_C.md) reproduces the operative rule. Their operative conclusions agree.

TXO affirmed 5–4. Five Justices supported the adequacy of that particular instructed-jury, trial-review and state-appellate-review process, including the conclusion that absence of a separate written trial explanation did not itself deny due process. No majority adopted a substantive ratio, potential-harm arithmetic, Kennedy's whole substantive ground, Scalia's rejection of substantive review, or a general substantive-excessiveness test. Haslip's governing force survived. The replacement's refusal to derive a constitutional figure from TXO is accurate; assembly must not transform the absence of a substantive majority into a prior majority holding that *all* substantive review is unconstitutional or unavailable. The replacement supplies Stone's present proposed answer, not a fictional TXO holding.

Oberg reversed 8–1 on procedural due process. Its rule requires a meaningful opportunity for judicial examination and lawful correction of the **amount**, or an adequate functional substitute. Oregon's legal-error and no-evidence review, clear-and-convincing entitlement rule, evidentiary gate, actual instructions, optional award and complaint-based ceiling together left that function unperformed. Review directed only to whether some punitive liability was authorized is insufficient. Oberg neither selected a dollar figure nor decided substantive excessiveness; it prescribed no precise constitutional review standard, ratio, mandatory remittitur, automatic new trial, written-opinion form or required number of reviewing courts.

This distinction is material. The replacement's listed instruction, entitlement, evidence, statutory-limit and impermissible-consideration checks cannot be treated as an exhaustive checklist that eliminates Oberg's additional amount-review function. The text itself preserves meaningful review, allows reprehensibility, ratios and comparable sanctions to inform state-law review or another established legal inquiry, permits a court to find under applicable law that a verdict cannot stand, and permits conditional remittitur with further jury proceedings upon rejection where governing law preserves that right. Those express provisions permit examination and lawful correction of an arbitrary or excessive amount under governing law without authorizing the reviewing court to impose its independently preferred figure over the protected party's objection. No new qualification must be supplied to obtain that reading.

Stone's published Oberg concurrence required examination of the actual record, punitive standards and whether the amount was arbitrary or excessive; a new punitive determination followed only if adequate review could not lawfully correct the defect. The approved replacement preserves those procedural functions and now states his rejection of a separate federal substantive magnitude test. His earlier TXO dissent remains a published, record-specific inadequate-review position; it is not prior Court law or authority to override the new express approval. A reading of the replacement that insulated every amount once punitive entitlement existed would conflict with both Oberg's Court rule and the replacement's own express limits. ## Extraterritorial evidence, correction and petition scope

**Appendix 16a–19a:** The Alabama court found the original $4 million jury award based in large part on the nationwide transactions. It allowed other transactions as evidence of a pattern, practice and intent, but rejected using their number as a punitive multiplier. It expressly stated that its own reasonable-relationship assessment of the amount did not consider acts occurring in other jurisdictions. It then applied Hammond/Green Oil review and comparative cases before selecting the conditional reduction. The court's original-verdict finding and its description of the surviving judgment are distinct. Neither the original $4 million multiplier nor the petitioner's claim that the reduction failed to cure it may be restated as an adjudicated finding that the surviving $2 million still punished foreign conduct.

The replacement accurately distinguishes admissible evidence of knowledge, intent, deliberateness or common conduct from punishment beyond Alabama's lawful reach. Its affirmance does not endorse the discarded nationwide multiplier, hold every foreign transaction unlawful, bar relevant evidence merely because it arose elsewhere, or declare that all procedural defects are cured whenever a court halves a verdict. Its actual application recognizes the lawful conditional correction here; no fresh dollar calculation, nationwide-profit disgorgement or reconstructed jury allocation may be added to justify that conclusion.

**Petition questions, page i:** BMW asked both whether the state court provided a meaningful remedy for the asserted extraterritorial defect and whether the remitted $2 million was grossly excessive. **Petition pages 9–10:** BMW argued for a new punitive trial or reduction to no more than $56,000, using $4,000 times the asserted maximum 14 Alabama transactions. Those are BMW's arguments, not an established constitutional ceiling or the lower court's chosen formula. **Conclusion, page 29:** BMW requested summary reversal or plenary review. The replacement's discussion of policing forbidden punishment, lawful correction, the actual conditional reduction and affirmance addresses the remedial component as well as its rejection of an independent substantive magnitude objection.
