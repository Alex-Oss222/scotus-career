## Provisional judgment and propositions

All eight Associates would **vacate the categorical jurisdictional dismissal and remand to the Ninth Circuit** for application of the ordinary legal/factual appealability boundary. This is a bounded appellate-jurisdiction disposition. It neither grants immunity nor instructs the Ninth Circuit to entertain any factual-sufficiency component.

The minimum shared proposition is that a prior qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later appeal concerning a separable legal immunity issue arising on a materially different procedural or claim basis. The revived termination claim demonstrates why an inflexible one-appeal rule cannot determine jurisdiction without examining what is now presented. The Court need not decide that Behrens's particular factual assumptions are correct or that every asserted issue qualifies for immediate review.

All eight also retain Johnson: take the relevant district-court assumptions favorable to the nonmovant; identify likely assumptions when unstated without resolving truth or reweighing the evidence; dismiss ordinary factual-sufficiency components. A reference to disputed facts does not itself answer whether a distinct legal question is presented, but calling an issue legal does not itself establish jurisdiction.

The same eight would reject respondent's categorical contention that a remaining claim requiring trial necessarily eliminates review of a separate legal immunity denial. Immunity from having to defend one claim is not lost solely because some different claim may still require litigation. This does not make the remaining claim reviewable, nor decide whether these particular claims and assumptions in fact yield an appealable legal question.

## Adversarial comparison by separable question

**Numerical bar.** Sustaining a one-appeal rule would limit expense, protect district-court control, and prevent serial litigation of the same defense. Its strongest doctrinal form treats an initial legal appeal as exhausting the opportunity to avoid suit, with later factual developments governed by Johnson. The counterexample here is not a mere reweighing request: the termination claim was outside the first appellate merits determination because it then stood dismissed. Mitchell's legal-review category, Johnson's express preservation of that category, and the claim-specific character of immunity supply a sufficient ground to reject automatic exhaustion. The eight commitments do not assert that a second denial is always appealable.

**Actual legal versus factual scope.** Immediate merits review could be proper if the appellant accepts the district court's relevant favorable-to-plaintiff assumptions and presents a legal clear-notice question. Affirmance could be proper if the actual appeal only disputes the sufficiency of evidence or insists on contrary factual assumptions. Both parties present substantial arguments about that classification. The limited commitment sends it to the Ninth Circuit, which dismissed solely under its categorical rule, rather than adjudicating immunity or factual sufficiency here. No omission from the full record is inferred; the Joint Appendix was not read in this stage. A broader definitive classification requires additional clean record review and, if material, renewed neutral commitments.

**Surviving claims.** The argument against partial immediate review stresses that the official must still endure a trial and therefore cannot preserve an absolute freedom from litigation. The opposing ground is that qualified immunity operates on the claim or conduct for which it applies. Allowing trial on one claim does not eliminate the immunity entitlement respecting another; Johnson and Swint still prohibit using that legal appeal to bring every remaining issue along. The eight support this claim-specific answer without deciding whether each pleaded theory here creates a distinct appealable legal issue.

**Remedy.** Vacatur removes the categorical dismissal without affirmatively granting immunity or requiring review of fact-bound questions. Reversal with a command to adjudicate the merits of the whole appeal goes beyond the common commitment. Affirmance on Johnson would require a conclusion about the actual appealed assumptions that this handoff does not establish. No immediate trial result, damages determination, or order affecting the separate United States tort claim is authorized.
