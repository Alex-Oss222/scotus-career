# Previously verified neutral source material

## Bank One — material factual correction and validated jurisdiction frame

Replace the original packet's description of a delayed nonpayment notice with the actual lower judgment's description: the receiving/depositary bank alleged that Midwest returned a $64,294.27 check unpaid without advising it that the reason was insufficient funds. The cited duties were 12 C.F.R. §§229.30(d) and 229.33(b)(8), with ordinary-care liability invoked under §229.38(a). The district court entered summary judgment and $43,912.06 for the plaintiff. The Seventh Circuit vacated that judgment and remanded with instructions to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction. It did not decide the merits of the alleged regulatory breach.

The lower caption is First Illinois Bank & Trust v. Midwest. Use the supplied Supreme Court caption Bank One Chicago, N.A. v. Midwest Bank & Trust Co., No.94-1175, for the event. This addendum makes no independent finding of the mechanism or date of any corporate succession; it is unnecessary to the statutory issue, and no identity dispute appears in the supplied packet or lower opinion. Argument November 28, 1995; event January 17, 1996. All nine participate.

The lower court read §4010(a)'s exclusion of claims against a depository institution by another such institution to govern §4010(d)'s permission to sue in federal district court. It suggested administrative resolution under §4009(c)(1), or perhaps a state action, and adhered to that view after the Federal Reserve Board reported that no administrative dispute-resolution mechanism existed. The Board, New York Clearing House Association and Standard Bank supported rehearing. The panel denied rehearing; no judge requested an en banc vote.

The statutory text gives two distinct liability arrangements. Section 4010(a) creates the direct customer-oriented remedy with actual damages, specified individual/class additional damages, and costs and reasonable attorney fees. It expressly excludes another depository institution from that remedy. Section 4010(f) expressly authorizes the Board to impose or allocate loss and liability among depository institutions for any aspect of the check payment system. Its ordinary liability ceiling is the amount of the check; bad faith permits additional damages proximately caused by the act or omission. Section 4010(d) authorizes any action under the section in a United States district court or another competent court within one year of the occurrence. Section 4008(c) gives the Board payment-system regulatory responsibility. None of these texts says that interbank disputes must be adjudicated administratively.

Sections 4010(c) and (e) remain distinct: the former protects a depository institution proving by a preponderance that an unintentional violation resulted from a bona fide error despite reasonably adapted preventive procedures, excluding an error of legal judgment; the latter protects good-faith conformity to a Board rule, regulation or interpretation despite its later amendment, rescission or invalidation. Whether a particular statutory or regulatory defense applies is not decided by identifying federal jurisdiction.

The competing lawful readings are (A) the words any action under this section include Board-established interbank liability under (f), even though the different (a) customer remedy excludes interbank plaintiffs; (B) subsection (d) concerns only subsection (a), leaving administrative enforcement or a state-law route for the delegated interbank rules. A has the stronger integrated textual reading because (f) is in the same section and supplies express delegation of liability; B's strongest point is the explicit interbank exclusion in (a), which must retain force. Route A preserves that force by leaving customer additional damages and attorney fees in (a) and using (f)'s separate liability and limit. Touche Ross and Transamerica require congressional authorization rather than judicial creation of a remedy; the express (d)/(f) arrangement is the proposed authorization, not a right inferred solely from the Act's beneficial purpose or an agency's preference.

The proper available decree on A is reversal of the jurisdictional dismissal and remand for the court of appeals to address properly remaining challenges to the district judgment. Neither damages, liability, a late-notice finding, nor a final injunction is newly imposed. NationsBank and current-term Town & Country require independent statutory boundaries before respect for agency judgment; they do not permit the Board to create jurisdiction Congress withheld. The (d)/(f) text must therefore do the jurisdictional work.
