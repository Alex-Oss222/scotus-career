#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Post-assignment waiting within transportation

* **Controlling Reasoning Below / Institutional Posture:** The Seventh Circuit treated waiting for return transportation as part of the transportation interval, rather than work or rest.
* **Petitioner or Applicant’s Position:** Waiting is not literal transportation, and workers remain subject to the railroad’s direction rather than free to rest; fatigue favors on-duty treatment.
* **Respondent or Competing Position:** Transportation includes its necessary waiting incidents; making the interval on duty would disregard Congress’s deliberate third category.

* **Alternative or Unreached Grounds and Limits:** The statutory category must be fixed before considering administrative weight or policy consequences. The period at issue is not automatically statutory rest simply because it is excluded from on-duty computation.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Lawful Paths and Consequences:** Classifying waiting as on duty requires reversal; classifying it as incident to return transportation supports affirmance. Either route must separately preserve the statutory rest requirement.
