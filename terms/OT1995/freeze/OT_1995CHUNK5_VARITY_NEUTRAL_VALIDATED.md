## 1. Event, channel, questions and requested relief

The supplied event is merits review under28U.S.C.§1254(1), after argument on November1,1995, of Howe v. Varity Corp.,36F.3d746(8thCir.1994), clarified at41F.3d1263(8thCir.1994). The supplied roster is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; no case-specific nonparticipation is established. Historical Supreme Court petition treatment is not imported to establish any new action.

The petition and petitioner's merits brief present: (1) whether§502(a)(3) permits participants or beneficiaries to sue for their own benefit, rather than on behalf of a plan, for breaches of ERISA fiduciary duty; and(2) whether§404 permits fiduciary liability notwithstanding a disclosed reservation of amendment/termination rights when the employer does not disclose its expectation of future benefit termination. Respondents dispute the latter characterization: they defend a duty against intentional affirmative misrepresentation, including misleading half-truths, when an administrator communicates about benefits. The United States similarly frames intentional misrepresentation in fiduciary capacity. The Court must decide the capacity issue on the actual findings and communications, not presume either side's formulation resolves it.

Petitioner asks reversal insofar as the appellate judgment affirms its liability. Respondents and the United States seek affirmance. There is no request here to restore punitive damages, lifetime contractual vesting, the terminated employees' severance claims or settled disability/pension claims.

## 2. Material record and distinct claims

The district court made its own Rule52 findings after a17-day trial. The Eighth Circuit expressly records that defendants did not contend those findings were clearly erroneous. They remain findings, not allegations; their legal significance and statutory characterization remain contested.

Massey-Ferguson maintained an unfunded/self-insured welfare plan covering health and related benefits. Varity controlled M-F. The district found fiduciary status and administration/control, and the appellate opinion records no denial of their general fiduciary status. That does not decide whether the particular communications were fiduciary functions under§3(21)(A). The JA allocates different board, committee, administrator and trustee duties; no blanket proposition that every corporate act is fiduciary follows.

The1986 ProjectSunshine restructuring transferred selected operations, substantial debt and benefit obligations to MCC. The findings describe MCC as having negative net worth and poor survival prospects from its formation, executives' knowledge of those conditions, retention of profitable assets elsewhere, and a purpose of relieving Varity/M-F of losses and benefit costs. Broader figures of roughly1,500 active employees and4,000 retirees are background, not the size of either respondent group.

* **The83-member Retired Class:** M-F employees accepted transfer to MCC and retired before its March4,1988 receivership. The findings describe the letters, video and meeting, a side-by-side benefits comparison, assurances that benefit programs would remain unchanged, a bright-future/viability presentation, intentionally misleading or incomplete answers, and detrimental reliance. MCC adopted the existing benefits program; the claim does not require finding that every statement about present plan terms was literally false. The dispute includes whether the combined communication falsely represented the security of continued benefits and whether that communication was plan administration.
* **Ten individual retirees:** They had already retired from M-F when their benefit obligations were purportedly assigned to MCC without their knowledge or consent. Their claim does not depend on their attending the transfer meeting, accepting employment or receiving a misrepresentation. The distinct alleged violation is administration contrary to governing instruments and loyalty obligations through an unauthorized unilateral transfer of benefit obligations. Anita Crowe's one-time later project does not merge this group into the83-member class.

MCC ceased paying benefits after receivership. The M-F plan remained in existence. Reservation of power to amend or terminate does not establish that M-F actually adopted a valid amendment terminating these plaintiffs' rights. JA§7.4 reserves board amendment/termination, preserves accrued claims and forbids diversion except the referenced plan provision; the1983 medical summary also contains express reservation language. The parties dispute disclosure's adequacy and legal effect, while the rejection of lifetime contractual vesting is no longer presented for review.

The actual employee communications also say that future employment conditions depend on MCC's success and necessary or appropriate changes may be made. They must accompany the assurances in the record account. The JA is a selected exhibit appendix, not all trial evidence. It contains no finding that the only practical alternative was immediate termination of every respondent; testimony advanced by petitioner does not itself create such a finding.

## 3. Judgments below and operative remedy

The district set aside the contract/lifetime-benefit and freestanding fraud verdicts, the Terminated Class's claims and punitive damages. It sustained relief for the Retired Class and individual retirees on fiduciary duty,§510 interference and equitable-estoppel grounds, and offered an election between large past/future monetary awards and reinstatement plus accrued-benefit amounts. Plaintiffs elected the larger jury awards.

The Eighth Circuit rejected the cross-appeal restoring the dismissed claims, sustained fiduciary liability under§404/§502(a)(3), and did not decide the alternative estoppel and§510 grounds. It set aside compensatory awards of$7.6million and$712,332 under Mertens/Novak and substituted reinstatement and amounts described as restitution for lost benefits, including intervening accruals. Hansen joined PartsI–V but dissented on remedy: the alternative had been rejected below and was not the appealed judgment, and some amounts—including replacement-insurance premiums—were consequential compensation rather than restitution. He would remand for appropriate equitable relief.

The December8,1994 clarification is part of the judgment under review. Reinstatement gives no rights beyond those generally enjoyed by members of the M-F plan as it now exists. Past entitlement, including posttrial benefits, is to be adjusted on remand for intervening events. Future modification remains available to the extent the earlier opinion permits, subject to applicable law and any claim that a later change retaliates or discriminates against these plaintiffs. The district may hear the parties and relevant additional evidence and enter a more detailed conforming decree. Reinstatement is therefore **not** a grant of immutable lifetime benefits.

The stated Retired Class past-benefit figure is$696,195. The district's ten itemized individual figures sum to$82,812. One appellate passage prints$81,812, while another and the district items support$82,812; this packet records the discrepancy and does not create an independent money judgment. A disposition need not settle that clerical/implementation detail to describe the actual relief under review.

The prior896F.2d1107 decision rejected retirement alone as a vesting event while preserving specified injury/disability entitlements and further proceedings on liability and class scope. The current claim is not simply that rejected lifetime-contract theory. Settled disability claims are not revived.

## 4. Strongest party positions, government position and presentation limits

**Petitioner.** Sections409 and502(a)(2) specifically establish liability/remedies for fiduciary breach, and Russell's plan-centered construction cannot be evaded through the general wording of§502(a)(3). Section502(a)(1)(B) already supplies individual benefits relief; allowing a fiduciary label to overcome the failed benefits claim would nullify written-plan limits and established review rules. The reply expressly acknowledges that plaintiffs seek equitable relief; it contests the recipient and statutory authority for it. The asserted representations concerned corporate viability and employment, while current benefit terms were identical and reservation language disclosed. The functional definition does not transform corporate forecasts into plan administration merely because an unfunded plan depends on employer solvency. General disclosure duties cannot replace the specified statutory scheme, and a reasonable-employee perspective lacks sufficient statutory limitation. The Secretary's new litigation interpretation is also disputed.

**Respondents.** Section502(a)(3) expressly reaches violations of any relevant statutory provision, including§404, and contains no plan-only recipient limitation. Sections409/502(a)(2) and502(a)(3) perform complementary work; Russell reserved the latter question, and the actual Mertens holding concerns relief category, not whether fiduciary violations may ever support individual equitable relief. Communicating with participants about benefit status is within discretionary administration; the findings establish knowing misleading statements and reliance. They do not ask the Court to resolve every affirmative-disclosure obligation. Power lawfully to amend a plan does not excuse fraudulently inducing relinquishment of existing membership, and this relief restores membership without vesting benefits for life.

**United States.** The Secretary supports individualized equitable redress for distinct beneficiary injury and reads duty of loyalty in light of traditional candor in fiduciary-beneficiary dealings. The proposed capacity inquiry considers what a reasonable participant would understand from the entire context: speaker's role, occasion and other content. Financial statements ordinarily are corporate; current-benefit descriptions ordinarily concern administration; statements about future benefits may fall on either side. The government says the meeting deliberately combined current-plan information and financial assurances without a signaled change of capacity. It argues for deference to the Secretary's considered interpretation, a proposition disputed by petitioner and not supplied automatically by John Hancock. Its restitution explanation rests on the self-insurer's avoided benefit costs; it does not establish a judicial finding of traced assets or every amount's equitable character.

**Ten retirees are not a conceded component.** Respondentspp.43–44 and UnitedStatesp.18n.16 argue that their unauthorized-transfer ground is distinct and inadequately challenged by the opening brief. Petitioner's replyp.13n.15 expressly responds: absent misrepresentation and contractual entitlement, it says, there is no basis to affirm this group either. That response must be assessed against the actual assignment/plan-administration ground. Silence in the opening brief is not converted here into an irrevocable merits concession.

**Relief category is a presentation boundary.** Respondentsp.12n.5 invoke Rule24.1(a) and state that the precise equitable characterization of the award is outside the questions presented; an amicus cannot enlarge those questions merely by disputing restitution. UnitedStatespp.7–8n.9 states petitioner has not challenged the relief's equitable nature; petitioner's replyp.2 calls the relief equitable and argues whether it may run individually. Hansen's dissent is a real lower disagreement, but it does not by itself preserve a new Supreme Court remedial issue. Do not silently restore the rejected compensatory awards, overrule Mertens, decide every equitable money remedy or adjudicate a new damages claim. If a proposed path requires reaching exact monetary classification, it must first justify that reach under the actual questions and submissions.

Petitioner challenges individual relief by invoking§502(a)(1)(B)'s specificity, while respondents distinguish their rejected lifetime-benefits theory from restoration of membership. Whether other statutory relief is actually adequate here is therefore an issue to analyze, not an assumed finding. No universal rule allowing or forbidding every alternative§502(a)(3) pleading has already been decided in this case.

## 6. Statutory text and exceptions

The[official1994Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title29/html/USCODE-1994-title29.htm) supplies the complete saved sections, including amendments/effective dates. These text copies, not a party's paraphrase, are the statutory inputs:

* [§1002](../sources/chunk5_a/varity_29usc1002_1994.txt), particularly **§3(21)(A)–(B)**: except as provided in(B), fiduciary status exists only to the extent of discretionary authority/control over plan management, authority/control over management or disposition of plan assets, compensated investment advice or authority/responsibility for it, or discretionary authority/responsibility in plan administration; designated persons under§1105(c)(1)(B) are included. Subparagraph(B) says investment in securities of a registered investment company does not, by itself, make the company, adviser or principal underwriter a fiduciary or party in interest, except their action in connection with a plan covering their own employees; other-law duties are not limited. That exception does not establish immunity for the communications here.
* [§1104, complete§404](../sources/chunk5_a/varity_29usc1104_1994.txt): the(a)(1) duty is to discharge duties **with respect to a plan** solely in participants' and beneficiaries' interests and for the exclusive purpose of providing benefits and defraying reasonable administration expenses; prudent care/skill/diligence is required; diversification minimizes large-loss risk unless clearly prudent otherwise; governing instruments control only insofar as consistent with TitlesI andIV. The opening qualification is subject to§§1103(c),(d),1342 and1344, concerning asset/termination provisions; no such asset-return or pension-termination exception is invoked by this unfunded-welfare communication record. Under(a)(2), eligible individual-account plans' holding/acquisition of qualifying employer securities/real property does not violate diversification or prudence **only to the extent prudence requires diversification**. Subsection(b) forbids indicia of ownership outside district-court jurisdiction except as authorized by Secretary regulations. Subsection(c) applies to an individual-account **pension** plan permitting participant control: control as determined under regulations does not itself make the participant a fiduciary and excuses others only for loss/breach resulting from that control. Subsection(d)'s single-employer pension-termination election requires compliance with the specified tax-law asset-transfer, benefit-increase, replacement-plan participation, receipt and allocation rules; it supplies no general welfare-plan exception.
* [§1109, complete§409](../sources/chunk5_a/varity_29usc1109_1994.txt): a fiduciary breaching TitleI duties is personally liable to make good losses **to the plan**, restore profits derived through plan-asset use **to the plan**, and such other equitable/remedial relief as appropriate, including removal. Subsection(b) excludes breaches committed before fiduciary status begins or after it ends. The dispute is whether these plan remedies exhaust the statutory consequences of breach or coexist with the separate express individual remedy claimed.
* [§1132, complete§502](../sources/chunk5_a/varity_29usc1132_1994.txt): (a)(1)(B) authorizes a participant/beneficiary to recover benefits due under plan terms, enforce those rights or clarify future benefits; (a)(2) authorizes the Secretary, participants, beneficiaries or fiduciaries to obtain appropriate§409 relief; (a)(3) authorizes participants, beneficiaries or fiduciaries to enjoin any act/practice violating TitleI or plan terms, or obtain **other appropriate equitable relief** to redress violations or enforce those provisions/terms. These are distinct grants; the word **equitable** and each predicate/recipient limitation retain independent force.
* The rest of§502 is preserved in the complete text. Secretary(a)(5) relief parallels the statutory equitable provision, but(b)(1) restricts enforcement of participation/vesting/funding requirements for the enumerated tax-qualified or qualification-pending plans to Treasury request or written participant/beneficiary/fiduciary request with the required protective finding; (b)(2) forbids Secretary-initiated§1145 enforcement. Section(a)(9), added in1994, separately authorizes specified relief, including necessary security and reasonable prejudgment interest, for fiduciary/plan violations in purchasing insurance/annuities upon termination of participant status. Its pending-proceeding effective rule and no-limitation-of-standing/no-change-to-fiduciary-duties savings text are included. That pension-annuity provision does not silently govern this welfare transfer. Section(d)(2) limits execution of a plan money judgment to the plan unless individual liability is independently established. Section(l)'s Secretary assessment and good-faith/hardship waiver rules do not themselves create the private claim here.
* [§1108(c)](../sources/chunk5_a/varity_29usc1108_1994.txt) permits a fiduciary also to be an officer, employee, agent or representative of a party in interest; it does not eliminate function-specific duties. [§1140/§510](../sources/chunk5_a/varity_29usc1140_1994.txt) separately prohibits adverse action for exercising protected rights, intended interference with attainment of rights and specified retaliation for giving information/testimony, enforced through§502. Its alternative lower ground was not adjudicated on appeal.

All quoted fragments are checked against the official text; the full sections retain qualifications not operative on these facts. No pension protection, asset rule, statutory duty or remedial exception is silently transferred to the unfunded welfare claim.

## 7. Supported decisional paths to be assessed independently

1. Give§502(a)(3)'s individual equitable action its asserted textually distinct scope, find the proven transfer communications within plan administration, and sustain the reviewed liability/restoration within the clarified decree and presentation limits. The explanation must answer§409 specificity and the corporate-capacity objection, not rely only on wrongdoing or remedial need.
2. Accept availability of individual equitable relief but reject or narrow the communication-capacity theory under§3(21)(A). That need not dispose of the ten retirees' distinct unauthorized-transfer claim. Each group requires its own ground and disposition; general fiduciary status alone is insufficient.
3. Read§§409/502(a)(2) as the exclusive fiduciary-breach remedial route, or find the requested use of§502(a)(3) foreclosed by the more specific benefits remedy in these circumstances. This requires confronting§502(a)(3)'s express language, Russell's reservation, distinct injuries and the actual scope of the failed lifetime-benefit claim. Reversal of the fiduciary theory is not automatically dismissal of every alternative preserved theory.
4. Resolve only the duty against knowing material misrepresentation in established fiduciary communication, leaving broader affirmative disclosure, negligent speech and generic corporate-forecast duties open. Both respondents and the government support this narrower reach. Alternatively, the functional boundary may be resolved without reaching a general duty-to-disclose rule.
5. Where a legally necessary function, injury or alternative-ground question is not resolved by the accepted findings, a bounded remand remains possible. It may not invent factual disputes over findings not challenged below, revive settled claims, guarantee recovery or treat every source limit as a mandatory new trial. Any reliance on§510 or estoppel needs its own preservation, legal and remedy analysis; the lower court's alternative reasoning is not already Supreme Court law.
6. Keep the original large compensatory awards rejected. Monetary classification of the substituted award is a distinct presentation issue; an individual-action ruling can preserve the existing clarified remedy without announcing that all unpaid benefits or all trust compensation constitute restitution. Any new reach requires a supported procedural basis and application of actual Mertens, not a remedy label.

The contextual communication approach, a narrower plan-content/function approach, and the specific-remedy structural approach are supported opposing possibilities. No scoring, presumed coalition or required outcome attaches to them.
