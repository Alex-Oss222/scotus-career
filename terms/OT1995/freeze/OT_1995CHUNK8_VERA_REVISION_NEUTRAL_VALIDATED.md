## Operative reconstructed record

**Court and judgment under review.** The expressly reconstructed lower judgment is from the three-judge United States District Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division, convened under 28 U.S.C. §2284, *Vera v. Richards*, Civ. A. No. H-94-0277: opinion of August 17, 1994, 861 F. Supp. 1304; order of September 2, 1994; amended order entered September 20, 1994. It is an adopted reconstruction, not a recovered historical judgment in this timeline. The published lower opinion supplies compatible judicial recitations and findings identified separately below; its reliance on historical Supreme Court districting law does not supply governing law here.

**Plaintiffs and allegations.** The six registered-voter plaintiffs reside in District 25 (Chen), District 18 (Blum and Powers), District 29 (Thomas and Vera), and District 30 (Orcutt). They alleged that the 1991 congressional plan, HB 1/Plan C657, was an unconstitutional effort to segregate the races for purposes of voting and challenged 24 of the 30 districts under the Fourteenth Amendment. Each of the five residents of Districts 18, 29 and 30 alleges that the State, relying on race, assigned that plaintiff to the district in which the plaintiff lives and votes. None alleges vote dilution, exclusion from voting or candidacy, unequal participation, discriminatory treatment in casting or counting ballots, or another injury beyond that assignment. Chen alleges no assignment to Districts 18, 29 or 30 and no personal connection to the classifications asserted in drawing them. Those injury limits remain exact; neither the expanded question nor the evidence discussed below changes them.

**Standing below.** The State defendants and Lawson intervenors contested plaintiffs' standing to challenge districts in which they did not reside. The reconstructed court held that Blum, Powers, Thomas, Vera and Orcutt had standing to challenge their own districts because each asserted personal race-based assignment traceable to the plan and redressable by relief against its use, without further alleged injury. It rested the declaration and injunction on those residents and did not decide the nonresidence objection. Chen's challenge to his own District 25 failed on the merits. This specified standing treatment controls over any broader standing discussion in the historical lower opinion.

**Merits and relief below.** The court held Districts 18, 29 and 30 unconstitutional under the Fourteenth Amendment and rejected the challenges to the other 21 districts. Its correctness is now within the revised review scope. It declared the three districts unconstitutional; allowed the fall 1994 congressional elections to proceed under C657; directed the Legislature to develop a new congressional plan by March 15, 1995, conforming to the opinion, with a remedial hearing to follow; directed plaintiffs to apply for fees and costs within 30 days; and, by the September 20 amended order granting the State's unopposed motion for an explicit injunction, enjoined state defendants and persons acting in concert with them from conducting the 1996 congressional elections, including candidate qualifying, under the C657 districts. All other relief sought in the post-trial submissions was denied. No fee award is supplied.

**No independent support for the three-district decree.** Plaintiffs dismissed their Voting Rights Act §2, state-constitutional and Fifteenth Amendment claims before trial. Their complaint also cited the Fifth Amendment, but the relief rests solely on the five residents' Fourteenth Amendment racial-districting claims. Chen supplies no independent injury supporting relief for Districts 18, 29 and 30. Testimony about split precincts, voter confusion, misdirected ballots and Blum's 1992 congressional campaign is district-configuration evidence. No plaintiff relies on it as a distinct personal injury, and the reconstructed court made no such finding. No unpleaded racial candidate-eligibility restriction, constituent-service discrimination, ballot label, legally inferior membership or religious-gerrymandering claim may be inferred from the lower court's rhetoric or discussion.

**Appeals and limits.** The Governor and other state defendants appeal in No. 94-805; William Lawson and the private defendant-intervenors appeal in No. 94-806; the United States as defendant-intervenor appeals in No. 94-988. Each invokes §1253 from the declaratory and injunctive orders. No plaintiff appeal is part of this event, and the expanded review does not reopen their unsuccessful challenges to the other 21 districts. No stay pending appeal, new legislative or judicial plan, 1996 election administration, new fee award, or compliance or noncompliance with the March 1995 direction is supplied. Elapsed calendar time establishes none of those events.

**Participation.** The seated Court is Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No case-specific nonparticipation or argument date is established. No recusal or substitute submission is invented.

**Common evidence and dispute.** The opinion describes Texas's population growth and three added congressional seats; a legislative effort to create new minority-opportunity districts while protecting incumbents; racial population data available down to census-block level through REDAPPL; and political election information available at voting-tabulation-district level but not through that software at block level. Other political information could be supplied through legislators' knowledge and other sources. The availability of detailed racial information is evidence, not itself a finding that every boundary was racial. The State and intervenors attributed boundary choices to incumbent protection, political preferences, neighborhoods, communities and opportunity goals. The trial court found those explanations insufficient for the three invalidated districts while rejecting the attacks on other districts. Its factual purpose findings and their constitutional consequences must be evaluated separately.

**Purpose findings below.** In Parts IV.A.1–2 the court found the three districts' configurations not adequately explainable apart from the selected racial composition, with traditional districting considerations subordinated. It did not hold every intentional majority-minority district unlawful and did not invalidate all race-aware boundary adjustments. Its literal language about districts being unexplainable except by race coexists with its acknowledgment of incumbent and other political purposes; a reviewing Justice must address that record rather than assume no mixed motive existed. Miller supplies ordinary clear-error review of districting-purpose findings and independent review of their legal significance. The fact that the lower court used historical constitutional language is not by itself a reason to discard supported historical facts.

**Statutory-compliance defenses below.** The State, Lawson intervenors and United States defended minority electoral opportunity and Voting Rights Act compliance. The opinion recites Texas's history of discrimination and disputed polarization evidence. State expert Lichtman identified substantial Anglo bloc voting against minority candidates; plaintiffs' expert Weber acknowledged some polarization but disputed its legal consequence. Evidence that Black and Hispanic voters did not always prefer the same candidates is not proof of a legally established coalition. The court did not adjudicate a preserved §2 plaintiff count; that count had been dismissed. No liability finding under §2 may be manufactured from these competing accounts.

The court treated VRA compliance as potentially compelling but required fit between that interest and the districts. It said the State did not seriously defend the precise shapes as narrowly tailored and separately addressed the United States' affirmative fit defense. It relied on more compact alternatives, including C500 and C606 in Dallas and C606 and C676 in Houston, and testimony that greater respect for traditional criteria was possible. Defendants objected that alternatives impaired incumbents and minority opportunity; the opinion rejected incumbency protection as an interest equivalent to statutory compliance. The source establishes the competing positions and the lower court's findings, not the correctness of a universal least-irregular-map rule. The State's reported litigation position is not converted into a jurisdictional concession, default or waiver of the United States' separate defense.

The court distinguished preservation of an existing opportunity district from creation of new ones and rejected the proposition that §5's nonretrogression duty required new Districts 29 and 30. Its assertion that no adequate §2 justification had been shown is a lower legal conclusion, not a statutory command or an independently established fact that polarization or unequal opportunity never existed. Its footnote discussion of citizenship, cohesion and future proof requirements likewise creates no entering Supreme Court rule. Preclearance was reported granted on November 18, 1991, with an express reservation concerning other constitutional law; it neither immunizes the plan nor supplies this Court with a collateral federal-preclearance review action.

**Standing and review channel.** Miller's unanimous Parts I-A and I-B distinguish personal exposure from ultimate success. Voters personally subjected to the challenged electoral assignment may litigate alleged unequal treatment when personal exposure, causation and redressability are established; residence alone is insufficient. A serious constitutional districting claim does not become jurisdictionally frivolous merely because Shaw supplies a strong merits answer. The required three-judge court's merits grant of injunctive relief supplies the present §1253 channel; Miller involved a merits denial, while nonmerits dismissals require their own channel analysis. Lujan retains concrete and particularized actual or imminent injury, traceability, likely redress and litigation-stage proof. Northeastern Florida recognizes unequal competitive treatment without proof of winning the ultimate benefit. Those rules do not predetermine whether territorial composition is an unlawful racial classification.

The operative Standards are **Personal districting exposure and direct apportionment review** (Miller, June 29, 1995); **Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury** (Shaw, June 28, 1993, as clarified but retained by Miller, June 29, 1995); **Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing** (Lujan, June 12, 1992); and **Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing** (Northeastern Florida, June 14, 1993). Read their complete qualifications in the slice.

**Actual substantive rule.** Shaw and Miller hold that deliberate or predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, including subordination of ordinary criteria, does not alone establish the asserted equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. Racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership and other independently cognizable wrongs retain their own elements, burdens, justification requirements and remedies. This is a substantive boundary, not heightened pleading, a universal requirement of a second governmental act or downstream loss, a rational-basis rule for an admitted racial burden, or an extra constitutional-injury condition for §2. A status injury can be complete in government's own unequal treatment; the shared district's racial composition alone does not establish racial eligibility, imposed beliefs, inferior membership or a representative legally answerable only to one race.

Miller's merits Parts II–III have direct support from Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Its permission-versus-compulsion rule, evidentiary treatment of shape and ordinary review of purpose findings are unanimous at their stated limited levels. Absence of a federal command to create the precise district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. No maximization duty, proportional-officeholding right, immunity for a stated inclusive goal, or automatic factual safe harbor follows. Unusual shape is neither necessary nor sufficient for a recognized violation. Miller's conditional statutory-defense discussion was nonessential, although all nine joined it on its hypothesis; it is not an alternative controlling invalidation or a current predominance-scrutiny rule.

**Continuing statutory law and current-term applications.** Voinovich separates voluntary state choice from federal compulsion. Growe preserves applicable Gingles proof, including political cohesion and ordinarily defeating majority bloc voting; a common coalition cannot be assumed from separate racial groups. Holder and De Grandy preserve a legally grounded comparison, the distinct causal predicates and totality inquiry, no maximization, no proportional-results entitlement and no conclusive proportionality safe harbor. These authorities do not revive plaintiffs' dismissed statutory counts. The current-term [SCLC v. Sessions decision](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK8_REVISION_VERA_PUBLIC_LAW/Southern_Christian_Leadership_Conference_of_Alabama_v_Sessions_merits_1995-12-18.md) applies Shaw/Miller's territorial-design limit while retaining statutory results proof and concrete remedial fit. Its race-aware-remedy holding supplies no presumption that a judge or representative serves only one race. [Morse](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK8_REVISION_VERA_PUBLIC_LAW/Morse_v_Republican_Party_of_Virginia_merits_1996-03-27.md) retains separation of §5 coverage, its designated preclearance process, substantive purpose/effect and relief. Beer supplies nonretrogression; Pleasant Grove preserves the distinct purpose requirement; Morris restricts review of federal preclearance determinations. No holding in this appeal may silently set aside federal preclearance or merge §§2 and 5.

[Wisconsin](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK8_REVISION_VERA_PUBLIC_LAW/Wisconsin_v_City_of_New_York_merits_1996-03-20.md) concerns a concrete representation injury connected to an alleged lost seat; [Romer](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK8_REVISION_VERA_PUBLIC_LAW/Romer_v_Evans_merits_1996-05-20.md) concerns an actual class-specific withdrawal and foreclosure of legal protection. ## Supported adversarial paths and decree discipline

These are analytical alternatives under Engine §14(4), not assertions that counsel filed particular new briefs, and not current judicial commitments.

**Standing recognition followed by application of existing merits law.** The five residents allege the very personal governmental assignment to which they are subjected, with relief directed against its use; Miller separates that nexus from ultimate constitutional entitlement. On the merits, the reconstructed allegations provide only territorial racial design, however deliberate or predominant. Under Shaw/Miller the direct legal argument against the decree is that the sole adjudicated theory does not establish the required constitutional deprivation. Supported purpose findings may be accepted without accepting the lower court's legal inference. The strongest opposition is that race-based individual assignment itself is the equal-treatment injury and should trigger scrutiny despite retained voting rights. That objection must confront Miller's rejection of the same claimed classification/status inference; changing district shape or moving from allegation to findings does not alone distinguish the rule. The resulting decree, if this path is chosen, must terminate relief dependent solely on the rejected merits theory in each appeal; it cannot preserve an injunction on dismissed counts or invent a new-injury investigation.

**Standing recognition followed by express constitutional reconsideration.** The contrary merits path is supported by Kennedy's published Miller dissent and the other dissenting writings: official assignment predominantly by race, with ordinary criteria subordinated, should itself trigger strict scrutiny, without requiring electoral defeat or a later discriminatory act. Its strongest objection is that it replaces two directly controlling, recent decisions, including Miller's accepted-predominance application, and risks treating lawful recognition of actual electoral conditions as an individual disability. A Justice choosing this path must identify the precise proposition being displaced, the stare decisis justification and the trigger's limit; mere racial awareness, a fixed percentage, unusual shape or failure of federal compulsion cannot silently substitute for the announced standard.

If that trigger is adopted, the three districts require record-specific treatment. The lower findings support predominance, but the State's political, incumbent, community and geography evidence must receive the applicable review; factual findings cannot be relabeled constitutional conclusions or discarded merely because the desired judgment differs. Strict scrutiny would require genuine justification and fit, not automatic invalidation. The strongest compliance defense invokes documented racial electoral conditions, preservation of District 18's opportunity and reasonable prevention of statutory violations; federal law need not command one exact map before a legislature can act. The strongest response invokes the lower findings concerning feasible alternatives, the selected racial targets, inadequately established statutory necessity and incumbency-driven departures. The Court must identify whether it sustains the lower rejection on a supportable standard or changes that standard. It must not adopt the lower court's least-irregular-shape language, its citizenship observations, or an assumption that §§2 and 5 demand the same thing without separate analysis.

**Claimed independent inequality within existing law.** Actual exclusion, inferior membership or materially unequal participation would remain a distinct merits route under Shaw/Miller. This record expressly excludes reliance on such an additional injury. General confusion evidence, lower-court predictions of stereotyping, or a map's racial composition cannot be rewritten as a plaintiff-specific finding. If a Justice finds the assignment itself independently cognizable, the opinion must explain whether that genuinely applies the retained rule or instead changes the rule Miller applied to the same asserted inference. There is no authorized new claim or new fact to avoid that issue.

**Jurisdictional disagreement.** A contrary threshold path may contend that territorial awareness or composition alleges no concrete personal inequality. It must explain Miller's unanimous personal-exposure holding and the five residents' different position from Chen, rather than equate failure on the merits with lack of standing. If the Court actually rejects jurisdiction over the only claims supporting relief, its decree must vacate the merits declaration and dependent injunction and specify the appropriate jurisdictional consequence. It may not then adjudicate those constitutional merits. Chen's lack of a stake in the three districts cannot by itself dispose of the five residents' claims; conversely residence alone is not sufficient.

**Scope of an actual mandate.** All three appellants challenge the same three-district declaration and injunction and need a coherent disposition by docket. Recognition of standing now requires a merits decision. A remand may implement the merits disposition or address a specifically necessary, legally supported remedial matter; it may not continue a standing-only reservation, fabricate new injury, reopen the dismissed statutory counts, or request research documents merely to fill the operator's archive. Reversal on the sole design-only theory removes the basis of the dependent declaration, prospective injunction and compulsory-redraw proceedings. Affirmance of a proved constitutional violation would leave the lawful remedy stage to the district court and State under the adjudicated limits, without inventing a replacement map or present election circumstances. Either route must distinguish a direction to apply for fees from an actual fee award, address dependent relief expressly, preserve unappealed rulings and avoid declaring the entire plan lawful or unlawful on unpresented grounds. No special election, new timetable, continuing stay, completed election or automatic enforcement fact is supplied.
