**Kurth**

**Current rule:** An extraordinary drug assessment operates as punishment when its combined features make it part of the criminal sanction: liability depends on illegal possession and arrest, collection follows criminal enforcement and is subordinate to fines and forfeitures, and the assessment taxes confiscated contraband no longer possessed at exceptional rates. On the same-offense premise, the State cannot impose that punitive assessment after completed criminal punishment. The combination controls; neither a high rate, unlawful subject matter nor a revenue label alone resolves the issue, and no universal cost ceiling or mathematical ratio is established.

**Open question:** Reverse sequencing, different sovereigns and independently administered confidential taxes are not decided. The rule does not abolish civil taxation of illegal activity.

**Austin**

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No comprehensive formula, ratio, mandatory list of factors, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. Austin leaves truly innocent ownership's constitutional protection unresolved; Alexander's independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct.

**James Daniel Good**

**Current rule:** The Government ordinarily must provide the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before civil forfeiture seizure of real property; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment duty. To proceed without prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as recorded notice of the claim, a restraining order or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use. Emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process. The protection includes control and income from rented property, not only physical occupancy.

**Witte**

**Holding:** The possibility of an overlapping later sentence does not itself bar this prosecution, and any sentence following conviction must satisfy the then-applicable coordination provisions and independent constitutional limits. Under received § 5G1.3, subsection (a) first requires the instant sentence to run consecutively to the undischarged term if the instant offense occurred during service of that term, including work release, furlough or escape, or after sentencing for that term but before service began; only if (a) does not apply does subsection (b) require concurrency when the earlier offenses were fully taken into account in the instant offense level; in all remaining cases, policy statement (c) requires consecutiveness to the extent necessary for reasonable incremental punishment, with the commentary's approximation task, not an automatic total or repeated departure.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, conditional coordination discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this statutory and remedial proposition. Stevens still dissents from allowing the prosecution, and Kennedy does not join the detailed coordination discussion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coordination requires an actual conviction, actual undischarged term, relevant-conduct calculation and then-effective Guidelines. Lawful departure authority may address unusual circumstances, including substantial-assistance effects, but permits rather than commands repetition of the earlier departure. Coordination cannot cure a valid successive-prosecution bar.

**Holding:** Consideration of relevant uncharged conduct in selecting a sentence within the authorized statutory range for the offense of conviction does not itself constitute prosecution or independent punishment for the uncharged offense. Witte's marijuana-attempt sentence therefore does not bar the distinct cocaine indictment under the retained Grady rule; this holding does not approve a separate disguised penalty, punishment beyond statutory authority or disregard of independent sentencing safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, constitutional-characterization discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this rationale. Scalia and Thomas affirm only in the judgment on the narrower distinct-prosecution ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The prior judgment imposed 144 months after substantial assistance within the five-to-forty-year statutory range; 292–365 months was the Guidelines calculation. Actual cocaine aggravation does not make that judgment an independent cocaine penalty. No acquitted-conduct rule, statutory-maximum increase rule, complete sentencing-jury test or same-transaction bar is adopted. The plea-agreement contention remains outside review.

**United States v. Dixon**

**Current rule:** Evidentiary use of an uncharged crime does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. A conspiracy and its substantive objects remain distinct offenses; prosecuting the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because acts help prove both. Grady's essential-conduct rule remains operative and does not displace the established agreement/substantive-offense distinction or become a same-evidence or same-transaction rule. Ordinary evidentiary restrictions retain their separate force. Beyond Blockburger, Grady bars a later prosecution when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense already prosecuted; evidence reuse or a common episode alone does not suffice. Completed nonsummary criminal contempt places the accused in criminal jeopardy despite private initiation or a protective purpose. When contempt necessarily prosecutes a substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same offense again. Coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns remain outside Dixon. Foster II-V may proceed under its case-specific judgment, but no controlling rationale governs those counts: the Blockburger-only/overruling and special judicial-order rationales are incompatible, not logical subsets. Grady survives without an invented general contempt exception.

## United States v. Ursery and United States v. $405,089.23


Sources: objective opening narrative of [518 U.S. 267](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-345.ZO.html), identifying the lower judgments at 59 F.3d 568 and 33 F.3d 1210 (and denial of rehearing 56 F.3d 41). This extraction does not adopt the source's ensuing Supreme Court analysis. The lower courts' characterization of the governing precedents is their reasoning, not entering law.

Ursery's house was the subject of a civil action under 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(7), alleging use to facilitate unlawful processing and distribution of a controlled substance. He paid $13,250 to settle that claim in full. Shortly before settlement was consummated he was indicted under §841(a)(1); he later received a jury conviction and 63 months' imprisonment. The Sixth Circuit reversed the criminal conviction on double-jeopardy grounds, treating the completed civil forfeiture as prior punishment. The reviewed judgment is the conviction's reversal, not a direct appeal from the settlement.

Arlt and Wren's case has the opposite order. The Government filed the civil complaint before their criminal trial, alleging property involved in money laundering under 18 U.S.C. §981(a)(1)(A) and drug proceeds or facilitating property under 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(6). The parties deferred civil litigation during the criminal prosecution. Following convictions under §§846, 371 and 1956, the District Court granted summary judgment in the forfeiture action more than a year later. The Ninth Circuit reversed that forfeiture judgment on double-jeopardy grounds. The claims against various property cannot all silently be characterized as proven proceeds; the complaint invoked overlapping statutory routes. No new finding as to a particular item's provenance or value is supplied.

These distinct judgment objects and sequences must remain separate. Determining a sanction's constitutional character does not alone decide retained Grady/Blockburger identity questions if a lawful path makes those questions material. Neither this extraction nor the packet establishes an unlitigated innocent-owner ruling or an Eighth Amendment excessiveness determination.
