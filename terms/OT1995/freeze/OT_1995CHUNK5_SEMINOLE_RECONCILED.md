## What the comparison changes

1. **O'Connor and Kennedy join the broader Article I ground.** Their provisional Commerce-only ceilings have no concrete changed premise. Scalia and Thomas likewise support that broader ground as their actual constitutional rationale, not merely a separate optional observation. Particular other statutes and remedies remain unadjudicated.
2. **Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer prefer the Young-only disposition.** Their constitutional opposition to State immunity remains available if the State question must be reached. It is broader than application of Union Gas to a prospective Indian-Commerce suit: it treats Hans immunity as federal common law subject to deliberate congressional displacement under valid Article I authority, while retaining Hans absent that displacement. They do not rest their position on the complete reasoning of the Union Gas plurality.
3. **The clear-statement register is qualified by that preferred route.** Those three do not join an insistence that unmistakable direct-State abrogation must be adjudicated before officer relief. They construe IGRA to permit the connected officer action and avoid the constitutional question. Their alternative direct-State position recognizes sufficient congressional intent. This is neither a finding that IGRA fails every clear-statement inquiry nor a primary vote to affirm the State dismissal.
4. **Stevens retains serious Article III doubts.** His opposition to both immunity grounds is preserved, but his provisional unqualified join in J is withdrawn. Historical Part V expressed a serious unresolved doubt, not a vote to dismiss on Article III grounds. Actual Wisconsin supplies a material analogy about partial redress, but does not decide the judicial-function question or eliminate his concern about this statutory judgment. The other seven Associates retain J.
5. **The Secretary substitute route remains undecided.** Preserve the enacted sequence and refuse to invent an automatic bypass, but do not change the lower court's separate substitute-remedy determination through this immunity mandate. The provisional instruction to correct the lower mandate insofar as it could permit a shortcut is withdrawn. Historical n.18 expressly reserved that matter; no changed scope or posture supports deciding it here.

These are reconciliation corrections within the already validated constitutional-power, clear-statement, officer-only avoidance, Article III and remedy branches. No new neutral factual premise, external proceeding, statutory qualification or materially different ground was discovered and injected. No clean-neutral return is presently necessary. Any later proposal relying on an additional such premise must return through neutral validation and affected modeling/refreezing before assembly.

## Preserved record and complete statutory sequence

The plaintiff is the Tribe, and Florida and Governor Chiles in his official capacity are distinct defendants. Alabama is not a party to this docket. The June 1992 denial of immunity was immediately appealable under actual Puerto Rico Aqueduct, but appealability establishes no substantive immunity. This review supplies no finding that Florida negotiated in bad faith.

The **September 22, 1993 merits summary judgment for Florida and the Governor and the stayed Eleventh Circuit appeal, No. 93-5256, remain separate**. Removing an immunity bar does not vacate that merits judgment, decide its appeal, establish a game entitlement, or direct mediation as though bad faith had already been found. Historical Court n.6 independently confirms the existence of this distinct proceeding; it supplies no new simulated disposition.

The complaint's January 29 request and September 19, 1991 filing allegations remain distinct from the United States' December filing account. Both intervals exceed 180 days. No amended-complaint history is supplied. The actual prayer for a compact within sixty days, later mediation and a game-scope declaration remains different from the government's proposed narrower negotiating or declaratory relief. Negotiations by the Governor's representatives establish a connection to the challenged conduct, not waiver or unlimited authority to bind Florida.

All commitments preserve the following statutory operation, including when a Justice concludes the particular enforcement route unavailable:

- Section 2710(d)(3)(A) imposes the good-faith negotiating duty after the qualifying tribal request. It does not require agreement on preferred terms. A failure-to-negotiate action under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after the 180-day period closes. The effective-compact action under (A)(ii) and the Secretary's enforcement action under (A)(iii) remain separate causes.
- Under (B)(ii), tribal evidence that no compact exists and that the State failed to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's burden to prove good faith. Nonagreement alone is not a bad-faith finding.
- An actual adverse finding triggers (B)(iii)'s order to the State and Tribe to conclude a compact within sixty days. The court **may** consider public interest, public safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming; it **shall** consider a demand for direct taxation of the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. These are different directions, not a single mandatory factor list.
- Nonagreement during that ordered period triggers (B)(iv): each side submits its last-best proposal to the court-appointed mediator. The mediator selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law, and the court's findings and order. Section (B)(v)'s submission to both sides starts the separate sixty-day consent period.
- State consent makes the selection an agreed compact under (B)(vi). Nonconsent during the second period triggers notice to the Secretary and (B)(vii)'s mandatory prescription, in consultation with the Tribe, consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The direct findings/order language is in (iv), not repeated verbatim in (vii). The Secretary does not have unrestricted power simply to withhold all action.
- No automatic award of the Tribe's proposal follows from state nonparticipation. The United States' qualified prediction is not a textual default rule. The adopted statutory construction does not add contempt or a coercive order compelling agreement merely because agreement or participation during the ordered period fails; the specified progression is the consequence. It does not adjudicate every possible sanction for separate obstruction or disobedience.
- Section (d)(4)'s no-new-state-tax-power provision preserves the agreed regulatory-cost assessment under (d)(3)(C)(iii); it does not erase the different mandatory evidentiary treatment of direct-tax demands. Lack of other taxing authority supplies no reason to refuse negotiations.
- Approved tribal legislation, the State-permits-such-gaming condition, an effective compact subject to the applicable remedial provisions, and licensing remain distinct. Section (d)(8) allows disapproval for IGRA violations, other federal law **not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands**, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction yields approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA. Federal Register publication remains necessary.
- Section 2721 preserves remaining provisions if a provision is invalid. It does not itself satisfy a court finding, either waiting period, mediation, selection or nonconsent, and creates no replacement federal program. No present commitment licenses gaming, dictates terms, awards damages or grants taxing power.

The Secretary-route reservation means that none of those predicates is deemed fulfilled by an immunity dismissal. It also means this docket does not adjudicate a distinct asserted substitute remedy or unpresented administrative program. Preserve the difference between declining to create or endorse such a route and affirmatively reversing it.

## Reconciled component definitions

**J — judicial character.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the categorical advisory-function objection on the independent handoff's full reasoning. A valid judgment in the federal negotiating dispute conclusively changes legal relations and activates constrained statutory steps, supplying real partial relief although no particular compact is guaranteed. The answer addresses the judgment's legal consequence, not merely whether the Secretary can edit individual findings. Aetna and Nashville prevent a universal execution requirement; Brimson supports adjudication assisting administration while retaining its actually enforceable-order setting; Ferreira and the properly identified Gordon/Hayburn materials require attention to legal effectiveness and executive revision. Actual Wisconsin supplies a limited redress analogy, and Morgan Stanley/Plaut supplies no general decision of this different problem. No blanket preclusion, coercive remedy or new cause is invented. Stevens's distinct reservation appears below; he is not counted as joining this affirmative proposition or as voting for an independent Article III dismissal.

**I — direct-State intent, when reached.** The specified action, State evidentiary burden, order to State and Tribe, and successive State-directed steps unmistakably express direct-State amenability. General jurisdiction or scattered references alone would not do so. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt that determination in their primary route. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept it on the alternative direct-State route but prefer construing the action to include the connected officer and leaving State abrogation undecided. They reject an inference that State-directed language excludes Young. Do not report unconditional eight-Associate adoption of the first route's sequencing.

**W — no demonstrated consent.** No express state waiver, automatic reciprocal tribal/state convention consent, constructive waiver from negotiating, or delegation of the United States' immunity exemption establishes this suit. The scope of constitutional immunity and the power of congressional displacement are different questions. Stevens's and Souter's accounts of Hans do not convert this record into consent. The United States is an amicus, not the plaintiff.

**P−A — Article I limitation and express precedent correction.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas conclude that Article I authority cannot circumvent the constitutional immunity limiting federal judicial power and authorize this private action against a nonconsenting State. The prohibition is not limited to the Commerce Clause or retrospective money. Union Gas's contrary constitutional conclusion is expressly overruled, rather than silently ignored because its rationale was fractured. Its clear-statement analysis is not thereby overruled. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority survives. The Indian/interstate and proprietary/governmental distinctions do not furnish an alternative basis to retain Union Gas. This general constitutional premise does not itself adjudicate every application of another federal statute, grant immunity to every public entity, foreclose United States enforcement, or eliminate ordinary Young relief.

**P+S — Stevens's direct-State ground.** Outside the Eleventh Amendment's actual textual restriction, Hans immunity is a judicial doctrine Congress can displace through its valid legislative powers. A statute cannot alter the Constitution's text or confer jurisdiction outside Article III. IGRA supplies the affirmative federal direction absent from Hans; sustaining it requires neither overruling Hans's actual result nor limiting congressional power to this prospective Indian-Commerce context. Union Gas also supplies a binding constitutional conclusion that should not be overruled for the reasons offered. Stevens leaves the textual problem concerning out-of-state or foreign-citizen plaintiffs open; the Tribe is not silently converted into such a plaintiff. He does not demand elimination of all sovereign immunity to decide this case.

**P+H — Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's alternative direct-State ground.** If the constitutional issue is reached, valid Article I authority and deliberate congressional intent can displace Hans immunity. The latter remains a federal-common-law rule, retained as a matter of stare decisis absent such displacement, not a constitutional obstacle Congress can never remove. They independently reject constitutionalizing the nontextual immunity rather than merely adopting Brennan's full Union Gas rationale. They would follow Union Gas's constitutional conclusion if needed, but their view of constitutional text, the limited holdings of the immunity precedents and legislative control of common law supplies an independent ground. The power is not confined to prospective relief or to Indian and interstate commerce. The present suit nevertheless awards no damages and adjudicates no unrelated statute. Their Hans retention does not conflict with criticism of its origins: stability counsels retaining its actual default rule without extending it into an unalterable restriction.

**Y+ — connected, prospective officer action within IGRA.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer permit the officer action. Young supplies the established jurisdictional route for prospective enforcement of an ongoing federal violation; it does not create unrestricted substantive remedies or retrospective monetary recovery. Mandatory good faith is distinct from discretion over compact terms. The official's negotiating connection suffices for the threshold claim; unlimited compacting authority is unnecessary to reject categorical dismissal. State-directed wording does not exclude the officials through whom a State acts, and incorporated state gaming law does not by itself make this a Pennhurst state-law claim. The statute's own limits continue to govern relief.

For Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, congressional exclusion of this established federal equitable route requires a clear indication, not an inference from intricacy or State wording alone. They accept that Congress may limit enforcement of a statutory right, but find no adequate indication here. The usual contrast between inventing an additional personal damages action and using existing officer equity matters. Reading Young into this arrangement does not discard either sixty-day period or the mediator/Secretary conditions. This is their proposed application of received equity and clear-statement principles, not a holding already made by Sea Clammers, Smith, Wilder, Varity or Meghrig. Stevens supports the route consistently with his own remedial-intent analysis; no automatic formal join in another Justice's full theory is manufactured.

**Y− — IGRA-specific exclusion.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas deny this officer action. Congress deliberately chose the State-party action, a reciprocal State/Tribe order and the limited substitute progression. An ordinary free-standing Young injunction would permit broader immediate judicial enforcement, potentially including contempt, that Congress did not choose for this duty. Matching the statutory deadlines in a recast officer action does not overcome the further evidence that Congress selected this defendant and enforcement form. The answer therefore extends to the government's confined proposal; it does not depend only on supposing that proposal seeks unlimited coercion. The distinctive sovereign-compacting arrangement reinforces the statutory inference without establishing that every affirmative duty, discretionary act or State-directed provision defeats Young. Congress can authorize an officer action with limited remedies; these Justices find it did not do so here. They preserve ordinary Young and do not make remedy gaps an authority to rewrite this Act.

**A — preferred avoidance for Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** As to **Governor Chiles**, reverse the officer dismissal and remand for the statutory claim to proceed through its lawful threshold and merits stages. As to **Florida itself**, vacate the appellate direction to dismiss on immunity and remand without deciding the constitutional abrogation question. The State claim remains pending for consideration of its equitable necessity after the officer route is recognized; the vacatur does not establish jurisdiction to adjudicate merits against Florida. A lower court must obtain a lawful jurisdictional basis before adjudicating that claim, or resolve it on a properly supported nonmerits ground. The Tribe may choose to forgo the State claim, or adequate officer relief may support discretionary denial of duplicative State relief, but neither event is found to have occurred. No automatic waiver, mootness, abandonment, required dismissal, indefinite stay or entitlement to final compact relief is ordered. The separate September 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal remain distinct.

The historical dissent's concluding request to reverse did not spell out a defendant-specific mandate for its preferred avoidance route. This vacatur/reversal specification implements that route and the already-validated United States n.5 alternative without falsely claiming that the historical writing prescribed these exact formal verbs. Keeping the State dismissal affirmed would adjudicate against the Tribe the constitutional question the route leaves open; an unconditional reversal establishing State amenability would decide it in the other direction. This nonmerits vacatur does neither.

**Conditions for P+H and I becoming operative joins:** Each of Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer firmly supports the alternative direct-State result if the disposition rejects application of Young and adjudicates Florida's still-live immunity claim. In that setting each would reverse Florida's immunity dismissal under I and P+H and is willing to join that broad common-law/Article I rationale, while continuing to disagree with the denial of officer relief. They need not adopt all of the Union Gas plurality's reasons, Stevens's distinct textual reservations, or an opinion claiming that Hans has been judicially abolished. If the operative disposition permits Y+ and leaves the State claim unresolved under A, their constitutional analyses remain alternatives, not actual joins in an unnecessary abrogation holding. The mere proposal of a broader opinion, or the Governor's unproved power to conclude every compact term, does not activate them. A materially different later record, a new reason that State adjudication is unavoidable, or a circulation proposal asking them to abandon that avoidance limit requires a further scoped reconciliation; it is not preauthorized by the phrase “if reached.”

**T — nonreach.** Do not decide the distinct Tenth Amendment invalidation first advanced on appeal and not decided below. It is not fairly included in the presented immunity/officer questions in the sense needed to decide it here. Actual X-Citement Video permits an appropriate prevailing-respondent defense without compelling decision of this distinct constitutional issue; actual Rule 14 doctrine remains prudential. No jurisdictional powerlessness, automatic forfeiture of every new argument, or independent mediator-appointments invalidation follows. The Article III inquiry does not make these other issues reachable. Actual New York and Voting Rights Coalition retain their different constitutional bases and reservations.

**R — remedy and nondecision.** Preserve the full sequence, actual prayer/possible lawful relief distinction, separate September 1993 merits judgment and appeal, absence of present bad-faith or gaming findings, and all limitations stated above. Affirmance of the two immunity dismissals creates no replacement enforcement program. A reversal removes only the adopted threshold barriers and permits the appropriate further proceedings, not automatic compacting relief. Expressly leave the distinct Secretary substitute-remedy matter unadjudicated.

### O'Connor

Historical full joinder covered the constitutional Article I rule, Union Gas overruling, direct-State relief neutrality and statute-specific Young exclusion. Her Union Gas constitutional dissent already supported the general structural distinction between original Article I power and the later Fourteenth Amendment. Actual Casey requires special justification beyond renewed disagreement; her actual Allied-Bruce concurrence and Hubbard dissent take statutory precedent and administrability seriously. Those positions make the independent handoff's careful stare-decisis treatment necessary, but do not supply a concrete reason to reject the broader Article I rationale while accepting its Commerce application. Neither actual Hess's institutional-control dissent nor Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies such a distinction. Her narrower constitutional ceiling is therefore corrected.

She continues to find the unresolved conflict between Union Gas's commerce exception and constitutionally grounded immunity, combined with its fractured rationale and deficient integration with the immunity precedents, sufficient to correct that constitutional conclusion. The missing majority rationale is a reason for examination, not proof that the five-vote conclusion never bound. The strongest objection remains the P+H distinction between default immunity and legislative displacement; she rejects that construction of the Constitution. No invented finding of zero reliance or empirical impossibility of administering a clear-statement rule is required. Her Smith majority/Wilder dissent and actual Varity dissent/Meghrig join support the particular Y− inference while preserving its express statutory limits.

### Stevens

Actual Hess, Casey and Hubbard neither constitutionalize all Hans immunity nor change the reason to respect Union Gas's actual constitutional vote. P+S and his refusal of wholesale Hans overruling are therefore preserved; broader reasoning is not confused with a decree adjudicating every Article I statute or damages remedy.

Historical Part V, 517 U.S. 99–100, expresses serious doubt about the judicial character of an intermediate step leading to executive relief, particularly in light of the lower court's supposedly dispensable judicial step. It does **not** adopt that ground as a judgment of dismissal. The independently frozen J took the opposite affirmative position without carrying that qualification. His actual Wisconsin Part I specifically recognizes concrete partial redress through further governmental action, but addresses standing and authoritative reconsideration, not whether IGRA assigns a nonjudicial judgment. His earlier actual Morgan Stanley/Plaut position likewise does not settle this question. No changed statutory facts, eliminated executive discretion, binding Secretary-route adjudication or directly contrary prior public commitment removes the historical concern. The unqualified J join is therefore corrected to a serious reservation, while his primary opposition to the immunity judgment remains. The preparation's distinctions about Gordon's paper, Hayburn's communications, Ferreira and binding partial relief remain available; they do not justify silently attributing a definitive resolution to him.

His Sea Clammers/Smith/Wilder positions and actual Varity/Meghrig joins support Y+ within Congress's actual constraints. He also accepts a lawful Young-only route as a narrower sufficient alternative; this does not erase his own affirmative direct-State ground or convert his agreement with reasons in the historical Souter dissent into a separately recorded formal joinder in every proposition.

### Souter

His language cannot accurately be reduced to a default **constitutional** immunity plus a Commerce exception, nor to a prospective-only power rule.

His own Varity and Meghrig opinions distinguish a lawful route from unrestricted relief; they do not require complete ultimate relief as a condition for invoking a connected officer's jurisdiction. His Wisconsin and Barnett joins reinforce that threshold relief may leave independent merits predicates unresolved. The provisional reason for preferring constitutional resolution—unproved complete compacting authority—is therefore not a concrete changed premise: that limitation was expressly confronted by his historical reasoning. A and P+H replace the narrower frozen positions. J and the detailed Y+ limits remain.
