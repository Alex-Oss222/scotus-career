## Historical comparison and its limits

The complete official report, **517 U.S. 314–342**, controls the comparison. Contrary to the comparator's shorthand “Reversed,” the operative historical judgment at 332–333 is **vacated and remanded**. Breyer's entire opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg. Rehnquist concurs in the judgment, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Official page 334 supplies the complete concurrence join line; Cornell's damaged opening cannot supply it. Historical authorship supplies no assignment here.

The historical majority's Part II, 319–321, connects the stay to dismissal: the appellate stay vacatur is lawful only if dismissal would be lawful, because execution would otherwise defeat adjudication. It expressly extends *Barefoot*'s initial-appeal principle to a first district petition, permits Rule 4 dismissal and lawful expedition, and relies on the apparent substantiality of Lonchar's claims and the State's failure to argue otherwise. Part III, 322–332, rejects an additional equitable dismissal sanction; preserves structured judicial discretion and established procedural grounds; enforces Rule 9(a)'s prejudice condition and Congress's deletion of the five-year presumption; and leaves the Rules' ultimate application undecided. The majority reserves what prejudice would suffice in a last-minute case because the State disavowed Rule 9(a), and allows litigation of the asserted state procedural bar on remand. Its treatment of next-friend litigation, the withdrawn state petition and subjective motive does not adjudicate the underlying constitutional claims.

The historical concurrence, 334–342, agrees that the stated dismissal ground fails, but rejects the necessary equation of stay refusal and dismissal. It accepts claim substantiality for the stay inquiry and the absence of established Rule 9 predicates, yet treats delay and manipulation as independent stay considerations under *Gomez*. First-petition status strengthens the interest in review without immunizing misconduct. It would remand because the district court had not properly conducted that distinct inquiry and had counted Lonchar's resistance to next-friend proceedings against him. Asserting competence and control could not itself be blamed; proved collusion would present a different case. Its three notes preserve the limits of last-day expedition, distinguish delay caused by State scheduling from deliberate refusal, and cite the later lower *Fierro* result to illustrate that constitutional merit and stay entitlement differ. Those historical record assessments and that lower result are not newly admitted facts or law here.

**The principal changed legal premise is the actual public *Gomez*.** Its April 21, 1992 opinion makes independent equity the sole controlling ground, expressly assuming an open procedural route and no abuse-of-writ bar. Thus the historical majority's explanation at 329–330 that *Gomez* merely carries successive-abuse rules across procedural labels cannot be adopted here. Four previous petitions remain facts of *Gomez*, not a minimum trigger. Actual *McFarland* separately requires a reasonable statutory preparation opportunity while preserving individualized denial for proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction. Neither decision answers whether Lonchar has received all process due.

**The competing affirmance path remains substantial but insufficient on this supplied record.** The lower findings describe prolonged refusal, repeated last-minute changes and an extraneous purpose; *Gomez* and *McFarland* require those facts to receive real consideration. The complete *Barefoot* distinctions remain material to C and to the reservations:

- **First district petition:** filing alone supplies no automatic suspension. Rule 4 screening and lawful procedural disposition remain; C identifies an extension of meaningful-review reasoning to whatever district process is actually required.
- **Initial appeal with a certificate of probable cause:** meaningful merits opportunity and appellate decision are required. Where execution would moot review and time prevents considered merits disposition, a stay must issue. Adequate notice and an opportunity to address the merits permit expedited or simultaneous merits/stay disposition. The certificate requires more than nonfrivolity: a substantial federal showing, including reasonably debatable issues, possible different resolution or questions deserving further consideration; it requires neither ultimate success nor certification from sentence severity alone. No such final denial and certified appeal are established here.
- **Second or successive petition:** Rule 9(b)'s actual predicates and possible expedition remain; a stay should reflect substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Failed next-friend proceedings alone do not trigger that setting for this petition.
- **Pending certiorari after appellate habeas denial:** no automatic stay; there must be a reasonable probability that four Members would find the issue sufficiently meritorious for a grant of certiorari or notation of probable jurisdiction, a significant possibility of reversal, and likely irreparable harm. The application must supply materials for careful assessment; relief should first be sought in the court of appeals, whose decision generally receives considerable weight in that setting. These are not the merits criteria for Lonchar's already-argued challenge.
