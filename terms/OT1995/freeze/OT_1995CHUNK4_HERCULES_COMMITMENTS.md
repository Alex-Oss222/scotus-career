## Common component positions

**R1 — genuine contract and sovereign authority.** Section 1491 permits adjudication of claims on actual express or implied-in-fact government contracts; it does not create the substantive promise asserted here or authorize an implied-in-law duty to reimburse on grounds of fairness. Spearin recognizes a genuine contractual implication from government specifications. Hatzlachh rejects the converse mistake that tort immunity, by itself, prevents recovery on a genuine contract. A contract claim therefore cannot be defeated simply by saying that the veterans could not sue the United States in tort. Sutton requires actual governmental authority for implied as well as express undertakings. Neither procurement authority nor a military objective alone proves authority for an additional open-ended indemnity.

These propositions are compatible. An ordinary authorized contractual warranty is not automatically void under the Anti-Deficiency Act merely because breach damages are uncertain at formation. Conversely, calling a proposed indemnity a warranty does not evade limits on authorized obligations. The official texts of §1341(a)(1)(A)–(B) and §1431 preserve those distinctions. No applicable delegation, extraordinary-contract approval, appropriation, contractual indemnity clause or other positive authorization is found as a new fact here. Section 1431's special authority is not a promise that its conditions were met in these contracts.

**R2 — DPA's shield and the asserted contractual inference.** Thompson's separate DPA indemnity theory fails. Section 101 authorizes priorities, compulsory acceptance/performance and allocations; §707 protects against specified damages or penalties arising from compliance. Section 707 does not promise Treasury reimbursement. The conclusion does not require deciding whether its immunity extends beyond displaced commercial contracts to particular accelerated-production or product-injury claims.

Petitioners' stronger argument is considered: they disclaim a private statutory cause of action and ask for an implied-in-fact promise from the entire setting—compulsion, specifications, control, asserted superior knowledge and statutory protection. Even assuming the disputed features in their favor for this legal issue, obedience to a priority command is not a manifestation that the Government undertook to pay later tort settlements and defense expenses. Control and knowledge may bear on an otherwise cognizable contract duty; they do not add the missing reimbursement undertaking to the DPA order. To impose it simply because compelled suppliers should not bear the loss would be an obligation implied in law. No ruling on the full scope of §707 immunity, a standalone takings right, or all possible contracts made under the DPA follows.

**R3 — factual and remedial limits.** Hercules supplied under fifteen contracts, with the Government identifying initial competitive awards; Thompson's two contracts followed its initial refusal. The distinct alleged 1967 increased-production command must not turn every contract into an involuntary transaction from inception. Nor is a finding made that all fifteen Hercules contracts were competitively awarded. Dioxin was an unwanted byproduct; the parties dispute which specifications, processes, warnings and knowledge caused what. Hercules's later elimination of measurable dioxin and Thompson's knowledge history are not interchangeable. The 1984 settlement, the subsequent opt-out litigation, and Boyle's 1988 articulation are different events. Tort-settlement approval does not establish government contract breach or recoverable damages. Neither does the absence of a final tort judgment automatically establish that all settlement losses were gratuitous.

The full lodged contracts, original specifications and delegations are not in the inspected source set. The printed complaints contain allegations; the Government's 1984 opt-out brief is advocacy, not a factual finding or an admission of all present contract elements. The lower litigation involved material beyond bare pleadings, so its record is not described as empty. The asserted settlement shares and defense costs remain claims, not adjudicated amounts. No Associate orders payment, fixes damages, finds particular specifications defective, or treats the later government-contractor defense as a proven fact of 1984 certainty.

## Coverage and causation fork

**A — affirmance on the coverage argument (O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas).** The ordinary Spearin implication assures the suitability of prescribed specifications for performing the procurement undertaking. It does not, solely from the Government's furnishing specifications and accepting conforming goods, add an undertaking to bear the supplier's liability and litigation costs to third persons exposed to the delivered product. Spearin concerned failure of government-designed works and the resulting performance/cancellation loss. Its consequences language must be read with the promised performance and breach it addressed; it is not an unlimited indemnity for every later loss connected with the product.

Ryan and Weyerhaeuser, 355 U.S. 563, establish that an actual contractual duty can generate foreseeable third-party liability damages without an express indemnity clause. Their stevedoring service undertaking included safe, workmanlike performance for the shipowner, with the indemnity consequence rooted in that undertaking. They defeat a universal 'express indemnity only' rule but do not prove the additional promise alleged in these procurement contracts. Hatzlachh similarly preserves a real contract claim against an independent tort-immunity defense; it does not supply whatever promise a claimant needs. The present legal theory cannot convert standard specifications, priority compulsion and allegedly superior information into the pleaded litigation-loss coverage merely by invoking those cases.

This is a rejection of the asserted legal implication, assuming the favorable allegations needed to test it; it is **not** a factual determination that the uninspected documents contain a particular limiting clause or that every conceivable implied undertaking was disproved after trial. The conceded absence of an express indemnity and the presented sources of the claimed implied term identify the legal issue. No new technical or scientific finding is needed to decide it. The holding does not foreclose third-party consequential damages under a different, proven contractual undertaking or categorically limit every contract remedy to losses incurred before delivery.

For these five Associates that ground suffices to affirm denial of settlement recovery. They do not adopt the Federal Circuit's alternative equation between a successful warranty claim and a certainly successful government-contractor defense. A tort defense is not itself a contract disclaimer; the timing, elements and settlement uncertainties make that equation unnecessary and inappropriate to announce here. They also do not adopt a factual no-breach/no-injury ground or make absence of a particular appropriation an independently established fact.

Defense expenses require their own disposition. They are not eliminated merely by an argument that settlement was unnecessary: defending successfully still costs money. The same absent litigation-loss undertaking, however, defeats the claim for these expenses under the warranty asserted here. This is sufficient to affirm that portion as well. Kania arose from a nonprosecution agreement, actual-authority/Tucker Act issues and remote litigation expenses; its language is not converted into a universal prohibition on third-party litigation expenses as damages for breach of an ordinary procurement contract. Fees for this contract litigation and previously incurred tort-defense expenses are distinct. No independent new fee award is made.

**B — vacate the specifications-warranty disposition and remand (Ginsburg, Breyer).** The ordinary warranty cannot be rejected categorically just because the claimed economic loss arrived through third-party tort litigation. Spearin's undertaking that the contractor not be responsible for the consequences of defective required specifications, Ryan's recognition of consequential indemnity without an express clause, Weyerhaeuser's insistence on the contractual duty's separate assessment, and Hatzlachh's rejection of tort-immunity displacement leave a legally possible contract claim. These authorities do not prove this claim, but they make the asserted temporal/category limit too broad for summary disposition on the described record.

The Federal Circuit selected a different ground: no causal loss because the government-contractor defense would have defeated tort liability. It left material contract questions unresolved and treated settlement as voluntary by reference to defense rulings made before and after the 1984 settlement. The availability of a later articulated tort defense does not necessarily eliminate reasonable settlement loss or the economic consequences of defending. The Government's postsettlement advocacy in opt-out litigation also illustrates dispute rather than establishing either side's version. On this approach, the record calls for assessment of the actual contractual duty and its authorized scope, breach, relative knowledge where legally material, the ex ante litigation risk, causal connection, foreseeability, reasonableness and allocation of any claimed loss. Neither general tort risk nor reasonable settlement alone substitutes for a contract breach.

The same remand reaches the specifications-warranty claim for tort-defense expenses. Kania's distinct sovereign/prosecutorial setting and remote-damages analysis do not settle whether such expenses were recoverable consequences of breach of an authorized procurement duty here. These Associates would require that separate question to be addressed under the actual contract and appropriate damages rules. They do not find that every claimed fee was foreseeable, necessary or reasonable. They affirm rejection of Thompson's independent DPA indemnity theory under R2 and do not reopen the unpresented causes. Their remedy is **vacatur in part and remand**, limited to the preserved specifications-warranty claims and associated damages requests for both petitioners; all other disposed claims remain undisturbed.
