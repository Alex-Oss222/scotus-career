## Historical comparison and changed-law test

The historical judgment reversed the operative First Circuit judgment unanimously. The exact historical writing joins matter independently of that result: Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg joined every part of the principal opinion; Souter joined I, II, III, V, VII and VIII, and also O'Connor's concurrence; Scalia joined I, II, VII and VIII and wrote separately; Thomas joined I, II, VI and VII and wrote separately, **not joining VIII**. O'Connor's concurrence was joined by Rehnquist, Souter and Breyer. Thus III/V had four votes, IV three, VI four, VII six and VIII five. There were three separate writings and no dissent. These are comparator facts, not a proposed simulated topology.

Part V found both insufficient material advancement and unreasonable fit. The O'Connor concurrence assumed advancement and independently rejected fit. Souter's dual joins do not erase his affirmative Part V position or count him twice. Scalia's reliance on existing jurisprudence did not expressly adopt either sufficient ground in Part V. Thomas rejected balancing for the particular asserted purpose. Part VII expressly disavowed LaRue's Twenty-first Amendment rationale without questioning its holding; O'Connor instead distinguished LaRue. Part VI rejected Posadas's highly deferential paternalism, greater-power syllogism and freestanding lawful-vice exception; O'Connor relied on subsequent, more searching Central Hudson applications. There is no automatic narrowest-opinion rule that imports all of O'Connor's reservations into every other writing.

The actual precedents are tested for what changed, rather than counted as earlier joins:

- **Edenfield:** responsive justification and material advancement remain required, with O'Connor's prophylaxis disagreement preserved. The earlier agreements of Scalia and Thomas, and Stevens's protection of truthful information, are not newly changed premises merely because they appear in the public record. They cannot by themselves justify departing from these Justices' historical Liquormart rationales.
- **Rubin:** the actual Court expressly makes reasonable fit independently sufficient, applies advancement to the operating scheme, and considers the state-assistance rationale on the merits. That strengthens the available doctrinal route but does not require every Justice to join it in every later case. The historical Liquormart writings themselves discuss Rubin's alternatives analysis. Its familiar eight-Justice alignment and Stevens's separate objection therefore supply no sufficient explanation for moving Thomas into balancing, Scalia into an express fit join, or Stevens out of every conditional fit analysis.
- **Lanphere:** the added Supreme Court merits precedent subjects speech-selected access to otherwise available records to advancement and fit scrutiny. Kennedy, Stevens, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined that application; O'Connor and Scalia dissented. Privacy, abuse and record access differ from suppressing accurate prices to manipulate lawful purchasers. The decision neither makes Central Hudson exclusive for an independently illegitimate purpose nor forecloses a conditional Central Hudson ground. It supplies no universal right to advertise out-of-state transactions.
- **Edge:** actual controlling law requires meaningful reduction and reasonable fit on the particular record; broader category-level reasoning lacks a majority. This materially removes a potential deferential governing premise and reinforces scrutiny here. But the separate positions of O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas favoring broader latitude remain available; Stevens's Bigelow dissent remains a dissent. Actual Edge still sustains the particular border-broadcast restriction, so its narrower rationale neither requires a new categorical anti-ignorance rule nor establishes interstate-information immunity.
- **Florida Bar:** the actual judgment invalidates the finite waiting period on its record, unlike history. Its advancement-and-fit analysis is now governing law. Kennedy, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's personal objections and O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer's support for the immediate-privacy restriction remain substantively recognizable. The changed judgment strengthens the scrutiny of this permanent price ban, but it does not force the latter four to reject all prediction or require the former four to abandon their distinct views of paternalism. Historical passages citing an upheld waiting period cannot be carried forward as law. Privacy-based consumer protection remains distinguishable as an interest; this particular waiting period does not remain valid.

These differences support adapting the citations and applications. They do not sufficiently support the provisional material changes to Thomas's rationale, Scalia's express fit commitment, Stevens's exclusion from conditional fit, or the historically supported breadth of the other individual joins. Those commitments are corrected here, rather than rationalized by unchanged earlier voting patterns.

## Reconciled grounds and individual checks

The grounds in this table have the following exact scopes:

**Advancement and fit:** The third-prong ground rejects the claimed material consumption effect on this trial record; it does not deny every price or demand effect. The fourth-prong ground independently finds this blanket restraint disproportionate, using concrete direct price/tax alternatives without prescribing a replacement or requiring the least restrictive means. Ordinary Central Hudson application and its conditional use as an alternative are distinguished.

**Special care and anti-paternalism:** Part III-type protection of truthful commercial information and special care for complete bans is distinct from Part IV-type more rigorous review of suppression unrelated to consumer protection. Neither alone is Thomas's categorical rule. Thomas's rule treats an asserted interest in keeping lawful purchasers ignorant to manipulate their choices as per se illegitimate; he reserves balancing for other kinds of interests. Stevens retains his objection to this informational objective, but his alternative application of existing doctrine is affirmative and his support for special care is not recorded as a universal strict-scrutiny rule.

**Twenty-first Amendment:** The broader ground says it does not qualify First Amendment freedom of speech and expressly disavows LaRue's reliance on it; LaRue's holding remains supported by police powers. The narrower treatment reaches the same no-presumption result for this restraint by distinguishing licensed-premises conduct regulation, without joining that express disavowal. Neither treatment invalidates LaRue or Bellanca's conduct holdings.

**Posadas:** The broader treatment rejects its highly deferential permission to choose informational suppression for paternalistic ends, the greater-power/lesser-power argument, and a freestanding exception for lawful vice advertising. The narrower treatment applies subsequent searching Central Hudson review and rejects automatic authorization here. Neither is a wholesale overruling of every Posadas holding. Constitutional speech rights are not surrendered merely by accepting a liquor license.

## Record, Peoples and relief retained

**Record and alternatives.** All eight retain the frozen fact/preservation boundaries. The district court found **no significant consumption effect** after trial. Those adopting advancement failure consider Saffer's search-cost mechanism, price responsiveness, opposing expert concessions, measurement difficulty and the general advertising evidence; they distinguish some possible effect from demonstrated material advancement. A missing state-specific estimate is part of this record, not a universal quantitative-proof requirement. Those assuming advancement do not replace the trial finding with an appellate finding of increased consumption. Constitutional sufficiency remains distinct from historical facts and credibility. Expected private profits do not establish market-wide consumption. The fit ground addresses the State's interstate-shopping and antitrust objections: they do not establish that all direct controls are unavailable or that this indirect mechanism avoids leakage. No all-sources-at-once requirement, automatic invalidation from the exceptions, or least-restrictive-means test follows.

**Peoples.** Its preserved claim concerns truthful Rhode Island advertisements for lawful Massachusetts sales; its Commerce Clause claim stays waived. The historical writings invalidated the media prohibition without announcing a separate Bigelow disposition. Stevens's earlier Bigelow/Edge dissent is substantially the same personal premise historically; actual Edge's narrower governing rationale still sustains a particular interstate restriction. Accordingly, correct the provisional **additional independently dispositive interstate ground**: Stevens may use Bigelow to reinforce protection of Peoples' lawful information within his adopted grounds, but no separate rule immunizing lawful out-of-state advertising is adopted here. This does not withdraw Peoples' full First Amendment relief. Thomas's categorical rationale also reaches this presented restraint, while his reservation concerns the materially different situation where the forum State actually prohibits or regulates the underlying domestic purchase. The other Associates need no separate interstate rule. No Justice decides the waived commerce theory.

**Exact reach.** Section 3-8-7 permits price signs/tags **attached to or placed on merchandise for sale within licensed premises**, subject to departmental rules; this is not permission for unrestricted indoor advertising. Regulation 32 bars price references visible from outside Class A premises. Section 3-8-8.1 covers Rhode Island publication and broadcasting, including Peoples' ads, with the recognized trade-journal exception. Section 3-8-8's general enforcement authorization is not independently invalidated. Nonprice product promotion remains allowed. Calling the restraint complete refers to the challenged price-information category with these exact exceptions, not a ban on all alcohol-related speech.

**Precedent and remedy.** The independent freeze's bounded treatment of Queensgate is retained: its summary disposition is a merits decision, Mandel limits its precise reach, and no invented factual distinction or categorical permission displaces plenary application of the developed doctrine. No protectionist-pretext finding or new forfeiture theory is added. Each Associate continues to favor reversal of the **October 24, 1994** judgment directing judgment for defendants, with an implementing remand preserving the district court's constitutional declaration against the challenged prohibitions in §§3-8-7, 3-8-8.1 and Regulation 32. The historical decree states reversal; the implementing remand specifies the ordinary consequence of removing the appellate reversal, not a different merits remedy or reopened trial. No adopted ground requires new factfinding. The withdrawn July appellate opinion is not operative. The paid $400 fine and unappealed administrative proceeding remain outside relief; no damages, refund, compulsory tax/price program, new antitrust adjudication, unrelated alcohol-rule invalidation or special mandate date is supplied.
