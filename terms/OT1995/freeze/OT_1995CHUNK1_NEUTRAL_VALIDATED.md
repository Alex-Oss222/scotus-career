### Statute in its material terms

Section 248(a)(1) prohibits intentional injury, intimidation, interference, or attempt, accomplished by force, threat of force, or physical obstruction, either **because** a person is or has been obtaining/providing reproductive health services **or in order to intimidate** that person, another person, or a class from obtaining/providing them. The parallel worship branches exist in the text but are not adjudicated merely because they are neighboring provisions.

The definitions give interference its freedom-of-movement meaning, intimidation its reasonable-apprehension-of-bodily-harm meaning, and obstruction its impassable or unreasonably difficult/hazardous passage meaning. The parent/legal-guardian exception removes **any penalties under the section** for described conduct directed **exclusively at that minor**. **Current rule:** A full and fair state opportunity does not itself bar federal habeas review of a claim that a conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda; Powell's Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction does not extend to that claim. Ordinary habeas gates, merits and harmlessness remain. A court may not grant relief on an independent postwarning involuntariness theory neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent when the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of custody and warnings alone is not consent; later presentation remains subject to pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.
