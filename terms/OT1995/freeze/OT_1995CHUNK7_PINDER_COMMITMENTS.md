## Common legal and record limits

All eight accept the established §1254(1) review channel and a lawful §1291 legal-immunity appeal. Mitchell and the inherited Johnson/Kimberlin rules, applied in Doe and Behrens, permit assessment of the legal consequences of proper favorable assumptions. They do not permit selection of an official's preferred account or review of factual sufficiency alone. No second pretrial appeal is established; Breyer and Stevens's Behrens objection therefore supplies no threshold disposition here.

The district court's express Rule 12(b)(6) treatment and the appellate descriptions of summary judgment remain discrepant. For this legal question the operative account includes the arrest after specific violence and threats, notice of the earlier arson attempt, the overnight assurance in response to Pinder's safety inquiry, her alleged consequent withdrawal of private protection, release that evening, and the absence of a warning. These are assumptions, not findings. A legal notice question is available on that account; the discrepancy does not require dismissal of the entire appeal.

All eight reject a custodial special relationship founded merely on knowledge, a promise of aid or custody of Pittman. DeShaney requires restraint of the injured person's ability to act for its custodial protection rule. The distinct question is whether Johnson's own conduct affirmatively and culpably displaced protection the family otherwise would have retained. DeShaney's no-creation/no-increased-vulnerability explanation matters to that inquiry; it is not a complete affirmative-danger test.

Anderson requires apparent unlawfulness at a sufficiently particular level, without demanding a previously adjudicated identical act. Elder requires all relevant preconduct law, not merely cited or successful plaintiff cases. Bowers, Archie and Jensen must be evaluated with their actual dispositions and limits. Later decisions, including Collins, Doe, Hodge, Love, Sheets and BMW, inform current analysis and each Justice's public commitments but provide no retrospective notice. Neither disagreement among judges nor an official's subjective view of legality independently decides immunity.

The applicable inherited Standards retain their exact scope: **Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties** (Collins, February 26, 1992); **Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review** (Elder, February 23, 1994); and **Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency** and **Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity** (Johnson/Kimberlin, June 12, 1995). No later wording authority changes these entries. Love permits, but does not command, a notice-only disposition. Sheets's affirmative-disclosure holding and BMW's punitive-award rules supply no Pinder-specific duty or remedy.

District footnote 8 leaves the substantive culpability standard unraised and undecided, while permitting favorable pleading inferences of intentional or conscience-shocking conduct. No Justice treats that possibility as a finding, imports an equal-protection allegation as an adjudicated due-process mental state, or requires direct evidence contrary to Kimberlin. District footnote 13 precludes treating more serious charges as an established means of preventing release. No commitment depends on knowing falsity, favoritism, a mandatory detention rule, the disputed commissioner representation, or the disputed probation account.

The fourth path does not impose a general guarantee of private safety. Its necessary current proposition is that custody is not an indispensable condition when an official's own sufficiently culpable conduct actually causes the loss of otherwise available protection. The claimed deprivation must be attributable to that increased exposure, not simply to the pre-existing threat. Intentional or conscience-shocking conduct remains to be properly assessed; the minimum culpability necessary below those forms is not selected. A warning is a possible correction of the alleged induced vulnerability, not a freestanding constitutional warning entitlement.

## Individual commitments

### Stevens

**Judgment:** Vacate the en banc individual due-process immunity disposition and remand for the bounded affirmative-danger claim. Reject the promise-based custodial theory; reject categorical immunity for the sufficiently culpable assurance/reliance mechanism on the favorable pleading account.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** His DeShaney join concerns state failure to protect where the State did not create or increase danger. His Collins opinion expressly leaves a different affirmative-danger claim open. Bowers, Archie, Jensen and DeShaney provided a preconduct distinction between declining protection and affirmatively displacing it. His Anderson dissent and Hunter position reinforce attention to the actual favorable account; they do not replace Anderson's controlling notice rule. The alleged loss of the mother's protection against this identified immediate threat is sufficiently particular without a prior identical assurance case.

**Counterargument and limits:** His Hodge and Doe-Caplinger joins show that a serious injury or broad right alone cannot defeat immunity. Here he finds the affirmative causal mechanism, rather than injury alone, distinguishes those notice deficiencies. His Love Part A supports remand where an affirmative claim was mischaracterized, while reserving its elements. He would not join a general promise duty, automatic culpability inference, release-prevention finding or unconditional trial order. Remand leaves ordinary proof and any immunity question on materially different supported facts available under existing procedure.

### O'Connor

**Judgment:** Affirm Johnson's immunity on the presented individual due-process claim, solely for lack of sufficiently clear conduct-date notice; reserve constitutional sufficiency of the affirmative-danger theory.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** Her DeShaney and Anderson joins, authorship of Hodge, and Doe notice dissent distinguish an established underlying interest from notice of this official's particular obligation. Even accepting induced withdrawal, the earlier cases do not adequately settle whether noncoercive reassurances, followed by release and no correction, amount to the State's constitutional deprivation rather than an inadequately performed undertaking. Bowers rejected liability for release; Archie rejected the advice claim despite its private-aid discussion; Jensen's broader relationship basis was rejected in DeShaney.

**Counterargument and limits:** She accepts that Jensen's distinct affirmative-danger discussion and Archie's rule survive at their proper scope. Their relevance does not make this application apparent, and physical coercion is not declared universally necessary. Her Collins and Sheets joins foreclose converting this notice ground into blanket approval of affirmative injury. She would join a genuine notice-only affirmance, but not broad policy dicta rejecting every noncustodial danger claim or an identical-facts requirement. No fresh evidentiary proceeding is necessary to sustain this bounded immunity ground.

### Scalia

**Judgment:** Affirm on particularized March 1989 notice; do not reach the entire current constitutional claim.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** His Anderson opinion requires both specificity and recognition that analogous law can give notice. The question is not whether bodily security matters or whether officers may directly inflict harm, but whether the alleged assurance-induced substitution of private protection makes subsequent private violence a constitutional deprivation by this officer. DeShaney's rejection of knowledge and undertakings, together with the different mechanisms and rejected claims in Bowers and Archie, leaves that step uncertain. His actual Collins, Elder, Hodge and Doe-dissent joins preserve this distinction.

**Counterargument and limits:** The active-tortfeasor language is stronger than a general due-process maxim and must be considered. It nevertheless does not identify which noncoercive undertakings cross the constitutional line; rejecting its automatic application is not requiring a factual twin. His Albright and Sheets source objections reinforce the need to identify the guarantee, but establish no categorical bar to recognized bodily integrity and supply no March 1989 notice. He would not join a new general danger-creation test, state-tort cure, adverse culpability finding or universal custody condition. The independent notice ground suffices for affirmance.

### Kennedy

**Judgment:** Affirm on historical notice, reserving the distinct affirmative-danger merits.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** He joined DeShaney, Anderson, Collins, Elder, Hodge and O'Connor's Doe notice dissent. Those commitments require separating an officer's own federal deprivation from the underlying attack and from an obligation inferred merely from official involvement. He accepts the alleged loss of private protection for review, but finds that the preconduct danger cases did not clearly resolve when an assurance of another person's continued detention creates this federal responsibility. Their actual coercive, placement or state-facilitation settings differ in constitutionally relevant ways; DeShaney made reliance on the broader undertaking theory especially problematic.

**Counterargument and limits:** Archie supports a claim where government actually cuts off private aid, and DeShaney does not immunize affirmative state injury. Kennedy nevertheless finds the extension to this alleged noncoercive substitution insufficiently apparent in March 1989. His Sheets join confirms that an affirmative act may invade protected liberty today; it does not settle this mechanism or historical notice. His Albright concurrence is no general state-remedy defense to substantive wrongdoing. He joins bounded notice affirmance without endorsing the en banc policy discussion or deciding disputed causation.

### Souter

**Judgment:** Vacate the reviewed immunity disposition and remand on the distinct assurance/reliance theory; reject a custodial relationship based on promises alone.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** His Collins and Elder joins, all three Doe joins, Love Part A join and Sheets join require identification of the official's own substantive contribution. On the favorable account, the claimed contribution is removal of existing private protection, with exposure to the specific immediate threat, not the State's mere failure to provide a benefit. Archie articulates that private-aid distinction; Bowers and Jensen recognize affirmative placement in danger; DeShaney's no-increased-vulnerability limitation preserves its significance. These preconduct principles give adequate notice for the potentially intentional or conscience-shocking form without settling all failures of reassurance.

**Counterargument and limits:** Hodge and Caplinger demonstrate his willingness to affirm on notice where a broadly described interest does not resolve individual responsibility. Here the asserted causal alteration and mortal bodily injury provide the additional substantive deprivation absent from the claim he rejected in Albright. He would not infer notice from the severity of harm alone. He joins no general constitutional contract of protection or universal recklessness rule. The remand must assess the unresolved culpability and actual causal predicates, with no liability or automatic trial determination.

### Thomas

**Judgment:** Affirm immunity on the notice ground and reserve the affirmative-danger merits.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** Thomas did not participate in DeShaney or Anderson; those are governing preconduct law, not invented personal joins. His own Collins, Elder, Hodge and Doe-dissent joins support applying their particularized distinction between a federal guarantee, the defendant's personal contribution, and historical notice. Neither the danger formulations nor a broken official undertaking made sufficiently clear that this noncustodial, private-violence account constituted Johnson's federal deprivation in March 1989. The absence of an established detention counterfactual further prevents treating release facilitation as a demonstrated alternative basis.

**Counterargument and limits:** The assurance-induced loss of protection is assumed, not denied, and could materially distinguish a different affirmative-injury claim. His conclusion concerns the unsettled legal classification of that mechanism, not a demand for identical precedent. His Love II-A and Sheets dissent positions require an independently demonstrated federal wrong; they do not authorize a general rule that state remedies cure one. He would join notice affirmance without deciding every affirmative-danger theory, rejecting all noncustodial liability, or converting an unresolved culpability issue into a plaintiff's evidentiary failure.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment:** Vacate the immunity disposition and remand for proper treatment of the affirmative-danger claim, with no restoration of a mere knowledge-and-promise special relationship.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** Her Elder opinion requires consideration of the full relevant preconduct landscape rather than a demand for an earlier successful assurance plaintiff. The relevant combination is Archie's withdrawal-of-private-aid principle, Bowers and Jensen's affirmative-danger distinction, and DeShaney's express absence of increased vulnerability. Her Doe opinion requires personal, culpable and causally effective conduct; it does not make Pittman's private act official. Here the pleaded official contribution is the induced abandonment of private protection itself. At the potentially intentional or conscience-shocking level permitted by the favorable pleading account, the preconduct rule supplies sufficient notice to reject categorical immunity.

**Counterargument and limits:** Bowers, Archie and Jensen did not award relief on these facts, and Doe concerns abuse through state authority. She treats those differences as limits on attribution and culpability, not a reason to ignore the distinct mechanism altogether. Her Hodge join preserves notice-only adjudication where the actual duty remained insufficiently defined; her Love Part A and Sheets joins support assessment of the affirmative act here. Her Behrens opinion forbids resolving the disputed commissioner's statement, mental state or causation on appeal. Remand orders neither unrestricted discovery nor an automatic trial and preserves unreviewed counts.

### Breyer

**Judgment:** Vacate the reviewed immunity disposition and remand for the bounded affirmative-danger inquiry; retain the legal/factual appellate boundary.

**Sufficient ground and sources:** His Johnson opinion and Behrens conditional joins permit legal assessment of the assumed conduct while reserving historical facts. His Doe, Love Part A and Sheets joins distinguish a personally attributable affirmative contribution from a general obligation to prevent harm. The account alleges an actual substitution for an identified source of protection against an immediate, known threat. Archie supplies the closest preconduct functional distinction, with Bowers, Jensen and DeShaney showing why a sufficiently culpable increase in vulnerability differs from nonprotection. The distinction can give notice without making police guarantors of every assurance.

**Counterargument and limits:** Archie's outcome and its rejection of hindrance on its own facts make ordinary causation or poor advice insufficient. Breyer therefore supports remand for the potentially intentional or conscience-shocking displacement theory, not a rule imposing liability whenever reassurance influences behavior. His Hodge and Caplinger joins preserve particularized immunity for lesser or materially different conduct; they do not require affirmance across every favorable form of this allegation. He would not join a mandatory merits-first sequence, resolve the procedural discrepancy against Pinder, or order trial to settle a question that is legally dispositive. The ordinary lower-court inquiry remains bounded by his Johnson rule.

## Handoff and source limits

The affirmance positions preserve Johnson's individual immunity only on the reviewed due-process claim and require no new merits or damages proceeding. The vacatur positions remove the categorical immunity disposition only for the distinct, sufficiently culpable affirmative-danger theory; they do not conclusively deny every immunity defense, decide the minimum culpability threshold, establish actual reliance or causation, or grant compensation. None reaches official-capacity or municipal liability, equal protection, §1985, state tort counts, or an unpresented procedural claim.
