Three-judge United States District Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division, convened under 28 U.S.C. §2284, *Vera v. Richards*, Civ. A. No. H-94-0277: opinion of August 17, 1994 (861 F. Supp. 1304), order of September 2, 1994, and amended order entered September 20, 1994. *Plaintiffs and allegations:* The six plaintiffs are registered voters residing in District 25 (Chen), District 18 (Blum and Powers), District 29 (Thomas and Vera) and District 30 (Orcutt). They alleged that the 1991 congressional plan (HB 1, Plan C657) was an unconstitutional effort to segregate the races for purposes of voting and challenged 24 of the 30 districts under the Fourteenth Amendment. Each of the five plaintiffs residing in Districts 18, 29 and 30 alleges that the State, relying on race, assigned him or her to the district in which he or she lives and votes. None alleges vote dilution, exclusion from voting or candidacy, unequal participation, discriminatory treatment in casting or counting ballots, or any other injury beyond that assignment; whether the assignment itself supplies Article III injury is the question for decision. Chen, who resides in District 25, alleges no assignment to Districts 18, 29 or 30 and no personal connection to the racial classifications alleged in drawing them. *Standing below:* The State defendants and the Lawson intervenors contested the plaintiffs' standing to challenge districts in which they do not reside. The court held that Blum, Powers, Thomas, Vera and Orcutt have standing to challenge the districts in which they reside, treating each plaintiff's race-based assignment to that district as itself a sufficient personal injury, traceable to the plan and redressable by relief against its use, without any further alleged injury. Because each of Districts 18, 29 and 30 has at least one resident plaintiff, the court rested the declaration and injunction on those residents' standing and did not decide the nonresidence objection; Chen's challenge to his own District 25 failed on the merits. *Merits and relief:* The court held Districts 18, 29 and 30 unconstitutional under the Fourteenth Amendment and rejected the challenges to the other 21 districts; that ruling is stated to identify the relief, and its correctness is outside the reconstructed question. The court declared the three districts unconstitutional; allowed the fall 1994 congressional elections to proceed under Plan C657; directed the Legislature to develop a new congressional plan by March 15, 1995, in conformity with the court's opinion, with a remedial hearing to follow; directed the plaintiffs to apply for attorneys' fees and costs within 30 days; and, by the amended order granting the State's unopposed motion for an explicit injunction, enjoined the state defendants and those acting in concert with them from conducting the 1996 congressional elections, including candidate qualifying, under the Plan C657 districts. All other relief sought in the parties' post-trial submissions on relief was denied; no fee award is supplied. *Independent support:* None. Before trial the plaintiffs dismissed their Voting Rights Act §2, state-constitutional and Fifteenth Amendment claims; the complaint also cited the Fifth Amendment, but the court granted relief only under the Fourteenth Amendment, so the declaration and injunction rest only on the five resident plaintiffs' Fourteenth Amendment racial-districting claims, whose sole asserted personal injury is their assignment. Chen remains a party but supplies no independent injury supporting relief concerning Districts 18, 29 and 30. Testimony about split precincts, voter confusion and misdirected ballots in Harris County, and Blum's 1992 congressional campaign in District 18, was received as evidence of district configuration; no plaintiff relies on it as a personal injury, and the court made no such finding. *Appeals:* The Governor and the other state defendants (No. 94-805), William Lawson et al., private defendant-intervenors (No. 94-806), and the United States as defendant-intervenor (No. 94-988) each appealed under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from the orders granting declaratory and injunctive relief; no plaintiff appeal is part of this matter. No stay pending appeal, legislative or court-drawn remedial plan, or 1996 election administration is supplied; none is inferred.
