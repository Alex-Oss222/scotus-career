## Current law and the amount-review boundary

[TXO, June 25, 1993](../../OT1992/records/TXO_Production_Corp_v_Alliance_Resources_merits_1993-06-25.md), affirmed 5–4. Five directly supported the record-specific adequacy of instructed jury proceedings followed by actual trial and state appellate review; lack of a separate written trial explanation was not itself unconstitutional. No majority established a substantive ratio, potential-harm arithmetic, general substantive-excessiveness test or Scalia's rejection of substantive review. Haslip's governing force remained. The final replacement accurately identifies TXO's absence of a controlling formula. Its further proposed rejection of independent federal magnitude review is Stone's present position, not a prior TXO majority holding.

[Oberg, June 24, 1994](../../OT1993/records/Honda_Motor_Co_v_Oberg_merits_1994-06-24.md#public-projection), requires meaningful judicial examination and lawful correction of the **amount**, or an adequate functional substitute. Oregon's retained instruction/legal-error and no-evidence review, together with its actual trial safeguards, failed because punitive entitlement could exist while an arbitrary amount remained insulated from correction. The exact Standards entry is **“Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts,” present operation effective June 24, 1994**. The current [entering-law C slice](../entering-law/OT_1995CHUNK6_C.md) agrees with the public holding. Oberg prescribes no precise constitutional review standard, permissible ratio, maximum award, mandatory remittitur, automatic new trial, opinion format or required number of reviewing courts; it reserves substantive excessiveness.

The final text expressly preserves meaningful amount review and identifies Alabama's actual review as adequate. Its examples of legal availability, instructions, evidence, impermissible considerations and statutory limits therefore are **not an exhaustive entitlement-only checklist**. State-law or otherwise established scrutiny of an arbitrary or excessive amount remains compatible with its rejection of a separate federal power to select a preferred sum. Reprehensibility, ratios and comparable sanctions may inform that lawful review. Do not omit the amount-review function, claim that any minimally instructed jury verdict necessarily suffices, or declare Oberg overruled. The approved adequacy conclusion concerns Alabama's actual process and correction in this case; it is not a general presumption that any remittitur cures any constitutional defect.

Stone's earlier public TXO inadequate-review dissent and Oberg concurrence do not supply authority to replace the final approved case position. Their concern with actual examination and lawful correction remains compatible with the final text. No prior private theory or upstream source may add a new final branch.
