## Independent evidence framing

The new material supports independent assessment of the offered evidence, rather than adoption of the lower court's ultimate conclusion. Spitz and Arden are not merely criticizing Tillman's credentials. Both list Whitaker's testimony among the materials reviewed. Their later-death conclusions conflict substantively with the State's earlier-death chronology. Arden also challenges the adequacy of observations and samples, the value of the exhumation, and interpretations of alleged sexual injury and binding. These are expert assertions offered by the applicant, not established findings, and they do not automatically establish a separately pleaded sentencing or trial claim.

The scans recover material statements dropped by OCR. Spitz opines to a reasonable degree of medical certainty that the initial three-to-five-day estimate was proper, relying on decomposition, weather and water temperature (printed p.23). Arden states the same interval to a reasonable degree of medical and scientific certainty (p.24). His specific supports include apparent rigor at the scene, limited skin slippage and marbling, the condition of organs, lack of purging, water temperature, lack of aquatic disturbance, and the distinction between photographs at recovery and an autopsy after twenty-four hours out of the water (pp.25–27). The OCR of pp.26–27 interleaves columns; read each physical page separately. His ultimate opinion rejects fourteen days and again gives three to five days (pp.29–30). Do not describe these as only unsupported professional credentials or only a challenge to one witness's qualifications.

The adverse evidence remains material. JA 19–20 quotes Whitaker's own testimony favoring death near disappearance, about fourteen days before discovery, while allowing that three to five days was possible. JA 20 supplies his qualifications. JA 21 recounts the abandoned car, disappearance, call from Felker's home, unchanged clothing and other evidence. The quoted passage supplies evidence independent of Tillman's opinion. The petition's claim that all physician testimony supports innocence, or that only a nonphysician supported the earlier date, cannot be accepted as an uncontested fact. Conversely, the lower opinion's observation that the affidavits do not challenge Whitaker's qualifications does not answer their substantive challenge to the forensic chronology and underlying observations.

JA 24–25 characterizes the new affidavits as cumulative and declares innocence unpersuasive. That is the lower court's assessment, not the neutral validator's finding and not a substitute for the applicable simulated rule. The experts' asserted certainty is also not dispositive. Independent analysis must assess the added evidence against the entire probative record, the source and credibility of the competing observations, and the legal standard for the particular inquiry. Any assertion that the complete trial record lacks an answer to a new expert point requires the full material source review; these bounded extracts do not establish that absence.

The moral-certainty words cannot alone decide validity, and other correct language cannot be presumed automatically curative. No separately unpleaded instruction claim is created from the additional text.
