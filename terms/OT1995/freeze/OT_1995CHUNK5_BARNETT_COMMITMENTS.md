## Common propositions adopted by each Associate

The following are separate commitments, not interchangeable grounds. All eight adopt each affirmative proposition at the stated scope; all eight reserve the Florida-purpose issue identified in item 3. Their individual authority and qualification statements follow.

### 1. Section 92 supplies an affirmative protected agency authorization

Section 92 adds a defined insurance-agency power to the powers of a national banking association meeting its conditions. Its operative permission to act as agent by soliciting and selling insurance and collecting premiums, with agreed compensation and express limitations, establishes an affirmative federal business authorization. A State cannot make that authorization unavailable solely because the agent is affiliated with the national bank exercising it. Florida's challenged affiliation prohibition forbids the very agency activity authorized by §92 for a qualifying association. It therefore conflicts with that grant, even though a bank could avoid simultaneous violations by abandoning the authorized activity.

This conclusion depends on the particular grant and its banking-law setting. It does not follow from the word “may” alone or from a rule that every federal permission overrides every state prohibition. Franklin National Bank treats an affirmative banking power as protected against state obstruction; de la Cuesta establishes that a protected option need not be federally compulsory. Section 92 here expressly adds the activity and defines who may conduct it, unlike mere federal silence or failure to prohibit a preexisting practice. The retained condition that the insurer be state-authorized does not say that the State may negate the national bank's agency capacity because of its affiliation.

Respondents' competitive-equality account does not supply a condition absent from this grant. The branching precedents enforce Congress's incorporation of state restrictions in that distinct scheme. Section 92 contains no corresponding incorporation of a State's decision whether national-bank affiliates may conduct the authorized agency business. Equal treatment of state and national banks is relevant to respondents' account of purpose but does not reconcile an outright prohibition with the enacted national-bank authority. McClellan's recognition of compatible general state rules remains intact.

The operative conflict is thus identified without finding physical impossibility, deriving a banking field immunity, requiring proof that this bank would fail without insurance income, or inferring an unexpressed agency policy. Ordinary indirect costs and compatible regulation remain distinct. Gade's rejection of automatic displacement from overlap and Freightliner's requirement of an actual operative federal basis are both respected: overlap is insufficient, while this case supplies the affirmative authorization missing in Freightliner.

### 2. Section 92 specifically relates to the business of insurance

The federal exception in §1012(b)'s first sentence applies to this authorization. Section 92 directly addresses acting for fire, life and other insurers in soliciting and selling policies, collecting premiums and receiving agency compensation; it also prescribes insurance-related limits on the bank's undertaking. This is a particular relationship to the business of insurance, including bringing insurer and policyholder into the insurance transaction. It is more than an incidental reference to an insurer or a general statute applied to an insurance company.

The section can advance banking purposes and specifically relate to insurance simultaneously. Neither its placement within a broader banking enactment nor the desire to provide bank income removes its express regulation of insurance-agency activity. “Act of Congress” does not require that every provision in the containing enactment concern insurance. The enacted federal exception asks whether the law specifically relates to the business; it does not repeat the distinct state-law requirement of enactment for the purpose of regulating that business.

The exception also does not require an additional express statement that state insurance law is preempted. Fabe's actual holding concerned a federal priority statute concededly lacking the requisite insurance relationship. Its protection of policyholder performance and necessary administration does not replace the exception's language with an express-preemption formula. John Hancock confirms that an enactment addressing another broad subject can also contain operative insurance-specific provisions. Its ERISA holding is instructive, not a disposition of §92 by analogy alone.

Section 92's 1916 enactment does not defeat the exception. The then-prevailing Commerce Clause treatment of insurance did not prevent Congress from conferring powers on its national banks; the exception supplies no post-1944 enactment condition. National Bank of Oregon settles the authorization's survival, while leaving this different preemption inquiry open.

The federal exception removes McCarran-Ferguson's obstacle to ordinary preemption here. It does not itself establish the conflict; item 1 supplies that independently necessary conclusion. The antitrust proviso in §1012(b) and the Sherman Act boycott, coercion and intimidation provision in §1013(b) are different provisions and do no decisional work in this case.

### 3. Florida's insurance-regulatory purpose is assumed, not decided

Each Associate assumes for decision that the challenged Florida prohibition was enacted for the purpose of regulating the business of insurance. No Associate commits to affirming or rejecting that classification as an alternative holding. The insurance-specific federal exception and actual conflict dispose of the asserted McCarran protection even on respondents' strongest state-purpose premise.

Fabe's functional policyholder inquiry remains controlling. The lower courts' account of coercion, resource concentration and insurer solvency is not erased, and the absence of a challenge to bench findings supplies no new finding of bank misconduct. Conversely, the statute's insurance-code placement or state constitutional validity does not conclusively establish the federal classification. Barnett's competition and affiliation arguments remain genuine arguments, not adopted factual conclusions. No least-restrictive-means test, categorical exclusion of prohibitory regulation, presumption of protectionism, or requirement of a particular legislative record is added to McCarran-Ferguson.

### 4. The federal conditions and unresolved cross-appeals limit relief

Section 92 applies to an association “located and doing business” in a place whose population does not exceed 5,000 under the last preceding decennial census. The insurer must be authorized by the State in which the bank is located to do business there; the activity remains subject to the Comptroller's rules. The agency may solicit and sell insurance, collect premiums and receive agreed fees or commissions. The bank may not assume or guarantee payment of a policy premium or guarantee the truth of the insured's application statements. The agency authorization supplies no underwriting or guarantee of the insurer's undertaking.

Belleview's population does not resolve whether this association, headquartered in Ocala with a Belleview branch, satisfies the statutory location requirement. The preserved cross-appeal challenging that issue returns for decision. The separate cross-appeal concerning sales to customers outside the qualifying locality also returns. Neither the statutory-survival decision nor NationsBank's annuity classification resolves either question. No particular geographic answer, deference outcome, renewed evidentiary hearing or agency proceeding is directed.

Any eventual declaration or injunction must correspond to a proved entitlement under §92 and the conflict actually adjudicated. The court of appeals and district court must conform the disposition after resolving the preserved matters; this Court does not grant the requested unconditional final relief now. The judgment does not invalidate all of Florida §626.988 or all insurance regulation, decide independent licensing or conduct requirements, dispense with compatible state law, award damages, or dictate a special mandate date. State rules genuinely compatible with the federal authority remain operative; the validity of a materially different requirement depends on its own terms and operation.

The federal population limit is not the Florida independent-bank exclusion: the latter uses less than 5,000 and also excludes bank-holding-company affiliates from its benefit. Barnett's affiliation defeats that particular state exclusion. Credit life and credit disability are separately excluded from the defined restricted agency activities. The other verified exclusions are not found applicable. Provisions appearing only in the historical proposed Florida bill do not become current operative exceptions or new grounds for Barnett's relief.

## Tested alternatives and why they do not change these commitments

**Affirm for want of ordinary conflict.** Respondents' strongest path treats §92 as removal of a federal corporate-capacity restriction, designed to equalize competition only where state law allows insurance sales. The references to state-authorized insurers, the traditional state insurance role, and Massachusetts Medical Society make that a substantive statutory argument. Pacific Gas & Electric and Exxon also defeat a universal inference from permission to immunity. Its difficulty here is the affirmative addition of a particular national-bank agency power, supported by Franklin, without incorporation of a state veto over the bank's status. The State's insurer-authorization role remains express and effective, but does not convert the grant into permission only where the State also accepts bank affiliation. Each Associate rejects this path on that statutory distinction, not on the bare proposition that federal permission always controls.

**Affirm under McCarran-Ferguson despite ordinary conflict.** This path needs both qualifying state insurance-regulatory purpose and a federal enactment outside the insurance-specific exception. The lower findings and Fabe give the first premise substantial support. Banking purpose, the provision's institutional placement, the pre-1944 constitutional setting and Fabe's clear-statement description supply respondents' strongest argument for the second. But the second cannot overcome the text regulating insurance agency, policy sales and premiums. The exception addresses subject matter, not exclusive institutional identity, and imposes no additional express-preemption or enactment-date condition. All eight reject that second premise while leaving the first undecided. They do not infer that every federal reference to insurance is sufficiently specific.

**Reverse because Florida does not regulate the business of insurance.** Barnett's strongest alternative points to the affiliation prohibition, protection of independent-agent competition, the proposal's stated concerns and existing rules against coercive conduct. Kennedy, Scalia, Souter and Thomas's Fabe dissent illustrates the need for a real policy relationship; Stevens and O'Connor's Fabe joins equally reject remote benefits as sufficient. Against this path stand the lower courts' accepted policyholder-protection account, National Securities' attention to directly and indirectly protected policy relationships, and the possibility that exclusion itself regulates. The availability of narrower conduct rules is not a statutory least-restrictive-means requirement. No Associate finds it necessary to decide this disputed classification when the federal exception resolves the case even assuming it. It remains an unadopted alternative, not a finding that the record could never sustain either answer.

**Reverse and order immediate final nationwide relief.** The ordinary-conflict and McCarran conclusions do not establish that Barnett qualifies through its branch or that §92 permits customers anywhere. Both preserved cross-appeals were expressly left undecided. Each Associate rejects immediate unconditional relief and preserves their determination on remand. Travelers and Dalton require attention to the actual unresolved component and actual conflict; Meghrig supplies no general equitable enlargement beyond the authority in the action. Conversely, the source limits do not require a remand to reconsider the legal preemption premise just resolved or a mandatory new evidentiary proceeding.

**Defer to the Comptroller as an independent route.** NationsBank permits bounded deference in the actual banking delegation there. It does not resolve the meaning of McCarran or establish that every agency preemption view controls. The supplied descriptions of government advocacy do not substitute for a full agency instrument. All eight decide the two statutory questions judicially on the identified law; no administrative-deference holding or rejection of a specific unproduced rule is necessary. Wisconsin's census-specific latitude adds no OCC authority.
