## 2. Channel, questions and available relief

**28 U.S.C. §1254(1):** “Cases in the courts of appeals may be reviewed by the Supreme Court” “[b]y writ of certiorari granted upon the petition of any party to any civil or criminal case, before or after rendition of judgment or decree.” **Section 2101(e)** permits an application for certiorari before appellate judgment “at any time before judgment.” This is not a §1254(2) certification, a direct district-court appeal, or an emergency application. Continuing district-court supervision does not itself defeat this channel.

**1995 Rule 11:** “A petition for a writ of certiorari to review a case pending in a United States court of appeals, before judgment is entered in that court, will be granted only upon a showing that the case is of such imperative public importance as to justify deviation from normal appellate practice and to require immediate determination in this Court. See 28 U. S. C. §2101(e).” Source: official Rules, 515 U.S. 1204, [local text](../sources/chunk4_a/SCOTUS_Rules1995_515US1195_1254.txt), lines 382–390; [official PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov/ctrules/rules/Rules_1995.pdf).

The strongest supported reason for immediate review is a discrete governing-law issue within an ongoing statewide remedy: an improper categorical limitation could affect library remediation as existing expansions proceed. Against that, narrow legal error ordinarily receives intermediate appellate review; litigation age, statewide importance and an ongoing project do not alone establish imperative need for immediate Supreme Court determination. No imminent library-specific deadline, unrecoverable expenditure, or emergency injury has been established. The reconstruction provides the prior-review premise, not a finding that every Justice must accept its exceptional justification. A threshold objection or dismissal of improvidently granted review remains distinct from merits affirmance; no new petition-stage vote is assigned here.

**Questions within review:**

1. Does the published library denial apply the continuing dismantlement duty correctly, including the State's burden and the interaction of inherited mission assignments and library allocations?
2. May necessary repair of an established segregation-caused educational injury be categorically refused because additional white enrollment is not predicted, and does the actual library ruling rest on such a limitation rather than a sufficient independent ground?
3. If an error is identified, what limited correction can follow without making new injury, necessity or expenditure findings?

Section 2106 permits affirmance, modification, vacatur, reversal and appropriate remand only for the order lawfully before the reviewing court and as just under the circumstances. A merits correction must identify **the denial of additional library relief**, not vacate or affirm the statewide decree wholesale. Retained district-court jurisdiction continues under the existing decree. No new hearing, reopened trial, special mandate deadline, monitoring mechanism or expenditure is automatically required.

The actual private class plaintiffs and the United States as plaintiff-intervenor remain distinct.
