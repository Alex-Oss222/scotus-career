## Threshold alternatives validated for independent assessment

**Jurisdictional dismissal of the entire assigned review.** Section 1257 requires a federal question at the proper scope. Cardinale v. Louisiana, 394 U.S. 437, 438–439, and Webb v. Webb, 451 U.S. 493, 495–501, require affirmative establishment from the record of the federal claim presented for review. Webb permits a presumption from a state court's failure to address the federal issue, subject to an affirmative contrary showing; it requires substance rather than inflexible citation formalities. But Webb examined the actual record and state submissions, and Cardinale relied on an admission of nonpresentation. Our unknown research material cannot be converted into a finding that the Court's record lacks the necessary showing or that a litigant failed a burden. Those authorities prevent assumed jurisdiction; they do not independently supply the missing historical premise here. Webb's Powell concurrence also preserves exceptional plain-error review to prevent fundamental unfairness; no supported plain-error predicate is supplied in this adoption vehicle. The current Yee holding leaves the jurisdictional/prudential classification of its particular nonpresentation limit unresolved.

**Independent state ground on the supported federal-minimum argument.** The original majority's positive Illinois statutory and evidentiary basis can support nonreview of a claim that federal law merely permitted Illinois to choose a different adoption policy. Long, the operative Evans holding, Sochor, and Middlebrooks preserve the distinction between an independent ground and federal interweaving. That analysis does not dispose of a properly presented federal attack on the validity of the state rule itself. It therefore cannot, without more, dispose of this assigned adult-right question. The proposed disposition originally frozen on that shortcut remains withdrawn.

**Prudential dismissal of the existing plenary review.** The original petition's stated constitutional-minimum questions and the assigned adults' independent-entitlement question are materially different. The published adoption decision resolves the statutory predicate and contains no analysis of that independent adult right. A Court may reconsider whether this judgment and petition are a suitable vehicle for deciding the broader constitutional question without finding that the claim was never raised below. The question-presentation distinction in the actual Yee, Izumi, and Lebron holdings and the prudential dismissal form recognized by Tacon v. Arizona, 410 U.S. 351, 352, support that available course. Tacon's finding of actual nonpresentation is not imported. Unlike its facts, the possible DIG here rests on the demonstrated mismatch and the published decision's actual decisional scope, not a finding of litigant default.

This prudential ground survives either answer to the missing-presentation question: even if a state submission established preservation, the original petition still asked the different questions described above and the published adoption judgment still did not analyze the assigned independent adult claim. Those facts may justify declining the vehicle; they do not compel dismissal, establish a new jurisdictional rule, or require lower courts to write an opinion on every federal contention. The Court's permission to consider a sufficiently related or exceptional question remains a competing discretionary path. A statutory-ground discussion, if included, explains why deciding the petition's narrower argument would not substitute for deciding the assigned claim; it is not a second disposition of that claim.

**Retain review or obtain clarification.** The strongest alternative is to retain the existing review to identify and assess a supported state presentation of the adults' claim, or to consider whether a properly founded clarification proceeding is justified. Such a path could improve the vehicle and permit an informed jurisdictional determination. It cannot presume presentation, promise a merits outcome, or manufacture an in-world production obligation to remedy an operator's research gap. Existing authority permits reconsidering review; it imposes no obligation to keep an unsuitable vehicle pending indefinitely. No new order or event is entered by stating this alternative.
