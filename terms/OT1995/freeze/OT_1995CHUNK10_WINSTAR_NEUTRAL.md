## Channel, judgment and remedy scope

Three consolidated liability disputes reach this Court through §1254(1) after certified interlocutory Federal Circuit review under §1292. The Court of Federal Claims granted partial summary judgment on contractual liability; the en banc Federal Circuit affirmed, 64 F.3d1531, August30, 1995. Damages remain undetermined. The Tucker Act, §1491(a)(1), confers the relevant contract jurisdiction but creates no promise.

The trial court treated Winstar as implied-in-fact; the en banc court found an **express agreement** from the documents and did not reach implied formation independently. The other two written undertakings are distinct. The question is whether actual authorized promises and applicable defenses establish liability after FIRREA's regulatory changes. It is not whether these institutions generally deserve financial relief.

Affirming liability would leave damages and remaining trial proceedings; it awards no amount and does not suspend FIRREA. Reversing any liability component requires a precise description of the consequence for that institution and any unadjudicated alternatives, including the takings claims not reached below. No takings merits judgment follows by implication, and no injunction disabling Congress is requested or supplied.

## Three transactions and distinct alleged obligations

The annex's document table supplies the actual contractual clauses, resolutions, letters, conditions and contested constructions. The following corrects shorthand in the original derived input:

- **Glendale,1981:** The integrated writings preserve contemporaneous Board/FSLIC documents. The accountant's treatment has **$18 million over twelve years** and **$716.666 million over forty years**, straight line; do not describe all goodwill as forty-year goodwill. The resolution requires a satisfactory accountant's opinion and R-31b compliance. The Government disputes incorporation, any future-law risk promise, and the effect of unlawful-action and ten-year termination terms; the lower court rejected those constructions.
- **Winstar,1984:** The letter permits purchase-accounting intangibles to be amortized straight line over **not more than thirty-five years** for regulatory reporting and distinguishes those reports from GAAP financial statements. The issue is the meaning of the combined documents, including the Net Worth Maintenance Stipulation and unlawful-action/termination language. Permission is not a duty to take the maximum period; reporting approval is not automatically an unqualified promise to freeze every capital requirement.
- **Statesman,1988:** The writings permit no more than twenty-five years for goodwill, subject to an accountant analysis within ninety days. The separate **$26 million capital credit** combines $21 million of the FSLIC contribution and $5 million debenture principal; it is not the approximately $25.8 million acquisition goodwill. The explicit regulatory-capital clause exists. Its forward operation, regulatory-reference and five-year-term effects remain disputed.

The different documents cannot be collapsed into one generic promise. Ordinary formation, integration, actual authority, promised performance, risk allocation, later impediment and defenses each require a separate legal conclusion. A regulated accounting practice can be ordinary permission; an actual bargain can incorporate an accounting commitment. Neither proposition proves what each writing means here.

FIRREA sets capital rules, including limits on supervisory goodwill. The lower opinion separately attributes treatment of capital credits as supervisory goodwill to OTS's §567.1(w) regulation; do not attribute that exact administrative treatment to statutory words Congress did not use. Compliance with later law, breach of an earlier promise and excusal from liability are distinct.

## Strong positions and independently supported alternatives

**Government:** The writings authorize then-permissible accounting rather than guarantee regulatory treatment against future legislation. Compliance, regulatory-reference, unlawful-action, net-worth-maintenance and termination clauses can confine any undertaking. General authority to contract or assist mergers is different from delegated power to bind future sovereign regulation. If the undertaking is protected at all, the Government invokes unmistakability and reserved powers; alternatively public and general legislation and ordinary impossibility can discharge liability. The existence of a major rescue program is not itself a finding that every asserted future-law risk was assumed.

**Institutions:** Integrated writings and transaction-specific accounting/capital terms induced assumption of failing institutions' liabilities and constituted an authorized bargained exchange. Promising financial consequences of a regulatory change is different from preventing Congress from legislating. Genuine contractual risk allocation may make ordinary impossibility unavailable, and the operative impairment may not be a public-and-general sovereign act for discharge purposes. The lower express-contract judgments and actual authorization documents cannot be displaced merely by describing all agency action as regulation.

Both sides must confront Hercules: fairness alone supplies no agreement, but uncertain future damages and sovereign regulatory context do not invalidate every authorized warranty. Their disagreements are legal and transaction-specific; no missing delegation, inevitable bankruptcy, complete damages measure or motive of Congress is newly found.

## Channel and judgment scope

No.95-865 concerns three consolidated interlocutory appeals. The Court of Federal Claims granted **partial summary judgment on contractual liability**, with damages still undetermined. The Federal Circuit accepted certified interlocutory review under §1292 and affirmed en banc. Supreme Court review is under §1254(1); the absence of a final damages award does not itself defeat that channel. The Court of Federal Claims' contract jurisdiction rests on §1491(a)(1); that statute does not create the substantive promise. The alternative takings claims were not reached below. Upholding contractual liability would leave damages and remaining proceedings to the trial court; it would not itself award a stated amount, invalidate FIRREA, or issue an injunction against Congress. An adverse contract ruling would require attention to the unadjudicated alternatives and actual scope of remand.

The lower court found an implied-in-fact agreement in Winstar and express agreements in the others. The **en banc court held Winstar also had an express agreement** on the documents and therefore did not reach implied-in-fact formation as an independent basis. Do not describe the en banc judgment as finding only an implied Winstar promise.

## Authority and risk allocation

The agencies did not purport to prevent Congress from enacting laws. Whether an authorized agreement shifts the **financial consequences** of later change is separate from whether Congress can legislate and the regulators must apply FIRREA. A demand for damages still requires a proved promise and authorized assent; calling a remedy damages cannot alone establish either.

The relevant statutory sources include former **12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(3)**, authorizing FSLIC “to make contracts,” and the transaction-effective **§1729(f)** assistance powers. Glendale's filed brief reproduces the 1976 contract authority and then-effective §1729(f) text at appendix A1–A2, including assistance to prevent default, merger assistance and guarantees against merger loss, and the liquidation-cost limit with the essential-community-service exception. It identifies the pertinent amendment as the **1978** Financial Institutions Regulatory and Interest Rate Control Act, notwithstanding its citation to the 1982 Code Supplement. Winstar/Statesman's brief cites the 1988 §1729(f)(2)(A),(3) assistance authority and §1725(d). The Board resolutions independently record the transaction-specific statutory determinations and approval. These are actual authority sources; no missing-delegation finding is made.

The Government's merits brief, pp.36–43, argues that general contracting/regulatory power does not specifically delegate a power to bind future legislative regulation; it invokes *Home Telephone*, *Stone v. Mississippi*, *Merrill*, and *OPM v. Richmond*. Respondents answer that the authorized undertaking exchanges merger assistance/accounting benefits for assumption of liabilities and carries damages when the Government changes the promised treatment; they do not require an agency delegation to bar Congress from legislating. These competing descriptions must be tested against the actual contracts and *Hercules*, rather than assuming that the word “contract” settles future-law risk or that regulation erases every negotiated obligation.

**FIRREA and regulation:** 12 U.S.C. §1464(t) imposes uniformly applicable capital standards and constrains supervisory goodwill. The lower opinion distinguishes tangible capital (no goodwill), phaseout of goodwill from core capital, and limited qualifying-goodwill amortization for risk-based capital. It separately attributes the treatment of **capital credits** as qualifying supervisory goodwill to **OTS regulation, 12 C.F.R. §567.1(w)**; FIRREA did not expressly use “capital credits” to impose that exact treatment. Do not attribute the agency's treatment verbatim to Congress. The Government defends the reforms as public and general; respondents contend that the particular impairment bears directly on the obligations undertaken and that assumed risk defeats impossibility. Neither statutory validity nor regulatory compliance is equivalent to contractual discharge.
