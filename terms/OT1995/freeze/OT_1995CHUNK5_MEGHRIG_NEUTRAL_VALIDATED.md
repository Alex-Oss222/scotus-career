## 1. Event, questions and reachability

The authorized event is argued merits review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Ninth Circuit's March 1, 1995 judgment in No. 92-56597, reported at 49 F.3d 518. The supplied argument date is January 10, 1996. The petition, filed July 13, 1995, identifies denial of Ninth Circuit rehearing on April 14, 1995. This lower-court chronology is source-verified; no historical Supreme Court petition disposition is imported.

The petition actually presents two questions, rather than the runtime packet's single reconstructed formulation:

1. Whether §6972(a)(1)(B), authorizing district courts to restrain contributors or order them to take other necessary action, authorizes a private suit for reimbursement of cleanup costs.
2. Whether its requirement of waste that “may present an imminent and substantial endangerment” permits a suit concerning contamination fully remediated years before commencement.

These are faithful formulations of petition p. i, not a claim to quote every word. They are the two submitted issues addressed in the merits briefs. No additional granted issue or historical grant limitation is assumed. One question can supply a sufficient statutory ground without deciding the other; petitioners urge resolution of both, while the United States expressly urges disposition on the timing prerequisite with the general restitution question left open.

The March 18 public roster supplies Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer at both argument and decision. No Meghrig-specific nonparticipation is established. Nonparticipation in another case does not transfer here. ## 2. Record, pleading posture and procedural qualifications

**This is review of a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal, not a trial finding or federal summary judgment.** The Ninth Circuit reviewed de novo, limited review to the complaint, accepted material factual allegations, and construed them favorably to KFC. Its factual account and the parties' competing narratives must retain that status.

- KFC alleges that it bought the property at 340 North Western Avenue, Los Angeles, from Alan and Margaret Meghrig in 1975 and operates a restaurant there. The pleadings use both September and October 22 in describing the conveyance; the precise transaction day does not perform legal work in either submitted question.
- During construction work in October 1988, KFC discovered petroleum-contaminated soil. The complaint describes the City's corrective notice stopping construction pending soil analysis and health-department clearance, a consultant's investigation, and excavation/off-site disposal and recycling in conjunction with the health department. The court below described government-required cleanup. The underlying agency order, consultant report and clearance document are not independently reproduced in the reviewed packet.
- KFC alleges that defendants contributed to past handling, storage, treatment or disposal; that the soil contained “solid waste”; that defendants negligently managed petroleum operations; and that KFC purchased without knowing of the contamination. Contribution, knowledge, waste classification, actual risk and responsibility were not adjudicated in this federal pleading appeal.
- Cleanup was completed in 1989, before the May 29, 1992 original federal complaint. The amended complaint alleges that contamination **“may have presented”** an imminent and substantial endangerment, including threatened groundwater and potential risk to patrons/public users, at the earlier stage. It alleges no qualifying endangerment remaining at filing.
- The amended complaint seeks equitable restitution of at least **$211,296.23**, other costs associated with off-site disposal, attorney fees, and appropriate further relief. Its allegations that off-site disposal expenses continued do not allege a continuing endangerment or make this a suit commenced while endangerment existed. Neither a cleanup-cost award nor the asserted amount's reasonableness is established.
- KFC requested reimbursement in June 1990. The amended complaint alleges an October 1, 1990 notice to EPA, with copies to defendants and California's health department; nonresponse and no known government action/costs of the described kind; and service on the Attorney General. JA docket entries also identify service on EPA and the Attorney General. These are pleaded procedural facts, not independent proof of every notice requirement.

**Federal proceedings.** The September 21, 1992 order dismissed with ten days' leave to amend. It addressed both the proposed remedy and absence of current danger and also noted that the original complaint did not allege the required notice. The October 1 amended complaint added the notice allegations and equitable-restitution terminology. The final district order again rejected both statutory theories and denied further leave to amend. The Ninth Circuit reversed and remanded for further proceedings; it did not order payment or find the Meghrigs liable. Its footnote expressly says notice compliance is not an issue on appeal. The United States' amicus footnote referring to the earlier notice omission does not silently revive a decided or preserved notice defect in the amended complaint.

**Precise source discrepancy.** Petition Appendix A21–A23 reproduces the signed final district order dated November 9, 1992, expressly dismissing the action **with prejudice** and directing final judgment. The JA docket lists November 10 entries for dismissal without leave and an order labeled **without prejudice**. Use the actual reproduced order for its stated terms, identify November 10 as the docket-entry date, and preserve the discrepancy. It does not alter the demonstrated existence of an appealable dismissal and the Ninth Circuit reversal; no new remedial distinction should be based solely on the contrary docket label.

**Later state proceedings are distinct.** JA pp.23–28 reproduce an August 21, 1995 California summary-judgment order, also appended to petitioners' merits brief. It states, among other things, no showing that the Meghrigs operated the station, contributed to contamination, or knew of it; earlier service-station activity; removal of tanks in 1964; cleanup by March 12, 1989; and failures of proof and limitations problems in the state nuisance/trespass claims. Petitioners rely on those findings to contest the portrayal of proven polluters. Those findings do not become federal Rule 12 findings merely by appearing in the JA. This packet makes no finding of their preclusive effect, state finality, satisfaction of every preclusion element or admissibility on this review. State-law relief is therefore neither guaranteed nor categorically nonexistent.

**Voluntary-dismissal motion.** The JA docket shows KFC's September 1995 request to dismiss without prejudice, petitioners' opposition/conditional fee or stay request, and the district court's October 10 action taking the motion off calendar pending this review. It does not show an actual dismissal or settlement. No mootness or automatic vacatur follows from filing that motion. A disposition based instead on a later settlement, final state preclusion or completed voluntary dismissal would require the missing operative event and legally sufficient supporting record.

## 3. Statutory text and complete material qualifications

The date-eligible full §6972 reproductions are petition Appendix A27–A32 and respondent's statutory appendix. Both were checked against the complete [official 1994 Code §6972 extraction](../sources/chunk5_a/meghrig_42usc6972_1994_official.txt), including its amendment notes. They agree on the material provisions below, including the absence of an additional filed-earlier/120-day/90-day override. There is no conflict between these three sources on that point. Briefs' compressed descriptions of automatic government priority are not substitutes for these actual conditions.

**Section 6972(a), governing opening and paragraph (1)(B):**

> Except as provided in subsection (b) or (c) of this section, any person may commence a civil action on his own behalf—
>
> … (B) against any person, including the United States, and any other governmental instrumentality or agency, to the extent permitted by the eleventh amendment to the Constitution, and including any past or present generator, past or present transporter, or past or present owner or operator of a treatment, storage, or disposal facility, who has contributed or who is contributing to the past or present handling, storage, treatment, transportation, or disposal of any solid or hazardous waste which may present an imminent and substantial endangerment to health or the environment …

Paragraph (1)(A) separately authorizes actions against a person alleged to be in violation of an effective RCRA permit, standard, regulation, condition, requirement, prohibition or order, including the United States and other governmental entities within the stated Eleventh Amendment limit. Paragraph (2) separately reaches an Administrator's failure to perform a nondiscretionary RCRA act or duty. KFC's asserted federal cause is paragraph (1)(B), not either neighboring route.

**Venue and remedial clause:** Paragraph (a)(1) actions belong in the district where the alleged violation occurred or endangerment may occur. Paragraph (a)(2) actions may be brought where the violation occurred or in the District of Columbia. The district court has jurisdiction without regard to amount in controversy or citizenship:

> to enforce the permit, standard, regulation, condition, requirement, prohibition, or order, referred to in paragraph (1)(A), to restrain any person who has contributed or who is contributing to the past or present handling, storage, treatment, transportation, or disposal of any solid or hazardous waste referred to in paragraph (1)(B), to order such person to take such other action as may be necessary, or both, or to order the Administrator to perform the act or duty referred to in paragraph (2), as the case may be, and to apply any appropriate civil penalties under section 6928(a) and (g) of this title.

The words direct an order **to such person to take action**; they do not simply grant the court power to take any action it considers desirable. Whether an order to reimburse can itself be necessary equitable action is the contested construction, not settled by this grammatical observation.

**Paragraph (b)(2), the actual conditions for a (1)(B) suit:**

- **(A), notice:** Ordinarily no commencement until ninety days after notice of the endangerment to the Administrator, the State where it may occur, and each person alleged to have contributed or to be contributing to the listed waste activities. An action respecting a violation of **subchapter III** may commence **immediately after notification**. That is an exception to waiting, not an exception to notification or an automatic rule for every allegation of solid waste. No qualifying subchapter-III violation is established here.
- **(B), federal action:** The bar applies if the Administrator acts **in order to restrain or abate acts or conditions that may have contributed or are contributing to activities that may present the alleged endangerment**, and (i) has commenced and is diligently prosecuting an action under §6973 or CERCLA §106; (ii) is actually engaging in CERCLA §104 removal; (iii) has incurred costs to initiate a §104 remedial investigation and feasibility study **and is diligently proceeding with a CERCLA remedial action**; or (iv) has obtained a court order, including a consent decree, or issued an administrative order under CERCLA §106 or §6973 pursuant to which a responsible party is diligently conducting removal, an investigation/feasibility study, or proceeding with remedial action. For the administrative order in (iv), prohibition extends **only to its scope and duration**. Mere government awareness, an unspecified expenditure or any local cleanup direction does not establish these conditions.
- **(C), state action:** With the same specified restraint/abatement purpose, the State must (i) have commenced and be diligently prosecuting a (1)(B) action; (ii) actually be engaging in CERCLA §104 removal; or (iii) have incurred costs to initiate a §104 investigation/feasibility study **and be diligently proceeding with CERCLA remedial action**. The reproduced date-eligible text contains no additional filed-earlier/120-day/90-day override; none is imported from a different edition.
- **(D), siting/permit limit:** A person **other than a State or local government** cannot commence a (1)(B) action respecting siting of a hazardous-waste treatment, storage or disposal facility, or to restrain/enjoin issuance of its permit. No such claim is presented here.
- **(E), intervention:** A person may intervene as of right in a federal (1)(B) action upon claiming an interest relating to its subject and being situated so disposition may practically impair or impede protection of that interest, **unless the Administrator or State shows adequate representation by existing parties**.
- **(F), service:** A (1)(B) plaintiff must serve the complaint on the Attorney General and the Administrator.

**Other distinct qualifications retained:** Subsection (b)(1) governs paragraph (1)(A), with sixty days' notice to EPA, State and alleged violator, the same immediate-after-notification subchapter-III exception, and a bar when EPA or the State has commenced and diligently prosecutes a civil or criminal court action to require compliance; any person may intervene as of right in a federal (1)(A) action. Subsection (c) governs paragraph (a)(2), ordinarily sixty days' notice to EPA, again with the subchapter-III immediate-after-notification exception, prescribed notice methods, and its stated district limitation for alleged violations. These different provisions must not be collapsed into the ninety-day endangerment route.

Subsection (d) permits EPA intervention as of right when it is not already a party. Subsection (e) **permits**, when appropriate in a final order, litigation costs including reasonable attorney and expert-witness fees to a prevailing or substantially prevailing party; it does not require an award. A court **may** require a bond/equivalent security under the Federal Rules when temporary or preliminary relief is sought. Subsection (f) preserves any person's existing statutory or common-law right to enforce waste-management standards/requirements or seek other relief, including relief against EPA or a state agency. It neither itself creates every claimed federal cause nor guarantees a successful state remedy. Subsection (g) protects a rail common-carrier transporter from being deemed a contributor to handling/storage/treatment/disposal after waste leaves its possession/control **only if** transportation arose from a sole contractual arrangement under a published tariff and acceptance for carriage and the transporter exercised due care in the listed past/present waste activities. That exception is not an exception for former landowners generally.

**Section 6973 and its separate qualifications.** The complete [official 1994 Code §6973 extraction](../sources/chunk5_a/meghrig_42usc6973_1994_official.txt), including notes, was independently read. Subsection (a), notwithstanding other RCRA provisions, permits EPA upon receipt of evidence of the prescribed imminent/substantial endangerment to sue on behalf of the United States against contributors, including the listed past/present generators, transporters and facility owners/operators, for restraint or other necessary action or both. It contains the same qualified published-tariff, sole-arrangement, due-care rail-transporter protection for activities after possession/control ends. EPA **shall** notify the affected State of such a suit. EPA **may**, **after notice to the affected State**, separately take other action, including orders necessary to protect public health and environment; private plaintiffs lack that administrative power. Subsection (b) provides a fine of no more than $5,000 per day, in a district-court enforcement action, for willful violation or failure/refusal to comply with an (a) order; this supplies no private cleanup-cost award. Subsection (c) requires immediate notice to appropriate local agencies upon information of hazardous waste at a site that **“has presented”** imminent/substantial endangerment, and requires prompt posted notice at the site. That past-tense local/public-warning duty is distinct from (a)'s State notice, the private ninety-day mechanism, and the disputed private cause/remedy. Subsection (d) requires notice, opportunity for a public meeting in the affected area and reasonable comment before final entry of a proposed covenant not to sue, forbearance or settlement. The decision to enter or not enter that decree/covenant/agreement is not final agency action reviewable under RCRA or APA chapter 7. Neither identical remedial wording nor differing enforcement structures alone decides governmental reimbursement authority; that issue is not presented here.

**CERCLA comparison.** The parties agree CERCLA separately provides a cost-recovery regime, including private necessary response costs consistent with the National Contingency Plan, contribution rules and limitations; their dispute concerns the inference to draw from that regime. CERCLA's petroleum exclusion is not a RCRA petroleum exception. The case proceeds on the pleaded petroleum contamination and the absence of a pleaded CERCLA cause; it determines neither every petroleum mixture's classification nor whether contaminants in a different case fall outside that exclusion.

## 5. Lower-court reasoning and strongest positions

**Ninth Circuit majority.** The court treated §6972(a)(1)(B) and §6973's similar wording/liability standards as supporting similar relief, relying on Aceto and NEPACCO for government restitution and on Price's abatement/reimbursement discussion. It construed endangerment as a limit on the kind of site rather than the filing time; read other necessary action to include restitution; found post-cleanup notice still useful to EPA/States; and treated laches as protection against prejudicial delay. It distinguished cases under the earlier ongoing-violation route and emphasized prompt government-directed cleanup by innocent owners, the petroleum exclusion, and possible inadequacy of state remedies. Its legislative-history footnote acknowledged contrary language describing the citizen right as limited and directed toward abatement. Its account of innocence/liability is pleading-based.

**Brunetti dissent.** Past contributing conduct and current endangerment are different statutory concepts. “May present” does not say “may have presented”; the relief language supplies abatement, not reimbursement after removal of the threat. Notice, specified government priority, lack of a RCRA limitations period, and CERCLA's express cost provisions support that construction. Aceto assumed the remedy rather than resolving its availability, and Price involved a present threat with reimbursement discussion not necessarily under RCRA. The saving clause preserves other remedies instead of creating this one. Urgent-cleanup policy does not supply missing statutory authority; the record does not establish inability to sue before completion. The dissent accepts the textual similarity between governmental and citizen provisions while disputing the inference drawn from the cited cases.

**Petitioners.** Either of two grounds defeats the particular RCRA claim: completed cleanup leaves no filing-time endangerment, or the enacted remedy does not cover the requested payment. “Restrain” and other necessary action cover prohibitory and mandatory injunctions, including affirmative cleanup. They concede that past conduct can create actionable **current** danger. Even if language is ambiguous, they urge this natural reading, the notice/government-priority scheme, specific fee authorization, and Congress's deliberate separate CERCLA cost regime. In the alternative, treating the demand as an implied cause/remedy fails under Cort/Sea Clammers; they acknowledge the Ninth Circuit itself relied on express remedial wording. In reply they distinguish disgorgement of a defendant's gain from compensation measured by a plaintiff's loss and stress that not every restitution claim is equitable. They seek reversal, vacatur of the appellate opinion, and dismissal. They urge deciding both questions; their factual reliance on the later state judgment remains subject to the posture limitations above.

**KFC.** RCRA expressly creates the cause, so the question is the scope of its equitable remedy, with Franklin/Porter/Hecht presumptions and “other action” supporting restitution. KFC **concedes the relevant statutory language precludes money damages at law**; it characterizes the requested payment as undoing unjust enrichment from discharging defendants' cleanup duty, not lost profits, diminished earning power or punitive damages. It acknowledges overlapping historical legal/equitable restitution categories. Its timing construction treats “may present” as identifying the dangerous waste category, reinforced by repeated past/present language; an alternative characterization is that cleanup changes necessary relief without extinguishing already incurred responsibility. Prompt abatement should not destroy a remedy. Notice can still encourage settlement and inform governmental inventories, though KFC acknowledges the principal abatement/intervention purpose. Laches requires prejudice, not time alone; courts can assess reasonable costs with experts and useful CERCLA guidance without wholesale incorporation. CERCLA and state saving provisions support coexistence, not displacement. KFC acknowledges Furrer's description of Aceto/NEPACCO jurisdiction as assumed sub silentio. It seeks affirmance and remand to determine whether restitution is justified on the actual circumstances, not an immediate award.

**United States.** Its November 1995 amicus supports the petitioners' requested judgment **on timing**, while disagreeing with categorical exclusion of restitution in a properly commenced action. A citizen must allege a current threat of substantial danger and satisfy the other statutory conditions. Imminence concerns a presently threatened harm, not necessarily immediate physical injury; old conduct and delayed consequences can qualify. Partial emergency abatement does not defeat an action while a substantial threat remains. Complete prefiling elimination does. If a proper citizen action exists, traditional equitable powers may permit reimbursement, including emergency expenses incurred before filing while the longer-term danger remains; the Court need not determine that hypothetical remedy here.

The government's proposed restitution is expressly conditional: Restatement §115 requires unofficious performance of another's public duty with intent to charge, providing things/services immediately necessary for public decency, health or safety. Ordinarily the dutyholder must first be requested to perform, unless request would be useless because of refusal or time prevents it. Section 106 ordinarily denies restitution for a benefit merely incidental to performing one's own duty or protecting/improving one's own property; KFC's own legal cleanup obligation could therefore matter if that remedial stage were reached. Recovery requires factual examination and equitable discretion, generally reasonable abatement cost rather than every expenditure or future cost. These are government arguments for a possible remedy, not newly adopted RCRA prerequisites or findings about this record. The government expressly leaves §6973's reach outside this case. Its litigation submission is not itself a legislative rule or binding administrative adjudication.

## 6. Materially supported alternative paths

1. **Filing-time prerequisite alone.** Construe “may present” to require a current threat when suit begins. On the amended complaint's complete prefiling cleanup, the particular RCRA action fails; a disposition on that sufficient statutory ground can reserve restitution in a properly instituted action and the government's separate powers. The strongest contrary argument is KFC's past-contributor language and prompt-cleanup/equity reading. Any answer must distinguish past conduct from present threat and must not equate absence of current actual injury with absence of endangerment.
2. **Remedy alone, or an independent additional remedy ground.** Construe the enacted necessary-action remedy as unavailable for this freestanding completed-cleanup reimbursement demand, whether characterized as compensation or restitution. The strongest contrary argument is the traditional equity presumption, the asserted discharge of another's duty, and the express necessary-action language. A ruling must specify whether it reaches all private monetary restitution, only this demand, or a suit begun after complete abatement; those are different holdings. Petitioners request both grounds, but judicial economy does not make both obligatory.
3. **Both timing and remedial construction favor continuation.** Accept the historical-danger/category reading and an equitable-restitution power. The appellate remand could then stand for adjudication of contribution, qualifying waste/endangerment at the legally relevant time, actual equitable character and defenses, eligibility and amount. The strongest contrary argument is the present-tense statutory structure and the separate CERCLA regime. This path cannot order the pleaded amount or treat allegations as proved, and it must answer why ordinary cause/remedy limits do not foreclose the action.
4. **A narrower remedial distinction.** A distinction between damages at law and genuinely restitutionary relief, or between a properly instituted ongoing-danger action and complete prefiling abatement, is supported by KFC's concession, Mertens's limited rule and the government's position. Such a distinction may leave important remedial issues unresolved even if it supplies a rule adequate for this complaint. Recognition of a possible future remedy does not cure this case's failure of an independently adopted filing prerequisite.
5. **A genuinely established threshold development.** An actual later dismissal, settlement or legally preclusive state judgment could change reachability. The reviewed record establishes no sufficient such alternative. Do not resolve this merits appeal on one without the operative source, preservation and legally applicable predicates. Lack of present endangerment is not automatically an Article III ruling; statutory coverage, jurisdictional characterization, cause and relief must remain distinct.

None of these alternatives determines state/common-law recovery, EPA §6973 restitution, an award of RCRA fees, costs incurred during a properly pending abatement suit, every petroleum mixture, or every form of prospective relief. No special mandate date, trial, discovery entitlement or immediate monetary award is supplied by the predecision record.
