## Exact common judgment and rationale commitments

**Judgment commitment of each of the eight Associates:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains striking Brian Degen's civil-forfeiture claims and defenses on the asserted fugitive-disentitlement ground, and remand for further proceedings consistent with the limited authority holding. The resulting forfeiture disposition cannot stand against Brian to the extent it rests on that defense bar. The remand restores his opportunity to contest forfeiture through lawful procedure; it does not adjudicate any defense or disturb an independently sufficient ground not resolved by this review. Karyn's separate judgment and Brian's criminal proceedings remain undisturbed.

The eight independently support and can join **all four common propositions below at the stated level of generality**. They form a common rationale coalition as well as a judgment coalition; no aggregation of inconsistent grounds or narrower-opinion synthesis is needed. Their authorities and limiting concerns differ as recorded below. No Associate requires a separate writing on these commitments.

### P1. This inherent-power defense bar exceeds the justified authority

A federal court's inherent authority protects the adjudication and integrity of its proceedings; its existence does not establish the permissible scope of a particular sanction. Where the court controls the forfeiture property and can enforce the ultimate judgment, the claimant's refusal to return for a related criminal prosecution does not alone justify extinguishing his timely civil-forfeiture defenses. The asserted sanction must have an adequate connection to a legitimate need of the proceeding in which it operates. This record does not establish the necessity for this wholesale bar.

Chambers preserves restrained inherent litigation-sanction authority; it does not make every form of disobedience a sufficient basis for every sanction. Ortega-Rodriguez requires a sufficient appellate connection in its own preappeal-flight setting. Its attention to the relationship between misconduct, proceeding and sanction is persuasive here, while its specific appeal rule is not transferred wholesale to civil forfeiture. Hovey, Hammond and Societe distinguish punitive exclusion from an appropriately grounded litigation sanction or evidentiary consequence. Those distinctions constrain the asserted inherent power without requiring a new constitutional holding.

### P2. The Government's asserted interests do not establish this sanction's fit

The court's control of the res removes the claimed need to secure enforceability by forcing Brian's physical return. He has appeared through counsel, filed verified claims and answers, and contested the allegations. His refusal to face criminal charges supplies no determination of drug guilt, forfeiture probable cause, ownership, or the validity of his property defenses.

Related criminal and civil litigation can create genuine discovery difficulties. A claimant must not use civil procedure improperly to obtain protected criminal information, and a court need not tolerate actual obstruction of the forfeiture case. But a risk of misuse is not an established discovery violation or a demonstrated inability to adjudicate. Deterrence, the dignity of the criminal process and the undesirability of selective submission to judicial authority do not, without a sufficient need in the forfeiture proceeding, justify complete exclusion from defending the property. The distinction between denying affirmative appellate assistance and depriving a claimant of his defense to a pending property deprivation also prevents automatic transplantation of criminal-appeal disentitlement.

This is not a holding that every claim of overseas absence must be disregarded, that no serious litigation impairment could support a substantial sanction, or that inherent power exists only where a statute expressly creates it. It rejects the particular power and sanction justified by the grounds established here.

### P3. Available management powers remain conditional and separate

The court may address an actual litigation problem through applicable rules and other lawful authority. Under the received 1994 version of **21 U.S.C. §881(i)**, a qualifying indictment or information concerning a related civil forfeiture **shall, upon motion of the United States and for good cause shown, stay the civil forfeiture proceeding**. A qualifying charge, the required relationship, a Government motion and good cause remain distinct predicates. Neither this decision nor the existence of a related indictment establishes that a stay was requested, that good cause has been shown, or that a stay is adequate for every indefinite absence. No stay is ordered.

Protective orders and Rule 37 sanctions likewise require their actual legal predicates, notice and process, and a scope justified by the established problem. An appropriately supported later sanction, including a sanction affecting a claim or defense when the applicable authority permits it, remains possible. No future discovery violation is presumed and no new discovery command is issued. These tools demonstrate the distinction between managing an established litigation need and imposing this categorical exclusion; the decision imposes no universal exhaustion-of-lesser-sanctions or least-restrictive-means requirement.

Carlisle preserves inherent authority while refusing to use it to deliver relief that governing Rules foreclose. Degen involves no identical Rule 29 prohibition; Carlisle therefore supplies an insistence on the actual source, function and limits, not a dispositive textual conflict. Quackenbush similarly requires authority for the remedy actually used and does not validate it merely because a different, conditional remedy could be lawful. Its Burford damages-surrender limit does not itself decide a defense sanction in a retained forfeiture action. Kokkonen's source-and-function analysis is relevant without suggesting that jurisdiction over this pending action is absent.

### P4. The remedy is an opportunity to defend, within existing forfeiture law

The lower courts must allow Brian's claims and defenses to receive lawful consideration without the rejected categorical bar. Section 881(a)(6) and (7), their distinct innocent-owner qualifications, §881(d)'s qualified incorporation of customs procedure, and §1615's antecedent Government probable-cause showing followed by the claimant's burden retain their operation. The Court determines none of those merits and creates no new burden, factual presumption, hearing format or entitlement to win.

No immediate return of property or proceeds, damages, compensation, criminal dismissal, release from custody, or complete new forfeiture trial is ordered. Any eventual return requires a legally sufficient entitlement and the applicable return or payment authority; Republic National Bank does not turn this procedural success into an immediate money judgment. Good's real-property process rule remains controlling within its actual conditions, but its pre-seizure exigency analysis is not converted into a universal sanctions formula. Karyn's failure to oppose her own motion supplies neither Brian's misconduct nor a reason to reopen her separate judgment.

## Enhanced comparison of supported opposed paths

### A. Sustain discretionary disentitlement

**Supported case for the path:** Chambers recognizes inherent litigation powers not exhausted by the Rules. Closely related forfeiture and criminal proceedings can permit civil discovery to undermine criminal restrictions; requiring submission can protect administration, deterrence and respect for judicial process. The Government need not accept an indefinite ineffective stay merely because §881(i) exists. Ortega did not establish a universal least-restrictive sanction requirement or decide this civil setting.

**Controlling objection on this record:** These propositions support attention to an actual litigation need, not the wholesale bar entered. The res is controlled and the property judgment enforceable. Brian's timely participation through counsel defeats any equation of overseas residence with complete failure to appear in the forfeiture case. No supported discovery default, impermissible request, disobedience of a valid civil order, or inability to conduct the proceeding supplies the asserted necessity. Punishment for refusing criminal prosecution cannot by itself extinguish these civil defenses. Each Associate rejects affirmance on that ground while preserving a materially different, adequately supported exercise of litigation authority.

### B. Reverse on confined inherent-power grounds

**Sufficient basis:** P1–P4 distinguish the power actually invoked, the need asserted for its use, the controlled property and the unestablished civil misconduct. Ordinary statutory and rule-based tools preserve the ability to respond to proved problems without presuming their predicates or ordering one now.

**Strongest objection:** A confined authority ruling might obscure that courts sometimes must act before an anticipated abuse fully occurs, and that no enumerated tool is guaranteed to address every future problem. The commitment does not impose that limitation. It requires a sufficient supported connection for this drastic act, leaves genuinely grounded preventive management available, and avoids declaring the alternatives invariably sufficient. None of the eight conditions reversal on first trying every lesser sanction or on proof that a stay would be perfect.

### C. Decide a constitutional hearing prohibition

**Supported case for the path:** Good and the received Hovey/McVeigh/Societe line protect an opportunity to resist deprivation and distinguish punishment from lawful evidentiary or litigation consequences. Exclusion from defending controlled property presents a serious constitutional question.

**Reason not adopted by any Associate:** The preserved scope-of-power ruling completely answers the judgment under review. A constitutional rule would require additional decisions about its breadth and about justified discovery sanctions without altering this remedy. The common opinion may explain the hearing concern and the precedents' restraint; no Associate commits to an independently sufficient due-process holding. This reservation does not weaken the independently binding Good rule in its proper setting.

### D. Resolve Swiss status, unclean hands, or the mandatory/discretionary error

**Supported case for a narrower path:** The admitted arrest and United States request for Swiss prosecution can affect how Brian's absence and the Government's equitable position should be understood. The Government's corrected account and petitioner's reply raise a real preservation dispute. The district court's mandatory treatment was erroneous according to the Ninth Circuit.

**Reason not adopted:** The Swiss alternative requires resolving contested legal consequences, preservation and causal responsibility for the deficient record; the received propositions establish neither present freedom to return nor the contrary conclusion. The mandatory/discretionary objection has a separate adverse preservation ruling that is not overcome merely by identifying the error. The common power ground can be decided even assuming the Government's asserted fugitive characterization for that limited purpose, without finding that characterization true or excluding the Swiss developments from all future relevance. No unsupported waiver, unclean-hands finding or OCR completion is used. The known missing words on government brief p.47 do no decisional work.
