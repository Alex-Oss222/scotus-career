## Reachability and separable decisions

All eight Associates accept §1259(1) review of CAAF's affirmed convictions and sentence, with the submitted statutory-authorization, clear-authorization, categorical-delegability and guidance questions properly before the Court. All nine supplied participants were present at argument and decision; no recusal, missing quorum or independent threshold bar is established. The merits may be resolved without reopening individual aggravator validity, unrelated conviction claims or a new service-connection jurisdiction objection.

The commitments below distinguish five conclusions: (1) Articles 18 and 56 authorize the challenged presidential restriction of an already legislatively authorized punishment, with Article 36 supplying relevant procedural authority; (2) no additional capital-specific express-word requirement defeats that authorization; (3) neither Article I nor the assumed capital-narrowing requirement categorically bars this delegation; (4) the actual statutory delegation is sufficiently bounded in this military setting; and (5) an independent Article II power to prescribe capital eligibility need not be decided. Acceptance of the first four does not imply acceptance of the fifth as an alternative ground.

## Shared limits on the propositions each Justice accepts

These limits are part of each commitment, not supplementary dicta proposed for an opinion.

- **Congress's punishment choice and the President's limiting role remain distinct.** Article 118 itself makes clauses (1) and (4) punishable by death or life. Articles 18 and 56 permit presidential limitations on punishment; they do not permit making clauses (2) or (3) capital, adding a capital offense, contradicting Congress's prescribed punishment or exceeding a statutory limit. Reading a conditional restriction as a punishment limit does not establish that every substantive criminal rule is a procedure under Article 36. Article 36's criminal-law/evidence direction applies so far as the President considers practicable, its consistency prohibition remains, and its pre-1990 uniformity-as-practicable and reporting requirements are retained.
- **Delegation boundaries are not identical to Eighth Amendment narrowing.** The enacted offense categories and penalty choice identify the field and outer limits of the delegated authority. They are not a finding that Article 118 alone satisfies every assumed capital-eligibility requirement. On the government’s bounded position, further narrowing is assumed necessary and the substantive adequacy of the three factors, if legislatively prescribed, is not contested for this authority question. The question remains who may prescribe them. Neither the panel's unanimous findings nor a concession about hypothetical congressional enactment answers that question.
- **The constitutional holding is confined to this delegation.** Mistretta permits guided sentencing discretion; Touby rejects categorical invalidity of criminal rulemaking while leaving the need for greater specificity open. Congress's identified capital offenses, death-or-life choice, express presidential limiting function and consistency constraints operate in a field in which the President has actual executive and Commander-in-Chief responsibilities. Those responsibilities inform the sufficiency and scope of the enacted delegation. They are not a freestanding source of authority to create capital liability contrary to or without Congress's law. No general military exemption from separation of powers or universal capital-delegation rule follows.
- **Safeguards retain their separate work.** The pre-1991 RCM requires the applicable unanimous conviction; notice, subject to later notice/proof unless specific prejudice cannot be cured by continuance or recess; a unanimously found listed factor beyond reasonable doubt; and unanimous substantial outweighing of extenuation/mitigation by admissible aggravation. Mitigation, instructions, factor ballots and announcement remain separate requirements. Compliance with such executive procedures does not manufacture a missing congressional delegation. Article 18's law-of-war and noncapital-referral provisions, RCM 1004(d)'s Article 106 spying exception and paragraph (e)'s qualified alternative-punishment provisions remain outside the present murder application, not silently abolished.
- **Actual capital law remains intact.** Arave's genuine eligibility narrowing, Tuilaepa/Proctor's bounded selection rule after unchallenged lawful eligibility, effective mitigation under Graham/Johnson, and the independent error-and-cure rules in Stringer/Sochor/Espinosa are not displaced. Stevens and Ginsburg's Tuilaepa Part II–V joins are not expanded into Part I joins. Tuggle precludes using surviving eligibility to defeat an independent assistance injury. None of those cases decides which civilian doctrines govern every military proceeding, supplies an unpresented error here or turns harmlessness into an alternative basis for sustaining an unauthorized capital rule.

## Independent Justice commitments

### Stevens

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm CAAF on the presented delegation challenge. Congress selected capital murder alternatives and assigned the President authority to limit their punishment. On that enacted foundation, these additional eligibility restrictions may be prescribed for the military prosecution presented. Articles 18 and 56 do the principal authorization work; Article 36 supports the implemented procedures without requiring a conclusion that capital eligibility is merely procedural. No special formula expressly naming “aggravating factors” is necessary here.

**Own authority and scope:** Stevens joined Mistretta, including its conditional note 11, and Touby; both permit meaningful criminal-sentencing authority within legislative boundaries. His Solorio judgment concurrence, 483 U.S. 451–452, counsels against deciding a broader military question when the actual application can be sustained. His Weiss Parts A/B and Ryder joins require constitutional review within military institutions. His Tuilaepa Parts II–V joins and his unanimous Tuggle opinion preserve the distinction between eligibility and other sentencing protections.

**Competing path and answer:** A narrow adverse statutory construction is stronger than a categorical prohibition: death eligibility matters substantively, and Article 106a shows Congress can specify factors expressly. Nevertheless, the direct language assigning punishment limits to the President covers a rule restricting death within Congress's already capital murder classes; the express espionage scheme neither removes that earlier grant nor makes murder factors an extension of capital liability. Unlike the body criticized in Mistretta's dissent, the President also has actual responsibility for military administration. Those grounds answer the authority question without broad civilian-military incorporation.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join the confined authorization and constitutional sufficiency holdings. Require reservation of independent Article II power and broader military capital applications. Fay's active-duty status and killing on Fort Hood support the case's actual military setting, but supply no new finding about Sharbino or a revived jurisdictional service-connection requirement. No separate writing is indispensable if those limits are explicit; a scope concurrence would be warranted if the opinion made unnecessary claims about every military capital offense. Affirmance grants no execution approval and orders no resentencing.

### O'Connor

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm. Articles 18/56 supply authority for the additional restrictions, and the statutory scheme gives adequate direction to this President exercising the limiting role in the military system. Reject categorical nondelegability and an extra express-words rule. The result does not require deciding that criminal or capital delegations can never demand greater specificity: even assuming that contention applies, the bounded offense, punishment and institutional assignment here suffice.

**Own authority and scope:** O'Connor authored Touby, 500 U.S. 164–169, and joined Mistretta and Solorio. Her Weiss Parts A/B and Ryder joins preserve structural scrutiny, while her Arave and Tuilaepa opinions distinguish sufficient eligibility guidance from later selection discretion. Her Graham/Johnson commitments prevent treating admission of mitigation or some relevant sentencing channel as invariably enough.

**Competing path and answer:** Touby's numerous express drug-scheduling criteria support demanding scrutiny of the fewer substantive factor-selection directions here. But Touby did not prescribe its particular list as the minimum for every criminal delegation. This statute fixes which murders are capital, restricts the choice to death or life, authorizes presidential punishment limits and bars inconsistency with the Code; the recipient's own military responsibilities further confine the task. This is a statutory capital-punishment implementation, not authority to designate whatever conduct the Executive chooses as a new capital offense. The Eighth Amendment objection cannot itself convert every constitutionally required sentencing detail into a nondelegable congressional task.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join the actual-scheme sufficiency holding, with Touby's enhanced-standard reservation intact. Do not join a claim that military deference alone supplies standards, that Article 36's practicability qualification disappears, or that independent Article II authority must sustain any otherwise unauthorized factor. No harmlessness finding substitutes for the no-authority-error ground. Affirm only the challenged judgment; Article 71 remains operative.

### Scalia

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm on the enacted limiting authority and its constitutional exercise by the President. Articles 18/56 naturally cover restrictions on a punishment Congress specifically allowed. Congress need not separately use the modern term “capital aggravator” to authorize that limitation. The challenged rule neither transfers Congress's entire legislative responsibility nor creates an autonomous body empowered only to legislate.

**Own authority and scope:** Scalia's Mistretta dissent, 488 U.S. 413–427, expressly accepted the sufficiency of the statutory standards there and distinguished rulemaking ancillary to genuine executive or judicial duties from naked legislative power. He joined Touby and Solorio. In Weiss he joined Part A and historical description, using a historical-practice ground for tenure rather than Part B's contextual sufficiency analysis; Ryder and Neal reflect his enforcement of real structural and statutory limits. His Arave/Tuilaepa joins do not erase those limits or establish wholesale military incorporation.

**Competing path and answer:** Petitioner's characterization of capital eligibility as legislative choice presents the strongest structural objection. Yet Congress may confer discretion in the execution of its law, including binding rules ancillary to executive responsibility. Here Congress enacted the crimes and authorized death, then expressly permitted limits on that punishment. The President's military role provides the institutional connection missing from the Commission as Scalia analyzed it. Article 106a's explicit additional-factor language does not negate the separate enacted language already covering punishment limits. No constitutional “clear statement” can be used simply to disregard that text.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join affirmance, statutory authority and rejection of the actual delegation challenge. Do not join an amorphous balance of institutional convenience as a replacement for the Constitution's structure, a claim that all powers may be transferred if sufficiently useful, or a demand to treat capital factors as procedure by definition. A separate concurrence has a function only if the common opinion relies on such a broader theory rather than the actual executive assignment. Do not reach independent Article II authority. Retain the sentence and convictions without an execution order.

### Kennedy

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm. Read the UCMJ provisions together: Article 118 makes the legislative punishment choice, Articles 18/56 authorize restrictions within it, and Article 36 governs the related procedural implementation. The authority is sufficiently definite for this military sentencing task; no separate express-factor formula or categorical ban is supported.

**Own authority and scope:** Kennedy joined Mistretta and Touby, authored actual Weiss and the unanimous Neal decision, and joined Ryder. Neal's statutory/Guidelines distinction requires preserving Congress's punishment choice; it does not forbid discretionary sentencing rules within that choice. His Stringer opinion and Arave/Tuilaepa joins distinguish eligibility, selection and actual correction.

**Competing path and answer:** Neal provides a substantial contrary analogy if the President's factors are characterized as rewriting statutory exposure. It fails because these factors add conditions before an already authorized death sentence may be selected; they neither increase the punishment nor substitute a conflicting statutory construction. Congress expressly made that limiting role available. The requirement of narrowing remains substantive, but neither Mistretta nor Touby makes substantive significance alone disqualify an otherwise guided delegation. Article 106a corroborates Congress's ability to define a different, more detailed scheme without implied repeal of Articles 18/56 for murder.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join a statutory and constitutional holding tied to the actual delegation and presidential responsibilities. Do not make independent Commander-in-Chief authority an alternative holding or transform Weiss's distinct appointment/tenure rules into a general military exception. Judicial review and capital error correction remain available for their proper claims. Affirmance of the presented challenge leaves Article 71 finality and presidential approval independent; no further judicial sentence proceeding is ordered.

### Souter

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm. Congress's capital-offense selection, express assignment of punishment limits and statutory constraints permit the challenged factors. The statute does not leave the President free to create crimes or choose any class for death. This actual delegation survives without deciding whether an enhanced criminal/capital specificity standard is invariably required.

**Own authority and scope:** Souter joined Touby, Weiss Parts A/B, Ryder and Neal. His Graham opinion and Johnson join require effective mitigation; his Sochor opinion separates a valid narrowed factor, an actual weighing defect and adequate appellate cure. His Arave/Tuilaepa and Tuggle joins demonstrate that an eligibility rule does not dispose of every later constitutional sentencing issue.

**Competing path and answer:** The best adverse path is inadequacy of congressional guidance, not a universal constitutional ban. Article 118 gives no legislative list of murder aggravators, and an executive's self-imposed safeguards cannot cure an invalid delegation. The answer rests instead on Congress's prior designation of the capital offenses and penalty alternatives, its assignment of a limiting function, the statutory consistency restraints and the President's established military responsibilities. The executive factor list implements that assignment; its substantive adequacy on the bounded assumption is a distinct question. Neither general deference nor the panel's verdict supplies missing authority.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join the confined delegation holding with the enhanced-standard question reserved and without adopting independent Article II power. Require an explicit distinction between validating who prescribed these factors and deciding an unpresented mitigation, weighing or individual-factor claim. The Article 106 spying exception and Article 36 qualifications preclude a universal description of the safeguards. Affirm; do not declare any unrelated error harmless or require a new sentencing hearing.

### Thomas

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm. The enacted presidential power to prescribe punishment limits includes limiting the capital class Congress specified in Article 118. The conventional nondelegation authorities do not require invalidation of that assignment to the President in administering military justice. Reject the categorical claim and added express-factor requirement; leave freestanding Article II authority undecided.

**Own authority and scope:** Thomas joined Weiss Part A and the historical-practice tenure ground, not Part B's contextual sufficiency rationale. His Ryder and Neal joins enforce actual constitutional appointments and statutory sentencing limits. His Arave/Tuilaepa joins and Tuggle join provide direct capital-law evidence without attributing to him pre-appointment votes in Mistretta, Touby or Solorio. Those pre-divergence decisions are controlling authorities he applies, not his own prior joins.

**Competing path and answer:** A structural objection based on Article I's assignment to Congress remains real: military necessity cannot erase that assignment, as the continued applicability of the Appointments Clause in Weiss/Ryder confirms. Here Congress exercised it by prescribing the offenses and available punishments and by assigning a circumscribed limiting role to an officer with military executive duties. That differs from an unauthorized new offense or an appointment to an unrelated office. Article 106a supplies no textual withdrawal of the murder-related limiting authority. No unsupported historical assertion about founding-era aggravator lists is needed.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join the textual authorization and actual-delegation sufficiency propositions. Do not attribute a balancing rationale to his Weiss position, adopt an independent presidential power to disregard Congress's limits, or announce that all civilian capital doctrine governs the military. There is no basis to convert Ryder's defect-specific remedy into automatic guilt reversal; here no remand is required because the presented authority challenge fails. Article 71 approval and finality remain conditions on execution.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm. The ordinary meaning and integrated operation of Articles 18/56 authorize restrictions on Congress's specified death penalty, with Article 36 performing its actual procedural work. Constitutional capital narrowing does not make this implementation categorically nondelegable. The statute and recipient's military responsibilities supply adequate boundaries for the assignment presented.

**Own authority and scope:** Ginsburg joined Weiss Parts A/B, authored Ryder and joined Neal. These establish review of real military structural limits and respect for Congress's sentencing choices. Her actual MCI join distinguishes delegated implementation from replacement of an enacted statutory obligation. Her Tuilaepa Parts II–V joins, withholding Part I, and Tuggle join support preserving the eligibility/selection limits rather than importing an unadopted broad capital framework.

**Competing path and answer:** The most substantial contrary argument combines Article 106a's express language with the significance of death eligibility to demand clearer authorization. But the murder provisions already include death, and the distinct Articles 18/56 speak directly to presidential punishment limits. This does not erase Congress's scheme as the MCI rule did: death remains authorized only for Congress's capital classes, subject to additional legally operative conditions. Article 106a's express statutory factors for a different offense do not establish that these generally applicable limitations clauses cease to operate. Ryder requires scrutiny, not invalidation whenever a military institution exercises discretion.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join the bounded statutory and constitutional grounds, with all independent mitigation and weighing protections preserved. Do not turn her Tuilaepa nonjoin into opposition to the actual eight-Justice selection holding, or approval of that holding into a view on full military incorporation. Independent Article II power is unnecessary. Affirmance neither ratifies every RCM factor nor determines execution readiness; no sentence remand follows.

### Breyer

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm. Congress determined the capital murder classes and authorized the President to limit punishment within them. The resulting assignment is a guided implementation of military sentencing law rather than a transfer of authority to choose new capital crimes. Articles 18/56 provide the delegation, so neither a special express-factor formula nor an independent Article II holding is needed.

**Own authority and scope:** Breyer joined Ryder and Neal, requiring lawful military adjudication and preserving the separate operation of statutory sentencing law and delegated rules. He joined Tuggle's full holding, preserving independent constitutional sentencing injuries despite surviving eligibility. Mistretta and Touby supply governing nondelegation law; no pre-appointment Supreme Court join, Sentencing Commission authorship or biographical inference is used as a substitute for his eligible adjudicative record.

**Competing path and answer:** The adverse path is that “limits” merely authorizes a numerical maximum and supplies too little guidance for selecting who may die. The language does not impose that numerical restriction: a condition withholding a higher authorized punishment from part of an offense class is a limitation on punishment. Congress's own death-or-life decision and identification of the relevant murder offenses remain operative, while the President implements additional restraints within the military system. Neal's insistence on both statutory and delegated rules retaining their own fields favors that distinction. Practical utility alone would not suffice, and the RCM's own safeguards cannot replace the enacted assignment.

**Join limits and remedy:** Join the actual statutory and constitutional sufficiency propositions. No need to resolve the enhanced-standard question beyond this scheme, all civilian-military incorporation, or free-standing executive capital authority. Preserve Tuggle's distinction between a valid eligibility rule and an independently supported injury. Affirm the challenged judgment without sentence recalculation, a speculative error inquiry or execution approval.

## Alternative dispositions and compatibility

The statutory-insufficiency route would stop before the broader constitutional question: vacate or reverse the affected capital-sentence affirmance and remand through CAAF for lawful sentence proceedings. The distinct categorical-delegability or inadequate-guidance routes would likewise require sentence-level relief, without automatic acquittal, release, a new guilt trial or a judicially selected life sentence. Article 118's lawful noncapital alternative and the military system's actual remedial authority would have to govern the remand. It would be improper to preserve an unauthorized death sentence merely because the same factors could have been legislated, because the panel was unanimous, or by transplanting state weighing-error harmlessness into a delegation defect. None of the eight adopts these adverse routes on the final packet.

The eight judgments align on affirmance of the presented challenge. All eight support statutory authorization, rejection of a special express-factor requirement, constitutional delegability and sufficiency of this actual assignment. Those shared propositions must retain the limits stated above; they do not aggregate a freestanding Article II rationale with a statutory rationale. All eight decline to decide the independent Article II ground. Stevens's application-focused limit and Scalia's objection to an unstructured institutional-balance rationale are material circulation constraints. O'Connor's assumed-more-demanding-standard explanation and the other Justices' reservation of the general standard are compatible at the level of sufficiency of this scheme; they do not create a universal enhanced-standard holding.
