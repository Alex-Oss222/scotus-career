## Lonchar — the judgment is stay vacatur

The lower court **vacated the district execution stay**; it did not affirm an already-entered district merits dismissal. The district court had declined the State's proposed dismissal of Lonchar's first personal federal petition. Earlier failed next-friend proceedings do not automatically make that petition successive. The lower opinion's extreme-delay and purpose findings remain the lower court's findings; they supply neither an independently established Supreme Court fact nor claim-specific merits resolution.

The available neutral inquiry distinguishes an asserted independent equitable ground for dismissing a first petition from the discretion to deny or vacate an execution stay. Section 2243's “law and justice” direction and initial nonentitlement exception must be read with the enacted Rules. Section 2251 says “may,” includes its precise temporal and subject-matter limits, and expressly preserves State proceedings when no stay is granted. Neither provision supplies an automatic merits hearing, writ or stay. Any legal correction and remand must address the actual stay-vacatur judgment and preserve genuinely independent claim-specific grounds without inventing their satisfaction.

The retained enacted Rule 9(a) requires filing delay that prejudices the State's ability to respond, with the stated reasonable-diligence exception. It contains **no five-year presumption**. The retained Code's 1976 amendment note expressly records deletion of the proposed presumption; the older advisory discussion cannot restore it. Rule 9(b)'s successive/abusive-petition conditions are distinct. Execution-date inconvenience alone is not the Rule 9(a) textual prejudice finding.
