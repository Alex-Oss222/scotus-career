### First-petition dismissal and independent stay discretion remain distinct

Delay may support a disposition authorized by Rule 9 or another lawful source only upon that source's actual predicates. That first-petition holding does not eliminate the separate rules governing successive petitions.

Independent stay equity survives. ### Necessary-process protection has an actual setting and conditions

Lonchar Part III states:

> If execution would prevent completion of process actually required by the governing habeas statute and Rules, and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time, a court must provide the interim protection necessary to make that identified opportunity meaningful. Mere filing, first-petition status or a capital sentence establishes neither condition.

The actual setting is a **first personal district-court petition**. Part III extends Barefoot's meaningful-review reasoning **by analogy** to genuinely required district process. It does not directly apply Barefoot's initial certified-appeal rule, and it does not silently displace the separately preserved successive-petition substantial-grounds standard.
