The relevant statutory terms must travel together. Section 4371(1) imposes four cents per dollar or fraction of premium for casualty insurance/indemnity bonds issued to or for an insured defined in §4372(d). Section 4372(a) covers a nonresident alien individual, foreign partnership or foreign corporation and includes obligations in the nature of an indemnity bond; the version after the 1976 amendment excludes a foreign government or municipal/other corporation exercising taxing power. Casualty insurance in §4372(b) covers a nonlife policy or other instrument making, continuing or renewing insurance. Section 4372(d)(1) covers a domestic corporation/partnership or resident individual for risks wholly or partly within the United States; (d)(2) separately covers a foreign corporation/partnership or nonresident individual engaged in domestic trade or business for domestic risks. The challenged export application is within that broader scheme.

For the tax period, §4373(1)'s exemption concerns a policy/bond/annuity signed or countersigned by the insurer's officer or agent in a State or D.C. where the insurer is authorized to do business. Section 4373(2) separately exempts specified indemnity bonds securing federal payments or replacement/payment of federal instruments. Section 4374 identifies persons making, signing, issuing, selling or benefiting from the instruments, and exempts the United States and its agencies/instrumentalities. The 1982 Code notes the 1976 wording and liability changes; no new question about those transitional details is presented. Do not back-project a later effectively-connected-income version of §4373 onto these assessments.

Section 3(21)(A) makes fiduciary status attach **to the extent** of discretionary plan management/control; authority/control over management or disposition of assets; compensated investment advice or authority/responsibility for it; or discretionary plan administration. The separate investment-company exception in (B) prevents fiduciary/party-in-interest status solely from the plan's investment, with its own-employees-plan qualification and preservation of other-law duties. Nothing in this record makes that exception operative.

Section 406(a) begins with the §408 exception and requires a fiduciary causing the plan to enter a transaction with the specified actual/constructive knowledge. The text identifies sale/exchange/lease, lending/credit, goods/services/facilities, transfer/use of assets for a party in interest, and certain employer-security/property acquisitions; §406(b) separately addresses fiduciary self-dealing, adverse representation and personal consideration. Employer status supplies a §3(14)(C) party in interest, not by itself a finding that the employer performed a §3(21) fiduciary function. No §408 exemption is claimed in the reported controversy. The preserved implementation allegation must be separately identified before deciding any §406 scope issue.

OBRA §9204(a)(1) applies §§9201–9202 to plan years beginning on/after January 1, 1988 and employees with an hour of service in a covered year. Section 9204(a)(2) has a distinct transition for pre-March 1, 1986 collective bargaining agreements: the specified first plan year after the earlier of January 1, 1990 or the later of January 1, 1988 and termination of the last qualifying agreement, disregarding extensions after February 28, 1986. No fact invoking that exception is supplied here. Section 9204(b) separately applies §9203 to covered plan years **and service performed on/after** January 1, 1988. Section 9204(c) conditionally permits delayed formal plan amendment until the first plan year beginning on/after January 1, 1989 if the plan operates in accordance with the new requirements in the interim and the amendment covers that interim period retroactively. Formal-amendment delay does not itself postpone substantive operation. The lower opinion's disputed characterization of prior-service credit as retrospective is not the statute's command; apply actual simulated Landgraf to each provision's operation.

* **Investigative facts, judicial recitation:** Indiana police operated Circle City Hydroponics in 1991; Myers made a suspicious purchase and asked about growing technology. Investigators examined electrical use and observed no curbside garbage for three weeks. Their inference that he was disposing of marijuana clippings remains an investigative inference, not a proved fact of interior activity.
* **Thermal acquisition, judicial recitation:** On March 12, 1992, police used an Agema 210 on his residence. It measured emitted heat and displayed shaded differences; it emitted no rays/beams and did not penetrate the viewed object. The recorded heat pattern indicated high-intensity indoor lights associated with marijuana cultivation. Roof vents and lack of concealment of emissions are recited in the lower opinion. Those facts do not themselves resolve privacy under the actual Pinson holding.
* **Later warrant and recovery, judicial recitation:** Police sought a warrant using the scan and other evidence. The warrant issued and was executed April 22, 1992; officers recovered growing/processed marijuana and equipment. The scan and physical entry are distinct acts.
* **Preservation, judicial recitation:** Myers sought suppression on the ground that the warrant rested on the allegedly unconstitutional thermal scan. After suppression was denied he entered a conditional guilty plea to manufacturing and possessing with intent to distribute under §841(a)(1), reserving suppression review. His derivative-warrant contention is expressly reported. This is not a source for transferring Pinson's case-specific affidavit waiver to Myers.
