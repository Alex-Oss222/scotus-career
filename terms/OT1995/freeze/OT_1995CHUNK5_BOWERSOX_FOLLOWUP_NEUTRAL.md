### Successive-petition stay and review standard

*Barefoot v. Estelle*, 463 U.S. 880, 895 (1983), and *Delo v. Stokes*, 495 U.S. 320, 321–322 (1990), require substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted for a stay pending a second or successive federal petition. *Stokes* applies abuse-of-discretion review to the actual claim and prior availability; it does not establish an automatic third-petition rule. A certificate is relevant but does not independently establish substantial grounds in this setting. An unexplained stay order does not establish their absence. The existing public interim order expressly preserves those propositions.

*Barefoot*'s initial certified-appeal rule is distinct: meaningful merits presentation and appellate determination are required, and a necessary stay protects that review when lawful considered disposition cannot occur in time. Its separate certiorari-stage probability-of-review, possibility-of-reversal and irreparable-harm criteria are not the decision rule for this referred vacatur application. The heightened “flagrant abuse” and All Writs discussions identified in the primary-source addendum are separate writings, not additional Court holdings. *Delo v. Blair*'s simulated equal division supplies no controlling rationale.

### Claim availability and distinct procedural predicates

April 9 precedes AEDPA. The certificate is a certificate of probable cause. The later authorization, limitations, certificate-of-appealability and §2254 deference regimes are unavailable.

Rule 9(b) permits dismissal of repeated grounds after a prior merits determination, or of new grounds whose earlier omission constituted abuse. The then-effective §2244(b) subsequent-application provision follows a federal denial after an evidentiary hearing on a material factual merits issue or a hearing on a legal merits issue; its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground. The State's clear and particular abuse pleading precedes the prisoner's resulting burden. Default, exhaustion and its absence/ineffectiveness exceptions, independent merits grounds and any applicable gateway remain distinct. None is resolved by an unallocated list of reported classifications.

Rule 9(a) concerns delay prejudicing the State's ability to respond, subject to its reasonable-diligence qualification; it contains no enacted five-year presumption. Rule 4 requires prompt examination and permits facial dismissal when nonentitlement plainly appears, otherwise an answer or other appropriate action with service. Neither §2243 nor the Rules guarantee an evidentiary hearing or a writ upon filing.

The operative Standards retain their exact settings:

- **Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar** — *Gomez*, April 21, 1992: the State's enforcement interest and unjustified withholding of a long-available challenge may matter independently of a habeas bar. Serious claims and irreversibility do not alone compel late relief; this is not a categorical late-filing rule or minimum-petition-count requirement.
- **Capital habeas counsel before a merits petition and discretionary stays** — *McFarland*, June 30, 1994: a qualifying statutory preparation proceeding may be pending for §2251 purposes. Reasonable preparation is protected, while proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction may justify denial. No automatic, indefinite or fresh preparation entitlement follows from another petition.
- **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims** — *Schlup*, January 23, 1995: reliable new evidence evaluated with the whole probative record must show that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict. Reliability and likely credibility, including pertinent delay and inconsistency, matter; neither inmate nor impeachment evidence is categorically excluded. Possible acquittal, ordinary merits prejudice or a reviewing judge's doubt does not suffice.
- **Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims** — *Sawyer*, June 22, 1992: clear and convincing evidence must show that absent constitutional error no reasonable juror would find the state-law eligibility facts. Additional discretionary mitigation alone does not qualify.

Neither gateway establishes an underlying constitutional violation or automatically supplies a hearing, writ or stay. Their distinct thresholds cannot be exchanged.

### Lonchar and the limits of necessary-process protection

The April 1 public *Lonchar* decision rejects an additional equitable sanction terminating a first personal petition on the reported delay and litigation purpose without the statutory or Rules predicates. It preserves independent stay equity and successive-petition standards. Its controlling necessary-process rule requires identifying process actually due under the governing statute and Rules, execution's prevention of that process, and inability to finish lawful prompt disposition in time. It establishes no missing Williams claim, uncompleted process or necessary duration; an argument setting alone supplies none of those predicates.

Section 2251 authorizes an eligible federal justice or judge to stay the covered State proceeding before final judgment, after final judgment **of discharge**, or pending appeal. Its “may” is discretionary; mere pendency does not suspend State action. *Lonchar*'s restoration of its own displaced district stay was a consequence of correcting the particular appellate error. It is no automatic remedy for this application. Complete standalone 1994 §§2253 and 1651 remain unrecovered in the supplied source validations; the verified *Barefoot* certificate proposition remains available, but no new statutory construction is inferred from that source limitation.

### Additional validated law for the actual claim subjects

The [same-ground authority note](../sources/chunk5_bowersox_followup/SAME_GROUND_AUTHORITY_NOTE.md) rests on a complete official reading of *Sanders*, 373 U.S. 1–32. Same-ground prior merits consideration and omitted-new-ground abuse must be distinguished. The applicant bears the ends-of-justice burden for redetermination; its examples are nonexhaustive, and a sound exercise of discretion remains distinct from a duty to reopen. The 1963 statutory quotation is not substituted for the 1966 §2244(b) text. *McCleskey* constrains abusive new grounds; the supplied *Kuhlmann* innocence formulation is a plurality, not an exclusive majority rule silently forbidding every other same-ground reconsideration. The current simulated *Schlup* and *Sawyer* gateway rules remain controlling when their respective gateways are invoked. The [instruction and proportionality note](../sources/chunk5_bowersox_followup/INSTRUCTION_AND_PROPORTIONALITY_AUTHORITY_NOTE.md) rests on complete official readings of *Hopper*, 456 U.S. 605–614; *Pulley*, 465 U.S. 37–74; and *Hicks*, 447 U.S. 343–351. *Hopper* applies *Beck*'s evidentiary qualification: a lesser-included instruction is required when the evidence warrants it, and only then; the evidence must permit a rational lesser conviction and acquittal of the greater. Williams's evidence is not Evans's repeated confession. *Pulley* rejects an invariable federal entitlement to comparative proportionality review, while not converting every state review defect into an unreviewable matter. *Hicks* protects the particular state-created liberty interest against arbitrary deprivation. The latter is not a general rule that every state procedural error warrants a federal writ. The Court and separate writings' scopes are retained in the notes; no unquoted Missouri statutory duty or new amendment rule is supplied.

The State has a concrete argument beyond petition count and appellate silence. On the penalty claim, the reported choice to withhold permission because death seemed preferable can be evaluated as **legally insufficient cause even if that account is accepted**, without inventing incapacity, disbelieving missing experts or deciding undisclosed mitigation. The district's contrary causation finding, active pursuit of conditions litigation and reported all-legitimate-challenges representation strengthen the State's position. The later appellate opportunity bears on diligence, while not creating an automatic remand-request rule. If no-cause is independently sufficient on a properly presented abuse objection and no supported applicable exception changes that conclusion, unknown IAC prejudice need not be decided.

For the first-degree instruction ground, the State can rely on actual prior merits consideration, the prior majority's treatment of the kidnapping issue, and finality. The previously known transport and impeachment evidence is not new evidence, and an old dissent is not itself a reason to require another adjudication. The State may argue that these established circumstances supply no sound reason for discretionary redetermination and no substantial available ground for the present stay. That is an evaluative application of law and record, not a jurisdictional rule against all successive review.

This route must also account for the second-degree claim and proposed amendment if either can independently sustain the stay. The second-degree presentation cannot simply be merged with first-degree adjudication. A model may reason through alternative supported classifications, but it must not select a disputed presentation history as fact.
