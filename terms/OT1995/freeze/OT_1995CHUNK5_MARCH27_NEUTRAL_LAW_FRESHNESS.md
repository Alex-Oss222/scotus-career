## New law that must enter the three packets

**Varity — distinct statutory actions and bounded equity.** The controlling §502(a)(3) holding permits appropriate individual equitable redress for the identified fiduciary violation despite the distinct plan-centered §§409/502(a)(2) remedy. It interprets an **express equitable grant**, rather than deriving a private cause solely from remedial usefulness. Traditionally equitable categories remain required. Adequate relief under another ERISA provision **normally** makes further equity unnecessary and inappropriate; the controlling rule is not an absolute prohibition on every alternative pleading. The stricter adequate-remedy condition in the separate concurrence is not the Court's rule. The unpresented classification of particular monetary items, rejected compensatory awards, and distinct ten-retiree assignment ground remain as the public decision states. Its preservation holding leaves that lower ground undisturbed without deciding a new general assignment rule. These are relevant limits on analogies, not a general action-creation or remedial-exclusivity code for other statutes.

**Meghrig — enacted relief and substantive prerequisites remain separate.** RCRA's citizen action requires a qualifying endangerment when suit begins; a past contributor can cause a current threat, and imminent threat does not require present physical harm. Its specified prospective restraint and abatement remedies do not create general reimbursement for earlier cleanup merely because the plaintiff invokes restitution or a current danger remains. The Court separately reserves (1) an injunction requiring payment of cleanup costs arising after a properly commenced action and (2) otherwise recovering costs paid after invocation of RCRA's statutory process. Neither is affirmatively authorized; invocation is not equated with filing. The full remedial construction has direct majority support despite a narrower additional join. Porter, Hecht, and Franklin remain subject to congressional limits, while Sea Clammers and Transamerica support attention to this specific enforcement arrangement. The decision does not classify all money as damages, reject all restitution, create a universal monetary-equity bar, or alter ordinary equitable principles across other statutes. Its temporal condition is a statutory prerequisite, not a new constitutional mootness rule.

**Wisconsin — concrete partial redress, presentation, and bounded administrative review.** Identified individual voters may establish a continuing personal representation injury with a concrete connection to the census determination and substantial likelihood of redress through the statutory allocation process. They need not show certainty that reconsideration will yield their preferred dataset or obtain an injunction against the President, and the State's appellate choice does not surrender their own claims. This is a specific application of continuing-injury and likely-redress doctrine, not a holding that every procedural opportunity or declaration necessarily redresses an injury. The Secretary's completed determination remains final agency action under actual Franklin; available APA review does not reopen the district court's distinct APA disposition in the constitutional appeal. Constitutional reasonableness is not every APA requirement. The census standard and its application do not govern unrelated exercises of discretion. The Court reserves the disputed sampling permission/prohibition questions, preserves actual-agency-ground requirements, and orders no new technical or APA proceeding.

**Barnett — actual federal entitlement before preemption and relief.** The general national-bank rule protects conferred enumerated and incidental powers against state prevention or significant interference, subject to the particular federal scheme and incorporated state restrictions. The specific §92 holding defeats Florida's categorical affiliation prohibition for qualifying activity. The possibility of compliance by abandoning a protected federal option does not remove the conflict, but the word “may” alone does not make every federal permission preemptive. McCarran-Ferguson's federal exception removes one obstacle; a demonstrated statutory conflict remains separately necessary. Compatible state regulation survives, with no categorical licensing safe harbor, general field immunity, or new agency-deference rule. Relief still requires resolution of the preserved association-location and customer-geography cross-appeals and compliance with §92's conditions. The decision neither awards an unconditional injunction nor resolves every state regulatory requirement. Its prospective officer-relief vehicle, identified in the source notes, supplies no separately adjudicated general Young or remedial-exclusivity rule.

## Case-specific relevance and required neutral framing

### Seminole Tribe

The new decisions materially inform, but do not decide, the statutory-remedy and Article III forks. The independent questions of clear abrogation intent, congressional power, consent, officer relief, underlying federal duty, constitutional judicial function, and permissible remedy remain distinct.

Meghrig strengthens the need to test whether requested relief fits Congress's actual arrangement. A proposed injunction, declaration, contempt mechanism, or Secretary route cannot arise merely because it would make IGRA effective. Its application of enforcement-structure reasoning is relevant to the Governors' argument and any asserted displacement of Young. It does **not** hold that every detailed scheme forecloses officer relief, or decide whether an IGRA officer suit confined to the enacted sequence invokes rather than supplements that scheme. Varity supplies the complementary caution: separate remedies do not defeat an express residual grant merely by existing, and adequacy is addressed at the injury and statutory-provision level. ERISA's express grant and qualified adequacy rule cannot be transplanted as an independent cause of action or constitutional immunity exception under IGRA.

Wisconsin supplies a concrete intervening example in which a judicial ruling may redress injury through later executive implementation without assuring the plaintiff's preferred outcome. This must accompany the Tribe's partial-relief argument. Its limits must accompany the Governors' answer: Wisconsin does not decide whether IGRA's particular judicial product is advisory, subject to executive revision, or sufficiently binding, nor whether findings alone suffice when the judgment's relief is disputed. The statutory consequences, finality, and constrained later functions identified in the Article III addendum still require analysis. Standing redressability, Article III judicial-function limits, and the availability of the particular remedy cannot be merged.

Barnett demonstrates protection of an actual affirmative federal entitlement against a state prohibition despite voluntary exercise. It does not turn Congress's Indian-commerce authority into a general waiver of immunity, establish abrogation, prove that Florida must agree to a compact, or make every federally permitted tribal activity immune from relevant state-law constraints. Its banking-specific significant-interference rule is not a new IGRA test. The prospective relief there neither decides IGRA's distinct remedial exclusivity question nor cures a genuine Article III defect.

### Morse

Varity and Meghrig materially reinforce separate inquiries into **private action**, **statutory coverage/liability**, and **available relief**. Varity applies an express ERISA equitable grant; it does not imply a §10 Voting Rights Act action from beneficial purpose or ordinary equity. Conversely, its rejection of a categorical plan-only objection does not support treating the existence of an express Attorney General route as automatically exclusive without construing §10 and the Act's related provisions. The original Allen/Suter/Central Bank/Franklin questions remain in their actual simulated form.

Meghrig requires source-specific authority for a monetary request but does not categorically exclude Morse's pleaded $45 refund as money or restitution. A valid refund theory must be assessed under the actual Voting Rights Act action and remedy; its presence is neither automatic proof of a live claim nor irrelevant to mootness. RCRA's filing-time danger requirement cannot become a requirement of continuing convention conduct for every election claim. The convention's expiration, recurring prospective claim, and particular monetary request retain their separate analysis.

Wisconsin reinforces personal injury and likely redress, including a plaintiff's independent claim rather than dependence on a government's litigation choice. It does not establish these voters' standing, reasonable likelihood of recurrence, proper refund defendant, or a §10 action. Its treatment of representation injury separately from racial purpose supplies no new discriminatory-purpose prerequisite for §5 coverage. Its census methodology standard cannot replace Presley's voting nexus, the party-authority/public-function analysis, or §51.7's two conditions and exclusions.

Barnett offers no convention-coverage, party-state-action, association, or §10 holding. Its insistence on identifying the actual federal grant before determining conflict and relief is a bounded interpretive comparison only; it supplies no general significant-interference test for voting regulation. The §5 coverage action must remain distinct from preclearance merits, and no public release decides the preserved scope of a nomination-method challenge or converts contested 1964 practices into findings.
