**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Congress regulates manufacture and transfer of post-1986 machineguns; possession controls can prevent that defined market prohibition from being defeated by an untraceable supply of newly made or unlawfully transferred weapons. The grandfather distinction helps identify the regulated scheme. This direct anti-evasion rationale differs from general crime costs or an assertion that every object that once traveled remains fully regulable. Lopez and Harris leave room for that bounded economic regulation. It is unnecessary to make Stone's entire proposed Commerce framework binding or to add an interstate element the statute does not contain.

**Arms reasoning:** A personal right to ordinary lawful arms need not protect these actual automatic weapons in the same way as an ordinary handgun. Stone would distinguish sustained automatic fire by function and its relation to ordinary lawful civilian defense, not appearance or military ancestry alone. Miller's discussion of military usefulness does not itself establish the claimed personal exemption. Rejecting this claimed right does not decide that every rifle, semiautomatic firearm, suppressor or specially regulated item is unprotected. A separate individual-right explanation can advance the line while the Court assumes the premise for this narrow judgment; this federal prosecution presents no incorporation question.
