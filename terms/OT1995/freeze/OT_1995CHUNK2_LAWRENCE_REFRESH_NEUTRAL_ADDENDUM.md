## Admitted North Carolina law

Source: [North Carolina Session Law 1995-424](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/SessionLaws/HTML/1995-1996/SL1995-424.html), all three sections read in full, and the [sanitized admitted-source public projection](../entering-law/PUBLIC_North_Carolina_paternity_amendment_statutory_effectiveness_1995-10-01.md). The amendment is effective October 1, 1995 and is part of the law entering the January 8 event.

Section 1 amends G.S. 49-14(c)'s restriction on commencing an action or entering judgment after the putative father's death. The exceptions are an action begun before his death; an action begun within one year after death if no estate-administration proceeding began within that year; or, if estate administration began within that year, an action begun within the estate-claims period specified by G.S. 28A-19-3(a). A paternity judgment under this subsection must take effect as of the day before the father's death.

Section 2 amends G.S. 49-14(d). In a contested case brought more than three years after the child's birth **or** after the putative father's death, paternity cannot be established without blood- or genetic-marker-test evidence.

Section 3 limits the act to actions commenced on or after October 1, 1995 but before October 1, 1998, regardless of the father's death date. The act expires October 1, 1998. The two temporal restrictions—the qualifying filing alternatives and this commencement window—both retain their force.
