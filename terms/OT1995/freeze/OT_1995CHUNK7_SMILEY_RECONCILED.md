## Material correction and changed-premise audit

**All eight retain affirmance; all eight now join R1–R6 below.** The sufficient ground includes deference to the Comptroller's reasonable interpretation of federal interest. It does not declare that classification to be the uniquely correct reading, or independently select it as the Court's best reading sufficient without the agency ground. The independent freeze offered a substantial permissible textual argument, but did not identify a Justice-specific changed premise explaining why any Associate would abandon the historical choice between reasonable agency construction and judicial best interpretation.

The actual *NationsBank* agency-review rationale was adopted by each of the eight. Its conditions are consequential: determine the statutory boundary, identify the delegated responsibility and actual agency explanation, then respect a reasonable choice within the remaining range. Those conditions do not turn every banking classification into independent judicial selection. Here §§93, 1818, 1813(q) and 93a supply actual responsibility relevant to §85, while the published rule supplies an official reasoned interpretation. Section 86's private remedy does not eliminate that administrative responsibility or make this an *Adams Fruit* attempt to control a judicial remedy outside the delegation.

The actual *MCI* Court rationale enforces the enacted tariff obligation; its four-Justice concurrence additionally enforces the special-circumstances condition. Both remain operative evidence. Neither establishes a missing statutory predicate here. Section 43 was enacted before the historical Smiley decision as well as this event; it is not an intervening simulated restriction. A source's failure to prove every §43 predicate is neither a factual change nor a judicial finding of violation, applicability, preservation, default, or an exception. It cannot explain dropping an otherwise supported historical ground. Conversely, this reconciliation does not erase §43 or deem every procedure satisfied.

*Marquette*, *Daggs*, *Donnell*, *Bowen*, and *Adams Fruit* were available in the historical setting. Their exact recovery corrects possible overstatements of their reach; it does not change the law or the fees. *Marquette* supplies interstate permission after federal classification, *Daggs* the allowed-rate mechanism, and *Donnell* rejection of the percentage-overdraft penalty argument. None had decided the present flat-fee question. The current text-first public holdings do not supply the missing categorical exclusion, an established procedural defect, or a different prior agency policy. Different authorship of an earlier public opinion is not itself a changed premise.

## Final common propositions and exact joins

**R1 — Federal meaning and delegated responsibility.** All eight join. Federal law defines §85 interest; South Dakota supplies the allowed rate after that classification, with Citibank's location and permission for these charges undisputed. Section 85 leaves room on the disputed fee classification. The Comptroller's statutory enforcement responsibilities and qualified §93a rulemaking authority support authoritative interpretation of that substantive obligation. *Adams Fruit* does not convert §86's private remedy into exclusive judicial administration of all §85 questions. All statutory limits incorporated from the independent freeze remain, including §93a's express-and-exclusive-other-agency, §36 and Glass-Steagall exclusions and the conditions governing actual enforcement. No enforcement remedy is entered.

**R2 — Reasonable interpretation applied to the pleaded charges.** All eight join. Under the actual *NationsBank*/Chevron framework, the OCC reasonably includes these contractual credit-card late fees in interest. The distinction between charges for extension, availability or default on credit and specified reimbursements for other services is rational; it does not depend on establishing actual administrative loss. “Interest at the rate” does not compel a periodic percentage, proportionality to balance or time, or calculability at inception. Penalty purpose and continuing finance charges do not categorically exclude default charges. *Donnell* supports that limited penalty point, not an already adjudicated flat-fee holding; *Meilink*'s express statutory contrast between penalty and interest does not govern §85.

P2's account terms, accepted pleading allegations, limited use of *Daggs*, *Donnell* and *Marquette*, and exclusions of unrelated fees remain incorporated. The legal assessment is reasonableness within the statutory range, not an economic finding or an independent holding selecting the best reading. It resolves the Classic and Preferred charges actually challenged; it does not adopt every category or every possible application of §7.4001.

**R3 — Authoritative interpretation; two distinct answers about change.** All eight join both R3(a) and R3(b). Statutory age and litigation impetus do not categorically bar deference. The proposed and final published rule, its comment process and stated explanation distinguish this instrument from a bare unsupported litigating position; these facts are not certification of every statutory procedure.

**R3(a):** A changed interpretation is not invalid merely because it changed. A lawful, adequately explained change within delegated ambiguity can receive deference. Sudden unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious; consideration of agency reasons remains necessary. This answer independently defeats a categorical inconsistency objection, not every possible challenge to any changed policy.

**R3(b):** Independently, the two cited letters do not establish a contrary settled official policy displaced here. The 1964 informal statement and the 1988 deputy-counsel response differ in authority and context and conflict with each other on whether the category is national or state-defined. Their existence and contents are supplied neutral facts; the conclusion about their failure to establish binding settled guidance is a legal assessment. The rule's stated clarification of uncertainty supplies the actual agency explanation; no invented litigation rationale or newly found reliance facts substitute for it. R3(a) is not dropped merely because R3(b) also suffices to answer the prior-policy objection.

**R4 — Judicial preemption analysis and constitutional boundary.** All eight join. Assume without deciding that the question whether a statute preempts state law must always be decided de novo by courts. The agency here reasonably interprets a substantive term; its view does not itself supply a freestanding preemption power. *Marquette* establishes §85's interstate operation, and these federally classified, home-state-permitted fees cannot be prohibited through the conflicting application of California §1671. The particular federal permission, not unsupported empirical interference, supplies the consequence. Actual *Barnett* reinforces that bounded conclusion and preserves compatible state regulation. P3–P4's remaining limits survive: no general bank immunity, blanket invalidation of §1671, independent §24 disposition, California-1995-law alternative, or abstract Article I/IV decision. South Dakota is not defining federal interest.

**R5 — Antecedent transactions, with the historical qualification.** All eight join. The April 1, 1996 effective date is not backdated. In resolving the meaning of the governing statute for antecedent transactions that lacked clear prior agency guidance, the Court may use the agency's current authoritative interpretation. The no-clear-prior-guidance conclusion rests on R3(b); the mere general permissibility of changing policy in R3(a) would not by itself establish lawful retrospective use. If a later regulation displaced a clear earlier agency interpretation, the retroactivity objection could have substance; that materially different case is reserved. No universal rule permits retrospective legislative change, and no factual reliance finding is made. The historical footnote's account of how the objection was briefed or pursued is not imported as a present waiver or preservation ruling.

**R6 — Procedural/source limits and remedy.** All eight join. Section 43's complete trigger, notice-content and thirty-day-comment duties, consideration and final-publication requirements, and differently scoped exceptions remain incorporated verbatim in substance from the independent freeze. General notice and 112 comments do not establish every required fact. Interpretive form, litigation use, §93a authority, and the absence of clear prior guidance do not themselves establish a §43 exception or compliance. No finding settles applicability, satisfaction, violation, waiver, preservation, or an exception. In particular, R4's substantive/preemptive distinction is not a ruling that §43 can never apply to such an instrument.

The statutory-merits deference ground does not purport to adjudicate every potential independent challenge to rule validity. No such defect or independently dispositive procedural determination is established by these inputs; missing documentary proof supplies neither an alternative disposition nor a material changed premise for removing the historical ground. This is not a holding immunizing a demonstrated procedural violation, and no source-gap presumption is adopted in either direction.

Each Associate affirms the California Supreme Court's judgment sustaining judgment on the pleadings against the challenged California late-fee theory. No cost or market-effect hearing, agency-procedure remand, damages, restitution, injunction, class certification, or special mandate date is ordered. P1's detailed §85 alternatives, §86's separate remedies and predicates, all conditional enforcement qualifications, and the public rule's nonexclusive categories, ordinary exclusions and preserved state-law functions remain operative limits, not additional issues adjudicated.
