## Material corrections and their basis

**Thomas: change affirmance to vacatur/remand, joining the full R1–R4 allocation below.** His independent position extends his actual Thompson dissent's distinction between a legal definition and a concrete reasonable-person inference to probability assessments. That is a substantial argument, but it is not a new Justice-specific premise explaining departure from historical Ornelas. His historical Thompson dissent already emphasized fact-bound inference, trial-level comparative advantage, objective reasonableness's insufficiency to require independent review, and the limited force of uniformity. He nevertheless subsequently joined the historical Ornelas opinion in full.

The actual simulated Thompson dissent retains that competing classification argument **within §2254(d) habeas review**. It establishes neither a controlling presumption for direct federal criminal appeals nor a personal undertaking to defer on every mixed constitutional application. Its omission of historical Thompson's additional merits and Teague arguments changes no premise on which direct warrantless-search review depends. Different authorship of the Thompson majority and the absence of another Justice from the dissent are not substantive changed premises.

Historical Ornelas supplies strong evidence of Thomas's issue-specific resolution: independent appellate application gives national Fourth Amendment standards practical content, promotes consistent guidance and preserves appellate legal responsibility; historical findings and local expertise still receive their proper protection. Gates's warrant-issuance deference has a distinct institutional justification. Those reasons remain available in the actual law. First Options, Markman, Sheets and Pinder do not overturn them or settle every mixed question in favor of deference. Thomas's actual First Options, Markman and Pinder joins are compatible with this allocation; no Casarotto Part III join is attributed to him. His actual Thompson dissent remains intact in its own setting. The final correction follows the unchanged issue-specific premises, not an automatic historical vote rule.

**The due-weight qualification is real.** Historical Ornelas expressly directs due weight to inferences drawn by resident judges and local law-enforcement officers, describes contextual inferences as deserving deference, and identifies the trial court's assessment that an officer was credible and his inference reasonable, 517 U.S. at 699–700. The independent freeze's categorical phrase “no direct appellate deference to police” must not erase that qualification. Preserve its valid objection to automatic police correctness, while stating positively that grounded experience and local knowledge receive genuine due weight in the independent assessment.

This is neither a presumption that the search was lawful nor clear-error protection for the ultimate constitutional answer. Nor is it merely a direction to notice and then categorically disregard the trial-level assessment. The appellate court must account for the supported inferential advantage while itself determining constitutional sufficiency. No new source or changed premise supports weakening that common historical qualification for any of the seven final vacatur commitments.

## Final propositions and exact joins

| Proposition | Precise allocation or disposition | Exact Associate joins |
|---|---|---|
| **R0 — review and question** | §§1291 and 1254(1), following the source-supported conditional pleas, permit this review. Parties' agreement does not decide the standard. Preserve independent ultimate constitutional judgment under R1. | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| **R3 — functional and warrant limits** | Independent applications develop and maintain national constitutional boundaries even though many fact patterns provide limited precedent. Mixed-question labels alone decide nothing. Preserve Gates's distinct substantial-basis review of warrant issuance and its warrant preference; do not transplant it to this retrospective nonwarrant review. | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| **R4 — appellate remand** | Vacate both reviewed Court of Appeals judgments, 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, and remand for that court to apply R1–R3 to both ultimate determinations. No suppression outcome or additional evidentiary hearing is ordered. | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| **D1 — deferential alternative** | Affirm the reviewed allocation: independently correct legal definitions and legal-rule errors, but defer to the district court's concrete reasonable-suspicion/probable-cause applications under the circuit's clear-error formulation. Institutional advantage and limited law-clarifying benefit favor that allocation. No direct presumption favoring officers or warrant-issuance test is substituted. | Scalia only |

All eight preserve ordinary historical-fact and credibility protection and correction of legal error. Scalia does **not** join R2 as part of a de novo/due-weight combination: he regards that combination as unstable and favors D1. Historical footnote 3 calls “abuse of discretion” the preferable label for the rejected deferential **mixed-question** standard and reserves “clear error” as the factual-review term. That terminology does not create an intermediate majority rule or change the operative contrast with D1's ultimate deference.

R2 gives no numerical weight, officer-credibility preference or special evidentiary burden. Whether the panel was loose is a historical finding; the grounded significance an experienced officer draws from that fact deserves due weight; whether the whole established account justifies the challenged intrusion remains independently decided. Experience cannot establish an unproved observation, validate a database identification, revive discredited testimony or convert every expert conclusion into a protected factual finding.
