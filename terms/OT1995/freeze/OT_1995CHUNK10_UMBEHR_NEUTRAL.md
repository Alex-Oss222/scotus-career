# Board of County Commissioners, Wabaunsee County v. Umbehr — frozen neutral packet

**Stage:** Fresh neutral validation, commitment-free.
**Event:** No.94-1654; argued November28, 1995; merits decision group June28, 1996.
**Channel and scope:** §1254(1) review of the Tenth Circuit, 44 F.3d876, January4, 1995, rehearing denied February10. The question is protection against retaliatory termination of an existing contractor relationship for public criticism, with government interests, causation and defenses kept separate.

## Corrected record and procedural frame

The existing 1985 renewable arrangement followed a 1981 contract. The county **did not itself buy trash-hauling services**. Cities could endorse and ratify the agreement, and were not required to participate. They could leave on ninety days' notice. The agreement renewed yearly unless a party gave sixty days' termination notice or ninety days' notice to renegotiate. County and participating cities promised not to engage another residential-refuse provider while it operated. Do not convert the agreement into an at-will direct procurement contract.

Umbehr served six of seven cities, criticized landfill rates, public-record charges, asserted open-meetings violations and road/bridge management at meetings and in newspapers. His accusations are speech; their underlying truth was not adjudicated. An attempted 1990 termination failed and the contract renewed. January1991 termination followed another two-to-one vote. Heiser and Spencer supported termination. McClure opposed the earlier action and was out of office by effective termination. Umbehr later contracted directly with five of six served cities; those arrangements do not conclusively negate injury from loss of exclusivity, coordinated negotiation or notice protections.

For summary judgment the district court **assumed**, without trial findings, employee-equivalent speech protection, motivating retaliation and injury. It entered judgment on categorical contractor status and alternatively personal immunity; McClure separately prevailed on causation. The Tenth Circuit rejected categorical constitutional exclusion and remanded the surviving official-capacity claims, while affirming personal immunity and McClure's disposition. The current review does not reopen those latter judgments. Standing is supported below by asserted monetary loss and valuable contractual benefits; no property entitlement is needed merely to state the speech theory.

The source-specific lower opinion corrects the section-I generic “no conflict” line. There is an acknowledged circuit conflict, including the Seventh Circuit's contractor approach. That makes review intelligible but does not decide the proper extension.

## Strong competing legal positions and supported alternatives

**Commissioners' position:** Contract form, the contractor's operational independence and other clients, government responsibility for services, and retained termination power can justify a different relationship than public employment. Extending employee litigation can burden effective administration. A narrower alternative would retain the government's actual service-interest showing and Mt. Healthy defense. None of those policy submissions establishes the actual motive for this termination.

**Umbehr's position:** He remains a citizen subject to economic retaliation for criticism; lack of tenure or employment cannot alone make a retaliatory condition lawful. Contractors may lack many workplace disruption concerns. He seeks stronger protection for viewpoint retaliation or, at least, employee-derived protection. His proposed remand limited to motive/independent reason is advocacy, not an already adjudicated finding that the speech satisfies every applicable test.

Independent paths include:
1. Affirm the Tenth Circuit's rejection of categorical exclusion and adopt an appropriately explained Pickering analogy, preserving public concern, genuine governmental interests, causation and a preponderance same-decision defense for proceedings below. Do not declare liability on assumed summary-judgment facts.
2. Protect this existing relationship on a narrower unconstitutional-conditions basis while reserving a universal employee/contractor equivalence; specify the operative standard actually adopted rather than leaving lower courts an unexplained protection.
3. Accept a materially stronger speech-protection rule proposed by the contractor only if its departure from ordinary government-service balancing and its scope are justified. Do not silently treat the appellate rule as already that stronger rule.
4. Sustain the categorical contractor distinction, or a different lawful governmental-control ground within the presented scope, after confronting Perry, the valuable benefits lost, and the actual arrangement. A policy concern about initial bidding does not transform this case into an unsuccessful-new-bidder case.
5. Resolve a genuinely supported threshold issue if necessary; existing appellate standing and the assumed injury must be confronted rather than replaced by “five cities remained, therefore no injury.”

Any remand preserves personal immunity and McClure's separate judgment, ordinary capacity/attribution and damages requirements, and unresolved actual factual issues. It orders no automatic renewal, reinstatement, damages amount, complete immunity or trial. Neutral performance and service-based grounds remain available if actually proved. Initial competitive applicants, every future contracting decision and pure affiliation patronage are distinct scope questions; reservations decide none of them.

## Umbehr, No.94-1654

### Jurisdiction, judgment and actual contract

The petition invokes 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) after a §1983 action within 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1343 and an appeal under §1291. The Tenth Circuit judgment was entered January 4, 1995, with rehearing denied February 10. Petition pp.i–ii and 1–2 identifies the petitioners as Heiser and Spencer in their official capacities as county commissioners. The pending merits review does not reopen the individual-capacity immunity judgment or McClure's separate causation judgment.

The full appellate opinion, 44 F.3d 876, establishes these particulars:

- The operative 1985 contract, succeeding a 1981 arrangement, allowed Umbehr to haul residential refuse for cities that endorsed and ratified it. The county did not itself purchase trash-hauling services under that contract. Cities were not obliged to participate and could opt out on ninety days' notice. The agreement automatically renewed for one-year terms unless a party gave sixty days' notice of termination or ninety days' notice of an intention to renegotiate. The county and participating cities agreed not to contract with another residential-refuse provider while the contract operated. These are distinct notice provisions, not immediate termination at whim.
- Umbehr served six of seven cities from 1985 to 1991. He criticized landfill rates, charges for public records, possible Open Meetings Act violations, and alleged road-and-bridge mismanagement in commission meetings and newspapers. Those criticisms are described as allegations and speech; the courts did not adjudicate their truth in resolving this motion.
- The attempted 1990 termination was ineffective, and the contract renewed. The valid January 1991 termination followed another two-to-one vote. Heiser and Spencer supported termination; McClure opposed the earlier action and was no longer in office at the effective termination. Umbehr later made direct contracts with five of the six cities; the county made no replacement hauling contract. The successor arrangements do not by themselves establish zero injury.

The district court **assumed solely for summary judgment** that the speech would have been protected had Umbehr been an employee, that the comments motivated the termination votes, and that he suffered injury. It did not find those propositions after trial. It granted judgment on a categorical contractor-status ground and, alternatively, individual qualified immunity. It also found insufficient evidence of McClure's causation. The Tenth Circuit rejected the categorical constitutional bar, applied Pickering to contractor retaliation, affirmed individual immunity and McClure's disposition, and remanded the surviving official-capacity claims. The appellate court separately held standing supported by alleged monetary harm and loss of valuable contract benefits, including exclusivity, centralized negotiation and notice protection; a property entitlement is not a prerequisite to the speech claim. See 44 F.3d 877–879, 883–884 and nn.1–3, 6.

The lower court did not find damages, impose liability, or direct a particular award or reinstatement. Its opinion notes that damages may be small and difficult to prove. Personal legislative immunity and official-capacity liability are distinct: the appellate opinion invokes Kentucky v. Graham's entity-based treatment of the latter and does not allow individual immunities to become entity immunities by changing the caption.

### Actual competing submissions and record limits

Petition pp.i–ii asks whether Pickering protection extends to terminable government contracts, how the test should be adapted, and whether government affirmative defenses survive. Petitioners' merits brief pp.12–20 distinguishes retrospective criticism from patronage affiliation and broad prospective speech bans. It emphasizes the government's retained responsibility for services, the contractor's day-to-day independence, and termination as an important means of control. It invokes Mt. Healthy to oppose an automatic tenure rule and argues that the employee/contractor distinction can matter to managerial needs. Its assertion that protection makes services ungovernable is an argument, not a record finding.

Respondent's merits brief pp.13–19 argues that an independent contractor remains a citizen protected against economic retaliation, seeks demanding review of viewpoint-based retaliation, and argues that ordinary workplace disruption interests may be absent outside an employment setting. He asks for affirmance and a remand limited to motive and legitimate independent reasons, contending the speech is protected under any applicable standard. That is a litigant's requested limitation on remand, not a finding already supplied by the lower judgment. The difference between his stricter proposal and the appellate Pickering rule is material.

The commissioners' petition also describes disputes over landfill charges and related state litigation. Its description of arrearages or misconduct investigations is advocacy and does not establish an actual reason for the challenged votes. Motive, public-concern characterization, operational effects, same-decision proof and injury must retain their separate source status. Possession of a possible lawful ground is not proof that the decision would have occurred without protected speech.

### Conflict and available legal issues for fresh validation

The brief's “no material conflict” statement is inaccurate. The Tenth Circuit expressly acknowledged conflict with the Seventh Circuit's LaFalce/Triad/Downtown Auto Parks approach and decisions from other circuits. It rejected generalized assumptions that contractors always depend less on government than employees and considered Rutan's extension to lesser employment benefits material. Its analysis also distinguishes public-issue retaliation from political affiliation. This conflict and the scope of an existing renewing relationship, rather than initial competitive bids, must be preserved. A narrow remand retaining ordinary speech, causation and government-interest defenses is a supported litigation possibility; so are the parties' opposing threshold and stricter-protection positions. No result is assigned here.

# Exact Waters standard

### Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

**Current rule:** An employer acting on a genuine, reasonable account of speech that would be disciplinable under the governing public-employee-speech rules is protected even if that factual account is mistaken. Conversely, an unreasonable and incorrect account does not defeat a claim when the employee actually spoke protected speech and that speech caused the adverse action. The employee must show protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may establish by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech. These limited propositions do not impose liability for every investigation defect or adopt the plurality’s complete investigative procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- Seven-Justice genuine-and-reasonable-account safe harbor: [Waters v. Churchill — A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md#public-projection), 1994-05-31. O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.
- Six-Justice unreasonable-and-false-account limit: [Waters v. Churchill — An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md#public-projection), 1994-05-31. O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.
- Motive and same-decision proof: [Waters v. Churchill — The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md#public-projection), 1994-05-31. O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Present operation effective:** Waters v. Churchill, 1994-05-31.

**Open question:** Unreasonable belief where the actual speech was unprotected, and a universal investigation checklist, are not resolved. The four-Justice complete reasonableness framework is noncontrolling.

### Prospective restrictions on employees’ citizen expression

**Governing question:** Justification of a broad advance restriction on employees’ compensated speech as citizens.

**Current rule:** Under Pickering, the Government must supply a justification commensurate with the actual reach and burden of a broad prospective restriction on employees’ future compensated citizen expression, including the burden on listeners. A general concern about misuse of office does not by itself justify prohibiting protected expression unrelated to employment; the required connection must be adequately established. Narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Authority by component:**

- A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden: [A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_National_Treasury_Employees_Union_merits_1995-02-22.md#public-projection), 1995-02-22. Justice Stevens's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and explanation. Justice O'Connor agrees with invalidity for unrelated expression on her separate, case-specific Pickering ground; her vote is not counted for this prospective-burden formulation.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-02-22; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This prospective-burden rule does not constitutionalize every payment or decide materially different senior-employee settings. The existing honoraria ban’s classwide remedy has its own five-Justice statutory-remedial ground; it creates no universal injunction for unrepresented employees or judicially rewritten employment-nexus exception.
