## Original request and distinct claims

The petition's pp.1–2 invokes **28 U.S.C. §§2241, 2254(a), and 1651(a)**. It asks for a stay so the Court may consider and grant original habeas relief and conduct appellate review of the Eleventh Circuit action. Its explanation under Rule 20.4 is that authorization to proceed in district court was refused. It alleges state exhaustion through Georgia's May 2 action; exhaustion is an assertion to assess, not a new finding by this preparation.

1. **Reasonable-doubt claim, petition pp.10–21.** Felker attacks the final guilt instructions' use of “moral certainty,” together with the prosecutor's related voir-dire questions. He claims the combined language diluted proof beyond a reasonable doubt under *Winship* and *Cage*. He invokes the retroactivity of *Cage*, argues that the claim became available only when *Sullivan* demonstrated retroactivity, and argues that a prima facie authorization inquiry must not become final adverse merits adjudication. He alleges that his attempted first federal filing preceded *Sullivan*. The Eleventh Circuit responds that *Cage* existed before the 1993 first petition, that *Sullivan* preceded formal docketing, and that an amendment remained possible before the August 6, 1993 answer. This is a dispute about legal availability and filing/amendment history, not a finding that the trial charge is new evidence.
2. **Trial-process/evidence constitutional claim, petition pp.21–32.** He contends that permitting state crime-laboratory employee Warren Tillman, a nonphysician, to supply the asserted critical time-of-death testimony denied due process and Eighth Amendment reliability. The petition describes this as a claim warranting a newly announced constitutional rule of a fundamental kind; it is distinct from an assertion that an already-announced new Supreme Court rule authorizes a successive petition. It relies on *Ake*, *Ford*, and *Eddings* and asserts that deficient forensic work undermined the conviction. It does not make every disagreement with an expert independently sufficient for relief.
3. **Independent innocence claim, petition pp.21–31.** He expressly asserts that he is innocent of murder and that executing him would violate the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments. He presents this as substantive habeas relief and also as a reason AEDPA is unconstitutional if it precludes that relief. This claim must not disappear into the procedural innocence gateway. The petition cites historical *Herrera*; only the actual simulated *Herrera* rule may govern.
4. **Innocence as gateway.** The same medical evidence is also offered to overcome procedural barriers to the independent trial-constitutional claims. The lower opinion examines that gateway separately from a possible freestanding innocence claim. Actual simulated *Schlup*, the new statutory conditions, and a freestanding claim are distinct inquiries with different requirements; no conclusion in one automatically establishes the others.
5. **Statutory/constitutional challenges.** The original petition challenges the review bar, prima facie requirement and “previously unavailable” language, compelled state-court deference, and asserted restrictions on new or innocence claims under Article III/separation of powers, the Supremacy Clause, and the cited Amendments. ## Statutory distinctions and jurisdiction sources

- **§2244(b)(1):** a claim presented in a second or successive §2254 application that was presented in a prior application shall be dismissed.
- **§2244(b)(2):** a new claim in such an application shall be dismissed unless **(A)** it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral cases **by the Supreme Court**, **or (B)** both due-diligence unavailability of the factual predicate **and** facts which, if proven and viewed with all evidence, establish by **clear and convincing evidence** that **but for constitutional error** no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. The two requirements of (B) are cumulative. A circuit holding of retroactivity is not the statute's stated Supreme Court condition.
- **§2244(b)(3)(A)–(D):** before filing in **district court**, apply to the appropriate court of appeals; a three-judge panel acts; authorization requires a prima facie showing; the court shall act within 30 days. **(E)** makes authorization grants/denials not appealable and not subject to rehearing petitions or certiorari petitions. **(4)** separately requires the district court to dismiss an authorized claim unless the applicant establishes the statutory requirements. Authorization does not finally determine entitlement.
- **§2241(a)–(b):** expressly authorizes the Supreme Court and its Justices, district courts, and circuit judges to grant writs within their jurisdictions; the Supreme Court/Justice/circuit judge may decline and transfer to a district court with jurisdiction. Title I leaves the text of §2241 untouched. Distinguish this original-application source from §1254 certiorari review and from §2244(b)(3)(E)'s gatekeeping-review bar; preserving a source of jurisdiction does not itself decide the substantive successive restrictions or extraordinary-writ discretion.
- **§2254(b), (d), (e):** the enacted exhaustion alternatives (available state remedies exhausted, absence of process, or ineffectiveness), permission to deny unexhausted claims on merits, express-only state waiver, state-merits adjudication conditions, presumption of factual correctness, and failed-development hearing restrictions have separate triggers. - **§2254(a)** remains the federal-law custody predicate. **§2242/Rule 20.4(a)** requires reasons for not applying to the district court; the petition must state how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, and show exceptional circumstances warranting this Court's discretionary power and absence of adequate relief in another form or court. - **FRAP 22(a)** as amended directs applications to district court and transfers circuit-judge applications; it does not expressly name the Supreme Court in that transfer direction. Constitutional Article III appellate character and §2241's statutory direct-filing channel should not be confused with Article III original jurisdiction limited to enumerated cases.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: [Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Landgraf_v_USI_Film_Products_merits_1994-04-26.md#public-projection), 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule.
