## Questions, record and lower judgment

The questions are whether Rule 4 supersedes SAA §742's requirement that the libelant serve the United States “forthwith”; alternatively, whether the court can extend that statutory service period in appropriate circumstances. The Government additionally defends the application of “forthwith” to this record. Henderson seeks reversal and remand for trial; the Government seeks affirmance. Removing the service dismissal would establish no injury liability or automatic entitlement to trial without ordinary further proceedings.

Henderson alleges an injury on or about August 27, 1991 aboard the United States-owned DELMONTE; the sources contain conflicting incidental date recitations, not a resolved merits finding. Following disallowance of his administrative claim, he filed April 8, 1993, invoking SAA/PVA. The Government maintains that the Clarification Act places this claim under SAA and that PVA would incorporate the same service provision. The injury claims, negligence, unseaworthiness, maintenance/cure and damages remain unadjudicated.

The Attorney General received the mailed complaint and summons May 25, day 47. Service upon the United States Attorney occurred September 3, day 148, after the initial 120 days but within the district court's **fifteen-day** extension. The extension was signed August 31 and filed September 1; it contains no express finding of good cause, diligence or clerk fault. Counsel's asserted problems with forms, seals and mail support his explanation; the Government disputes their sufficiency and his initial diligence. Neither characterization is adopted as a finding. The later September 9 delivery at DOJ supplies no earlier United States Attorney service. The “150 days” recitations do not supersede the computed 148 days.

The Government's dismissal motions invoke §742, not a distinct Rule 4 defect, and describe service as properly effected September 3. Henderson's December 16 district response also asserted that the Government's earlier venue-transfer motion waived a service/personal-jurisdiction objection. The actual motion and complete appellate papers are unavailable in the recovered record. That theory was raised below, but appellate preservation and the transfer motion's legal effect are not established; a nonjurisdictional characterization alone does not establish Rule 12 waiver.

The district court first denied dismissal, then followed Holmberg and dismissed without prejudice under Rule 12(b)(1) in June 1994. The Fifth Circuit affirmed: it treated “forthwith” as a waiver condition and jurisdictional prerequisite unaffected by a Rule 4 extension; found 148 days not forthwith even assuming good cause and a reasonableness approach; and applied forthwith to **both** designated recipients. It did not decide whether day-47 Attorney General service independently satisfied the statute. It did not reach due process because Henderson had not argued there that refiling would be time-barred. His later assertion that limitations had expired does not establish a separately presented constitutional question; “without prejudice” does not establish timely refiling.

## Statutory and rule snapshot

The [1920 Act](../sources/A_Henderson_SAA_41Stat525_1920.txt) and [1960 amendment](../sources/A_Henderson_SAA_amend_74Stat912_1960.txt) control over variant brief quotations. Section 742's amended first sentence permits an appropriate nonjury in-personam proceeding where the specified privately owned/operated vessel, privately owned/possessed cargo, or private person or property would support an admiralty proceeding, against the United States or a corporation mentioned in §1. The 1960 amendment applies to proceedings brought after enactment. Venue is in the district where a suing party resides or has its principal place of business, or where the charged vessel/cargo is found. The service sentences provide:

> The libelant shall forthwith serve a copy of his libel on the United States attorney for such district and mail a copy thereof by registered mail to the Attorney General of the United States, and shall file a sworn return of such service and mailing. Such service and mailing shall constitute valid service on the United States and such corporation.

The remainder permits a cross-libel or setoff when the United States or covered corporation sues, and discretionary transfer on either party's application. The present dispute does not establish a separate division-based venue defect or present a registered-versus-certified-mail dispute.

Section 743 directs private-party principles and practice. Its other terms separately permit costs and specified money-judgment interest, preserve admiralty appeal/revision, allow an elected in-rem-principles proceeding without excluding proper in-personam relief, excuse government bonds/stipulations, and provide for cancellation of qualifying prior security on the Government's assumption of liability. The practice sentence does not erase those distinct provisions by itself. [Section 745 and amendment notes](../sources/A_Henderson_46USCApp745_LII.txt) supply the two-year commencement period, exclusivity against responsible federal agents/employees where an SAA remedy exists, the narrowly dated one-year-after-December-13-1950 savings provision for specified wrong-defendant cases, and the prohibition on pre-suit interest after June 30, 1932 unless an express contract provides it. That old savings clause creates no present general refiling extension. The source is a later archived codification with notes showing no amendment after 1950, rather than a recovered official 1994 volume.

Rule 3 commences an action by filing. Section 2072(a) authorizes general practice/procedure/evidence rules for the designated federal courts. Section 2072(b) prohibits abridging, enlarging or modifying substantive rights and makes conflicting laws ineffective after the rules take effect. Rule 82 forbids construing the Rules to extend or limit district-court jurisdiction or venue and retains its Rule 9(h)/§§1391–1393 admiralty venue qualification. None of those texts itself decides how to characterize this particular service direction.

Congress itself enacted former Rule 4(j) in [Pub. L. 97-462](../sources/chunk7_a/frcp1982_act.txt), effective 45 days after January 12, 1983. It provides:

> If a service of the summons and complaint is not made upon a defendant within 120 days after the filing of the complaint and the party on whose behalf such service was required cannot show good cause why such service was not made within that period, the action shall be dismissed as to that defendant without prejudice upon the court's own initiative with notice to such party or upon motion. This subdivision shall not apply to service in a foreign country pursuant to subdivision (i) of this rule.

Section 5 prevented the separately delayed 1982 proposals from taking effect. The Government accepts direct congressional enactment and treats §2072(b) as evidence of congressional intent while disputing its technical application to “such rules.” That concession is not a concession of supersession.

**Transition source gap resolved.** This validator recovered the [official April 22, 1993 order and complete amended Rule 4](../sources/chunk7_a/rule4_1993_official_order_and_text.txt), with a [bounded official PDF](../sources/chunk7_a/rule4_1993_official_order_and_text.pdf), from [507 U.S. 1091, 1103–1110](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/507bv.pdf). The order makes the amendments effective December 1, 1993 for newly commenced civil cases and, “insofar as just and practicable,” proceedings in then-pending civil cases. This supersedes the source receipts' statement that the original transition order remained unrecovered.

Former 4(j) governed the April–September 1993 conduct. Amended 4(m) says that, absent service within 120 days, the court on motion or its own initiative after notice shall dismiss without prejudice **or direct service within a specified time**; if good cause is shown, it **shall extend** for an appropriate period. It excepts foreign-country service under (f) or (j)(1). The [1993 subdivision (m) note](../sources/chunk7_a/rule4_1993_notes.txt) confirms discretionary relief without good cause and identifies a limitations bar as a possible reason; that is permission, not compelled relief. The new rule's potential application to the 1994 dismissal and future proceedings is distinct from retroactively relabeling the 1993 order. Any use must apply the just-and-practicable qualification. No transition rule establishes a new Rule 4 objection, requires a new good-cause hearing, or supplies a finding absent below.

## Governing law and supported paths

The opening snapshot's **Federal sovereign immunity from affirmative bankruptcy money judgments**, present operation effective *Nordic Village*, February 25, 1992, requires clear consent to the actual monetary remedy and distinguishes consent from general jurisdiction/property labels. Its original §106(c) holding does not classify every procedural direction in every waiver statute as jurisdictional. *McNeil* enforces §2675(a)'s actual prefiling exhaustion sequence, including its six-month election and specified third-party exceptions. *Keene* enforces §1500's particular filing-time jurisdictional bar. Their operative limitations remain distinct from postfiling service here.

*Pioneer* permits excusable-neglect relief only where the particular Rule authorizes it; counsel attribution, all relevant circumstances and the specific enlargement exceptions remain. Its bankruptcy rule is neither the former good-cause test nor an SAA tolling rule. *Landgraf* requires attention first to an express transition direction and then to the actual legal consequences; it does not authorize automatic reopening of completed acts.

Actual current-term *Carlisle* (April 29) treats the combined Rules 29/45 deadline as a limitation on the requested motion remedy, not criminal subject-matter jurisdiction, and rejects general inherent authority to perform the same act an applicable specific rule forecloses. Its preservation of genuinely authorized procedural discretion, pending-rule qualifications and unresolved forfeiture scenarios must accompany any analogy. May 13 *Armstrong* likewise distinguishes a particular Rule's nonauthorization from the separate possibility of supported constitutional authority; it creates no civil-service remedy. May 13 *Noland* preserves both compatible statutory functions and lawful discretion within their terms; *Brown Group* distinguishes statutory authorization, constitutional requirements and separate remedy predicates. None independently determines §742. *44 Liquormart* supplies no material service rule. These are actual May 13 entering decisions, not merely an earlier March cutoff.

The supported alternatives are:

1. **Procedural supersession.** Henderson's *Hanna/Murphree* argument distinguishes service implementing an existing waiver from alteration of subject-matter jurisdiction. Uniform civil/admiralty procedure, §743 and the affirmative service scheme support displacement if an actual conflict is found. Congress's direct adoption of former 4(j) supplies an additional statutory-intent route requiring its own coexistence analysis.
2. **Coexistence without a jurisdiction holding.** The Government argues that an outer dismissal deadline gives no affirmative entitlement to wait 120 days, so compliance with the more prompt specific statute and the Rule is possible. Its *Walker* distinction and specific/general argument may support affirmance without declaring the direction jurisdictional. A characterization as procedural alone does not answer this path.
3. **Waiver-condition jurisdiction.** The Government independently invokes the statutory location, *Sherwood/Mitchell* consent principles, §2072(b) and Rule 82. Henderson answers that procedural implementation of consent is separate from the grant itself. The status of this actual direction must be resolved; *Nordic Village*, *McNeil* and *Keene* cannot do that by label.
4. **Surviving statute with qualified extension/reasonableness.** Henderson invokes *American Pipe* and circuit *McCormick* tolling by analogy and proposes reading Rule 4 with §742. The Government disputes transferring another limitation's tolling rule and argues the accepted declarations still show insufficient dispatch. “Forthwith” is not an established absolute 24-hour rule. Extension authority, actual sufficiency and the existing order are separate questions. If factual or discretionary work remains, a bounded remand must identify it without presuming good cause, lack of diligence or an independent Rule 4 defect.
5. **Preservation-dependent narrower route.** The district Rule 12 waiver contention may become relevant if the statutory direction is nonjurisdictional. It cannot support a final appellate waiver holding without resolving preservation and the actual triggering motion. That limited gap does not block the independently presented supersession/coexistence/extension questions. The separate due-process claim is not presented for a constitutional merits disposition.

Every route must answer the lower court's both-recipient construction and actual delay to the extent necessary. Reversal of the §742 dismissal does not automatically adjudicate a new Rule 4 defense; affirmance without prejudice does not promise a viable new action. No finding of actual governmental prejudice, waiver, clear clerk fault or injury liability is supplied.
