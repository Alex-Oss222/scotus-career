## Melendez, No. 95-5661, June 17

Historical judgment: affirmance, 7–2. Thomas's full opinion is joined by Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six). O'Connor and Breyer join Parts I–II only (eight at that level), dissenting from the disposition. Stevens concurs in the judgment alone. Souter also writes separately while retaining his full formal join.

The shared statutory premise is that section 3553(e) requires governmental authorization to go below the statutory floor and the Commission cannot dispense with that enacted condition. The Court expressly does not require two separate papers or a citation of section 3553(e): what matters is the request or authorization in substance. The actual motion limits the requested departure to the Guidelines range. The opinion declines the belated plea-agreement argument and does not decide an unraised unconstitutional refusal or independent safety-valve entitlement.

Part III distinguishes a possible Commission rule conditioning all Guidelines assistance departures on authorization below an applicable statutory minimum. The Court does not decide whether the Commission could create that system; it holds the existing Guidelines do not do so. Its first reason also preserves the absence of governmental authorization in this case. Do not misdescribe the dissent as giving the Commission power to override a statute.

Souter views both sentences of section 5K1.1 as addressing Guidelines departures; the Application Notes, with policy-statement force under section 1B1.7, extend those standards to authorized statutory departures and discharge section 994(n). His disagreement concerns the majority's sentence-by-sentence characterization, not the governmental motion condition or result.

Stevens thinks the Commission intended a unitary system, but Congress did not authorize one that converts a Guidelines-only request into authorization below the statutory floor. Congress's choice of a floor above a Commission sentence confirms prosecutorial, not Commission, control of that exception. He affirms on section 3553(e) without joining the Court's contrary reading of Commission intent.

Breyer, joined by O'Connor, agrees the Commission cannot alter Congress's command but reads its own Guidelines and commentary as conditioning a Guidelines departure on the same sort of motion that would authorize a statutory departure. Identical assistance language, commentary, delegated authority and Rule 35(b)'s analogous system support his construction. He would remand to decide whether the actual motion sufficed for both departures or was insufficient for either. This is a remand position, not an automatic below-minimum sentence.

## Leavitt, No. 95-1242, June 17

Historical published action: grant limited to severability, summary reversal and remand, 5–4. The per curiam has Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's support; Stevens dissents, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This identifies published support for the disposition, not an independently disclosed private certiorari poll. The petition's other question and the current timeline's review scope must be modeled separately.

The per curiam applies Utah's legislative-intent severability rule. Section 317's express independent-enactment language is stronger than the ordinary savings clauses in the cited Utah cases; sections 302(2) and (3) can operate independently, and a cross-reference does not itself establish dependence. The relevant choice after invalidation is the remaining provision or none, not the legislature's preference for the whole original enactment. Section 317.2's reenactment contingency is distinct from severability. The opinion does not decide that every application of section 302(3) is constitutional. Its statutory quotation includes the pre-abortion police-report conditions for rape and incest and the actual life, grave-health-damage and grave-fetal-defect exceptions.

Stevens's dissent concerns certiorari restraint on a state-law question, the limited national role and precedential effect of a federal state-law decision, and the 1980 removal of state-law error as a listed review consideration. He distinguishes Wichita Royalty's Erie setting and treats Steele as an exceptional old example. The four do not thereby adopt every lower-court merits ruling or supply a definitive construction of the abortion restrictions. Actual Casey, the present petition record and the admitted 1996 Utah enactment must be compared rather than importing this historical summary action.

## Gray, No. 95-6510, June 20

Historical judgment: vacate and remand, with a five-Justice opinion by Rehnquist joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens files a solo dissent and also joins Ginsburg's dissent; Souter and Breyer join Ginsburg. The four seek reversal restoring sentencing relief. A shorthand 'affirmed 5–4' is wrong. The published dissents do not establish a separate nine-person Brady vote.

The Court rejects the standalone Brady claim on state procedural default: unpresented in state court, facts available at the first state petition, section 8.01-654(B)(2) barring a successive claim, and no demonstrated cause/prejudice. Those are historical adjudications that require comparison with the actual packet, not neutral stipulations established by citation alone. It separately finds ambiguity concerning presentation of a prosecutorial-misrepresentation claim and remands that claim, including whether the Commonwealth preserved its defenses. It treats the notice-of-evidence claim as seeking a new rule at 1987 finality, distinguishes Gardner's secret information from disclosed testimony, relies on Weatherford and the absence of a formal continuance request, and rejects the watershed exception. It does not abolish Teague's two contemporary exceptions.

Ginsburg's four-Justice dissent treats the case as application of the established right to a meaningful opportunity to meet capital sentencing information. The prosecutor's statements-only assurances caused justified reliance; the sudden photographic/forensic case prevented useful investigation and adversarial testing. Gardner, the older meaningful-hearing authorities and the findings support an old-rule application, not a general discovery deadline. She rejects splitting that injury into artificial notice/deception categories, distinguishes Weatherford by the source of the assurance, capital setting and demonstrated prejudice, and would restore the sentencing writ. Her note 11 acknowledges no formal continuance motion, emphasizing the actual preparation objection and absence of a cited Virginia rule making that form indispensable. Note 8 acknowledges the district court's Brady default ruling; it is not a merits Brady holding or a separate express join to the Court's disposition.

Stevens additionally regards the weak evidence linking Gray to the uncharged Sorrell murders as fundamentally unfair capital proof even with timely notice. That additional view is his alone, not Ginsburg's four-Justice ground or a finding that the acquitted/uncharged conduct rule generally changes.
