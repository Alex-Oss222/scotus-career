## Law entering the issue

**Enacted first-petition process.** Pre-AEDPA §2254(a) restricts the federal writ to custody violating federal law; subsections (b)–(c) retain exhaustion and the absence/ineffectiveness qualifications. The then-effective factual-presumption provisions are §2254(d)–(f), not later statutory deference. Section 2243 requires the prescribed initial action unless nonentitlement appears and directs disposition as law and justice require. Rule 4 requires prompt judicial examination; plain nonentitlement calls for summary dismissal and notice, otherwise an answer, other pleading or other appropriate judicial action. Neither statute nor Rule 4 guarantees an evidentiary hearing, an answer in every case, or a writ. The Rules structure the exercise of habeas judgment; “law and justice” does not itself authorize ignoring their operative conditions.

**Delay and repetition.** Rule 9(a) permits dismissal for delay prejudicing the State's ability to respond, unless the petitioner shows that reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. There is no five-year presumption; Congress deleted it. Disappointment of an execution date and generalized finality costs do not themselves establish the specified response prejudice. Rule 9(b)'s prior-merits/new-ground/abuse predicates and §2244(b)'s qualified subsequent-application permission are distinct. Caption counts, past next-friend litigation, or the bare label “abuse” establish neither set of predicates.

**Stay authority.** Section 2251 says “may” and does not suspend State action upon filing alone. It applies before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal to covered proceedings against the detained person concerning a matter involved in the pending habeas proceeding. The statute's no-stay consequence establishes the effect of pendency alone, not an answer to every question about necessary protection of required judicial process.

The operative public Standards remain:

- **Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar**, effective *Gomez*, April 21, 1992: independent consideration of the State's enforcement interest and unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim, with the seriousness of the supported claim and irreversibility also considered. It is neither an automatic late-claim bar nor an automatic stay. Four prior petitions describe that record, not a numerical trigger. *Delo*, *Barefoot* and *McCleskey* retain their respective domains.
- **Capital habeas counsel before a merits petition and discretionary stays**, effective *McFarland*, June 30, 1994: the statutory preparation proceeding and a reasonable preparation opportunity receive protection, but a stay is discretionary, neither automatic nor indefinite; proved inexcusable disregard of the opportunity and obstruction may justify denial. The counsel predicates and the preparation posture are not findings about Lonchar.
- **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims**, effective *Schlup*, January 23, 1995: the demanding probability gateway and whole-probative-record inquiry remain; a legal error requires the bounded reassessment actually ordered there, not an automatic merits proceeding. Section 2244(b)'s two conditions and clear, particular State abuse pleading remain distinct.
- **Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims**, effective *Sawyer*, June 22, 1992: clear and convincing proof concerning death eligibility remains a different gateway. Neither innocence standard is an entrance requirement imposed on every first petitioner.

**Barefoot's actual triggers.** The official report, 463 U.S. 880–938, was read in full, including every writing and footnote. Pages 888–894 require meaningful treatment of an initial habeas appeal once a certificate of probable cause issues; where execution would moot that review and time prevents considered disposition, a stay is required. Adequate notice and expedited, simultaneous merits/stay disposition are permitted. Footnote 4's substantial-federal-showing standard does not require ultimate success; the sentence alone does not compel certification. Page 895 separately addresses successive petitions and substantial grounds. Pages 895–896 separately address stays pending certiorari, including the probability of review, significant possibility of reversal, irreparable harm and the weight accorded appellate action in that setting. None is silently made the screening rule for a still-pending first district petition or the merits test in this argued review. No final district denial or certified initial appeal is established here.

Its conditional State-amenability views and three-Justice sequencing are not controlling law. *Morse* distinguishes provision-specific enforcement, merits and remedy. *Rutledge* permits bounded correction without reopening unrelated judgments. ## Propositions and disposition used in the individual commitments

The labels below identify exact scope for this handoff; they are not a proposed opinion structure.

**A — No additional dismissal sanction on the asserted grounds.** A first personal federal petition cannot be dismissed solely for the described delay and litigation purpose through an ad hoc equitable ground that dispenses with the governing statutory and Rules conditions. The conclusion addresses this proposed sanction; it does not declare every conceivable equitable consideration unavailable, erase exhaustion/default or other lawful claim-specific grounds, or immunize fraud or actual obstruction from their applicable law. No such additional predicate is adjudicated here. Reading §2243's law-and-justice direction with the Rules preserves structured judicial judgment rather than unlimited forfeiture authority.

**B — Stay discretion survives but must be exercised on the correct premise.** *Gomez* remains law. First-petition status does not categorically exclude equitable consideration of deliberate delay, the State's interest or proved obstruction. Equally, the availability of a separate stay inquiry does not validate a stay vacatur expressly resting on the supposed power to terminate the first petition by the sanction rejected in A. A court must distinguish a lawful disposition or management of the petition from a refusal of interim relief; it cannot establish the former merely by labeling the latter equitable. The reported findings may be considered below within the correct framework. They do not supply a present finding of Rule 9 prejudice, an established procedural bar, claim insubstantiality, or completion of the required examination.

**C — Necessary protection of required first-petition process.** When execution would prevent completion of process actually required by §2243 and the Rules, and lawful prompt disposition cannot be completed in time, a court must supply the interim protection necessary to make that process meaningful. This is an application by analogy of *Barefoot*'s meaningful-review principle, reinforced by *McFarland*; it is not direct application of the initial-appeal/CPC rule. The process must first be identified under the governing law. Rule 4 screening, lawful procedural dismissal, appropriate expedition and the absence of any automatic evidentiary hearing remain. Mere filing, a capital sentence or the designation “first petition” does not establish the condition. The present record supports correction and reassessment, not a finding that a particular undisclosed claim requires a particular period of review. Individual commitments below specify who adopts this additional proposition and who reserves its general application.

**D — Vacate the appellate stay-vacatur judgment and remand for prompt redetermination.** Set aside the appellate order because its stated dismissal premise is legally erroneous. Remand through the Eleventh Circuit for the District Court promptly to examine the actual petition and any proper State objections under the governing statute and Rules, and redetermine the need and terms of §2251 protection on that correct basis. This is not an instruction to dismiss, grant a writ, conduct an evidentiary hearing, find a gateway satisfied, or conduct full constitutional-merits adjudication irrespective of lawful barriers.

The immediate procedural effect is removal of the appellate order's displacement of the existing district stay. That stay is restored as protection pending the prompt redetermination required on remand, subject to lawful modification or dissolution; the mandate does not approve indefinite continuation on first-petition status alone. No separate Supreme Court execution stay, new execution date or numerical stay period is invented. Once the required examination permits a lawful disposition or shows protection unnecessary, the existing order supplies no entitlement to further delay. Conversely, the redetermination must not assume that vacating the erroneous appellate judgment has already decided every question of necessary interim protection. This restoration follows correction of the actual order under review and preservation of the proceeding for the directed decision, not a rule automatically staying every first petition.

## Adversarial comparison and rejected extensions

**Affirm the stay vacatur through independent equity.** This is the strongest competing judgment path. The lower findings concern more than mere eve-of-execution filing: six years of refused opportunities, repeated reversals and an extraneous purpose. *Gomez* expressly permits an independent equitable ground, and its four petitions are not a minimum requirement. *McFarland* permits denial for proved disregard and obstruction. Section 2251's “may” and no-stay consequence also defeat a categorical filing-based entitlement. Those considerations remain available; the commitments do not dismiss them as legally irrelevant.

The difficulty is the actual appellate decision, not simply the lack of a completed district dismissal. Its vacatur rests on the conclusion that the District Court could dismiss the first petition by this free-standing sanction and therefore committed legal error in declining to do so. Accepting the lower conduct findings does not establish the distinct lawful ground needed after that premise is rejected. No supplied petition or order establishes Rule 4 nonentitlement, actual Rule 9 response prejudice, a completed required examination, or another sufficiently supported disposition. Treating execution itself as the substitute for that examination would reproduce the disputed forfeiture without identifying what law permits it. The individual commitments select corrective remand rather than independently make those missing determinations. That conclusion does not prohibit affirmance of a stay refusal on a genuinely established independent ground in another case, or its consideration below here under the correct standard.

**Require a stay merely because this is the first personal petition.** This competing rationale is too broad. Section 2251 expressly preserves State action absent a stay; Rule 4 permits summary dismissal and other appropriate action. The meaningful-review holding at *Barefoot* 893–894 has the initial-appeal/CPC trigger, which this record does not establish. Necessary-protection reasoning must identify the first-petition process required here by analogy and explain why lawful expedition cannot supply it. C preserves those conditions; D does not find them satisfied for every future stage. No row supports an indefinite stay or automatic full evidentiary hearing.

**Affirm a merits dismissal under Rule 9, §2244(b), default, nonexhaustion or claim insufficiency.** No completed district merits dismissal is under review, and the supplied record does not establish the necessary predicates for a new claim-specific disposition. Failure of next-friend standing is not an earlier merits rejection of Lonchar's personal grounds. Motivation to postpone does not identify the contents or legal insufficiency of his pleading. There is no operative five-year presumption to fill the prejudice gap. The State retains proper objections on remand, subject to the actual governing burdens and procedures.

**Erase all equitable judgment from habeas.** A does not support this alternative. *Gomez*'s stay doctrine remains; Rule 9(a) itself is permissive after its conditions are met; Rule 4 permits appropriate judicial action; and *McFarland* recognizes individualized denial for established obstruction. The judgment can correct the asserted additional dismissal sanction without overruling those rules or answering every possible inherent-power question.
