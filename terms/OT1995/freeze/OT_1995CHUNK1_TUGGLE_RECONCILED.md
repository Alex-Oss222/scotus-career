## Historical comparison and its limits

The historical report grants certiorari and vacates and remands through the Fourth Circuit. Its per curiam opinion rejects the proposition that one valid aggravator always excuses an independent constitutional defect affecting sentencing evidence. *Zant* involved evidence properly admitted and fully open to explanation; loss of psychiatric rebuttal and mitigation assistance could instead affect selection of death even if vileness remained unaffected. Part III expressly leaves whether harmless-error analysis applies for further proceedings; it does not itself direct a particular collateral standard. The supplement's reference to a harmlessness inquiry must be read with that reservation.

The report publishes Scalia's concurrence and no dissent or other separate writing. Scalia accepts the state court's constitutional determination as an antecedent in an opinion not before the Court, treats the problem as impermissible sentencing evidence, invokes *Satterwhite* for direct-review harmlessness, and invokes historical *Brecht* for the collateral standard. He agrees that *Zant* supplies no answer and that remand is appropriate. His narrower evidentiary characterization is an important qualification to the per curiam's assistance discussion, not support for affirmance through vileness alone. The report's silence about additional separate positions supplies no private petition poll. No historical authorship or unnamed joinder is imported into the present event.

The independent commitments agree with the historical disposition and its central *Zant* distinction. The historical per curiam does not settle the presently reserved collateral rule. Historical *Brecht*'s general collateral rule cannot supply that rule here: the actual *Brecht* controls preserved *Doyle* error, and the actual *O'Neal* controls the preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category. Their remaining categories are open. Current public positions support the separate standard preferences below without turning any of them into law.

**Historical departure:** Scalia's historical concurrence expressly relied on general *Brecht* law to identify the collateral standard; that controlling premise is absent here. His actual *Brecht* dissent join and authored *O'Neal* dissent still support a prisoner substantial-injury requirement, but only as his proposed rule for this reserved category. He therefore retains that preference while accepting a narrower remand that does not announce it as existing law. This changes the asserted legal basis and scope of his commitment, not his support for vacatur. His actual *O'Neal* affirmance rested on an existing no-influence assessment; Tuggle's categorical eligibility rationale supplies no comparable assessment.

No other material judgment departure is supported or proposed. The historical per curiam's general reservation is compatible with each other Associate's offered narrow join. Their different preferences, if the standard were reached, are grounded in actual prior writings rather than attributed to an unreported historical disagreement.

## Common predicate, rule, and remedy limits

These limits form part of every commitment below.

1. **An existing judicial ruling and a withdrawn concession remain distinct.** The Virginia Supreme Court affirmatively found penalty-stage *Ake* error in 1985, despite the request qualifications it recited. The Commonwealth expressly withdrew its earlier concession in its 1995 opposition. There is no current concession on which to decide the violation; withdrawal alone does not erase the judicial ruling. Neither complete preclusion of all defenses nor unrestricted reopening is established.
2. **The present rule may rest on an express assumption.** Assume a properly available denial of the psychiatric assistance *Ake* requires solely to test the appellate surviving-aggravator rationale. Do not convert that assumption into this Court's final finding of violation, preservation, default, actual prejudice, or entitlement to a writ. The historical report's acceptance of the state ruling is not authority for bypassing the present neutral packet's documented dispute. This is a clarification of what the present limited disposition decides, not a finding that the underlying historical record changed.
3. **Narrow common ground.** In a nonweighing State, a valid aggravator may preserve death eligibility, but its survival alone neither disposes of an independent, properly available *Ake* assistance claim nor establishes that the assumed violation had no effect on selection of death. *Zant* retains its application to the defective narrowing factor in its admissible, accurate, explainable-evidence setting; it does not make every invalid factor require automatic sentence reversal. The Court need not adopt a weighing-state correction regime to reject the categorical shortcut here.
4. **The actual defense functions remain relevant.** Further consideration must address the relevant prosecution evidence and any actual loss of examination, evaluation, preparation, cross-examination assistance, rebuttal, and supported mitigation. Do not equate denial of a preferred second examiner with denial of constitutionally adequate assistance; do not equate two examinations with adequate assistance merely by counting them. No favorable hypothetical expert opinion, inaccurate State testimony, or actual sentencing influence is found. *Estelle* warning/waiver and counsel-consultation claims remain distinct and undecided.
5. **Bounded remedy.** Vacate the challenged appellate *Ake*/sentence-review disposition and remand through the Fourth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the narrow rule. Reserve whether harmless-error review applies to the established claim, the governing collateral error-effect standard, actual effect, and ultimate relief. Any antecedent defense must be legally available in light of the actual state ruling and preservation history; the remand neither reopens closed issues nor revives forfeited defenses. No automatic reinstatement of every District Court ground, writ, resentencing, life sentence, guilt retrial, release, execution authorization, or stay follows. The weighing precedents do not establish authority for state appellate correction of this particular assistance violation. Eventual relief requires a properly available violation warranting relief under the applicable rule and may allow a reasonable opportunity for a lawful penalty disposition.

## Final individual commitments

**John Paul Stevens.** Grant the limited petition question and support summary vacatur and remand on the stated assumption. Join the common rule and all five limits above. His authored *Zant* and *Ake* join independently support the evidence-and-explanation distinction; the full comparator confirms its application without requiring a new holding on the violation. No join is offered for *Chapman*, prisoner loss in equipoise, structural classification, actual influence, or automatic restoration of the writ.

**Sandra Day O'Connor.** Grant limited review and support bounded summary vacatur and remand; join the common rule and limits. If the standard were decided, she would newly adopt *Chapman* for a preserved, properly available capital-sentencing denial of required psychiatric assistance where the State presents psychiatric future-danger evidence: the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the whole sentencing record that the violation did not contribute to selection of death. Her actual *Brecht* concurrence and authored *O'Neal* reasoning support that preference. She also joins an express reservation, consistent with the historical per curiam's limited remand and her prior distinction between identifying an error and fixing relief. **Antonin Scalia.** Grant the confined legal question and support bounded summary vacatur and remand; join the common rule and limits.
