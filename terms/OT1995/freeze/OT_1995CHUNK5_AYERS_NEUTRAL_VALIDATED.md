## 3. Lower-court findings, party positions, and independent grounds

### Admissions and existing scholarships

The court found that the old differential undergraduate admission standards trace to the dual system and continue channeling students, especially when combined with overlapping programs and racially identifiable institutions. It did not prohibit every ACT use. It found ACT scores plus high-school performance educationally sound and rejected open admissions. The original proposal admitted students regularly through these alternatives: a 3.20 GPA in the specified college-preparatory core; below 3.20 but at least 2.50 core GPA **or** top-half class rank, together with ACT 16; or core completion with GPA 2.00 and ACT 18. The proposed screening process separately assessed students who did not qualify, and could place them in regular work with or without support or in the summer program. Successful summer completion permitted admission with mandatory year-long academic support. Failure or nonparticipation did not become guaranteed admission. Footnotes 297 and 300 materially qualify the terminology: after Department of Education review, the Board substituted “full admission” for “regular”; former conditional admittees received full-admission status and former provisional admittees “full admission with academic deficiencies,” to preserve federal financial-aid eligibility (BDX 713 Supplement; defendants' proposed findings pp. 22–23). A terminology change is not proof that every student satisfies aid or final enrollment conditions. Footnote 298 says the accuplacer was designed for placement, not screening/admission per se. See 879 F. Supp. at 1430–35, 1477–83 and nn. 295–325. The complete core requirement must be recovered from BDX 202 or the actual order: note 295's recovered list specifies English, science, social studies, computer applications, and electives but has no separate mathematics-unit entry, an unresolved source/completeness issue rather than permission to invent the missing term.

The court compared the proposed standards both with 1987 standards and with the lower HBI standards resulting from the 1989 test change. It found increased eligibility against the former baseline and decreased direct regular eligibility against the latter, while anticipating a remedial summer route. Do not recast anticipated success as an observed result; the complete summer system had not been implemented at trial. A challenge to that prediction is different from a categorical attack on every test or on uniform standards.

Private plaintiffs proposed ACT 10 plus a high-school diploma for regional institutions, temporary open admissions at JSU for eight years, and the Board's proposed standards at the white comprehensive institutions. They opposed ACT-only scholarship decisions and proposed suspending graduate/professional test cutoffs pending examination. The United States' witnesses supported less exclusionary ACT/GPA combinations, including the 1992 unadopted Board proposal, and statewide requirements; the court describes a suggested 2.50 overall-GPA alternative. Footnotes 310–311 additionally report the United States' pretrial proposal for ACT 15 plus graduation statewide, alongside the alternative GPA proposal. A witness endorsement is not a binding litigation concession or a later amendment's exact language. The February 16, 1996 alternative must be independently obtained.

**Existing scholarship rejection, 1433–35:** Board alumni scholarships waived nonresident fees for an out-of-state applicant with ACT 21 who was a nonresident alumnus's child. Plaintiffs also challenged institutions' ACT-only academic scholarship cutoffs. The court found neither a link to the de jure system nor evidence that these practices currently fostered separation, and found no discriminatory purpose. It therefore denied reform. Note 36 further rejects presumed choice effects in light of ACT-based scholarships set aside for black students, identifying MUW Heritage and ASU examples; that evidentiary rationale does not itself establish the legality or effectiveness of every scholarship. Those are separate causal/effect findings and a new-policy intent rationale. Fordice forbids demanding fresh intent for an established traceable remnant; it does not eliminate the ordinary intent inquiry for a genuinely new, unconnected equal-protection claim. Whether findings are clearly erroneous, legally incomplete, or unaffected requires the actual preserved challenge. Disparity by itself does not resolve either finding.

**Graduate testing, 1435–36:** The court noted misleading catalog language and possible GRE aggregation/cutoff use, distinguished actual practice, and required catalog submission/reform consistent with the defendants' undertaking. Its later summary calls graduate policies nontraceable. This is separate from undergraduate admission and scholarship issues; no general graduate-test ban follows. Paragraph 13 and any postponed report must be preserved within actual scope.

### Missions, programs, equipment, libraries, and funding

The findings do not classify every resource disparity alike. At 1477 the court expressly identifies inherited mission assignments, mission-linked funding, and mission-linked equipment/library allocation as traceable; it separately finds no traceability for current capital-improvement and repair/renovation allocation. Admissions, missions, proximate duplicative offerings, dual land-grant operation, and continued institutional organization have separately stated current-effect findings. Treating them all as either proven or disproven would erase the distinctions below.

**Funding, 1446–53:** The court describes the eight-component formula, institutional autonomy, greater formula returns for upper-level and graduate instruction, the dependence of research/support funding on program structure, and historical mission/funding interaction. It says the 1987 formula locked institutional positions in place. It also finds no educational-unsoundness contention against the formula as such, no per se traceable funding policy, and no warrant for institutional funding equity. The resulting refusal to alter the formula must be separated from relief for an established educational injury through a mission/program change. No universal per-student equality or new statewide formula is implied by Fordice or Jenkins.

**Equipment and libraries, 1456–59:** Equipment quality and type bear on student preparation for work and recruitment; fixed technical/scientific equipment at HBIs was inferior. Library holdings and space also differed, with JSU and ASU expansions underway. At 1458 the court says that absent proof HBI library holdings influence choice to the extent of excluding those institutions as viable choices for white students, expansion beyond existing missions is not educationally sound. This is an identifiable place where the enrollment rationale must be tested against the continuing-duty and violation-linked repair rules. It is not an appellate finding that every requested expansion is necessary. Separate current capital/repair findings remain intact unless properly challenged.

**JSU, 1483–86:** The court expressly finds arrested development traceable and asks whether enhancement will produce a significant white presence. It approves specified new programs, an endowment and physical measures. It declines broader immediate program transfers and UMMC affiliation for distinct evidence, feasibility, educational-soundness, and institutional reasons: the duplication analysis alone does not describe program content/quality; the record does not establish how UMMC affiliation changes diversity; JSU's own witness did not see a need for it. Its engineering/law/pharmacy and broader-uniqueness remedy is a study, not installation. An enrollment-only legal objection therefore does not automatically defeat every independent reason for refusing a particular transfer.

**ASU:** Its limited land-grant role is traceable. The court regards dividing extension roles as educationally unsound on the record, but approves the Small Farm Development Center match, endowment, and Natchez MBA. The exact match cap, timing, and capital-funding condition matter. Approval of institutional repair does not decide a distinct objection to white-only scholarship eligibility.

**Duplication and organization, 1486–92:** Proximate DSU/MVSU duplication interacts with differing admissions; remote duplication was separately assessed. The court expects uniform admission and JSU/ASU improvements to lessen effects of nonproximate duplication, subject to the studies ordered. It finds the proposed DSU/MVSU consolidation insufficiently researched, questions capacity and abandonment of investment, recognizes MVSU's service to academically underprepared students, and requires examination of less drastic alternatives and the new admission policy. Paragraph 12 does **not** order merger. The rejected MUW/MSU merger was found not constitutionally required; the opinion does not enjoin the State's separate fiscal/policy choice. Do not turn either conclusion into an order to close, preserve forever, or equalize all universities.

### Presented below versus preserved here

Appendix allegations specifically include private B1 (interacting admission/resource limits on black students' educational opportunities), B4 (ACT/alumni scholarships), C2–C6 (funding and adverse educational effects), C8–C14 (programs), C15–C17 (facilities/equipment; the reporter repeats equipment language), and related land/institution issues. United States items 24–28, 32–50, and 53–60 identify parallel but not identical claims. Footnotes 4–5 distinguish the private plaintiffs' broader discrimination theory, including an alternative discriminatory-purpose/Title VI-regulation allegation for nontraceable practices, from the government's desegregation framing. This authenticates trial presentation, **not** appellate preservation or a general private disparate-impact cause.

The strongest available defense is educationally supported, practically effective dismantlement respecting clear-error findings and legitimate institutional differences; the original proposed reorganization is evidence of a proposed remedy, not completed compliance. The strongest available challenge is that identified inherited policies and their interactions remain insufficiently repaired, with a legally incomplete burden or an improperly exclusive enrollment yardstick. Each requires its own record application; neither side has an automatic entitlement from good intentions, expenditure levels, current imbalance, or an institution's label.

1. **Standards: “Dismantling a formerly segregated public university system”; present operation effective Fordice, June 26, 1992.** The State must demonstrate dismantlement of policies traceable to its established dual system that continue fostering segregation when effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices. Neutral wording/good faith and a later neutral purpose do not alone discharge duty. Examine interacting admissions, duplication, missions, organization, and properly challenged funding. Current imbalance alone is insufficient; no fresh discriminatory intent is required for a traceable remnant. Existing findings are not automatically set aside because an earlier legal standard was incorrect. New unconnected policies retain ordinary equal-protection requirements. No universal ACT ban, proportional enrollment, mandated closure, fixed funding award, general Title VI disparate-impact cause, or heightened proof quantum follows. Public Holdings: civil-rights.md, Fordice's four propositions.
2. **Standards: “Violation-linked educational salary support”; present operation effective Jenkins, June 12, 1995.** Supported educational-repair and personnel-necessity findings can sustain salary/support costs repairing established segregation-caused educational injury; each implementation cost need not itself be a distinct constitutional wrong. Incidental attraction does not defeat an independently sufficient repair ground. No general educational-improvement guarantee, automatic future increase, unproved interdistrict remedy, or reopening of settled foundations outside review follows. Apply the principle to the actually established injury; do not presume that every proposed university expenditure is necessary. The historical contrary Jenkins disposition is not governing law here.
3. **Standards: “Partial withdrawal of school-desegregation supervision”; present operation effective Freeman, March 31, 1992, with Jenkins's June 12, 1995 application.** Only if release is properly sought, require defendant-specific full and satisfactory compliance, practicable vestige elimination, conduct showing whole-decree good faith, and necessary causal/remedial connections. Assess whether retaining control is necessary or practicable elsewhere and prevent obstruction of remaining relief; define duties released/retained. Genuinely independent demographics do not require perpetual balance. Low scores alone establish neither liability nor completion; national parity is not indispensable. Release **may**, not must, follow a satisfactory showing. No Rufo changed-circumstances prerequisite, plaintiff veto, consensus, reviewer endorsement, or all-components-at-once rule is added.
4. **Race-specific eligibility, if properly presented:** Public Adarand, June 12, 1995, preserves Metro Broadcasting's important-objective/substantial-relation rule for benign **federal** classifications supported by Congress and Fullilove at its actual scope; it expressly does not displace Croson's state/local law. There is no general benign-remedy exemption and no license to apply the federal contracting standard automatically to Mississippi student eligibility. Institutional repair and particular race-based eligibility need separate justification, actual challengers/injury, scope, and record. No unpresented challenge is manufactured here.
5. **Remedy and review:** §2106 authorizes affirmance, modification, vacatur, reversal, and appropriate remand only for matters lawfully before the reviewing court and as just under the circumstances. Legal standards/burden allocation require legal review; established factual findings receive ordinary clear-error review; remedial choices remain subject to lawful equitable limits and appropriate deference. Neither clear-error review nor discretion shelters a wrong rule, and neither legal error nor a new legal emphasis permits appellate factfinding on unestablished necessity or automatic reopening of all findings.

Equal Protection forbids a State to deny any person within its jurisdiction equal protection of the laws. Section 601 of Title VI bars race/color/national-origin exclusion, denial of benefits, or discrimination in a federally assisted program/activity. Section 602's agency implementation authority, insurance/guaranty exception, presidential-approval requirement, notice/voluntary-compliance predicate, hearing/express-finding and recipient/program limits on funding termination, congressional report and thirty-day delay are distinct provisions; they do not become newly prescribed judicial-decree procedures.
