## Governing propositions and common adverse path

The operative Standard is **Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions**, present operation effective **Lanphere & Urbaniak, April 18, 1995**. Central Hudson/Fox require protection of lawful, nonmisleading information, a substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable proportional fit. Discovery Network, Edenfield, Ibanez, Lanphere and Rubin require justification responsive to the actual restriction. Supported prediction is permissible; empirical studies in every case, least restrictive means and simultaneous regulation of every source are not required. Rubin, April 19, 1995, independently establishes advancement and fit failures for its actual alcohol-information restriction. Its eight-Justice framework cannot be combined with Stevens's distinct judgment ground to attribute that framework to him. Florida Bar's June 21, 1995 majority and dissent both retain these requirements while disagreeing about the actual immediate-privacy evidence and finite waiting period. Edge's five-Justice rule rests on meaningful actual reduction and reasonable fit; its broader category-level analysis is not controlling.

**Strongest affirmance path:** Saffer's search-cost mechanism, accepted price responsiveness and opposing experts' concessions support a reasonable prediction that price advertising reduces average prices and increases consumption. Price advertising can differ materially from other promotion. A price-only restriction permits substantial product advertising; Fox allows legislative choice, taxes risk interstate purchases, and some minimum-price systems face antitrust difficulties. LaRue/Bellanca and Queensgate strengthen the asserted alcohol-control authority. The district court's preferred experts do not themselves decide the constitutional question.

**Common answer supporting the framework commitments below:** Even assuming some material advancement, the State has not justified the proportionality of suppressing the whole category of truthful retail-price information. The objective is the price and quantity of alcohol sold, and the record supplies direct price/tax measures addressing that objective without suppressing consumer comparison. This is a concrete comparison under Fox, not an order to exhaust alternatives. Interstate-shopping concerns do not establish why the indirect price-raising mechanism avoids the same problem or why a blanket informational restriction is proportionate. Midcal and 324 Liquor do not establish that every directly imposed price measure is unlawful. No substitute program is judicially selected. The merchandise and trade-journal exceptions do not alone invalidate the law, and permission for nonprice promotion does not alone defeat a price-specific mechanism; they are considered with the actual burden and justification. A complete numerical-price and implied-price-reference prohibition differs from the finite privacy interval defended in Florida Bar.

For the Associates who additionally find advancement inadequate, the existence of a plausible price relationship is distinguished from sufficient support for a material consumption effect of this restriction. General-advertising studies, acknowledged measurement difficulties and testimony supporting the mechanism must be considered. They do not erase the trial finding, the missing state-specific estimate or the concessions that advertising can redistribute market share without materially increasing total consumption. This conclusion rests on this record as a whole, not a universal numerical-proof requirement. For the Associates who reserve advancement, no appellate finding that consumption would increase is substituted for the district court's finding.

## Individual grounds, adverse alternatives and join barriers

**Stevens.** His Rubin concurrence, Discovery Network authorship and Edge dissent under Bigelow support a sufficient independent ground: the government may regulate alcohol's harms directly but may not keep consumers ignorant of accurate prices of a lawful product because informed choices may increase purchases. Better proof that ignorance reduces consumption would not cure that defect. The strongest contrary path is Posadas's deference to the legislature's choice between prohibition and promotional restraint. He rejects its greater-power/lesser-power reasoning and its permission to justify this suppression simply by the desired informational effect, following his own earlier objection rather than treating Rubin's Court opinion as his framework. He would disavow those propositions to the extent they authorize the present restraint; he does not commit to overruling every holding in Posadas. Peoples' lawful Massachusetts information independently reinforces his conclusion under Bigelow, consistently with his Edge dissent. He would join an opinion containing the factual, remedial, Twenty-first Amendment and specified anti-paternalism propositions, but not one adopting Central Hudson's acceptance of this suppression objective or an independent Fox holding. His position need not become a separate out-of-state-only judgment.

**O'Connor.** Her actual Rubin joinder and Discovery Network joinder support reversal for unreasonable fit while retaining Central Hudson. Her Edenfield dissent and Florida Bar dissent supply the strongest affirmance argument: experience and legislative prediction can support prophylaxis without awaiting definitive empirical proof. She gives the price mechanism and expert concessions real weight and does not decide that they necessarily fail advancement. Unlike the profession-specific risks or temporary intrusion she defended, the present restriction continuously withholds an entire class of accurate price information to achieve an indirectly higher price. The concrete direct-control comparison remains unanswered at the level Fox requires. Her join excludes a universal study requirement, judicial selection of the best tax, all-sources-at-once regulation and the broader anti-paternalism ground. Her broader Edge joinder does not excuse fit on this different record. She accepts ordinary review here without deciding the validity of LaRue's licensed-premises regulation or generally overruling Posadas.

**Scalia.** His Edenfield, Discovery Network and Rubin joins support the real-justification requirement and Rubin's independently sufficient fit route. His broader Edge position and Florida Bar dissent favor legislative latitude in a supported incremental scheme; the strongest affirmance route therefore treats the economic inference as enough and the available nonprice advertising as meaningful narrowing. He reserves advancement but finds the actual suppression disproportionate for the same concrete reasons that supported his Rubin fit joinder. Price responsiveness is not itself a sufficient answer to the burden on every truthful price reference. He will not join reasoning that makes every content distinction fatal, demands regulation of all promotion or equates every commercial message with noncommercial speech. He joins the limited rejection of an alcohol-based First Amendment presumption and of automatic Posadas authorization, while reserving broader precedent revision and any categorical theory unsupported by his date-eligible public positions.

**Kennedy.** His authorship of Edenfield, Lanphere, Rubin and Florida Bar supports two independent grounds: inadequate demonstrated material advancement on this trial record and disproportionate fit. He considers supported prediction constitutionally available but concludes that the First Circuit gave the plausibility of one link the work of justifying the complete advertising-to-price-to-consumption chain. The district finding is material, while its credibility judgments do not dictate the constitutional rule. His Edge joinder and Souter concurrence provide the strongest contrary example: a supported real reduction can sustain an incremental accommodation. This record does not supply a comparable showing; in any event, fit independently fails. He joins ordinary speech scrutiny despite the Twenty-first Amendment and rejects automatic vice or greater-power authorization. He does not join Stevens's ground making an effective information-based restriction categorically invalid, a general requirement of empirical studies or a general overruling of every Posadas application.

**Souter.** His Edge concurrence, joined by Kennedy, distinguished an actual-effect resolution from broader categorical deference; his Rubin and Florida Bar joins support the same distinction here. He joins the record-specific advancement failure and the independent fit failure. The adverse path is that the price mechanism gives a more concrete causal relationship than the unsupported profession-related assertions in Edenfield or Lanphere. It deserves evaluation, but the absence of a demonstrated material consumption effect after the trial, together with the acknowledged limits of the studies and estimates, defeats the proposed substitution of reasonable inference for the actual burden. He does not require certainty or a specified study design. His joins exclude an automatic rule against incremental regulation, a broad interstate-information immunity that would contradict his Edge position, and Stevens's categorical alternative. Ordinary scrutiny applies here; the conduct holdings of LaRue/Bellanca and the full future reach of Posadas remain outside his necessary ground.

**Thomas.** His Rubin framework and fit joinder, together with his Edenfield and Ibanez joins, supplies a date-eligible ground for reversal. His Discovery Network dissent and broader Edge joins give the strongest support to affirmance: government may address a portion of a harm and preserve other commercial channels without adopting the least restrictive means. He consequently reserves whether this economic record is insufficient at advancement. The decisive defect is the unexplained burden of withholding all accurate retail prices when the offered justification operates through the price charged and the record identifies direct controls. This conclusion does not reject incremental regulation or turn the mere availability of another measure into automatic invalidity. He joins the present no-presumption and no-automatic-vice-exception propositions already consistent with Rubin, not a wholesale repudiation of commercial-speech scrutiny or an unrecorded later categorical position. No source-based basis is supplied for treating his agreement in the judgment as a join in Stevens's independent rationale.

**Ginsburg.** Her Rubin, Lanphere, Florida Bar and distinct Ibanez joins support both the record-specific advancement failure and the independent fit failure. The State's strongest response is that its theory addresses a real price mechanism rather than imagined misleading information and therefore warrants predictive latitude. She accepts the distinction and still finds the constitutional showing inadequate in light of the trial finding and the mismatch between broad advertising studies and this price-only restraint; the State cannot transfer its burden by invoking temperance in the abstract. The fit ground remains independently sufficient even if the prediction is accepted. Her joins preserve adequately justified regulation and factual disclosure, do not require numerical proof in every case and do not equate the stipulation's presumed truthfulness with a stipulated consumption effect. She joins ordinary scrutiny and rejection of automatic Posadas authorization, while withholding Stevens's categorical alternative and any unneeded general revision of licensed-premises precedent. No preappointment Edge position is attributed to her.

**Breyer.** His Rubin and Lanphere joins establish a responsive-justification and proportional-fit ground; his Florida Bar dissent establishes that he can accept a supported predictive judgment and a finite restriction despite disputed evidence. The economic mechanism and expert concessions thus make affirmance a substantial alternative, and he does not join a holding that they necessarily fail advancement. The present permanent information ban nevertheless lacks the demonstrated proportional relationship that the immediate-privacy and waiting-period rationale supplied in his Florida Bar position. Direct price/tax controls are concrete comparisons, not mandated replacements, and the antitrust/interstate objections do not justify treating all such comparisons as unavailable. He joins the narrow Twenty-first Amendment and Posadas conclusions necessary to retain ordinary scrutiny. He reserves a broader ban on paternalistic regulation, any universal measurement requirement and any independent interstate rule beyond the common speech holding. No lower-court authorship or participation in this matter is used as a source of his commitment.

## Precedent scope, alternative grounds and remedy

**Twenty-first Amendment.** Each Associate supports the stated proposition that the Amendment supplies no added presumption relaxing the First Amendment burden for these off-premises truthful-price prohibitions. State authority to regulate sale, price and importation identifies legitimate regulatory subjects; it does not establish the fit of this speech restraint. LaRue/Bellanca's licensed-premises conduct holdings are distinguished and not overruled. The commitment is not merely that the State loses despite an assumed lower burden, and it is not a universal holding about every interaction of the two Amendments.

**Posadas.** Each supports rejecting an automatic inference from the power to prohibit liquor sales to permission to suppress truthful information about sales that remain lawful. The seven framework commitments apply the actual Central Hudson/Fox requirements and preserve supported predictions and incremental measures. They do not join a general overruling of Posadas's casino-advertising judgment or every aspect of its analysis. Stevens separately supports disavowing its greater-power and informational-deference propositions insofar as they authorize this paternalistic suppression. Those different scopes must remain distinct in circulation.

**Queensgate.** Its predivergence summary dismissal is a merits disposition, not a nullity because unexplained. Mandel confines it to issues necessarily decided; it does not adopt every sentence of the Ohio court's reasoning. The respondents' asserted factual identity and the district court's asserted distinction have both been considered. These commitments do not rely on an invented factual difference: the present plenary decision would apply the subsequently developed Fox/Discovery Network/Edenfield/Rubin requirements to this established trial record and would decline to extend Queensgate into a rule that comparable liquor-price restrictions invariably satisfy them. The precise force of that summary judgment in other applications remains open. Its existence does not require the Supreme Court to reproduce the asserted categorical extension in this merits review.

**Other alternatives.** Stevens supports Bigelow as an additional ground for Peoples. The others obtain full relief on the common speech ground and do not decide a broader interstate-information rule, particularly in light of Edge's actual holding. Nobody revives the waived Commerce Clause claim. No commitment rests on a finding that the State's declared purpose was protectionist pretext or that every formulation of its temperance interest was forfeited. The intervenor's small-retailer rationale cannot independently justify the speech burden. Later submitted studies retain their disputed record status; unavailable research materials supply no default or need for a production order.

**Individual remedy commitments.** Each Associate would reverse the October 24 First Circuit judgment for defendants and remand for implementation of the district court's constitutional declaration against the challenged price-advertising prohibitions in §§3-8-7 and 3-8-8.1 and Regulation 32, consistently with the ground that Associate adopts. The completed trial supports that relief; no unresolved fact essential to the adopted sufficient ground requires a new evidentiary trial. An implementing remand does not reopen the abandoned commerce theory. No damages, $400 refund, new antitrust adjudication, compelled tax or price program, invalidation of unrelated alcohol regulations or special mandate date is included. False or misleading advertising and direct regulation of alcohol retain their independent governing law. A broader decree or renewed merits factfinding would require a separately supported need, not simply disagreement with the district court or the absence of a complete research archive.
