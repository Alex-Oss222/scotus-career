The District Court denied relief after an evidentiary hearing on April 12, 1993; the Ninth Circuit, No. 93-35549, reversed September 6, 1994, 34 F.3d 870; rehearing was denied November 16, 1994. Its conditional remedy permitted a retrial on premeditation within a reasonable time or reduction to simple first-degree murder. A January 13, 1995 District Court order implemented that mandate. No subsequent compliance or new Supreme Court action is established.

The consequential guilt issue is premeditation and aggravated first-degree murder, rather than identification of the robber or an assertion that Bartholomew fired no shots. He admitted the robbery and shootings but described accidental discharge and denied an agreement to leave no witnesses. Aggravated murder exposed him to death or life without parole; after penalty proceedings he was serving life without parole. Simple first-degree felony murder carried life with parole. The original death-stage history does not make the present claim a penalty-only Brady claim.

### Prior non-Stone jurisprudence relevant to the competing paths

Souter authored Kyles's collective lawful-use rule; Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer joined it. Stevens preserved his earlier request-sensitive criticism of Bagley without withdrawing his Kyles join. Their common prior application does not establish that unsupported speculation is enough here. Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, accepted collective assessment in dissent but required an adequately supported change in the evidentiary picture; they did not impose a categorical bar on all uses of an absent witness's statements, an innocence prerequisite, or a per-item threshold. The Kyles disagreement therefore supplies competing record applications, not opposite universal admissibility rules. See the complete bounded [Kyles public entry](../sources/A_PRIOR_Kyles.md).
