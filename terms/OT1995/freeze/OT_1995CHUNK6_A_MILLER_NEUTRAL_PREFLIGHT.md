## Exact enacted obligation and safeguards

The appellate opinion reproduces §34-23A-7 in footnote 2, 63 F.3d at 1454–55. It begins: “No abortion may be performed upon an unemancipated minor or upon a female for whom a guardian has been appointed because of a finding of incompetency, until at least forty-eight hours after written notice of the pending operation has been delivered in the manner specified in this section.” Notice goes to the parent's usual abode and must be delivered personally by the physician or an agent. Alternatively, certified mail requires return receipt and restricted delivery to the addressee. With certified mail, delivery is deemed at noon on the next day on which regular mail delivery occurs after mailing. Thus the duty is not simply a 48-hour period measured from mailing.

No notice is required if:

1. The attending physician certifies in the minor's medical record, based on good-faith clinical judgment, a medical emergency necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or for which delay will create a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function, **and insufficient time exists to provide notice**.
2. The person entitled to notice certifies in writing that he has been notified.
3. The minor declares or supplies information indicating that she is abused or neglected under §26-8A-2, and the physician reports the alleged or suspected abuse under the cross-referenced reporting provisions. The social-services department, state's attorney and law-enforcement officers receiving the report must keep confidential that she sought or obtained an abortion and take necessary steps against parental disclosure.

The original opinion's statutory quotation prints one reporting cross-reference as §28-6A-8; no independent correction of that typographic/source issue is adopted. The decision does not turn on its number. A parent certification acknowledges actual notice; it is no maturity or best-interests bypass. The emergency exception is conditional, with its own insufficient-time requirement, and is no blanket answer for nonemergency mature minors.

Section 26-8A-2, reproduced in footnote 9, includes abandonment/mistreatment, lack of proper care, an injurious environment, failure of necessary care, homelessness or improper domicile, threatened substantial harm, demonstrated substantial emotional/mental impairment, and sexual abuse/exploitation. The legal argument must acknowledge that this is broader than already completed physical assault. Nevertheless, the text neither authorizes a neutral determination of maturity nor independently directs a best-interests authorization.

The appellate opinion describes the abuse route as allowing abortion 24 hours after reporting. Section -7 itself does not insert a new 24-hour abuse waiting period; the separate generally applicable information provision has a 24-hour obligation. Keep those sources distinct and do not reopen the latter here.

## Supported record and its evidentiary status

The following are published judicial recitations of the summary-judgment submissions, at 1461–63, not claims that the original declarations were recovered or that this Court has independently found every prediction true:

- Mary Jones's declaration ¶5 and the cited AMA material described previously stressful but nonabusive families becoming abusive after learning about pregnancy or abortion. This explains why a preexisting-abuse route may fail a nonabusive family whose reaction is reasonably feared; it does not make every parental disagreement abuse.
- Buck Williams's declaration ¶28 described an honor-student minor who feared her antiabortion parents would prevent abortion; after her father learned she was at the clinic, he assaulted staff and forced her to leave. Other declarations (Gail Kelly ¶8, Carol Knudtson ¶7, Joan Williams ¶19) supported concern about parents obstructing abortions contrary to a daughter's interests. This is concrete evidence of practical interference distinct from a formal parental veto.
- Knudtson ¶8, Jones ¶¶3–4 and Penny Virchow ¶9 described secrecy, self-blame, protection of an abusive parent and reluctance to report; Jones described consequent delay and risk of self-harm. These support an argument that a reporting alternative must be actually usable, not merely written into the code. They do not establish an inevitable response by every abused minor.
- De Vee Dykstra's declaration, Exhibit E, using 1990 census information, reported roughly 18% of South Dakota minors lived in single-parent homes. One-parent notice cannot simply be answered by assuming everyone has a safe second parent. That statistic is not the percentage constitutionally injured.
- The appellate court identified §§26-7A-29, -37, -58 and -60 as permitting parental access to juvenile proceedings, reports and relevant statements. It reasoned that discovery, testimony, or the identity of the sole abortion provider could expose the abortion despite the confidentiality command. This is its analysis of the statutes and record, not proof of an actual postreport disclosure in a named proceeding.

## Entering law and proper analytical sequence

Use the complete “Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation,” present operation effective Casey, June 29, 1992, in the entering-law slice. The five direct joins are Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. The stable population derives from the obligation's operation, and supported compelled-disclosure subgroups must be identified before deciding injury. Do not average across all pregnant people, count only successful claimants, or turn 18% single-parent households into a dispositive fraction.

In sequence: identify the command/exceptions/enforcement and baseline; identify actual function and purpose; establish additional interference against the system without this notice duty; consider independent baseline violation, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle; if those do not decide the matter, consider remaining severe interference under compelling-interest/narrow-tailoring review and remaining meaningful interference under substantiated justification-and-fit review; preserve clinical/communication protections; match relief to the demonstrated defect. Challengers supply duty, causation, population and interference. State justification duties arise at the applicable trigger, not automatically because abortion is regulated. Purpose is not established merely from opposition to abortion.

Fargo, February 13, 1995, Part I (Souter; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer joining), expressly applies that whole standard and rejects an independent all-applications barrier or numerical facial threshold. The separate emergency-vagueness ruling does not deem every narrow emergency provision substantively sufficient. Fargo decided no new parental-involvement question.

Casey's “One-parent informed consent with an independent judicial bypass,” also effective June 29, 1992, sustains the particular structure unanimously. The minor may elect confidential, expedited authorization based on maturity/capacity with informed consent **or** best interests without first requesting parental permission. Its specific counsel and decision deadlines were sustained safeguards; the decision does not declare every detail indispensable in every notice law. The parental-notice inquiry remains distinct from whether formal consent is required.

Casey's spousal-notice rule has direct support from Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter. Its recognition that formal notice can produce practical control is relevant by analogy; it does not erase the State's distinct permissible interests in minors' welfare and parental involvement.

Dalton, March 18, 1996, unanimous public decision, requires effective relief against the established conflict while preserving severable lawful applications. It expressly creates no new facial-review formula and recognizes that a proved defect in an indivisible mandatory condition can warrant broader relief. It supplies no constitutional answer about this notice law. ## Strongest supported alternatives; no modeled commitments

**Sustain the notice invalidation on the actual Casey framework.** Compulsory notice, the 48-hour interval and absence of a maturity/best-interests route can give parents practical power to defeat a supported subgroup's timely choice. The declaration recitations identify concrete interference, and the abuse route omits mature, nonabused minors and creates supported practical reporting/confidentiality concerns. The State's permissible welfare and consultation interests do not automatically establish fit for compulsory disclosure by every minor. A usable independent authorization route is a concrete less burdensome alternative, supported by Casey's sustained structure. The strongest objection is that notice is not consent, one parent's advice is materially different from an adult spouse's control, and the abuse definition is broad. The answer must be source-specific proof and the exact Casey trigger, not the categorical statement that all notice laws equal consent laws.

**Preserve some operation or remand for application of the corrected framework.** A notice obligation can facilitate medical history, assistance, informed consideration and parental support without legally transferring decision authority. H.L. v. Matheson, 450 U.S. 398 (1981), addressed an immature dependent minor without an established mature/best-interests claim; Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502 (1990), sustained an available bypass without deciding that every notice law requires one. A court could regard the lower court's historical fraction formulation or categorical equivalence as too broad and require application-specific reconsideration. The strongest objection is that a framework correction is unnecessary if the already supported record resolves the actual Casey inquiry; remand must identify a legally material unresolved application or remedial task and cannot make the operator's inability to recover declarations a litigant failure.

**Possible relief distinction.** Separate an affirmative constitutional rule requiring a usable maturity/best-interests route for affected minors from a decree commanding the legislature to enact a specified court system. An injunction can prevent enforcement of the unconstitutional compulsory condition without inventing a new statutory tribunal, prescribing every deadline, or declaring the entire abortion act invalid. If relief is application-limited, identify the applications and how protection will operate; a theoretical mature-minor exception with no usable procedure cannot simply be assumed to cure the burden. The adult-incapacity sentence, postviability applications and independent penalties/information rulings are not silently decided.
