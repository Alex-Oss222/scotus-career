## Reconciled operative rule and exact limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support A: Elrod and Branti's protection extends to an existing independent contractor or regular service-provider relationship such as the recurring towing referral list. Government may not withdraw that relationship because of refused partisan support or opposing political allegiance unless it demonstrates that political affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance of the work. The inquiry is functional; “contractor,” “policymaker” or “confidential” is not a substitute for showing the actual requirement. General benefits of patronage, a wish to reward allies or dislike of the provider's association do not alone establish that exception. The accepted allegations supply no such towing qualification, so categorical contractor status cannot sustain dismissal.

This rule does not displace Pickering for public-concern speech claims or materially mixed speech and affiliation disputes. The courts below may determine on appropriate further proceedings which framework governs the developed claim. Pickering requires a case-sensitive balance adjusted to the actual service relationship; Branti requires the particular functional affiliation inquiry, not merely an undifferentiated managerial-interest balance. Neither rule creates automatic liability. The claimant must establish the relevant protected conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor causation; the city may establish by a preponderance that it would have made the same decision absent that conduct. A hypothetical permissible explanation is not proof of that defense.

Northlake's role is control of referrals, not purchase of all towing services; owners paid and there was no direct municipal service contract. That fact confirms the appropriate description of the benefit and prevents a fictional employment relationship. It does not alter the historical constitutional premise of a recurring governmental benefit. There is no property entitlement holding. Loss of referrals, retaliation and mayoral direction remain allegations accepted on Rule 12(b)(6), not findings. Neutral safety, responsiveness, reliability, economy and service qualifications may justify genuine decisions. This disposition does not decide initial unsuccessful bids, every public allocation, every later mixed dispute, or criminal bribery liability.

The six reverse only the First Amendment dismissal and remand. They do not reopen the separate procedural-due-process disposition or direct restoration, permanent referrals, a hearing, trial, a damages amount, municipal attribution, personal liability or a new immunity outcome. Summary judgment remains available under ordinary standards if the developed record presents no genuine material dispute and warrants it.

Scalia and Thomas retain B: affirm the reviewed dismissal on nonextension grounds, accepting the allegations for the motion. Their sufficient reason is that employment patronage restrictions should not be expanded to this nonemployment service allocation, given historical political discretion, commercial alternatives and procurement-litigation consequences. Lack of a property entitlement alone is insufficient; neither Justice converts a contested neutral motive or service defect into a finding. Their position does not overrule existing employee doctrine or authorize direct sovereign restriction of advocacy. If protection controls, their insistence on actual causal proof and lawful service choices is conditional and supplies no merits join.
