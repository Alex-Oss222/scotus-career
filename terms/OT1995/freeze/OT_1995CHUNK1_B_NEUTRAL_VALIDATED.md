### Statute in its material terms

Section 248(a)(1) prohibits intentional injury, intimidation, interference, or attempt, accomplished by force, threat of force, or physical obstruction, either **because** a person is or has been obtaining/providing reproductive health services **or in order to intimidate** that person, another person, or a class from obtaining/providing them. The motive alternatives are distinct. Section (a)(3)'s reproductive-facility branch separately concerns intentional property damage/destruction or attempt because the facility provides the services. The parallel worship branches exist in the text but are not adjudicated merely because they are neighboring provisions.

The definitions give interference its freedom-of-movement meaning, intimidation its reasonable-apprehension-of-bodily-harm meaning, and obstruction its impassable or unreasonably difficult/hazardous passage meaning. Reproductive health services include medical, surgical, counseling, and referral services relating to the reproductive system, pregnancy, or termination, in the defined facilities; the statute is not textually confined to abortion services. A facility includes the building/structure and grounds, with the separately defined worship setting.

The parent/legal-guardian exception removes **any penalties under the section** for described conduct directed **exclusively at that minor**. It is not an exception for every parental presence, every familial objection, or all religious conduct. Its text must accompany claims of universality, neutrality, general applicability, or least restrictive means; its legal comparability to plaintiffs' alleged conduct is an issue, not a settled fact in either direction.

Criminal ordinary first/subsequent penalties, reduced exclusively nonviolent-obstruction penalties, and enhanced bodily-injury/death penalties remain distinct. Private civil relief may include injunctions, compensatory/punitive damages, and costs/fees; the eligible claimant may elect $5,000 per violation before final judgment **in lieu of actual compensatory damages**. Election is permission, not a mandatory award or replacement of every other remedy. The private standing restrictions differ from federal and state Attorney General enforcement and their civil-penalty schedules; criminal fines, government civil penalties, and privately elected statutory damages are not interchangeable.

Section 248(d) preserves protected expressive conduct including peaceful picketing/demonstration, creates no new outside-facility speech/religion interference remedy regardless of viewpoint, preserves existing remedies and state/local law, and does not interfere with state/local reproductive-health regulation. A savings clause requires meaningful construction but cannot itself establish that every covered application is constitutional. The Act's severability clause covers provisions and applications, and its effective date covers conduct on or after May 26, 1994.

Original RFRA independently applies to later federal laws unless explicitly excluded by reference to RFRA; FACE contains no such exclusion. It requires the claimant to establish substantial burden, then the government to produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to the person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means. Ordinary Article III and appropriate-relief limits remain. Smith constitutional neutrality is not a substitute for that enacted test; the statute preserves belief and the Establishment Clause.

### Every supported merits route

1. **Commerce: identify the regulated class and actual connection.** The challengers characterize local noneconomic protest without a case-specific interstate element and object to remote inference chains and federal police power. The government characterizes direct interference with an actual interstate medical-services market and relies on interstate patients, staff, supplies, and service disruption. These legislative predicates are reported in the lower opinion; they are not findings that each plaintiff personally crossed state lines. Determine whether the regulation addresses that market/conduct class rather than merely relabeling general social costs. A facial challenge also requires identifying the challenged branches and applications; no conclusion about every branch follows from one example.
2. **Separate commerce routes and their limits.** The government advances protection of persons/things in interstate commerce and substantial effects. The first route requires its legally sufficient connection; interstate travel by some patients cannot silently become an element in every prosecution. The second requires the enacted class and supported commercial relationship under the actual Lopez/Harris limits, with Robertson's direct-enterprise route separately bounded. The absence of an individual nexus is relevant but not automatically fatal under a universal rule the Court has not adopted; the presence of legislative findings is relevant but not conclusive. Do not judicially add an absent element to the unambiguous statute.
3. **Alternative §5 and federalism questions.** The Fourth Circuit did not reach Fourteenth Amendment §5. A commerce ground that suffices may leave it open. A ground rejecting commerce must identify whether the preserved §5 alternative needs decision or further proceedings before prescribing the full disposition. Bray's statutory private-conspiracy limitations do not themselves resolve all congressional enforcement-power questions. Before adopting a §5 holding, examine the actual proposed power, constitutional right/state-action relationship, and relevant date-eligible authorities; this source set does not establish a new general power or categorical ban. Direct federal regulation of private persons and compelled state administration are also different; a Tenth Amendment label alone supplies no additional veto.
4. **Content/viewpoint classification.** Examine the statutory motive branches, covered services, conduct definitions, comparative exclusions, and actual operation. Petitioners invoke differential treatment of labor/environmental obstruction and targeting of antiabortion expression; the government invokes neutral protection of medical access and Mitchell's treatment of relevant motive. A benign purpose does not neutralize content-based operation, and differential subjects do not automatically establish viewpoint discrimination. The statute's coverage of services on both sides of abortion does not by itself decide every selective-burden objection. A finding of viewpoint neutrality does not alone establish content neutrality.
5. **Expression and fit.** Force, threats, property destruction, obstruction, peaceful persuasion, and expressive but obstructive conduct require distinct treatment. The circuit subjected potentially expressive peaceful obstruction to intermediate scrutiny rather than stopping at 'conduct.' If the provision is content neutral, apply the applicable O'Brien/Ward framework, including substantial nonsuppressive interest, actual advancement, burden no greater than the governing test permits, and usable alternative communication. If content based, use the appropriate strict inquiry. Congress's predictive judgment receives the controlling Turner deference with independent review; its importance is not a substitute for sufficient fit. The Madsen heightened injunction test is not automatically the statute test, and its case-specific distances are not general safe harbors.
6. **Vagueness, overbreadth, and threats.** Analyze the enacted definitions, scienter/motive, protected-conduct savings, and legitimate sweep. R.A.V.'s actual controlling rule is substantial protected coverage not curable by permissible construction; Scalia's within-category content-selection theory is a four-Justice public position, not the Court's law. Mitchell permits relevant motive proof but not punishment of abstract belief. The present record does not authorize a new universal true-threat mental-state rule, criminalization of harsh political persuasion, or immunity for every politically phrased threat. Specify the limiting construction that is textually available rather than relying on the savings clause as an unexplained answer.
7. **Statutory damages and attribution.** Distinguish unprotected violation-based elected damages from compensation for losses caused by protected persuasion. Claiborne's personal-responsibility rule prevents imposing another's wrongdoing solely through shared views, membership, or association. The legal question whether statutory damages may exceed actual loss does not decide the reasonableness of $5,000, number of violations, entitlement, punitive damages, criminal punishment, or a particular person's liability. No award or group-wide culpability is under review.
8. **Constitutional free exercise.** Test objective religious targeting and comparable secular exceptions under Smith/Lukumi, including the statutory parent/minor exception and the challengers' asserted secular-obstruction comparisons. Religious motivation is distinct from the statute's service-related motive. Adverse impact or the prominence of religious opponents does not alone establish targeting, while a secular description of purpose does not defeat an objectively discriminatory operation. Do not presume that every difference in coverage is either comparable or irrelevant.
9. **Independent RFRA application.** First identify the pleaded conduct and any genuine threshold dispute; alternatively state the assumed substantial burden explicitly. If burden is established or assumed, the government bears both production and persuasion on applying the restriction to these persons by the least restrictive means. Direct injury to service-seekers, reliable medical access, and health/safety can matter; the label 'access' and successful intermediate speech scrutiny do not complete RFRA. Address why proposed accommodations preserve or fail the protected interest and why the parent/minor exception is or is not legally comparable. Swanner forbids treating commercial participation or an antidiscrimination label as automatic justification. Its dissent supplies the contrary application-specific argument concerning direct third-party denial and ineffective referral/notice, without changing the governing burden. A remand may preserve inquiry without granting an exemption; a record-sufficient alternative justification must identify its actual pleading and legal predicates.

### Effective authorities and individualized public evidence

Lopez's controlling opinion is Kennedy's bounded commercial-connection rule, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas among the Associates; the Chief Justice's broader six-route concurrence is not governing law.
