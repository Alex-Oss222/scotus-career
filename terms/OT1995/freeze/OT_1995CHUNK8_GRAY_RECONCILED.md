## Complete comparator and strongest competing paths

The complete official report, **518U.S.152–186**, was independently read: the Court opinion and all four notes, Stevens's whole separate dissent, and Ginsburg's whole dissent and all twelve notes. Historically, Rehnquist's opinion was joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It rejected the independent Brady claim on default, rejected the notice-of-evidence claim under Teague, and vacated/remanded for the distinct misrepresentation claim and its procedural defenses. Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, would reverse the Court of Appeals and restore sentencing relief under the existing opportunity-to-answer rule. Stevens added an independent objection to the unreliable uncharged-crime proof even with adequate notice. Historical authorship is evidence of positions, not a simulated assignment. The published dissents do not establish a separate nine-Justice Brady poll.

**Notice versus meaningful opportunity.** The strong argument for the historical majority's ordinary-notice position is that Gardner involved information never disclosed, whereas Gray knew the evidence before it was presented and could cross-examine and testify; Weatherford establishes no general right to discovery. A broad fairness principle cannot make every requested preparation period dictated in1987. The opposing argument is already present in the historical dissent and the actual lower findings: a prosecutor's definite statements-only assurance, reasonable reliance, a materially different eve-of-sentencing presentation, and the resulting inability to investigate and answer a graphic mini-trial can deny the old right itself. Mere eventual disclosure is not an effective opportunity. The five accepting that argument adopt this record-bound distinction; the other three do not accept it as a basis for avoiding Teague on the ordinary-notice component.

**Remand versus sentencing relief.** Remand has a real purpose for a Justice who has not accepted the violation or its effect, or who regards misrepresentation as a distinct claim whose availability remains unresolved. But those tasks cannot be attributed automatically to a Justice who has already accepted the reopened, adequately presented meaningful-opportunity claim and the findings demonstrating both violation and injury. The historical four did exactly that. The original District Court opinion at Supreme Court JA351–353, reproduced in the petition appendix at its printed15–17, finds fundamental, irreparable unfairness and a substantial probability of a different sentencing result. Ginsburg expressly recounts and accepts the nonharmlessness determination, 518U.S.178, and explains the deprivation's operation at182–186. Stevens adds that this evidentiary presentation was constitutionally unreliable even with advance notice. These are positive adjudicative findings and the Justices' acceptance of them, not a lack of source material to be repaired on remand.

The strongest argument against immediate relief is that the Commonwealth challenges the findings and that substantial other aggravating proof existed, including Gray's prior violent convictions; the surviving vileness finding still establishes eligibility. That argument was present on the same record historically. The four accepting the findings distinguish eligibility and abstract sufficiency from the actual selection of death after the graphic Sorrell proof was insulated from effective testing. The inability to investigate, the lost third-party and police-investigation impeachment material, the weakly corroborated linkage, and the actual preparation objections support their conclusion that the error affected that selection. They do not find Timothy Sorrell guilty or grant relief merely because evidence was uncharged.

## Actual-law comparison: what changed and what did not

**Graham and O'Connor.** Actual Graham, January25,1993, replaces the historical Teague rejection of the youth/background application with a controlling holding, joined by O'Connor, that the prefinality functional mitigation requirement reaches a supported deprivation despite formal admission and some permissible use of the evidence. Its explanation rejects a demand for identical earlier facts, while retaining the dictated-at-finality requirement. That is a concrete changed precedent and a changed personal commitment for O'Connor. Here her application of Gardner similarly asks whether the supported opportunity actually existed, rather than treating public presentation and the formal ability to cross-examine as dispositive. Her Lankford join and actual Simmons rule join explain why the assurance and lost preparation matter to her, but those considerations alone are not presented as newly discovered premises. Caspari, her own actual opinion, continues to require a genuine comparison with pre1987 law. Graham does not mechanically decide Gray or turn general fairness into an exception to Teague.

**Keeney.** Actual Keeney retains Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification for inadequately developed material facts on a properly presented claim. It displaces Hall's negligence-to-cause/prejudice alternative. O'Connor authored that rule; Stevens and Kennedy joined it; the other Associates apply its controlling scope without an invented personal endorsement. It does not remove an independent claim default, prove a mandatory hearing predicate, or reopen Brady. It supplies no reason to weaken a writ already supported by a completed federal evidentiary record. The four historical dissenters' entitlement-to-relief premise is strengthened, not displaced, by removing the historical negligence bar.

**Brecht, O'Neal and effect.** Actual Brecht retains Chapman for preserved Doyle error. Actual O'Neal extends it to its preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category; its generalization has only three joins. Neither already decides the Gray category. This genuine legal difference requires precision, but does not itself justify sending every established violation and effect finding back for reconsideration. The remands in those cases addressed an inadequate no-injury determination under the wrong burden, and in O'Neal an assumed combined violation whose existence had not been decided. Gray's District Court instead found the violation and substantial prejudicial effect; the four historically accepted those findings.

Stevens's actual Brecht dissent and O'Neal concurrence favor demanding whole-record Kotteakos review outside the binding Chapman categories. His assessment of the Gray record satisfies that approach; no unexplained switch to universal Chapman is necessary. Souter joined the actual broader Chapman position in O'Neal and the proposed capital-assistance extension in Tuggle. Those positions make State-borne uncertainty, rather than an additional barrier to relief, relevant to him; the accepted findings defeat harmlessness on that view as well. Ginsburg and Breyer reserved O'Neal's general extension. They need not abandon that reservation here: the established deprivation and actual impact defeat harmlessness under either of the competing Chapman and whole-record substantial-influence approaches. This is a record-specific conclusion, not a new general selection between those standards or an assumption that every due-process violation is structural.

**Tuggle and other later law.** Actual Tuggle prevents surviving nonweighing eligibility from automatically curing an independently available injury, but neither selects a universal collateral standard nor finds a violation on its assumed premise. It reinforces the distinction between eligibility and actual sentence selection; its narrower assumed-violation posture is not a changed fact in Gray. Actual Simmons's rule and remedy positions remain relevant to individual judgment, with O'Connor and Kennedy's remedy reservations preserved. Kyles governs available Brady claims and is not a vehicle for reopening the dismissed one. Wood remains a grant only. Rise and the three June17 public entries supply no new capital-disclosure or Teague holding and no cause to revisit closed claims.

**Temporal and statutory limits.** The existing distinctions between Virginia §8.01-654(B)(2) and the separate July1,1995 capital-filing provision remain. No retroactive sixty-day bar to the1990 state petition or actual-knowledge finding is invented. AEDPA's enactment is not proof of a qualifying Virginia chapter154 system or a general decision about every pending-case provision. No new evidentiary hearing is ordered, so no new entitlement is built on an assumed exemption from §2254(e)(2). No identified changed AEDPA predicate supplies a reason to depart from these same-date historical relief positions; the statute's mere existence is not converted into a new defense or a missing-record remand. This handoff makes no general temporal holding or exemption from an otherwise applicable provision.

**Statutory reservation attached to the four-Justice relief direction.** The four decide the reopened claim's prefinality constitutional rule, violation and prejudicial effect on the existing record; they do not decide every1996 amendment's reach in this already-pending case. Before entering an operative writ, the receiving court must resolve any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory requirement material to that relief. In particular, an applicable amended §2254(d) restriction on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court cannot be bypassed merely by the constitutional conclusion here; its temporal reach and actual predicates require their own analysis if material. The same is true of an applicable state-fact presumption or chapter154 requirement. Section107(c)'s pending-case clause does not establish Virginia's qualification or settle chapter153's transition. No express exhaustion waiver, statutory exception or satisfaction of an unadjudicated condition is invented. This is a confined reservation of an actually applicable legal task, not a direction to reopen settled issues or to withhold relief while searching for a hypothetical impediment. The completed federal hearing and a proposed new hearing remain distinct; no new hearing is authorized.
