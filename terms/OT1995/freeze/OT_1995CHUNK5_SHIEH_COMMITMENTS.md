## Present petitions: three separate Rule 39.8 determinations

Each selected ground declines the proposed Rule 39.8 denial **on the present showing**, based on the concrete procedural content below. This is not merely indefinite reservation until the entire lower record is obtained. Nor is it a finding that every contention is arguable, preserved, true or certworthy. Rule 39.8 permits rather than compels a supported denial; ordinary pleading failure is not automatically frivolousness. Denton's factual screen is informative within its limits, not the complete Rule 39.8 test.

**R7 — Kakita, No. 95-7587.** The petition's complete-ban and no-appellate-review characterizations are contradicted by the October 27 order: the court states it reviewed the opening brief and record and closes only the identified dockets to further submissions. The actual appeal concerns March 3 denials of vacatur of June 20, 1994 judgments, not unrestricted direct review. The purported foreign indictment establishes neither misconduct nor new admissible evidence.

Nevertheless this paper identifies an allegedly unserved summary-judgment motion and dismissal order and expressly connects that nonservice to relief or reconsideration. Its identified notice/reopening objection is distinct from the broad corruption narrative and addresses the postjudgment setting. The review recital and closed-docket provision do not answer it. Missing original judgment and reconsideration materials prevent finding service failure or preserved error, but do not establish an unchallenged independent ground defeating the entire contention. The combined defects supporting Martin II are therefore not established. Martin I, Anderson, Attwood and Jones support declining the present denial while leaving finality, jurisdiction, immunity and merits obstacles intact.

The strongest denial path treats the paper as repetitive reopening litigation based on implausible accusations and demonstrably inaccurate descriptions; Zatko and Martin II preserve that route where the actual predicate exists. Its decisive weakness here is failure to dispose separately of the specific nonservice/reopening contention. The strongest objection to R7 is that notice vocabulary cannot immunize an otherwise frivolous paper. This conclusion depends on the identified motion, order and asserted reopening ground, not that vocabulary alone. No malice finding independently supplies the missing basis.

**R8 — Ninth Circuit restriction, No. 95-7588.** This paper directly challenges the December 13 prospective order in No. 95-80348. It gives a concrete sequence: alleged nonreceipt in Taiwan of the October 27 show-cause order, an assertedly inadequate fourteen-day response period, receipt of the December order on January 16, and an unsuccessful January 17 request for the show-cause paper before the January 22 petition. The fax/transmission evidence supports that the request was made, not failed mailing or the alleged telephone account.

The visible order recites show cause and no timely response, excludes counsel-represented and district-certified nonfrivolous appeals from paragraph (1), and permits a reasoned lifting request no earlier than January 1, 1998. These facts defeat an absolute-ban characterization. They do not settle notice or the full permission procedure: the middle page and original show-cause/service materials are absent. The particular notice allegation and documented request remain independently arguable under Anderson, Attwood and Jones.

The strongest denial path relies on the show-cause recital, retained routes and exaggerated description to treat this as a frivolous attack on valid process protection. Its weakness is that those points do not resolve the distinct alleged failure of notice. Conversely, an unproved nonreceipt assertion should not invalidate an order's recital. R8 invalidates nothing; it grants only financial access. It adopts no universal actual-receipt requirement, fourteen-day rule, finding of unlawful restriction or inference of malice.

**R9 — Krieger, No. 95-7589.** This separate paper presents six questions about Taiwan service, treaty/assistance provisions, foreign proceedings, summary judgment/discovery, res judicata and amendment. Its de novo summary-judgment discussion cannot enlarge the actual reopening appeal. Its foreign-prosecution allegations do not establish grounds for comity, discovery or amendment.

Its own body, however, specifically alleges nonservice of the summary-judgment motion and district order and discusses Molloy and Rule 60(b)(6) as grounds for relief from judgment. The October 27 order's Molloy citation for abuse-of-discretion review does not itself resolve that particular notice argument. The March 3 order supplies the denials of reconsideration, discovery and amendment, not the missing papers' contents or proof of service. This independently identified service/reopening issue supports declining denial, without borrowing No. 95-7588's different show-cause allegation or treating Kakita as identical.

The strongest denial path emphasizes reargument, extravagant allegations, the no-new-arguments ruling and the limited reopening posture. It fails to dispose of this paper's express postjudgment nonservice argument on the established record. Conversely, invoking Rule 60 alone cannot establish arguability. Here it accompanies the identified allegedly unserved motion and order; R9 accepts neither the allegation nor Shieh's legal construction. No treaty, general discovery, amendment or foreign-proceeding entitlement is adopted. No specific malice finding is established.

## Prospective control: independent ground and alternative

**P — decline the proposed new condition on future noncriminal certiorari petitions.** Martin III, Day, Sassower, Attwood and Jones require applicant-specific and category-specific support, including why treatment of particular submissions is inadequate. Lower restrictions and repeated litigation warrant scrutiny but do not alone establish abuse of this Court's relevant category or inadequacy of individual treatment. No admitted sequence of earlier Supreme Court Shieh denials, warnings or failed limited responses is established. The lower December 1994 and December 1995 orders are not Supreme Court warnings. This is an evidentiary limit, not a universal warning prerequisite.

**Strongest alternative:** A properly supported prospective fee/form condition retaining paid and criminal routes could protect limited judicial resources under the surviving targeted-control authorities, including McDonald and Sindram within their distinct settings. Serial screening consumes resources; not every conceivable lesser measure must first fail. The objection to refusing control is that lower abuse predicts continuing burden. The decisive objection to imposing it here is that narrowing the remedy and predicting burden do not supply the missing applicant/category/inadequacy connection, as Attwood and Jones expressly explain. The current concrete procedural issues reinforce the distinction without categorically foreclosing future control on a different record. No extraordinary-writ or habeas category is added, and no existing lawful restriction is vacated.
