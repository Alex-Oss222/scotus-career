### Application and limits that all eight retain

Bedingfield was not a class action, and Richards and Hill were not parties. The Alabama majority's stated adequacy basis was essentially identical interests, supplemented by public fiscal finality; respondents additionally emphasize the City's resources and adversarial incentive. These considerations do not establish constitutional authority to determine petitioners' own tax obligations and claims for previously paid sums. The conclusion does not rest on reviewing whether the earlier litigants pursued every argument competently or on requiring this Court to resolve the tax classifications.

The no-notice contention remains a litigant assertion with the packet's cited support. The Court need not convert it into an appellate finding, decide a universal notice formula, or determine every possible connection between the two groups. The supplied record contains no finding of these petitioners' participation in or control of the earlier litigation; that is a limit on the offered basis for this judgment, not an affirmative finding that every conceivable connection was absent. The reversal corrects the constitutional sufficiency of the rationale actually used. It does not direct an exploratory hearing to create a different ground for preclusion.

Hansberry and Martin preserve genuine adequate representation, litigation control and special remedial arrangements consistent with due process. Neither absence of a formal class nor the presence of refund relief is independently a universal rule against representative adjudication. Here the relevant combination is the direct disposition of these petitioners' personal obligations and recovery claims on an asserted representative basis established only through shared interests and prior adversarial resources. The possible practical effects of another person's judgment on members of the public are distinct from conclusively extinguishing their own legal claims. Notice of another lawsuit, were it established, would not by itself create a duty to intervene or supply representative authority; no actual-notice finding is needed for that legal limit.

Actual Matsushita Parts I and IV-A reinforce the difference between settlement capacity, common interests and authority to bind absent persons. Even notice, objections and opt-outs cannot independently cure constitutionally inadequate representation. The present ruling does not decide the full scope of permissible collateral attack reserved by Matsushita IV-B, authorize retrial of ordinary state-law approval errors, or reopen the underlying Bedingfield judgment between persons properly bound by it. Section 1738 does not independently require Alabama to bind these strangers to its own earlier judgment; the constitutional minimum remains independently operative.

Ordinary proceedings on the preserved claims resume on remand subject to legally available defenses and remedial conditions. No existing state-count ruling, earlier Tax Injunction Act dismissal, other taxpayer's properly binding judgment, or federal employees' Buck Act proceeding is reopened. The mandate creates no new claim, directs no particular trial or tax remedy, and guarantees no ultimate recovery. It supplies no special deadline or further Supreme Court event.

## Adversarial alternatives and their consequences

**Affirm on public-right representation.** The strongest case for affirmance is that all affected taxpayers seek the same constitutional answer, the City and prior taxpayers had resources and incentive to contest the tax, and repeated litigation threatens settled public financing. Genuine representative public litigation may bind people who do not individually appear. All eight retain that possibility. It does not sustain this judgment: sameness of interests and vigorous advocacy are not themselves a constitutional representative relationship, while the bar here directly extinguishes petitioners' own liability and recovery claims. Fiscal reliance strengthens the policy for valid finality but cannot establish who was validly bound. The Court need not announce that every constitutional challenge to public action requires individual relitigation.

**Affirm only as issue preclusion.** Respondents' concession that broad claim preclusion may reach too far supports examining whether a necessarily decided exemption issue could supply a smaller bar. This is a real distinction from claims that merely could have been litigated. It does not cure the logically prior defect in binding these nonparties. Neither pleading federal theories in Bedingfield nor the majority's statement that they could have been, and arguably were, adjudicated establishes actual and necessary resolution of each exemption issue. The eight reject this alternative as a basis to sustain the present judgment without deciding that no relevant issue was ever adjudicated. No further retrieval is needed for that conclusion; a different disposition turning on precise prior issue adjudication would require the packet's identified passage-level verification through control.

**Vacate solely for a new adequacy inquiry.** The strongest narrower-remand objection is that the packet does not establish every possible fact about notice, control or authorization. A court should not infer factual absence from an operator's source limits. The commitments accept that caution but do not find a missing-paper remand necessary: the supplied judgment rests on an identified, constitutionally insufficient representative rationale. Reversal corrects that legal error without finding the unestablished facts and without mandating discovery or an evidentiary opportunity to construct a new bar. Properly available issues remain subject to ordinary law on remand; the Court does not promise a second chance to prove a new theory or foreclose a separately established recognized relationship by invented facts.

**Dismiss for lack of §1257 finality.** The permissive interlocutory label and continuing Buck Act litigation create the strongest threshold objection. The supplied orders nevertheless finally determine the federal nonparty-binding question and leave Richards and Hill's own claims practically concluded; the remaining implementation cannot make their objection disappear. Cox's first category supplies the specific basis. Casarotto's distinct fourth-category arbitration rationale is not transplanted, and Quackenbush's §1291 routes do not replace §1257. Each Associate accepts review on the supported first-category ground, not on a nonexistent later final judgment or an invented concession by counsel.
