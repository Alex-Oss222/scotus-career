# Beadle neutral party-record supplement

The preparation context read all 41 PDF pages of the City's May 17, 1995 opposition (`sources/neutral-b/Beadle_02.txt`) and all 15 PDF pages of Beadle's May 26, 1995 reply (`sources/neutral-b/Beadle_09.txt`). These are party submissions, not findings adopted by this Court. No current Supreme Court outcome is supplied. The authentic lower findings in the petition appendix remain essential to distinguish evidence, advocacy and findings.

## City's record and argument

The City describes a 16-week field-training program: two weeks' ungraded orientation, four weeks' graded training on the initial squad, two four-week rotations, then two weeks with the initial squad and training officer. The changes expose a recruit to different supervisors, techniques and areas. The City cites R1-41-VIII-M, DX9, and R4-71-107–108, 180–183, 206, 237–239. Trained field-training officers grade recruits; a one-time surplus-recruit arrangement during orientation did not eliminate that grading requirement (R4-71-239–241). The downtown weekday squad was not a training squad (R4-71-205–208). Squad days off rotated one day every 56 days; the opposition later calls the first cycle fifty days, so the source inconsistency should not be silently converted into a new finding.

The collective agreement's section 11.3 treats classification seniority as a factor when skill, performance and ability are equal and the supervisor determines the task is unaffected, but expressly says seniority is not a consideration when training affects assignments. Thus no mandatory seniority bar to the proposed training transfer follows merely from the existence of the agreement.

The City allowed June 18, 1988 off without pay once, said future accommodations could not be made, and describes Beadle's ensuing meetings and resignation on June 22 or 23. It relies on Fox's testimony about consistency, multiple trainers and potential liability (R4-71-182, 238–240), describing a single-trainer arrangement as educationally inferior. It also invokes Beadle's asserted admission that he knew of no accommodation (R4-71-141–142). This is the City's characterization, disputed by the reply's multiple-trainer alternatives. Expert testimony from the Hillsborough case was applicable to both actions by counsel's agreement; both experts regarded safety as paramount, but the City's further inference about morale and safety is not itself proof that a specific alternative produces a particular risk.

Opposition pp. 16–25 frames the proposal as Dawson's squad for the whole training period followed by the downtown assignment. It contends the rotations are necessary and a future suitable permanent assignment speculative. It acknowledges that accommodation arguably would cost no additional money but argues operational inefficiency and safety are independently material. It defends the lower court's public-safety treatment, denies a categorical police exception, and separately argues that a permanent religious preference would raise Establishment Clause concerns. Its release/waiver defense was rejected below and it expressly states that issue is not before the Court (p. 13 n.4). Its neutral-rotation-as-accommodation argument is incorporated from the separate Hillsborough petition opposition rather than developed here (p. 16 n.5); the two controversies must remain distinct.

## Reply's disputed alternatives and precise concessions

Beadle does not contend that only cash expenditures count. He expressly accepts tangible lost efficiency as an economic cost (reply p. 1 n.1 and pp. 2, 5). He contends generalized inconvenience or employee resentment is insufficient. He accepts that leaving him with one trainer throughout would lose the benefit of other trainers (p. 6), while insisting his actual proposals retained multiple trainers. He also accepts that proved inadequate training under all alternatives could establish undue hardship (pp. 7–8). None of these is a concession that every proposed accommodation compromises safety or training.

The reply identifies three alternatives in the trial appendix at 25a–26a; the petition/appendix must supply their full terms. It specifically describes retaining Dawson's squad while rotating between two training officers. Cook allegedly admitted this would neither compromise Beadle's training nor adversely affect the program and that the department used such training by his deposition: Cook Dep. R2-66-43:22–44:10; 126:18–129:20 (reply p. 7 and n.6). Beadle invokes that later practice as evidence about feasibility, not as the historical 1988 arrangement. He quotes the trial court's inability to conclude the transfer deprived another trainee of a neutral-scheduling benefit (App. 27a; reply p. 3 continuation of n.3).

For temporary absence, Beadle cites Fox's testimony that regular squad officers maintained efficiency when a trainee was away (R2-67-194:6–195:24), other street officers could cover regular-officer absence (R2-67-186:1–12), and the City's expert considered patrol absences easier than jail-detention absences (R3-70-24:2–25:8), reply p. 2 n.2. These are party-supplied transcript references, not independently read transcripts.

For permanent assignment, the reply quotes App. 18a: the downtown squad offered a possible permanent accommodation, worked weekdays, and assignments lay in commanders' discretion. It disputes the City's characterization as necessarily a favored job, pointing to Pegram Dep. R2-62-26:8–27:15 and the trial court's advantages/disadvantages discussion. A possible assignment is not a finding of an available guaranteed vacancy.

The reply quotes EEOC's appellate position that the City's actual hardship evidence must be evaluated as to the particular alternatives, with job and operational consequences considered. It maintains that the lower court's quoted general police-work statement at App. 27a–28a did not do that work. This is a contest over both the legal threshold and the factual sufficiency of the actual alternatives; neither side's characterization resolves it. No retrospective finding about safety, feasibility, resignation causation or ultimate entitlement is supplied here.
