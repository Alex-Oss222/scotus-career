### Exact provisions and attributed arguments

Petitioner's merits brief appendix 1–3 and respondent's brief 2–4 reproduce §3553(e), §994(n), and §5K1.1. Section 3553(e) gives authority, “[u]pon motion of the Government,” to impose a sentence below a statutory minimum to reflect substantial assistance in the investigation or prosecution of another person who committed an offense. Its second sentence requires imposition in accordance with Commission guidelines and policy statements under 28 U.S.C. §994. Section 994(n) directs the Commission to assure that its Guidelines reflect the general appropriateness of lower sentences for that assistance, including sentences below statutory minima. Section 5K1.1 begins with a Government motion stating that the defendant provided the specified assistance and permits departure “from the guidelines.”

Section 5K1.1(a) leaves the appropriate reduction to the court for stated reasons that may include, but are not limited to: the court's assessment of significance/usefulness taking account of the Government's evaluation; truthfulness/completeness/reliability; nature/extent; injury or danger/risk to the defendant or family resulting from assistance; and timeliness. Those are nonexclusive authorized considerations, not a requirement that every factor be proved. Application note 1 permits a below-statute sentence under the circumstances set forth in §3553(e) and §994(n); note 2 separates assistance from acceptance of responsibility; note 3 gives substantial weight to the Government's evaluation, particularly where assistance is hard to assess. No text allows the Commission to amend a statute merely by commentary.

Petitioner's stated merits question is whether the assistance motion supplies authority beneath both the range and statutory floor even when the Government does not seek the latter departure. His brief 5–8 and 41 argues a unitary motion/Commission scheme, judicial determination of extent once assistance is acknowledged, commentary support, and lenity as a last resort. Respondent's brief 2–7 and 20–24 argues that the statutory relief requires Government authorization of that relief and that §994(n) and Guidelines commentary preserve rather than displace §3553(e). Whether authorization must cite a particular section or appear in a separate piece of paper is analytically distinct from whether a request confined to Guidelines relief activates statutory authority. The source record does not require inventing a second mandatory document.

### Material statutory details the runtime paraphrase omits or abbreviates

Petition 2–4 quotes the 1995 statute:

- §302(2)(a) concerns necessity to save the woman's life in the attending physician's professional judgment.
- §302(2)(b) covers pregnancy resulting from rape or rape of a child under §§76-5-402 and 402.1 **reported to law enforcement before abortion**. Section (c) separately covers incest under §76-5-406(10) or §76-7-102, likewise **reported before abortion**. The reporting conditions must not disappear if the exceptions are recited.
- Sections (d) and (e) respectively concern prevention of grave damage to the woman's medical health and prevention of birth of a child with grave defects, in the attending physician's professional judgment.
- §302(3) permits abortion after twenty weeks from conception only for the purposes and circumstances of (2)(a), (d), and (e). It does not textually use individualized viability or retain rape/incest as independent exceptions.
- §307 applies when the unborn child is sufficiently developed to have any reasonable possibility of survival outside the womb. It requires the procedure that, in the physician's best medical judgment, gives the child the best survival chance, and forbids a procedure designed to kill or injure unless necessary in the woman's physician's opinion to prevent grave damage to her medical health.
- §308 requires use of all the performing physician's medical skills to attempt to promote, preserve, and maintain the life of a child sufficiently developed to have such a reasonable possibility of survival, consistently with saving the woman's life or preventing grave damage to her medical health.

Section 317 declares invalid provisions and applications severable and says the legislature would have passed each provision irrespective of invalidity elsewhere. Section 317.2 separately provides: “If Section 76-7-302 as amended by Senate Bill 23, 1991 Annual General Session, is ever held to be unconstitutional by the United States Supreme Court, Section 76-7-302, as enacted by Chapter 33, Laws of Utah 1974, is reenacted and immediately effective.” That conditional reenactment text is distinct from the general severability clause.

The Tenth Circuit, petition appendix A9–A13, viewed (3) as an interdependent modification of (2)'s comprehensive ban and treated the substantive legislative purpose as prevailing over general severability language. It relied on Utah decisions concerning interdependent statutes, including Salt Lake City v. International Ass'n of Firefighters, 563 P.2d 786; State v. Salt Lake City, 445 P.2d 691; and Carter, 399 P.2d 440. It additionally read §317.2 as a specific contingency concerning §302. The State disputes those readings; they are lower-court reasoning, not an authoritative Utah Supreme Court construction of this abortion statute. The State did not appeal the invalidation of §302(2). Respondents additionally argue that even the severed provision would have forbidden previability abortions and that the choice-of-method duties require an impermissible maternal-health tradeoff. The State's description of twenty weeks as postviability is a litigant characterization, not a medical finding that every fetus is viable then.
