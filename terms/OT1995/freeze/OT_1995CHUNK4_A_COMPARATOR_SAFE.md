## Official-source comparator verification

These historical facts enter only reconciliation; they are not simulated law or a model of any Justice's vote.

- **Peacock, 516 U.S. 349, 350, 360–362:** Thomas's opinion is joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stevens alone dissents (8–1). Stevens accepts that imposing the entire original debt is excessive but would preserve jurisdiction to restore assets fraudulently diverted by a person controlling the judgment debtor. He distinguishes a new prejudgment co-liability claim from postjudgment frustration of collection. The full report, including his footnote on how the parties characterized the suit, was read. [Official full text](../sources/chunk4_control/USREPORTS-516-349.txt); [official PDF](../sources/chunk4_control/USREPORTS-516-349.pdf).
- **Jones, 516 U.S. 363–364:** Breyer took no part; the seven other historical participants support the per curiam order and Stevens dissents by reference to his earlier filing-control dissents. The order denies the fee motion, preserves payment and Rule 33.1 compliance through March 18, 1996, and adds a prospective noncriminal-certiorari fee-and-form restriction. It does not itself decide the underlying petition's merits. The full report was read. [Official full text](../sources/chunk4_control/USREPORTS-516-363.txt).
- **Maine, 516 U.S. 365–366:** Souter took no part; no published numerical vote accompanies the decree. The published decree supplies five closing lines and retained jurisdiction. Objective participation and schedule facts remain distinct from importing the historical action. The full report was read. [Official full text](../sources/chunk4_control/USREPORTS-516-365.txt); [official PDF](../sources/chunk4_control/USREPORTS-516-365.pdf).
