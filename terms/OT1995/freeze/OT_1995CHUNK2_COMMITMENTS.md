## Klinger v. Department of Corrections — December 4, 1995

### Review scope and common legal conclusions

Reach the expressly assigned merits review of the identified Eighth Circuit interlocutory judgment, No. 93-2928, 31 F.3d 727. The historical petition identifier 94-7589 identifies the supplied source, not a newly invented grant. No separate grant wording or subsequent final judgment is presumed. Section 1292(b) explains the appeal below; the present task supplies the Supreme Court merits assignment. The review concerns program equal protection, not the separate Title IX or access-to-courts claims.

**Provisional disposition of all eight:** Affirm the reversal of the district court's existing equal-protection liability determination, vacate the direction to dismiss the reviewed equal-protection claims, and remand through the Eighth Circuit for application of the proper claim-specific standard. The district court's assumption that sex-separated housing plus an inferior program necessarily establishes purposeful sex discrimination cannot itself sustain liability; the appellate court's categorical rejection of comparison across the selected institutions cannot establish that no challenged program decision is discriminatory. The table's references to vacatur/remand use this expressly divided judgment, not restoration of district-court liability.

Similarity is assessed in relation to the challenged treatment. Population, security, duration of confinement, feasible program operation, and resource allocation can materially distinguish particular decisions; differences between entire institutions do not settle every program claim in advance. Neither identical programs nor equal expenditure per inmate is constitutionally required by this holding. It does not authorize comparison with new male institutions after the denied amendment or create an unpleaded discriminatory-funding claim.

For an actual sex classification in providing the challenged benefits, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between the classification and that objective. An actual classification may appear in the policy or its administration; a writing expressly admitting discrimination is unnecessary. If the relevant choice is genuinely sex neutral, disparate outcomes and awareness of those outcomes do not themselves prove discriminatory purpose. The claimant must establish that the challenged choice was made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; circumstantial proof remains available. Housing segregation alone does not decide which branch governs each program decision. Turner supplies attention to actual prison administration and its legitimate constraints; it supplies no categorical immunity from this inquiry and does not automatically convert every sex-based benefit allocation into an ordinary institutional distinction.

These are applications of Craig, Hogan, Davis, Arlington Heights and Feeney, informed by the current Kelley and J.E.B. holdings. Kelley's Title IX proportionality safe harbor and sports-specific alternatives are not prison equal-protection tests. J.E.B.'s peremptory-strike burden-production sequence is not imported as a new general prison procedure. The inquiry described here uses the ordinary distinction between an established classification and purposeful discrimination under a neutral policy.

## Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States — December 6, 1995

### Use component and proposed remedy

The supplied posture permits merits review of the firearm-use convictions in Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492. The question is the meaning of use, not the validity of the separate drug convictions or every possible carrying theory. All eight provisionally reach that question.

**Provisional disposition of all eight:** Reverse the appellate judgments insofar as they sustain the §924(c)(1) convictions on the supplied possession/storage-as-use ground, and remand. Actual employment of the firearm in the predicate offense is required to establish use. Keeping it available, storing it near drugs or proceeds, or relying on its possible future availability does not itself satisfy that element. The separate during-and-in-relation-to condition still must be established; facilitation or potential facilitation cannot substitute for the separate act of using or carrying.

Current Smith remains binding: offering a firearm as consideration in a drug exchange is actual employment and is use even though the gun is employed as merchandise rather than as a weapon. This case neither overturns Smith nor restricts use to firing. Reading use to require an actual employment gives independent work to the use/carry language and avoids rewriting it as possession in connection with trafficking. The Government's contention that weapons kept near contraband strengthen the criminal operation addresses a meaningful danger and may support the relation element; it does not satisfy the different conduct element Congress enacted.

## 1. Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue — December 11, 1995

**Non-Stone disposition:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would **affirm the Eighth Circuit's reversal and remedial remand**. Stevens would **reverse the Eighth Circuit and leave the school-favoring district-court judgment in place**. ### Law, reach and record

The speech question is preserved: the lower opinion identifies the posttrial argument and the district court's decision on viewpoint discrimination. The case seeks declaratory and injunctive access relief. The actual 3–6 p.m. arrangements, not the possibility of access after 6, define the dispute. The district court found both the Scouts and the Club concerned with moral development while distinguishing secular skill activities from Christian faith instruction. The policy allows Scout speech only with an express exclusion of religious speech and activity. The legal question is whether that is a neutral activity definition or exclusion of a perspective from an admitted subject.

**Applicable register:** “Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits,” present operation through Rosenberger and Chabad, June 29, 1995, preserving Lamb's Chapel, June 7, 1993. Lamb's Chapel forbids viewpoint exclusion even in a nonpublic school forum but does not abolish genuine subject limits. Chabad requires actual private attribution and rejects a categorical location defense; Pinette creates neither universal forum immunity nor a mandatory disclaimer formula. Rosenberger's printing program does not itself decide this facilities case. Lemon remains governing establishment law; no later school-club decision is used.

The operative categories remain distinct:

- Section 21103(b)(1) says that duty time begins on reporting and ends at final release.
- Section 21103(b)(2) makes time engaged in or connected with train movement duty time. Section 21103(b)(3) makes other service for the carrier duty time during a 24-hour period in which the employee is engaged in or connected with train movement. These service commands survive the transportation classification.
- Section 21103(b)(4) counts deadhead transportation **to** a duty assignment as duty time, but transportation **from** an assignment to final release as neither duty nor off-duty time.
- Sections 21103(b)(5)–(6) count an interim rest period outside a designated terminal, or one shorter than four hours at a designated terminal, as duty time. Section (b)(7) separately excludes from duty an interim period of at least four hours with suitable food and lodging facilities when a casualty, track obstruction, act of God, or qualifying unknown and unforeseeable derailment or major equipment failure prevents return to the designated terminal. None supplies a finding about an interim rest period here.
- Under §21103(a), subject to its distinct subsection (c) emergency provision, an employee may not remain or go on duty without eight consecutive hours off in the prior twenty-four hours; after twelve consecutive hours on duty, the employee must have ten consecutive hours off. The subsection (c) permission concerns a wreck or relief crew's emergency-related work for up to four additional hours in twenty-four, ending when the track is cleared and the line reopens. This classification does not invoke that permission or excuse required off-duty time.

## Competing lawful paths and common sufficient ground

**Affirmance path:** Transportation denotes the return undertaking, including waiting for its conveyance after the work assignment is complete. It need not mean only minutes when a vehicle moves. Section (b)(4) is a specific classification within the general reporting-to-final-release span in (b)(1). The post-assignment waiting described in the packet is consequently neither on-duty nor off-duty time, so the en banc judgment should be affirmed on the submitted question. Actual service under (b)(2) or (b)(3) still counts as duty; movement to a new assignment remains the opposite, on-duty branch of (b)(4).

**Strongest objection:** Waiting is not physical conveyance; (b)(1) otherwise runs duty to final release, the carrier retains control, and fatigue during an extended wait implicates the Act's safety purpose. Sections (b)(5)–(6) show that inactivity is not ordinarily enough to stop the duty clock. **Answer:** The return-transportation exception already reaches a period before final release when the employee is not fully free. Control and lack of rest therefore cannot themselves distinguish excluded transportation from its waiting incidents. The interim-rest provisions and actual-service provisions continue to govern their own factual predicates. Congress's express refusal to count return transportation as rest prevents the supposed implication that all excluded waiting replenishes the employee's statutory rest.

**Reversal path:** Read transportation as actual conveyance, apply (b)(1) to pre-conveyance waiting, and reverse the categorical exclusion of that waiting from duty. Its strongest support is the general final-release rule and the protective function of counting otherwise constrained inactivity. **Decisive difficulty:** Neither the word transportation in its setting nor the separate off-duty restriction requires this moving-vehicle boundary. That boundary divides an otherwise unified return process without changing the employee's freedom or service obligations. The Associates below reject it on this record; none treats the statute's safety purpose as a reason to erase a specific classification Congress enacted.
