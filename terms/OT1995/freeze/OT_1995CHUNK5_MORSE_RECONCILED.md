## Final Justice-specific commitments

### Stevens

**§5:** Reverse. Virginia's statutory recognition of this qualifying party's choice, certification and preferential general-ballot access confer power over an official stage of electoral choice. A monetary prerequisite to participation in that choice affects voting under §§5/14, Allen and the White Primary Cases; using a convention does not exempt the function. The two conditions in §51.7 are met here, and its primary example is not exhaustive. His rationale is not limited to a constitutional rule that only virtually certain electoral winners perform public functions. He may explain the party-office/delegate route and the practical-primary allegation, with disputed practice and historical baseline expressly reserved.

His historical opinion and actual Presley dissent/Holder positions support this functional account. He treats the statutory text and history as sufficient without requiring administrative deference to create authority the statute lacks. The actual Holder coverage holding replaces its historical counterpart as law; it does not turn §2's distinct comparison and liability rules into §5 rules. Williams is not an independently controlling exclusion on this record; its incomplete source cannot support an enlarged account of either rationale.

**Association:** The specified fee-preclearance duty does not compel a party message or candidate. The historical opinion recognizes associational rights, rejects the asserted impediment to this fee's submission, and leaves hypothetical applications open. Hurley's actual material-expression rule reinforces attention to the burden; it does not provide a new general exemption or require proof that every internal rule is unprotected.

**§10:** Reverse. Allen's private-enforcement reasoning, the Act's rights and enforcement provisions, its contemporary interpretive context and the 1975 amendments establish congressional intent for this specific private action. This is not a holding that Allen already decided §10 or that every useful statutory suit exists. Actual Central Bank gives received enforcement and later legislative treatment weight within defined statutory responsibility; it supplies support by analogy, not the VRA action itself.

**Remedy/reservations:** Reverse both dismissal grounds and remand; retain the case-specific mootness conclusion above. No poll-tax merits finding, refund, new convention, general damages rule or constitutional-count adjudication. He can join a narrower correct fee application without withdrawing his supported broader explanation, but his views do not supply other Justices' joins.

### Breyer

**§5:** Reverse. The Act's text, history and purpose foreclose an exclusion that would leave convention substitutes for nominating primaries outside voting protection. This pleaded convention closely resembles a primary: qualified voters willing to profess loyalty can participate subject to the payment, and its nominee receives Virginia's statutory ballot advantages. That combination suffices. He does not need to establish when all party activity is state action or which other rules are constitutionally susceptible to preclearance.

The fee is sufficiently connected to voting and sufficiently removed from the central concerns about regulating platforms, debate and expressive choice to require submission here. His historical invocation of Presley and the §51.7 exclusions supports this line. The Twenty-Fourth Amendment analogy identifies the type of participation burden; it is not a merits finding that this private convention charge violates that Amendment or §10. The provisional purely official-nomination formulation must include these factual and constitutional limits rather than treating every possible nomination procedure as interchangeable.

**§10:** Reverse for the Act-specific congressional-intent reasons in his historical concurrence: Allen applies with similar force; textual differences are considered but not dispositive; Congress's 1975 retention and amendment of §10 with recognition of private voting enforcement confirm the inference. Actual Varity and Meghrig require the separate action/remedy inquiries without defeating it. No personal position is assigned from pre-appointment cases he did not decide.

**Remedy/reservations:** Reverse and remand both statutory grounds without deciding fee legality, refund or the separate nomination-method claim. The exact constitutional boundaries for other party rules remain open, and no administrative-deference rule is needed to overcome a statutory absence of authority.

### Scalia

**§5:** Affirm on the statutory objections reflected in his full historical join of Thomas, with an independently important avoidance objection. Political parties are not themselves the “State or political subdivision” Congress named; constitutional state-action analogies do not by themselves expand the enacted actor category. The statute's enumerated primary, special and general elections and the regulation's own delegated-authority condition cannot simply be replaced by the proposition that nomination affects the eventual election. The preserved Virginia autonomy, private selection and independent-candidacy features reinforce the conclusion. This is stronger than a finding that the current plaintiffs have merely failed to produce enough delegation evidence.

He also regards party candidate selection as central expressive association and prior federal permission for convention voting changes as a substantial constitutional difficulty. His historical separate writing calls the disputed preclearance an objectionable prior restraint and insists on the distinction between unconstitutional racial exclusion and the ability to change ordinary party rules without prior federal permission. That concrete timing/control objection cannot be omitted as merely a generalized dislike of regulation. It supports the statutory construction and avoidance; it does not add a separate facial constitutional judgment. Hurley does not foreclose it: prior governmental control over the party's nominating process is the asserted material burden, not ordinary customer identity alone.

His related overbreadth and uncertainty objections also remain: even if preclearance of this fee could constitutionally be required in isolation, that as-applied conclusion does not answer the substantial range of protected party activity reached by the interpretation. Leaving the broader covered class undefined can chill party activity now, and reserving every difficult application for later supplies neither the limiting construction nor the treatment of overbreadth that he considers necessary. He rejects the premise that Congress had to address every discriminatory practice through this particular preclearance provision; some party regulation under other parts of the Act does not prove §5 coverage. These are reasons for rejecting the broader construction, not a separate decree invalidating the Act or a finding that hypothetical practices occurred here. Thomas's full join in Scalia's writing includes these overbreadth and uncertainty objections, as well as the prior-restraint and avoidance grounds; no changed premise supports dropping them for either Justice.

**§10:** Affirm for the provision-specific reasons in Thomas Part II: the express Attorney General direction and accompanying scheme do not establish the additional private action. Allen's §5 action is left intact. Actual Central Bank's received-action holding governs securities assistance, while his recorded dissent and Suter position support this distinct statutory inquiry; historical Central Bank cannot be quoted as current Court law.

**Remedy/reservations:** Affirm both statutory dismissals; no determination of the separate constitutional claims or the fee's ultimate poll-tax status. His statutory construction does not give political parties immunity from the Constitution or immunize a covered State's own changed electoral rules. No all-purpose invalidation of §5 or §51.7 is committed.

### Kennedy

**§5:** Affirm on his own statutory entity and state-responsibility ground. Section 5's extraordinary prior-approval obligation applies to the named covered governments; the constitutional doctrine making some private conduct state action does not automatically make a political party itself a covered statutory entity. A State's election practice, including a change by which it makes party rules legally operative or facilitates discriminatory nomination, can be challenged as the State's practice under §5. That is distinct from making every party directly submit its procedures because its nominee receives ballot recognition. Virginia's existing recognition and party autonomy do not establish the particular statutory basis for the direct duty asserted here.

His historical dissent questions the practical boundary of federal control over party procedure and the constitutional costs of assigning that role to a politically accountable federal officer. Retain that concern without inventing misconduct by any official. He does not adopt Thomas's entire statutory-election or White Primary analysis; his historical Thomas join is Part II only. Neither a general party immunity from constitutional law nor a holding that only government-operated primaries can ever be protected is attributed to him.

In particular, Kennedy does not decide whether these appellants would prevail on constitutional state-action principles or define the full reach of Congress's Reconstruction Amendment enforcement powers. Smith and Terry remain available to the separately pending constitutional claims. His statutory construction is an independent ground even though he regards avoidance as additional support if ambiguity were found.

**§10:** Affirm under the separately joined Thomas Part II congressional-intent analysis. His actual Central Bank dissent concerns new statutory responsibility, his Musick, Peeler opinion permits allocation within established shared liability, and Varity gives an express residual grant effect. They leave a coherent basis for requiring the missing specific authorization here without treating all implied remedies as impossible.

**Remedy/reservations:** Affirm Counts III/IV on these independent statutory grounds. Do not decide the remaining constitutional counts, award money, or convert the incomplete Williams materials into an independently sufficient holding.

### Thomas

**§5:** Affirm on each of the distinct grounds in his historical Part I, subject to the actual entering-law qualifications. First, the named statutory actors are governments, not every private association treated as a state actor for some constitutional purpose. Constitutional state action would not itself erase that textual distinction; in any event, Virginia has not delegated the particular governmental function he regards as required, and its preferences do not convert this private nomination into a state election. Second, the statutory voting definition identifies primary, special and general elections; references to party office and nomination do not independently add every convention. Third, a contrary construction creates substantial party-association and prior-approval difficulties, supporting avoidance. His full historical Scalia join confirms the last concern.

The White Primary Cases remain constitutional authority; he distinguishes their governmental delegation or effective substitution features without requiring racial discrimination as a prerequisite to preclearance of a change that actually is covered. His actual Holder/De Grandy position concerning ballot access rather than dilution remains his noncontrolling view and is not imported as governing law. Actual Holder's contrary §2 coverage holding does not decide the separate §5 actor and convention-definition questions. The regulation cannot extend §5 beyond its authority, while Presley's substantial-consideration rule remains binding within the statutory boundary.

He also rejects treating a party's legal concession about the regulation as controlling the statute's meaning; the presented §5-coverage question permits deciding its actual boundary. That response does not reopen an unpresented factual claim. His association argument includes the Party's asserted funding and organizational choices and the risk of compulsory admission after denial of preclearance; the financing justification remains advocacy, not a found fact. His alternative constitutional-attribution analysis supports the statutory result without disposing of the separately pending constitutional counts. His historical reliance on Williams is recorded, but the incomplete neutral source does not authorize making an unverified Williams rationale independently dispositive here.

**§10:** Affirm under his historical Part II statutory-intent ground, with actual Central Bank accurately distinguished. Section 10's declaration and express enforcement direction are not, in his reading, a privately enforceable statutory command comparable to the §5 protection recognized in Allen. Sections 3, 12 and 14 demonstrate private enforcement elsewhere without conclusively identifying this new action. The opposing historical concurrence's Allen/context argument is directly rejected; its usefulness alone cannot establish congressional intent. Actual Central Bank is a confined received-action holding against which he dissented, not current authority rejecting all further implied actions.

**Remedy/reservations:** Affirm both statutory dismissals, leave substantive poll-tax and constitutional merits open, and award no relief under the rejected theories. Broader constitutional exclusion of parties from all federal voting protection is not part of this commitment.
