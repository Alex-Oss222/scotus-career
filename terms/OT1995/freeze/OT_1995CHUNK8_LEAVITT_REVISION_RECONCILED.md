## Complete statutory/current-law qualifications

Section 302(2)(a) concerns life-saving necessity in the attending physician's professional judgment; (b) pregnancy from rape or rape of a child reported to law enforcement before abortion; (c) incest likewise reported before abortion; (d) prevention of grave damage to the woman's medical health; and (e) prevention of birth of a child with grave defects, the last two also assessed in the attending physician's professional judgment. Section 302(3) applies after twenty weeks from conception and incorporates only (a), (d) and (e). It supplies neither individualized viability nor separate rape/incest exceptions. The State's viability characterization is advocacy, not a medical finding.

Section 307 applies when there is any reasonable possibility of survival outside the womb, requires the procedure offering the best survival chance in the physician's best medical judgment, and forbids a procedure designed to kill or injure unless necessary in the woman's physician's opinion to prevent grave damage to her medical health. Section 308 requires use of all medical skills to attempt to promote, preserve and maintain the life of a child sufficiently developed for that survival possibility, consistently with saving the woman's life or preventing grave damage to her medical health. Their actual triggers and exceptions cannot be replaced by assumed Casey-compliant language.

HB206's §310.5 has its own defined dilation-and-extraction and saline procedures, excludes the listed dismemberment-before-removal, suction-curettage and suction-aspiration methods from the first definition, and uses the physician's best clinical judgment on known particular facts and reasonably available information/technology to determine realistic viability with or without temporary artificial support. The procedure prohibition yields when **all other available abortion procedures** would pose a risk to life or health; its own felony clause requires intentional, knowing and willful violation. The disclosed device-word transcription uncertainty is immaterial to this review's chosen scope and is not silently resolved.

Amended §314 retains its distinct intentional unauthorized-abortion offense, exempts the woman from criminal liability for seeking or obtaining her own abortion, retains willful-violation penalties for the listed provisions including §§307–308 and §310.5, and treats other violations as class A misdemeanors. Section 315's insufficient-time serious-emergency exception lists §§302, 304(2), 305(2) and 310.5, not §§307–308. Section 4 confines the new viability definition to §310.5 and its purposes and disclaims effects on other provisions/litigation. These distinctions defeat an assumption of automatic repeal but do not themselves adjudicate repeal or mootness.

If federal merits are later properly reached, the **entire** current Casey rule remains governing: identify the enacted duty and protected baseline; function and purpose; attributable interference and stable affected population; independent invalidity for baseline violation, objectively proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle; compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference; substantiated justification and fit for remaining meaningful interference; clinical and truthful-communication safeguards; and corresponding relief. Challengers first prove triggering interference. Individualized viability and timely necessary physical and mental health care remain protected. Fargo's notice/clinical clarity holdings do not establish substantive sufficiency of every health exception. Miller's indivisible-condition remedy and Dalton's preservation of independent lawful applications coexist. This reservation makes no new merits holding or additional facial-review test.
