## Event, participation and review

Certiorari review of the Federal Circuit's affirmance, 59 F.3d 1234, of the Court of Federal Claims' refund judgment holding 26 U.S.C. §4371 unconstitutional as applied to the export-transit insurance premiums. The Government seeks relief from that judgment; IBM seeks its preservation. The submitted question is whether the Export Clause bars that application. No broader facial challenge or separate grant is presumed.

The event-date Court is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Stevens does not participate; eight Justices participate.** No reason for that nonparticipation is supplied or inferred.

The relevant statutory terms must travel together. Section 4371(1) imposes four cents per dollar or fraction of premium for casualty insurance/indemnity bonds issued to or for an insured defined in §4372(d). Section 4372(a) covers a nonresident alien individual, foreign partnership or foreign corporation and includes obligations in the nature of an indemnity bond; the version after the 1976 amendment excludes a foreign government or municipal/other corporation exercising taxing power. Casualty insurance in §4372(b) covers a nonlife policy or other instrument making, continuing or renewing insurance. Section 4372(d)(1) covers a domestic corporation/partnership or resident individual for risks wholly or partly within the United States; (d)(2) separately covers a foreign corporation/partnership or nonresident individual engaged in domestic trade or business for domestic risks. The challenged export application is within that broader scheme.

For the tax period, §4373(1)'s exemption concerns a policy/bond/annuity signed or countersigned by the insurer's officer or agent in a State or D.C. where the insurer is authorized to do business. Section 4373(2) separately exempts specified indemnity bonds securing federal payments or replacement/payment of federal instruments. Section 4374 identifies persons making, signing, issuing, selling or benefiting from the instruments, and exempts the United States and its agencies/instrumentalities. The 1982 Code notes the 1976 wording and liability changes; no new question about those transitional details is presented. Do not back-project a later effectively-connected-income version of §4373 onto these assessments.

## Supported contrary paths and reach constraints

1. Retain and apply Thames & Mersey to this insurance application: export immunity may attach to a transaction sufficiently bound to exportation even within a general nondiscriminatory tax. The opposing argument is that nondiscrimination and the changed treatment of analogous taxes undermine the older rule; that argument requires comparison of the distinct constitutional texts and functions, not transfer by label.
2. Reconsider the older Export Clause rule on the Government's actual nondiscrimination submission: a generally applicable foreign-insurance tax may be characterized as an ordinary transaction charge rather than a prohibited export burden. The contrary case is the Clause's categorical language and the direct export-insurance precedent; incidental economic effects and taxes on this insurance cannot be assumed equivalent.
3. Independently test the narrower goods-versus-services or constitutional-incidence argument only after resolving its actual presentation and lawful reach. The reported oral statements support neither an invented unequivocal separate submission nor automatic forfeiture. If reached, consider the shipment-specific premium facts and the precedent's scope; if declined, explain why the preserved nondiscrimination question remains sufficient. This is an analytical possibility, not a fabricated filing or selected disposition.
