## Question, facts and lower grounds

The presented question is whether FAA §2 preempts Montana's requirement of a particular first-page notice as a condition of enforcing an arbitration agreement. Petitioners DAI and Lombardi ask for reversal and instructions to reinstate the district stay until arbitration under the agreement. Respondents Paul and Pamela Casarotto ask for affirmance. The case concerns the arbitration forum, not adjudication of the underlying fraud, contract or other damages claims.

DAI's April 25, 1988 franchise agreement identifies and is signed by **Paul** Casarotto. The lower opinions frequently call both spouses parties; the petition asserts that only Paul asserts claims against DAI/Lombardi, but the complete amended complaint is not reproduced. Do not turn that litigant characterization into a finding about every plaintiff. Lombardi is an **alleged** agent for purposes of the district order; respondents contest his ability as a nonsignatory to invoke arbitration. Claims against the Hudson defendants and D&D Subway are outside the stay and this appeal.

Paragraph 10(c), on page nine, provides:

> Any controversy or claim arising out of or relating to this contract or the breach thereof shall be settled by Arbitration in accordance with the Commercial Arbitration Rules of the American Arbitration Association at a hearing to be held in Bridgeport, Connecticut and judgment upon an award rendered by the Arbitrator(s) may be entered in any court having jurisdiction thereof. The commencement of arbitration proceedings by an aggrieved party to settle disputes arising out of or relating to this contract is a condition precedent to the commencement of legal action by either party. The cost of such a proceeding will be borne equally by the parties.

Paragraph 12 selects Connecticut law and contains an integration provision with the stated exceptions for representations in the agreement, offering circular and franchisor advertising. Paragraph 13 acknowledges reading, understanding and consent; paragraph 10(f) provides severability. There is no first-page arbitration notice. The selected offering circular discloses binding AAA arbitration in Bridgeport and shared costs; the agreement recites timely receipt of the prospectus. Paul swears that he read the agreement, that it was nonnegotiable, that arbitration/Connecticut law was not explained, and that he did not understand the loss of a Montana judicial forum until 1992. Those sworn statements and the signed recitals are distinct evidence, not findings of either informed assent or ignorance.

The lower appellate court accepted the misconduct allegations as true for review of the stay: the promised preferred location was allegedly awarded to another franchisee, causing business loss. It made no merits finding that the alleged misconduct occurred. On June 2, 1993, the district court found actual interstate commerce, that the claims against DAI and its alleged agent Lombardi arose out of/related to the agreement and fell within the clause, and that DAI had demanded arbitration. It stayed those claims, invoking FAA §3. Its July 8 Rule 54(b) certification did not adjudicate liability or damages. No arbitration award is established.

The December 1994 Montana majority held that Montana law applied under Restatement §§187–188 despite the Connecticut clause: Montana had a materially greater interest, and the notice statute supplied fundamental public policy. It then held notice noncompliance not preempted under its reading of *Volt*, because informed consent furthers arbitration's purpose. The August 1995 judgment reaffirmed that disposition and distinguished *Allied-Bruce* as concerning categorical prohibition and commerce, rather than notice. Neither majority independently found fraud, unconscionability, adhesion-based invalidity or lack of actual awareness. Its adverse general observations about costs and safeguards are stated public-policy reasons, not findings of these parties' actual arbitration costs.

The lower dissents separately disputed the statute's application to an agreement choosing Connecticut law because subsection (4) says “pursuant to this chapter.” They also found preemption, distinguishing *Volt*'s chosen state procedure preserving arbitration from unchosen law invalidating the arbitration agreement. The special concurrence supported the notice rule as an aid to assent in a nonnegotiated form contract. Preserve these as separate lower-court rationales, not additional majority findings.

## Complete operative statutory terms

The [official 1994 FAA §2](../sources/chunk7_a/faa_1994_sec2.html) provides:

> A written provision in any maritime transaction or a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce to settle by arbitration a controversy thereafter arising out of such contract or transaction, or the refusal to perform the whole or any part thereof, or an agreement in writing to submit to arbitration an existing controversy arising out of such a contract, transaction, or refusal, shall be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract.

The [official 1994 FAA §3](../sources/chunk7_a/faa_1994_sec3.html) provides:

> If any suit or proceeding be brought in any of the courts of the United States upon any issue referable to arbitration under an agreement in writing for such arbitration, the court in which such suit is pending, upon being satisfied that the issue involved in such suit or proceeding is referable to arbitration under such an agreement, shall on application of one of the parties stay the trial of the action until such arbitration has been had in accordance with the terms of the agreement, providing the applicant for the stay is not in default in proceeding with such arbitration.

The district's invocation of §3 establishes its chosen ground, not a Supreme Court holding importing that section wholesale into state courts. Actual *Allied-Bruce* reserves wholesale state-court incorporation of §§3–4. Referability, a party's application, agreement-conforming arbitration and the applicant-default condition must remain distinct if §3 is used; no default finding is supplied here.

The entire contemporaneous Montana §27-5-114 is reproduced in the [sanitized record facts](../sources/chunk7_a/casarotto_SANITIZED_RECORD_FACTS.md#montana-statute-including-all-exceptions), visually checked against the filed statutory appendix. It is a contemporaneous party-filed reproduction, not independent official-code authentication:

> **27-5-114. Validity of arbitration agreement—exceptions.** (1) A written agreement to submit an existing controversy to arbitration is valid and enforceable except upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract.
>
> (2) A written agreement to submit to arbitration any controversy arising between the parties after the agreement is made is valid and enforceable except upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract. Except as permitted under subsection (3), this subsection does not apply to:
>
> (a) claims arising out of personal injury, whether based on contract or tort;
>
> (b) any contract by an individual for the acquisition of real or personal property, services, or money or credit where the total consideration to be paid or furnished by the individual is $5,000 or less;
>
> (c) any agreement concerning or relating to insurance policies or annuity contracts except for those contracts between insurance companies; or
>
> (d) claims for workers' compensation.
>
> (3) A written agreement between members of a trade or professional organization to submit to arbitration any controversies arising between members of the trade or professional organization after the agreement is made is valid and enforceable except upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract.
>
> (4) Notice that a contract is subject to arbitration pursuant to this chapter shall be typed in underlined capital letters on the first page of the contract; and unless such notice is displayed thereon, the contract may not be subject to arbitration.

The subsection (2) exclusions, expressly qualified by (3), are not exceptions written into (4). The lower judgment rests on (4), not application of one of the subject-matter exclusions. The required typing, underlining, capital letters, first-page placement and “pursuant to this chapter” qualification cannot be reduced to an ordinary conspicuousness standard. No transition dispute over the 1988 agreement is established.

## Entering law and independent alternatives

The operative Standard **Actual commerce and state-court enforcement of arbitration agreements**, present operation effective January 18, 1995, derives from actual *Allied-Bruce*: §2 governs covered state-court as well as federal-court agreements; the saving clause preserves grounds for revocation of **any contract**, while a State may not impose a categorical disability on predispute arbitration. Coverage follows actual commerce within Congress's power, with the outer constitutional ceiling and remote-history sufficiency reserved. The district commerce finding and appellate assumption support coverage here; this is not an invented unresolved-commerce-record case. Ordinary consent, formation, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability, party assent and scope retain their own requirements and preservation limits.

*Allied-Bruce* retains *Southland* and applies *Perry*, while *Volt* continues to protect an actual contractual selection of state arbitration rules. *Mastrobuono* construes the actual integrated agreement and preserves genuine restrictions without automatically reading a general governing-law clause as a withdrawal of arbitral power. It does not make every state choice-of-law determination invalid. The operative Standard **Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability**, present operation effective May 22, 1995, derives from *First Options*: ordinary contract law must establish clear and unmistakable delegation; company assent supplies no owner's personal assent; legal questions receive independent appellate review and findings clear-error review. Its unaddressed nonsignatory theories remain unaddressed. A broad clause or incorporated AAA rules are not here treated as an adjudicated delegation of this dispute.

The pre-May20 projection supplies actual current-term law through May 13. *Barnett Bank* preserves statute-specific conflict analysis and compatible state regulation; its national-bank significant-interference standard is not an FAA test. *Noland* preserves distinct statutory functions and authorized discretion rather than judicial replacement of enacted conditions. *Brown Group* separates enforcement authorization from independent constitutional and remedy requirements. *Armstrong* and *44 Liquormart* add no arbitration-notice holding. Neither these decisions nor the same-day Henderson event answers the present question by analogy alone.

Supported paths, with the strongest opposing point and necessary limit, are:

1. **Preemption of this arbitration-specific condition.** Petitioners rely on §2's “any contract” language, *Perry*'s prohibition on principles whose meaning depends on arbitration, and actual *Allied-Bruce*. The statute conditions this enforcement on a formality not demanded of contracts generally. Respondents' strongest answer is that notice implements the ordinary consent/adhesion principle rather than hostility. The legal inquiry must address that characterization, not infer consent from federal coverage or bad faith from the rule's purpose.
2. **A permissible notice/consent rule.** Respondents' recovered merits brief pp.15–24 invokes adhesion, reasonable expectations, Restatement §211, *Passage* and *Chor*; pp.25–33 separately invokes ordinary conflict preemption, voluntary assent, modest compliance costs and state contract authority. They argue that informed agreement advances the FAA rather than frustrates it. Petitioners answer that the facial condition operates regardless of bargaining, surprise, sophistication or explanation and singles out arbitration. Neither “consumer protection” nor “arbitration-specific” is a substitute for construing the saving clause and the actual operation of this law.
3. **Generally applicable defense, if properly before the courts.** Ordinary contract defenses survive either preemption answer. But the source record establishes no independently sufficient defense finding here. The reply asserts that common-law adhesion was raised in the February 1993 trial memorandum, implicitly rejected and abandoned in state appellate briefing; the full appellate briefs are not reproduced. Treat that as a preservation dispute, not established waiver. The Court may preserve genuinely available defenses without ordering a new hearing on an unpreserved defense or deciding their facts itself.
4. **State-court FAA application reconsidered.** The actual *Allied-Bruce* Standards entry identifies Scalia and Thomas's distinct state-court ground. A Justice may reconsider that question on his own recorded premises, but the current seven-Justice state-court holding remains governing law unless expressly changed; the historical result of this matter supplies no support. A present position requires attention to stare decisis and the actual later joins, not an assumed categorical disposition.
5. **Narrower statutory/contract scope or remedy.** The lower dissent's “pursuant to this chapter” and Connecticut-choice analysis is supported as an alternative argument, but an independent challenge to Montana's conflict-of-laws decision is not within the presented question as framed by both sides. A preemption disposition need not decide that separate state-law issue. If the notice bar is removed, the choice between directing reinstatement of the existing stay and a bounded remand must account for the district's actual coverage/scope rulings, any properly preserved general defense and the specific parties; it must not command arbitration of all plaintiffs, all defendants and every claim by inference.

**Actual-knowledge qualification is not established law.** Respondents cite *Chor* for an asserted exception when a signer knows and understands the clause. The source team fully read *Chor*: the majority rejected an adhesion/expectations challenge on its testimony, but did not expressly adjudicate an exception to §27-5-114(4); lack of statutory notice appeared in dissent. Preserve respondents' construction as advocacy. Neither the affidavit nor the signed acknowledgment resolves actual understanding, and neither creates a settled statutory safe harbor.

## Reviewability, scope, remedy and participation

The notice-preemption issue was raised and decided. Montana's choice-of-law ruling selects the notice rule whose federal validity is contested; the recovered majority supplies no independently sufficient general-contract ground sustaining the denial of arbitration. A state-law label alone therefore does not eliminate the federal question. No wider review of Montana conflicts doctrine is necessary merely because petitioners prevail on preemption.

State Rule 54(b) certification does not itself satisfy federal finality. The source team rechecked the petition and all three merits submissions: they invoke §1257(a), but no separate Cox/finality analysis was recovered. This is not litigant default. For the neutral threshold inquiry, [*Southland*, 465 U.S. 1, 6–8, Part II-A](../sources/chunk7_a/casarotto_Southland_finality_extract.txt), verified from the [official bounded PDF](../sources/chunk7_a/casarotto_Southland_finality_extract.pdf), applies *Cox Broadcasting*, 420 U.S. 469, 482–483, to a state judgment finally denying arbitration despite further merits proceedings. It explains the risk that the arbitration proponent could prevail later on nonfederal grounds, that federal reversal would remove the relevant merits dispute from court, and that waiting through litigation would seriously erode the federal arbitration policy. Its then-§1257(2) appeal route is not the present certiorari route; the finality reasoning, not the old subsection, supplies the relevant authority.

That doctrine supports present review of the finally resolved notice bar and requested restoration of the arbitration stay. It does not deem the underlying damages claims adjudicated, eliminate actual party/scope objections, or create universal review of interlocutory state rulings. If a proposed disposition depends on an independent unresolved defense that would defeat the arbitration route regardless of preemption, its effect on scope and finality must be addressed rather than presumed. The established record presently supplies no such independently sufficient lower ground.

On a preemption reversal, no liability judgment, damages award, completed arbitration, universal state-court application of §§3–4, or binding of nonsignatories follows automatically. On affirmance, the grounds must identify whether statutory notice, a properly established general defense, or a separately reconsidered federal-state rule controls; an unproved knowledge or unconscionability finding cannot support it. Pamela's status, Lombardi's claimed enforcement rights and nonparty claims retain their actual bounded posture.
