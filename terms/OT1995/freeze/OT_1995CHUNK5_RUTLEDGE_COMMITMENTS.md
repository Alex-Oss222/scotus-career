## Participation, reach and record limits

All nine current Justices were seated at argument and decision; no Rutledge-specific nonparticipation is established. The eight Associates modeled here are Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The plenary appellate channel under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) is available on the submitted posture. No threshold disposition is warranted on the supplied record. This statement does not reconstruct historical petition treatment.

The actual petition challenges entry of both judgments and concurrent sentences when the same conduct proves the conspiracy and a necessary CCE component. Both parties agree that the §846 conspiracy and the §848 concert element concern the same criminal agreement here. The Government's reservation about a hypothetical unwitting participant is not a concession of the general elements question, but neither does it require adjudicating every possible enterprise to decide this pair. The lower court already treated this conspiracy as included and sustained both judgments because the prison terms were concurrent and did not exceed the CCE maximum.

The December 29, 1992 judgment contains six convictions, with life on Count 1, concurrent life without possible release on Count 2, and an independent concurrent life term on Count 3. It also contains Count 4's ten-year term and the consecutive firearm terms on Counts 5–6. The $300 assessment covers all six counts at $50 each. No fine, incarceration costs or restitution was ordered. These are existing verdicts and judgments, not new findings of guilt by this Court. The separate trial and sentencing objections rejected below are unpresented.

## Common commitments and precise scope

The following positions have the express support of each of the eight Associates, subject to the separately identified constitutional-rationale distinction.

**A. Included agreement in this prosecution.** Accept and apply the lesser-included relationship for the reviewed pair: the same knowing unlawful narcotics agreement that establishes Count 2 supplies the concerted-activity component of Count 1, while CCE requires the additional continuing-series, five-person, management and substantial-income/resource features. Section 846 supplies no additional overt-act element under actual Shabani. The conclusion concerns the agreement legally included in this charged CCE, not mere reuse of evidence or a common episode. It does not hold that every conceivable §848 prosecution necessarily includes this §846 offense, decide a different agreement or subchapter-II offense, or conclusively resolve the Government's hypothetical involving unwitting participants. Jeffers assumed the included-offense premise; it is not falsely cited as having conclusively decided that categorical question.

**B. Statutory authorization and dual convictions.** Congress did not authorize cumulative judgments and punishments under §§846 and 848 for this same agreement. Jeffers's treatment of this pairing and Garrett's express distinction between conspiracy and substantive predicates establish the relevant legislative-intent boundary. CCE's additional elements, separate statutory location and more severe penalties do not establish an intention to preserve a second judgment for the already included agreement. Ball's conviction-versus-sentence distinction then applies: concurrency does not remove the second judgment or its legally material consequences. The existence of two separately defined statutes does not itself supply authorization to enter both convictions.

This is consistent with actual Harris and the February 5 Bishop/Stokes decision. Their express §924(c) additional-and-consecutive authorization governs that statutory pairing even assuming Blockburger overlap. It is absent here. No blanket rule forbids every cumulative conviction for overlapping offenses, and no general same-conduct or same-transaction rule replaces legislative-intent analysis. Garrett continues to permit its substantive-predicate/CCE cumulation; Felix's agreement/substantive-object distinction remains intact.

**C. Constitutional level of agreement.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally adopt the bounded Double Jeopardy ground: entering the unauthorized additional conviction and sentence for this included agreement imposes cumulative punishment that the Clause forbids. Scalia and Thomas agree with the statutory construction, illegality of the additional judgment and complete remedy but withhold assent from an independently sufficient constitutional multiple-punishments theory. They do not vote to preserve either unauthorized judgment, overrule current law, or disturb their prior joins in Harris/Bishop–Stokes. Their agreement is not counted as a join in the six-Associate constitutional proposition.

**D. Assessment and presentation.** The existing $50 assessment attributable to the conviction selected for vacatur must be removed with that conviction. This is a subsidiary remedial consequence of the expressly challenged dual judgments; it is not a new demand concerning an unrelated count. The aggregate assessment becomes $250 for the five remaining convictions. No payment or collection is found, and no separate refund amount is ordered here.

The disposition does not depend on treating the assessment as the independently sufficient reason to reverse. Ball's unauthorized-conviction ground resolves the issue even apart from an additional monetary charge. Accordingly, the Court need not decide whether a freestanding assessment challenge was separately preserved below, or whether the Government's broad account of forfeiture would defeat such an independent claim. The entered amount and §3013's operation support count-specific correction; they do not silently establish waiver. The received individual-felony amount is $50, not a later $100.

**E. Formal judgment and count choice.** Reverse the Seventh Circuit insofar as it affirms entry of both Count 1 and Count 2 judgments, and remand with instructions to have the District Court vacate one of those convictions, its entire corresponding life sentence, and its $50 assessment. Following Ball, leave the selection between Count 1 and Count 2 in the first instance to the sentencing court's lawful discretion. Rutledge expressly requests either-count vacatur; the Government's narrower question formulation does not convert that request into a concession that only Count 2 may be removed.

Vacating only a prison sentence, describing both convictions as one combined judgment, or leaving the existing second conviction in place with its execution stayed would not implement this holding. No new composite offense is created. The surviving challenged conviction and the four unpresented convictions remain undisturbed, together with their existing sentences and assessments. This limited remand does not authorize a new sentencing calculation, general resentencing, reopening of unrelated errors, acquittal, new trial or release.

**F. Later CCE reversal and retained-verdict limits.** The present decision directs actual vacatur, not a presently effective conditional second conviction. It does not categorically bar the Government from later seeking entry or reinstatement of judgment on an otherwise valid original conspiracy verdict if Count 2 is vacated and Count 1 later fails. An asserted failure confined to a CCE-specific requirement—such as the requisite number of persons, management, continuing series or income—presents a different question from a ground also invalidating the conspiracy verdict. The latter ground cannot be ignored merely because the conspiracy once received a guilty verdict.

All eight reserve the legal availability and form of relief for a concrete later application under otherwise applicable law. No automatic reinstatement, blanket retrial permission, finality exception or unconditional immunity from further proceedings is adopted. Vacatur here is for unauthorized duplication, not an acquittal for evidentiary insufficiency. That distinction preserves the issue; it does not decide the consequences of a future insufficiency ruling, a common trial error, or any independent defect in the conspiracy verdict. The Government's interest in preserving lawful punishment thus does not justify retaining two present convictions. Nor does hypothetical failure of CCE imply release: the other four counts, including Count 3's independent life term, are outside this decision.
