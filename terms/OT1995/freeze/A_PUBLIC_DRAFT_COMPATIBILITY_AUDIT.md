## Corrections requested and verified

1. **Doe's operative culpability rule.** The first draft said the supervisor deliberately failed to take available protective action. That could encompass an official who reasonably responded without using every possible measure. The revised rule expressly requires deliberate unreasonable disregard and protects reasonable responses even if injury occurs. It also states authority, actual awareness, noncompelled circumstantial inference and injury-or-continuation. These are the frozen D1 requirements, not a new protection invented during circulation.

2. **Doe's strongest contrary authority and date distinction.** The revised explanation identifies Ford's participation/approval formulation, Reimer/Vela's preventive-policy versus affirmative-unlawful-policy distinction, and Chinchello's narrower affirmative-role/communicated-approval reading. It does not falsely describe them as identical holdings. Wanger/Bowen supply the competing own-causal-inaction principle at the restricted level of the assumed conduct. No finding that Lankford communicated approval to the teacher is made. The explanation also separately treats pre-May omissions and later 1987 continuation: Jefferson's direct-restraint setting and Lopez's school-supervision analysis and actual rejection of liability remain material; neither supplies retroactive notice or a general monitoring duty. Source check: Doe lower opinion pp.454–459 and Garwood pp.466–473, directly reviewed in the clean reconciliation; the neutral packet and final A freeze preserve the same competing paths.

3. **Wood lower posture and existing evidence.** The final Event supplies the material district-denial/appellate-reversal correction and exact conditional form of the lower relief. The explanation considers the existing record supporting the State without inventing a gun-testing resolution, increasing the burden to proof of innocence, or treating witness importance alone as materiality. The strongest counterargument remains lawful collective preparation use; the draft answers it by the unsupported consequential change in this presented claim, not by an automatic inadmissibility rule.

4. **Hodge claim identification.** “Expunge or otherwise handle” was replaced with the actual asserted disclosure, expunction and procedural-treatment duties. The preserved claim and conduct dates still confine the immunity holding. The strongest opposing theory—an informational injury or substantive state-created entitlement without public disclosure or monetary loss—is expressly reserved, not declared impossible. The full Maryland historical text remains unnecessary to that selected immunity ground and necessary to any dispositive new entitlement construction.

5. **Clerical substitutions.** Literal question marks introduced in the new Wood gun possessive and Doe linking punctuation were reported and removed. The final directly checked copies contain none of those substitutions. These repairs changed no legal commitment.
