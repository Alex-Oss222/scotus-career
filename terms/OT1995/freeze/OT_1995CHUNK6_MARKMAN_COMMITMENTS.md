The opinion distinguishes that application from construction strictly understood.
- [Bischoff v. Wethered, 76 U.S. 812, 814–16](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep076/usrep076812/usrep076812.pdf): identity or diversity of inventions in the anticipation setting can require jury evidence about the mechanisms and technical descriptions. Its allocation does not make every technically informed question construction or assign all subsidiary technical facts to the judge.
- [Heald v. Rice, 104 U.S. 737, 749](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep104/usrep104737/usrep104737.pdf): facial comparison of original and reissued patents is pure construction if no extrinsic explanation of terms or application of descriptions is needed. Its qualification preserves auxiliary matters of fact for a jury in an action at law.
- [Singer Manufacturing Co. v. Cramer, 192 U.S. 265, 275](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep192/usrep192265/usrep192265.pdf): judicial resolution of infringement on comparison of the patents depended on the absence of necessary extrinsic technical or descriptive evidence. Its treatment of excluded evidence depended on that evidence's irrelevance or lack of necessity where the specification admitted one construction.

## Proposed propositions and judgment

All eight Associates support **affirmance of the judgment of noninfringement of claims 1 and 10** on the following compatible grounds. Agreement does not adopt every sentence of the Federal Circuit's categorical explanation.

**P1 — Legal action and preserved jury function.** A patent infringement action seeking damages is a legal action carrying Seventh Amendment protection. The jury ordinarily resolves disputed infringement facts under the court's construction. A jury right in the action does not make the jury the final expositor of the legally operative scope of the patent instrument; neither does calling an issue construction eliminate an otherwise protected factual dispute.

**P2 — Judicial responsibility for the patent's operative meaning.** The court construes the patent's legally operative claim boundary by reading the claim with the specification and prosecution history. Technical explanation may inform that task. On this record the court may determine that inventory includes clothing and cannot be satisfied solely by the retained cash/invoice totals. This is interpretation of the scope of the public grant, not a judicial decision that disputed events occurred or that an accused device possessed disputed features. Historical instrument-construction principles support the allocation; consistent public scope corroborates its function but cannot override contrary constitutional history. This commitment does not establish that every subsidiary dispute about historical technical usage or credibility is itself legal, or that every underlying factual finding receives de novo appellate review.

**P3 — Independently sufficient record-specific ground.** Even if a different patent dispute can require jury resolution of genuinely disputed technical facts, petitioners' proffer here does not support the cash-only reading in the context of this instrument. The judgment is sustainable under the ordinary reasonable-jury/JMOL inquiry without rejecting a witness's credibility. No reasonable interpretation of the admitted public claim materials, accepting the material technical proffer in petitioners' favor, permits the asserted cash-only inventory to discharge the article-related detection/localization function. Consequently the interpretation proposed to support the verdict supplies no substantial evidentiary basis for infringement, given the parties' stated concession. The judgment stands under either a wholly judicial ultimate-construction allocation or a legal-construction approach preserving material subsidiary factfinding.

The propositions answer the presented question to the extent necessary to decide this case. They leave unresolved the trial allocation of a genuinely material historical technical-usage dispute in another record, the appellate standard for subsidiary findings, and any procedural format or timing for obtaining a construction. They do not mandate a separate pretrial hearing, prohibit all extrinsic evidence, alter §112 paragraph 6 generally, decide validity or damages, or create an infringement presumption.
