## Supported propositions

1. **Trial motion and notice dispute, JA 24–28.** Defense counsel renewed a motion to exclude uncharged-crime evidence, explaining that the last day's disclosure afforded inadequate preparation. Counsel distinguished the anticipated inmate/confession statements from photographs and other evidence about the Sorrell murders, disputed the prosecutor's characterization of the earlier notice, and asked that the evidence be excluded. The court relied on disclosure at the end of the guilt phase the preceding day and overruled the motion. This exchange supports the precise exclusion request and the preparation complaint. It does not turn that complaint into a formally requested continuance or prove what an unrequested continuance would have achieved.

2. **State habeas hearing, JA 110–116.** Counsel emphasized prejudicial and unreliable uncharged-crime evidence and Tucker's incentives. These are advocacy assertions; they are not new findings that Gray did not commit the other crimes.

3. **State final order, JA 117–119.** The order identifies separate numbered claims disposed of under Brooks/Hawks, Slayton, noncognizability, insufficient ineffective-assistance allegations, and merits grounds. Claim IX appears in the previously adjudicated group. The order is signed May 14, 1990; the table of contents describes a May 23 order. Do not silently resolve that document-date difference or make it material where no result depends on it.

4. **State petition-for-appeal excerpt, JA 120–125.** JA 124 expressly asserts that late disclosure of the intention to use Sorrell evidence prevented reasonable preparation and invokes fundamental unfairness and due process. That presentation also intertwines uncharged crimes, reliability, the asserted standard of proof, and preparation. The legal question whether the presentation fairly encompasses each present federal claim remains for analysis; neither the presence of the words due process nor a label such as notice mechanically decides it.

5. **Virginia Supreme Court order, JA 126–127.** The January 11, 1991 order applies Hawks to the listed claims including IX, Slayton to a different listed group, and Penn to specified ineffective-assistance allegations, describing these refusals as procedural default and citing Harris v. Reed. It refuses remaining questions on the merits. The reach and adequacy of a particular ground must be attached to the actual claim; this document does not merge all the claims into one default.

6. **Federal opinion, JA 335–353.** The District Court states that its evidentiary hearing concerned whether introducing Sorrell evidence in the penalty phase violated due process (335–336). It reproduces the prosecutor's statements-only assurances (340–341) and characterizes them as unequivocal (342). It describes counsel's request as a plea for additional preparation time (343); that characterization must be distinguished from the actual exclusion motion at JA 24–28. It finds that surprise impaired preparation, investigation, impeachment, evidentiary objections and legal objections (346, 350–351), and that absent the surprise introduction there was a substantial probability of a different sentencing result (353). Those are identifiable lower-court findings and reasoning, not independent Supreme Court findings or an automatic answer to Teague, exhaustion, default, review standard or relief. The opinion vacated the death sentence and directed resentencing; it expressly separated an unaffected firearm conviction (335 note 1).
