## Proposal and determination

The proposed formulation is to **reverse the Ninth Circuit's categorical one-appeal jurisdictional dismissal and remand** to separate any reviewable legal qualified-immunity issue from barred evidentiary-sufficiency disputes, accepting the district court's properly assumed facts favorable to the nonmovant. No immunity is awarded, no whole appeal is automatically heard, and no repeated appeal of an unchanged ruling is authorized.

**All six existing remand supporters may join that formal reversal:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Their adopted legal ground already rejects the categorical jurisdictional rule on which the Ninth Circuit's November 17, 1994 dismissal rests. Reversal of that erroneous dismissal, followed by the same bounded remand, supplies the relief their own grounds support. It neither requires finding an actual merits issue reviewable now nor changes the factual assumptions or immunity merits. Stevens and Breyer retain affirmance; the change in decree does not answer their objection to a second appeal as of right.

The original commitment expressly excluded **reversal with a command to decide the whole appeal's merits**. It did not identify the word reversal itself as the barrier. The sanitized decree removes that command and retains the legal/factual sorting, possible dismissal of unavailable issues and no-immunity limits. The prior reconciliation's preserved source limit concerned a definitive affirmative classification of Behrens's actual legal argument, not authority to reject the established categorical dismissal. That source limit survives. Vacatur and reversal are not declared interchangeable in all cases; this specified reversal rests on an already adopted legal error and changes no other operative consequence.

## Exact mandate and opinion boundaries

The target of reversal is the **Ninth Circuit's categorical jurisdictional dismissal**, not the District Court's denial of summary judgment and not any judgment awarding or denying immunity on its merits. On remand, the Ninth Circuit determines whether a separable legal immunity issue is actually presented under the otherwise applicable collateral-order requirements. It accepts the district court's relevant, sufficiently supported assumptions favorable to the nonmovant. Where those assumptions are unstated, it may identify the likely assumptions in the manner permitted by Johnson; it may not decide what actually happened, reweigh evidence or replace them with Behrens's preferred version.

Ordinary evidentiary-sufficiency disputes and otherwise unavailable issues remain subject to dismissal. A legal label does not establish jurisdiction, while the mere presence of factual disputes does not negate every separable legal question. The remand does not require the circuit to hear the entire appeal or to find any particular issue reviewable. It does not authorize relitigation of an unchanged ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate. This limit is a reservation of existing restrictions, not a newly announced absolute rule deciding every possible repeated-appeal scenario.

No immunity, constitutional violation, property interest, factual regulatory condition, damages, trial outcome, stay, certification or appellate permission is adjudicated. The distinct United States negligence claim and earlier substitution ruling remain outside the decree. Broader factual-materiality, actual-appeal-classification or immunity determinations still require the clean record work and renewed handoffs identified in the neutral addendum and prior reconciliation.

Stevens and Breyer continue to support **affirmance** on their one-ordinary-pretrial-appeal/importance and finality grounds, with permission-based review available only on its actual statutory conditions. Formal reversal instead of vacatur still permits the second appeal they oppose; no remedial accommodation cures that objection. Their agreement with Johnson's legal/factual distinction and conditional claim-specific immunity does not become a join in successive-appeal authorization. Their potential separate writing retains the substantive function recorded in the prior reconciliation.
