# O'Hare Truck Service, Inc. v. City of Northlake — frozen neutral packet

**Stage:** Fresh neutral validation, commitment-free.

## Corrected record and procedural frame

This is a Rule12(b)(6) dismissal. The described retaliation facts are **well-pleaded allegations assumed for that motion**, not findings after trial. The police had a towing rotation list dating at least to1965, calling companies in turn and moving to the next if one could not respond. O'Hare alleges long participation and assurances that satisfactory service would preserve its place. Northlake neither paid O'Hare nor entered a direct municipal towing-services contract; vehicle owners paid. The relevant governmental benefit is recurring access to controlled referrals, not necessarily a municipal purchase.

Before the1993 election the mayor's committee requested a contribution. Gratzianna allegedly declined, supported an opponent and displayed opponent posters. After the incumbent won, the police chief allegedly removed O'Hare at the mayor's direction. These allegations support the submitted causal theory at the pleading stage. A government denial of motive or generalized claim of efficiency cannot be treated as contrary findings defeating that pleading.

The complaint asserted no statute, ordinance or written list rule creating an entitlement; the lower court rejected the implied-property claim under Illinois law. That procedural-due-process ruling is separate and is not reopened merely by review of the speech/association claim. A lack of property entitlement does not by itself resolve unconstitutional retaliation.

The appellate court followed its contractor cases, declined to extend Elrod/Branti/Rutan and affirmed. It cited lesser dependence, procurement litigation and institutional concerns, and acknowledged contrary Fifth/Tenth Circuit authority. This specific conflict corrects the original section-I generic conflict statement. It is not an instruction to follow either side.

## Strong competing positions and independent paths

**Contractor's position:** Loss of government-controlled business because one refuses a contribution and supports an opponent burdens speech and association irrespective of formal employment or property status. Existing regular referrals are materially different from an unsuccessful first bid. Political affiliation is not shown here to be an appropriate towing qualification. A government-benefit condition can coerce expression even where other customers exist.

**City's position:** Traditional patronage, contractor independence, other sources of business, efficient service organization and litigation burdens counsel against extending employee patronage doctrine. The City may argue that nonemployment referrals occupy a different category or that narrower protections leave substantial governmental discretion. Those policy arguments cannot supply service inadequacy or a neutral actual reason absent from accepted allegations.

Supported alternatives include:
1. Extend Elrod/Branti's prohibition to this existing referral relationship, preserving Branti's functional affiliation exception and distinguishing speech-performance balancing. The Court must explain what counts as an appropriate political requirement and why the pleadings do or do not invoke it; no source establishes such a requirement for towing here.
2. Use a narrower unconstitutional-conditions rule against pleaded coercion of contribution/support without adopting every employment rule for every contractor. State a usable scope and preserve genuine neutral qualification/performance decisions.
3. Use an employee-derived balance for materially mixed speech/association disputes, if supported, while explaining its relationship to affiliation coercion. An automatic broad managerial-interest balance cannot silently replace Branti's particular inquiry; conversely a pure-affiliation rule cannot decide every later contractor criticism dispute.
4. Retain the categorical contractor/referral exclusion after addressing Perry, the accepted adverse support-based reason, and the scope of Rutan. Distinguish refusal to expand a precedent from overturning it, and identify the relevant holding if seeking that broader change.
5. If a real threshold obstacle arises, resolve it within the actual pleadings and review scope. No absence-of-contract/property shortcut, unsupported immunity judgment or assumed bidding dispute supplies such an obstacle.

Reversal of the First Amendment dismissal would permit proceedings under the stated rule, not establish the truth of retaliation, personal liability, municipal policy attribution, a property right, damages or entitlement to permanent referrals. Any lawful actual same-decision or neutral service defense remains distinct from a categorical right to demand political support. The separate due-process disposition stays outside the submitted issue unless a supported independent scope ground is established.

## O'Hare Truck Service, No.95-191

### Jurisdiction, pleadings and separate claims

Review lies under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Seventh Circuit's February 10, 1995 judgment, 47 F.3d 883, rehearing denied March 3. The petition's question is the First Amendment consequence of terminating business because the contractor supported the incumbent's opponent. The lower court also rejected procedural due process; that separate holding is not enlarged into the submitted First Amendment question.

The case was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6). The following facts are **well-pleaded allegations accepted for that motion**, not established findings:

- The police maintained a rotation list of towing businesses, calling each in turn and advancing to the next if one could not respond. The system existed since at least 1965; the petition alleges O'Hare's participation over that period.
- Northlake did not pay O'Hare or make a direct towing-services contract; vehicle owners paid the charges. This is recurring access to government-controlled referrals, not an assumed procurement contract paid from municipal funds.
- Successive mayors allegedly assured the business that satisfactory service would preserve its place. Before the 1993 election, the mayor's committee requested a contribution. Gratzianna refused and supported the opposing candidate; the petition also identifies opponent posters in O'Hare's business. After the incumbent won, the police chief allegedly removed O'Hare at the mayor's direction.
- The complaint alleged no statute, ordinance or written list rule creating entitlement. The lower court rejected implied-contract/informal-rule entitlement under Illinois law. That property question is distinct from the First Amendment theory; lack of a property right alone is not the same proposition as a lawful retaliatory reason.

The full appellate opinion, including notes, is 47 F.3d 883–886. The district court dismissal was 843 F. Supp. 1231. The appellate court followed its own contractor precedents, expressly declined to extend Elrod/Branti/Rutan, and affirmed both claims. Its First Amendment rationale rested on perceived lower dependence, procurement litigation burdens and the view that any extension should come from the Supreme Court. It acknowledged contrary Fifth and Tenth Circuit approaches. There is therefore a concrete conflict, contrary to the neutral brief's generic “no material conflict” line.

### Strongest recorded positions and possible scope

The petition pp.3–6 and 12–18 frames the injury as economic retaliation for refusing support and choosing an opponent. It argues that the ordinary unconstitutional-conditions and patronage cases cannot turn on formal employment status and distinguishes Umbehr's public-criticism theory from political association. The City merits brief pp.5–11 invokes the traditional role of patronage, contractor independence and other clients, the risk of turning every unsuccessful public bidder into a constitutional plaintiff, and the government's ability to organize services efficiently. It cites Scalia's Rutan dissent and LaFalce, Sweeney, Horn, Triad and Downtown Auto Parks. Its claims of lesser economic burden or neutral service authority are arguments; the Rule 12(b)(6) posture cannot convert them into contrary facts defeating the accepted retaliation allegation.

The existing referral relationship, refusal to contribute, affirmative opposing support, and possible distinction between affiliation demands and speech-performance disputes must remain separate. Elrod and Branti contain a functional exception where political affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance of the position; no source here establishes that partisan affiliation is an appropriate qualification for this towing referral. A remand on the First Amendment claim would not itself establish motive, municipal liability, personal immunity, a damages amount or a property entitlement, and would not automatically reopen the separate due-process judgment. Neutral qualifications, service defects and a properly proved same-decision defense remain different from a categorical right to demand partisan support.

# Exact Waters standard

### Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

**Current rule:** An employer acting on a genuine, reasonable account of speech that would be disciplinable under the governing public-employee-speech rules is protected even if that factual account is mistaken. Conversely, an unreasonable and incorrect account does not defeat a claim when the employee actually spoke protected speech and that speech caused the adverse action. The employee must show protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may establish by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech. These limited propositions do not impose liability for every investigation defect or adopt the plurality’s complete investigative procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- Seven-Justice genuine-and-reasonable-account safe harbor: [Waters v. Churchill — A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md#public-projection), 1994-05-31. O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.
- Six-Justice unreasonable-and-false-account limit: [Waters v. Churchill — An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md#public-projection), 1994-05-31. O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.
- Motive and same-decision proof: [Waters v. Churchill — The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md#public-projection), 1994-05-31. O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Present operation effective:** Waters v. Churchill, 1994-05-31.

**Open question:** Unreasonable belief where the actual speech was unprotected, and a universal investigation checklist, are not resolved. The four-Justice complete reasonableness framework is noncontrolling.

### Prospective restrictions on employees’ citizen expression

**Governing question:** Justification of a broad advance restriction on employees’ compensated speech as citizens.

**Current rule:** Under Pickering, the Government must supply a justification commensurate with the actual reach and burden of a broad prospective restriction on employees’ future compensated citizen expression, including the burden on listeners. A general concern about misuse of office does not by itself justify prohibiting protected expression unrelated to employment; the required connection must be adequately established. Narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Authority by component:**

- A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden: [A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_National_Treasury_Employees_Union_merits_1995-02-22.md#public-projection), 1995-02-22. Justice Stevens's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and explanation. Justice O'Connor agrees with invalidity for unrelated expression on her separate, case-specific Pickering ground; her vote is not counted for this prospective-burden formulation.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-02-22; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This prospective-burden rule does not constitutionalize every payment or decide materially different senior-employee settings. The existing honoraria ban’s classwide remedy has its own five-Justice statutory-remedial ground; it creates no universal injunction for unrepresented employees or judicially rewritten employment-nexus exception.
