## Decisional propositions and exact joins

### C0. The finally resolved federal arbitration bar is reviewable now

Review lies under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) on this record. Montana finally decided the federal notice-preemption question in rejecting the arbitration stay. Under Southland, 465 U.S. 1, 6–8, applying Cox Broadcasting, postponing review through the merits litigation risks both loss of later review if petitioners prevail on nonfederal grounds and erosion of the claimed federal right to an arbitral forum. Federal reversal removes the adjudicated statutory obstacle to the existing arbitration request. The present record identifies no independently sufficient lower-court contract-defense ground that would make that relief futile.

The state Rule 54(b) certification is not itself a federal finality rule. Neither the damages claims nor every party's contractual position has been finally adjudicated. The unresolved possible defenses are not converted into established independent grounds defeating review. Conversely, this holding does not make every interlocutory arbitration dispute immediately reviewable: an actually sufficient independent ground defeating the arbitral route would require its own analysis. Southland's former §1257(2) appeal channel is not imported into present certiorari jurisdiction.

Montana's conflicts ruling supplies the rule whose federal validity is challenged; it does not independently establish enforceability of that rule despite federal law. There is therefore no adequate-independent-state-ground bar to deciding notice preemption. The Court need not review the conflicts ruling on an independent state-law theory.

**Joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas's contrary answer on the FAA's statutory reach does not remove jurisdiction to decide that federal question. No constitutional or contractual merits ruling follows from C0.

### C1. Apply the retained state-court rule to the established covered transaction

FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court. Southland's construction, expressly retained in actual Allied-Bruce on January 18, 1995, remains governing law. The district court found actual interstate commerce; the state appellate court accepted or assumed that premise rather than reversing it. This franchise therefore presents no missing-commerce-record ground for affirmance or mandatory evidentiary remand.

The operative Standard is **Actual commerce and state-court enforcement of arbitration agreements**, present operation effective **Allied-Bruce, January 18, 1995**. It preserves coverage and ordinary consent/contract predicates without importing every direction of FAA §§3–4 into state courts. The present case requires no new definition of Congress's outer commerce power and no conclusion that remote interstate history alone suffices.

The original statutory objection to Southland remains substantial: the FAA's judicial machinery expressly addresses federal courts, and its opponents deny that §2 separately commands state enforcement. Six Associates already joined Allied-Bruce's retention of the rule after considering that objection. Its settled statutory operation, reliance and Congress's ability to revise it support continued adherence. Scalia applies the existing rule in the present notice dispute for the distinct reason stated in his individual commitment. Repeating the statutory objection does not itself convert the dissenting position into current law.

**Joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas refuses C1, would replace its state-court premise, and supplies no alternative commerce holding.

### C2. Section 2 preempts this arbitration-specific first-page condition

For an agreement covered by §2, Montana may not deny arbitration solely because the contract lacks the notice demanded by §27-5-114(4): notice that the contract is subject to arbitration “pursuant to this chapter,” typed in underlined capital letters on its first page. The statute's consequence that the contract “may not be subject to arbitration” without that notice conflicts with §2's command that the covered written agreement be valid, irrevocable and enforceable, subject to grounds at law or equity for revocation of **any contract**.

The defect is the additional condition's operation, not a finding that Montana acted with hostility. This provision makes arbitration, rather than a general defect in making an enforceable contract, the trigger for the prescribed notice and nonenforcement consequence. The statutory bar operates here without an independently adjudicated finding of fraud, duress, unconscionability, lack of agreement or another generally applicable ground. Perry, as applied in actual Allied-Bruce, preserves ordinary contract law while rejecting a state disability whose operative meaning depends on arbitration. The fact that Montana allows arbitration when the special formality is met does not remove the disability when it is not.

Respondents' strongest argument is that the notice requirement implements ordinary consent, adhesion and reasonable expectations, including Restatement §211, rather than frustrating arbitration. Informed agreement is a legitimate concern, and federal coverage supplies no missing assent. But describing a particular arbitration-only condition as an implementation of a general principle does not make its legal trigger and categorical consequence generally applicable. Ordinary formation and revocation doctrines remain available on their own terms, taking the actual contractual circumstances into account; this statute supplies a different rule keyed specifically to arbitration.

Compliance may be inexpensive and possible before contracting. Physical ability to satisfy both state and federal law does not answer whether a State may withhold §2's enforceability for a condition outside the saving clause. Nor is the State's consumer-protection purpose alone decisive. Barnett Bank's statute-specific approach is consistent with that inquiry, but its banking significant-interference standard is not the FAA test. The analysis comes from §2's actual command and saving clause.

No settled actual-knowledge exception to subsection (4) is established. The source team's recovered Chor majority addressed a common-law defense on its evidence, not an express statutory exception; respondents' contrary description remains an argument. Neither Paul's affidavit nor his signed acknowledgment resolves actual understanding in this Court. Preemption of the applied notice bar therefore does not depend on a new factual finding for either side. No broader ruling on a genuinely generally applicable disclosure requirement is necessary.

**Joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each adopts both the rule and its ordinary-contract-defense qualification. Thomas does not reach or join this conditional state-court preemption conclusion; his affirmance is not an adoption of the notice rule as a saved defense under a state-applicable §2.

### C3. Volt does not turn the forum's notice policy into the parties' selected arbitration rule

Volt preserves actual contractual selection of state arbitration rules. It does not authorize a State to invalidate the arbitration provision under an unchosen notice condition merely because that State describes the condition as promoting consent. Here the contract selects Connecticut law and AAA arbitration in Bridgeport; no actual selection of Montana's first-page condition is established. The Montana conflicts ruling selecting local law cannot itself insulate the resulting notice bar from §2.

Mastrobuono requires attention to the integrated contract and distinguishes an actual contractual limit from automatic incorporation of a special state allocation of arbitral power through general governing-law language. It neither establishes that Connecticut law must control every issue in this case nor supplies an independent ground reversing all of Montana's conflicts analysis. The lower dissent's construction of “pursuant to this chapter” presents a possible narrower state-law route, but that independent challenge is outside the presented federal question as framed in the supplied papers. The preemption disposition does not decide it.

**Joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is the necessary answer to Montana's Volt rationale, not a general invalidation of state choice-of-law rules. Thomas supplies no alternative merits join.

### C4. Reverse the notice judgment and remand within the existing party and issue boundaries

Reverse the Montana Supreme Court's judgment sustaining §27-5-114(4) as the ground for denying arbitration, and remand for further proceedings consistent with C1–C3. The state courts must resolve the arbitration-stay request free of that notice bar. The district court's actual commerce, clause-scope and demand determinations are not set aside by this disposition. No new commerce inquiry or general evidentiary hearing is required simply because the case is remanded.

Petitioners requested instructions unconditionally reinstating the June 2, 1993 stay. The narrower mandate is appropriate because the preserved availability of a general contract defense and the particular parties' enforcement rights have not been conclusively resolved in the supplied appellate record. If no genuinely available, properly preserved independent ground defeats the existing stay, the rejected notice objection cannot prevent its restoration. This is not a direction to reopen waived defenses, find that an independent ground exists, or create a new mandatory sequence of hearings.

The prior stay concerns claims against DAI and Lombardi, described in the district order as DAI's **alleged** agent. Paul signed the agreement. The petition's statement that only Paul presses those claims is not a judicial finding resolving Pamela's entire status; the complaint is incomplete in the recovered materials. Respondents contest Lombardi's nonsignatory right and characterize it as unresolved on appeal. Neither actual agency nor his entitlement is determined here. Claims against the Hudson defendants and D&D Subway remain outside this stay and appeal.

The operative Standard **Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability**, present operation effective **First Options, May 22, 1995**, preserves antecedent assent, clear and unmistakable actual delegation, independent review of legal conclusions and clear-error review of findings. The broad clause and AAA reference do not here establish an adjudicated delegation; company or one signer's assent supplies no missing personal assent of another party. That rule does not decide every independent nonsignatory theory, which First Options itself reserved.

The district court's invocation of §3 is not a holding that the entire federal procedural mechanism applies in state court. Section 3's “courts of the United States” wording and its separate predicates—written agreement, referable issue, court satisfaction, a party's application, arbitration in accordance with the agreement, and no applicant default in proceeding—remain intact. No default is found, and no new §3 default inquiry is ordered. The state courts must implement §2's substantive rule through lawful procedure; wholesale state incorporation of §§3–4 remains reserved.

No damages, fraud, other liability, actual arbitral costs, completed arbitration or award is adjudicated. No arbitration is ordered against all plaintiffs, all defendants or all claims by inference. The agreement's actual forum, cost, severability and integration terms retain their proper contractual setting; no term is rewritten. The general-contract-defense reservation does not adjudicate a challenge to any of those terms.

**Joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas opposes reversal and restoration under the FAA, and would leave Montana's denial of the stay and remand for merits proceedings in place. His position does not adjudicate liability or a separate state-law basis for arbitration.
