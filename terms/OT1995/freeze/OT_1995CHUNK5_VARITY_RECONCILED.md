## Verified historical comparator

The complete [official report, 516 U.S. 489–539](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-489.pdf), was read, including the syllabus, Breyer's opinion and Thomas's dissent with all 17 notes. An independent source check read the complete [Cornell dissent](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/94-1471.ZD.html), including its notes. There are two writings and no partial joins: Breyer wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; Thomas dissented with O'Connor and Scalia. The historical judgment affirmed both 36 F.3d 746 and 41 F.3d 1263. The supplied [comparator](../runtime/OT_1995CHUNK5_VARITY_COMPARATOR.md) was accurate but incomplete; its instruction to verify topology is satisfied here. Historical Rehnquist supplies no simulated seat position.

The Court held that the integrated benefit information and assurances were plan administration in the circumstances found below; that knowing, significant deception for the employer's benefit violated loyalty; and that § 502(a)(3) permitted individual equitable redress for fiduciary breach. Its capacity analysis combined the actual plan-related activity, the speakers' authority and the communication's context. It did not make participant perception alone the statutory test. It expressly left broader duties to volunteer truthful information or answer inquiries unresolved. Its remedial explanation treated § 502(a)(3) as a safety net and stated a qualified expectation: where Congress otherwise provides adequate relief, further equitable relief normally would not be appropriate. It did not announce a categorical rule governing every alternative pleading. See 516 U.S. at 498–515.

Three historical limits are material:

* **Monetary classification:** The Court said the monetary relief was not at issue, and recorded petitioner's acceptance of its equitable character while contesting individual authority. Id. at 495, 508. No holding classified replacement-insurance premiums, every accrued-benefit payment or all trust compensation as equitable. Thomas likewise treated respondents as seeking equitable relief and disputed its statutory availability to them. Id. at 518–525.
* **Ten individual retirees:** The Court described their unconsented transfer separately. Id. at 494. At 496, it confined its discussion of the three questions to the deceived workers and explained that the petition did not sufficiently call the retirees' distinct breach determination into question. It did not decide that assignment breach anew. Its statement at 515 that the plaintiffs had left the M-F plan cannot silently become a factual or remedial finding that these already retired, nonconsenting individuals voluntarily surrendered membership.
* **Dissent and remedy:** Thomas advanced independent statutory-action and class-capacity objections. He did not separately resolve the ten retirees' component, direct disposition of § 510 or estoppel, or prescribe a specific remand. Those implementation matters must come from the neutral lower proceedings, not be attributed to his historical writing.

Thomas's historical dissent nevertheless expressly acknowledged Russell's § 502(a)(3) reservation and supplied an extensive, independent construction of §§ 409 and 502(a)(2)–(3). **John Hancock does not supply a vote switch.** Its actual rule separates statutory function, breach and relief, and its agency holding rejects an interpretation outside the enacted exclusion without categorically rejecting litigation positions. **Peacock supplies a predicate requirement, not a general individual-action prohibition.** Its original-ERISA ground requires a statutory or plan violation rather than equitable relief at large. Here § 404 violations are the asserted predicates. The question is their statutory reach and enforceability. Its new-debtor and ancillary-jurisdiction holdings neither decide that question nor convert this dispute into a jurisdictional dismissal. Its express monetary reservations remain intact.

Reconciliation therefore changes her commitment to reversal of the reviewed individual fiduciary-breach basis, with the bounded remand below.

## Distinct groups, presentation and relief

**The 83-member Retired Class.** The affirming commitments reach the communication-capacity and deliberate-loyalty questions on the accepted findings and the full communications, including their warnings about future conditions. They reject parsing accurate current-benefit descriptions from the deliberately linked assurances about future security in this administrative setting. The reversing commitments accept the findings but reject that legal classification and, independently, the individual-action route. None decides negligent misstatement, a general duty to volunteer information or every statement about corporate solvency.

**The ten individual retirees.** The affirming commitments leave the lower court's independent assignment-breach determination undisturbed; they do not newly hold that every comparable assignment necessarily violates § 404. The neutral packet already supplied respondents' pages 43–44, the United States' note 16 and petitioner's reply page 13 note 15. The reply contests recovery, so the component is not conceded. But its assertions that there was no misrepresentation and no contractual lifetime right do not identify error in the distinct unauthorized-assignment ground. The petition's second question concerns nondisclosure despite amendment rights, not the legality of administering these retirees' existing obligations by unilateral assignment. This supports the narrower presentation treatment at historical page 496 without adding a new neutral fact.

The petition's first question asks generally whether participants and beneficiaries may sue individually for fiduciary breach; respondents expressly acknowledge at page 44 that this argument affects both groups. In this reconciliation that statutory-action question supplies the common issue: the affirming Justices reject the categorical plan-only objection and preserve the clarified relief; the reversing Justices apply their statutory-action objection while assuming the undisturbed retiree breach. This is the modeled application of the presented question, not a claim that either historical writing separately articulated this exact group disposition. A capacity-only reversal could not dispose of the retirees' claim.

**Other statutory relief.** The rejected lifetime-contract theory does not establish that a benefits action adequately remedies the class's procurement of lost membership or the retirees' unauthorized assignment. Conversely, that defeat does not prove every other benefits remedy unavailable. The affirming Justices can accept the qualified principle that genuinely adequate statutory relief normally makes further equitable relief unnecessary; they do not adopt an absolute bar on alternative pleading. For the ten retirees, do not import the class's voluntary departure as a reason § 502(a)(1)(B) is categorically unavailable. The preserved relief restores the existing benefit relationship under the clarified decree; it grants neither additional vested rights nor a duplicate compensatory recovery. No comprehensive rule for overlapping actions is needed.

**Equitable category and decree.** The independent monetary-presentation limitation is confirmed by the full historical opinions and remains binding on every commitment. The $7.6 million and $712,332 compensatory awards remain rejected. Affirmance preserves reinstatement and the existing clarified lower judgment without classifying each substituted monetary item as equitable. Reversal rests on statutory authority and, for the class, capacity; it does not introduce a new monetary-category ruling. The $696,195 class figure and $81,812/$82,812 individual discrepancy are not a new Supreme Court award. The lower court may adjust past entitlement for intervening events, receive relevant further evidence and enter a conforming decree. Reinstatement carries only the rights of members of the M-F plan as it now exists; lawful future changes remain possible, subject to applicable law and preserved retaliation or discrimination objections.

**Alternatives and remand.** Affirmance on the reviewed fiduciary ground does not adopt § 510 or equitable estoppel. Reversal of that ground leaves any legally preserved alternative for appellate consideration, rather than directing dismissal of the entire action or guaranteeing recovery. This remand derives from the lower court's unresolved alternatives, not an express historical dissent direction. It revives no terminated-class, punitive, lifetime-contract, settled disability or pension claim. No new payment date, mandate date, amount or amendment is supplied.
