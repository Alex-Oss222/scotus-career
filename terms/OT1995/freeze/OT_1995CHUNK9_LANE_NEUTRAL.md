## Sanitized objective addition (copied)

## Lane v. Peña — 95-365


Lane entered the United States Merchant Marine Academy in July 1991 after satisfying appointment requirements including a Defense Department physical examination. A private physician diagnosed diabetes during the first year; Lane reported it. The Academy's September 1992 medical hearing reported insulin-dependent diabetes. In December 1992 the Academy separated Lane because diabetes disqualified him from the identified Navy/Merchant Marine Reserve or Naval Reserve commissions. The Academy is administered through the Maritime Administration within the Department of Transportation. These are objective published record recitations, not a fresh finding about medical fitness.

Lane unsuccessfully challenged separation administratively, then sued for reinstatement, compensatory damages, attorney fees and costs under Rehabilitation Act §504(a). The District Court granted Lane summary judgment on liability and ordered reinstatement. The Government did not contest the injunction. The court first permitted damages while deferring their amount; after the D.C. Circuit decided *Dorsey v. Department of Labor*, 41 F.3d 1551 (1994), it vacated that damages ruling for lack of an unequivocal federal damages waiver. The D.C. Circuit denied initial en banc reconsideration of *Dorsey* and summarily affirmed. Thus the reviewed damages issue does not reopen the discrimination determination or the unchallenged reinstatement.

The published record recites §504(a), 29 U.S.C. §794(a), as prohibiting exclusion, denial of benefits or discrimination against an otherwise qualified individual with a disability solely by reason of disability under a program or activity receiving federal financial assistance, or conducted by any Executive agency or the Postal Service. Section 505(a)(2), §794a(a)(2), makes Title VI remedies, procedures and rights available to a person aggrieved by an act or failure to act by a recipient of federal assistance or a federal provider of such assistance under §504. These are distinct enacted formulations. Complete official 1994 Code text, including all additional statutory clauses, is being obtained; these recitations are not a claim that the complete sections have yet been independently verified.

Objective-source support: published record narrative at 518 U.S. 187, 189–191, independently downloaded official [United States Reports PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep518/usrep518187/usrep518187.pdf). The neutral reader must not open the full source because it also contains comparator material. Only the narrative and quoted enactment above are extracted. ## Lane: §504(a), 29 U.S.C. §794(a)


Official 1994 Code, Title 29, pp. 283–284 ([PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title29/pdf/USCODE-1994-title29.pdf)):

> No otherwise qualified individual with a disability in the United States, as defined in section 706(8) of this title, shall, solely by reason of her or his disability, be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance or under any program or activity conducted by any Executive agency or by the United States Postal Service. The head of each such agency shall promulgate such regulations as may be necessary to carry out the amendments to this section made by the Rehabilitation, Comprehensive Services, and Developmental Disabilities Act of 1978. Copies of any proposed regulation shall be submitted to appropriate authorizing committees of the Congress, and such regulation may take effect no earlier than the thirtieth day after the date on which such regulation is so submitted to such committees.

Section (b) defines “program or activity” to encompass all operations of its listed State/local instrumentalities, educational institutions and other entities when the statutory assistance conditions are met. Section (c) states that small providers need not make significant structural alterations to existing facilities for program accessibility **if alternative means of providing services are available**, with terms construed by reference to regulations existing March 22, 1988. Section (d) incorporates the specified ADA standards for **employment-discrimination complaints**. Neither the facility alternative nor the employment standard is the academy damages-waiver question presented here. The complete (b)–(d) text is preserved in the downloaded Code, and these provisions must not be silently applied to a different subject.

## Lane: §505, 29 U.S.C. §794a

Official 1994 Code, Title 29, p. 284; the source records no amendment to this section after its November 6, 1978 enactment.

> (a)(1) The remedies, procedures, and rights set forth in section 717 of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. 2000e–16), including the application of sections 706(f) through 706(k) (42 U.S.C. 2000e–5(f) through (k)), shall be available, with respect to any complaint under section 791 of this title, to any employee or applicant for employment aggrieved by the final disposition of such complaint, or by the failure to take final action on such complaint. In fashioning an equitable or affirmative action remedy under such section, a court may take into account the reasonableness of the cost of any necessary work place accommodation, and the availability of alternatives therefor or other appropriate relief in order to achieve an equitable and appropriate remedy.
>
> (2) The remedies, procedures, and rights set forth in title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 [42 U.S.C. 2000d et seq.] shall be available to any person aggrieved by any act or failure to act by any recipient of Federal assistance or Federal provider of such assistance under section 794 of this title.
>
> (b) In any action or proceeding to enforce or charge a violation of a provision of this subchapter, the court, in its discretion, may allow the prevailing party, other than the United States, a reasonable attorney’s fee as part of the costs.

The (a)(1) federal employment channel, (a)(2) §504 channel, and (b) discretionary fee channel are distinct provisions.

## Lane: Rehabilitation Act Amendments of 1986 §1003

Official enacted text, Pub. L. 99–506, 100 Stat. 1845 ([Act PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-100/pdf/STATUTE-100-Pg1807.pdf)); codified at 42 U.S.C. §2000d–7:

> (a) GENERAL PROVISION.—
> (1) A State shall not be immune under the Eleventh Amendment of the Constitution of the United States from suit in Federal court for a violation of section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, the Age Discrimination Act of 1975, title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, or the provisions of any other Federal statute prohibiting discrimination by recipients of Federal financial assistance.
> (2) In a suit against a State for a violation of a statute referred to in paragraph (1), remedies (including remedies both at law and in equity) are available for such a violation to the same extent as such remedies are available for such a violation in the suit against any public or private entity other than a State.
> (b) EFFECTIVE DATE.—The provisions of subsection (a) shall take effect with respect to violations that occur in whole or in part after the date of enactment of the Rehabilitation Act Amendments of 1986.

The enactment date is October 21, 1986. The text's State-immunity provision and comparative-remedies provision must be distinguished from the disputed federal-waiver interpretation.
