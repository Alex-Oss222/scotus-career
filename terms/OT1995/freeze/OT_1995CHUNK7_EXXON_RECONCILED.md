## Material corrections and final positions

**All eight retain affirmance of both submitted components and join final I–V below.** Two rationale corrections are material:

1. **Contract legal causation replaces independent wholesale avoidable-loss adjudication.** The historical opinion separately explains why contractual warranty recovery also requires legal causation, distinguishes *Italia*, and applies the undisturbed sole-proximate-cause determination. The independent freeze instead makes all stranding damages unrecoverable through a separately sufficient avoidable-consequences finding. HIRI presented that argument, and the frozen analysis makes it substantial; presentation and plausibility do not supply a Justice-specific changed premise for substituting or adding that holding. The final commitments do not independently adjudicate complete mitigation of every stranding-loss item. They retain a distinct contract analysis on the legal-causation ground described in IV.
2. **Limited concurrent-findings review replaces affirmative appellate ratification of the particular causal application.** The legal doctrine is reviewed as law; its application to the found circumstances receives limited review. Under *Graver Tank*, the Court declines to disturb concurrent lower findings absent a very obvious and exceptional showing of error. That showing is not made here despite the acknowledged tension between the continuing hose concern and restored-safety findings. The Court does not necessarily ratify the lower courts' particular application. The independent statement that the same ultimate causal conclusion would be reached on independent review is not an additional final holding.

These corrections do not decide strict warranty versus due diligence, excuse post-breakout duties, convert the phase-one assumptions into individual findings, or enlarge the judgment beyond stranding damages.

## Final common propositions and exact joins

**I — Submitted questions, phase-one premise and review: all eight.** Review the tort/comparative-fault and warranty questions in the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the stranding-only Rule 54(b) judgment. Historical findings receive Rule 52 protection; legal rules remain independently reviewable. Proximate and superseding cause involve application of law to fact ordinarily left to the factfinder subject to limited review. Applying the concurrent-findings principle of *Graver Tank*, there is no obvious and exceptional showing justifying disturbance of the lower courts' ultimate causal conclusion on this record. This is not a declaration that legal errors are immune from correction or a universal review standard for every mixed question.

The continuing hose concern and Exxon's original-risk argument remain part of the analysis. The safe-position finding at 1830, available time/personnel, failure to plot fixes and final shoreward turn remain lower-court findings, not newly reconstructed appellate facts. The lower courts expressly characterized the navigation as independent and unforeseeable. Their concurrence is left undisturbed **without necessarily ratifying their particular application**. The historical footnote's fuller narrative is not used to expand the neutral's present factual findings. HIRI's abandonment contention is not converted into an additional waiver holding.

Phase one assumes collective factual contribution and relieves Exxon of proving defendants' fault or strict liability for the breakout. It does not establish each respondent's breach, negligence, corporate obligation or percentage of responsibility. The separate causal inquiry can be resolved on that assumed basis.

**II — Comparative allocation preserves legal causation: all eight.** *Reliable Transfer* displaces automatic equal division with proportional allocation among legally responsible causes; equal division remains where fault is equal or cannot fairly be measured. It did not abolish proximate causation. A later independent and unforeseeable cause may supersede an earlier factual cause, leaving no liability to apportion to the earlier actor for that injury. A plaintiff who is the superseding and sole proximate cause cannot recover part of that loss merely because others' blameworthy conduct contributed in fact.

The degree of the master's negligence alone is insufficient. This is not revival of the major-minor doctrine, an automatic rule for ordinary escape efforts, or a holding that grounding is never a foreseeable risk of breakout. The independent freeze's treatment of the strongest §442B/*Kinsman* argument remains, subject to I's restrained review. The Court retains “comparative fault” without deciding between allocation by comparative culpability alone and allocation also considering the degree of causal contribution, assuming a meaningful distinction exists. Existing settlement-credit, contribution-protection and prejudgment-interest rules are unchanged.

**III — Effect of the undisturbed tort-causation findings: all eight.** The accepted lower determination is that the master's extraordinary, independent navigation was the superseding and sole proximate cause of the stranding. Under II, that determination defeats tort recovery for the reviewed loss. The restored-safe-position and safe-departure findings supply the lower courts' asserted separation from the original peril; their force is not reduced to comparative grossness or elapsed time alone. The continuing-hose tension is acknowledged, not declared nonexistent. Affirmance follows from the legally permissible causal doctrine and the undisturbed determination, not a fresh appellate finding of the complete causal sequence. No present comparative percentage or finding that respondents were blameless is required.

**IV — Distinct contract causation, with strict duty assumed: all eight.** The warranty component receives its own legal analysis. *Italia Societa* recognizes breach of workmanlike service through nonnegligently supplied defective equipment; it does not decide or remove the legal-causation limitation on warranty damages. Contractual duties can exceed negligence duties while recoverable damages remain causally limited. *East River* and contract foreseeability principles support that distinction. Contract foreseeability may be more restrictive in some settings; there is no categorical holding that all contractual causation is identical to tort causation or always stricter.

Assume the stricter submitted safe-berth/workmanlike undertaking and relevant breach rather than resolving the reserved duty construction or finding compliance with strict performance. On the undisturbed causal determination, Exxon's navigation is the sole proximate cause of this stranding loss, and assumed breaches contributing only in fact do not support recovery of that loss under the submitted warranty theories. The factual findings concerning safe departure and available assistance retain their causal significance; the district court's selected due-diligence standard does not become a strict-warranty no-breach finding.

All contract-source qualifications in the independent freeze remain essential. The quoted undertaking covers safe arrival, departure and remaining afloat, includes single point mooring, and retains its exclusion for public channels, fairways, their approaches, anchorages and other publicly maintained areas inside or outside the port. No new finding places this grounding within that exclusion. The quotation is “in relevant part,” not the certified complete executed agreement. No unquoted risk transfer, filing default or promise to insure every later navigational loss is inferred in either direction.

Joint Trial Exhibit 92 remains a separate servant/imputation and indemnification instrument, with the master's retained responsibilities and final decisions. Its interaction with the safe-berth undertaking is not comprehensively adjudicated; no blanket imputation or indemnity shortcut supplies the holding. The safe-berth duty continued after breakout: an earlier superseding force cannot by itself excuse a later breach. The assumed stricter duty and preserved later-breach theory are subject here to the independent contractual causal requirement, not erased through the due-diligence concession. Individual corporate responsibility and the reserved strict-versus-diligence choice remain undecided.

HIRI's avoidable-consequences argument remains a supported alternative contention. It is unnecessary to decide whether it independently makes every stranding-loss component wholly avoidable. The final holding neither converts ordinary promisee negligence into forfeiture nor treats mitigation as always proportional. Its sufficient contract ground is legal causation under the submitted undertakings and undisturbed findings. A different express allocation of later losses would require its own supported terms and preserved theory; the present sources establish no such different premise.

**V — Bifurcation distinction and remedy: all eight.** Reject the legal contention that superseding causation can never be determined separately because comparative fault invariably requires comparing all parties first. Legal responsibility precedes apportionment, and the phase-one assumptions permit that inquiry. This does not adjudicate the excluded free-standing contention that this particular bifurcation was an abuse of discretion or denied due process. Rules 14.1(a)/24.1(a) and the supplied question limits remain operative; neither *Yamaha* nor *Quackenbush* enlarges them.

Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment sustaining the Rule 54(b) judgment against Exxon for stranding-related damages on both submitted components. No remand, new hearing, allocation, damages award, contribution order, contract-construction proceeding or corporate-liability determination follows. The still-pending breakout oil-spill proceedings retain their actual posture and all undecided liability/damage issues. Ordinary mandate procedures apply; no special timing or additional Supreme Court event is supplied.
