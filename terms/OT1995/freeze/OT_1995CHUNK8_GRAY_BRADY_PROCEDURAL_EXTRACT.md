**Petitioner, merits pp.15–19, especially p.19.** The federal Ground A quotation includes both late disclosure impairing preparation and the allegation that police evidence suggesting another perpetrator was not released. Petitioner describes the Commonwealth's opposition to discovery and its assertion that the Brady aspect was defaulted, petitioner's response that the evidence was germane to the general Sorrell constitutional challenge, and the pretrial request for Brady material. Petitioner reports the April1,1993 dismissal, then quotes the amendment: “The Court will amend its Final Judgment by finding that such claim was consistently raised in the State courts and is not procedurally defaulted.” The immediately preceding description identifies that claim as the due-process challenge to admission of Sorrell evidence at the penalty phase. This is positive partial reopening, but the quoted excerpt does not say that all other dismissed grounds were reopened. It is compatible with the separate published recitation that the remaining dismissals were unchanged.

**Respondent, merits pp.48–49, subsection II-B.** Respondent expressly relies on the April1 ruling at C.A.A.194 that the Sorrell Brady claim was not presented in state proceedings, invokes Virginia Code §8.01-654(B)(2), and additionally contends that Gray did not appeal that independent dismissal. The latter contention points to a mention at Fourth Circuit opening brief p.30 n.10 and to its issues statement at pp.2–3. This remains respondent's separate appellate-abandonment argument: the original opening brief has not been fully recovered here, and the published recitations above do not themselves establish that additional appellate bar. Footnote18 argues publicity and ability to investigate before the February1986 state posttrial deadline; its extension to reasonable availability is argument, not a substitute for authenticating the relevant state rule and its exact predicates. Footnote19 distinguishes the separately appealed Smallwood Brady issue.

**Petitioner, reply pp.4–8, with notes8–13.** Petitioner insists the fundamental complaint throughout has been affirmative prosecutorial misrepresentation depriving counsel of a meaningful opportunity to meet the Sorrell proof. At p.6 the reply disclaims the respondent's formulations as a new discovery rule and identifies the submission as the prohibition against deception that keeps the opposing party from developing its case on a determinative issue. It invokes supplementation/refinement of a preserved claim rather than a requirement to plead every legal theory. At pp.7–8 it describes suppression as part of the impaired investigation and trial preparation, including Bagley's account of reliance on an incomplete answer to a Brady request. This supports the integrated due-process/deception theory's asserted continuity; it does not expressly contest the distinct April1 determination that an independent Brady claim was absent from the state proceedings or state that the independent dismissal was reopened.

**Petitioner, reply pp.17–18 and note27: a real but bounded factual disagreement.** Petitioner contests the respondent's assertion that trial counsel already knew at the time of the1985 trial that Timothy Sorrell had been a suspect. It says Moore did not know whether he learned that before or after trial, and Eason did not recall such knowledge; it also denies a duty to keep repeating an already raised objection before the February1986 posttrial deadline. This disagreement must be retained. It addresses counsel's trial/posttrial knowledge, the content of the preparation complaint and the asserted need for further objection/investigation then. It is not by itself a contest of the quoted determination about availability when the later **state habeas petition** was litigated, nor proof that every favorable item was disclosed.
