## Posture, scope and operative sources

All eight non-Stone Justices were seated at argument and decision; no case-specific nonparticipation is established. Review is of the Eighth Circuit's final judgment affirming the district court's Rule 12 standing dismissal **without prejudice**. The complaint's material allegations are taken as true and favorably construed. The accepted allegations and undisputed representative status establish, at this stage, injured represented employees and germaneness. No WARN violation, employee eligibility, amount or litigation adequacy has been tried.

The statutory-authorization issue remains disputed here despite the concession below. The third Hunt condition is within the petition's representational-standing question: petition pp. 14–20 and reply pp. 18–19 n.15 expressly present it. Calling the union's principal theory direct or statutory representation does not remove that alternative. The denial of postjudgment amendment was **not petitioned**, as the United States states at pp. 8–9 n.8; none of these commitments reviews that separate ruling.

The operative statutory combination is 29 U.S.C. §§2101(a)(4), 2102(a) and 2104(a)(1), (5), (7), (b). Section 2101 defines the exclusive employee representative; §2102 gives it the notice function. Section 2104(a)(1) identifies the employees entitled to back pay and benefits. Section 2104(a)(5) expressly includes their representative among persons who may enforce that liability for others. Its enforcement language performs work alongside its venue language; the representative need not become the beneficiary of the employees' recovery. This construction does not authorize a union to recover a local government's penalty or create damages payable to the union.

Surviving Warth, 422 U.S. 490, 500–501, 511, 515–516; Hunt, 432 U.S. 333, 343–345; and Brock, 477 U.S. 274, 281–290, supply the distinction between a concrete represented controversy and the conditions on associational litigation. Warth's refusal of individualized damages and Brock's reservation of individual entitlement to state proceedings must be confronted; neither decided a congressional authorization of this representative damages action. Actual Lujan, June 12, 1992, retains concrete injury, traceability, redressability and stage-appropriate support. Actual Northeastern Florida, June 14, 1993, retains Hunt's three conditions without deciding whether Congress may displace the third.

The operative Standards entries are **Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing**, effective June 14, 1993, and **Associations seeking statutory fee relief**, effective January 12, 1993. Rowland's then-effective §1915 construction supplies a limited statutory-representation analogy, not a constitutional standing holding or a ruling on PLRA's later changes. Actual Matsushita, February 27, 1996, preserves constitutionally adequate representation as a distinct prerequisite to binding absent persons. Its class-settlement and collateral-review rules are not wholesale imported into this uncertified WARN action. Actual Varity and Meghrig, March 19, distinguish statutory authorization, entitled beneficiaries and available remedies.

Carlisle's specific-rule holding supplies no bar against the representative Congress expressly authorized and no new authority to disregard applicable procedure. Sheets concerns a plaintiff's own confidentiality right and resolves neither representative standing nor damages here. AEDPA concerns habeas; PLRA's admitted prison-relief, prisoner-suit and filing provisions create no identified predicate governing this pleaded WARN action. No changed standing rule is inferred from those enactments.

## Supported paths and adverse arguments

**A. Statutorily authorized associational action.** Reverse the standing ruling because Hunt's third condition is a prudential restriction that Congress may displace and has displaced for this WARN representative suit. The represented workers' alleged employment loss, deficient notice and statutory monetary entitlement provide the concrete injury and redressable controversy. The need to prove particular entitlements does not itself destroy that controversy. The rule does not make individual participation unnecessary as a factual matter; it removes that need as a categorical Article III objection to the representative's suit.

The strongest contrary argument combines Warth's damages limitation with Hunt's constitutional language: without each injured employee as a party, differing claims, entitlement proof and uncertainty about binding effect may prevent constitutionally effective redress. The answer is limited. Established representative standing already permits the injured person and named representative to differ. Individualized proof and adequate representation remain necessary where applicable, but they do not turn the third Hunt condition itself into an unalterable constitutional restriction. Concrete member injury and constitutionally adequate binding procedures remain independent constraints. No ruling about the constitutional or prudential status of Hunt's second condition is needed.

**B. Affirm on the statute or the constitutional characterization.** Respondent reads “aggrieved under paragraph (1) or (3)” to restrict every listed plaintiff to a person individually entitled to the payment, treating “representative” as an obsolete remnant in a venue provision. Its historical-development argument concerns deletion of an earlier union-specific remedy. The enacted text nevertheless retains the defined employee representative within the persons permitted to enforce “such liability.” Enforcing employee liability does not recreate the deleted union remedy. The individualized-proof objection remains substantial for procedure and binding effect, but does not justify this categorical standing dismissal once the express authorization is given effect.

**C. Direct injury or a broader legal-representative theory.** The union and United States separately contend that deficient notice impaired the union's bargaining and adjustment functions and that employee compensation redresses that impairment; fiduciary and other authorized-representative analogies also support their position. Respondent answers that compensation reaches employees, the union's pleaded functional injury is insufficiently identified, and certification does not itself establish every asserted fiduciary characteristic. These grounds need not be decided to resolve A. None of the commitments treats failure to give the union notice as automatically establishing its own injury and redressability, requires an assignment, or holds that every statutory representative can litigate every beneficiary's claim.

## Scope, remedy and unresolved matters

The supported disposition reverses the Eighth Circuit's categorical standing ruling and remands for further proceedings on the union's WARN claim. It does not find an employer violation, order back pay, resolve any employee's eligibility or award fees. It does not affirm, reverse or reopen the unpetitioned amendment ruling. No declaratory-relief claim is newly added by this decision.

Section 2104's employee-specific rates, duration limits, benefits and medical-expense requirements, mandatory offsets, permissible defined-benefit service credit and employer-proved good-faith reduction remain operative. The separate local-government penalty and its three-week-payment exception are unaffected. Fees remain discretionary under paragraph (6). Section 2104(b) retains exclusive statutory remedies and denies authority to enjoin a plant closing or mass layoff. The notice exceptions and reduced-notice conditions remain unadjudicated.

Individual participation needed to establish claims, lawful joinder or certification, representative adequacy, avoidance of duplicate recovery, binding effect and any properly presented jury question remain governed by their own law and supported record. No class has been certified, no assignment established, and no employee testimony or other participation declared universally necessary or unnecessary. The reply's payroll-record and medical-claimant-joinder proposals remain advocacy, not findings. The remand prescribes no new mandatory safeguards beyond applicable law.
