## Governing law and the two principal paths

**Original responsibility:** Mississippi v. Louisiana establishes this Court's exclusive jurisdiction over the interstate controversy and preserves distinct private-title litigation; it previously decided no geographic merits. Kansas supplies independent examination of a Master's work with appropriate weight for technical and witness assessment, not an appellate Rule 52(a) command or conclusive adoption of an earlier district judgment. It does not impose its Compact's factual elements or a universal proof quantum here. Nebraska likewise separates claims, findings and final relief; its enforcement/modification burdens are not the burden for Stack Island.

**Path R — accept the supported report within a stated legal rule:** A boundary established on one side of a continuing island does not switch to the other merely because the main navigation channel changes sides. Missouri supplies that island-side rule. Indiana confirms that altered river geography and eventual channel filling do not alone transfer an established island boundary, while its low-water cession remains distinct. The Arkansas decisions establish that an old boundary channel may continue to change by gradual erosion/accretion while it remains a running stream, even after another channel carries the main flow; when the old channel ceases running and becomes stagnant, its boundary fixes and subsequent filling does not continue accretion across that line. Applying that latter principle to a divided island channel preserves the original boundary side without falsely treating the navigation shift itself as a proven sudden new-bed avulsion. It is the governing channel's continued running and later cessation, not the word “frozen,” that distinguishes the two stages.

The strongest objection to Path R is Louisiana's combination of alleged breaks in island continuity, hydrographic/elevation objections and a proposed line far from the live channel. Those objections could matter: disappearance, a different origin of southern formations or an inaccurately located former boundary channel cannot be answered by a place name or a general preference for stable borders. R supplies a reasoned contrary determination for each, including a mean-high-water alternative and an explanation of the surveyor's changed line. The accepted result below rests on those reasons, with the coordinate inconsistency reserved; it is not an independent reading of unread exhibits.

**Path L — sustain an exception and return for a new hearing or different boundary:** A demonstrated legal requirement that the channel fixed immediately at the navigation shift, a supported finding of total island destruction, or a failure to locate the former boundary channel would defeat all or part of the recommended boundary. The strongest basis is the technical specificity of L's objections, including the asserted 1883 overlay, 1948 depths, farmland and the surveyor's earlier different line. The decisive objections to this path on the validated record are that it conflates the live navigation and boundary channels, does not overcome R's separately explained continuity/formation determinations merely by citing technical credentials, and does not identify an additional opportunity that was denied in the full supplemental process. Independent review permits rejection of a report; it does not require a new trial whenever expert evidence conflicts. No Justice treats the historical district judgment or the Master's prestige as dispositive.

**Acquiescence is a separate alternative, not a substitute finding:** M argues prescription and sovereign acquiescence, and L disputes the necessary official conduct and occupation. R makes no recommendation on that basis. Indiana demonstrates why genuine sovereign conduct and long acquiescence may matter; it does not establish those facts here. Because the selected island/channel ground suffices, all eight decline that independent alternative. No adverse-possession judgment among private persons is treated as binding either State's sovereignty.

## Component conclusions and bounded rule adopted

### Exception A — movement of the boundary channel

Overrule. The island-side boundary stays in the original boundary channel when the main navigation channel shifts to the other side, but erosion/accretion can move that still-running boundary channel. It fixes when that channel ceases running and becomes stagnant, rather than at the earlier change in the preferred navigation route. The Arkansas avulsion cases support the running/stagnant distinction; Missouri supplies the island-side premise. Their conjunction does not declare the navigation shift an avulsion or allow any former Mississippi land to migrate across an active boundary channel without legal consequence.

### Exception B — alleged disappearance

Overrule at the scope of R's supported continuity determination. The early-history alternatives lead to the same 1881 Mississippi-side origin under the expert recognition identified in R pp.14–15; no finding that the original pre-statehood island unquestionably survived every earlier event is necessary. For 1883, R pp.19–21 does not rest solely on the new exhibit's questioned provenance: it also evaluates the navigation light, published contemporary map, sworn 1885 accounts of residence/cultivation since 1882 and the Commission's 1883 work description. For 1948, R pp.21–23 identifies vegetation at the corresponding location and the 1949 elevation evidence, as well as the map sequence and disputed absence of precise elevations. The report's finding of continuity is accepted on that cumulative basis, not a newly measured depth or the false proposition that Louisiana offered no hydrographic evidence.

The disagreement between R's 1949 elevation reference and M's 1948 description remains unresolved. No holding relies on converting the 1949 point into a proved 1948 observation. The finding adopted is continuous identity under the report's cumulative reasoning, not an independent modern reconstruction or a rule that overlapping outlines invariably prove survival.

### Exception C — island definition

Overrule on the report's **assumed mean-high-water alternative**, without adopting a universal practical-permanence definition for every river landform. R pp.22–24 expressly assumes Louisiana's mean-high-water formulation in testing this record and finds no convincing significant failure under that assumption. The report also supplies other continuity evidence; labels alone would not suffice. Because the same result follows within that assumed framework, the Court need not decide whether public-land, tidal or surveying definitions mandate the rule for every interstate island dispute. This disposition does not hold that any wet shoal qualifies as an island, that mere recurrence at a former location is identity, or that a physical loss of all relevant land could never matter. “Convincing” in the report is not adopted as a new universal clear-and-convincing proof burden.

### Exception D — Smith line, south-end formation and location

Overrule the challenge to R's identified former-channel boundary recommendation, while reserving the exact implementing schedule. R pp.25–28 finds the southern sandbars on the Mississippi side of the boundary channel before they merged with the continuing island and before the old channel ceased carrying flow. Its explanation concerns the position of the confluence, not a general claim that every downstream deposit belongs to the island. It distinguishes Smith's use of another surveyor in the earlier litigation from his own later determination. The claimed distance from the present navigation channel does not, without more, locate the former boundary channel or disprove it. No new geometric measurement or ruling on the identity of a particular farmer's parcel is made.

The line merits are adopted as the old boundary channel identified by R, subject to verification of the inconsistent call before a precise decree. Neither Appendix A nor E is incorporated now as an operative coordinate schedule. This limitation is not a finding that the whole report lacks technical evidence.

### Exception E and new-hearing petition

Overrule E's legal/technical-treatment objection for the preceding reasons and separately deny the requested new trial of the supplemental hearing. R pp.9–11,17–28 describes independent consideration of the stipulated earlier record, extensive additional testimony and exhibits, cross-examination, argument and two site views at different stages. Louisiana's objections receive independent examination; a disagreement about what the technical proof establishes is not itself denial of an opportunity to present it. R's specific documentary and expert grounds, including its assumed-test analysis, answer the accusation that it simply reinstated a jurisdictionally defective district judgment or relied on names alone. No general exclusion of expert testimony or categorical preference for lay witnesses is adopted.

### Private-title cancellation

Deny Louisiana's prayer to cancel the Houston Group's claim of title, solely in the controversy Louisiana presents. M p.31 identifies the absence of an exception, and L's enumerated exceptions and prayer concern the boundary. R pp.28–32 rejects the disappearance premise and explains that Louisiana, a stranger to these Mississippi titles, cannot obtain cancellation by asserting unidentified other persons' supposedly superior claims. Accept that limited ground and disposition; do not independently decide the alternate federal-patent presumption, laches, deed-description merits or every southern riparian title. No affirmative decree quiets title against absent owners. Mississippi v. Louisiana preserves their otherwise lawful separate claims and distinguishes ownership from sovereign boundary jurisdiction.

### Exact decree implementation

Support an interlocutory disposition of the exceptions and cancellation prayer, retain original jurisdiction, and require an authenticated consistent proposed boundary schedule before the implementing decree. The official report prints Point 3 as **32°48′47″ N, 91°09′37″ W** in Appendix A and **32°49′47″ N, 91°09′37″ W** in Appendix E. The Court cannot enter both or silently choose one. The needed source is the cited survey exhibits P-32D, LA-1A, P-32E and/or Mississippi counterclaim paragraph 5, or an authenticated correction that resolves the discrepancy. The later December 4 event must await that verification. Arkansas (1970) and Indiana illustrate the lawful separation of a decided boundary principle from the survey requiring subsequent Court approval; they supply no substitute coordinate here.

This is an exact implementation blocker, not a reason to turn every independently reviewable exception into an open evidentiary trial. No coordinate, correction, injunction against an identified private parcel, allocation of costs or final closure is invented.
