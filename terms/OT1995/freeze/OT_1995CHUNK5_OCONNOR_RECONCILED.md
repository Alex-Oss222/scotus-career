## Operative propositions and material comparisons

**1. Assumed framework and scope.** The parties' uncontested use of McDonnell Douglas permits answering the submitted replacement question without deciding that framework's wholesale applicability to the ADEA. Historical pp.310–311 make precisely that assumption. Note 1 expressly limits review to treatment as a non-reduction-in-force case. The present validated scope has no material difference requiring a different assumption or expanded review. No universal Title VII/ADEA equivalence is adopted.

**2. General statutory answer.** Section 623(a)(1) addresses discrimination because of the individual's age; §631(a) limits coverage to persons at least 40. A replacement's membership in that protected group does not preclude an inference of discrimination against the claimant. Crossing the age-40 line has no independent probative significance making it a proper necessary prima facie element. Historical p.312's 40/39 and 56/40 comparison confirms the frozen statutory distinction. This is a general rejection of the categorical condition, not an exception limited to O'Connor's particular sixteen-year gap. The validated statutory exceptions, including their conduct-date qualifications, establish no different predicate here and create no new burden.

**3. Inference and relative age.** Within the assumed framework, each prima facie element must have a logical connection to the prohibited discrimination, and the evidence must adequately support that inference. When replacement age supplies the claimed inference, an **insignificant age difference alone does not supply it**; a substantially younger replacement is a materially more reliable indicator than protected-class membership. Historical pp.312–313 state both propositions. Teamsters and Burdine already appear in the validated neutral authority; no new neutral ground is introduced.

The provisional reservation of the precise treatment of small gaps is retained as to numerical boundaries and particular applications, but **not as a reason to omit the supported general insufficiency proposition**. The history's 68/65 illustration and its insignificant/substantial distinction establish no ten-year or other mandatory numerical rule. Nor does that opinion establish replacement by a substantially younger worker as indispensable where other age-related evidence supplies the inference or no replacement can sensibly be identified. The insufficiency of a small gap alone is distinct from the sufficiency of an entire record containing other proof. The sixteen-year difference cannot be excluded because Finnell was 40; it does not automatically establish every prima facie element, satisfactory performance, actual motive or a triable ultimate claim.

**4. Production, persuasion and legal form.** Historical pp.310–311 cite Hicks for the production framework and the conditional consequence when a prima facie case is established by a preponderance and the employer remains silent. That condition differs from disbelieving reasons the employer actually produces. The simulated Hicks decision has a different topology and narrower controlling formulation than its historical counterpart, but it does not displace Burdine's governing allocation or these initial-production propositions. Its rejection of automatic liability from falsity, and permission to infer discrimination without a special extra category of proof, remain governing. No respondent silence is found here. Hazen's actual-motivation requirement remains an ultimate inquiry, not a command to prove the whole case before obtaining the initial inference.

The supported form is a categorical rejection of an irrelevant eligibility condition together with an evidence-based relative-age proposition. Neither a numerical presumption nor a newly compulsory sequence, additional factor, direct-evidence category, hearing or burden shift is justified. Conversely, merely answering that this particular protected replacement is permissible would unnecessarily withhold the supported general rule.

**5. Formal judgment and bounded remedy.** Historical p.313 reverses the Fourth Circuit and remands; it does not award liability or direct trial. The independent commitments already identify a legal error requiring removal of the reviewed merits affirmance and lower-court reconsideration. Their preference for vacatur identifies no changed legal, factual or remedial premise making reversal inappropriate. The frozen neutral packet expressly allowed either reversal/remand or vacatur/remand; reconsidering that existing choice introduces no new ground. Each Associate's date-eligible McKennon position confirms that reversal of an ADEA categorical bar can leave liability and relief undecided. The formal commitment is therefore revised to **reversal of the reviewed ADEA merits affirmance and remand**, preserving the provisional limits on further proceedings.
