## Actual question, record and statutory qualifications

The submitted question is:

> Is a prima facie claim of age discrimination precluded by the fact that the employee who secures the job that the claimant was denied is within the class of persons protected against age discrimination?

That does not authorize petitioner to reopen every adverse ruling or permit a new Supreme Court evidentiary record.

Section 623(a)(1) forbids the covered adverse employment treatment “because of such individual's age.” Section 631(a) limits the prohibitions to individuals “at least 40 years of age.” Neither text states a replacement-age element. Section 623(f)(1)'s bona fide occupational qualification, reasonable factors other than age and qualified foreign-law provisions, and §623(f)(3)'s good-cause permission, retain their separate terms. Performance and restructuring may supply nondiscriminatory reasons; assertion alone establishes no defense, fact or shifted burden. No BFOQ or foreign-workplace predicate is presented.

## Governing law and public individual authorities

**Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness**, present operation effective **Hazen Paper, April 20, 1993**, requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Correlated characteristics do not automatically establish age motivation. Actual proxy or mixed age/pension motivation remains possible; disparate impact and age-triggered vesting remain unresolved. The willfulness component concerns knowing or reckless disregard and supplies no new prima facie element or present damages entitlement.

**Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof**, present operation effective **Hicks, June 25, 1993**, rejects automatic liability from a proven prima facie case plus disbelief of the employer's explanations, while permitting those circumstances to establish discrimination without a required additional category of direct or independent proof. Burdine's ultimate persuasion allocation remains governing. No new causal standard, universal Title VII/ADEA equivalence or burden shift is proposed here.

McDonnell Douglas, Furnco, Burdine and Aikens preserve the distinct work of an initial inference, employer production and ultimate persuasion. The parties proceed on use of the indirect framework in ADEA litigation; resolving the submitted replacement issue on that assumption need not decide its universal application. Anderson and Celotex govern summary judgment: supported favorable inferences belong to the nonmovant, but speculation and merely colorable evidence are insufficient. Rejecting a formal prima facie condition does not itself establish a triable ultimate claim.

## Commonly specified propositions and remedy for the individual entries

For precision, the entries use these substantive formulations. An entry's stated join limits remain controlling for that Justice.

**Replacement proposition:** Replacement within the protected group does not, by itself, preclude the initial inference of age discrimination. The claimant's protected minimum and the replacement's age do different work. Where replacement age is relied upon, the relevant evidentiary consideration is relative age in the circumstances; a materially younger replacement can support an inference even when at least 40. This sixteen-year difference cannot be disqualified merely because Finnell had reached 40. That does not establish every prima facie element, satisfactory performance, actual discriminatory cause or liability.

**Reserved proof questions:** Do not require a replacement under 40; do not prescribe a ten-year cutoff or other numerical minimum. Do not declare every younger replacement sufficient regardless of context, or every qualified protected employee's discharge plus continued need sufficient without circumstances supporting an age inference. Conversely, do not make a substantially younger replacement an indispensable element where other evidence supplies the inference or replacement cannot sensibly be identified. The precise treatment of small age gaps, older replacements, mass reductions and every alternative prima facie formulation is unnecessary here. No direct-evidence requirement, pretext-plus rule, mandatory extra proof category or change in ultimate persuasion follows.

**Selected merits remedy:** Vacate the Fourth Circuit's judgment **insofar as it affirms summary judgment on the ADEA merits**, and remand for reconsideration under the corrected replacement rule and existing proof and summary-judgment law. The court below may sustain summary judgment on a genuinely sufficient remaining ground after that reconsideration; the remand directs neither trial nor liability and does not set a new evidentiary standard. No separate reversal or vacatur of the discovery or Rule 59/60 rulings is selected. Dennis's affidavit is not added to the original record. No damages, reinstatement, backpay, willfulness finding, new discovery or compulsory hearing is ordered.

This remedy does not hold that every alternative ground below was erroneous or necessarily dependent on replacement age. The reduction-in-force formulation itself referred to retained unprotected persons; its application compared performance while treating contested current-performance explanations as decisive, and the ordinary-proof discussion incorporated that comparison. Petitioner identifies the volume memorandum, comparative material and timely age remark as contrary evidence. These connections make a fresh appellate assessment under the corrected inference rule material. The remand rests on that concrete relationship, not on a general rule that one mistaken ground always defeats every other ground or that an incomplete source collection alone requires favorable relief.

## Alternative-path conclusions and preservation for reconciliation

The **categorical-rule affirmance path** has been tested against its strongest basis: a judicial presumption needs circumstances connected to discrimination, and coverage provides an administrable line. Each individual commitment rejects that line because the statute's plaintiff-eligibility boundary does not distinguish whether a materially younger protected replacement supports age discrimination. Retaining ordinary proof as a second route does not make an unsupported restriction on the assumed indirect route proper. This conclusion does not accept petitioner's broader presumption or excuse the remaining prima facie showing.

The **reject-the-rule but affirm-on-another-ground path** remains lawful in principle. Neither Rule 14 nor the missing cross-petition categorically bars it. Each entry declines to select it on this record for the stated relationship between the lower formulations, incorporated comparative analysis and actual contrary evidence. No entry finds the employer's reasons false or holds that every alternative ground fails. The selected remand expressly permits a properly supported summary judgment after reconsideration; it does not turn a limited question into a full evidentiary appeal.

The **reject-the-rule and order trial or liability path** lacks the requisite present determination of the remaining proof questions and, for affidavit-dependent versions, an authorized change to the postjudgment ruling. It is not selected by any individual entry. The record supplies no damages amount, willfulness finding or remedial entitlement.

No commitment rests on a mandatory ten-year line, a universal substantial-gap element, a new general test for all ADEA cases, a categorical same-protected-group safe harbor, a direct-evidence demand, or a changed ultimate persuasion allocation. Differences from a later comparator must be evaluated only after this handoff is frozen; no historical match or departure is claimed here.
