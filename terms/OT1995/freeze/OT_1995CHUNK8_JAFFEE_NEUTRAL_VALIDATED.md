## Source-supported record, rule and qualifications

Source: the published [Seventh Circuit opinion, 51 F.3d 1346](https://openjurist.org/51/f3d/1346/jaffee-v-redmond), especially 1348-1352, 1354-1358 and notes 3-9, 13, 17-19. The source receipt identifies these factual, privilege and footnote passages; this validation used its sanitized extract. The case involves a federal excessive-force claim and a pendent Illinois wrongful-death claim, not diversity litigation alone. The jury awarded $45,000 on the federal claim and $500,000 on the state claim. The Court of Appeals rejected the separate objection to the deadly-force instruction, but reversed the judgment and ordered a new trial because of the privilege ruling and associated instruction.

The lower opinion reports conflicting eyewitness testimony about whether Allen was armed. Those are competing accounts, not an established finding that Redmond's shooting was lawful. Redmond sought counseling from Karen Beyer, a licensed clinical social worker, beginning three or four days after the shooting, continuing two or three sessions a week through at least January 1992. Beyer worked for the Village. The court describes her master's degree and extensive psychotherapy experience; her professional role is more specific than an unlicensed counselor or casual confidant.

The district court denied the motion to quash and ordered disclosure. It originally barred Redmond's shooting testimony but vacated that sanction before trial. The sanction actually delivered was an adverse-inference instruction: the jury was entitled to presume the withheld notes unfavorable to Redmond and the Village. The court also told jurors that withholding them lacked legal justification. Do not describe this as an actual disclosure of all counseling communications or as a continuing exclusion of Redmond's trial testimony. Beyer answered general credential and scheduling questions and some questions about the incident, and produced three redacted pages; the record recitation does not by itself settle waiver of all other treatment communications.

The Seventh Circuit recognized a privilege for confidential treatment communications, including licensed social workers, but made protection depend on balancing evidentiary need against privacy in the interests of justice. It treated other eyewitness testimony as reducing need in this record. Recognition, professional coverage, the availability of case-specific balancing, and the consequence for the trial instruction are distinct review questions.

The contemporaneous Rule 501, enacted at 88 Stat. 1933-1934, states:

> Except as otherwise required by the Constitution of the United States or provided by Act of Congress or in rules prescribed by the Supreme Court pursuant to statutory authority, the privilege of a witness, person, government, State, or political subdivision thereof shall be governed by the principles of the common law as they may be interpreted by the courts of the United States in the light of reason and experience. However, in civil actions and proceedings, with respect to an element of a claim or defense as to which State law supplies the rule of decision, the privilege of a witness, person, government, State, or political subdivision thereof shall be determined in accordance with State law.

The [official enactment](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-88/pdf/STATUTE-88-Pg1926-2.pdf), PDF pages 8-9, supplies the text. The 2011 restyling is not the operative wording. The state-law sentence must not disappear, although the court below treated the common federal privilege question as governing the evidence relevant to both claims. The proposed specific privilege Rule 504 was not enacted by Congress; it is not an operative federal rule. The lower opinion's report that all States recognize some form of psychotherapist privilege supports the existence of widespread state recognition, not uniform scope, qualifications, exceptions or professional coverage. Its Illinois excerpts identify a patient/therapist privilege under 740 ILCS 110/10 and a definition including social workers under 110/2.

Source-supported issues: Rule 501's permission for reason-and-experience development versus the cost of withholding probative evidence; whether the treatment relationship depends on reliable confidentiality; whether licensing and treatment function justify including clinical social workers; and whether individual balancing protects access to evidence or defeats advance assurance of confidentiality. These issues arise from Rule 501 and the lower opinion. They do not themselves decide recognition, scope or remedy. Daubert and Williamson regulate other evidence questions and do not decide privilege.

## Supported contrary paths and remedial consequences

The recognition path derives a confidential-treatment privilege from Rule 501's reason-and-experience authority, the treatment function and the support of state practice. Its strongest objection is that privilege withholds relevant evidence and the supplied state consensus proves no uniform federal scope. The nonrecognition or narrower-recognition path emphasizes that cost and the need for a defensible common-law boundary; its counterargument is that reliable prospective confidentiality may be essential to the treatment relationship Congress left courts authority to protect.

Professional coverage is independent: licensing, training and actual psychotherapeutic function support inclusion of Beyer's clinical social-work treatment; a boundary confined to particular medical professions would have to explain why the function and state practice do not suffice. No equivalence to an unlicensed confidant is assumed.

Case-specific balancing offers access where evidentiary need is strong; the competing path holds that uncertain future disclosure can undermine the privilege's treatment purpose. Preserve the lower court's balancing ground as an actual ground, not an already rejected option. If a privilege applies, the adverse-inference/no-legal-justification instruction requires a remedy analysis tied to the judgment below. If protection is rejected or narrowed, identify the consequence for the appellate new-trial disposition without assuming that every other issue or every note has been adjudicated. Partial disclosure does not establish waiver of all treatment communications.
