## Reconciliation result and controlling record

The provisional disposition positions survive. The common assumption that Rule 16 can be left undecided does **not** survive for every Justice. The historical exclusion, Breyer's affirmative Rule 16/materiality ground and Stevens's discretionary-power ground must be assessed separately. Neither the parties' historical materiality arguments nor a general preference for the narrower available ground supplies a changed premise justifying their replacement.

The neutral record remains controlling: Beller's 24 Black defendants came from the defined federal-public-defender cocaine-base cases **closed during 1991**, with the stated exclusions. That sample is neither all federal cases filed that year nor the eligible charging population. Reed's firsthand professional comparison and the relayed treatment-center observations have different foundations; neither provides the pertinent drug-quantity, firearm, evidence and criminal-history comparison. The Government supplied developed reconsideration proffers, including Scheper's particular charging explanations and the investigators' referral accounts. Those submissions must be assessed but are not findings of neutrality. Non-Black federal prosecutions do not logically refute the precisely bounded sample.

The judge stated that she considered all the evidence; disputed reliance does not remove the supplemental declarations. The discounted newspaper and extra-record Berk–Campbell study do not cure the comparison gap. The adjudicated order covers cocaine-base **and federal-firearm** charges, race, investigating agencies and selection criteria; the hearing specifies 1989 through 1992. The later crack-only exchange remains ambiguous and supplies no silent expansion. These defendants had no preceding state charges. This is review of the discovery predicate for a refusal-based dismissal, not a discrimination merits judgment, acquittal or direct interlocutory discovery appeal. ## Component reconciliation

**Rule 16.** Historically O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg accepted that subdivision (a)(1)(C) does not authorize discovery to prepare a selective-prosecution claim. The rule distinguishes a defense against the prosecution's criminal case from an independent constitutional challenge to the decision to prosecute; the historical structural reasoning also relied on subdivision (a)(2)'s work-product treatment. Souter and Ginsburg confined their acceptance to the selective-prosecution issue. Breyer affirmatively rejected the case-in-chief limitation: the three disjunctive categories perform different work, and materiality limits the residual category. Stevens agreed Rule 16 was not the source of the judge's power.

The safe reply, printed 1–8, confirms that the Government argued materiality and disclaimed creating another categorical exemption; the lower courts did not decide Rule 16. The historical opinion itself acknowledges that presentation posture and that both parties addressed the Rule here. Those facts therefore do not justify a different simulated treatment. They also do not establish waiver or require attributing the eventual construction to the Government. An affirmative interpretation remains available on this presented question.

The changed Williams supervisory holding does not construe “defense.” Kimberlin preserves circumstantial proof in civil motive litigation; Klinger requires treatment-specific equal-protection comparisons. Neither changes Rule 16's text or determines its source-of-authority question. Carlisle preserves rule-consistent supervision but forbids using a general power to perform the same act a specific rule forecloses. It does not equate this Rule's nonauthorization with a universal prohibition on constitutionally warranted discovery. These sources support careful limits, not replacing the identified historical Rule 16 positions with a uniform assumption. The six reversing Justices who exclude Rule 16 proceed, as in Wade, on the assumption that discovery could be available on an appropriate showing; their resolution establishes no additional source of discovery authority. Breyer's affirmative Rule 16 ground and Stevens's discretionary-power ground remain distinct.

**Discovery threshold and comparator scope.** The reversing commitments support a meaningful preliminary showing: some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, with the present insufficiency resolved on **effect**. For the indirect racial-pattern theory presented, there must be evidence that persons of another race relevantly similarly situated for federal prosecution could have been prosecuted but were not. The showing is less than a prima facie merits case. “Clear evidence” used in discussing ultimate rebuttal of prosecutorial regularity must not become the discovery burden; nor does Wade's substantial-threshold language decide a separate purpose deficiency here.

The requirement concerns relevant similarity, not perfect identity, and supported statistical or circumstantial comparison is not categorically inferior to named examples. Klinger and Kimberlin reinforce those propositions without eliminating the necessary selection comparison. The provisional statement that direct evidence can establish the required inference must be confined: **whether direct admissions of discriminatory purpose dispense with the similarly-situated requirement remains open**, as in historical footnote 3. No such admissions appear here. The decision does not create a universal list of mandatory comparator characteristics; quantity, firearm allegations and evidence strength matter to this federal-selection record.

**Application.** The selected sample and general state observations fail to connect the observed racial concentration to relevantly comparable federal decisions. That application can consider the Government's developed explanations without finding them true. The historical writings already considered those explanations and the same sample limitations; they are not changed facts. Nor does the historically available Rule 16 argument become new briefing because the independent model gave it greater weight. The need to discover more information is substantial, but it does not itself establish the missing comparison. The historical expectation that a valid claim should permit some state/federal comparison is a conditional evidentiary inference, not an adjudication that every state record is accessible or every subpoena enforceable. No such factual guarantee is added.

**Stevens's distinct inquiry.** Correct the provisional Rule 16 coverage and any implication of a defendant's discovery entitlement. His historical position expressly accepted that these defendants established neither the selective-prosecution defense nor a right to discovery under Rule 16 or inherent authority. He nevertheless sustained the district judge's discretion to make an appropriate inquiry on this disturbing evidentiary showing. That permission-versus-entitlement distinction remains legally consequential. The cumulative racial pattern, Reed's experience, the federal/state penalty consequences and the judge's consideration of the response support his refusal to treat every inquiry as an abuse of discretion. They do not prove discrimination, supply a representative denominator or shift ultimate persuasion. His acknowledgment that the order might be broader than necessary is preserved; it is not converted into a chosen criteria-only remand or unconditional access to protected files.

**Remedy.** For each reversing Justice, reverse the Ninth Circuit's affirmance and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of this discovery order; the dependent refusal-based dismissal cannot stand on that predicate. Ordinary pretrial prosecution resumes under ordinary law. No conviction, new trial after acquittal, compelled evidentiary supplementation or automatic renewed discovery follows. Stevens would affirm the reviewed discovery/sanction judgment on the discretionary ground, without inventing another opportunity to refuse and then comply. Neither position decides the appropriate remedy for **proved** racial selective prosecution, a question the historical opinion expressly reserved. Williams's without-prejudice remedy does not transplant a prejudice label into this distinct sanction.

## Eight Justice-specific checks

### 1. Stevens

**Historical premise:** Rule 16 supplies no authority and this showing creates no discovery right, but a judge may exercise bounded inherent discretion to inquire; the reviewed exercise was permissible. **Changed-premise test:** His actual Williams position now supports controlling bounded supervisory law, strengthening the available judicial-power basis. His actual Carlisle dissent also distinguishes a party's failed entitlement from a retained judicial function. The latter is substantially his historical position, not a new premise; neither source changes the Rule 16 language or makes this showing sufficient to demand discovery. His Kimberlin and Klinger joins permit circumstantial proof but do not create that entitlement. Wade's same meaningful screening principle remains.

**Reconciled commitment:** Affirm, with the discretionary inquiry and consequent sanction ground stated above. **Correction:** Remove affirmative Rule 16 coverage and any discovery-entitlement reading of Path A. Preserve his substantive disagreement with the reversing application, the possible scope qualification and the distinction between judging a supported inquiry and adjudicating discrimination. The changed supervisory law reinforces rather than displaces his historical ground; no material historical departure is retained.

### 2. O'Connor

**Historical premise:** Accept the Rule 16 exclusion, require the preliminary selection comparison and find it absent. **Changed-premise test:** Her actual Williams join accepts a confined supervisory duty, but not a new Rule 16 construction or unlimited access to charging files. Her actual Klinger opinion makes the challenged treatment the proper comparison unit; that rejects perfect-identity requirements while supporting attention to the armed, substantial, well-supported federal charges here. Kimberlin admits circumstantial proof. Her Wade screening commitment and Carlisle rule-consistency position do not change the historical source-of-authority premise.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand on the stated discovery/sanction ground. **Correction:** Replace assumed Rule 16 coverage with its exclusion of selective-prosecution preparation. Preserve relevant-similarity, nonconclusive Government-proffer and below-merits-proof limits. No material historical departure is retained.

### 3. Scalia

**Historical premise:** Rule 16 does not authorize this discovery; the constitutional claim's discovery threshold is not met. **Changed-premise test:** Actual Carlisle's specific-rule analysis supports examining this Rule's text and structure; it does not require avoiding an affirmative interpretation merely because another ground suffices. Actual Williams changes the law he must apply but supplies a grand-jury duty, not a Rule 16 entitlement. His Kimberlin/Klinger joins preserve legitimate circumstantial comparison without eliminating screening, consistent with his Wade commitment.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Withdraw the provisional insistence on assuming coverage as an unnecessary-to-decide issue; accept the selective-prosecution exclusion and the separate insufficient-effect ground. Preserve the bar against general supervision circumventing governing limits without declaring all inherent power abolished. No material historical departure is retained.

### 4. Kennedy

**Historical premise:** Join the Rule 16 exclusion and the insufficient-comparison application. **Changed-premise test:** His Carlisle dissent recognizes a retained function where the specific rules do not withdraw it; it is not a newly changed position and does not establish every asserted inquiry as permissible. Actual Williams, Kimberlin and Klinger retain limited supervision and meaningful circumstantial proof without construing Rule 16 or curing the missing selection evidence. His Wade position supplies the continuing distinction between constitutional review and discovery entitlement; Cooper addresses a different ultimate-persuasion problem.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Adopt the Rule 16 exclusion rather than merely assuming coverage. Retain constitutional review, the preliminary/ultimate distinction and no automatic criteria-disclosure duty. His distinct Carlisle position does not compel Stevens's application here. No material historical departure is retained.

### 5. Souter

**Historical premise:** Accept the discovery threshold and record failure, and join the Rule 16 discussion **only as applied to this issue**. **Changed-premise test:** His actual Wade opinion provides essentially the same review-with-screening distinction used historically; its authorship or prior joins alone are not changed premises. Kimberlin and Klinger preserve circumstantial, treatment-specific proof, not discovery without the required comparison. Carlisle's surviving rule-consistent authority and Williams's particular grand-jury duty do not change the precise Rule 16 question.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Accept exclusion of selective-prosecution claims under subdivision (a)(1)(C), while declining a rule for all defenses unrelated to guilt. This qualification performs legal work; an unqualified case-in-chief-only proposition would exceed his commitment. Preserve the below-merits threshold and open direct-admission question. No material historical departure is retained.

### 6. Thomas

**Historical premise:** Accept Rule 16 exclusion and the deficient comparison. **Changed-premise test:** His changed Williams join recognizes confined supervisory authority, but a known-evidence grand-jury duty and its separate sanction conditions do not expand “defense” in Rule 16. His actual Mezzanatto rule-text/background method operates at that case's limited waiver scope; no generalized new regularity presumption is imported. Wade, Kimberlin, Klinger and Carlisle respectively preserve screening, permissible proof, pertinent comparison and rule consistency. None supplies the missing textual or evidentiary change.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Adopt Rule 16's selective-prosecution exclusion in place of the provisional assumption. Retain circumstantial-proof availability and reject an inference of entitlement from the selected numerator alone. Constitutional review is preserved independently of Rule 16. No material historical departure is retained.

### 7. Ginsburg

**Historical premise:** Expressly agree that subdivision (a)(1)(C) excludes selective-prosecution claims, while reserving other contexts, including affirmative defenses unrelated to the merits. **Changed-premise test:** Her actual Kimberlin opinion is a concrete new governing source concerning evidentiary form. It preserves discovery limits and does not contradict the historical comparison requirement, which itself does not demand direct proof of purpose. Klinger supplies no different Rule 16 construction. Her Carlisle join and the changed Williams rule preserve the distinction between authority, restrictions and entitlement.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Replace the provisional unnecessary-to-decide Rule 16 assumption with the precise historical exclusion and express unpresented-defense reservation. Preserve her no-direct-evidence-only and no-completed-merits-proof qualifications. Do not represent her evidentiary-form concern as a rejection of the similarly-situated showing or as agreement with Breyer on the Rule's application here. No material historical departure is retained.

### 8. Breyer

**Historical premise:** Rule 16 affirmatively provides a possible source, its three categories defeat the proposed case-in-chief restriction, and this request nevertheless fails materiality for the missing relevant comparison. His partial agreement does not include the Rule 16 exclusion. **Changed-premise test:** His actual Kimberlin/Klinger joins strengthen the explanation that legitimate circumstantial comparison remains possible; they do not cure this proffer. Carlisle bars evasion of an applicable restriction but does not make all nonauthorization prohibition. It neither forces a narrower Rule 16 interpretation nor requires deciding hypothetical work-product exceptions. His earlier joins and the historically identical briefing do not justify retreating to mere assumed coverage.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand on Rule 16 materiality and the shared record insufficiency. **Correction:** State affirmative Rule 16 availability, rather than assuming it, and exclude his support from the contrary Rule 16 proposition. Preserve his structural/drafting rationale, independent-source possibility and reservation of any necessary work-product exception: this case establishes none. Proportionate discovery can follow a sufficient predicate but cannot supply one. No invented numbered partial-join sections or guaranteed easy access to state records. No material historical departure is retained.
