## Component reconciliation

**Rule 16.** Historically O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg accepted that subdivision (a)(1)(C) does not authorize discovery to prepare a selective-prosecution claim. The rule distinguishes a defense against the prosecution's criminal case from an independent constitutional challenge to the decision to prosecute; the historical structural reasoning also relied on subdivision (a)(2)'s work-product treatment. Souter and Ginsburg confined their acceptance to the selective-prosecution issue. Breyer affirmatively rejected the case-in-chief limitation: the three disjunctive categories perform different work, and materiality limits the residual category. Stevens agreed Rule 16 was not the source of the judge's power.

The safe reply, printed 1–8, confirms that the Government argued materiality and disclaimed creating another categorical exemption; the lower courts did not decide Rule 16. The historical opinion itself acknowledges that presentation posture and that both parties addressed the Rule here. Those facts therefore do not justify a different simulated treatment. They also do not establish waiver or require attributing the eventual construction to the Government. An affirmative interpretation remains available on this presented question.

The changed Williams supervisory holding does not construe “defense.” Kimberlin preserves circumstantial proof in civil motive litigation; Klinger requires treatment-specific equal-protection comparisons. Neither changes Rule 16's text or determines its source-of-authority question. Carlisle preserves rule-consistent supervision but forbids using a general power to perform the same act a specific rule forecloses. It does not equate this Rule's nonauthorization with a universal prohibition on constitutionally warranted discovery. These sources support careful limits, not replacing the identified historical Rule 16 positions with a uniform assumption. The four reversing Justices who exclude Rule 16 proceed, as in Wade, on the assumption that discovery could be available on an appropriate showing; their resolution establishes no additional source of discovery authority. Breyer's affirmative Rule 16 reading is a historically supported alternative that Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg adopt on their own grounds below; Stevens's discretionary-power ground remains his distinct addition.

**Discovery threshold and comparator scope.** Every commitment supports a meaningful preliminary showing below merits proof. For the four reversing Justices it is some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, with the present insufficiency resolved on **effect**: for the indirect racial-pattern theory presented, there must be evidence that persons of another race relevantly similarly situated for federal prosecution could have been prosecuted but were not. For Path S it is a substantial threshold showing, retaining Wade's screen, of specific and reliable evidence that raises a substantial question in the relevant enforcement setting and identifies the needed information, with full comparator evidence not indispensable when its reasonable unavailability is shown. Neither is a prima facie merits case. “Clear evidence” used in discussing ultimate rebuttal of prosecutorial regularity must not become the discovery burden; nor does Wade's substantial-threshold language decide a separate purpose deficiency here.

The requirement concerns relevant similarity, not perfect identity, and supported statistical or circumstantial comparison is not categorically inferior to named examples. Klinger and Kimberlin reinforce those propositions without eliminating the necessary selection comparison. For the four reversing Justices the provisional statement that direct evidence can establish the required inference must be confined: **whether direct admissions of discriminatory purpose dispense with the similarly-situated requirement remains open**, as in historical footnote 3. Path S decides only that direct evidence of a discriminatory directive can justify inquiry without comparators; the merits question remains open. No such evidence appears here. The decision does not create a universal list of mandatory comparator characteristics; quantity, firearm allegations and evidence strength matter to this federal-selection record.

**Application.** For the four reversing Justices, the selected sample and general state observations fail to connect the observed racial concentration to relevantly comparable federal decisions. For Path S, the same submissions, assessed under the reasonable-unavailability rule together with Reed's professional state/federal contrast, the severity of the federal charging consequences and the unanswered gaps in the Government's initial explanations, ground an inference sufficient for a limited inquiry into bounded existing records, though not for the original order. Either application can consider the Government's developed explanations without finding them true. The historical writings already considered those explanations and the same sample limitations; they are not changed facts. Nor does the historically available Rule 16 argument become new briefing because the independent model gave it greater weight. The need to discover more information is substantial, but it does not itself establish the missing comparison. The historical expectation that a valid claim should permit some state/federal comparison is a conditional evidentiary inference, not an adjudication that every state record is accessible or every subpoena enforceable. No such factual guarantee is added.

**Stevens's distinct inquiry.** Exclude any implication that his discretionary ground alone creates a defendant's discovery entitlement. His historical position expressly accepted that these defendants established neither the selective-prosecution defense nor a right to discovery under Rule 16 or inherent authority, and he nevertheless sustained the district judge's discretion to make an appropriate inquiry on this disturbing evidentiary showing. That permission-versus-entitlement distinction remains legally consequential for the source he adds: his commitment rests a defendant's entitlement on Path S's Rule 16 reading, as his own independent reading of “defense” did, and treats his discretion as operating within the staged inquiry. The cumulative racial pattern, Reed's experience, the federal/state penalty consequences and the judge's consideration of the response support his refusal to treat every inquiry as an abuse of discretion. They do not prove discrimination, supply a representative denominator or shift ultimate persuasion. His acknowledgment that the order might be broader than necessary is preserved and answered by a proportionate order confined to existing records, not by unconditional access to protected files.

**Remedy.** For each reversing Justice, reverse the Ninth Circuit's affirmance and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of this discovery order; the dependent refusal-based dismissal cannot stand on that predicate, and ordinary pretrial prosecution resumes under ordinary law. For each Path S Justice, vacate the affirmance, set aside the order and dependent dismissal, and remand for the limited inquiry the record justifies, with any replacement order confined to appropriately bounded existing, material, unprotected records, reassessed if suitable records do not exist, and binding when entered. No conviction, new trial after acquittal, compelled evidentiary supplementation or automatic renewed discovery follows under either path. Neither position decides the appropriate remedy for **proved** racial selective prosecution, a question the historical opinion expressly reserved. Williams's without-prejudice remedy does not transplant a prejudice label into this distinct sanction.

## Eight Justice-specific checks

### 1. Stevens

**Historical premise:** Rule 16 supplies no authority and this showing creates no discovery right, but a judge may exercise bounded inherent discretion to inquire; the reviewed exercise was permissible. **Changed-premise test:** His actual Williams position supports controlling bounded supervisory law, strengthening the available judicial-power basis. His actual Carlisle dissent distinguishes a party's failed entitlement from a retained judicial function. His Kimberlin and Klinger joins permit circumstantial proof, and Wade's same meaningful screening principle remains. His own independent reading that information material to a genuine selective-prosecution defense is not categorically outside Rule 16's “defense”, read with his McCleskey and Carlisle dissents, which resist treating evidentiary or procedural difficulty as insulation from constitutional review, and with his historical acknowledgment that the order may have been broader than necessary, supplies a supported ground for Path S's reading of “defense” to reach a properly presented constitutional defense capable of defeating the prosecution and for a limited-inquiry remand; it does not make this showing sufficient to sustain the order as entered.

**Reconciled commitment:** Vacate and remand on Path S, with his discretionary inquiry operating within the staged framework. **Departure retained:** He departs from his historical affirmance and from his historical agreement that Rule 16 is not the source, on the supported ground just stated. Preserve his substantive disagreement with Path R's application, the possible scope qualification, the distinction between judging a supported inquiry and adjudicating discrimination, and his refusal to give the Government a new unconditional opportunity to refuse; a valid replacement order binds when entered.

### 2. O'Connor

**Historical premise:** Accept the Rule 16 exclusion, require the preliminary selection comparison and find it absent. **Changed-premise test:** Her actual Williams join accepts a confined supervisory duty, but not a new Rule 16 construction or unlimited access to charging files. Her actual Klinger opinion makes the challenged treatment the proper comparison unit; that rejects perfect-identity requirements while supporting attention to the armed, substantial, well-supported federal charges here. Kimberlin admits circumstantial proof. Her Wade screening commitment and Carlisle rule-consistency position do not change the historical source-of-authority premise.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand on the stated discovery/sanction ground. **Correction:** Replace assumed Rule 16 coverage with its exclusion of selective-prosecution preparation. Preserve relevant-similarity, nonconclusive Government-proffer and below-merits-proof limits. No material historical departure is retained.

### 3. Scalia

**Historical premise:** Rule 16 does not authorize this discovery; the constitutional claim's discovery threshold is not met. **Changed-premise test:** Actual Carlisle's specific-rule analysis supports examining this Rule's text and structure; it does not require avoiding an affirmative interpretation merely because another ground suffices. Actual Williams changes the law he must apply but supplies a grand-jury duty, not a Rule 16 entitlement. His Kimberlin/Klinger joins preserve legitimate circumstantial comparison without eliminating screening, consistent with his Wade commitment.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Withdraw the provisional insistence on assuming coverage as an unnecessary-to-decide issue; accept the selective-prosecution exclusion and the separate insufficient-effect ground. Preserve the bar against general supervision circumventing governing limits without declaring all inherent power abolished. No material historical departure is retained.

### 4. Kennedy

**Historical premise:** Join the Rule 16 exclusion and the insufficient-comparison application. **Changed-premise test:** His Carlisle dissent recognizes a retained function where the specific rules do not withdraw it; it is not a newly changed position and does not establish every asserted inquiry as permissible. Actual Williams, Kimberlin and Klinger retain limited supervision and meaningful circumstantial proof without construing Rule 16 or curing the missing selection evidence. His Wade position supplies the continuing distinction between constitutional review and discovery entitlement; Cooper addresses a different ultimate-persuasion problem.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Adopt the Rule 16 exclusion rather than merely assuming coverage. Retain constitutional review, the preliminary/ultimate distinction and no automatic criteria-disclosure duty. His distinct Carlisle position does not compel Stevens's application here. No material historical departure is retained.

### 5. Souter

**Historical premise:** Accept the discovery threshold and record failure, and join the Rule 16 discussion **only as applied to this issue**. **Changed-premise test:** His own independent commitment assumed Rule 16 applicable, with materiality and the threshold still to be satisfied. His reservation declined an exclusion reaching every defense unrelated to guilt, and his Wade opinion subjects prosecutorial decisions to constitutional review with a substantial threshold; together they support Path S's reading that a properly presented constitutional defense capable of defeating the prosecution falls within the Rule, while nonconstitutional defenses remain undecided. His Wade opinion's review-with-screening structure is Path S's first stage, and his distinction between completing a grounded inference and searching for a missing contrast is the line between the bounded inquiry this showing grounds and the original order's broader components. Kimberlin and Klinger preserve circumstantial, treatment-specific proof, not discovery without the required comparison. Carlisle's surviving rule-consistent authority and Williams's particular grand-jury duty add nothing contrary.

**Reconciled commitment:** Vacate and remand on Path S. **Departure retained:** He departs from the historical Rule 16 exclusion and reversal on the supported ground just stated. Preserve the below-merits threshold, the reservation of nonconstitutional defenses and the open merits question about direct admissions. No ordered new evidentiary submission; the bounded inquiry completes the inference this showing grounds and does not restore the original order.

### 6. Thomas

**Historical premise:** Accept Rule 16 exclusion and the deficient comparison. **Changed-premise test:** His changed Williams join recognizes confined supervisory authority, but a known-evidence grand-jury duty and its separate sanction conditions do not expand “defense” in Rule 16. His actual Mezzanatto rule-text/background method operates at that case's limited waiver scope; no generalized new regularity presumption is imported. Wade, Kimberlin, Klinger and Carlisle respectively preserve screening, permissible proof, pertinent comparison and rule consistency. None supplies the missing textual or evidentiary change.

**Reconciled commitment:** Reverse and remand. **Correction:** Adopt Rule 16's selective-prosecution exclusion in place of the provisional assumption. Retain circumstantial-proof availability and reject an inference of entitlement from the selected numerator alone. Constitutional review is preserved independently of Rule 16. No material historical departure is retained.

### 7. Ginsburg

**Historical premise:** Expressly agree that subdivision (a)(1)(C) excludes selective-prosecution claims, while reserving other contexts, including affirmative defenses unrelated to the merits. **Changed-premise test:** Her actual Kimberlin opinion is a concrete new governing source concerning evidentiary form; it preserves tailored discovery limits and does not contradict a comparison requirement that itself does not demand direct proof of purpose. Her own independent commitment found a categorical “not a defense” construction unnecessary and stressed the danger of requiring inaccessible proof, and her reservation of affirmative defenses unrelated to the merits kept open the inclusion Path S adopts for a properly presented constitutional defense capable of defeating the prosecution, leaving nonconstitutional defenses reserved. Together these supply a supported ground for Path S's Rule 16 reading and for its acceptance of a justified showing that comparator evidence is reasonably unavailable, which on this record supports a bounded inquiry. Klinger supplies no different Rule 16 construction. Her Carlisle join and the changed Williams rule preserve the distinction between authority, restrictions and entitlement.

**Reconciled commitment:** Vacate and remand on Path S. **Departure retained:** She departs from the historical exclusion and reversal on the supported ground just stated, retaining her reservation of nonconstitutional defenses. Preserve her no-direct-evidence-only and no-completed-merits-proof qualifications. Do not represent her evidentiary-form concern as a rejection of the similarly-situated showing or as support for the original order. No directive to reopen evidence and no guaranteed criteria-only alternative.

### 8. Breyer

**Historical premise:** Rule 16 affirmatively provides a possible source, its three categories defeat the proposed case-in-chief restriction, and this request nevertheless fails materiality for the missing relevant comparison. His partial agreement does not include the Rule 16 exclusion. **Changed-premise test:** His actual Kimberlin/Klinger joins strengthen the explanation that legitimate circumstantial comparison remains possible; they do not cure this proffer. Carlisle bars evasion of an applicable restriction but does not make all nonauthorization prohibition. His own commitment preserves proportionate discovery once a grounded predicate exists. Under Path S's reasonable-unavailability rule the full submission supplies that predicate for a bounded inquiry into existing records, though not for the original order, and a less intrusive request does not itself supply it; that is a supported ground for vacatur and a limited-inquiry remand rather than reversal on his historical materiality application.

**Reconciled commitment:** Vacate and remand on Path S on Rule 16 materiality as framed by the staged framework. **Departure retained:** He departs from the historical reversal on the supported ground just stated. State affirmative Rule 16 availability, as his commitment does. Preserve his structural/drafting rationale, independent-source possibility and reservation of any work-product question beyond subdivision (a)(2): this case establishes none. Proportionate discovery can follow a sufficient predicate but cannot supply one; under the framework this record supplies one for the bounded inquiry only. No invented numbered partial-join sections or guaranteed easy access to state records.
