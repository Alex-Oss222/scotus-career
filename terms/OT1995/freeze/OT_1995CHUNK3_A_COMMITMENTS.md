## Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines; Korean Air Lines v. Zicherman

**Dockets and cutoff:** Nos.94-1361 and94-1477; January 16, 1996. Arguments November 7, 1995. All nine members participate; eight non-Stone positions are modeled here. Ordinary merits review reaches the supplied loss-of-society questions. Both relatives' society claims are distinct judgment components even though the legal answer is common.

**Provisional result:** All eight non-Stone Justices reject a treaty-created loss-of-society entitlement and reject judicial supplementation of DOHSA on the accepted United States-law premise. They support eliminating the sister's dependency remand insofar as it concerns society damages and denying the mother's request for such damages. The Court should reverse the challenged portion permitting the sister potentially to recover society damages and remand for proceedings consistent with the categorical exclusion of that damage category; the mother's society exclusion remains in place, with the family obtaining no enlargement. The decree must preserve the difference between those two components and must not reopen other award components merely because the lower opinion discusses them.

### Shared sufficient propositions

1. Article 17 identifies a qualifying accident, passenger injury and carrier liability; its general reference to damage does not itself prescribe every beneficiary or compensable loss. Article 24(2) expressly leaves the identification of persons entitled to sue and their respective rights without prejudice. The treaty therefore does not require the domestic court to create a uniform, independently complete compensatory code for these claims.
2. When the parties agree that United States domestic law supplies the measure of loss for a death within DOHSA's geographical field, §762's restriction to pecuniary loss governs society damages. A dependent relative cannot recover that nonpecuniary category merely by satisfying a beneficiary or dependency condition. General maritime law cannot supply an additional society remedy contrary to the applicable statute.
3. This determination neither chooses an international conflicts rule nor decides the treaty's exclusivity for every possible local cause of action. It does not adjudicate mental injury, survival damages, predeath suffering, support, inheritance, prejudgment interest, the identity of beneficiaries for other categories, or carrier liability. The supplied domestic-law agreement and geographical premise are sufficient here.

**Authority and principal contrary route:** DOHSA §§761–762; Warsaw Articles17 and24; Higginbotham's rejection of nonpecuniary supplementation of DOHSA; Offshore Logistics' high-seas statutory exclusivity; Miles' insistence on Congress's occupied maritime remedial choices; and current simulated Yamaha Part II, January9,1996, joined by all eight. Yamaha preserves territorial-water remedies precisely because the relevant federal statutory field is unoccupied there. It does not authorize disregarding the high-seas statute here. American Dredging concerns forum administration, not substantive additional damages. Saks and Floyd prescribe interpretation of the treaty's actual text and shared legal meaning, rather than enlargement by a generalized compensation purpose.

The contrary path takes a treaty cause of action to imply a uniform federal common-law damages measure, relying on Gaudet-type society damages and then either retaining or rejecting a dependency limit. Its strongest argument is that different measures for land and water crashes undermine uniform compensation. Its barrier is that the proposed uniform measure is absent from the treaty, while Article24 preserves respective rights and the governing domestic statute supplies an explicit answer. Neither describing the cause as treaty-based nor pointing to willful misconduct changes the domestic compensable-loss category: withdrawal of a Convention liability cap would not create a new category of domestic damages. Gaudet's territorial-waters remedy cannot supersede DOHSA's occupied field. No Justice in this freeze adopts that competing path.

## Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc.

**Docket and cutoff:** No.94-2003; January16,1996. Argued January8. Stevens does not participate. Seven non-Stone participants are modeled; the eighth participant is Stone, whose position is not supplied here. No aggregate Court result, equal division, authorship or public lineup is inferred at this stage.

**Components:** (1) the displayed Emulation Interface's copied command words and hierarchy; (2) the Key Reader's use of the same hierarchy and initial command letters to execute existing macros. No copied source/object code, screen-display appeal or long-prompt claim is included. The command hierarchy's actual operation, the conceded copying and the district court's creative-choice findings are accepted as the record described below; statutory classification remains an independent legal question.

### Competing lawful rules and remedies

**Exclusion route E:** The particular command hierarchy through which users issue executable commands and through which existing macros operate is a method of operation within §102(b). Its functional status is not lost because the developer could originally have chosen different command terms or arrangements. Section102(a) originality is necessary but not sufficient; §102(b) excludes a method in whatever expressive form it is embodied. Baker permits use of an operating system rather than giving an author control of its use; Feist leaves unprotectable subject matter free despite a copyright in the overall work. E would affirm as to both implementations and leave fair use unneeded. It would not declare all interfaces, code, displays or explanatory text uncopyrightable, and would not create an independent compatibility immunity.

**Expression route X:** Section102(b) denies ownership of functions, procedures and methods, but does not automatically exclude an original selection and arrangement merely because users employ it in operating software. Feist preserves original arrangement even where constituent items remain free; the distinction between unprotectable operation and an implementation's expressive choices must be examined rather than collapsed by calling every operative command a method. Baker protects use of the system but does not require that every particular presentation of that system be unprotected. X would reverse the First Circuit's categorical ruling for both implementations and remand for consideration of the properly remaining infringement and defenses. It does not decide that individual common commands are owned, that all469 choices are protectable, that literal copying necessarily defeats all defenses, or that an injunction must immediately be reinstated.

**Strongest objections and treatment:** X's strongest objection to E is that all software operates a machine and therefore the First Circuit's literal classification may swallow Congress's protection of literary computer programs; the presence of many possible hierarchies makes the factual-compilation analogy substantial. E's answer is that this case concerns the user-side commands themselves, not independently written implementing code or separable visual presentation: expressive choice does not negate a separately named statutory exclusion. E's strongest objection to X is that protecting the precise instructions makes lawful use of that method and users' own macros conditional on Lotus permission, even though Borland wrote its own code. X's answer is that compatibility establishes the copying's purpose and possible defense, not necessarily the absence of protectable expression from inception. These are statutory disagreements at the identified level of generality, not claims that Feist already decided this case.

**Alternative fair-use/privilege route:** The record does not permit treating compatibility as an automatic fair-use defense. Campbell's full four-factor inquiry, the defendant's burden and examination of legitimate market substitution remain necessary. The district court rejected defenses; the First Circuit did not decide them. No participant here adopts a new categorical privilege or directs an immediate defense judgment. A Justice on routeX may require that properly preserved defenses be heard on remand; a Justice on routeE can leave them open as unnecessary.

## Bank One Chicago, N.A. v. Midwest Bank & Trust Co.

**Docket and cutoff:** No.94-1175; January17,1996. Argued November28,1995. All nine participate. **Provisional result:** All eight non-Stone Justices reverse the Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal and remand for consideration of properly remaining questions. They do not order reinstatement of the district court's $43,912.06 award without completion of that appellate review.

### Shared sufficient rule and explanation

Section4010(d)'s authorization of any action under the section reaches a civil action enforcing interbank liability lawfully established by the Board under §4010(f). Subsection(a)'s exclusion of another depository institution limits the separate direct customer remedy; it does not erase (f) or silently replace the words of(d) with actions under(a). Section4008(c) supports payment-system regulation, but jurisdiction comes from Congress's(d)/(f) arrangement rather than the agency's assertion of power.

This construction keeps the regimes distinct. It does not award subsection(a)'s customer additional damages or fees in an(f) action. Subsection(f) caps ordinary liability at the check amount, permits additional proximately caused damages for bad faith, and leaves the legally applicable defenses and proof requirements in force. The ordinary-care breach, causation, comparative responsibility if applicable under the governing regulation, defenses and damages still require adjudication in their proper posture. The Court decides no regulatory violation merely because the action belongs in court.

Touche Ross and Transamerica caution against manufacturing a private remedy from a broad statutory purpose or an isolated jurisdictional clause. The present ground uses an express congressional delegation to create interbank liability inside the same section as its express judicial-action provision. It therefore does not rest on beneficial purpose, convenience, an agency's litigating position or absence of an alternative remedy alone. The Board's statement that no administrative mechanism exists defeats the lower court's assumed practical alternative but is corroboration, not a delegation of ArticleIII jurisdiction. No exhaustion condition for a nonexistent adjudication is invented.

**Tested contrary route:** The strongest argument to affirm reads the explicit exclusion in(a) as Congress's deliberate reservation of bank-to-bank disputes to administration or state law. That preserves the exclusion but fails to account for(f)'s distinct liability power and(d)'s whole-section reference. The supported reversal respects the exclusion in its own operative field while giving independent effect to all three provisions. NationsBank (January18,1995) and Town & Country (November28,1995) require the same sequence: determine statutory boundaries independently, then recognize lawful agency implementation within them. The source of district-court authority must remain the statute.
