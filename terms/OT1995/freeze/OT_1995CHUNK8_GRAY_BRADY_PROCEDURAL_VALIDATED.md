## Comparison with the positive party passages

**Federal pleading and partial reopening.** Petitioner's merits pages 15–19 positively quote federal Ground A as alleging both late notice impairing preparation and police information suggesting another perpetrator that was not released. Page 16 describes the Commonwealth's continued withholding, its default objection, and Gray's answer that the requested information was germane to the general constitutional challenge to the Sorrell evidence. Gray also invokes his pretrial request for Brady material. These passages preserve his contention that the suppression evidence belongs within the existing constitutional challenge; they are not merely a neutral concession that every distinction drawn by the Commonwealth was proper.

Page 19 reports the April 1, 1993 dismissal and quotes the amendment's treatment of the due-process challenge to admission of the Sorrell evidence during the penalty phase as consistently raised in state court and not procedurally defaulted. That affirmative quotation supports reopening of the identified due-process claim. Its stated object is compatible with the published report's separate reservation of the remaining dismissals. A pretrial request for favorable evidence, federal pleading of suppression, and reopening of an admission/preparation claim are distinct procedural facts; none automatically supplies the prior presentation or revival of every independent claim.

**Integrated deception and preparation theory.** The complete reply positively presents a sustained claim that affirmative misrepresentation deprived counsel of a meaningful opportunity to meet the Sorrell evidence. Pages 4–6 and notes 8–9 argue continuity from the trial objection through later proceedings, reproduce earlier presentation passages, and defend refinement of legal arguments while maintaining the substance of the claim. Page 6 describes the proposed rule as a prohibition on deception that prevents development of the opposing party's case on an outcome-determinative issue. Pages 7–8 and notes 11–13 describe suppression as affecting investigation, defense strategy, and the evidentiary picture, invoking Bagley and Kyles. These are Gray's positive arguments for the integrated claim, not findings that the independent dismissal was legally correct or that every present theory was preserved.

Their substantive relationship to the reopened claim remains a legal question for independent modeling. The published reservation of other dismissals does not itself define away a properly presented due-process claim merely because suppression evidence also bears on it. Conversely, evidentiary overlap and Gray's continuity argument do not themselves erase the separately reported dismissal.

**Trial and posttrial knowledge remain disputed.** Reply pages 17–18 and note 27 directly dispute the assertion that trial counsel already knew at the 1985 trial that Timothy Sorrell had been a suspect. Gray describes Moore as uncertain whether he learned that before or after trial and Eason as not recalling such knowledge. He also disputes a supposed obligation to repeat an already raised objection or investigate further before the February 1986 posttrial deadline, invoking the promised Brady disclosure and lack of an investigator. Those arguments must remain in the packet. They concern trial/posttrial knowledge and preparation; the quoted initial order addresses availability when the later state habeas petition was litigated. No unqualified statement that all relevant knowledge was admitted accurately preserves both sources.

**Two distinct respondent objections.** Respondent's pages 48–49 expressly rely on the initial independent Brady ruling and invoke Virginia Code §8.01-654(B)(2). Note 18 argues publicity and investigative opportunity before the February 1986 deadline, invokes Slayton, and asserts that B(2) reaches reasonably available facts, citing Waye. That construction and application remain respondent's arguments; they do not replace the statutory text or prove the legally required knowledge predicate. The existing presentation validation's distinctions between B(2), the separate capital filing deadline, and factual development remain intact.

Respondent separately argues that Gray did not appeal the independent Brady dismissal, pointing to the Fourth Circuit opening brief's page 30 note 10 and issues statement at pages 2–3. Note 19 distinguishes the Smallwood Brady issue. This **appellate-abandonment contention remains independently qualified**: neither that original opening brief nor the full footnote has been read in this validation, and the new judicial recitations do not themselves establish that additional bar. Survival of the District Court dismissal and proper preservation of appellate review of it are different propositions.
