## Controlling premises and the competing rules

The complete unnumbered Mont. Code Ann. §45-2-203 paragraph in the validated packet governs. Its exception is proof that the defendant did not know the ingested substance was intoxicating; it is not an exception for every involuntary intoxication. The statute states both criminal responsibility despite intoxication and the prohibition on considering intoxication when determining an offense's mental-state element. The actual charge required purposeful or knowing homicide. The jury heard intoxication evidence but could not use it for that mental-state inquiry. Admission for memory or physical capacity does not itself answer the constitutional objection.

**Substantive-culpability path:** Read the provisions together as Montana's express qualification of the culpability the State must prove: purpose or knowledge is assessed without treating knowingly induced intoxication as a reason to negate the required state of mind. The State must still prove the killing and the culpability so defined beyond reasonable doubt; intoxication is not affirmative proof of guilt. Patterson's legislative latitude, the historically restricted role of voluntary intoxication and the express statutory direction support this rule. Gaudin assigns actual elements to the jury but does not forbid the State to define this qualification. The strongest objection is that the charge retained subjective definitions and the legislature described the forbidden inquiry as determining an existing element. A mere label cannot answer that objection. The selected justification must be that the express enactment itself qualifies substantive culpability, not that all proof restrictions are definitions.

**Retained-element path:** Montana elected to require actual purpose or knowledge and instructed the jury accordingly, then disabled the defense from using relevant evidence to rebut that requirement. Martin expressly distinguished that kind of proof restriction from a permissible burden on a separate affirmative defense. Gaudin and Winship protect the retained element; Patterson's definition authority does not allow a court to silently replace it. The strongest objection is that this reading fails to give substantive effect to the companion intoxication provision and constitutionalizes an intoxication doctrine States need not adopt. The response is limited: Montana may define offenses and remove an affirmative intoxication defense, but this actual charge retains the mental facts while barring their factual contest. The claim does not require a constitutional subjective mental element in every crime.

**History and fairness:** Actual Medina does not impose an exclusive historical method; O'Connor/Souter did not join such a controlling proposition. Actual Cooper preserves the preponderance competency allocation while invalidating Oklahoma's higher burden. Its unanimous joinder is not a vote about intoxication. Hopt construes Utah's express intent statute and does not constitutionalize it. Rock and Chambers establish bounded limits on arbitrary restrictions; neither establishes an absolute right to use all evidence for every purpose. Martin's pertinent majority was joined by Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia. See [authority support](../research/chunk8-model-b/AUTHORITY_SUPPORT.md) for verified writings and the exact joins.

The June 10 decisions supply no new criminal-proof rule. ## Remedy and common reservations

The affirming positions sustain the actual state new-trial disposition for the identified instructional restriction. They neither find a precise level of impairment nor announce that every such error is structural under Sullivan; the supplied record supports no new harmlessness finding. The reversing positions set aside the federal constitutional ground and remand for treatment of the unresolved lay-opinion, sufficiency and dangerous-offender assignments. They do not restore all convictions through first-instance findings on those assignments.

All eight retain the unknown-intoxicant exception in its own terms. None equates blackout with lack of purpose, physical capacity with purpose, or the admission of evidence for one purpose with permission to use it for every other purpose. No unsupported waiver, new theory, transcript concession or default is supplied.
