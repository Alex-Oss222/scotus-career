## New supported premise and its limits

The complete [fresh procedural validation](OT_1995CHUNK8_GRAY_BRADY_PROCEDURAL_VALIDATED.md) and [sanitized procedural extract](OT_1995CHUNK8_GRAY_BRADY_PROCEDURAL_EXTRACT.md) now positively establish, through published recitations of the orders, that the District Court initially dismissed the independent Sorrell Brady subclaim as procedurally barred. The court determined that this claim had not been presented to the Virginia Supreme Court on direct review or state habeas and that its factual basis was available when the state habeas petition was litigated. The subsequent amendment reopened an identified due-process challenge to admission of the Sorrell evidence and expressly left the remaining dismissals unchanged. That includes the independent Brady dismissal.

The report also characterizes Gray as not contesting the initial nonpresentation and state-habeas factual-availability determinations in this Court. That is an attributed description of the presentation, not a newly invented stipulation. The party passages positively maintain an integrated deception/preparation theory, continued withholding, an earlier Brady request, and disputed trial/posttrial knowledge. They do not directly contradict the reported reservation of the other dismissals. This context accepts that limited procedural history under Engine §14 without claiming to have read the complete original orders.

The new evidence resolves the former uncertainty about **survival of the independent dismissal**. It does not establish every reason for its legal correctness, the exact contents of every original appellate filing, or the truth of the suppression allegation. In particular:

- Factual availability when the state habeas petition was litigated is not actual knowledge of every favorable item at the 1985 trial. The reply's contrary trial-knowledge contentions remain attributed and live within their actual scope.
- Respondent's asserted reasonable-availability construction of Virginia Code §8.01-654(B)(2) remains an argument. Neither this addendum nor the reported order converts the statute's knowledge language into a judicial finding of actual knowledge or decides adequacy and independence.
- The assertion that Gray failed to appeal the independent dismissal remains a separate, qualified appellate-abandonment contention. The original opening brief's full footnote and issues statement have not been supplied through this addition. No abandonment finding follows from that absence.
- The amended order's positive recognition of a consistently presented due-process claim remains separate. It neither revives the independent Brady ground nor establishes that every later deception formulation falls within the reopened claim.

## Affected component decision

**All eight Associates now expressly leave the entered independent Brady dismissal undisturbed in this review and exclude that ground from the affirmative remand.** Neither the reopened due-process judgment nor the proposed correction of its appellate treatment supplies a basis to restore the independent Brady claim. A general direction to redetermine the independent claim's availability is no longer part of the commitment. This is a limitation on the scope of reversal and remand, not a newly adopted holding that respondent has proved every element of a Virginia procedural bar or an independent appellate abandonment.

The strongest contrary path would treat Gray's pleaded suppression allegations and integrated theory as a basis to reopen the Brady dismissal or to remand its validity generally. Federal Ground A did contain suppression allegations, and Kyles recognizes lawful preparation and impeachment uses of favorable evidence. But the existence and possible strength of a pleaded claim do not undo an entered dismissal. The later order expressly preserved that dismissal while identifying the due-process claim it reopened. The positive arguments supporting the integrated claim provide no sufficient basis here to disregard that distinction or to award independent Brady relief. Kyles supplies a merits rule for an available claim; it does not supply reopening, appellate presentation or relief from a separate procedural judgment.

The opposite overbroad path would extend the independent Brady dismissal to every use of related evidence or every Sorrell constitutional theory. That also fails. The amended order positively reopened a due-process claim, and the meaningful-opportunity/deception arguments have their own alleged conduct, presentation and injury. Evidence relevant to that claim is not automatically excluded because it also could support the dismissed ground. Its use must establish an element or effect of the properly available due-process claim, rather than obtain the dismissed Brady relief under a new label. The receiving court must compare the actual claim and operative facts under Picard, Anderson and Vasquez. Neither an identical word nor a common body of evidence decides that comparison.

Thus no new independent Brady merits determination, materiality finding, suppression finding, cause/prejudice ruling or hearing order is supported. The original analysis of actual Kyles remains an accurate account of law but is not an instruction to decide the dismissed independent claim on remand. References to Bagley or Kyles in the integrated argument do not themselves convert that argument into a revived Brady ground or prove its constitutional validity.
