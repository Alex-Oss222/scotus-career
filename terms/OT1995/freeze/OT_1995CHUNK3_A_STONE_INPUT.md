## OT1995-021 — Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.; Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. v. Zicherman

**Event:** 1996-01-16 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 217

**Docket(s):** Nos. 94-1361 and 94-1477

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the entering law and any material record or participation change at the event date; do not treat this position as a rendered decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in part and reverse in part: affirm rejection of the mother’s loss-of-society claim; reverse the ruling permitting the sister’s society claim to depend on further dependency findings; remand for a conforming damages judgment. This is Stone's approved vote.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Eliminate loss-of-society recovery for both claimants. Preserve other components not disturbed by the questions presented. Do not vacate liability or order a new trial of unrelated damages.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Treaty and statutory measure of recovery

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the category of damages within both petitions. Do not reopen liability or decide damages not presented.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the treaty’s domestic-law reference and DOHSA’s enacted pecuniary limit. Keep that statute distinct from territorial-water remedies.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** For this high-seas death governed by United States law, the Warsaw Convention does not displace DOHSA’s limitation to pecuniary loss. Neither claimant may recover loss-of-society damages, irrespective of dependency.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A treaty reference to compensable damage does not enact a worldwide damages code or authorize federal judges to replace Congress’s selected high-seas measure. Once DOHSA supplies that measure, a dependency inquiry cannot revive a barred category. The mother’s exclusion is therefore correct, while the remand to consider the sister’s dependency serves no legally relevant purpose. [P1, 223–231]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling on every Warsaw damages category, another country’s law, or a territorial-water death. Other preserved pecuniary components remain for ordinary application.

## OT1995-022 — Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc.

**Event:** 1996-01-16 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Merits submission

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 233

**Docket(s):** No. 94-2003

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Seek a fair-use disposition protecting the command compatibility necessary for users' existing macros; vacate and remand for the unresolved, fact-sensitive §107 defenses rather than decide the entire command hierarchy through an unlimited interfaces rule.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Apply simulated Campbell's integrated four-factor analysis. Permitting users to operate their accumulated macros with competing software has a concrete functional purpose beyond copying expressive material for its own sake. The utilitarian character of commands reduces the force of an expressive-copying objection. The amount taken must be connected to actual interoperability, not assumed necessary merely because the defendant invokes compatibility. Analyze legally cognizable substitution and licensing markets; loss from lawful competition is not itself protected market harm, but a genuine expressive market cannot be ignored. A commercial purpose is relevant, not dispositive, and the assessment of users' investment does not create a general property right in every learned interface.

**Mechanism, remedy and limits:** The First Circuit did not resolve every fair-use question. Direct it to address the preserved defense and, if factual findings are necessary, remand for that limited work; allow an alternative statutory ground to be considered within the grant without requiring an advisory copyrightability holding. Reserve protection of source code, screen expression and unnecessary copying. Stone does not decree that all compatibility copying is fair or that all software structure is unprotectable.

**Participation and authority:** Stevens does not participate. Boudin’s alternative analysis supplies persuasive reasoning rather than a Supreme Court holding.

## OT1995-024 — Bank One Chicago, N.A. v. Midwest Bank & Trust Co.

**Event:** 1996-01-17 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 264

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1175

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Permit Bank One to pursue the Act’s express civil-liability remedy, subject to the statute’s elements, defenses, causation requirements, and measure of recovery.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Determine first whether the claimant and violation fall within the express liability text; no implied-cause-of-action inquiry is necessary.
- **Supporting Authorities:** 12 U.S.C. §§4001, 4008, and 4010; Regulation CC; *Touche Ross & Co. v. Redington*, 442 U.S. 560; *Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis*, 444 U.S. 11.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** The Expedited Funds Availability Act’s express civil-liability provision reaches a depository institution’s regulatory violation with respect to another bank when the claimant qualifies as a statutory person and proves the loss and other requirements Congress prescribed.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Congress used an express liability provision reaching a depository institution that fails to comply with duties imposed by the Act or its valid regulations with respect to another person. The statutory definitions do not confine that protected person to a consumer depositor, and the notice-of-nonpayment rules regulate interbank conduct central to the Act’s operation. Giving the enacted words their ordinary reach therefore requires adjudication under the express remedy rather than dismissal or creation of an implied one.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding creates no remedy beyond §4010, does not eliminate statutory or regulatory defenses, and does not decide Bank One’s ultimate proof of causation or damages.
