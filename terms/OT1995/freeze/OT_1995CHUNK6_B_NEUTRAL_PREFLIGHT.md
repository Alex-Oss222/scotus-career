### Governing law and components

Source text §1983 in AppB17a; color-of-state-law was uncontested at AppD37a. The constitutional interest must be identified independently of state promises or ethics law. Trial transcript discusses Utah §67-16-4's prohibition on official disclosure of confidential information for private benefit, but plaintiff expressly disclaimed making its violation the §1983 right; it is context, not an automatic federal entitlement or independently granted state claim (AppD30a–36a).

### Supported decisional alternatives and counterarguments

1. **Recognize and apply a confined confidentiality protection, affirm within reviewed question.** Distinguish legitimate investigation/archival retention from purposeful disclosure of personal marital information for a private author's publication; apply objectively intimate subject matter and supported limited-purpose expectation. The contrary concern is converting subjective preferences, state assurances or common-law privacy into substantive due process and ignoring open archives. Meet it expressly through constitutional source, objective subject and record limits, rather than inventing secret files. No decision of every governmental data disclosure is necessary.
2. **Reject asserted constitutional coverage on the preserved legal claim, or require materially narrower constitutional criteria and remand if the existing instruction/application cannot satisfy them.** Whalen's express reservation, Paul's limit and constitutional-source caution support testing whether routine personal information plus a promise suffices. Counterargument: the jury found intimate home/marriage information under an objective instruction, and Hodge/Albright did not foreclose such protection. Reversal cannot be justified by labeling all official information an arrest record or treating the deceased author's interest as the only interest.

Counterargument: court must first identify a constitutional right and cannot assume that lack of justification federalizes every disclosure. The full lower compelling-interest/least-intrusive formulation need not be endorsed to sustain this application.

## 44 Liquormart — fresh clean neutral validation, ready for chronological modeling

**Scope and posture.** Assigned May13,1996 merits event, No.94-1140. Review concerns Rhode Island's suppression of truthful retail liquor-price advertising to promote temperance. The operative lower judgment is the First Circuit's October24,1994 judgment, 39F.3d5, reversing the district court and directing judgment for defendants. The July8 opinion/judgment was withdrawn/vacated on rehearing; it supplies no independent operative holding. Aldrich wrote for Cyr, Aldrich and Stahl. The lower rehearing record expressly states that Breyer did not participate in the July29 rehearing/en-banc action. No own Supreme Court participation consequence is inferred; the authorized nine-person roster governs absent a supported case-specific issue.

### Exact enactments, relief and preservation

Rhode Island §3-8-7 bars a manufacturer, wholesaler, out-of-state shipper, or title/chapter license holder from advertising, in any manner, prices of malt beverages, cordials, wine or distilled liquor offered for sale in Rhode Island. Its exception covers price signs or tags **attached to or placed on merchandise for sale within licensed premises**, under departmental rules; it is not an unlimited permission for any indoor advertisement. Section3-8-8 authorizes enforcement rules and license suspension. Regulation32 bars any placard or sign visible from the exterior of ClassA retail premises that refers to an alcoholic-beverage price.

Section3-8-8.1 separately prohibits a Rhode Island-based newspaper, periodical, broadcaster or advertising business from accepting, publishing or broadcasting in Rhode Island an advertisement stating or referring to an alcoholic-beverage price. It supplies a misdemeanor fine of $50 for the first offense and no more than $100 for each additional offense, authorizes injunction proceedings on a retail licensee's or association's complaint, and excepts trade journals recognized and authorized by the administrator. Section3-1-5 declares promotion of temperance and reasonable control of the traffic in alcoholic beverages. The First Amendment speech/press clauses and Twenty-first Amendment §2 are the constitutional texts; §2 addresses transportation/importation for delivery or use in violation of state law, and contains no express commercial-speech exception.

The district court, after trial, declared §§3-8-7,3-8-8.1 and Regulation32 unconstitutional on advancement and fit grounds, 829F.Supp.543 (Aug10,1993). The requested reversal can restore that constitutional disposition, or a bounded remand can implement a narrower holding. It does not award damages, refund a fine, order taxes/minimum prices, decide an antitrust case, or invalidate every general alcohol-advertising regulation. The First Circuit found standing for both plaintiffs in every particular. Peoples originally advanced a Commerce Clause claim but **abandoned it on appeal**; the court expressly treated it as waived. Its distinct First Amendment claim concerning advertisements for lawful Massachusetts sales remains a supported route. A First Amendment ruling cannot silently resurrect the abandoned commerce claim.

### Trial record and evidentiary limits

44 Liquormart is a licensed Johnston, Rhode Island retailer; Peoples operates in New Bedford and Fairhaven, Massachusetts. The challenged 1991 advertisement displayed branded liquor bottles without numerical liquor prices, priced nonalcoholic goods, and placed “WOW” bursts near liquor bottles. Following a December17 administrative hearing, the administrator imposed a $400 fine and ordered the advertisement stopped; 44 paid and did not take the available administrative appeal. The merits litigation is not a direct administrative appeal or an adjudicated refund demand. Rhode Island media refused Peoples' proposed advertisements of lawful Massachusetts liquor prices because of §3-8-8.1.

The stipulation establishes vigorous enforcement, other warnings/fines, interstate media reach, and permission for alcohol advertisements apart from the prohibited price references. Each plaintiff **believed** lawful advertising would yield more than $100,000 annual benefit; that is not a finding that aggregate consumption would increase. The exact stipulation says the proposed speech concerns lawful activity and **presumably** would not be false or misleading. Both courts treated Central Hudson's threshold as satisfied. Rhode Island's substantial interest in regulating alcohol sales was stipulated, and temperance's substantiality was conceded. Neither concession proves a causal connection or the adequacy of the particular restriction.

The district court heard live testimony and two videotaped depositions. It credited Smart and Pittman and found that the price-advertising ban had **no significant impact on Rhode Island consumption**. It expressly declined to make findings about a general or partial general-advertising ban. Saffer supported a search-cost/price mechanism: suppressed comparison increases price dispersion, search expense and average prices; higher prices reduce purchases. He offered general-advertising studies, including seventeen countries over fourteen years, and invoked Ornstein/Hanssens concerning price-advertising restrictions. Smart acknowledged that real prices influence consumption, the search-cost inference was a reasonable expectation, and cumulative advertising effects can be hard to measure; he also characterized the search-cost inference as speculation without the underlying data. Pittman accepted a theoretical price decrease if advertising became lawful and knew of no empirical refutation of the asserted temperance effect. These concessions must be considered alongside, not substituted for, the experts' contrary conclusions and the trial findings.

The district court identified missing controls and differences between general-advertising research and the precise price-information restraint. Saffer had not estimated Rhode Island's consumption trend, the expected price reduction, or an optimal state price. He conceded that increased market share/profits can occur without increased total consumption, and that minimum prices or increased sales taxes could serve the consumption objective. The First Circuit did not merely find different witnesses more credible: it deemed reasonable economic inference sufficient, held that the district court could not reject that inference on the record described, and invoked the Twenty-first Amendment's asserted added presumption. The review therefore must separate historical/credibility findings, constitutional assessment of evidentiary sufficiency, legislative prediction and reasonable fit. No categorical empirical-study requirement, absolute deference to expert votes, or stipulation that the disputed causal chain is true follows.

Trial counsel referred to irresponsible purchases by poor consumers and students; respondents also invoke the statutory temperance interest in reduced overall consumption. Petitioners dispute expansion of the asserted interest. The record supports analyzing both the preservation objection and the merits under the genuinely asserted interest; it does not establish an adjudicated forfeiture of every broader temperance formulation. The Association's original intervention rationale included protecting small licensees against large competitors' advertising resources. That supports scrutiny of the proposed justification but is not a finding that the State's declared purpose was a pretext. The First Circuit expressly declined to find that purpose rebutted.

The Moreland report was excluded below; Waterson and the June1995 Hacker/Stuart material were offered later and their record status disputed. They are submissions, not admitted trial facts. ### Entering law and genuinely alternative paths

Canonical commercial-speech law remains Central Hudson/Fox: lawful, nonmisleading speech receives protection; government must demonstrate real harms, material advancement and reasonable proportional fit. No least-restrictive-means, all-sources-at-once, universal controlled-study or categorical vice-speech rule exists. Simulated **Rubin** (Apr19,1995) applies both advancement and fit to truthful alcohol information, allows supported prediction, and rejects unsupported state-assistance as a substitute for scrutiny. **Florida Bar** (Jun21,1995) similarly evaluates the actual restraint and record without making every temporal restriction invalid. **Edge** has a five-Justice record-specific meaningful-reduction rationale, not a majority categorical-level exemption. **Discovery Network**, **Edenfield**, **Ibanez** and **Lanphere** require the selected burden to answer the actual asserted harm; lawful direct controls and neutral privacy rules retain their operation.

1. **Reverse under ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny.** The State can fail material advancement because generalized price theory and studies of other restrictions do not establish the necessary effect of this information ban on this record. It can independently fail fit because it suppresses all truthful price references while permitting other promotion, and supported direct price/tax measures address the desired price level without suppressing comparison. The strongest response is that the mechanism has testimony, economic support and concessions, not mere unsupported conjecture; the scarcity of definitive measurement does not itself defeat supported prediction. A judgment resting on fit can assume some advancement without converting disputed effectiveness into a new factual finding.

2. **Affirm on a supported-prediction and proportionate-fit application.** A Justice can reason that the economic mechanism materially operates, that price advertising uniquely intensifies price competition, and that the price-only rule leaves substantial other advertising available. Fox allows a reasonable regulatory choice rather than exhaustion of all alternatives. This route must confront the district finding, distinguish Rubin's defects, explain why merchandise/trade-journal exceptions do not undermine the mechanism, and assess the real burden on lawful Massachusetts information. Neither a bare rational relation nor an automatic power-to-ban-sales/power-to-ban-speech syllogism supplies current majority law.

3. **Resolve the Twenty-first Amendment issue narrowly.** One route distinguishes LaRue/Bellanca's licensed-premises conduct from factual off-premises speech and subjects the prohibition to the ordinary burden. A competing route gives alcohol-control authority weight in evaluating supported legislative predictions while still performing commercial-speech review. Respondents say they prevail even without an added presumption. A decision should state whether it rejects the added presumption generally, limits it here, or finds it insufficient even assuming applicability; those propositions differ. Predivergence **Queensgate**, 459U.S.807(1982), is a summary merits dismissal, not legally irrelevant merely because unexplained. Its precise-issue force under Mandel, factual comparability, and relationship to subsequent governing doctrine require analysis. The First Circuit relied on its own scrutiny “with Queensgate or without”; avoiding the summary-disposition question remains possible if the independently sufficient ground makes it unnecessary.

4. **Narrower grounds or remand.** A First Amendment rule protecting truthful information about lawful out-of-state sales under Bigelow can address Peoples' distinct claim without reviving Commerce Clause review; it requires accounting for Edge's actual holding. A remand is supported if the appellate court used an erroneous burden and genuinely unresolved matters are material, but is not required merely because prediction is contested or original research materials were not recovered. If resolving both plaintiffs under common advancement/fit grounds, there is no need for a broader interstate-information holding. An anti-paternalism rule for accurate lawful-product information is a supported doctrinal alternative, particularly from Stevens's Rubin concurrence, but presently a separate rationale, not a Court holding or automatic strict scrutiny for all commercial speech.

Respondents' concrete answers to direct-price/tax alternatives are interstate shopping, burdens on the local market, and the antitrust problems identified in Midcal/324Liquor. Those are serious fit arguments, not established findings that all state minimum prices are unlawful or that a tax cannot work. Petitioners disavow any duty to adopt the least restrictive imaginable measure. The court can evaluate genuine alternatives without selecting or ordering a replacement regime.
