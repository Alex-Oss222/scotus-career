## Separate legal inquiries

Fair presentation concerns whether the state courts received the substance of the federal claim. Supplying relevant facts alone, or a somewhat similar legal complaint, need not suffice; conversely, identical verbal formulations or a precise constitutional citation are not invariably required. Compare the actual notice, alleged misleading assurance, and suppression presentations rather than deciding by their labels. [Picard v. Connor, 404 U.S. 270, 275–278](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/404/270); [Anderson v. Harless, 459 U.S. 4, 6–7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/459/4).

Additional evidence does not necessarily create a new claim. Vasquez's exhaustion holding permits supplementation that does not fundamentally alter the claim already considered; it leaves deliberate withholding of essential facts unresolved. That comparison requires identifying what the state presentation contained and what the federal evidence added. [Vasquez v. Hillery, 474 U.S. 254, 257–260](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/474/254).

Under the **actual Keeney holding** in the entering-law slice, Townsend's fifth hearing circumstance retains its deliberate-bypass qualification for a properly presented claim: ordinary counsel negligence in developing material facts does not itself bar the necessary federal hearing. Coleman remains operative for a demonstrated independent and adequate state **claim default**. Thus Hall's cause-and-prejudice reasoning concerning inadequate factual development is attributed lower-court reasoning, not governing law. Neither failure to produce all evidence nor an operator's inability to recover a filing supplies deliberate bypass. A supported independent default, including any asserted failure to preserve a distinct hearing issue, would need its own legal and factual demonstration. Conversely, the absence of such a default does not establish entitlement to a hearing or relief without the applicable material-fact and hearing predicates.

## Virginia provisions: verified text and bounded effect

The preserved [official Chapter 503 HTML](../sources/CHUNK8_C/VA_1995_CHAP0503.html) verifies the extraction's two quotations. The full chapter text was read; neither provision is deleted text. Section 8.01-654(B)(2) states:

> Such petition shall contain all allegations the facts of which are known to petitioner at the time of filing and such petition shall enumerate all previous applications and their disposition. No writ shall be granted on the basis of any allegation the facts of which petitioner had knowledge at the time of filing any previous petition.

The first sentence imposes pleading and enumeration requirements. The second conditions its prohibition on knowledge of the allegation's facts at the filing of a previous petition. These are not a universal filing deadline or an evidentiary-hearing rule. Application requires identifying the allegation, previous petition and relevant factual knowledge; the text does not say that mere operator access to evidence, later discovery, or what the petitioner should have known establishes actual knowledge. An allegation outside the prohibition does not thereby establish entitlement to a writ. Chapter 503 reproduces this subsection as existing text; if an earlier-version dispute becomes material, the 1995 reenactment alone should not substitute for the applicable earlier text or authoritative construction.

New §8.01-654.1 states:

> No petition for a writ of habeas corpus filed by a prisoner held under a sentence of death shall be considered unless it is filed within sixty days after the earliest of: (i) denial by the United States Supreme Court of a petition for a writ of certiorari to the judgment of the Supreme Court of Virginia on direct appeal, (ii) a decision by the United States Supreme Court affirming imposition of the sentence of death when such decision is in a case resulting from a granted writ of certiorari to the judgment of the Supreme Court of Virginia on direct appeal, or (iii) the period for filing a timely petition for certiorari has expired without such a petition being filed.

This is a distinct capital-habeas consideration deadline, using the **earliest** of the three specified direct-review events. It contains no express known-facts qualification borrowed from B(2). Chapter 503's approval was March 23, 1995; the [official SB969 history](../sources/CHUNK8_C/VA_1995_SB969_history.html) specifies **July 1, 1995** as its effective date. The chapter supplies no separate express transition clause resolving prisoners whose direct review ended before then. The present record therefore does **not** prove that new §654.1 governed or rendered untimely Gray's old 1990 state petition. Its possible operation on a later attempted state petition is another question requiring temporal and claim-specific analysis, not an inference from the elapsed time since direct review. Neither statutory text alone establishes an adequate and independent bar in this case. The new capital-jurisdiction and hearing arrangement in §654(C) is separate and cannot be imported through a citation to B(2).
