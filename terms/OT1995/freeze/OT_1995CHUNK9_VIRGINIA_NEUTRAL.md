**J. E. B.**

**Current rule:** The State may not strike a juror because of sex or a stereotype that sex predicts competence or impartiality. Sex classifications must substantially serve an important governmental objective; a generalized prediction of sympathy does not suffice. The objector first must make the required prima facie showing, after which the striking party must produce a genuine sex-neutral explanation, without having to establish cause. The court decides purposeful discrimination with the ultimate persuasion burden on the objector. A correlated individual characteristic may be a genuine reason but cannot serve as pretext or proxy. Established discriminatory selection requires a new trial before a lawfully selected jury, not a proportionally composed jury.

**Kelley**

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Missouri v. Jenkins**

**Holding:** A desegregation court may fund instructional and noninstructional salary support reasonably necessary to deliver educational measures repairing established segregation-caused injury, without treating low salaries as a separate constitutional wrong. These challenged orders are sustained on their independently sufficient intradistrict educational-repair and personnel-necessity findings; incidental voluntary attraction does not negate that ground, and the broader magnet foundation is not reopened.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this holding and affirm both reviewed salary judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The independently sufficient educational-repair findings sustain instructional and critical support-staff costs. November salary findings were not challenged as clearly erroneous; distinct December labor-market and support-staff challenges received that review. No automatic future increase, general educational-improvement guarantee, unproved interdistrict remedy or reopening of the broader magnet foundation follows.

**Holding:** Partial withdrawal may occur only after the defendant seeking release proves full and satisfactory compliance and practicable elimination of the relevant vestiges, whole-decree good faith shown by conduct, and the necessary causal and remedial connections; retained control necessary or practicable for compliance elsewhere and nonobstruction of remaining relief must be assessed. Missouri’s proof of installing programs does not make that complete showing, so continued funding and denial of partial release are affirmed; low scores alone establish neither continuing constitutional injury nor completed compliance, and national parity is not an indispensable release condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the complete existing Freeman rule and its present-record application.

The State and district’s respective responsibilities must be assessed separately; one defendant’s conduct does not establish the other’s compliance. ## United States v. Virginia, Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107


### Participation and path below

Justice Thomas took no part in consideration or decision. This handoff supplies no reason beyond the published nonparticipation and no other historical Supreme Court voting information. Source: [public participation notation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/94-1941.ZS.html).

The United States sued on March 1, 1990 under 42 U.S.C. §2000c-6 on behalf of a female high-school student seeking VMI admission. VMI was a public institution, under state control, with a men-only admission policy and approximately 1,300 cadets. It had received more than 300 inquiries from women in the two years before suit. The district court initially sustained the policy after a six-day trial. In 1992, the Fourth Circuit vacated and required a constitutionally adequate plan, timetable and supervision, leaving Virginia an initial choice among admitting women with program adjustments, a parallel institution/program, abandonment of state support or another lawful option. It did not itself order immediate coeducation or find every parallel program adequate. Source: [976 F.2d890, 892–900](https://static.case.law/f2d/976/html/0890-01.html).

On remand Virginia proposed VWIL at the private Mary Baldwin College. The district court approved the plan, directed operation for the fall 1995 academic year, retained jurisdiction and required six-month reports. The Fourth Circuit affirmed and remanded for additional monitoring of qualified leadership, promotion to candidates, near-term funding and continuing professional review. The court used a “substantive comparability” approach. That is the judgment and standard below, open to review, not a rule supplied by this handoff. Source: [44 F.3d1229, 1233–1235, 1240–1242](https://static.case.law/f3d/44/html/1229-01.html).

### Programs, record evidence and material distinctions

VMI combined a military environment, the first-year rat line, class and mentoring systems, an honor code, barracks life, close behavioral regulation, physical rigor and four years of ROTC. The district court treated the methods as integrated and predicted changes to physical training, privacy and adversative interaction with coeducation. Those are the trial court's findings and predictions, accepted on the first appeal; their constitutional significance is contested. The parties agreed that some women could meet the physical standards then imposed on men. Source: 976 F.2d at 893–898.

VWIL was designed as a smaller program within Mary Baldwin's undergraduate curriculum. It combined leadership courses and seminars, community service, an externship, physical education, confidence-building and four years of ROTC. It used cooperative methods and did not reproduce VMI's pervasive military regime, rat line or barracks system. Task-force witnesses justified that difference by their views about the educational needs of most women, while acknowledging that some women would want and suit a women's VMI. Those generalizations are identified as evidence and program-design judgments, not self-proving constitutional facts. No scientific demand survey was conducted. The anticipated initial enrollment was approximately 25–30. The proposed state payment per student equaled VMI's annual appropriation per cadet, with a $5.46 million permanent endowment commitment for VWIL. Source: 44 F.3d at 1233–1235.

The remand court's adopted factual appendix supplies concrete comparison evidence, including these qualifications:

- VMI offered degrees in liberal arts, science and on-campus engineering. Mary Baldwin did not offer a bachelor-of-science degree or on-campus engineering; its cooperative engineering option required three years there and two at Washington University in St. Louis. The latter two years lacked the proposed public tuition discount and required private tuition.
- Faculty Ph.D. proportions were reported as 86% at VMI and 68% at Mary Baldwin. Mary Baldwin's average entering combined SAT was reported around 930, approximately 100 below VMI; this is an admissions/cohort measure, not a finding of any individual's capacity.
- The comparative endowment finding gave about $19 million for Mary Baldwin and $131 million for VMI; another finding gave Mary Baldwin $20.5 million. Preserve that source variation rather than manufacture a single exact valuation date. The separate $5.4625 million VWIL commitment is a different figure, not Mary Baldwin's full endowment.
- The adopted comparison listed substantial differences in sports and physical-training facilities, including VMI's tracks, pool, rifle ranges, obstacle course and larger weight and basketball facilities.
- The VMI Alumni Association pledged recruiting help, placement-network access and support for VWIL graduates. That promised access is part of the record and must not be omitted. It is distinct from receiving a VMI degree, sharing its institutional history or possessing an already demonstrated equivalent alumni outcome. The appellate majority itself recognized that VWIL lacked VMI's historical degree prestige and required continuing commitment and oversight.

Sources: [852 F.Supp.471, adopted Appendix III.H–I, IV and VI, especially 497–503](https://static.case.law/f-supp/852/html/0471-01.html); 44 F.3d at 1234–1235, 1241–1242. The remand court expressly introduced its appendix as findings, mostly historical and uncontroverted, while using much of the defendants' proposed format and substance. Opposing arguments and expert predictions must remain separately attributed.
