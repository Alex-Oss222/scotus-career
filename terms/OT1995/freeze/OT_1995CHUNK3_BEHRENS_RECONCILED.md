## Verified comparator and source scope

The official United States Reports, 516 U.S. 299, was downloaded and all 26 pages (299-324), including every footnote and the entire dissent, were read. The syllabus and separate majority/dissent pages at Cornell were independently read. The official report controls differences from the older Cornell text. In particular, official page 312 says that denial of summary judgment often includes a determination of disputed material facts; this handoff does not adopt the Cornell slip text's broader suggestion that every denial necessarily does so.

Historically, Scalia's opinion was joined in full by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. Breyer dissented, joined by Stevens. There was no additional concurrence or partial join. The historical Court reversed and remanded. Rehnquist is a comparator participant only and is not counted in the present roster.

The historical majority rejected numerical exhaustion of qualified-immunity appeals, treated immunity as claim-specific despite other surviving claims, and preserved Johnson's legal/factual distinction. It concluded that Behrens could present the legal argument on the district court's assumed facts and required the Ninth Circuit to identify those assumptions. It did not award immunity or decide disputed factual truth. The revived termination claim was expressly before that historical Court: pages 304 and 309 discuss it. Its presence is therefore not a newly changed fact capable, by itself, of explaining a departure by either historical dissenter.

Breyer's dissent, joined by Stevens, accepted that literal separateness could exist at both procedural stages but argued that Cohen's purposes, importance requirement, and avoidance of duplicative appeals justified only one ordinary pretrial qualified-immunity appeal. The dissent treated statutory permission under section 1292(b) as the route for a later exceptional question and supported rulemaking rather than enlarging judge-made collateral review. It opposed the actual result even with the reinstated-claim circumstances identified by the majority. This is not a dissent resting on a factual finding that every question in Behrens's appeal was evidence sufficiency.
