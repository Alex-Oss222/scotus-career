## 2. Governing facts, authority and source limits

The Michigan **judgment has four votes; Riley's lead rationale has three**. Boyle and Mallett join Riley; Griffin concurs in the result only. Cavanagh's dissent is joined by Levin and Brickley, and Levin writes separately. References below to the lead rationale do not upgrade it to majority reasoning. The federal review reaches the judgment's constitutional effect without adopting the state dissent's statutory construction or exercising general supervision of state equitable discretion.

The safe opinion supports voluntary shared use of the jointly owned vehicle, its use to transport the participants and as the place of the prohibited act, and the lead opinion's assumption that Tina lacked knowledge of and did not consent to that illegal use. It does not establish the fraction of her ownership or a completed all-reasonable-prevention finding. Earlier encounters do not establish repeated illegal use of this particular vehicle. Officers did not observe a payment; the lead opinion relies on lewdness associated with prostitution and alternatively on circumstantial proof. Its neighborhood-nuisance theory is expressly limited to the existing nuisance condition. The federal dispositions do not independently find payment, repeated use, or new state-law offense elements.

Permission for shared lawful use is different from consent to the offense. Both are different from initial taking by a thief or stranger. The remanding associates do not deem John a thief or find the ordinary sharing involuntary. Their rule instead denies conclusive constitutional effect to ordinary sharing when the owner establishes the additional protective circumstances.

The packet's qualitative account of the judge's cost concern and another automobile remains packet-derived. It supports considering the existence of equitable alternatives, but supplies no verified sale accounting, replacement-car availability, hardship determination or de minimis constitutional exemption. The contested hearing occurred; a hearing does not itself answer the substantive question of what ownership may be taken.

The controlling law remains as fixed in the C slice and actual public decisions:

- **Austin, June 28, 1993:** the covered federal forfeitures are fines; five Justices adopt the historical partly punitive explanation; six decline an exclusive instrumentality remand. The constitutional status of truly innocent ownership, state incorporation, final excessiveness and a comprehensive formula remain unresolved. Stevens, O'Connor and Souter joined the historical explanation; Scalia did not and proposed his own instrumentality inquiry; Kennedy and Thomas separately reserved a universal punishment account. Ginsburg and Breyer did not participate.
- **Buena Vista, February 24, 1993:** the actual statutory defense is available to a donee and may be established before relation back defeats her interest. Eligibility is not innocence or a title decree. Stevens authored the controlling opinion; O'Connor and Souter joined; Scalia and Thomas reached the practical result through their distinct relation-back and statutory-procedure explanation. - **Good, December 13, 1993:** Kennedy's prior-process rule for civil real-property seizure was joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg among current associates; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas joined the separate statutory-timing holding, not that process holding. Movable-property exceptions retain their justification. This case's hearing and final-ownership issue are distinct.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer joined the particular sentencing/jury classification; Souter and Ginsburg reserved that constitutional issue and relied on actual understanding. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the limited rejection of a wholesale further factual-basis proceeding; Stevens sought independent statutory assurance. ## 3. Justice-specific reconciliation

The alternative paths are substantive: apply the existing entrustment cases to the established ignorance-only premise, or adopt the reserved qualified protection and make its missing predicates legally material. A different path would declare this vehicle's nexus constitutionally insufficient or its forfeiture excessive without more findings. Each associate's treatment of these paths follows.

### Stevens

**Retain vacatur and remand, with an express historical remedy and rationale departure.** Historically he would reverse because he found the nexus inadequate, the owner entirely nonnegligent, and the penalty excessive. The neutral record here establishes only assumed ignorance and missing reasonable-prevention findings. His historical reliance at 466 and 468–470 on complete blamelessness, asserted concessions and additional testimony cannot support an immediate favorable finding on this packet.

His actual Austin join matters independently. That decision reserves constitutional innocence, declines to decide final excessiveness and does not establish a binding rule that every isolated-location use fails instrumentality. Thus the historical Bennis assertion that Austin itself establishes a nexus rule requiring this forfeiture's defeat, 463–464, cannot be copied as governing simulated law. His nexus concern remains a reason to resist extending historical forfeiture mechanically, but it is not a second completed invalidity holding. Nor may his historical Part III supply an unpresented Eighth Amendment claim or decide incorporation. These are concrete legal-scope differences, not a claim that the historical transportation facts disappeared.

His own Buena Vista opinion provides the strongest individual support for separating a claimant's right to invoke protection from proof that she qualifies. His Libretti dissent similarly seeks assurance of lawful authority without automatically declaring each item invalid. Together with his Austin historical/punitive and Good joins, they support adopting the reserved substantive limit and requiring determination of its predicates. The strongest opposing authority is Calero-Toledo's sustaining the loss of an unaware entrusting owner's property; he responds by making protection depend on reasonable prevention as well as ignorance, while acknowledging that recognition of the defense is new law. No immediate return, half share, punishment-only theory for all historical forfeitures, or state statutory reversal is joined.

### O'Connor

**Retain affirmance on both reviewed constitutional objections.** Her historical majority join directly supports the ignorance/entrustment disposition, and no controlling simulated law displaces that holding-level premise. Her Austin and Buena Vista joins prevent calling civil labels conclusive or ignoring an enacted defense, but neither makes the reserved constitutional defense already operative. Her Libretti opinion preserves lawful authority without transferring every guilt-trial safeguard to forfeiture.

The strongest contrary path is Stevens's qualified protection; its adoption would make the missing prevention inquiry material. O'Connor instead applies the existing cases to the record's established premise and does not require remand merely because the reservation could be developed. She joins no finding that Tina neglected an available precaution, no holding about theft, and no adjudication of the reserved category on a proven showing. Unlike a wholesale adoption of the historical Bennis discussion, she need not pronounce the simulated Austin reservation mistaken: Goldsmith's specific theft reservation and Calero-Toledo's additional prevention reservation remain distinct. Valid forfeiture supplies the answer to compensation; Fuller's reasoning is confined to its proper analogy. Her actual prior joins warrant these limits without changing her judgment.

### Scalia

**Retain affirmance; preserve his distinct instrumentality position.** Historically he joined the Court's complete opinion without a separate Bennis writing. His actual Austin concurrence supplies individual support for concentrating on the property's traditional connection to the offense rather than requiring personal guilt or comparing relative values. His actual Buena Vista concurrence with Thomas preserves adjudication and statutory defenses without constitutionalizing them, and his Libretti joins retain authorized scope and the particular sentencing classification.

The strongest counterargument is the historical Stevens contention that an isolated act in an automobile is only location, analogous to a building where a single drug transaction occurs. Scalia's limited answer is that the admitted vehicle conveyed the participants and housed the proscribed act; the present application falls within the inherited entrusted-vehicle rule. This uses the neutral record, not the historical report's alleged instrumentality concession. It neither establishes that transportation was an element of the offense nor announces that every location is a forfeitable instrumentality. His Austin method remains his own excessiveness position; it is not transformed into a comprehensive due-process test here. The missing prevention finding does not require adopting a new defense. Compensation is not separately owed for the forfeiture he sustains, but neither an invalid forfeiture nor all governmental acquisition is immunized.

### Kennedy

**Retain owner-protection vacatur and remand, while keeping his ground distinct from Stevens's historical rationale.** The historical dissent at 472–473 rejects reliance on maritime tradition alone for an automobile tangentially involved in crime. It proposes no complete new proof code: its reference to even a strong presumption of negligent entrustment or complicity explains why an irrebuttable deprivation is unwarranted. It neither requires the State affirmatively to prove fault in every case nor joins Stevens's universal historical punishment account or Eighth Amendment conclusion.

Kennedy's authored Good process rule and his Libretti lawful-authority join support attention to the owner's interest and actual predicates. His Austin concurrence with Thomas expressly preserves his disagreement with a universal personal-punishment history. His Buena Vista dissent concerned a postoffense gift of already forfeitable proceeds and the Government's superior title; it does not make Tina's preexisting co-title a gift of tainted proceeds. No finding about her purchase contribution is needed to preserve that distinction.

The strongest affirmance case is Van Oster's entrusted automobile and the anti-collusion rationale. Kennedy retains the historical objection that such tradition does not make ordinary joint use conclusive where this owner's lack of involvement and reasonable prevention can be established. A claimant-burden protection supplies the rebuttable opportunity he regards as missing and does not require his accepting Stevens's historical explanation. The narrowed remedy follows the admitted record: ignorance is assumed, but whether Tina satisfies reasonable prevention has not been determined. His historical due-process dissent did not prescribe this exact remand; his actual Buena Vista disposition left unmade protected-title predicates below, and his Good rule likewise keeps protected process distinct from a final ownership decree. He therefore does not decide entitlement now, demand return of the whole car, or find her interest legally insignificant. The car's connection permits the State to invoke its forfeiture law; it does not itself settle the constitutional treatment of this interest.

### Souter

**Retain vacatur and remand, with the historical Stevens-join differences expressly recorded.** His historical join encompasses all of Stevens's dissent, including its nexus, blamelessness and Excessive Fines grounds. It cannot be reduced to a bare dissenting vote. Here the latter constitutional question is outside the validated scope, and the historical conclusion that all prevention predicates were established exceeds the admitted facts. His actual Austin join leaves final excessiveness and constitutional innocence unresolved; its open remand is inconsistent with treating Austin alone as a completed finding that this property connection necessarily fails.

His actual Buena Vista majority join supports an opportunity to prove a protection before title is extinguished, while his Good join and Libretti concurrence keep the relevant constitutional interest and actual informed relinquishment distinct from formal labels. They do not themselves establish substantive immunity. The strongest alternative remains affirmance under Calero-Toledo's holding; he chooses express adoption of its reserved protection because forfeiting an interest after every reasonable preventive act would serve no owner-deterrence purpose. Missing findings require remand, rather than the historical dissent's immediate merits conclusion. His Libretti join rejecting a wholesale extra proceeding is compatible with this confined newly material inquiry. He does not equate marriage with negligence or innocence, order a new hearing regardless of existing usable evidence, or supply a fixed share or compensation award.

### Thomas

**Retain affirmance, including strict attention to historical limits.** His historical concurrence is stronger evidence than an undifferentiated majority vote: he accepts the settled entrustment rule while warning that forfeiture detached from the historical meaning of instrumentality may exceed its justification. The provisional row's refusal to approve arbitrary nuisance labels is retained and sharpened by that express concern. His actual Austin join in Kennedy's concurrence—not Scalia's separate test—preserves the distinction between the accepted statutory coverage and a universal owner-punishment theory. His Buena Vista relation-back concurrence and Libretti joins retain lawful adjudicative and statutory predicates.

The opposing path emphasizes that this car was a one-time location rather than a contraband conveyance. Thomas finds the admitted transport-and-use facts sufficiently close to entrusted-vehicle precedent for this bounded application; he does not rely on a newly inferred failure to contest instrumentality. He would withhold assent from a rule authorizing any property loosely associated with wrongdoing. His historical costs/destruction discussion at 456 and its footnote was a possible additional justification, not a finding that the forfeiture was exclusively remedial. The packet cannot support such a finding, and his affirmance does not depend on it. The surviving historical premise is entrustment and actual criminal use; no concrete change warrants adopting the reserved owner defense on assumed ignorance alone. His takings position rejects additional compensation for this sustained forfeiture, with Fuller's limitations preserved.

### Ginsburg

**Retain the bounded affirmance, with equitable mitigation retained as material context.** Her historical concurrence identifies co-ownership, permission for ordinary use, the conceded power over the vehicle, equitable oversight, and practical offset considerations as key. The first two and the existence of equitable consideration are admitted. The packet also reports the qualitative cost/other-car reasons. Those features support the same application without importing a price, equal ownership, a transportation-availability finding, or a binding concession about the vehicle.

Her Good join and Libretti concurrence protect meaningful process and actual understanding; the frozen Matsushita refresh likewise preserves substantive adequacy separately from an opportunity to object. None recognizes the reserved substantive defense here. The strongest contrary position is that equity failed to protect a person who did nothing preventable, and that low value cannot erase constitutional property rights. She does not reject that argument on de minimis grounds or say a hearing always cures the defect. She applies the existing entrustment rule to assumed ignorance in an actual equitable abatement proceeding, while reserving a proved prevention category and materially different, exorbitant applications. Her affirmative judgment does not require restricting the case to proceeds by an unverified concession, nor does it turn state equitable discretion into a mandatory federal offset. If constitutionally required owner protection were established, discretion alone would not answer it. No earlier participation in Austin or Buena Vista is attributed to her.

### Breyer

**Retain vacatur and remand; preserve the scope of his historical Stevens join.** Historically he joined all three substantive parts of Stevens's dissent. The present narrower position departs from its completed innocent-owner finding and Eighth Amendment result for the same identified record and question-scope reasons, applied to Breyer individually. He did not participate in Austin, Buena Vista or Good; no prior vote there is invented.

His actual Libretti joins accept the particular sentencing classification and a bounded rejection of a wholesale new factual proceeding, while preserving statutory authority and interests outside the defendant's power. That combination supports distinguishing an unnecessary new hearing from an inquiry newly necessary to establish a recognized constitutional predicate. Calero-Toledo's care rationale and express prevention reservation furnish the principal substantive support; the historical Bennis join confirms that he regards blameless owner loss as a serious constitutional problem.

The strongest affirmance argument is anti-collusion and administrability under Van Oster. A claimant-burden defense preserves those interests unless the claimant establishes the protected circumstances. Breyer adopts that qualified protection rather than immunity from mere unawareness. His historical willingness to find the facts satisfied cannot operate when the admitted packet expressly leaves them unresolved; his actual Libretti record appraisal provides no license to fill the gap. The remand does not direct the result, discard usable evidence, create a new proof standard, award a half interest, or grant eminent-domain compensation. The tangential-location concern remains supporting context, not an independent Eighth Amendment holding.

The remanding four place the burden of establishing the protective circumstances on the claimant. They create no presumption from ignorance, marriage or co-title, no numerical or heightened evidentiary standard, and no universal duty on the State to disprove every owner's innocence. Reasonable prevention is practical, not omniscient supervision or prevention at any imaginable cost. Existing usable evidence may be considered; an automatic wholly new evidentiary hearing is not required. A meaningful opportunity and actual determination under the new rule are required.

If Tina establishes the protected circumstances, the State must preserve or restore **her proven ownership interest** through lawful limitation of forfeiture or corresponding relief. The direction does not prescribe one-half, a gross or net amount, payment of particular costs, sale of the car, ownership of the whole car, or eminent-domain compensation. If she fails, this defense does not defeat otherwise valid abatement. John's conviction and lawful forfeiture of his own interest remain untouched. No additional entitlement is inferred from the family having another vehicle.

This common remedial commitment is not a common adoption of every rationale. In particular, Kennedy does not join a claim that personal culpability explained every historical forfeiture; the other remanding associates do not import their historical Eighth Amendment ground into the present due-process question. The four affirming associates do not join unlimited nuisance or forfeiture power, process-as-universal-cure reasoning, or a depreciation of constitutional rights by low value. The frozen independent annex's adversarial analysis remains in force subject to these express comparison qualifications.
