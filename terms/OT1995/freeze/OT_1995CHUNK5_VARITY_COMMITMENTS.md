## Individual commitments

### Stevens

**Result:** Affirm the reviewed fiduciary liability and clarified relief for both groups, leaving the lower court's conforming-decree proceedings in place.

**Sufficient ground and application:** Section 502(a)(3) expressly permits individual equitable redress for a § 404 violation; § 409's plan-centered recovery under § 502(a)(2) does not exhaust that separate grant. For the class, the administrator deliberately used benefit explanations, comparisons and assurances about continued security to induce relinquishment of existing plan membership. The combined communication is an administrative act, and knowing deception for the employer's benefit violates loyalty. The ten retirees have a separate violation: the fiduciaries administered their existing obligations through an unauthorized assignment, contrary to the governing instruments and beneficiaries' interests. Their claim needs no communication or reliance finding.

**Decisive evidence:** Stevens's Russell majority carefully limited § 409 while expressly reserving § 502(a)(3); his join in White's Mertens dissent supports beneficiary restoration through the express redress provision. His John Hancock join demands an actual fiduciary function, and his Curtiss-Wright join prevents amendment power from substituting for lawful action. His Peacock dissent supports bounded restoration, not an unrestricted personal award.

**Join scope:** Can join contextual fiduciary-capacity reasoning and individual equitable redress. Does not require a decision about negligent speech, affirmative disclosure generally, or every overlapping benefits claim. His broader Mertens remedial view is not a condition of joining: the controlling category limit remains, and the unpresented monetary-classification question is reserved.

### O'Connor

**Result:** Affirm both groups' reviewed liability and clarified relief.

**Sufficient ground and application:** The express individual equitable action remains available for these injuries despite Russell's limit on § 409. A failed lifetime-contract claim does not decide whether the administrator unlawfully procured surrender of membership. The class's case establishes intentional deception within an actual exercise of benefit administration, not merely inaccurate business forecasting. The ten retirees' unconsented reassignment is independently administrative and disloyal; an unexercised reservation does not validate it.

**Decisive evidence:** Her Russell majority join supports differentiated statutory remedies but includes its reservation. Her White-dissent join in Mertens supports restorative equitable redress; her Curtiss-Wright and Peacock joins require lawful amendment action and an identified statutory predicate. Those predicates are supplied here by the particular communications and assignment, rather than remedial need alone.

**Join scope:** Joins a holding against knowing material misrepresentation in established fiduciary communications. Would not join participant perception as a substitute for the statutory function requirement, an employer-wide candor duty, or a rule automatically making § 502(a)(3) available whenever a benefits claim fails. The actual clarified remedy and presentation reservation permit affirmance without deciding those propositions.

### Scalia

**Result:** Reverse the appellate judgment insofar as it sustains either group's individual recovery for fiduciary breach under § 502(a)(3), and remand for consideration of any preserved alternative ground not reached on appeal. Do not direct judgment dismissing the entire action.

**Sufficient ground:** Sections 409 and 502(a)(2) specifically prescribe redress for fiduciary breach and its plan recipient. Read in that differentiated structure, § 502(a)(3)'s general language does not authorize a second individualized fiduciary-breach remedy that removes the specific limitation. Its language still has work for other statutory and plan violations. Russell did not decide this question; its structure-based reasoning supports deciding it adversely here. Mertens's antecedent reservation leaves the issue open, rather than supplying the answer.

**Capacity and distinct groups:** Independently rejects the class's proposed extension from corporate viability assurances to fiduciary administration: truthful descriptions of current benefit terms do not make the employer's forecast of its successor's prospects a discretionary plan function. He accepts the intentional-misleading findings but disputes their ERISA characterization. The ten retirees' assignment is a different, genuinely administrative act; he does not decide it by the class's communication ground. Even assuming its § 404 breach, his statutory-action ground defeats this individual fiduciary remedy.

**Decisive evidence and limits:** His Mertens authorship gives independent force to Congress's differentiated remedy language; his John Hancock join requires function-specific status; Curtiss-Wright separates sponsor powers from administration. His Peacock join rejects equitable need as statutory authority. No categorical damages ruling is necessary. A writing accepting individualized § 404 relief is a join barrier; §§ 510 and estoppel remain for lawful consideration below, not automatic recovery.

### Kennedy

**Result:** Reverse the reviewed § 404/§ 502(a)(3) basis for both groups and remand for unresolved preserved alternatives.

**Sufficient ground:** The specific plan-centered fiduciary remedy in §§ 409 and 502(a)(2) controls this use of § 502(a)(3). Section 502(a)(1)(B)'s separate benefits remedy reinforces the need to respect statutory allocation, but the rejected lifetime-benefits claim is not itself a finding that adequate benefits relief exists for these different injuries. The sufficient ground is the fiduciary-remedy structure, not an assumed factual adequacy of another action.

**Capacity and distinct groups:** Also rejects treating the class's entire employment-transfer presentation as fiduciary merely because benefit information and business assurances appeared together. Actual plan administration remains necessary; the employer's financial forecast does not become such an act solely through its relevance to an unfunded plan. He does not negate the findings of deception. The ten retirees' unconsented transfer cannot be resolved by that reasoning: it concerns actual administration, but the same individual-action limit defeats the affirmed statutory basis even assuming breach.

**Decisive evidence and limits:** His Mertens majority join requires enforcing the particular statutory remedy rather than the full reach of trust equity. His Curtiss-Wright join preserves both corporate amendment authority and the need for its actual exercise; his Peacock join requires an independent statutory basis. These precedents do not compel his new construction but support it. He would not join individualized fiduciary relief or a perception-only capacity rule. No ruling on the precise monetary category, a new lifetime entitlement, or the merits of §§ 510 and estoppel is needed.

### Souter

**Result:** Affirm both groups' reviewed liability and clarified relief.

**Sufficient ground and application:** Sections 502(a)(2) and (a)(3) operate on different remedial terms. The latter's express redress language reaches this § 404 violation and does not contain the former's plan-recipient restriction. The limitation to appropriate equitable relief remains substantive. The class's case involves actual discretionary benefit communication used to obtain surrender of membership, established by the content and administrative setting together. This is sufficient without making every corporate statement bearing on benefit security fiduciary. The ten retirees' rights were administered through an unauthorized assignment; that distinct act supports relief without deception or assent.

**Decisive evidence:** His Mertens join enforces an express equitable-category limit while reserving antecedent action; recognizing this express action does not contradict it. His John Hancock join and Curtiss-Wright authorship require identification of the particular function and actual authorized change. His Peacock join is satisfied by the pleaded and proven § 404 predicate.

**Join scope:** Can join the contextual application if statutory administration remains the anchor; reserves any free-standing reasonable-participant test. Will join the narrow deliberate-misrepresentation holding and separate assignment ground. No need to announce a universal exclusivity or pleading rule when another § 502 remedy is available. Does not join trust compensation merely because a historical equity court could award it.

### Thomas

**Result:** Reverse both groups' affirmed individual fiduciary-breach recovery and remand for unresolved preserved alternative grounds.

**Sufficient ground:** Section 409 expressly identifies a fiduciary's personal liability and directs its restorative remedies to the plan; § 502(a)(2) is the specified enforcement route. The residual equitable grant in § 502(a)(3) should not be read to rewrite that recipient limitation for the same fiduciary violation. Russell's reserved question must now be answered, not represented as already controlled. The text supporting respondents is substantial, but his construction gives the specific fiduciary-remedy provisions controlling work within the integrated scheme.

**Capacity and distinct groups:** Separately rejects the class's fiduciary-capacity theory insofar as it turns employer recruitment and corporate forecasts into plan administration because the speaker also administers benefits. The court's factual findings stand; their legal classification does not. The ten retirees present actual benefit administration and must not be folded into the communications issue. Their alleged plan-disobedience and disloyalty do not overcome his objection to this individual fiduciary-breach remedy; no finding that their transfer was valid is made.

**Decisive evidence and limits:** His Mertens majority join preserves the enacted remedial category against generalized trust-remedy reasoning. His Peacock authorship requires identifying a statute-created obligation and remedy independently; it does not itself hold this action unavailable. Curtiss-Wright prevents treating a reservation as proof of a valid termination, so that shortcut cannot defeat the ten retirees. Would not join individualized § 404 redress under § 502(a)(3). His disposition does not decide the unreviewed monetary classification or extinguish alternative claims without appellate consideration.

### Ginsburg

**Result:** Affirm both groups' reviewed liability and clarified relief.

**Sufficient ground and application:** The express § 502(a)(3) action protects an individual beneficiary against this identified statutory violation; § 409's plan remedies retain their different recipient and coverage. For the class, the administrator's deliberate combination of plan information and benefit-security assurances is an exercise of administration, assessed in its actual context. The warnings that future conditions depend on corporate success and benefits may change do not undo the findings of knowing deception and reliance. For the ten retirees, unauthorized administration of existing obligations supplies a separate § 404 ground.

**Decisive evidence:** Her John Hancock authorship insists that the statute's qualified text and actual function govern, while breach and remedy require their own analysis. Her Curtiss-Wright join distinguishes retained power from valid exercise. Her Peacock join requires the underlying ERISA violation established here and does not bar individual relief categorically.

**Join scope:** Can join contextual capacity, deliberate fiduciary misrepresentation, individual equitable redress and the independent assignment ground. Does not require deference to the Secretary to reach those results. Preserves Mertens, future lawful amendment, and the distinction between restoring membership and inventing lifetime vesting. No broader disclosure duty or ruling on all overlapping statutory claims follows.

### Breyer

**Result:** Affirm both groups' reviewed liability and clarified relief.

**Sufficient ground and application:** Section 502(a)(3)'s express equitable redress fills the remedial gap for these distinct beneficiary injuries, while §§ 409 and 502(a)(2) continue to govern recovery for the plan. That conclusion rests on the provisions' different text and tasks, not a guarantee that every wrong must receive compensation. The class's combined presentation used the administrator's benefits role to secure transfer by deliberate deception; its function is assessed in context, including what participants were being told about their benefits. The ten retirees separately suffered unauthorized benefit administration for the employer's advantage.

**Decisive evidence:** Mahoney, 973 F.2d 968 (1992), uses trust principles within the actual statutory setting and distinguishes accommodation among beneficiary groups from sacrifice to nonbeneficiaries. It supports loyalty analysis here without controlling this different transaction. His Curtiss-Wright and Peacock joins require actual authorized action and a statutory predicate; both remain separate inquiries.

**Join scope:** Supports contextual capacity and the express individual action without making subjective perception dispositive. Trust law illuminates loyalty but does not displace Mertens's governing remedy limit. Does not need general deference to the Secretary, a comprehensive disclosure rule, or a categorical resolution of alternative § 502 pleading. Accepts the clarified remedy and the presentation limit on monetary characterization.

## Alternative paths and scope of the commitments

**Individual action.** The affirmance path gives effect to § 502(a)(3)'s express coverage of statutory violations and absence of a plan-only recipient. Its strongest objection is that §§ 409 and 502(a)(2) specifically regulate fiduciary remedies and Russell cautions against changing that structure. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer answer that an express, equitably limited remedy for distinct personal injury neither converts plan recovery into personal recovery nor implies an omitted compensatory remedy. The suggested § 502(a)(1)(B) bar does not defeat this restoration: the dismissed lifetime-contract theory did not adjudicate the separate wrong of procuring surrender or making an unauthorized assignment of membership. The availability of benefits enforcement in some circumstances does not establish an adequate adjudicated alternative here, particularly for membership lost through the class's transfer. For the ten retirees, possible overlap with plan enforcement is real; these commitments permit the reviewed restoration for the independently established fiduciary violation without deciding that every overlapping claim is appropriate or that § 502(a)(1)(B) could never afford relief. They do not infer remedial inadequacy merely from the earlier contract defeat. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas instead give the specific fiduciary-remedy route priority. The strongest objection to their approach is that it narrows the otherwise unqualified redress language; they accept that reading as necessary to preserve the particular fiduciary-liability scheme. Neither camp treats Russell, Mertens or Peacock as already deciding the disputed action question.

**Fiduciary capacity.** The contextual path's strongest objection is that current benefit terms were accurately reproduced and corporate solvency is ordinarily a business subject. The affirming commitments depend on the found use of actual benefit administration to mislead participants about the transaction's effect on their security, not general fiduciary status, employer solvency alone, or a mandatory disclosure of every forecast. The narrower corporate-capacity path preserves the employer/administrator distinction, but must confront the integrated benefit comparisons and assurances and the unchallenged deception findings. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas nevertheless classify the disputed forecast as corporate speech and reject contextual combination as sufficient to change that function. They do not extend that conclusion to the ten retirees' administrative assignment. The latter group's distinct injury prevents a capacity-only reversal of the whole judgment.

**Reservation and benefits enforcement.** A blanket defense based on disclosed amendment rights fails for every commitment: power is not exercise, rejection of lifetime vesting is not authorization of an assignment, and the operative decree expressly preserves lawful future change. Conversely, reinstatement does not grant immutable benefits. The affirming Justices need no new trial on the accepted findings. The reversing Justices' remand addresses the unreviewed legal grounds, not invented factual disputes or guaranteed success on a revived benefits theory.

**Remedy and presentation.** Each commitment preserves the rejection of the $7.6 million and $712,332 compensatory awards. The precise equitable classification of the substituted amounts is outside the presented questions: respondents invoked Rule 24.1(a), the United States identified the absence of a challenge, and petitioner's reply called the relief equitable while disputing individualized authority. Hansen's lower-court dissent does not enlarge that presentation. The affirming commitments therefore preserve the clarified judgment without announcing that replacement-insurance premiums, all unpaid benefits, or all trust compensation qualify as restitution. The reversing commitments rest on statutory authority and, for the class, capacity; they need no fresh monetary-category holding. The existing remand may account for intervening events, hear relevant additional evidence and produce a conforming decree. No new amount, mandate date or payment order is supplied; the $81,812/$82,812 discrepancy remains an implementation matter.

**Alternative grounds and genuine reservations.** Section 510 and equitable estoppel were sustained below but not decided on appeal. Affirmance on the stated fiduciary ground does not adopt them. Reversal of that ground requires remand for any legally preserved alternative; the petitioner's reply contests the ten retirees, so their component is not treated as conceded. No commitment revives terminated-class, punitive, lifetime-contract, settled disability or pension claims. No broader affirmative-disclosure, negligent-misstatement, equitable-money, agency-deference or alternative-pleading rule is necessary. The packet is sufficient for these commitments; a different factual theory or a broader remedy path requires a new clean source handoff before reconsideration.
