## Record controls and remedy

The contextual definition does not make every sexual reference indecent or supply a blanket news, public-affairs or serious-merit exemption. Clinical/instructional and bona fide news applications and reasonable licensee judgment remain relevant. Alleged editing or suppression of particular works remains attributed advocacy; those broadcasts' sanctions are not before the Court. ACT acknowledges that its listed examples were not all found indecent.

FCC ¶¶37–38 **tentatively** retain a case-specific defense when there was no actual risk of a child audience in the station's **market at the time**. Market-wide data showing no appreciable child audience can raise a viable defense outside the harbor. Station/program ratings are considered inadequate **until convincing factual evidence establishes their relevance despite grazing**. This is neither an established low-child-rating exemption nor an absolute refusal to consider such proof. It qualifies the rule but proves no broadcaster's entitlement here.

Child possession of receivers measures access opportunity, not actual exposure to indecent programs. Television estimates retain their particular time periods and markets. Radio average-quarter-hour audiences differ from one-day cumulative reach; the 9.244million evening cumulative figure is not a simultaneous audience. The FCC acknowledged limited quantitative radio data for under12s. Public radio's 0.2percent share of teen listening and under5percent weekly teen reach do not mean no child listeners. Adult presence in survey categories is not active supervision or co-viewing; remaining late audiences do not establish universal adult opportunity. No judgment may turn these distinct denominators into interchangeable proof. The FCC considered alternatives; the question is adequacy, not a categorical absence of any consideration.
