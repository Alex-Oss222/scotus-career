## 1. Law and record that constrain the commitments

### Actual entering law

- **Commerce:** The register's “Required commercial connection and distinct economic-class regulation,” present operation effective April 27, 1995, incorporates Kennedy's five-Justice Lopez holding and O'Connor's eight-Associate Harris ground. Lopez rejects the stand-alone school-possession/crime-cost/education/productivity chain and forbids adding a missing commerce element. It does not categorically remove every local noneconomic act from a valid interstate regulatory regime. Harris sustains its economic vehicle-taking class and also requires the vehicle-history element that Congress actually enacted. American Life League, November 13, 1995, Kennedy's unanimous commerce portion, sustains direct protection of the interstate medical-services market without requiring every actor to cross a state line. Bishop/Stokes, February 5, 1996, unanimously applies Harris and preserves those distinctions. Neither later case transforms remote object history or general public safety into an independent federal power.
- **Arms:** Love, December 11, 1995, O'Connor Parts III-A–C, establishes a personally held guarantee, rejects formal militia enrollment or current service as a prerequisite, incorporates the guarantee, and protects acquisition necessary to protected keeping. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined those portions and the bounded application remand; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer's contrary arms disposition rested on retained state-nonapplication precedent, not a collective-only rule. That state premise is both displaced as law and inapposite to this federal prosecution. Love retains Miller's actual weapon-and-record inquiry and permissible regulation. It supplies neither an exhaustive weapon classification nor a scrutiny tier or a categorical rule about automatic arms. The Chief Justice's published separate application is not controlling law.
- **Scienter:** “Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession,” effective Staples, May 23, 1994, requires knowledge of the characteristics bringing a rifle within the whole-weapon branch of §5845(b), not knowledge of nonregistration or illegality. The holding leaves the separate parts branches open. No Hale instructions or preserved instructional claim are supplied. Bailey, December 6, 1995, concerns §924(c) use and does not erase a separately enacted possession offense.
- **Confrontation:** The register's “Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions,” effective White, January 15, 1992, preserves exception-sensitive confrontation analysis. Its unanimous Part I rejects a production/unavailability prerequisite for those accepted exceptions; its seven-Justice Part II rejects confinement of the Clause to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes. Thomas, joined by Scalia, left possible reconsideration concerning formalized materials open while joining the exception-based judgment. That reservation is not a new test. Roberts, Inadi, Bourjaily, Wright, Coy and Craig retain their distinct settings. Bishop's medical-booking holding concerns the Fifth Amendment and supplies no hearsay or confrontation exception here.
- **Institutional state:** All eight Associates—Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—are seated and participate. No nonparticipation is established. Bank One and the other intervening entries create no additional arms, registration, or confrontation rule applicable to this prosecution. The February 8 telecommunications repeal does not affect these statutes. Same-day and later cases are excluded.

### Verified record and statutory limits

The verdict comprises thirteen §922(o) counts and three §5861(d) counts. The described inventory is one MAC-10 .45 submachine gun; three Sten-type 9 mm fully automatic submachine guns; two M-1 carbines with kits enabling fully automatic fire; one .22 pistol with a silencer; five .223 rifles modified into M-16-type fully automatic machineguns; and one MAC-10, one Sten and one M-16-type receiver. The source does not match objects to numbered counts, state which conversion kits were independently charged, or establish that the pistol itself was automatic. No short-barreled weapon, grenade, bomb, magazine restriction, or semiautomatic-rifle prosecution is added.

Section 922(o) forbids transfer or possession of a machinegun, except government-related transfers or possession specified in (2)(A) and lawful transfer or lawful possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986 under (2)(B). Manufacture before that date alone is insufficient. Section 921(a)(23) imports §5845(b), whose whole-weapon, frame/receiver, sole-purpose conversion-part, conversion-combination and one-person-controlled assembly-combination branches retain their separate conditions. Neither an exception nor an individual count's exact theory is found from the inventory.

Section 5861(d) prohibits receipt or possession of an NFA firearm not registered to that person. It does not simply order every later possessor to file an application. Sections 5811 and 5821 impose transfer and making taxes; §§5812, 5822 and 5841 connect registration with lawful making and transfer. Transfer and making applications must be denied when the specified act or possession would violate law. A transferee does not perform the transferor's registration function. The ordinary $200 transfer tax has the stated $5 “any other weapon” rate; the making tax is $200, subject to any applicable exemption. No Hale-specific exemption, refused application, legally unavailable registration route, or count-specific post-1986 machinegun application is established.

The lower majority expressly described the weapons as military and most as capable of killing or maiming groups. That characterization is not a finding of each object's ordinary civilian prevalence, historical use, or constitutional status. Its principal Second Amendment application nevertheless turned on Hale's failure to show an individual relationship to organized military activity. Love removes that sufficient ground. Beam's concurrence rejected the broader collective-only position and agreed with the result, but supplied no developed constitutional rule sustaining every application of the prohibition.

The two ATF certifications report a diligent search of the registry from 1934 forward and absence of an application by Hale to register his weapons. Counsel conceded the rooted hearsay exception and Rule 902(1) authentication, not a general constitutional waiver or registry infallibility. The certificates themselves, indictment, full trial proof and specific Senate excerpts are absent. The appellate shorthand establishes neither a newly discovered search-scope defect nor perfect coverage of every legal registration route. The older material was a 1979 Senate subcommittee transcript offered against registry reliability; the lower courts found no basis for its admission or judicial notice. The material's truth or a continuing error rate is not established.

## 2. Component commitments and operative grounds

The following propositions state the grounds actually supportable for commitment; they are not a final opinion or assignment.

### A. Section 922(o): enumerated power

**Commitment of each Associate:** Reject the presented Commerce Clause challenge to the thirteen §922(o) counts. This is agreement on constitutional power, not agreement that every application survives the Second Amendment.

**Sufficient rule and application:** Congress may control possession as an integral part of the enacted prohibition on additions to, and transfers within, the private machinegun supply market. Section 922(o) directly regulates the traded articles and their transfer and possession; its government exception and preservation of the existing lawful stock define the remaining permitted channels. Permitting unregulated creation and possession of additional private stock while forbidding its ordinary transfer would leave the regulated supply outside the same control. That direct relationship to the regulated machinegun market distinguishes the unrelated school-location restriction in Lopez. The conclusion rests on the statute's actual transfer-and-possession design, not on a chain from guns to crime, drugs, productivity or national prosperity.

**Decisive authority:** Lopez, Harris, American Life League and Bishop/Stokes at their stated scopes; the retained economic-class principles of Wickard and Perez. American Life League confirms that the absence of an actor-specific crossing element is not invariably fatal. Harris's enacted vehicle-history condition does not become an unwritten element in §922(o).

**Alternative tested:** Reverse these counts because Hale's immediate possession is local and noncommercial. Its strongest point is that describing an object as traded cannot federalize every local crime involving it. The adopted ground answers that objection with the same subsection's direct transfer restriction and control of the market's available stock, not the object's remote history. No universal “anything marketable” rule, general police power, new nexus element or finding of a particular interstate transfer is accepted. Every Associate's prior direct join in American Life League and the Harris/Bishop economic-class ground supports this bounded distinction; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also retain their actual Lopez joins.

### B. Section 5861(d): independent constitutional authority

**Commitment of each Associate:** Reject the presented enumerated-power objection to the three registration convictions on the independent taxing-and-necessary-administration ground. Do not use a general power to maintain public order.

**Sufficient rule and application:** Congress may enforce registration of firearms within a genuine making-and-transfer tax system as an aid to assessment, collection and tracing taxable transactions. Prohibiting possession of a covered firearm unregistered to the possessor supports that system by preventing its record requirements from being bypassed after making or transfer. A separate transaction-specific interstate element is unnecessary to that tax ground. Sonzinsky sustains a genuine NFA tax and registration in aid of revenue despite regulatory effects; Miller rejects the general police-power objection to the then-present NFA provisions. Freed explains the revised lawful-transfer and registration structure, while Staples preserves its registration law and requires the appropriate factual knowledge for its particular rifle category.

**Boundary and competing path:** Sonzinsky did not uphold every NFA provision in every application; Freed did not decide the effect of §922(o), enacted later. The strongest contrary path concerns prosecution for nonregistration where the very weapon cannot lawfully be approved or taxed. The supplied record does not establish that premise for any of the three counts. No Associate adopts a holding either validating or invalidating that hypothesized application, nor orders an exploratory remand to create it. The general absence-of-commerce objection does not negate this independent tax basis. Statutory registration to the possessor, administrative approval, actual refusal, and the taxing source are not treated as interchangeable.

### C. Section 922(o): personal arms guarantee and appropriate appellate disposition

**Commitment of each Associate:** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it affirms the thirteen §922(o) convictions against the Second Amendment challenge, and remand that component for application of Love to the actual weapons, conduct and prohibition. Reject Hale's asserted automatic immunity based solely on military utility. Do not uphold the prohibition through the removed affiliation prerequisite or decide its ultimate constitutional application on a new unsupported classification.

**Sufficient rule:** A federal possession conviction challenged under the personal arms guarantee cannot be sustained merely because its possessor did not prove membership in, service to, or preparation for an organized militia. Conversely, military utility alone establishes neither an unrestricted immunity from regulation nor the invalidity of a particular possession prohibition. Where the lower court's application depends materially on that personal-affiliation prerequisite and supplies no adequate independent application of the surviving weapon-and-regulation inquiry, its judgment must be reconsidered under the governing personal-right rule.

**Concrete remand:** The lower court must determine the actual §5845(b) basis and object for each affected count and decide the preserved arms challenge under Love and Miller, keeping constitutional coverage distinct from permissible regulation. It must account for the admitted military character rather than treating lack of formal membership as dispositive. The restrictions' actual exceptions and application remain relevant. The remand does not presume that automatic arms or their regulated parts are protected, that they are excluded, or that a protected arm is immune from control. It requires no invented civilian-use survey, mandatory scrutiny tier or new categorical evidentiary burden. It permits lawful decision on the existing record or ordinary further proceedings as that record and the governing inquiry require.

**Why affirmance is not independently adopted:** A rule permitting a categorical prohibition on the particular automatic arms is a legally available competing path, not a result already dictated by Miller, Love, or the statutory label. The serious capacity for group harm supports regulation, but neither this observation nor grandfathering alone supplies a complete constitutional distinction between permitted regulation and destruction of protected keeping. Miller treated the relationship of the actual weapon to militia function as relevant and remanded the dismissal before it; it did not exclude weapons because they have military utility. Love's original evidence recognizes both private keeping and regulation. It does not decide this prohibition. Rejecting absolute immunity therefore does not exhaust the preserved claim that these arms fall within the guarantee. An independent merits affirmance here would require an additional scope or regulatory rule and an adequate account of its fit, not merely the lower opinion's conclusion. The present source set supports correcting the identified legal barrier and returning that actual application more strongly than adopting such a new categorical rule.

**Why outright reversal is not adopted:** Military suitability is not itself the complete constitutional inquiry; neither Love nor Miller creates immunity for all military-capable objects. Count/object, statutory-branch, date and exception details are not supplied. The Court cannot adjudge all thirteen applications unconstitutional or order acquittals from that premise. The remand vacates the appellate disposition on this component, not an assumed set of trial findings or every criminal judgment and sentence.

### D. Section 5861(d): separate arms challenge

**Commitment of each Associate:** Affirm rejection of the presented Second Amendment objection to the three registration convictions. No military-utility exemption from the tax-and-registration duty follows.

**Sufficient rule and application:** The personal character of protected keeping does not itself exempt a covered possessor from the NFA's identification and registration requirements that administer lawful making and transfer. Hale's military-utility theory supplies no independent constitutional right to possess covered arms unregistered. This is an ordinary recordkeeping and revenue-administration ground, not a holding that every firearm restriction or every denied registration is valid. Miller considered an indictment enforcing NFA registration and stamped-transfer conditions; it did not establish the demanded exemption for military-suitable arms. Freed supplies the modern registration structure; Love expressly keeps coverage and permissible regulation distinct.

**Competing path and limit:** Remanding these counts with the possession prohibition would be necessary if their validity depended on a shown registration barrier or some other preserved, materially different constitutional burden. No such individualized predicate is established. The fact that some seized objects are machineguns does not match the three counts to newly prohibited arms or establish an application that could not lawfully be approved. Neither the upheld registration duty nor this affirmance decides that hypothetical. A purely asserted entitlement to bypass registration because the object is useful militarily fails on a sufficient ground without deciding every weapon's coverage. That difference makes this affirmance consistent with the thirteen-count remand.

### E. Negative-record certifications: hearsay and confrontation

**Commitment of each Associate:** Sustain admission against the reported hearsay, authentication and confrontation objections; no evidentiary remand or harmless-error finding. Thomas and Scalia retain the specific reservation below.

**Sufficient common rule:** On the accepted Rule 803(10) and Rule 902(1) predicates, the described certification of a diligent search and absence of a record regularly maintained by the public registry may be received without first requiring the custodian's live testimony or proof of unavailability. The firmly rooted exception supplies reliability in this presented setting. Roberts does not impose an invariable unavailability gate; Inadi and White require attention to the particular exception and evidentiary function. The decision applies that approach to the narrow official-record search reported here, rather than extending White's spontaneous-statement facts mechanically.

The underlying registration record would exist independently of prosecution if the legally recorded event occurred. The certificates concern a search and nonentry; they do not purport to reconstruct possession, classify weapons by expert analysis, narrate observed criminal acts, or decide the jury's ultimate element. Their preparation for this prosecution is a reason to examine the function of the certification, not a reason under existing law automatically to displace a conceded rooted method of proving absent public entries. Calling the custodian could test the search, but no particular faulty search, altered record, or pertinent contemporary omission is established by the reported objection. This is not a finding that questioning could never help, that government records are infallible, or that every formal assertion satisfies the Constitution.

**Strongest contrary path:** Treat these prepared affidavits as replaceable assertions offered to establish an offense element, require live testimony or a justified substitute, and remand any affected registration count for appropriate constitutional error-effect review. Unlike conspiracy, spontaneous and treatment declarations, the searcher's report can be given at trial without losing its original contextual value. The common admission position does not deny this distinction: it rests instead on the accepted longstanding public-record exception and the limited search/nonentry function, within the received framework. Wright distinguishes an ad hoc residual exception from a rooted one. Coy concerns altered presentation of an actual trial witness and preserves the relevance of established exceptions. Neither those cases nor White already decides that every prepared public certification needs the former-testimony gateway. The record establishes no additional basis for rejecting this conceded exception in this application. No new general test of testimonial hearsay is adopted.

**Thomas and Scalia's exact boundary:** Their published White concurrence is relevant because these are signed formal materials. It does not commit them to rejecting every such document, and it joined the existing exception analysis. Both accept the bounded disposition under that governing analysis and the conceded predicates. Neither joins a statement that formalized prosecutorial affidavits categorically fall outside confrontation, that Rule 803 automatically decides constitutionality, or that their White reservation has been rejected anew. They leave possible reconsideration of the Clause's application to formalized accusations open. This reservation supplies neither an additional ground nor a competing controlling test; a separate concurrence is optional only if the final text otherwise obscures it.

**Preserved limits:** Authentication alone is not constitutional reliability. No finding determines whether the actual certificates proved every legally relevant registration route; the shorthand “application by Hale” does not establish a new preserved wording or sufficiency defect. The Government retains the burden of proving every statutory element to the jury. The admission holding supplies neither an irrebuttable nonregistration presumption nor a finding of sufficiency or harmlessness.

### F. The 1979 Senate excerpts: admission and judicial notice

**Commitment of each Associate:** Sustain the separately reported exclusion and refusal of judicial notice. The limited record identifies no demonstrated abuse of discretion or constitutional defense-presentation error; do not invent an error and then declare it harmless.

**Grounds:** A congressional hearing's existence does not render the truth of its disputed assertions an indisputable adjudicative fact under Rule 201. Hale sought to use the excerpts to establish unreliability; neither their governmental setting nor their age makes their contents judicially conclusive. Ordinary admission is a separate question: the reported offer relied on older out-of-court criticism, with no supplied exception, relevant nonhearsay use, passage-specific foundation, or demonstrated application to the registry searched here. The actual hearsay and relevance ruling is therefore sustained within its adjudicated scope. Age alone is not a categorical exclusion rule, and a properly founded reliability challenge remains legally available.

**Alternative tested:** Vacate exclusion because testing the registry could expose error in the negative certificates. That purpose is important, but the possibility of useful criticism does not authenticate the missing offer, supply a hearsay exception, show its bearing on these records, or require notice of disputed truth. Bishop/Stokes's recent eight-Associate ruling on the separately reported video objection reinforces the distinction between absent detail in an appellate account and affirmative proof that the trial court committed an evidentiary error. Its medical and physical-evidence doctrines are not imported. No freestanding right to introduce all government criticism, no prohibition of effective rebuttal, and no new error-effect rule is adopted.
