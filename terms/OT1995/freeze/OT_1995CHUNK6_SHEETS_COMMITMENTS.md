## Shared record and supported alternatives

The neutral supplement and [appendices](../sources/chunk6_b/sheets_2._Appendices_djvu.txt), [petition](../sources/chunk6_b/sheets_1._Petition_for_Writ_of_Certiorari_djvu.txt) and [opposition](../sources/chunk6_b/sheets_3._Opposition_Brief_djvu.txt) control the record qualifications. Gary asserts his own intimate marital/family injury, not Kathy's surviving legal personality, copyright, ownership or reputation alone. Farnsworth testified to the assurance and communication to investigators; Bell could not recall it, and George's notice was disputed. Intentional disclosure and causation rest on supported circumstantial jury inferences, not George's concession. The objective instruction and special findings are reported in authenticated advocacy, not a separately recovered verdict form. The unobjected intent instruction requires intentional acts causing deprivation, not specific knowledge of constitutional illegality.

After closure in 1987, the investigative file including diary was publicly inspectable. This is a material feature, not a secret-file case. It does not establish prior public dissemination of the quoted intimate passages or voluntary public exposure by Gary. George's selective access for Lindsey and the resulting publication remain distinct from legitimate investigatory sharing and from archival retention. Unconnected excluded publications do not establish that these diary passages were already public. The jury's historical inferences receive prevailing-party treatment on Rule 50; constitutional coverage remains a legal question.

The appellate presentation advanced no compelling governmental disclosure interest; trial counsel nevertheless invoked public interest in the sensational crime and its subjects. The affirmance path considers that advocacy rather than treating all public interest as conceded away: general interest in a crime does not, without a supported connection, justify officials' facilitation of publication of the intimate marital material entrusted for investigation. That conclusion does not decide that every discussion of a closed case lacks public purpose.

**Affirmance ground A:** The Fourteenth Amendment's protected personal liberty includes a confined interest against intentional governmental public dissemination of objectively intimate marital/family information entrusted for a limited investigative purpose, on this supported record and without sufficient governmental justification. The constitutional interest derives from the intimate subject and existing privacy authorities, not the assurance alone. The assurance and limited use bear on objective expectations. Public archive availability weighs against secrecy but does not by itself dispose of this distinct, affirmatively caused publication claim or turn the supported intimate material into an official arrest record. This resolves coverage and the present application without endorsing a comprehensive compelling-interest/least-intrusive test for all personal information, invalidating an archive-access regime or regulating the private author's speech.

**Reversal ground B:** Whalen reserved disclosure liability and Nixon's protected-screening decision does not establish the asserted substantive right. Even intentional official misconduct and intimate subject matter do not themselves supply the missing constitutional guarantee. Existing decisional-autonomy protections cannot simply be expanded into general control over information in official custody. This ground accepts the historical findings, Gary's own claimed injury and the distinction from reputation alone; it rejects the asserted constitutional extension rather than making contrary facts.

**Narrower-standard/remand alternative C:** Remand would be warranted if a Justice adopted a legally necessary coverage criterion the supported verdict and independent review could not establish. The affirmers find no such gap: the objective home/marriage instruction and supported record establish the confined interest they recognize. They do not remand merely to relabel the circuit's broader formulation. The reversers reject the right on the accepted facts, making a new factual trial unhelpful. No additional mandatory factors, heightened civil proof burden or new specific-intent element is supplied.

The judgment comprises $650,000 compensatory damages and a distinct $77,896.93 fee award. No punitive award is established, so Oberg does not govern it. The reviewed constitutional question does not independently reopen limitations, municipal policy/causation, evidence, remittitur, fee amount or personal immunity. Any actually preserved immunity question remains distinct from present constitutional recognition and County liability; no new immunity proceeding is invented.

## Individual commitments

### Stevens — affirm on ground A

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Whalen opinion deliberately separates disclosure of personal matters from decisional autonomy and preserves the actual-disclosure question. His Albright dissent distinguishes affirmative official misuse from Collins's protective omissions; his Hodge join concerns immunity, not constitutional absence. Here intentional dissemination of intimate marriage material realizes the injury that secure governmental collection avoided in Whalen. He recognizes Gary's own liberty interest and accepts the supported jury findings without turning a confidentiality promise into federal law.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground B correctly stresses Whalen's reservation, Nixon's safeguards and open archives; a reservation calls for deciding this materially different disclosure, not rejecting it categorically. He could support demanding justification for disclosure of this core material but need not constitutionalize every personal record or require universal least-intrusive disclosure. He will not join a reputation-only description, deceased-author limitation or rule that archive placement automatically extinguishes the claim. **Remedy:** Affirm the judgment within the reviewed constitutional question; no new award, factfinding or separate immunity/fees adjudication.

### O'Connor — affirm on ground A

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** Her Albright plurality requires identifying the constitutional wrong and respects independently applicable guarantees; it does not foreclose a liberty claim whenever no Fourth Amendment claim exists. Her Hodge opinion expressly reserves disclosure merits and separates the constitutional question from historical notice. Her Miller opinion applies protected liberty to an established interference with careful attention to actual operation. Whalen and Nixon support identifying this intimate marital confidentiality interest independently of state assurances; this record concerns affirmative publication, not merely maintaining a file.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground C is substantial if the jury were allowed to create a right from subjective preference. The authenticated objective instruction, intimate subject and supported intentional-disclosure findings defeat that characterization here. She refuses a general secrecy entitlement, automatic strict scrutiny of all personal information or transplantation of Miller's framework. She requires the opinion to address public archival availability expressly and to preserve legitimate investigative use. **Remedy:** Affirm on this confined constitutional application; no remand based solely on abandoning the circuit's universal formulation and no newly opened immunity defense.

### Scalia — reverse on ground B

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Albright concurrence requires an identifiable constitutional guarantee rather than constitutionalizing a common-law tort; his full plurality join and Miller dissent reinforce that source objection without erasing existing controlling holdings. Whalen's reservation and Stewart's source caution, together with Paul's refusal to derive a general disclosure right from decisional-privacy cases, support rejecting the proposed substantive extension. Nixon sustained screened governmental custody and assumed its asserted personal expectation; it did not establish this damages entitlement. Intentional breach of a promise and reprehensible disclosure do not independently establish a federal right.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground A properly identifies Gary's own intimate injury and distinguishes Paul, and his Hodge join left this issue open. Those distinctions establish that it must be answered, not that the Constitution supplies the affirmative answer. He does not depend on archives proving prior publication, a tangible-loss prerequisite, or an asserted universal Albright holding. He could respect a claim within an independently established specific constitutional protection, but that is not supplied by this disclosure theory. **Remedy:** Reverse the reviewed constitutional ruling, vacate the federal judgment dependent on that right and remand for disposition consistent with rejection of this §1983 theory. Any consequential prevailing-party fee treatment follows the changed judgment; no independent fee rule, state-tort adjudication or factual retrial is ordered.

### Kennedy — affirm on ground A

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Albright concurrence with Thomas is confined to the asserted prosecution interest and a conditional procedural-remedy alternative; it expressly does not cure an established substantive violation through state tort law. His Miller dissent retains protected personal choice and effective-control limits while resisting automatic extension of justification burdens to lesser interferences. His Hodge join reserves affirmative disclosure. With Whalen and Nixon, those positions support protecting actual intimate marital life from the intentional official publication established here, without requiring a universal confidentiality framework.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground B and the open archive materially question whether official custody plus a promise suffices; he agrees that they do not suffice alone. The objectively intimate marital content and actual public dissemination distinguish this claim from ordinary personal financial data or a mere expectation of careful administration. He will not join automatic heightened scrutiny for every personal fact, an independent state-promise right or the proposition that every privacy-related injury follows Miller's sequence. The narrower substantive protection does not rest on absence of a tort remedy. **Remedy:** Affirm within the reviewed question, preserving investigation, custody, conduct-date immunity and independent municipal requirements; no new trial or damages recalculation.

### Souter — affirm on ground A

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Albright concurrence reserves an independently substantial injury beyond the protection otherwise afforded; it neither requires monetary loss nor makes one amendment displace every other guarantee. His Hodge join retains that distinction for confidential retention. His Markman opinion separates the actual judicial legal task from different factual tasks at its patent-specific scope. Here actual public exposure of intimate marital life is the independently substantial invasion, and the Court can recognize legal coverage without choosing witnesses or retrying causation.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Grounds B/C would prevail if the record showed only routine information, embarrassment or a subjective jury preference. The supported intimate-home/marriage finding and intentional publication answer that objection; lack of exclusive investigator access does not eliminate a limited-purpose expectation as a matter of law. He refuses a right defined solely as anything someone wishes kept private, a tangible-injury prerequisite attributed to Albright, or general judicial redetermination of historical facts through Markman. **Remedy:** Affirm the constitutional ruling and resulting judgment as reviewed; no fresh inquiry merely to choose between strict scrutiny and balancing where neither supplies a sufficient defense here.

### Thomas — reverse on ground B

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Albright join with Kennedy rejects the particular freestanding substantive prosecution guarantee while preserving the distinction between substantive rights and procedural adequacy. His Miller join with Scalia states a constitutional-source objection to expanded substantive protection; his Hodge join resolves notice and reserves disclosure merits. Applied with Whalen's reservation, Paul's limited privacy holdings and Nixon's screened-custody posture, those positions support refusal to create this distinct constitutional damages right from intimate subject matter, official possession and an assurance.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground A identifies an injury materially different from retention or a public arrest record, and Kennedy's willingness to recognize this confined interest is a lawful alternative. Thomas nevertheless requires a demonstrated constitutional protection of this disclosure interest rather than an analogy to protected personal decisions. This is an inference from his own separate positions, not a vote dictated by Kennedy's Albright rationale or Scalia's current vote. He does not import a universal state-remedy defense, eliminate actual binding privacy precedents or make Kathy's authorship dispositive. **Remedy:** Reverse the reviewed constitutional ruling and vacate its dependent federal judgment, with the same confined consequential remand stated for Scalia; no determination of state liability, immunity, award reasonableness or a new fee standard.

### Ginsburg — affirm on ground A

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** Her Albright plurality join and separate explanation identify a meaningful constitutional source without foreclosing independent protections; her Elder opinion insists that legal authority and historical notice remain distinct from preservation of claims and evidence. Her Hodge join expressly reserves disclosure merits, and her Miller join attends to actual compelled-disclosure consequences rather than the label alone. **Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground C would warrant a limited remand if law had been delegated to a wholly subjective jury preference. The Court itself can resolve coverage on the objective instructions and supported facts; Markman supplies no contrary general allocation. Open archives remain relevant but do not prove that Gary made these passages public or eliminate the intentional disclosure claim. She refuses an ownership/deceased-person bar, automatic loss of confidentiality through any investigative sharing, or use of today's recognition as earlier immunity notice. **Remedy:** Affirm within the constitutional question; preserve the separate fee award and all unreviewed components without announcing new law about them.

### Breyer — affirm on ground A

**Own authority and sufficient ground:** His Hodge join expressly leaves privacy merits open while distinguishing confidential retention and conduct-date notice; his Miller join evaluates actual disclosure consequences and supported safeguards in its own protected-choice setting. His complete Markman join respects allocation by the task performed, not a general preference for judicial factfinding. He applies Whalen's governmental-use/protected-information distinction and Nixon's attention to the actual intrusion and safeguards to this intentional dissemination of intimate marital material. These are supported extensions to the present facts, not a claimed prior Supreme Court confidentiality-merits vote by him.

**Strongest alternative and barrier:** Ground B properly warns against turning every harmful official release into substantive due process; ground C would matter if the intimate character or objective application lacked adequate support. This record supports both and establishes actual publication. Considering trial counsel's public-interest argument does not establish a sufficient governmental reason to disclose these intimate passages. He can join the confined substantive protection without selecting universal strict scrutiny, a fixed balancing factor list, a new security duty or the Casey/Miller sequence. **Remedy:** Affirm the reviewed constitutional application and leave the existing judgment in place, without additional factfinding, a new immunity determination or independent damages/fee review.
