## Question, posture, and material record

The supplied merits question is whether a Government substantial-assistance motion seeking departure below the Guidelines range also authorizes departure below the statutory minimum when the Government has not sought that statutory relief. That is a source-grounded question, not an assertion that every presentation uses identical words. The Third Circuit affirmed the 120-month sentence; its majority required a Government request for statutory-minimum relief. Judge Huyett dissented on the unified statutory/Guidelines scheme and judicial sentencing discretion. The opinion below is 55 F.3d 130, reproduced at JA 22–38. Its rejection of the Government's trial-preservation objection concerning departure authority is distinct from preservation of a plea-promise theory.

The sentencing transcript at JA 2–3 identifies the Guidelines range as 135–168 months. The judge states that the Government moved for departure but not below the statutory minimum under §3553(e), and imposes 120 months. The November 23, 1993 judgment includes five years of supervised release. The ten-year statutory floor is below the Guidelines range; the two measures are not the same starting point.

The May 10 plea agreement, JA 4–12, identifies that minimum and conditionally promises a §5K1.1 request below the otherwise applicable Guidelines range if assistance and compliance conditions are met. It leaves sentencing to the judge subject to governing law and promises no particular sentence. The October 7 Government letter, JA 13–14, requests a sentence below the applicable Guidelines range pursuant to §5K1.1 and the plea agreements, recounts at least four law-enforcement meetings and answers, and supports a reduction from the Guidelines minimum. Its text does not establish an unexpressed request below the statutory minimum. Neither useful assistance nor the letter's existence settles the scope of legal authority it activates.

## Statutory and Guidelines provisions

The source supplement identifies the enacted text reproduced in petitioner's merits appendix 1–3 and respondent's brief 2–4. Section 3553(e) permits a sentence below a statutory minimum upon motion of the Government to reflect substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another person who committed an offense; its second sentence requires imposition in accordance with Commission guidelines and policy statements under §994. Section 994(n) directs the Commission to assure that the Guidelines reflect the general appropriateness of lower sentences for such assistance, including sentences below statutory minima. Section 5K1.1 requires a Government motion stating the qualifying assistance and permits departure from the Guidelines.

Section 5K1.1(a)'s possible reasons include significance and usefulness, taking account of the Government's evaluation; truthfulness, completeness and reliability; nature and extent; resulting injury or danger to the defendant or family; and timeliness. The considerations are nonexclusive and do not require proof of all five. Application note 1 refers to below-statute sentencing under the circumstances of §§3553(e) and 994(n); note 2 distinguishes assistance from acceptance of responsibility; note 3 calls for substantial weight to the Government's evaluation, particularly where assistance is hard to assess. Their statutory compatibility is a legal issue, not a permission for commentary to amend Congress's text.

## Competing positions and supported paths

Melendez argues that acknowledgment of substantial assistance through the Government motion activates a unitary statutory/Commission scheme, leaving the degree of reduction to the court. He invokes §994(n), commentary, sentencing discretion and lenity as a last resort. The Government argues that relief from the statutory floor requires its authorization of that relief, and that the Commission materials preserve rather than displace this condition.

The analysis must distinguish the substance of authorization from prescribed form: requiring Government authorization of below-minimum relief does not necessarily require a separate document or a particular statutory citation. Conversely, the presence of a motion bearing a Guidelines label does not by itself settle whether its actual terms request broader relief.

Supported alternatives include treating the actual assistance request as sufficient for both forms of departure and remanding for resentencing within the authority established; treating the request as limited to the Guidelines and leaving the statutory floor applicable; or resolving a genuine vehicle/scope issue without adding a merits holding. Any separate refusal challenge requires Wade's actual constitutional predicates. A remand recognizing sentencing authority would not command a below-minimum sentence or a particular reduction. No path is selected here.
