## Actual changed premises and their consequence

### Franklin: a changed binding rule, without a newly preserved claim

The historical Franklin held the Secretary's report nonfinal and the President outside APA review. Its official Part II, **505 U.S. 788, 796–801**, was checked directly, including footnote 1 and the stated conclusion. Its lineup appears at pages 789–790. In this timeline, actual [Franklin Parts IV–V](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK14.md) instead hold that the completed Secretary determination is final under §704, the census statutes supply law to apply, and the agency's actual contemporaneous explanation is subject to State Farm/Bowman review. Section 2a prescribes transmission and the allocation formula, not a new presidential population choice. The President's own APA status and coercive relief remain undecided.

That is a concrete change in the legal environment, not a cosmetic difference to be ignored because the historical Wisconsin result was unanimous. Stevens, Kennedy and Souter joined actual Franklin's controlling APA parts. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas opposed finality there but must apply the resulting precedent; their retained objections do not authorize a current nonfinality dismissal. Ginsburg and Breyer were not members of that Court and apply its law without attributed personal joins.

The change does **not** displace the historical Wisconsin constitutional premise. The historical Wisconsin opinion invokes Franklin's constitutional counting discussion, not its APA finality bar. Actual Franklin likewise sustains a supported counting judgment and distinguishes interstate counting from intrastate district-line equality. Nor does the changed availability of review create a preserved APA appeal. The frozen neutral packet already separates those matters: federal petitioners' brief, printed pages 20–25 and 51, characterizes the ordinary APA determination as unchallenged on appeal; respondents defend the constitutional remand. Current P4 therefore preserves actual Franklin while deciding no fresh APA merits issue and making no exhaustive forfeiture determination about papers not read.

### Standing: an express actual precedent and actual individual appellants

Actual Montana and Franklin expressly recognize continuing allocation injury and likely declaratory redress through the statutory sequence. The present packet identifies **John Mack and Olga Morales**, their California voter allegations and their appellate participation; the later corrected estimates identify a Wisconsin-to-California seat consequence. This differs from treating historical Wisconsin's unexplained merits reach as an affirmative standing holding. The affirmative current conclusion rests on these neutral sources and actual law.

His **actual public Franklin concurrence** instead applies actual Montana's express declaratory-redress holding while reserving coercive presidential relief. The changed binding Montana holding and his subsequent public application of it provide a Justice-specific basis for P1; neither an assumed change in his general approach nor a new historical Wisconsin standing rule is needed.

The State of California's own appellate designation remains disputed. The individual appellants' independently corroborated participation avoids deciding that discrepancy. Their pleaded residences are not transformed into independently proved, unchanged March 1996 residences; the supplied materials identify no contrary personal-status fact. The legal conclusion uses that undisputed connection, the specific seat-related injury and likely remedial sequence. The State's decision whether to continue cannot surrender these individuals' separate representation claim. This is affirmative jurisdiction before merits, not assumed standing because the challenge ultimately fails.

## Reconciled operative commitments

These propositions retain the independent freeze's operative scope. All eight Associates directly join each; there is no aggregation of different judgment-only grounds.

**P1 — Standing and constitutional reviewability.** Reach the presented constitutional claim on the California individual voters' own continuing representational injury. Mack and Morales are identified individual appellants; the concrete potential California seat gain supplies the allocation connection. Actual Montana/Franklin permit likely redress through authoritative review of the responsible census determination without a presidential injunction or certainty that a new lawful agency decision produces a chosen dataset. California's disputed state appeal, a municipality's different rights and Arizona's superseded projected gain do not negate this individual claim. No general standing determination for every plaintiff, proved present seat transfer or claim of unchanging residence is adopted.

**P2 — Constitutional standard.** The actual choice whether to supplement this nationwide enumeration with this PES must bear a reasonable relationship to enumerating the population and securing population-based representation. Courts enforce that boundary while respecting the methodological judgment delegated to the Secretary. Reject the panel's requirement that nonadjustment both further a legitimate governmental objective **and be essential to achieving it**. Genuine enumeration efforts and an explained choice addressing geographic accuracy and comparative uncertainty are not unconstitutional merely because another method improves some accuracy measures. Montana and actual Franklin support this reasoning; they did not already decide the precise PES standard. This decision would supply that application. No numerical tolerance, constitutional presumption of enumeration's superiority, automatic transplantation of Karcher or census immunity follows. A racial disparate-impact claim requiring discriminatory purpose remains distinct from the representation claim, which fails here on its own analysis, not merely for lack of purpose.

**P3 — Constitutional application.** Uphold this nonadjustment decision against the preserved constitutional challenge. The extensive enumeration and coverage effort and the Secretary's actual comparative geographic reasons satisfy P2. The acknowledged differential undercount and the district court's improvement finding “for most purposes” receive an answer: uncertain geographic shares, smaller-area heterogeneity and sensitivity to reasonable statistical choices leave a legitimate methodological choice on this record. Respondents correctly distinguish the proposition that another adjustment might be better from the different proposition that the proposed adjustment is worse than no adjustment. The conclusion does not confuse those propositions; it rests on the actual explanation for doubt about reliable comparative geographic improvement.

The historical opinion's acceptance of a **rebuttable administrative guideline** favoring the enumeration is compatible with the frozen packet's account of Guideline One. It is neither a constitutional requirement to adopt that presumption nor an irrebuttable rule. P3 leaves the guideline within this explained comparison and does not add historical practice as a newly sufficient ground. Technical disagreement alone, the Secretary's title or a count of advisers cannot replace the explanation; the deference source is the delegated constitutional task, and the methodological record supports its application. Do not adopt every future-incentive prediction, find actual PES manipulation, treat disruption as an independent cure for an unsupported count, or find a missed production deadline. Later corrections bear on injury and relief, not the Secretary's July 1991 information set. No newly imported exact error percentage or technical concession becomes decisive.

**P4 — APA.** Apply actual Franklin's reviewability rule and preserve its actual-reasons requirements. Decide no new ordinary APA merits challenge on the bounded appellate presentation. Constitutional reasonableness is not declared equivalent to every requirement of APA review, and the district court's “beyond the pale” shorthand is not adopted as the legal standard. The surviving ordinary APA disposition is not reopened. If a later proposal depends on a preserved APA defect, obtain the bounded appellate materials through neutral restoration before changing a commitment; do not treat this handoff as an exhaustive holding that every statutory claim was forfeited.

**P5 — Sampling authority reserved.** Do not resolve the categorical §195 dispute. Its operative text remains: “Except for the determination of population for purposes of apportionment of Representatives in Congress among the several States, the Secretary shall, if he considers it feasible, authorize the use of the statistical method known as ‘sampling’ in carrying out the provisions of this title.” The exception, mandatory term and feasibility condition must not be collapsed. Whether the apportionment exception prohibits sampling or removes that use from a mandatory-feasibility instruction remains open, as does the distinct question whether the Constitution prohibits this type of adjustment. Section 141(a)'s methodological language does not decide the conflict by itself. Permission does not establish constitutional compulsion.

Respondents' Rule 14.1(a) objection remains live, without a final ruling sustaining or rejecting it. Reserving an unnecessary alternative does not require resolving that objection. The federal petitioners' express rejection of a categorical ban is not attributed to Wisconsin or Oklahoma; the States' contrary argument is not attributed to the Secretary, who expressly disclaimed legal considerations as his decisional basis. Boston & Maine permits recognition of actual reasons, not that appellate replacement. MCI requires delegated authority before deference but does not compel adjudication of an unnecessary, disputedly presented alternative. Neither construction of §195 nor the panel's statutory reasoning receives a current merits endorsement.

**P6 — Judgment and implementation.** In **No. 94-1614**, **No. 94-1631** and **No. 94-1985**, reverse the Second Circuit judgment vacating the district court's nonadjustment determination; remand through the Second Circuit to implement rejection of the preserved constitutional challenge and restoration of judgment for petitioners on that challenge. This is the independent freeze's implementation direction, compatible with historical reversal, not a claim that the historical opinion expressly ordered a new hearing. The rejected essentiality inquiry ends; no additional technical findings or renewed APA proceedings are directed.

Order no adjustment, judicially selected dataset, seat transfer, presidential injunction, funding redistribution or new redistricting. Leave the distinct tape-disclosure/protective-order ruling outside this reviewed adjustment judgment. Do not issue a blanket disposition of unrelated or genuinely unreviewed matters, a special mandate date or continuing Supreme Court supervision.
