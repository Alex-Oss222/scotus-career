## 2. Shared comparison: six affirming commitments

The independent Path A identifies the same minimum authoritative disability as the historical majority: withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and exclusion of comparable protection through ordinary state and local institutions. It is broader than local preemption or a quota prohibition, yet does not assume disappearance of every criminal, tort, contract or public-service protection. The breadth of the actual disability, rather than breadth alone, must defeat the proposed connections to association, religion, fiscal priorities, family teaching and regulatory policy. Ordinary rationality still permits reasonable predictions, generalizations and imperfect classifications. Narrower exemptions are relevant to what the asserted interests concern; their availability alone does not invalidate a law.

All six affirming Associates continue to adopt this sufficient ordinary-review ground. They neither use the unappealed suspect-status claim nor treat strict-scrutiny failure below as proof of rational-basis failure. The preserved alternative is legally resolvable on this record, so no new merits remand is required merely because the state court selected another rationale.

## 3. Correction to Scalia and Thomas's sufficient reasoning

**Independent limitation being corrected.** Path C states that the two do not rely on *Bowers* as directly controlling this judgment; their individual paragraphs reduce it to a different conduct question and support for continued debate. Taken only as the observation that *Bowers* did not itself adjudicate Amendment 2, that statement is accurate. Taken as excluding a sufficient precedential inference in their own ordinary-review analysis, it is unsupported by the historical evidence.

**Historical premise.** Scalia's opinion, fully joined by Thomas, reasons that the constitutional power recognized in *Bowers* to criminalize the specified conduct supports the lesser decision to withhold the challenged protection. It treats preserving the electorate's conduct-based moral judgment as legitimate, distinguishes that judgment from hatred of persons, and regards orientation as a permissible proxy in this noncriminal policy. The dissent separately disputes the proposition that constitutional entrenchment invalidates a substantive policy that survives ordinary review. These are central sufficient grounds, not incidental rhetoric. See 517 U.S. at 640–644, 646–653 and notes 1–3.

**Changed-premise check for Scalia.** Actual *Heller* requires respect for its proof holding, but does not decide the relation between *Bowers*, conduct regulation and orientation-based protection. Scalia's continued support for Kennedy's condition-related reasoning and the participation holding permits him to distinguish the confinement disadvantage. His *Hurley* and religious-liberty positions require application-specific analysis; they do not displace *Bowers*. His actual *Liquormart* concurrence declines the broader *Posadas* discussion and supplies no general prohibition of conduct-based morality legislation. His *Sheets* dissent likewise identifies a different constitutional question. No concrete changed legal, factual or procedural premise supports removal of the historical *Bowers*/morality ground for him.

**Changed-premise check for Thomas.** Actual *Heller* binds Thomas on the confinement issue without settling this claimed conduct/protection connection. His *Beach* position preserves both conceivable legitimate grounds and *Moreno/Cleburne*. His actual *Liquormart* join rejects inferring a power to suppress independently protected truthful speech from a power to regulate a product; his own categorical objection concerns keeping lawful purchasers ignorant to manipulate their choices. Those are real limitations on his reasoning, but neither establishes a general rule against the ordinary-review inference he draws from *Bowers* here. The historical Romer dissent itself followed his historical commercial-speech position, further cautioning against treating those distinct grounds as necessarily inconsistent. The simulated *Liquormart* position supplies no additional outcome-material change requiring him to abandon that distinction. His race and intimate-disclosure positions supply no such change either.

**Reconciled ground for both.** Scalia and Thomas retain the regulatory, association, resource and statewide-policy explanations in Path C, and also regard *Bowers* and the preserved conduct-based morality rationale as sufficient support under ordinary rationality for withholding the disputed protection. They accept the amendment's authoritative statewide institutional reach, rather than substituting a merely local-preemption construction. They reject the inference that this policy necessarily amounts to a bare desire to harm the class, while preserving *Moreno/Cleburne*'s prohibition on solely illegitimate discrimination. They need not claim that *Bowers* literally decided this amendment, that every moral dislike is legitimate, or that state constitutional enactments escape federal review. The six affirming Associates reject their proposed connection; that disagreement remains substantive.

**Conditional facial and inseverability argument.** Restore the additional argument that, on their view that withholding protection for the conduct is constitutionally permissible, an asserted distinction for persons of orientation alone does not establish invalidity of the entire measure. Their historical invocation of *Salerno* belongs to that premise and does not independently resolve the other six's claim of a defect throughout the actual class-wide operation. The state court's determination that orientation, conduct, practices and relationships describe the same class remains authoritative. Scalia and Thomas can dispute the federal consequence: if that shared class premise applies, they regard their *Bowers* inference as reaching the common operation; if a materially distinct orientation-only application is posited, they regard the conduct applications as defeating the proposed wholesale invalidity on their reasoning. This does not order severance or substitute a factual finding that every member actually engages in conduct. Actual *Dalton* announced no replacement general facial-review formula; *Miller*'s abortion-specific framework does not remove this opposing argument. No unverified admission about any respondent's conduct is needed or adopted.

These corrections restore supported opposing grounds without changing either judgment, inventing a new source, overruling an actual simulated precedent or altering the frozen independent artifact. The historical dissent's polemical descriptions and every illustrative analogy need not become proposed opinion text.
