## Shared grounds and remedy tested

The historical opinion confirms the operative distinction: defendant-preponderance persuasion resolves equipoise; clear-and-convincing persuasion also permits trial when the evidentiary balance favors incompetence. The conclusion concerns the rule's operation, **not a probability finding about Cooper**. His judge expressly applied the challenged burden, and appellate sufficiency under that same burden supplies no independent valid determination. Conflicting diagnosis, behavior and malingering evidence remains for proper factfinding.

The historical reasoning combines tradition, contemporary practice and fundamental fairness. Its footnote 17 does not establish one identical burden in all forty-six other States: some place persuasion on the prosecution and some formulations are unclear. The frozen commitments appropriately do not turn the parties' survey into a newly verified universal count or empirical error rate. The historical opinion's prediction concerning successful malingering is not imported as a new record finding.

**Changed doctrinal premise:** Actual simulated Medina has six direct supporters of the bounded preponderance and allocation-only presumption holdings, but no majority for an exclusive criminal due-process method. History's account of Medina cannot displace that topology. The commitments therefore preserve Kennedy's, Scalia's and Thomas's methodological reservations, O'Connor's and Souter's retained practical-fairness approach, and Stevens's broader disagreement without overruling Medina or manufacturing a new methodological split in this case. Historical unanimity supports the common narrow ground; it does not authorize attributing assent to an incompatible exclusive methodology.

The heightened burden's direction matters. Addington protects against an erroneous deprivation through stronger proof required of the State; Oklahoma's rule increases the accused's risk of an incompetent trial. Competence, civil commitment, insanity and offense-element proof remain distinct. Historical footnote 19 also distinguishes Cruzan's surrogate decision and irreversible error, and Akron's largely ex parte bypass setting. Neither those authorities nor Miller settles criminal competence or supplies a procedure to transplant. No new custody or treatment order follows from historical discussion of Jackson's limited restoration-evaluation authority.

**Remedy comparison:** History orders general further proceedings consistent with its opinion. The freeze makes those proceedings more explicit: remove the burden-dependent appellate determination; assess whether reliable retrospective evaluation of trial-period competence is possible; permit a proper evaluation under a lawful allocation if feasible; and require the conviction and dependent sentence to be set aside if reliable evaluation is unavailable or establishes trial incompetence. A new trial requires competence, and the State may elect lawful retrial instead of seeking preservation through retrospective review. This is consistent elaboration of the remand, not a historical holding that retrospective evaluation was feasible.

That elaboration rests on the frozen supported contemporaneous record, actual Godinez's preservation of adequate inquiry and reliable retrospective review, and Riggins's distinction between a supported unreconstructable trial risk and one not yet established as unreconstructable. It does not automatically vacate the trial judgment, preserve it through the invalid old findings, impose a new feasibility proof quantum, or decide structural error or a general harmlessness standard. The original commitment's reversal of the legal ruling and vacatur of the dependent appellate affirmance describe that limited remand; assembly must not turn them into an unconditional new-trial order or omit remand from the formal result.
