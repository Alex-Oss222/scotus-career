### Comparator and governing-law test

The complete Eighth Circuit opinion, Bright dissent and footnotes confirm the judgment and the genuine forum dispute. The district court found common moral-development purposes and material differences in program content; accepting those historical findings does not itself determine the constitutional classification. The seven affirming Justices apply Lamb's Chapel to the admitted subject and the policy's express restriction on religious speech even within Scouts. Stevens's different classification rests on the actual activity and forum, rather than on a newly assumed absence of the common purpose or an invented finding of hostility.

The Eighth Circuit's access conclusion is consistent with the seven affirming positions. Its broader statements about children's ability to distinguish private speech and public speech are not adopted as universal facts. Current Chabad requires attention to actual context; Pinette supplies neither categorical forum immunity nor a compulsory sign formula. Rosenberger distinguishes facilities access from public payments. Those actual current holdings, rather than historical opinions carrying the same names, govern.

All seven affirming Associates share the operative speech rule and the concrete conclusion that establishment concerns do not justify this exclusion. O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally support the full contextual Lemon explanation recorded in the commitments. The other three are not counted as joining every sentence of that explanation. Assembly must preserve that distinction and test its actual formulations; it cannot infer a seven-Justice comprehensive establishment test from agreement on permissible access.

The live alternative remains school discretion in a genuinely restricted forum. Its strongest support is the secular activity distinction, younger audience and continuing extracurricular day. The seven conclude that those considerations do not remove otherwise comparable private moral-development speech from Lamb's Chapel's rule on this record. Stevens finds the activity distinction sufficient; his result does not depend on importing Bright's every rationale or treating a reasonable establishment concern as a universal defense to proven viewpoint discrimination.

### Remedy and reservations

The affirmed judgment is the **Eighth Circuit reversal and remand**, not the district court's contrary judgment. Proceedings below may supply appropriate nondiscriminatory declaratory or injunctive relief. Lawful space, scheduling, safety and genuine subject/activity restrictions remain. No damages, compulsory participation, reopening of instructional hours, required restoration of every term of the former 1986 policy, fixed disclaimer wording, permanent forum operation or universal access right is ordered. The independent religion-clause claims remain unnecessary to decide. The preserved viewpoint claim was raised and decided below; no waiver finding is invented.

### Coverage, comparison, proof and remedy remain distinct

The official Houston Lawyers opinion applies Section 2 to elections for trial judges who act independently and confirms that jurisdiction/electorate linkage is a legitimate, nonconclusive consideration. The current Holder majority reinforces the separation of coverage, a grounded comparison, proof and relief. Holder requires a reasonable and workable comparison with independent legal and practical roots; it does not require a uniquely ideal arrangement. Its five-Justice comparison and remedy holdings remain binding. De Grandy independently preserves necessary Gingles predicates, the totality inquiry, genuine candidate preference and the absence of a maximization duty, proportional-results entitlement or safe harbor.

Scalia and Thomas expressly withheld assent from those majority rationales in the current Holder and De Grandy decisions. Their persistence in their separate statutory view has direct personal support, notwithstanding the contrary majority law. The formal result differs from their De Grandy vacatur-and-dismissal alternative because the lower judgment here already rejects the claim: they seek to affirm that adverse judgment on their different legal ground. This posture distinction does not give their view current controlling force. O'Connor and Kennedy's two votes under the governing results inquiry cannot be added to the two noncoverage votes to establish four votes for a totality proposition or for an exemption.

### Testing the independent lower grounds

The full Eleventh Circuit majority opinion, Edmondson concurrence, Hatchett dissent, footnotes and reproduced stipulations were read. The majority expressly affirms on two independent grounds. A defect in its alternative remedial discussion alone cannot justify overturning the separate no-dilution ground.

The four remanding Justices retain their conclusion that the opportunity determination itself requires reconsideration under the correct legal framing. The inquiry must concern actual minority-preferred candidates and opportunity within each challenged jurisdiction, with contextual attention to appointments and unopposed incumbency; raw success or influence cannot automatically settle it. They do not substitute a candidate's race for preference, discard all evidence from white-only or statewide contests, impose a particular statistical formula, or make a universal evidentiary exclusion. Contextual local evidence from a broader election may remain relevant. Their remand does not declare that every challenged system violates Section 2.

O'Connor and Kennedy retain the strongest competing reading of the primary ground: the trial court considered both experts, different elections, preferences, party affiliation and incumbency, and permissibly found the claim unproved. Current De Grandy affirms that evidence of polarization and a feasible alternative do not alone command liability. For those two Justices, the supported factfinding survives without reliance on the alternative remedial discussion. They accept that genuine legal error would defeat deference; their disagreement with the four concerns whether the primary inquiry here contains that error, not whether an appellate court may insulate legal error with the clear-error label.

All six applying the governing results framework distinguish a real state administration interest from categorical judicial immunity or presumed racial partiality. The lower majority's use of historical Shaw and its claim that racial awareness necessarily undermines impartial justice cannot be adopted as current constitutional law after the actual Shaw and Miller holdings. The current Holder rule also defeats reliance on historical Holder's uniquely preferred-size objection as if it were governing. Neither change proves dilution in this record. The remanding Justices must preserve proof; the two record-affirming Justices must preserve the rejection of an automatic remedial or racial-design bar.

### Scope and mandates

The challenge is to circuit-judge elections in the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Tenth, Thirteenth, Fifteenth, Twentieth, Twenty-Third and Twenty-Sixth Circuits, and district-judge elections in Jefferson, Mobile, Montgomery and Russell Counties. A statewide plaintiff class does not enlarge it to all forty Alabama circuits or every judicial office. The constitutional-purpose claims are not revived.

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, the mandate returns through the Eleventh Circuit for reconsideration and any properly necessary district-court proceedings under the correct standards. Supported historical findings remain; all required predicates and the ultimate burden remain with the plaintiffs. The independent opportunity ground must be examined before imposing relief. If a violation is established, the lower court must consider lawful and practical alternatives and genuine judicial-administration interests without categorical racial-partiality assumptions. No present liability decree, designated plan, number of judgeships, guaranteed election of Black candidates, injunction, election delay or timetable is selected. The dissent's discussion of cumulative voting does not establish a preserved demand or mandate.

For O'Connor and Kennedy, affirmance leaves the judgment against the Section 2 claim in place on failure of proof, without making the alternative categorical remedial statements law. For Scalia and Thomas, the same decree rests solely on their contrary statutory construction. There is no common substantive rationale across those four votes and no Marks synthesis is asserted.
