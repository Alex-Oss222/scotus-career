## OT1995-023 — Commissioner v. Lundy

**Event:** 1996-01-17 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 235

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1785

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the entering law and any material record or participation change at the event date; do not treat this position as a rendered decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s judgment and remand. This is Stone's approved vote.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the determination that §6512(b)(3)(B) does not authorize refund of these payments. Dispose of any remaining tax computations consistently with that limit without inventing a separate refund route.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Hypothetical claim at the notice date

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the incorporated limitations provision; do not recharacterize every tax filing limit as a general jurisdictional rule.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Enforce the notice-date instruction Congress enacted despite the difference from some refund actions in other forums.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When no return has been filed by the deficiency-notice date, §6512(b)(3)(B)’s hypothetical refund claim receives the applicable two-year payment lookback. Lundy’s later return does not bring the earlier withholding within that window.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute specifies the counterfactual claim date, and the legal conditions at that date control. Counting a later return would alter the counterfactual Congress chose. Lundy’s payments were deemed made more than two years earlier, so the Tax Court cannot order their refund under this provision even though it can adjudicate his deficiency. The consequence is statutory, not a judicial determination that the Government should retain every overpayment in all circumstances. [P1, 242–252]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on materially different refund procedures, a timely return, or amended legislation. General equity cannot change the specified lookback.

## OT1995-028 — United States v. Hale +

**Event:** 1996-02-12 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 978 F.2d 1016

**Docket(s):** Supreme Court docket not supplied; Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Affirm rejection of the Commerce and Second Amendment challenges to these machinegun convictions on a bounded national-market rationale; preserve separate disposition of the registration and evidentiary issues under their own sources. State Stone's individual-right premise without requiring its adoption as a condition of the affirmance.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Congress regulates manufacture and transfer of post-1986 machineguns; possession controls can prevent that defined market prohibition from being defeated by an untraceable supply of newly made or unlawfully transferred weapons. The grandfather distinction helps identify the regulated scheme. This direct anti-evasion rationale differs from general crime costs or an assertion that every object that once traveled remains fully regulable. Lopez and Harris leave room for that bounded economic regulation. It is unnecessary to make Stone's entire proposed Commerce framework binding or to add an interstate element the statute does not contain.

**Arms reasoning:** A personal right to ordinary lawful arms need not protect these actual automatic weapons in the same way as an ordinary handgun. Stone would distinguish sustained automatic fire by function and its relation to ordinary lawful civilian defense, not appearance or military ancestry alone. Miller's discussion of military usefulness does not itself establish the claimed personal exemption. Rejecting this claimed right does not decide that every rifle, semiautomatic firearm, suppressor or specially regulated item is unprotected. A separate individual-right explanation can advance the line while the Court assumes the premise for this narrow judgment; this federal prosecution presents no incorporation question.

**Other issues and relief:** The thirteen §922(o) counts and three §5861(d) registration counts must retain their statutory and constitutional distinctions. The NFA's tax/registration authority is not displaced merely by discussing Commerce. Address the negative-record affidavits under the preserved objections, actual concessions and the then-existing hearsay/confrontation rules; do not import Crawford or apply Stone's future proposal as current law. A failure on an independently preserved evidentiary issue must receive its own remedy rather than be ignored to protect a doctrinal narrative.

**Authority limit:** Apply the enacted scheme and actual entering law. Later proposed Love, Rybar or other roadmap positions supply no authority at this event.

## OT1995-106 — Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21 of Wheeling Township

**Event:** 1996-02-12 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Simulation-assigned merits opinion

**Citation:** 980 F.2d 437 (7th Cir. 1992)

**Docket(s):** Seventh Circuit No. 91-1684

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the rejection of the establishment challenge and remand for prospective relief against the school-directed religious affirmation. Preserve the school's authority to teach civic responsibility and to conduct a lawful patriotic exercise.

**Reasoning.** A declaration that the nation stands under God expresses a religious proposition when the school selects it for children's daily affirmation. Its placement within a civic pledge does not make the proposition secular. The State may teach the history of the Pledge and the religious beliefs expressed in historical documents without asking children to participate in a current profession of those beliefs.

The freedom to remain silent answers the claim of compelled personal recitation. It does not answer the distinct claim of official religious sponsorship. Lee recognizes that distinction. Stone would apply its principle here while acknowledging that this extends the official-school-religion rule beyond the clergy-led prayer Lee actually decided. He would reject reliance on passing ceremonial references in earlier opinions as settling this particular daily elementary-school practice. The brevity of the phrase and its history deserve consideration, but neither changes who prescribes the affirmation or what it says.

**Relief and limits.** Enjoin the challenged official religious component to the extent severable and necessary to redress these plaintiffs' injury. Do not rewrite federal statutory wording, prohibit pupils' voluntary private religious expression or require all references to religion to disappear from instruction. No proof of individual psychological injury, compulsory exit or punishment is invented to sustain the claim.

**Character and precedent.** The updated Master Jurisprudence informs this individual position. The three roadmaps supply context only; this case presents no separate Second Amendment, Voting Rights Act or Commerce Clause question requiring an additional holding. Existing law, the presented claim and the actual record bound the disposition. Preserve applicable prior simulated law even where Stone would have preferred a different rationale. No vote or opinion assignment is predicted.


## OT1995-030 — Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner

**Event:** 1996-02-21 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 325

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1239

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the entering law and any material record or participation change at the event date; do not treat this position as a rendered decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the North Carolina Supreme Court’s judgment and remand. This is Stone's approved vote.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The state courts shall resolve severability and tax relief under state law subject to the federal nondiscrimination and due-process requirements. Do not prescribe an unsupported refund amount or exempt all stock from otherwise valid taxation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Discrimination and the compensatory defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the discriminatory tax and defense. Remit the choice among lawful state remedial routes rather than entering an uncalculated refund.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the controlled compensatory-tax doctrine and expressly displace Darnell to the extent it approved this incompatible discrimination.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The intangibles tax discriminates against interstate commerce, and North Carolina has not established a substantially equivalent intrastate burden that compensates for it. Darnell is overruled insofar as it authorizes the contrary discrimination.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The tax benefit tracks local corporate activity and changes a resident investor’s economic choice. A corporate tax on income is not shown to impose the same economic burden as the shareholder’s tax on ownership. The State’s general desire to reward local tax contributions cannot substitute for equivalent taxable events and incidence. Darnell’s contrary approval is incompatible with the developed, now-controlled nondiscrimination rule; retaining its label while rejecting its premise would obscure the change. Concrete reliance in tax administration is addressed through lawful remedies, not continued discriminatory collection. [P1, statute and Darnell issue; S1, Oregon Waste]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ordinary nondiscriminatory corporate and property taxes remain available. Refunds, severability, and any lawful cure must be resolved consistently with due process; invalidity does not by itself select a full refund formula.
