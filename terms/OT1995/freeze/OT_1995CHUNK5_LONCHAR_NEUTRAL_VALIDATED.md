This produces two linked but distinct issues: whether equity supplies an otherwise unauthorized ground to dismiss a first federal habeas petition, and whether the specific execution stay can be denied or vacated in the posture presented. A conclusion on one does not mechanically establish the other. The requested relief and any remand must address the actual vacatur judgment, not an invented completed dismissal. ## Entering law and full limits

The current Standards titles and effective authority are:

* **Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar** — *Gomez*, April 21, 1992. A court may weigh the State's enforcement interest and unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim independently of a habeas bar. A supported serious constitutional claim and execution's irreversible consequence require consideration but do not alone compel last-minute relief. Later strengthening evidence does not necessarily explain earlier withholding. This is a contextual equitable stay inquiry, not an automatic late-claim bar or automatic capital stay. *Delo*'s abuse-of-discretion review, *Barefoot*'s substantial-ground requirements, and *McCleskey* within its proper habeas domain remain intact. The four earlier petitions in Gomez describe that record, not a minimum numerical prerequisite. Neither later unexplained Gomez orders nor their case-specific authorization conditions create a general bar on later federal stays. The §1983/habeas boundary, a full abuse analysis there, and the underlying method's constitutionality were left open.
* **Capital habeas counsel before a merits petition and discretionary stays** — *McFarland v. Scott*, June 30, 1994. Qualified capital counsel may be obtained in the identified statutory preparation proceeding before filing the merits petition; that proceeding satisfies §2251 pendency and the statute supplies the express anti-injunction exception. A prisoner must have a reasonable opportunity to prepare; a stay is discretionary, neither automatic nor indefinite, and proved inexcusable disregard of the opportunity and obstruction may justify denial. Appointment alone authorizes no unidentified expense. The statute's counsel qualifications, good-cause exception, necessary-services findings and possible retroactive authorization remain distinct. These preparation rights cannot be generalized into an unconditional stay for every late petition, but neither can competence or reluctance alone erase the statutory channel.
* **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims** — *Schlup v. Delo*, January 23, 1995. Reliable new evidence and the probative record as a whole must show it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. Reliability and likely credibility, delay and inconsistency matter; no categorical exclusion of impeachment or inmate testimony follows. A mere possibility of acquittal, the reviewing judge's doubt, or ordinary merits prejudice is insufficient. This opens a procedural gateway only: no automatic hearing, merits success, writ, release, retrial, or stay. Claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and sufficient independent merits grounds remain. The current §2244(b) construction preserves both the new-ground and no-deliberate-withholding/other-abuse requirements and requires a clear, particular State abuse pleading before shifting the burden. It is not a requirement that every first petitioner first prove innocence.
* **Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims** — *Sawyer v. Whitley*, June 22, 1992. The distinct death-eligibility gateway requires clear and convincing proof that without constitutional error no reasonable juror would find eligibility under applicable state law. Additional discretionary mitigation does not satisfy it. Cause and prejudice and the offense-innocence gateway remain distinct; no freestanding innocence right follows.

These are public Court rules within their stated support. No separate position has been imported as Court law. The relevant verbatim public Standards passages are preserved in `sources/chunk5_d/ENTERING_STANDARDS_VERBATIM.md`.

The actual 1994 §2254 text and Rules were read in the authorized official-code copy `terms/OT1994/sources/oneal/1994-28usc2254.txt` (matching HTML retained there). Section 2254(a) concerns custody violating federal law; (b) requires exhaustion unless available corrective process is absent or ineffective; (c) preserves an available state procedure. Its then-effective factual presumption, eight exceptions, production directions, and convincing-evidence burden are those of pre-AEDPA §2254(d)–(f), not the later statutory deference test.

The enacted **Rule 4** requires prompt judicial examination, summary dismissal when lack of entitlement plainly appears from the petition and exhibits, otherwise an answer or other appropriate judicial action, and service of petition and order on the respondent and state attorney general. **Rule 9(a)** permits dismissal for filing delay that prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless the petitioner shows the grounds could not with reasonable diligence have been known before that prejudice arose. Delay alone and prejudice merely from losing an execution date are not the stated textual predicates. The enacted rule contains **no five-year presumption**: Congress deleted that proposed presumption in 1976 even though the original advisory discussion still describes it. **Rule 9(b)** addresses second or successive petitions: no new/different grounds and a prior merits determination, or new/different grounds whose omission was abuse. It is not a first-petition waiting-period rule. Whether a prior proceeding qualifies requires its actual disposition, not a count of captions. The 1976 amendment and advisory discussion's notice/opportunity-to-explain cautions were separately inspected.

Section 2244(b)'s full then-effective wording is quoted in the authorized runtime's public Schlup-law section. Section 2243's direction to dispose of the matter as law and justice require and §2251's discretionary stay power must be read with the enacted habeas scheme. A standalone verified copy of §§2243, 2251 and 2253 has not independently been obtained in this preflight; §2251's operative scope is supplied by the public McFarland holding. No AEDPA provisions, later certificate-of-appealability regime, later successive-application gatekeeping, or later deadline applies on April 1.

## Lawful paths and opposing arguments

One supported path treats the enacted first-petition framework as excluding a free-ranging dismissal sanction based solely on filing delay and motivation when no statutory or Rules predicate is established. That requires confronting the State's independent equitable-stay argument and explaining how the specific vacatur is to be handled. Returning for ordinary Rule 4 examination or claim-specific procedural analysis need not decide that a hearing or writ is warranted, nor immunize any actual abusive or defaulted claim. Any necessary stay must have a supported duration and purpose.

The opposing path relies on the lower court's extreme-delay findings, the State's interest, and the actual equitable discretion preserved by Gomez and McFarland. It must explain why a stay may be refused in this first-petition posture without inventing a new permanent merits-dismissal ground or preventing the process Congress and the Rules require. Gomez's prior procedural history is not a categorical bar to its equitable principle, but neither does Gomez resolve all first-petition statutory objections. McFarland expressly allows denial for proved inexcusable obstruction; it also protects a reasonable preparation opportunity. Applying either requires evidence and an explanation, not the label “equity.”

A narrower course can resolve the legal standard and remand for a reasoned application to the actual petition and record. It must identify what the lower court must reconsider and preserve genuinely sufficient independent grounds if supported. It cannot announce blanket immunity for a first petition, a blanket forfeiture for delay, or a merits answer to undisclosed constitutional claims. At this stage the available source does not establish that an innocence gateway, factual hearing, counsel failure, statutory prejudice, or abuse predicate is actually satisfied.
