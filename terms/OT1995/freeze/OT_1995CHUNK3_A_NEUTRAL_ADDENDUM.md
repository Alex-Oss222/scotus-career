## Zicherman — validation and fuller lower-court frame

The record and event are ready for independent non-Stone modeling. Both petitions concern loss-of-society damages; other award components are not silently added to the Supreme Court's scope. No case-specific nonparticipation is established. The event-date roster has all nine members and an ordinary merits quorum. Arguments were November 7, 1995; decision date is January 16, 1996 as supplied.

The Second Circuit applied its Lockerbie decisions to select general maritime law as a uniform domestic rule for Warsaw cases, notwithstanding DOHSA's contrary damages limit. It reasoned that the cause arose under a treaty and that applying different damages rules to crashes over land and water would impair treaty uniformity. It then applied a dependency limit drawn from general maritime decisions. The mother had conceded she was not dependent; the sister's financial-assistance evidence could support either dependency finding, so the latter issue was remanded. This is the lower court's rationale, not an established Supreme Court choice-of-law rule.

The lower judgment separately sustained predeath pain-and-suffering damages and the calculation of prejudgment interest, rejected mental-injury awards, and remanded support and inheritance awards. Their presence in the lower opinion does not establish a grant covering them. The submitted loss-of-society questions permit a decree confined to the mother's and sister's society claims. No holding about survival damages, mental distress accompanying physical injury, prejudgment interest, support, inheritance or liability is required.

The two live statutory/treaty routes are: (A) Articles 17 and 24 do not establish a complete compensatory code; ordinary domestic law supplies the damage categories, and on the parties' accepted United States-law premise the high-seas statute disallows society damages; (B) treaty uniformity supplies a federal common-law compensatory measure independent of DOHSA, potentially permitting society damages, with a further dispute over dependency. The strongest point for B is the difference between treaty liability and a domestic statutory cause of action; its principal obstacle is the absence of an affirmative treaty provision displacing the pecuniary-loss rule and Article 24's reservation of beneficiaries' respective rights. The strongest objection to A is nonuniform damages by geography; Saks and Floyd require uniform interpretation of the treaty's actual rules, not assumption of an unstated universal damages measure.

Entering simulated Yamaha, January 9, 1996, preserves state death remedies for uncovered nonseafarer deaths in territorial waters while expressly retaining DOHSA's exclusive high-seas field and the statute-bounded remedial approach of Miles and Offshore Logistics. Every participating non-Stone Justice joined Yamaha Part II. American Dredging's procedural forum rule does not authorize substantive damages supplementation. No external present-matter Supreme Court disposition was used to resolve these routes.

## Lotus — validation and scope clarification

The record is ready for independent modeling on the statutory classification issue. Stevens does not participate at the submitted and decision stages; no reason is inferred. Eight participate, making five sufficient for a merits judgment and a four-four division nonprecedential. The known participation fact establishes no vote. Argument was January 8, 1996; event date January 16, 1996.

The dispute includes two implementations: the visible Lotus Emulation Interface and the Key Reader, which continues to interpret some existing Lotus macros without displaying the Lotus hierarchy. The latter uses the hierarchy and the command names' initial letters. Both were held infringing below; the district court entered a permanent injunction. The original packet's general description of copying covers both, but each must remain visible in the commitment and disposition.

No source or object code was copied. Screen displays are outside the appeal because Lotus did not cross-appeal the finding that they were not copied. Long prompts were excluded by the parties' agreement and are not a basis for either judgment. Borland concedes a valid copyright in Lotus 1-2-3 as a whole and factual copying of the hierarchy; the scope of protected subject matter remains disputed.

The First Circuit holds that the operative commands are themselves the means of controlling the program, and therefore excluded by §102(b), even if the original design involved choices. Boudin concurs in that result while discussing a possible narrower privilege for reuse of users' own investment and macros; he does not establish a separate nationwide compatibility defense. The district court's contrary reasoning is that independently chosen command names and arrangement contain expression separable from the general idea of a hierarchical command system, supported by the availability of alternative arrangements.

The material alternatives are (A) affirm the exclusion of this operative command hierarchy, leaving fair use unneeded; (B) reject a categorical exclusion of the hierarchy and return for the appellate consideration of properly remaining infringement and defenses; and (C) rest an affirmance on a privilege or fair use even if expression exists. Route C is not a shortcut: simulated Campbell requires an integrated four-factor inquiry, keeps the defendant's burden and does not make compatibility or commerciality conclusive. The district court rejected fair use after the separate trials; the court of appeals did not decide that defense. A reversal of the categorical classification does not automatically reinstate the district court's injunction without consideration of preserved alternative grounds.

Feist distinguishes unprotectable facts from protectable original selection and arrangement; it does not answer whether particular command words embody a prohibited method. Atari Games distinguishes copyrightability from thin protection and requires analysis of a work's actual expressive arrangement, but concerns audiovisual presentation, which Lotus's appeal does not place at issue. Campbell preserves the protected nature of original creative expression while allowing fact-specific defenses; it creates no operative-interface holding. These analogies support genuine competing routes rather than mechanically determining all seven non-Stone votes.

## Bank One — material factual correction and validated jurisdiction frame

Replace the original packet's description of a delayed nonpayment notice with the actual lower judgment's description: the receiving/depositary bank alleged that Midwest returned a $64,294.27 check unpaid without advising it that the reason was insufficient funds. The cited duties were 12 C.F.R. §§229.30(d) and 229.33(b)(8), with ordinary-care liability invoked under §229.38(a). The district court entered summary judgment and $43,912.06 for the plaintiff. The Seventh Circuit vacated that judgment and remanded with instructions to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction. It did not decide the merits of the alleged regulatory breach.

The lower caption is First Illinois Bank & Trust v. Midwest. Use the supplied Supreme Court caption Bank One Chicago, N.A. v. Midwest Bank & Trust Co., No.94-1175, for the event. This addendum makes no independent finding of the mechanism or date of any corporate succession; it is unnecessary to the statutory issue, and no identity dispute appears in the supplied packet or lower opinion. Argument November 28, 1995; event January 17, 1996. All nine participate.

The lower court read §4010(a)'s exclusion of claims against a depository institution by another such institution to govern §4010(d)'s permission to sue in federal district court. It suggested administrative resolution under §4009(c)(1), or perhaps a state action, and adhered to that view after the Federal Reserve Board reported that no administrative dispute-resolution mechanism existed. The Board, New York Clearing House Association and Standard Bank supported rehearing. The panel denied rehearing; no judge requested an en banc vote.

The statutory text gives two distinct liability arrangements. Section 4010(a) creates the direct customer-oriented remedy with actual damages, specified individual/class additional damages, and costs and reasonable attorney fees. It expressly excludes another depository institution from that remedy. Section 4010(f) expressly authorizes the Board to impose or allocate loss and liability among depository institutions for any aspect of the check payment system. Its ordinary liability ceiling is the amount of the check; bad faith permits additional damages proximately caused by the act or omission. Section 4010(d) authorizes any action under the section in a United States district court or another competent court within one year of the occurrence. Section 4008(c) gives the Board payment-system regulatory responsibility. None of these texts says that interbank disputes must be adjudicated administratively.

Sections 4010(c) and (e) remain distinct: the former protects a depository institution proving by a preponderance that an unintentional violation resulted from a bona fide error despite reasonably adapted preventive procedures, excluding an error of legal judgment; the latter protects good-faith conformity to a Board rule, regulation or interpretation despite its later amendment, rescission or invalidation. Whether a particular statutory or regulatory defense applies is not decided by identifying federal jurisdiction.

The competing lawful readings are (A) the words any action under this section include Board-established interbank liability under (f), even though the different (a) customer remedy excludes interbank plaintiffs; (B) subsection (d) concerns only subsection (a), leaving administrative enforcement or a state-law route for the delegated interbank rules. A has the stronger integrated textual reading because (f) is in the same section and supplies express delegation of liability; B's strongest point is the explicit interbank exclusion in (a), which must retain force. Route A preserves that force by leaving customer additional damages and attorney fees in (a) and using (f)'s separate liability and limit. Touche Ross and Transamerica require congressional authorization rather than judicial creation of a remedy; the express (d)/(f) arrangement is the proposed authorization, not a right inferred solely from the Act's beneficial purpose or an agency's preference.

The proper available decree on A is reversal of the jurisdictional dismissal and remand for the court of appeals to address properly remaining challenges to the district judgment. Neither damages, liability, a late-notice finding, nor a final injunction is newly imposed. NationsBank and current-term Town & Country require independent statutory boundaries before respect for agency judgment; they do not permit the Board to create jurisdiction Congress withheld. The (d)/(f) text must therefore do the jurisdictional work.
