## Historical comparator and actual changed premises

Historically the Alabama judgment was **reversed and remanded, 5–4**. Stevens's Court opinion was joined in full by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Breyer's concurrence was joined by O'Connor and Souter, supplementing their full Court joins. Scalia dissented with Thomas; Ginsburg dissented with Rehnquist. There were no partial Court-opinion joins and no Marks issue. The judgment rejected the remitted **$2 million**, not merely the original $4 million. It left Alabama to decide initially whether the appropriate remedy was a new punitive trial or an independent appellate determination of an appropriate award.

The historical Court accepted Alabama's correction of the foreign multiplier and nonetheless found the remaining award grossly excessive. It identified legitimate state interests, a qualified territorial limit, fair notice of punishment's severity, and three guideposts. Breyer's additional ground concerned **both** ineffective constraints as applied and gross disproportionality, not the mere absence of hearings or a facial invalidity of Alabama's procedures. Scalia/Thomas rejected substantive amount review; Ginsburg emphasized federalism, state correction, the presumption of legitimacy and the difficulty of federal supervision without a workable measure. Her position was not Scalia's categorical denial of substantive protection.

The actual legal differences do not support converting those rationales into an exclusively unstructured, case-only reasonableness determination:

- **Haslip** remains controlling and unchanged. It provides reasonable constraints, actual review, a relationship to punishment/deterrence and no universal mathematical line. It does not immunize every later Alabama award. Its surviving law can support development of guideposts without treating them as an already enacted ratio.
- **Actual TXO** has only its five-Justice, record-specific procedural holding. Its divided substantive rationales supply no governing potential-harm formula. That requires accurate citation, not abandonment of all substantive development here: historical TXO likewise did not produce a majority adopting a single substantive formula. Stevens's own substantive approach, Kennedy's independent rational-punishment approach and Scalia/Thomas's opposition remain distinct. Actual Souter's narrower dissenting join is a real individual constraint on what can be attributed to his past position; it does not preclude his making a new, supported substantive determination in this case, which the independent freeze already does.
- **Actual Oberg** requires meaningful amount review or an adequate substitute, without a numerical rule. Its broader Court support does not change these Associates' pertinent personal positions. Alabama's actual hearing, review and remittitur differ from Oregon's removal of amount review. Neither the independent model nor this reconciliation treats the existence of review as necessarily proving a substantively lawful result.
- **Bennis, Cooper and the May 13 decisions** supply no punitive-damages test. Bennis's qualified protection of an innocent owner's interest does not resolve punishment for adjudicated fraud; Cooper's defective competency rule does not establish an unperformed BMW appellate review. Noland supplies no constitutional opinion-writing requirement, Armstrong no punitive proof threshold, Brown Group no aggregation rule, and Liquormart no protection for fraudulent nondisclosure or general foreign-transaction immunity. Breyer's and other Associates' joins in those decisions do not justify withholding historically supported BMW grounds merely because the older punitive cases left them open.

Thus no concrete changed premise justifies withholding the historical qualified guideposts, fair-notice analysis or supplemental concurrence grounds from the five reversing Associates. Their objections to an **exclusive checklist, automatic ratio or reconstructed harm** remain valid and are compatible with those grounds. The reconciliation makes affirmative joins rather than leaving substantive scope to later drafting convenience.

## Exact reconciled propositions

**P1 — Governing excessiveness inquiry and fair notice.** A grossly excessive punitive award violates due process in relation to the State's legitimate interests in punishment and deterrence. Identify those interests before assessing the award. Fair notice concerns both conduct subject to punishment and the possible severity of the penalty. This does not negate the adjudicated earlier disclosure duty or import all criminal procedural protections into civil litigation. Wealth does not remove the constitutional limit or supply missing notice.

**P2 — Three guideposts.** Adopt reprehensibility; the relationship between the award and **actual or potential harm** to the plaintiff; and comparison with civil or criminal sanctions authorized or imposed for comparable misconduct. These guideposts structure constitutional review; they are not merely optional observations supporting an unexplained impression of excessiveness. They establish neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a fixed ratio or dollar ceiling. Reprehensibility is especially important. Pure economic harm can justify substantial punishment, including for intentional affirmative misconduct or exploitation of vulnerable victims. Small compensation, egregious conduct, hard-to-detect harm or harm difficult to value can justify larger ratios. No categorical economic-injury immunity or bright line follows.

**P3 — Territorial boundary and evidence.** Alabama may not punish conduct **lawful where it occurred and having no impact on Alabama or its residents**, or use punitive deterrence to impose Alabama's policy on other States. Whether a State may seek to change a tortfeasor's **unlawful** conduct elsewhere remains open. Evidence of foreign transactions can illuminate pattern, intent and reprehensibility; the rule is not an evidentiary exclusion or universal immunity for foreign conduct. Alabama expressly removed foreign transactions from its amount determination. The remaining award is assessed on that basis, not on an invented finding that its judges secretly retained the multiplier.

**P4 — Application.** The remitted $2 million is grossly excessive on this record even after that correction. The purely economic injury, absence of established health/safety danger, character of the nondisclosure, supported actual/potential harm, 500-to-1 disparity and comparison penalties fail to support this magnitude. Variable disclosure laws inform notice and blameworthiness without making this Alabama sale lawful. Accepting the suppression finding and punitive entitlement does not require equating every deliberate omission with the most aggravated fraud. Conversely, repair cost alone is not the plaintiff's loss, and no unsupported nationwide loss or future injury is manufactured. Alabama's $2,000 statutory-sanction comparison is not an exclusive punitive cap.

**P5 — Supplemental constraints rationale.** A procedurally proper award ordinarily deserves a strong presumption of validity. Here the standards **as interpreted and applied** failed significantly to constrain discretion, and the result was grossly disproportionate to legitimate punitive objectives. The two considerations together overcome the presumption. Statutory entitlement does not adequately distinguish all permissible amounts; the harm, culpability and profit criteria must do constraining work. Wealth and litigation incentives cannot supply the missing limit, and inapplicable mitigation factors do not do so. The actual existence of hearings and appellate correction is acknowledged. No facial invalidation of Alabama's system, compulsory economic model, mandatory written calculation or automatic new trial follows. An economic theory can furnish a meaningful constraint when actually supported and applied; a hypothetical theory cannot replace the reasons and record before the state court. Historical comparisons likewise do not create a damages formula.

## Record and remedy safeguards

The preserved petition presents both correction and excessiveness. Historical Ginsburg's discussion of counsel's late multiplier objections helps explain her restraint position, but it is not converted into a new actual waiver ruling or an invented full-charge finding. The independent source boundary remains: no unquoted instruction, concession or missing transcript fact is necessary to these conclusions. The state majority's express correction and the preserved amount challenge suffice. Houston's concurrence does not replace the majority's account of its own calculation.

The estimated 11–14 Alabama transactions can inform pattern and culpability, but are not automatically adjudicated claims, established profits or a new aggregate damages base. The historical concurrence's illustrative arithmetic is not transplanted as a finding that BMW owed $56,000 or that this figure fixes lawful punishment. No question concerning multiple punitive awards for the same conduct is resolved; only this award is adjudicated. Proposed state reforms in the historical dissent's appendix remain proposals, not operative rules. The later Alabama disclosure amendment is not retroactively applied to eliminate the earlier duty.

For **Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer**, retain reversal only insofar as the operative Alabama judgment sustains $2 million in punitive damages, and remand for a lawful punitive disposition. Alabama determines in the first instance whether a new punitive trial or independent appellate redetermination is appropriate. A fresh jury is not automatically required, but more explanation for the same amount on the same record does not cure the adopted substantive excessiveness holding. No substitute sum, compulsory remittitur figure or fixed constitutional ceiling is selected.

For **Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg**, retain affirmance against the presented federal challenge, leaving the remitted $2,004,000 judgment and its ordinary certificate consequences in place. Their agreement in result does not merge their distinct rationales.

For each Associate, the $4,000 compensation, underlying state suppression liability, entitlement to some lawful punitive award, and separate dismissal of BMW AG remain outside alteration.
