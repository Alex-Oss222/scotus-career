## 2. Comparator premises and actual entering law

### A. The record remains sixteen convictions in two distinct classes

The authentic lower opinion establishes thirteen prohibited-machinegun-possession convictions under §922(o) and three unregistered-firearm convictions under §5861(d). Its inventory describes one MAC-10 submachine gun, three Sten-type automatic guns, two M-1 carbines with automatic-fire kits, one .22 pistol with a silencer, five rifles modified into M-16-type automatic guns, and three specified receivers. It does not assign those items to numbered counts, establish separately charged kits, or classify the pistol itself as automatic. No additional weapon, barrel length, magazine restriction or semiautomatic-rifle prosecution is supplied.

Section 922(o)'s actual exceptions remain material: transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the specified federal and state governmental entities; and lawful transfer or lawful possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Manufacture before that date is insufficient. The whole-weapon, frame/receiver, sole-purpose conversion-part, conversion-combination and one-person-controlled assembly-combination branches of §5845(b) retain their separate conditions. No exception or statutory branch is newly found satisfied or unsatisfied for a particular count.

Section 5861(d) concerns a firearm not registered **to** its possessor. It does not universally command the present possessor to file a registration application. The making and transfer taxes, advance approval requirements, transferor's registration function, registry entries and possession prohibition remain distinct. The ordinary transfer tax is $200, with the statutory $5 rate for an “any other weapon”; making is taxed at $200, subject to applicable exemptions. Sections 5812 and 5822 require denial when the specified transfer, receipt, making or possession would violate law. No Hale-specific exemption, refused application, count-specific forbidden post-1986 firearm or otherwise unavailable registration route is established.

### B. Commerce reasoning changes without changing the power disposition

The lower opinion relied on class regulation, legislative references to availability, violent crime and narcotics trafficking, and broad commerce findings. Actual Lopez prevents treating the crime/productivity chain or the fact that guns can affect commerce as a sufficient answer. Actual Harris, American Life League and Bishop/Stokes nevertheless retain the economic-class ground and appropriate inclusion of intrastate conduct in a valid interstate regime.

The frozen §922(o) ground identifies the enacted subsection's direct control of machinegun transfer and supply, including possession as part of control of that stock. It does not rely on a supposed interstate trip by Hale or his particular weapons, and does not insert an absent nexus element. Its direct transfer-and-possession relationship is materially different from the stand-alone school-location offense in Lopez. Conversely, labeling every object marketable would not be enough. This is a supported, bounded new application of the existing commercial-class principles, not a claim that those prior cases already adjudicated §922(o).

All eight Associates' actual joins in Harris and American Life League support that distinction; Bishop/Stokes confirms it without erasing enacted conditions. For O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, their Lopez joins additionally require the refusal to adopt generalized social costs or rewrite the offense. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply controlling Lopez while preserving their own direct commercial-class joins. No independent commerce ground need be decided for §5861(d), whose tax-administration ground suffices.

### C. Love removes the lower court's personal-affiliation premise, not all regulation

The lower majority rejected military utility as automatic immunity and then required a relationship between **this possessor's** keeping and a militia. Its discussion tested membership in a military organization or preparation for a military career; its application relied on Hale's failure to claim or prove such a relationship. Footnote 4 expressly acknowledged military character and the capacity of most weapons to injure groups. The lower judgment therefore cannot be recharacterized as a finding that the arms lacked military utility.

Love's Part III-A now establishes personal holders and rejects formal enrollment or current service as a prerequisite. The surviving Miller inquiry concerns the actual weapon and record. Love does not allow the removed affiliation condition to return merely under a demand for personal military preparation. Its incorporation holding is not needed to apply the guarantee to a federal statute; its limited acquisition holding likewise does not itself decide a machinegun prohibition. The controlling Love application remained reserved. Its noncontrolling affirmative-infringement position supplies no Court rule here.

The authentic Miller opinion reversed a judgment sustaining a demurrer and quashing an indictment, then remanded. It did not affirm a conviction, automatically protect every military-capable object, or exclude arms because of their military character. Sonzinsky's genuine-tax holding likewise is not an arms classification. The freeze correctly retains the difference between constitutional coverage and permissible regulation.

An independent affirmance remains a serious lawful alternative: automatic-fire capability and the statutory exceptions can matter to a supported regulation analysis, and rejecting Hale's absolute-immunity premise is correct. Beam's concurrence confirms that a judge can reject the majority's broad premise while agreeing with the result. But neither Beam's conclusion nor the lower majority's group-harm observation supplies a developed independent constitutional rule and application sustaining this particular prohibition under Love. Reconciliation finds no source-supported basis to substitute an unadopted weapons classification, scrutiny tier, historical-regulation rule or categorical public-safety rule for the remand.

The remand rests on this **identified change in governing law and the lower court's material use of the displaced prerequisite**. Missing retrievable files are not its ground. Reconsideration may use count and object determinations already established in the trial record; it does not presume that the lower courts failed to make them. The freeze's direction to determine the actual object and §5845(b) basis means to identify and apply the existing count-specific premises as necessary to decide the preserved claim. It creates no new element, freestanding fact-finding checklist, universal hearing duty or automatic reopening of an unchallenged statutory determination. Ordinary further proceedings are permitted only as the actual record and governing inquiry require.

### D. Registration has an independent answer to the presented arms objection

The distinct registration convictions do not depend on deciding whether §922(o)'s possession prohibition ultimately infringes protected keeping. Hale's proposed military-utility exemption supplies no right to bypass identification and registration in the lawful making-and-transfer tax system. Miller's actual NFA setting, Sonzinsky's revenue-administration principle and Freed's preserved registration structure support the bounded ground; Love recognizes real regulatory limits while leaving weapon coverage distinct.

A prohibition on possession cannot be relabeled harmless recordkeeping, and a prosecution for failing to obtain legally unavailable approval would raise a different question. Neither is silently resolved by this registration affirmance. The neutral preflight expressly qualifies the original packet's registration-impossibility contention: it is a potential theory, not a verified Hale-specific refusal or a recorded appellate concession. No individual three-count predicate supplies that different claim, and no exploratory remand is ordered to construct one. This preserves, rather than assumes, the open question.

### E. White does not decide these certificates automatically

The lower opinion records two concessions: Rule 803(10)'s firmly rooted character and Rule 902(1) authentication. It actually adjudicates confrontation. Those concessions therefore do not establish waiver of the constitutional claim, registry infallibility, or complete substantive proof of every element.

White Part I removed a production/unavailability prerequisite for the **accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions**, because their settings supply evidentiary value later testimony cannot recreate. White Part II rejected a categorical formal-affidavit-only boundary; it did not hold that every affidavit is admissible. Roberts, Inadi, Wright, Coy and Craig retain their distinct work. Applying that received framework here requires its own explanation because a registry search can be described by a live witness.

The freeze supplies that explanation at a defensible scope: the conceded rooted negative-public-record exception, accepted authentication, described diligent search and absence of a regularly maintained record support receipt without a separate production or unavailability gate. The certificate's reported function is search and nonentry, not reconstruction of possession or expert classification of the weapons. Even assuming it was prepared for use in this prosecution, that function and the accepted exception remain the grounds; precise preparation timing is not an additional factual finding. Availability is immaterial under the evidence rule, but the evidence rule alone does not decide the constitutional question.

The contrary path would require live search testimony or an adequate substitute because the prepared assertion helps establish an offense element and can be tested at trial. That is a meaningful objection, not answered by treating the absent specialist as a spontaneous declarant. The retained exception framework and this accepted foundation nevertheless support the bounded admission disposition. No specific defective search, altered entry or pertinent contemporary omission is established by the reported challenge. The ruling does not shift the Government's proof burden, foreclose competent rebuttal, turn authentication into reliability or find harmlessness.

Thomas's White concurrence, joined by Scalia, agrees with the exception-based judgment and analysis but leaves reconsideration of formalized testimonial materials open. Both join the present bounded application without adopting a new categorical test or surrendering that reservation. Their position is not that a prepared official affidavit falls outside confrontation. Nor does their earlier reservation itself require exclusion of every formal document. Final text must preserve this scope; a separate writing can serve that function if needed, but this stage assigns none.

The description “application by Hale” is not silently changed to proof of every possible registration route. Sections 5812 and 5841 distinguish registration to a transferee from an application by that transferee. The summary does not establish that the search was confined to personally submitted applications, however, and the actual affidavits and full proof are absent. That distinction limits a new sufficiency or certificate-wording adjudication; it supplies no present finding of deficient proof or ground for an evidentiary remand.

### F. Ordinary admission and judicial notice remain separate

The 1979 Senate excerpts were excluded as hearsay and too old to be reliable, and judicial notice of their contents was separately refused. Both rulings were reviewed below for abuse of discretion. Official publication does not make the truth of contested criticism indisputable under Rule 201. Notice that the hearing occurred differs from notice that its assertions were true or established the registry's condition in 1991.

Ordinary exclusion is sustained within the adjudicated presentation because no identified passage, relevant nonhearsay purpose, applicable exception, foundation or bearing on the searched registry is supplied that demonstrates the reported ruling was erroneous. This does not create a categorical age bar or an immunity from reliability challenges. Relevance and hearsay remain independent requirements; a Rule 201 explanation alone could not sustain ordinary exclusion. Bishop/Stokes's eight-Associate evidentiary ground confirms only that missing detail in an appellate report is not an affirmative finding that the lower court omitted the required review. Its medical and physical-evidence rules do no work here.
