## Governing distinctions

The frozen commitments are accepted at their stated bounded level. Hazelwood v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260, 270–276 (1988), preserves substantial authority over supervised curricular work, subject matter, academic method, maturity and quality. Its majority includes Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia. Its concrete tolerance of a reasonable scheduling mistake supports ordinary educational administration; it does not confer immunity whenever an educator supplies an asserted academic explanation. Its public-attribution rationale cannot be assumed for a paper submitted solely to the teacher for a grade. Applying its pedagogical principle here is an application to this assignment, not a finding that every student paper is school-endorsed speech or a forum open to every viewpoint.

Tinker's protection of student expression does not entitle Settle to replace a genuine research exercise with whatever expression she wishes. Conversely, the label “topic selection” cannot by itself put otherwise supported unconstitutional discrimination beyond judicial review. Epperson, 393 U.S. 97, 103–107, preserves curricular control while rejecting constitutional immunity for that control, and distinguishes objective academic study of religion from governmental religious instruction. Its actual religion-clause holding is not converted into a new cause of action in this free-speech review.

The operative Standards entry **Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits**, with present operation effective June 29, 1995 and the retained Lamb's Chapel components of June 7, 1993, preserves genuine program boundaries and forbids exclusion of an otherwise admitted religious perspective in the programs those decisions address. Actual Good News, December 11, 1995, applies that distinction to its after-school moral-development forum. Those decisions inform the difference between genuine assignment requirements and a categorical religious exclusion; none has already classified this graded assignment as a forum or ordered acceptance of every religious topic.

The operative **Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure** entry, effective Lee, June 24, 1992, addresses official worship and its separate substantial-participation-pressure ground. Actual Sherman, February 12, 1996, preserves those limits while distinguishing its civic Pledge. Academic consideration of a religious subject is not categorically forbidden by either decision. That legal point addresses the teacher's asserted justification; it neither decides an ungranted Establishment claim nor declares every proposed form of classroom devotion permissible.

The operative **Public-employee speech and the employer's mistaken factual account** entry, effective actual Waters, May 31, 1994, contains distinct limited agreements. A genuine, reasonable account of legally disciplinable speech supplies its seven-Justice safe harbor; an account not genuinely relied on does not. The full objective-investigation framework has four adherents, and Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas expressly reject an independent investigation duty. The separate six-Justice agreement about unreasonable false accounts does not turn the two overlaps into a unified methodology. Here Waters informs the distinction between a possible permissible reason and actual cause; the public-employment balance and complete inquiry framework are not transplanted to school assignments.

Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274, 283–287, preserves the separate causal questions. The claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role. Defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without it. The ability to identify a lawful reason is not proof that the decision would have been the same. Equally, proof that an impermissible consideration played a part does not automatically defeat a properly established same-decision defense or establish entitlement to relief. These propositions preserve the frozen commitments' existing ground rather than introduce a new decisional path.

## The lower-court alternatives and the supported record

The Sixth Circuit majority recognized a limit on pretextual punishment for religion and other forbidden considerations, but treated all six explanations as within academic discretion, including possibly mistaken reasons, and found no genuine motive dispute. Its no-dispute conclusion is the summary-judgment ruling being reviewed, not a trial finding that fixes actual motivation. The majority's transfiguration illustration is not recovered content from either outline. The majority also described Hazelwood's newspaper as a kind of open forum; Batchelder correctly noted that Hazelwood itself rejected public-forum status. No such classification error enters these commitments.

Batchelder's concurrence offers the strongest distinct affirmance rationale. A teacher controls a research topic; an assignment requiring objective research differs from an opinion exercise, and an inadequately explained assignment need not confer a constitutional right to substitute another topic. She accepts that some later explanations were unconvincing but treats them as excuses for an instructional error rather than suppression of expression. She rejects a general classroom ban on religious content. Her conclusion that the permitted comparator topics were properly distinguished is also reasoning supporting summary judgment, not an independent trial finding binding this review.

That narrower affirmance path receives substantial weight, particularly for Stevens's actual-activity distinction and the curricular deference of O'Connor and Scalia. It does not sustain this judgment at the frozen commitments' scope because the factual characterization on which it depends is materially disputed. The teacher's categorical legal explanation, the quoted account of mixed reasons, the original-choice answer and the express alternative-topic opportunity permit an inference of religious exclusion that cannot be resolved merely by relabeling the decision academic. This is not a rule that every weak explanation creates a constitutional case or that every research topic receives protection.

## Accepted disposition, remedy and limits

The reconciled commitments retain **vacatur of the Sixth Circuit's judgment and remand**, with the reviewed free-speech summary judgment set aside. The sufficient shared rule is confined to this setting: genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements remain permissible, but identifying a possible pedagogical justification does not establish entitlement to summary judgment when supported evidence permits a material inference that the work was rejected because of its religious perspective rather than legitimate application of those requirements. A sincere belief that law categorically forbids religious issues is not itself a legitimate educational ground.

This decision path neither determines the correct grade nor requires acceptance of either outline, reading or crediting the completed paper, removal of the zero, or an award of damages. It does not impose viewpoint neutrality on all curricular selection or forbid schools to define the knowledge, method or perspective taught. Objective study may be distinguished from personal testimony or devotional advocacy. Genuine academic judgments may be imperfect without becoming unconstitutional. The remand directs ordinary adjudication under those limits; it does not guarantee a trial on every issue or prevent another legally sufficient disposition on an actually available ground.

Graduation does not establish mootness of every claim where the petition reports damages and continuing academic-record relief. It does not itself establish entitlement to a broad injunction for future classes. The pleaded claims, remaining relief, individual and official capacity, municipal attribution and qualified immunity remain distinct. The district did not decide immunity; respondents' presumption of abandonment is not a conceded waiver. Conduct-date notice concerns March–April 1991, so later actual decisions cannot themselves establish what an individual defendant then should have known. No immunity award or denial, municipal liability finding, damages amount or new religion-clause or equal-protection claim is supplied.

No precise missing meeting date is repaired. The district and opposition's March 28 contact/March 27 meeting sequence remains unresolved; the April 1 board-review account and April 3 outline date are not merged. No additional original approval, permission request, comparator change history, finding of personal hostility, concession or outline text is created. Research-source absence is not litigant default.
