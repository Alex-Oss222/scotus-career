## What the new law establishes

Colorado's Part I has six express joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. It protects the genuinely independent party advertisement against the Senate-election expenditure ceiling and rejects party status alone as the candidate-connection predicate for the alternative contribution theory. It preserves the contribution/expenditure distinction and does not invalidate ordinary contribution channels, authorize every coordinated party expenditure, require proof of an actual bribe for every preventive rule, or adopt a general administrative-deference rule.

O'Connor's complete independent-expenditure explanation and proposed separate treatment of the counterclaim have only Souter and Breyer's additional joins. Kennedy's proposed protection of party-authored coordinated expression has Scalia and Thomas's joins. Neither three-Justice framework becomes controlling merely because all six protect the independent advertisement. The treatment of the remaining counterclaim supplies no six-Justice general rule requiring constitutional avoidance, separate proceedings or narrow relief in other cases.

Stevens's dissent, joined by Ginsburg and the Chief Justice, accepts the advertisement's independence but relies on the official party's distinctive institutional contribution-channel risk. It does not rely on equalization, actual coordination, or a general license to burden every independent speaker. This is published, noncontrolling evidence of those Justices' reasoning, not an added holding. Its institutional scope matters to any proposed analogy.
