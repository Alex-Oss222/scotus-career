## 1. Historical opinion verified in full

The [official U.S. Reports opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-479.pdf), independently checked against [Cornell's syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-83.ZS.html) and [opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-83.ZO.html), establishes **unanimous reversal**, an **opinion of the Court by O'Connor**, and **no concurrence, dissent, partial join or nonparticipation**. Each of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer historically joined the complete opinion. Historical authorship is evidence about that writing, not an assignment instruction for assembly.

Full coverage comprises the official ten pages, 516 U.S. 479–488: syllabus, counsel and amicus listings with their continuation note, Parts I–II, the sole substantive opinion footnote reproducing the relevant statutory language at 482, and the conclusion. There is no separate Justice writing or additional substantive footnote left unexamined. The syllabus is used for lineup and cross-checking, not as a substitute for the opinion.

- **Both grounds:** At 484, the opinion identifies two requirements that defeat the action. The remedial discussion at 484–485 construes restraint and other necessary action as prohibitory and affirmative abatement powers, not a private past-cleanup-cost remedy. The comparison with CERCLA's express cost-recovery and contribution provisions reinforces that reading. At 485–486, the opinion separately requires a presently existing threat when the action begins, even though its impact may occur later. Its conclusion at 488 repeats both defects. This is not a timing-only decision.
- **The material scope issue:** At 487, the opinion specifically describes the United States' proposed recovery of money already spent on cleanup in a properly brought suit while the waste still poses an imminent endangerment. It then rejects the inference of additional private remedies from general equitable power, relying on RCRA's enumerated relief, the CERCLA comparison, Sea Clammers and Transamerica. Merely satisfying the present-threat condition does not answer that independent remedial objection.
- **Two express reservations:** At 488 the Court does not consider an injunction requiring another party to pay cleanup costs **arising after a citizen suit has been properly commenced**, **or otherwise recovery of cleanup costs paid out after invocation of RCRA's statutory process**. Both branches must be preserved. The second phrase is not silently reduced to filing, and the opinion supplies no complete definition of when statutory-process invocation occurs or how every notice-stage expense is classified.
- **Disposition:** The historical decretal sentence reverses the Ninth Circuit. It states no monetary award, liability finding, discovery entitlement or special mandate date. The reconciled return for proceedings consistent with rejection of this pleaded RCRA claim implements that disposition; it does not attribute a detailed historical remand instruction that the opinion did not contain.

The frozen commitments' reservation of every proper-action monetary incident, expressly including emergency expenses incurred before filing while danger remains, therefore cannot all be treated as a historical match. The historical Court considered and rejected the general premise supporting that additional reservation. Conversely, its opinion does not establish that every monetary order is legal damages or that its two expressly reserved categories are forbidden.

## 4. Final components, grounds, joins and limits for assembly

**J — Disposition.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each support reversal of the Ninth Circuit and return for disposition consistent with rejection of KFC's pleaded §6972(a)(1)(B) claim. Its order permitting further proceedings on that theory cannot stand. No immediate payment, discovery, trial, equitable accounting or new liability finding is directed. This is a federal statutory disposition, not adjudication of independent state-law entitlement. The signed district order's dismissal with prejudice and the contrary docket label remain the neutral packet's identified discrepancy; the latter does not become a new remedial ruling.

**T — Independently sufficient filing-time ground.** Section 6972(a)(1)(B) requires waste that may present an imminent and substantial endangerment when the citizen action begins. Past contribution can create a present threat, and the threat's physical consequences need not occur immediately. The statute does not cover an allegation only that the waste may have presented such a danger before the entire threat was eliminated. KFC's completed prefiling cleanup and absence of alleged remaining endangerment defeat its complaint. Continuing off-site disposal expense alone is not a pleaded continuing threat. This statutory defect is not an Article III injury holding or an importation of summary-judgment proof requirements into Rule 12(b)(6).

**R — Independently sufficient remedial ground, at its reconciled scope.** Section 6972(a)'s authority to restrain a contributor and order other necessary action permits prohibitory and affirmative abatement relief. It does not supply a private reimbursement action for a past cleanup effort, whether the demand is called damages or equitable restitution. This excludes KFC's completed-cleanup demand independently of T. The text, RCRA's specified enforcement arrangements and CERCLA's contrasting express cost provisions also defeat the proposed general authority to reimburse earlier cleanup merely because a plaintiff later brings a proper citizen suit with an endangerment remaining. An emergency label and the equitable character claimed for a payment do not themselves supply that missing statutory remedy. This rule remains subject to the exact unresolved categories in L1; it must not be restated as a prohibition on every monetary equitable order or every expense preceding filing.

**L — Adopted boundaries and unresolved questions.** All eight expressly join these limits as part of T and R:

1. **Two reserved cost categories:** No determination whether a private plaintiff may obtain an injunction requiring another party to pay cleanup costs arising after a citizen action has properly commenced, **or otherwise recover cleanup costs paid after invocation of RCRA's statutory process**. Neither category is affirmatively authorized here. No rule equates every statutory-process invocation with filing, decides whether a particular notice-stage expenditure falls within the second category, or treats remaining danger alone as sufficient to reserve all earlier emergency expenses.
2. **Present danger and past conduct remain distinct:** Partial abatement does not defeat prospective statutory relief if a qualifying threat remains and the other prerequisites are satisfied. That proposition does not establish past-cost reimbursement. Delayed harm can constitute a presently existing threat; immediate actual injury is unnecessary.
3. **Equity and distinct statutory powers:** No holding that all restitution is legal damages, no general repudiation of Porter/Hecht/Franklin, and no determination of EPA reimbursement under §6973. The structural inference concerns this private RCRA remedy. The Restatement conditions urged by the Government are not adopted as new RCRA elements. The §6972(f) saving clause preserves otherwise existing rights without creating a cause or guaranteeing recovery.
4. **Actual statutory conditions control:** The endangerment route ordinarily requires ninety days after notice to EPA, the affected State and alleged contributors. An action respecting a qualifying subchapter-III violation may commence immediately **after notification**; no general emergency waiver of notification is created. Federal and state intervention bars retain the precise qualifying actions, abatement purpose, diligent-prosecution/remediation requirements and other conditions reproduced in the validated packet, including the scope-and-duration limit for the specified administrative order. No unspecified local cleanup direction or government awareness establishes a bar. The historical opinion's compressed account does not displace these full terms; no modern timing override is added.
5. **Costs, fees and CERCLA are separate:** No RCRA limitations period, NCP burden or universal unavailability of laches is invented from the CERCLA comparison. Section 6972(e)'s discretionary litigation-cost provision is distinct from cleanup reimbursement; no fee entitlement or amount is decided. CERCLA's petroleum exclusion is not imported into RCRA and no universal petroleum-mixture classification is reached.
6. **Posture remains bounded:** Allegations of contribution, qualifying waste, responsibility, knowledge and expenditure are not findings. No merits determination of unjust enrichment or reasonableness of the claimed sum follows. Notice compliance was not an appellate issue, and the original complaint's omission does not supply a new ground against the amended pleading. The state order and unconsummated voluntary-dismissal request establish neither preclusion nor mootness. No special mandate date is supplied.

**Proposition-level coalition:** J, T, R and L each have the same eight direct joins: **Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer**. T and R are separate sufficient grounds, not interchangeable votes aggregated into one rationale. No Associate supports only the judgment, no partial join remains, and no separate opinion is required by these commitments. Any final opinion must state both grounds and the actual limits; a timing-only opinion or a renewed blanket emergency-prefiling reservation would materially change these commitments.
