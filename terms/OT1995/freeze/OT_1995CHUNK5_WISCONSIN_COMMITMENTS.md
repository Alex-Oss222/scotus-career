## Governing law and supported scope

The exact operative Standards are **Constitutional review of interstate equal-proportions apportionment**, effective Montana, March 31, 1992; **Counting temporarily overseas federal personnel with enduring state ties** and **Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination**, effective Franklin, June 26, 1992; **An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision**, effective Boston & Maine, March 25, 1992; and **Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing**, effective MCI, June 17, 1994. Their current wording and authority appear in [Standards and Tests](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md). The corresponding [administrative](../../../state/holdings/administrative-law.md), [election](../../../state/holdings/election-law.md) and [federal-courts](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md) Holdings preserve their qualifications.

Montana permits constitutional adjudication and competing legitimate measures of interstate equality. It does not approve every population method. Franklin makes the completed census determination reviewable under §704 and requires actual, contemporaneous, reasonably discernible reasons under State Farm and Bowman; its residence holding does not decide PES adjustment. Boston & Maine permits recognition of an actual agency ground, never invention of one. MCI enforces the particular delegation before deference and creates no general sampling prohibition. Wesberry, Kirkpatrick and Karcher retain their intrastate population-equality requirements. Extending Karcher's burden to this census methodology dispute is the question to decide, not an existing rule.

Article I, §2 and Fourteenth Amendment §2 require enumeration and population-based representation. Section 141(a) assigns census form and content to the Secretary and expressly mentions sampling procedures and special surveys; its distinct reporting, tabulation and mid-decade provisions remain as set out in the neutral packet. Section 195 provides: “Except for the determination of population for purposes of apportionment of Representatives in Congress among the several States, the Secretary shall, if he considers it feasible, authorize the use of the statistical method known as ‘sampling’ in carrying out the provisions of this title.” The exception, mandatory language and feasibility qualification do different work. This model decides neither disputed construction of that exception nor the separate §141(e)(2) mid-decade prohibition's application to another census.

Review proceeds in the submitted granted-merits channel under §1254(1), without importing an additional historical grant or question. All nine members of the supplied roster participated at argument and remain seated; no recusal is established. There is a quorum and a five-vote Court judgment threshold. The eight Associate commitments below are not a nine-Justice Court tally.

## Committed propositions and judgment

### P1. Affirmative standing and constitutional reviewability

Reach the presented constitutional census challenge. The relevant standing basis is the California individual voters' own continuing representational injury, not a general interest in accurate government, municipal parens patriae authority or Arizona's superseded projected seat gain. John Mack and Olga Morales are specifically identified as California/Los Angeles voters in complaint paragraphs 20–21; the reproduced appellate docket and Second Circuit caption independently identify them as appellants. The federal merits brief acknowledges that California individual voters appealed. Both sides' later-adjustment accounts identify the concrete Wisconsin-to-California seat consequence. These sources support the individuals' connection to the asserted continuing allocation injury and the causal role of the challenged census determination.

This determination uses the identified, uncontroverted voter connection in the supplied party and appeal materials together with the concrete allocation evidence; it does not convert the complaint into trial testimony or find independently verified unchanged residence through March 1996. The federal standing argument supplied here disputes the individuals' legal entitlement to proceed without the State, rather than supplying a contrary personal-status fact. The voters assert their own representation interest recognized by Wesberry and the actual Montana/Franklin holdings. Even if California itself did not appeal, a State's decision to accept a judgment does not surrender the individual appellants' separate voting claim. The discrepancy between the Lungren docket label and California's caption/brief treatment therefore need not be resolved.

Under Montana and Franklin, authoritative relief addressing the legally responsible census determination has a substantial likelihood of redressing this ongoing allocation injury through the statutory sequence. The corrected estimate supplies a concrete potential California gain; it is not a judicial finding that a selected dataset must now be adopted. Successful review could remove the alleged legal obstacle to reconsideration without guaranteeing the Secretary's ultimate lawful choice or ordering the President. Completion of initial transmission is not itself mootness. This is an affirmative, record-specific standing conclusion, not assumed jurisdiction because the merits fail. No standing determination is necessary for every other plaintiff seeking the same relief, and no funding theory is used to confer someone else's voting rights.

### P2. Constitutional standard

For the presented choice whether to supplement this nationwide enumeration with the proposed statistical adjustment, require a reasonable relationship between the Secretary's actual methodological decision and the constitutional tasks of enumerating the population and securing population-based representation. Courts independently enforce that constitutional boundary while allowing the methodological judgment entrusted to the census decisionmaker. A genuine effort to enumerate, coupled with a reasoned choice addressing geographic accuracy and the comparative uncertainties of available methods, does not become unconstitutional merely because another method would improve some measures of accuracy.

Reject the Second Circuit's requirement that nonadjustment further a legitimate governmental objective and be **essential** to achieving it. Intrastate districting ordinarily starts with an accepted population dataset and concerns avoidable line-drawing disparities. This controversy concerns the reliability and geographic distribution of competing estimates of the population itself. Montana's indivisible-seat problem is not identical, but its refusal to constitutionalize one legitimate equality metric and Franklin's treatment of supported counting judgments preclude automatic transplantation of Karcher's burden. No percentage tolerance, presumption that enumeration is always superior, or immunity for census decisions follows.

The representation claim does not fail merely for lack of a discriminatory-purpose finding. Washington v. Davis and Arlington Heights require purpose for an ordinary racial-discrimination claim; they do not erase the separate population-representation question. Conversely, acknowledged differential impact does not itself establish that claim or trigger the panel's essentiality burden. Deliberate exclusion, an unsupported population connection, or a method unrelated to the constitutional enumeration task is not approved by this standard.

### P3. Application to the actual nonadjustment decision

The preserved constitutional challenge fails on this record. The extensive enumeration and coverage efforts, including measures directed at hard-to-count populations, establish a genuine counting effort. They do not erase the differential undercount. The Secretary's contemporaneous explanation addresses the central competing issue: whether the proposed estimates would reliably improve geographic shares, including at smaller levels, rather than only the national total. Residual geographic heterogeneity, disputed variance assumptions and sensitivity to reasonable alternative smoothing methods bear directly on that issue. The division among advisers reinforces that these were actual methodological disputes; neither the advisers' majority nor the Secretary's position is conclusive by title or head count.

Respondents' strongest objection is substantive: an alternative adjustment can be better than the proposed adjustment while both remain better than nonadjustment, and reducing a differential undercount can improve distribution as well as totals. The constitutional conclusion does not deny either possibility. It rests on the record-supported uncertainty about the comparative geographic estimates and the Secretary's explanation of why that uncertainty mattered. The district court's finding of improvement “for most purposes” does not establish reliable superiority for every relevant geographic measure or eliminate the methodological choice. The lower court's preferred result under de novo review is not a finding that the actual choice lacked the required constitutional relationship.

The decision does not find actual manipulation of the PES, bad faith, inevitable harm to participation, or a failure to produce adjusted figures by the deadline. It does not make speculative future incentives or administrative disruption a free-standing constitutional justification for a materially unsupported count. The explanation is assessed with its actual qualifications and statistical reasons. Later corrected figures inform the continuing injury and requested relief; they are not substituted for the Secretary's July 1991 information. No exact disputed loss-function percentage or expert concession is made dispositive.

### P4. APA availability remains; no newly opened APA merits claim

Franklin governs reviewability of this completed census determination. Its actual-reasons and relevant-factors requirements remain intact. The Secretary receives no census exemption from State Farm, Bowman or Chenery, and the district court's “beyond the pale” phrase is not adopted as a new review standard.

Availability of APA review does not establish preservation of a separate APA merits challenge. The inspected federal brief repeatedly identifies the district court's ordinary APA determination as unchallenged on appeal; respondents defend independent constitutional scrutiny and the constitutional remand. On this bounded presentation the model decides that constitutional issue and supplies no fresh APA remand or independent Supreme Court APA-merits holding. It does not purport to adjudicate the complete appellate papers or announce an all-claims forfeiture ruling. Any later proposal to revive a distinct APA claim requires the packet's bounded appellate-preservation restoration before changing these commitments. The surviving lower APA disposition is not reopened by the constitutional ruling.

### P5. Reserve the categorical §195 dispute

Do not reach the States' categorical sampling-bar theory. Respondents' Rule 14.1(a) objection is live, and its resolution requires the actual bounded petition/question material if that theory becomes necessary. The preserved constitutional challenge can be rejected even assuming the proposed adjustment was legally available; permission does not entail constitutional compulsion. Neither the federal petitioners' express concession that sampling is not categorically barred nor the States' contrary view becomes a Court holding.

MCI requires statutory authority before deference but does not require resolving an unnecessary and disputedly presented alternative ground here. The Secretary expressly stated that legal considerations did not supply his decisional basis. The States' statutory-bar theory therefore cannot be attributed to him under Boston & Maine. This reservation neither endorses the Second Circuit's statutory construction nor silently rejects it on the merits. No hypothetical constitutional challenge to a sampling prohibition is reached.

### P6. Disposition and bounded remedy

In **each of Nos. 94-1614, 94-1631 and 94-1985**, reverse the Second Circuit's judgment vacating the district court's nonadjustment determination and remand through that court for disposition consistent with rejection of the preserved constitutional challenge and restoration of judgment for petitioners on that challenge. The remand implements this legal conclusion; it is not another opportunity to justify nonadjustment under the rejected essentiality burden, a new technical trial or a direction to reopen the unchallenged ordinary APA determination.

Order no statistical adjustment, dataset selection, seat transfer, presidential injunction, funding redistribution or new redistricting. Preserve the distinct tape-disclosure/protective-order ruling outside the submitted adjustment review. The proposed mandate is limited to the reviewed census challenge and supplies no blanket disposition of unrelated or genuinely unreviewed matters. No special mandate date or continuing Supreme Court supervision is prescribed.

## Justice-specific commitments

Each Justice below commits to **reversal and the P6 remand in all three dockets**, reaches the merits under P1, and directly joins P2–P3 at the stated operative level. The separate entries identify why the strongest different lawful path does not carry that Justice, and preserve the material limits on APA and statutory joins. Prior public positions are evidence of legal commitments, not predetermined Wisconsin votes.

### Stevens

Stevens joins P1–P6. His own unanimous [Montana opinion](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK9.md) both vindicates judicial enforcement of representation rights and refuses to make one legitimate comparison constitutionally exclusive. His full [Franklin join](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK14.md), including the APA merits, shows that meaningful census review can sustain an explained choice among imperfect datasets. Those are the principal grounds for his constitutional-standard and application joins here.

The strongest alternative is affirmance of the panel's remand: feasible reduction of minority undercount and improved representation deserve an actual answer. He finds that answer in the Secretary's comparative geographic-accuracy discussion, not in the mere existence of delegated discretion. The lower “most purposes” finding leaves the decisive methodological uncertainty intact; it does not warrant essentiality review. His Boston & Maine join supports reliance on actual discernible reasons, not a court-invented §195 rationale. He rejects an APA remand without a preserved APA claim and reserves the States' statutory construction under P5. His Varity Part IV join supports attention to actual presentation but supplies no ruling about these petitions. He would withhold assent from any opinion treating the differential undercount as irrelevant, negating APA availability, or adjudicating every plaintiff's standing unnecessarily. No distinct separate-writing ground is needed if P1–P6 are preserved.

### O'Connor

O'Connor joins P1–P6. Her Montana join and Franklin constitutional join support judicial review without importing the intrastate burden into an uncertain population estimate. Her [Franklin concurrence](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK14.md) opposed APA finality, but that position did not prevail. She applies the controlling availability rule here without abandoning the previously published objection or converting it into a new jurisdictional dismissal.

For her, the strongest contrary path is a focused remand requiring a constitutionally adequate explanation despite statutory discretion. It fails because the detailed contemporaneous analysis already gives the relevant explanation, whereas the appellate essentiality requirement demands more than the constitutional counting obligation. Her [Boston & Maine join](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK7.md) permits recognition of the agency's real reasoning without a mandatory restatement. The present outcome does not require endorsing every future-participation prediction or the district court's shorthand APA language. She does not join a renewed APA merits ruling and will not use statutory finality to negate the continuing voting injury recognized in Montana/Franklin. Her P5 join reserves §195, rather than treating her Varity statutory-structure dissent as a census construction. She joins the limited mandate and would oppose a new accuracy hearing lacking identified necessary work. A reminder of her existing Franklin reservation may accompany a join, but supplies no different disposition or controlling rule.

### Scalia

Scalia joins P1–P6. His [Franklin concurrence](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK14.md) expressly applied Montana's actual declaratory-redress holding while reserving coercive presidential relief. That commitment supports P1 for the identified individual voting injury; it does not permit substituting a generalized accuracy interest for concrete injury. The California appellants' own interest makes the unresolved State-status discrepancy immaterial to this threshold conclusion.

The strongest statutory alternative is to resolve the §195 exception before constitutional review, consistent with his [MCI join](../../OT1993/output/OT_1993CHUNK7.md). But a serious textual argument does not establish its presentation, and rejecting the asserted constitutional duty even on the assumption of permission makes a broad bar unnecessary. He therefore reserves it rather than inventing a question or assigning the States' ground to the Secretary. On the constitutional issue his Montana/Franklin joins support P2's distinct treatment of census estimation; the actual efforts and geographic explanation satisfy that standard. The panel's correction of a perceived inferior estimate does not justify shifting to an essentiality burden. His Boston & Maine join supports the actual-ground boundary. He retains his earlier agreement with O'Connor on APA finality as a noncontrolling position, applies Franklin as governing law, and does not revive APA merits. He refuses a presidential injunction, automatic statistical-adjustment duty or a rule that all counting decisions escape review.

### Kennedy

Kennedy joins P1–P6. His full Franklin join supports both affirmative standing and completed-agency review. His own [Boston & Maine opinion](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK7.md) distinguishes adequate discernible reasoning from appellate invention. Those commitments make the Secretary's actual distributive-accuracy explanation, rather than institutional status alone, central to his vote.

The strongest competing path is a constitutional remand because the acknowledged undercount and the district court's findings raise a serious representation problem. He rejects it because the remaining disagreement concerns the comparative reliability of estimates, already addressed by the agency, rather than an unexplained abandonment of the constitutional counting task. Montana and his Franklin constitutional join allow that judgment without constitutionalizing a particular alternative. He would not join a formulation under which any conceivable post hoc explanation sustains a census choice. His MCI join also excludes deference beyond a statutory delegation, but P5 supplies no new delegation holding; it reserves an unnecessary disputedly presented question. P4 preserves Franklin without adjudicating an unestablished separate APA appeal. His Varity presentation join reinforces that separation. He supports judgment for petitioners on the reviewed constitutional claim and no unrequested expansion into the tape ruling, funding remedies or coercive presidential relief.

### Souter

Souter joins P1–P6. His Montana and complete Franklin joins preserve both constitutional review and reasoned administrative choice. His Boston & Maine join rejects remanding solely to obtain clearer wording when the actual decisional path is established. His [MCI dissent join](../../OT1993/output/OT_1993CHUNK7.md) treats express methodological flexibility in its statutory context; it does not excuse agency action outside governing law.

His strongest alternative is vacatur for application of a correct constitutional standard, leaving initial evaluation to the courts below. He finds no necessary unresolved factual task for that remand: the enumeration efforts, actual geographic concerns, adviser disagreement and lower “most purposes” finding are supplied and can be accepted without choosing a technically best estimator. P2 explains why those facts do not trigger the panel's essentiality requirement, and P3 resolves their constitutional consequence. He would withhold a join from a decision saying that APA reasonableness automatically answers every constitutional claim. P4 instead preserves the distinction and declines a fresh APA disposition for lack of an established preserved claim. His own Varity Part IV supplies a current presentation analogue, not a decision that §195 was omitted here. He reserves that statutory question and joins the implementation-only remand. His Meghrig opinion's remedy discipline does not create a new census remedy bar; the absence of a proved constitutional violation supplies the present reason not to compel adjustment.

### Thomas

Thomas joins P1–P6. His Montana and Franklin constitutional/standing joins distinguish reviewability from a judicial choice of the preferred count. His agreement with O'Connor's Franklin finality objection remains noncontrolling; he applies the governing decision rather than dismissing this case by repeating the rejected rule.

His strongest alternate ground is the §195 bar, in light of his MCI insistence on enacted limits and his recent Varity statutory-structure position. The present commitments do not choose that interpretation: the Rule 14 objection is unresolved, and permission alone cannot establish the constitutional duty respondents assert. He reserves the text question under P5. His [Boston & Maine dissent join](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK7.md) makes the actual-agency-ground objection especially important. Here the Secretary himself supplied extensive statistical and geographic reasons; the model does not cure missing reasoning with counsel's sampling-ban theory. That difference supports P3 without retreat from the explanation principle. The contrary constitutional-remand path would require treating selection among disputed estimates as failure of a genuine counting effort, despite the supplied record; he rejects that inference and the essentiality burden. He joins no new APA merits determination, generic exemption from review or presumption that timing defeats all judicial relief. The P6 mandate disposes of the reviewed challenge without deciding broader sampling power.

### Ginsburg

Ginsburg joins P1–P6. She was not a member of the Court for Montana or Franklin; those are governing precedents, not her personal prior joins. Her own [MCI join](../../OT1993/output/OT_1993CHUNK7.md) and Thomas Jefferson University dissent join require actual legal authority and reject an exclusion supplied only by explanatory policy. Her [Guernsey join](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK3.md) and [Sweet Home joins](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK8.md) show the complementary acceptance of a reasoned technical choice within the authority actually conferred.

The strongest contrary path for her is a remand taking the acknowledged differential undercount and proposed distributive improvement more seriously. She rejects it on this record because P3 examines that issue directly: the Secretary disputed geographic reliability, not just the relevance of minority undercount, and the lower improvement finding did not resolve every relevant comparison. She adopts P2 as an independently enforceable constitutional standard, not as wholesale incorporation of agency deference into voting rights. Her source-specific agency positions also block reliance on the disclaimed statutory-bar ground. She accepts P1's individual injury despite California's disputed status, without inferring standing from municipal funding injury. P4 leaves review available but does not create an unpreserved APA claim; her Varity Part IV join supports that attention to the question actually presented. P5 reserves both constructions of §195. She supports the bounded reversal and would refuse language immunizing deliberate undercount, deeming all sampling illegal, or treating “most purposes” as a finding that adjustment improves nothing.

### Breyer

Breyer joins P1–P6. His [Guernsey](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK3.md) and [Sweet Home](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK8.md) joins support reasoned technical administration within the statute, while preserving actual grounds and legal limits. Neither case supplies a census constitutional standard. He applies Montana/Franklin as controlling law without claiming personal participation in those earlier cases.

His strongest alternative is renewed consideration of an adjustment that may improve substantial measures of accuracy, given the Census Bureau leadership's recommendation and the effects on representation. He rejects making that policy judgment a constitutional command because the actual record discloses competing geographic estimates and methodological tradeoffs. Adviser preference is relevant evidence, not a constitutional decision rule; the Secretary's explained choice bears P2's required relationship to enumeration and representation. He joins rejection of the panel's essentiality burden and P3's record-specific conclusion, while refusing a rule that statistical uncertainty invariably defeats a constitutional challenge. He accepts the named voters' continuing seat-related injury under P1 and does not rest on the State's appellate choice. His Varity Part IV join supports keeping presentation distinct from substantive remedial preference; P4 supplies no renewed APA review and P5 no sampling construction. He joins P6 because no additional factual determination is necessary under the selected standard, not because delay or completed census transmission automatically eliminates effective judicial relief.
