## Reconciled operative commitments

The following A–F replace the corresponding working conclusions for assembly. Each of the eight named Associates supports the common propositions at the stated level of generality, subject to C's explicit distinction. The independent freeze remains the record of the unreconciled positions.

**A. Definitive agreement construction.** Section 848(c)'s requirement that the defendant act “in concert with five or more other persons” requires mutual agreement in a common criminal plan or enterprise. An organizer's dealings with persons wholly unaware of the unlawful plan do not satisfy that agreement requirement merely because their acts assist the organizer. Individual agreements between the organizer and the necessary participants can supply conspiracy; the decision need not impose an additional universal organizational form. Under actual Shabani, §846 requires knowing participation in the unlawful narcotics agreement beyond a reasonable doubt and no additional overt act. CCE adds its continuing-series, five-other-person, managerial and substantial-income/resource requirements. Consequently the §846 conspiracy is a lesser included offense of the §848 CCE offense in the statutory narcotics setting presented, and the same agreement supports both counts here.

This is a construction of the statutory concert element, not an evidentiary same-conduct test or an assumption true only because the parties agree. Jeffers, 432 U.S. 147–150 and n.14, supplies the textual reasoning but itself assumed the conclusion; the present commitment resolves it. Jeffers's n.12 distinguishes §846's subchapter-I coverage from §848's additional subchapter-II reach. Nothing here imports §846 into an offense it does not cover. A different agreement, separate substantive predicate, different offense or successive prosecution requires its own analysis.

**B. Congressional authorization and the second conviction.** Under Blockburger and Whalen, the included offense gives rise to the presumption against cumulative punishment. Congress did not authorize both convictions and punishments under §§846 and 848 for this same agreement. Jeffers's no-cumulation conclusion and Garrett's distinction between conspiracy and substantive predicates support that result. Separate section numbers, CCE's added elements and severe penalty, and a useful backup judgment do not supply contrary authorization. Jeffers's retained judgments do not establish approval of this dual-judgment arrangement: only the later CCE judgment was reviewed, waiver sustained the plurality's result, and White's different-offense position supplied his distinct basis. No controlling contrary rule arises from silence about retaining both judgments.

Ball makes a second conviction material independently of concurrent prison time. Its potential collateral effects cannot be erased by calling the judgments concurrent or one “combined” conviction. The received §3013 assessment also supplies a concrete additional sanction under D. Actual Harris and Bishop/Stokes are consistent: they apply §924(c)'s clear additional-and-consecutive direction to that statutory pairing. There is no corresponding direction here. Garrett's substantive-predicate holding and Felix's agreement/substantive-offense distinction remain intact.

**C. Common framework and the constitutional limit on joins.** All eight support the foregoing congressional-intent analysis, its use of the governing single-prosecution Hunter/Whalen framework, and its conclusion that the unauthorized second conviction and sentence must be removed. That framework can be stated in a common opinion even though the Justices differ about an additional constitutional explanation. No independent constitutional theory is necessary to that common disposition.

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also accept describing the unauthorized additional conviction as multiple punishment forbidden by the Double Jeopardy Clause within the governing one-prosecution rule. Scalia and Thomas do not adopt an independently sufficient constitutional multiple-punishments theory separate from the statutory limitation. Their Kurth Ranch and Witte positions remain intact, including their objection to treating authorized sanctions as a separate constitutional punishment problem apart from successive criminal proceedings. The common opinion need not deny those positions, overrule Halper, or rest on their proposed change to constitutional law.

The independent freeze's warning about making C independently dispositive is retained. It must not be extended into a bar on any opinion invoking constitutional precedents or roots: the actual historical opinion uses the Hunter/Whalen construction framework that they joined, and their actual Harris and Bishop/Stokes joins confirm its compatibility in this timeline. Conversely, assembly may not use the common statutory joins as eight Associate votes for a separately operative constitutional theory. If such an additional proposition is included, its support remains limited to the six just named. This is a substantive join boundary, not an instruction to publish a separate writing or create a fracture.

**D. Actual assessment and bounded consideration.** Each challenged felony conviction carries a mandatory $50 assessment under the received §3013. The second assessment is actual additional punishment attached by statute to the second judgment, even though the prison terms are concurrent. Considering that automatic consequence answers the preserved challenge to the dual judgments and the Government's claim of no additional punishment; it does not enlarge the controversy into a new monetary claim. Thus the Government's account that the assessment was not separately challenged below does not require the Court to disregard it for this purpose. No general waiver exception is adopted.

Ball also establishes that an unauthorized second conviction does not disappear with concurrency. No conclusive prediction about parole, future recidivist treatment, impeachment or marginal stigma is necessary given the existing assessment. Vacatur must remove the selected conviction's $50 assessment, reducing the existing six-count total from $300 to $250. No payment, collection, refund amount or expired obligation is found; no later $100 rate applies. A freestanding refund claim or independent challenge involving another count remains outside review.

**E. Judgment, count choice and remand.** Reverse the Seventh Circuit insofar as it sustains entry of both Count 1 and Count 2 judgments, and remand for the District Court to vacate one of those convictions, its entire corresponding life sentence, and its $50 assessment. Ball leaves the initial selection to the sentencing court's lawful discretion. Petitioner's safe reply expressly requests vacatur of either count; the Government's narrower formulation does not establish a conspiracy-only concession. Removing only a sentence, staying execution of an existing conviction, or preserving both judgments under a combined label is insufficient.

The surviving challenged conviction and Counts 3–6 remain undisturbed, with their existing sentences and assessments. This includes Count 3's independent life term. The mandate orders no release, acquittal, new trial, general resentencing, new sentence computation or reopening of unrelated appellate objections. It changes judgments, not the historical fact that the jury returned the verdicts. No prospective stay-of-entry system is required or adopted.

**F. Existing appellate power, reserved precise limits.** The Government's backup concern does not require two present convictions. Appellate courts may, in an appropriate case, direct entry of judgment on a valid lesser included offense when reversal of the greater conviction rests on a ground affecting only the greater offense. That possibility includes the already briefed setting of a CCE-specific proof failure with a valid conspiracy verdict. It is more than permission to file a motion whose legal availability is wholly undecided, but it is not automatic reinstatement whenever CCE later fails.

Tinder, 345 U.S. 565, 570, verifies the established §2106 corrective power: the actual order corrected the sentence after the felony treatment failed, without vacating the underlying conviction. It did not adjudicate revival of a previously vacated conspiracy conviction. Ward, 37 F.3d 243, 251, provides the closer, already briefed example: CCE failed for lack of the necessary five-person supervisory proof; the conspiracy conviction had been vacated; the defendant conceded reinstatement; the court reversed CCE and remanded accordingly. That concession must not be erased or converted into a holding that consent is always required or always irrelevant. The safe reply also identified Silvers's restoration practice. These authorities rebut an asserted necessity to retain both convictions now without resolving every later procedural vehicle.

The precise limits and form of relief in a future contested application remain reserved. A ground invalidating the agreement or otherwise tainting the conspiracy verdict cannot be ignored. No retrial entitlement, general finality exception, unconditional revival, finding that any future error is harmless, or compelled lesser-offense judgment follows. Present vacatur for duplication is not an evidentiary acquittal. Distinct questions involving an earlier separate conspiracy prosecution, previously unknown CCE facts, or a noncoterminous enterprise are not decided. Actual Dixon/Grady remains governing in its successive-prosecution domain; no retained-conduct rule is displaced through this remand.
