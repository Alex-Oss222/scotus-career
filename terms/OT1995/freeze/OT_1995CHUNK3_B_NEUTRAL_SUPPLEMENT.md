## Neal: Amendment 488

The [Sentencing Commission's original Amendment 488](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/amendment/488), effective November 1, 1993, directs use of 0.4 milligram per dose of LSD on a carrier medium **for the Drug Quantity Table**. When blotter paper does not mark individual doses, a quarter-inch by quarter-inch section is presumed one dose. For liquid LSD not yet placed on a carrier, the weight of LSD alone may inadequately reflect seriousness and an upward departure may be warranted. The background expressly preserves the statutory mixture-or-substance measure for mandatory minima under Chapman and §5G1.1(b). These are separate rules; the liquid-LSD qualification does not alter the present carrier issue.

The Commission gives its reasons: carrier weights vary widely and usually greatly exceed the drug's own weight, creating disparity between equal quantities and disproportionate Guidelines treatment relative to other drugs. It selects a weight above the DEA's typical pure dose to allocate some weight to the carrier. It does not purport to amend §841 or displace Chapman. No independent drug-quantity dispute, exception to the applicable statutory minimum, or replacement sentence is established by this supplement.

## Bishop and Stokes: effective law and bounded record verification

Bailey's actual December 6, 1995 public projection in the entering-law slice is effective: use requires active employment; relationship to an offense does not substitute for use or carrying. The reported act of putting a pistol to Bradley's head is active employment, unlike storage. The review scope does not independently present a use-instruction or sufficiency claim. No such claim, carrying determination or harmlessness conclusion is manufactured.

The [amended Third Circuit opinion, 66 F.3d 569](https://openjurist.org/66/f3d/569), introduction and footnotes 1–2, supplies these additional public particulars: Bradley and Rollins both observed the gunman during the taking; the separate station identifications occurred within ninety minutes. Police comments before the identifications were suggestive. The district court found the identifications came from each witness's own memory, and the appellate court sustained the findings and reliability conclusion. No exact prior description, duration of observation, or quoted certainty declaration is invented.

Stokes was seen leaving the driver's window of the crashed stolen vehicle, fled and was caught by the pursuing officer; two firearms were found on the car's floor. At booking an officer asked what was the matter with Stokes's leg, and he said he had hurt it in an accident. The report says both the answer and booking video were admitted over objection. The appellate footnote describes a medical-attention purpose and invokes a routine-booking exception. It does not reproduce the complete question sequence, suppression testimony, recording, specific relevance/prejudice grounds or trial use. Medical purpose is relevant but does not alone answer the Innis inquiry. A bare absence of fuller appellate discussion is also not proof that the lower court applied a legally erroneous test.
