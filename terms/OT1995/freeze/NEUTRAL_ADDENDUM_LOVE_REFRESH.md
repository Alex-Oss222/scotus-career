## Corrected scope and record

1. **Only the four officers in their individual capacities are within the damages appeal.** The amended complaint ¶¶3–6, App. 35a–36a, expressly says of each officer that he is sued only individually. For example, ¶3 says: “Defendant Pepersack is being sued, however, only in his individual capacity.” The district court rejected the officials' contrary capacity argument under Hafer, App. 12a: “The Court agrees with the plaintiff in this case that effectively what we have here is a claim against state officials but they are sued in their individual capacity.” Their alleged official acts establish color of law, not official-capacity pleading. Personal liability still requires each defendant's own legally sufficient participation and culpability.

2. **State dismissal and abandoned theories are outside review.** Opposition printed p. 3 n. 2, PDF p. 12, states: “Ms. Love did not appeal the dismissal of the State and has now abandoned any claim based on the contract clause or the Maryland Declaration of Rights.” The petition's questions and argument pursue the Second Amendment and substantive due process. The complaint's earlier freedom-to-contract language does not restore an independent appellate contract component. There is no new appellate Will or Eleventh Amendment disposition to make. The untouched state dismissal retains its prior effect; state constitutional and independent contract claims are not revived.

3. **Qualified immunity was not raised in the motion being reviewed.** Opposition printed p. 11 **n. 6**, PDF p. 20, visually verified, reads: “The issue of qualified immunity was not raised in the motion to dismiss. However, if this Court were to hold that the complaint states a claim upon which relief can be granted, respondents would allege qualified immunity in their answer.” The footnote cites Harlow, 457 U.S. 800, 815, and Rule 7(a). It is footnote 6, not footnote 5. The text at pp. 10–11 argues future immunity and vehicle suitability, not a prior adjudication of an actually asserted immunity defense. Nonassertion at this stage is not permanent waiver; the anticipated defense may be raised in an answer after remand subject to ordinary procedure. Neither counsel's expectation of eventual success nor availability of an abstract immunity theory establishes the present judgment on that defense.

4. **Pleading stage, not fact adjudication.** The district court granted Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal; the Fourth Circuit affirmed. The detailed complaint alleges truthful and complete application information (¶10); no found disqualifying conviction or other statutory disqualification (¶¶13,18,20); review of arrest printouts without investigating dispositions (¶¶12,15–18); a policy maintained for years of initial disapproval and shifting proof of disposition to the applicant (¶¶23–31); supervisory knowledge/participation (¶¶22–26,32–34); resulting inability to purchase and required state litigation (¶35); and willful, malicious or reckless conduct (¶¶36–37). These are allegations accepted within ordinary pleading rules, not findings that every official knowingly violated federal law. Bare legal descriptions of unconstitutional conduct do not establish their own legal conclusion.

5. **Contested inference about urgency.** Petition pp. 23–26 objects that the Fourth Circuit's extreme-time-pressure account was neither pleaded nor established. Opposition p. 4 n. 3 argues the inference follows from the dates: application executed September 13, received September 18, notice September 21. The dates and seven-day deadline are in the record. Whether an actual need to cut corners justified the continuing arrests policy is not an adjudicated fact. No model may convert respondents' inference into an uncontested finding or describe the alleged policy as an accidental clerical mistake.

6. **Statutory and administrative distinctions.** App. 3a–9a prints the statutory scheme, including later renumbering and an express note that the $10 fee was added in 1992. Conduct occurred in September 1990. The investigation/disapproval subsection then numbered §442(f) was later §442(g). A dealer could complete the transaction after the statutory waiting period absent effective disapproval; this was not an unbounded affirmative-license discretion. The district judge accepted counsel's correction of that terminology, App. 21a–22a. Authority to investigate is not necessarily discretion to disapprove a complete truthful application. The denial prevented the sale while it stood. The state court ultimately ordered approval. The transcript also records counsel's correction that Pepersack himself conducted the administrative appeal hearing, App. 17a. No new procedural-due-process claim is supplied from this fact.

7. **Relief.** The operative appeal seeks damages on the two preserved federal theories. Eventual authorization does not itself moot accrued damages. The complaint supplies no continuing post-order policy application to Love or claim for an injunction. The prayer for costs, fees, punitive damages and other appropriate relief does not establish liability or a present amount. No appellate court has found a constitutional violation or awarded federal damages.

- **Gomez v. Toledo, 446 U.S. 635, 640–641 (1980):** qualified immunity is an affirmative defense to be asserted by the defendant; a §1983 plaintiff need not anticipate it. Harlow's later objective test alters the merits of immunity, not this allocation of pleading. The present footnote supplies no pleaded or litigated defense.
- **Hafer:** personal-capacity suit remains such despite official conduct; applicable personal defenses remain available. The State is not reintroduced through capacity confusion.
- **Albright:** the seven-Justice judgment has no controlling general rationale under Marks; no broad state-remedy cure, tangible-loss prerequisite, or universal specific-amendment displacement rule may be imported.
- **Collins:** an assumed state-law entitlement does not establish constitutional arbitrariness; the actual holding concerns voluntary-employment protective omissions and leaves different affirmative deprivations open.

The holding concerns the liberty/process predicate, not automatic substantive-due-process liability for every statutory violation.
