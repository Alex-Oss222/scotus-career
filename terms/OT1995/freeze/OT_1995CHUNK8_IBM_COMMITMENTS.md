## Entering rules and review boundaries

The Export Clause prohibits a tax or duty on articles exported from a State. Thames & Mersey remains the direct precedent for a premium tax on insurance of goods during export transit. Its holding treats the insurance of the identified exported cargo during the voyage as integral to exportation. It expressly separates taxes on preliminaries before export, the insurance business generally, and genuinely distinct subjects. It does not establish immunity for everything that makes exports more expensive. Fairbank and Hvoslef supply the surviving export-document and export-charter background identified in the packet; no additional unverified factual proposition from those cases is needed here.

The operative Standard is **Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review**: present operation effective Lebron, February 21, 1995, for the deliberate-disavowal component, and X-Citement Video, November 29, 1994, for the distinct respondent-defense component. Lebron permits, but does not require, consideration of a legal argument within the preserved claim and presented question on established facts despite deliberate disavowal. It does not authorize a new claim, factual theory or enlarged relief. Government is petitioner here; X-Citement Video's rule for defending a favorable judgment supplies no independent enlargement of its review.

All seven Associates accept the certiorari channel and the preserved challenge to the as-applied refund judgment. None finds an established jurisdictional defect or seeks a dismissal as improvidently granted on the supplied facts. The scope issue concerns a legal argument within that challenge, not an independent tax or new claim. Counsel's statements establish both the absence of a separately pressed goods-versus-services submission and the statement that consideration was not foreclosed. They do not themselves dictate the Court's prudential election.

The section 4371 tax is broader than exports. Its premium measure and its period-specific statutory conditions and exemptions are those in the validated packet, including the distinction between section 4373(1)'s authorized-insurer/countersignature exemption and section 4373(2)'s federal-indemnity-bond exemption. The foreign-insurer and insured-person categories do not make this an export-exclusive levy. No alternative exemption, transitional liability dispute, tax calculation, or unknown insurance contract term is decided. The question is its application to the stipulated export-shipment insurance.

## Eligible individual authorities

1. **Itel Containers, February 23, 1993**, [public entry](../../OT1992/output/OT_1992CHUNK3.md): Kennedy's Court opinion, joined throughout by O'Connor, Souter and Thomas among the present Associates, keeps treaty/preemption, Commerce, and Import-Export inquiries distinct. Its constitutional holding concerns a local transfer of possession of containers, not goods in export transit, and preserves Richfield's direct-export question. Scalia's separate writing preserves a textual Import-Export inquiry while rejecting open-ended negative-Commerce balancing; he relies on the containers' not-yet-exported status for that component. Itel does not decide the federal Export Clause question now presented.
2. **Jefferson Lines, April 3, 1995**, [public entry](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK4.md): Kennedy's Court opinion, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, identifies the actual taxable transaction, permits taxation of the distinct local retail sale, and separates it from the carrier's multistate receipts. Scalia's judgment concurrence, joined by Thomas, uses discrimination and indistinguishable-precedent grounds without adopting the complete balancing framework. Breyer's dissent, joined by O'Connor, treats the actual interstate service as controlling over nominal consumer incidence and requires external apportionment. These are different constitutional positions, not present Export Clause votes.
3. **Fulton, February 21, 1996**, [public projection](../entering-law/PUBLIC_Fulton_Corp_v_Faulkner_merits_1996-02-21.md): Thomas's unanimous opinion requires actual matching burdens and equivalent taxable events, rather than general fiscal labels. Its express overruling of Darnell rests on an identified incompatibility with the governing Commerce Clause doctrine. All seven Associates joined. It supplies no rule that nondiscrimination answers a differently worded constitutional prohibition.
4. **Lebron**, as copied with exact joins in the IBM Standard: Scalia authored the reachability rationale; Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined it. O'Connor declined the disavowed argument as a prudential matter. Izumi, Caspari and Madsen retain their independent-question and exceptional-review limits. A permissible same-claim argument is not a mandatory argument.

## Common adversarial comparison

**Path A — apply the existing export-insurance rule.** The challenged premium tax concerns insurance attaching to identified export shipments; both the value of the cargo and its shipment risks determine the premium. The actual exported-goods connection, rather than a label or a general economic consequence, places the application within Thames & Mersey. The absence of discrimination does not answer the categorical federal Export Clause. Itel concerned a different local transaction under different constitutional text and expressly reserved direct-export taxation. This path affirms the refund judgment without extending immunity to other services or the insurance business generally.

The strongest objection is that insurance indemnifies a risk rather than transfers or taxes an article, and that modern service-transaction analysis undermines Thames & Mersey's treatment of commercial necessity as constitutional identity. A premium is not simply the value of an export: its rate depends on risk. The response supporting Path A is that Thames & Mersey already addressed an indemnity contract and expressly held export-transit insurance inseparable at the relevant level; the present risk features do not distinguish that precedent. The state-tax cases do not themselves overrule it or show that the federal Clause has become a nondiscrimination rule.

**Path B — reject the general nondiscrimination theory, but reach the transaction argument and displace Thames & Mersey's service extension.** The tax is imposed on a purchased indemnity service measured by the consideration for that service. Shipment values affect its price without making the tax one on the articles. The government need not be right that all nondiscriminatory export burdens are permissible to win this narrower application. This path requires a genuine prudential election under Lebron and an express refusal to retain Thames & Mersey insofar as it treats this service as the exported article or its constitutional equivalent. It cannot be presented as a factual distinction that the established record does not support.

The strongest objections are the directly applicable precedent, the insurance's actual attachment to shipment, and the Government's deliberate decision not to press a separate service argument. The response supporting Path B is that the question presented still asks whether this tax is on exports, the stipulated rate/value/risk facts suffice, and the precedent's commercial-necessity rationale gives the immunity no principled service boundary. This response does not permit a holding that every service is outside the Clause regardless of whether a tax actually targets articles or their exportation.

**Path C — make nondiscrimination sufficient by transferring Commerce or Import-Export doctrine wholesale.** No participating Associate commits to this ground. Itel and Fulton require analysis of the actual constitutional provision and taxable event; the federal Export Clause's text does not become a balancing or state-revenue provision merely because a tax is evenhanded. This rejects the Government's broad proposition without necessarily deciding its narrower application in IBM's favor.

## Individual commitments

### O'Connor

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach the preserved nondiscrimination question; prudentially decline the separately unpressed goods-versus-services argument. Affirm the Federal Circuit's refund judgment.

**Sufficient ground:** Retain Thames & Mersey for this export-transit insurance. Nondiscrimination does not cure the tax on the protected transaction under the existing federal Clause rule. Her Itel join accepted Michelin's state-tax analysis while expressly preserving the direct-export question; neither it nor Fulton supplies the doctrinal contradiction needed to discard the direct federal precedent. Her Jefferson Lines dissent further rejects using nominal transactional incidence to disregard what the charge actually reaches.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Lebron makes the narrower argument legally available and the existing facts may suffice. Her own Lebron position nevertheless supports declining an argument the petitioner deliberately declined to pursue separately, where the litigated nondiscrimination question has a complete answer under direct precedent. She does not treat disavowal as jurisdictional or categorical forfeiture. If another writing reaches the service argument, she can agree that Thames & Mersey has not been displaced, but a join in a new comprehensive goods/services test is not committed.

**Remedy and join boundary:** Preserve the challenged refund without a new amount, facial invalidation, or immunity for all export-associated expenditures. She can join a decision confined to rejection of the nondiscrimination challenge and application of the existing insurance rule. Her nonreach position must remain visible if a broader opinion resolves the separately unpressed ground.

### Scalia

**Threshold and judgment:** Elect to consider the same-claim service argument under his Lebron rule; affirm the refund judgment on the supplied record. Permission derives from the included legal question and established facts, not from a respondent-defense rule.

**Sufficient ground:** The express Export Clause supplies a textual prohibition separate from the negative Commerce doctrine. His Itel concurrence protected that separate textual inquiry, and his Jefferson Lines concurrence allowed a distinct local sale only where no indistinguishable contrary precedent governed. Here Thames & Mersey is the direct contrary precedent, not a remote analogy; its actual cargo-insurance application remains supportable under the text's protection against taxing exportation through its shipment documents. A nondiscrimination-only reformulation cannot reconcile that text and precedent.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** The phrase articles exported does not expressly mention indemnity services, and formal incidence favors the Government. He can tolerate an opinion treating insurance as legally distinct in other settings, but not a ground that equates the negative-Commerce limit with this express prohibition. The stipulated insurance is the same export-transit category as the surviving rule; a general objection to expansive Commerce balancing supplies no reason to repudiate it.

**Remedy and join boundary:** Affirm only this as-applied refund. Joinable ground: retain and apply Thames & Mersey while declining a general immunity for other services. He does not join a Michelin-style balancing test or a rule that the constitutional outcome turns on estimates of the tax's economic severity.

### Kennedy

**Threshold and judgment:** Elect to reach the narrower constitutional-incidence argument under his Lebron join. Reverse the Export Clause invalidation and remand for conforming proceedings on the challenged assessments.

**Sufficient ground:** Reject the proposition that nondiscrimination alone settles the federal Clause, but classify the premium tax as a charge on the separately supplied indemnity service. His own Itel opinion and Jefferson Lines opinion make identification of the real transaction decisive even when its price or purpose closely connects to protected commerce. The stipulated rate reflects route, handling, packaging and other risk as well as cargo value; service consideration is not a tax simply imposed on the articles because they are exported.

**Precedent and strongest objection:** Thames & Mersey actually governs this category and cannot honestly be distinguished merely because the current tax has a different name or general coverage. He would expressly overrule its extension of the article immunity to bona fide insurance-service premiums on this ground. The constitutional text and the separable-transaction reasoning supply the justification; Michelin is corroboration of analytical separation, not a controlling construction of the federal Clause. The strong opposing argument is that the service is integral to the shipment and the old precedent already knew indemnity's character. His objection to that precedent is its equation of commercial indispensability with taxation of the articles, which would obscure the boundary preserved in his Itel and Jefferson Lines opinions.

**Alternative and join boundary:** Can join a confined statement that a genuine indemnity premium tax is not a tax on exported articles even though insured value affects the premium. Cannot join a nondiscrimination safe harbor for an actual tax on articles, a factual finding that insurance was optional, or a claim that Thames & Mersey silently ceased to govern. If the Court declines this included argument, he agrees that the Government's broad nondiscrimination theory alone does not justify reversal; that conditional legal conclusion does not change his election to reach and his reversal commitment.

**Remedy:** No new tax amount or unrelated assessment is entered. Further proceedings must conform to rejection of this constitutional ground; any otherwise live computation or statutory issue remains subject to the actual case's bounds.

### Souter

**Threshold and judgment:** Treat the narrower argument as legally available under his Lebron join and consider it on the existing facts; affirm.

**Sufficient ground:** His Itel join and Jefferson Lines join establish that genuine distinct transactions may be taxed under their respective constitutional rules, but do not establish that an unchanged category directly adjudicated under the federal Export Clause may be redescribed without confronting its precedent. Thames & Mersey concerned the indemnity service itself, not a tax mistakenly thought to fall formally on goods. The current risk-based premium is therefore no material distinction. The federal Clause's categorical restriction and the direct precedent together support retaining the export-transit-insurance rule.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Kennedy's service construction gives a coherent narrower boundary than immunity for everything essential to exporting. Souter can join express language preserving taxation of genuinely separate services and antecedent transactions; he does not regard that clarification as sufficient reason to overrule the one direct insurance category. Itel expressly left direct-export questions open, and Fulton displaced an older rule only on a demonstrated contradiction within the same governing doctrine. No such contradiction is established here.

**Remedy and join boundary:** Affirm the existing refund, without expanding the precedent to unpresented services. He may join either an express rejection of the narrower legal argument or an opinion limited to the nondiscrimination submission, provided it does not describe the narrower argument as categorically unavailable. No comprehensive new export-service test is committed.

### Thomas

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach the included service argument on the established record under his Lebron join; affirm.

**Sufficient ground:** Give independent effect to the federal Export Clause's express prohibition. His Itel join did not reduce an express constitutional restriction to the Commerce Clause's nondiscrimination requirement; his Jefferson Lines concurrence with Scalia used precedent-specific limits rather than an unrestricted balancing inquiry. Thames & Mersey supplies the direct application to insurance on goods in actual export transit. The fact that section 4371 also taxes nonexport insurance does not change the incidence of this particular application under that surviving rule.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** The services construction draws force from the word articles, but the tax can burden the protected export transaction through its integral shipment insurance without being printed on the goods themselves. He rejects a broad economic-effects rule: the direct precedent and established shipment connection, not every increase in exporter expense, supply the sufficient ground. Fulton, which he authored, required precise comparison of the actual taxable event and justified overruling by a concrete doctrinal contradiction. The Government's interclause analogy does not establish one for Thames & Mersey.

**Remedy and join boundary:** Affirm the as-applied refund. Can join a direct precedent-and-text opinion that preserves distinct-service and pre-export limits; cannot join a rule validating actual export taxes merely because generally applicable or nondiscriminatory. No opinion on separate insurer-business taxation or an unclaimed section 4373 exemption.

### Ginsburg

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach the included narrower legal argument under her Lebron join; reverse the Export Clause ruling and remand.

**Sufficient ground:** Her Jefferson Lines join accepts that a tax's actual transaction may remain distinct even when the service purchased is connected with protected commerce and determines the price. Her Fulton join requires actual legal and transactional comparison rather than an aggregate economic equation. These premiums purchase indemnification against specified risks; using cargo value as one factor does not convert the tax on that service into an article tax. Nondiscrimination is relevant description, not the sufficient constitutional rule.

**Precedent and strongest objection:** Thames & Mersey expressly rejects the very distinction she finds sound; it must be displaced, not relabeled as factually inapplicable. She would overrule it to the extent it extends the Clause to this premium tax because the service is commercially integral to exportation. She preserves the ban on taxing the articles and genuine export-document taxes that in substance attach to the goods or their exportation. The strongest opposing consideration is reliance on a long direct precedent in a category never expressly reconsidered. The offered narrower correction supplies a constitutional boundary grounded in the actual taxable service without adopting the Government's much broader permission for all nondiscriminatory burdens.

**Alternative and join boundary:** Joinable formulation must decide the incidence question on the stipulated facts, acknowledge the direct precedent and state the extent of its displacement. She will not join a claim that every price-related service is immune, a blanket nondiscrimination safe harbor, or an unsupported factual distinction about contract necessity. If the included argument is declined, the broad Government theory fails; her own election remains to reach it.

**Remedy:** Reverse the constitutional ground and remand within the refund action's scope. No amount, new exemption ruling or facial validation of every section 4371 application is supplied.

### Breyer

**Threshold and judgment:** Accept availability of the narrower argument under his Lebron join and consider it; affirm the refund judgment.

**Sufficient ground:** His Jefferson Lines dissent focuses on the actual service burden rather than nominal buyer incidence. Here the insurance attaches to particular goods during their outward shipment, and its premium takes the goods' value and their shipment conditions as its measure. That practical integration, combined with the direct Thames & Mersey holding, supports retaining the existing application. The presence of risk pricing does not make the connection merely an incidental downstream effect; the insurance covers the very shipment. His Fulton join likewise requires examining actual operation, while its Commerce Clause nondiscrimination framework cannot decide the federal Export Clause by analogy alone.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** A genuine indemnity service has independent economic content, and a goods-only constitutional boundary is administrable. But Thames & Mersey already confronted that contract character and grounded its result in the insured export voyage. Breyer does not extend its rule simply to any commercially useful service; the existing directly adjudicated category and actual shipment facts are sufficient. His earlier rejection of nominal transaction labels supports refusing to make the statutory premium base alone decisive against that rule.

**Remedy and join boundary:** Affirm this refund without a new tax calculation. Can join an opinion applying the existing insurance rule and expressly reserving other service taxes. He does not require a new empirical indispensability test, an industry-wide factual inquiry, or a remand to quantify how much the levy impedes exports. He will not join an assumption that every tax affecting export prices is unconstitutional.
