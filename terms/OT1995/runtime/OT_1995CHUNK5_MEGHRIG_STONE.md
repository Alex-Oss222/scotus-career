#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand for dismissal of the RCRA claim as pleaded. This is Stone's approved vote.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No reimbursement is available through this citizen-suit claim. Preserve only independently pleaded or otherwise lawfully available alternative claims; do not imply that another statute necessarily authorizes recovery.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Endangerment at commencement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the temporal coverage question on the complaint as pleaded; do not find a remaining danger not alleged.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the current-threat language without converting imminence into a demand for completed present injury.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 6972(a)(1)(B) requires an endangerment that may presently exist when suit is filed. The reference to past contribution identifies responsible persons; it does not eliminate the current-threat condition. The pleaded completed cleanup does not satisfy that condition.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Past disposal can create a present threat, which explains coverage of a former contributor. That is different from treating a fully removed threat as presently imminent. The text gives both propositions work, and the statute’s prospective abatement design reinforces that reading. [P1, 485–486]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No demand that injury already have occurred; a supported ongoing or threatened exposure may qualify despite the age of the disposal.

##### Issue 2: Completed cleanup costs

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the remedy demanded, while reserving expense allocation arising during a properly commenced abatement suit.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statute’s remedial design rather than derive reimbursement from the desirability of cleanup.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** RCRA’s citizen-suit provision does not authorize this action solely to recover the costs of a completed cleanup. The claim must be dismissed without adjudicating independently available remedies.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The broad phrase concerning necessary action serves the elimination of a qualifying endangerment; it does not create the retrospective cost action Congress expressly formulated in CERCLA. Policy concerns about prompt cleanup cannot supply omitted liability, particularly in a scheme with notice and public-enforcement limitations not designed for a damages action. The United States’ support for broader equitable authority in some properly filed cases does not establish this particular remedy after the endangerment has ended. [P1, 483–488]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** State-law claims, any independently available federal cost action, and costs associated with relief in a properly pending RCRA case remain outside this holding.
