## Verified posture and record boundaries

The relevant appellate judgment is the Ninth Circuit's November 17, 1994 order in No. 94-56507, reproduced at Petition Appendix 1a. It grants dismissal for lack of jurisdiction with a citation to Nelson v. Silverman, 999 F.2d 417, 418–419 (9th Cir. 1993); denies the interim stay motion as moot; denies the requested stay pending certiorari; and denies sanctions. Review proceeds under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Section 1291's final-decision requirement, including the collateral-order doctrine, governs the underlying appealability question.

The September 6, 1994 District Court order, entered September 7 and reproduced at Petition Appendix 2a–7a, reinstates the Bivens job-termination claims against Behrens under a three-year limitations period. Its paragraph 6 states that material issues of fact remain concerning Behrens on the Bivens claims. Its operative paragraph 8 denies his summary-judgment motion on those claims. It does not itself identify every assumed fact for a legal qualified-immunity appeal. That is a description of the actual reproduced order, not a conclusion that the complete record lacks relevant material.

The November 7, 1994 District Court order, entered November 9, at Petition Appendix 8a–9a, certifies the appeal as frivolous and waived because of the prior one-appeal rule. This establishes the ground of that order. It does not establish that every legal issue now asserted is frivolous independently of the one-appeal rule.

The first appellate opinion, Pelletier v. Federal Home Loan Bank of San Francisco, 968 F.2d 865 (9th Cir. 1992), is reproduced at Petition Appendix 10a–33a. It held the continuing-interference allegations sufficient at the pleading stage. The job-termination claims were then dismissed as time-barred, so the court expressly did not decide the claimed liberty/property interest in that particular job. The court observed an apparent limitations error; the District Court later reinstated the claim. Do not say that the first appellate court itself entered the reinstatement order.

The first opinion also reversed denial of substitution of the United States on common-law tort claims. That distinct substitution ruling is background; the present review does not reopen it. The September 1994 order identifies the remaining Bivens claims against Behrens and a negligence claim against the United States. No present disposition of the latter follows from deciding Behrens's appealability.

Behrens's May 8, 1986 letter, the termination that followed it, and alleged later interference with employment are the underlying subject matter. The parties dispute whether the employment was subject to enforceable federal approval, the character and scope of any investigation, the authority to direct termination, and the assumptions appropriate to the legal immunity defense. Neither side's characterization of disputed facts as established is adopted here. No property interest, clear constitutional violation, lack of such a violation, or immunity entitlement is established by this addendum.

## Strongest submitted alternatives

**Petitioner.** A prior appeal cannot exhaust jurisdiction over a later denial that satisfies Mitchell and Cohen. Pleadings and a developed record can present different legal immunity questions. The reinstated termination claim was not decided in the first appeal. Petitioner maintains that he seeks a legal determination even accepting the letter, resulting termination, and subsequent inability to find employment. His broader argument also challenges a circuit's categorical restriction on jurisdiction and identifies targeted treatment of frivolous appeals as an alternative. These are contentions, not findings that every fact his formulation assumes is properly established.

**Respondent.** Johnson supplies an independent ground for dismissal if the proposed appeal depends on selecting Behrens's disputed facts. Respondent emphasizes the District Court's factual-dispute determination and argues that the defense cannot assume regulatory conditionality or factual authority that respondent disputes. He further argues that an appeal concerning termination cannot prevent trial on the surviving continuing-interference claim and therefore fails Cohen's conclusiveness/effective-unreviewability requirements. His broader position defends a one-appeal rule against repeated delay and litigation expense, while identifying permission-based review for genuinely exceptional later issues as an alternative.

**United States.** The amicus submission supports rejection of the categorical rule particularly for a legally distinct immunity defense unavailable in the first appeal. Its treatment of the revived termination claim supplies the narrowest submitted ground for relief. It argues that a general factual-dispute sentence need not foreclose a separable legal question, and that courts may identify the district court's favorable-to-plaintiff assumptions. It also advocates direct resolution that the appeal is legally proper; that further conclusion is disputed by respondent and is not established as fact here. The brief discusses targeted dismissal, summary procedures, and circuit frivolous-appeal certification. No new Supreme Court procedural rule is derived merely from those proposals.

**Conflict correction.** The original packet's statement that no material conflict was identified should not be treated as an affirmative finding of no circuit disagreement. The petition identifies contrary circuit approaches; the United States explains that several cited decisions discuss second appeals without actually adjudicating one. The legally relevant dispute is nevertheless established by the Ninth Circuit's operative categorical rule and the parties' opposing statutory positions. Do not turn the petition's circuit count or reversal-rate study into an independently verified finding.

## Entering law and available lawful paths

The supplied simulated Johnson v. Jones rule is controlling: legal qualified-immunity review on properly assumed facts remains available, but ordinary factual-sufficiency review is unavailable. Its treatment of unstated assumptions does not permit reweighing evidence. Kimberlin, as copied in the entering-law standard, likewise permits a separable legal challenge without permitting factual review. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law on an authorized legal immunity issue; it supplies no independent appellate jurisdiction. Swint rejects convenience-based expansion and leaves genuinely necessary connected review at its reserved scope. Digital Equipment requires satisfaction of the collateral-order conditions and distinguishes public immunity from ordinary private settlement rights. The copied contemporary Doe rule continues to exclude pure factual-sufficiency appeals. None decides a categorical numerical limit on qualified-immunity appeals.

Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511, 524–530 (1985), remains pre-divergence authority for legal immediate review and immunity from suit. Its discussion recognizes adjudication at both pleading and summary-judgment stages, but does not itself expressly announce an unlimited right to repeat the same rejected legal argument. Its procedural history is not a sufficient substitute for deciding the present statutory question.

Available paths include: (1) set aside the categorical bar and remand for the Ninth Circuit to determine the properly assumed facts and the genuinely separable legal issues; (2) affirm on Johnson if the Court establishes that the actual appeal raises only factual sufficiency, without adopting a categorical bar; (3) sustain the numerical limitation under a more restrictive understanding of finality; (4) reject the categorical rule and decide now that a specified legal portion is immediately reviewable. Paths 2 and 4 require more definitive resolution of the actual appealed issues and assumptions than path 1. No path authorizes a merits immunity award simply because an interlocutory appeal may lie.
