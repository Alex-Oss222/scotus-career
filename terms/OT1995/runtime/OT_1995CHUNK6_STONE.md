**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the injunction against the challenged parental-notice requirement under simulated Casey, within the reviewed scope and the lower decree's separable provisions.

**Application.** First, identify the actual compulsory notice and forty-eight-hour delay, with their authentic exceptions. Notice is not formal consent, but its practical operation may still give another person control over a mature minor's decision. Second, encouraging useful parental involvement is permissible; no finding of obstructive purpose follows merely from legislative opposition to abortion. Third, compare minors subject to this notice obligation with the same minors absent the obligation. The demonstrated extra delay, compelled disclosure and coercive consequences are attributable to the rule; burdens must not be averaged away across adults, or defined circularly by selecting only those who win. In remaining applications involving serious confidentiality interference, the State must meet Casey's severe-interference protection; for remaining meaningful interference it must substantiate fit. An independent, confidential maturity or best-interests route is a concrete established alternative, not a speculative demand to disprove every possibility. General reliance on parental guidance does not explain excluding that route for a minor whose circumstances make notification harmful or inappropriate. Fifth, preserve timely necessary care and genuine medical judgment; an emergency label alone cannot cure every notice injury.

**Remedy.** Sixth, keep relief tied to the compulsory provision and its supported scope. Because the mandatory condition lacks an independent route for the identified affected minors, voluntary notice by other families does not itself save its compulsory operation. The Court should not legislate a bypass into South Dakota law or transplant Pennsylvania's exact deadlines. Preserve independent provisions and rulings outside the grant. The result follows the supplied record under Casey; it creates no freestanding rule that every parental communication is unconstitutional.

## OT1995-054 — Cooper v. Oklahoma

**Event:** 1996-04-16 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 348

**Docket(s):** No. 95-5207

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Inherited-law qualification:** The supplied rejection of a clear-and-convincing competence burden remains an individual position. Medina cannot be cited for categorical exclusion of Mathews or for an already settled higher-burden rule.

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Require application of a constitutionally permissible burden in a reliable competency proceeding. A retrospective determination may be used only if the passage of time and available record permit a meaningful inquiry; otherwise provide the ordinary relief competency law requires before any retrial.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Apply the actual common holding of simulated Medina allowing the preponderance allocation in its setting. Assess the higher burden through the relevant history and risk of trying an incompetent defendant; Medina’s fractured rationale does not categorically exclude Mathews or settle this higher-burden question.
- **Supporting Authorities:** U.S. Const. amend. XIV; *Pate v. Robinson*, 383 U.S. 375; *Drope v. Missouri*, 420 U.S. 162; *Medina v. California*, 505 U.S. 437.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** A State may place on the defendant a preponderance burden to establish incompetence, but due process forbids a clear-and-convincing burden that permits trial even when the evidence establishes that the defendant is more likely than not unable to understand the proceedings or assist counsel. Sufficient doubt triggering a competency hearing is distinct from the ultimate determination of incompetence.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** History permits some state allocation of the competency burden, but the Constitution does not permit trial of a person unable to participate in the defense. The clear-and-convincing requirement places the decisive risk of error on the person least able to correct it and permits adjudication despite serious unresolved doubt. The State’s administrative interest cannot justify that asymmetrical risk.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding prescribes neither one hearing format nor a permanent incompetency finding and leaves lawful treatment, restoration, periodic reassessment, and retrial after competence to ordinary procedures.

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the constitutional confidentiality determination on the reviewed issue, preserving the judgment insofar as supported by the trial record and leaving unpresented issues undisturbed.

**Reasoning.** Giving investigators intimate information for a murder investigation does not surrender it to general publication. Sheets exposed his private life for a limited public purpose after receiving confidentiality assurances. Any preserved defense concerning individual notice, municipal attribution or the amount of damages must be resolved under its own law, not assumed away by recognizing the right. Consent to the investigation remains effective for that purpose. The judgment creates no general constitutional right to deletion, no inheritance of a deceased person's claim and no liability for every state-law confidentiality breach.
