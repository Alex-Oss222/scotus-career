## Positive findings accepted and their effect

The April order's JA303 note 1 affirmatively finds that the government had not demonstrated the requisite §853(p) unavailability conditions in answering its aggregate-proceeds/smaller-estate theory in the Wyochem/Porter discussion. This is not merely an absence of evidence in the source author's partial read. JA304 records that direct facilitation was not argued for the Wyochem funds. The tainted proportion and Porter's $1,333.45 claim remain for source findings. JA306 positively finds the ring bought with legitimate funds and separately finds Porter an innocent owner; the order directs its return. JA308 positively finds no drug nexus for the childhood accounts and orders return to the parents on their joint-interest claims. No execution of those orders is inferred. The district court's civil innocent-owner rationale is its reasoning, not the text of §853(n)'s two distinct superior/preexisting-interest and bona-fide-purchaser-for-value branches.

The April defendant-specific hearing direction remains a different component. The court of appeals held it jurisdictionally ineffective after the defendant's appeal, while preserving §853(n) third-party jurisdiction. The positive findings consequently must not be dismissed as nonexistent or all jurisdictionally void; equally they are not a completed, effective item-by-item judgment on Libretti's own motion. The original B3 reference to the “ineffective April 2 reconsideration” is confined to that defendant-specific reopening, not the whole April order or the third-party findings.

The December order expressly identifies the $8,416.23 General Chemical distribution check as substitute property (JA162 ¶NN). The separate Columbia IRA is JA158 ¶Q; Porter's $1,333.45 is neither. The appellate pension qualification is conditional on legal inalienability; it does not find either identified item inalienable. An agreement does not by itself demonstrate §853(a), §853(p), the value ceiling, or §853(d)'s two presumption predicates. Lawful acquisition also does not itself defeat a proved substitute-property route. The commingling branch's precise statutory condition remains inability to divide the property **without difficulty**, not a different tracing formula.

These findings make the strongest objection to procedural affirmance more concrete: the record contains affirmative reasons to doubt that a broad agreement and aggregate proceeds establish lawful forfeiture of every listed interest. All eight frozen rows already reject that substantive inference. Their L1 conclusion rests on the offense/sentence distinction and Rule 11(f)'s function, not on a finding that the assets actually satisfy §853. Their waiver disagreement concerns the statutory special-verdict right and the actual agreement/colloquy; none assumes an express personal admission of each substitute-property predicate. The confirmed personal sentencing objection remains preserved and is not recast as mere nonassertion.

## Remedy and downstream requirements

The six affirming rows remain confined to the Rule 11(f) and forfeiture-jury grounds presented. They leave the existing defendant sentence in place as a consequence of that procedural affirmance, without a new finding that every listed item is forfeitable. That consequence does not turn statutory legality into an irrelevant question or dispose of a properly presented independent substantive challenge. None has selected an additional own-asset merits ruling here. The independent petition and merits framing, rather than missing source review, define this scope.

The Stevens/Souter remand remains an actual waiver inquiry; it is not already satisfied by the ineffective defendant-specific April reopening. A supported existing informed waiver, a later valid waiver, a proper jury determination and rejection of a sentence-contingent bargain are distinct possible later states. Then-Rule 11(e)(1)(C)'s withdrawal consequence applies if that contingent bargain is rejected; this supplement does not find that condition or automatic severability. No return of all property, unconditional retention of every other plea benefit, or automatic reopening of guilt is ordered by either row.

The previous generic unread-appendix limitation is superseded by the source completion and clean validation. A future asset-level conclusion must name the specific item, statutory route, ownership, trial/hearing evidence and procedural force of any finding; an inalienability conclusion additionally needs the actual plan/distribution terms and applicable law. This is a precise further-path limit, not a claim that those records are absent. No retained procedural conclusion relies on absence of substantive findings, a universal substitute-property finding, a lost objection or a completed defendant-specific return order.
