## Component definitions and independently adopted common positions

These common propositions are adopted by each of the eight Associates unless a stated limit identifies a smaller group. They are commitments for later compatibility testing, not a present Court opinion.

**J — ordinary review and original jurisdiction.** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars the ordinary certiorari petition directed to the Eleventh Circuit's authorization denial; dismiss that part for want of jurisdiction. Its express removal of appeals, rehearing petitions and certiorari petitions does not repeal the distinct original-application jurisdiction expressly conferred on this Court by §2241. Paragraph (3)(A) specifically governs filing in district court. A direct application seeking relief from custody is different from reviewing the circuit's authorization decision, and its Article III character is appellate despite its original filing form. Section 1651 supplies no freestanding enlargement of jurisdiction or right to evade the review bar.

The contrary reading that (3)(E) abolishes every Supreme Court habeas channel cannot account for the retained §2241 text and the statute's channel-specific language. Yerger supplies the separate-source and no-implied-repeal principle; McCardle permits withdrawal of the specified appellate route, without deciding Congress's power to eliminate all judicial remedies. The contrary claim that every restriction on Supreme Court review necessarily violates Article III fails on this narrower statutory construction. Klein does not prohibit Congress from establishing generally applicable habeas standards; no provision here commands the Court to decide a particular factual issue falsely or to apply an unchanged rule to a legislatively dictated result.

**T — temporal operation.** Apply (3)(E) to the requested review of the May 2, 1996 authorization ruling. This regulates a post-April 24 authorization proceeding and its requested appellate channel; it does not increase punishment for the underlying crime or reopen a completed judgment. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg use their adopted Landgraf/Rivers sequential inquiry. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas use their separate relevant-activity approach, with the post-enactment application/review as the pertinent activity. Breyer applies the controlling sequential rule without inventing an earlier join. This conclusion does not transfer chapter 154's express pending-case clause to all Title I provisions or decide every new consequence of §§2254(d) and (e).

**S — successive standards and constitutional reach.** Preserving jurisdiction does not guarantee its exercise or exempt a state-custody application from substantive habeas law. Paragraphs (b)(1) and (b)(2) concern distinct claim categories; paragraph (3)'s prima facie authorization is not a final merits adjudication, and paragraph (4) preserves a later district determination. A demand to announce a new constitutional rule now does not satisfy (2)(A)'s already announced, previously unavailable, Supreme-Court-retroactive-rule condition. Paragraph (2)(B) requires BOTH due-diligence unavailability and clear-and-convincing whole-evidence proof that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. Neither a credible innocence contention nor a Schlup showing automatically supplies all these predicates.

O'Connor, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reserve the full direct reach of (b)(1)–(2) to original Supreme Court applications: their original-application conclusions below do not require resolving that general question. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas read the substantive successive state-custody language to apply to this original application as well; the district-specific wording in (3) does not narrow (1)–(2). Their additional textual conclusion is not attributed to the other five. All retain Rule 20's separate exceptional-circumstances and no-adequate-other-relief requirements. None treats (3)(E), by itself, as a suspension of the writ while §2241 remains available. No group decides that the survival of an empty or legally inadequate remedy would necessarily satisfy the Suspension Clause. Broader challenges to state-merits deference, failed-development hearing restrictions, or an established innocent prisoner's exclusion are not resolved merely by approving this review-channel restriction.

**C — the challenged reasonable-doubt instruction.** Deny relief on this claim after applying actual Victor to the whole charge and the prosecutor's related voir-dire statements. The charge keeps every essential fact within the State's burden throughout; explicitly distinguishes criminal proof from civil preponderance; locates doubt in evidence, its absence or conflict; requires acquittal if the jurors remain wavering, unsettled and unsatisfied; requires wholly circumstantial proof to exclude every other reasonable theory; rejects suspicion, conjecture and possibility as a basis for conviction; and again requires acquittal for reasonable doubt on each count. Moral certainty does not stand as an alternative authorization to use personal ethical belief. The complete context supplies no reasonable likelihood of conviction below Winship's standard.

The strongest contrary path treats the antiquated terminology, prosecutorial repetition and truth-seeking language as a substitute for the criminal burden. That reading is rejected in this charge because the instructions actually define the required doubt through the evidence and direct acquittal when that doubt remains; the result does not follow simply from finding a correct sentence elsewhere. No new unpleaded instruction challenge is created from other language in the complete charge. Sullivan would foreclose an evidence-strength harmlessness rationale if the proof standard were vitiated, but that trigger is not met. The disputed date of the attempted first filing, formal docketing after Sullivan and amendment opportunity need not be converted into a new cause, retroactivity or default holding.

**P — independent trial-process claim.** Deny the proposed categorical due-process/Eighth Amendment claim that a nonphysician may not supply this expert evidence. Ake, Ford and Eddings require their actual protections and reinforce reliability; they do not establish the new physician-only evidentiary rule sought here. Tillman's qualifications were exposed to examination; the defense offered contrary medical testimony; Whitaker independently supported the earlier chronology; and the charge left expert weight to the jury and excluded opinions whose assumed facts were not proved. The later medical challenge does not by itself establish that admitting Tillman's testimony made this trial fundamentally unfair under Lisenba. No knowing false testimony, concealed credentials or denial of a requested constitutionally required expert is established by these papers. This is a rejection of the presented constitutional trial ground, not an adjudication that all forensic observations were correct.

The contrary path has force because Arden attacks the autopsy's observations and omissions, not merely professional credentials. Those matters remain material to innocence and the reliability of the chronology. They do not eliminate the distinction between a later expert disagreement and a demonstrated constitutional defect in the trial process. All eight reject relief on the automatic physician-only theory; Stevens and Souter's further original-application examination below concerns freestanding innocence, not adoption of that categorical trial rule.

**Evidence assessment common minimum.** Spitz and Arden give a medically reasoned later-death opinion, not simply an additional vote on professional qualifications. Arden addresses apparent rigor at recovery, temperature, limited skin slippage and marbling, organs, absence of purging or aquatic disturbance, changes during the ensuing twenty-four hours in air, inadequate sampling and the limited value of exhumation. He also contests the physical interpretation of alleged binding and sexual injury. Both experts reviewed Whitaker's testimony. These opinions strengthen the defense chronology; describing them as only cumulative credentials would fail to address their content.

The opposing evidence is also real: Whitaker favored death near disappearance while allowing three to five days; his medical qualifications and examination are independent of Tillman's credentials. The abandoned car, the victim's disappearance, the November 24 call from Felker's home, and the same clothing when the body was recovered supply circumstantial support for an earlier death. The lower opinion also recounts similar-crime evidence; its permitted trial use is not enlarged into an unrestricted propensity inference. None of these circumstances alone supplies a medical time of death. The experts' asserted certainty likewise is not a judicial finding. The difference below is the legal significance of this combined showing at the original-writ screening stage.

## Individual enhanced commitments

### Stevens

**Result and scope:** Adopt J, T, S's reservation, C and P. Dismiss ordinary certiorari; retain the direct original application for a limited return and fuller threshold examination of its expressly pleaded independent innocence claim. Do not grant the writ, find innocence, authorize a district successive filing, or remand the barred circuit authorization judgment. On A-890, support limited §2251 protection against execution while that specified original-application examination remains pending, subject to prompt modification or termination when the Court resolves that need.

**Own authority and ground:** His join in Blackmun's actual Herrera legal/remand dissent recognizes an independent Eighth/Fourteenth Amendment injury upon probable actual innocence, exceeding merely showing that a juror might doubt guilt. His Schlup opinion requires the whole probative record and distinct gateway/merits inquiries; his Lonchar opinion forbids replacing identified required examination with generalized finality or delay, while preserving lawful screening. These commitments support obtaining the State's focused response and necessary underlying forensic record before terminating this direct innocence claim on the supplied characterization.

**Alternative paths and objections:** Denial under extraordinary-writ discretion has strong support in Rule 20, the already-litigated time-of-death dispute, Whitaker and the circumstantial record. But the detailed new forensic objections, coupled with the asserted post-November 25 alibi, are capable of bearing directly on probable actual innocence; the lower characterization that no one questioned Whitaker's qualifications does not answer them. Conversely, immediate relief or an automatic evidentiary hearing would overstate the affidavits, disregard the opposing evidence and skip statutory/procedural predicates. Choose limited examination, not either extreme. No Schlup gateway is declared met, and no diligence or exhaustion finding is supplied.

**Join barriers/remedy:** Refuse a categorical no-innocence right, a new universal statutory bar on original petitions, or terminal original denial based merely on the lower court's cumulative-evidence label. Require separate consideration of claimed inadequacy of other available remedies; the bare existence of a Georgia extraordinary-motion statute is not a finding of an effective current avenue. A return may establish a lawful terminal ground without a hearing. No district transfer is ordered to evade (3)(A). The temporary protection has a decisional ending event, not an invented present warrant date or an indefinite right throughout future litigation.

**Signed application ground:** A-890 should receive protection limited to the direct examination just identified because executing the applicant during that examination would irreversibly defeat it, whereas prompt threshold review may dispose of the request without granting the writ. This position rests on this detailed innocence submission and the pending original request, not on capital status or filing alone. It does not continue or dissolve a historical stay.

### O'Connor

**Result and scope:** Adopt J, T, S's reservation, C and P. Deny the original application and A-890. No further federal proceeding is ordered on this submission.

**Own authority and ground:** Her actual Victor opinion supplies the contextual charge analysis. Her actual Herrera plurality and Kennedy-joined concurrence preserve the intolerability of executing an established innocent person while permitting denial of an unpersuasive submission without deciding a freestanding federal remedy. Her Schlup join requires more than medical evidence that could persuade some juror; the whole record must make it more likely than not that no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict. Her Lonchar reservation rejects an unnecessary general process-protection enlargement, while her Bowersox final position insists on substantial grounds for successive temporary relief.

**Alternative paths and objections:** The strongest path to further examination is that two qualified experts supply concrete physiological reasons undermining the central chronology. Accept their probative importance. Nevertheless, their retrospective interpretation concerns a chronology already contested at trial; Whitaker's medically informed earlier estimate and the independent disappearance evidence remain evidence a reasonable juror could credit. These papers do not demonstrate the Schlup gateway or an exceptionally persuasive affirmative innocence showing sufficient to justify this Court's extraordinary intervention. Denial does not require finding the experts false. The broader categorical exclusion endorsed by Scalia and Thomas goes beyond her reserved Herrera position and is unnecessary.

**Join barriers/remedy:** Do not join an assertion that Herrera established a unified innocence burden, that clemency or a named state statute conclusively supplies an adequate remedy, or that late filing alone is disqualifying. No finding that every statutory bar applies is needed: Rule 20 and the lack of a sufficiently persuasive claim independently support denial. Do not decide broad §2254(d)/(e) constitutionality or Georgia's chapter 154 qualification.

**Signed application ground:** Deny A-890 because the charge and trial-process theories provide no substantial relief ground and the original innocence showing does not warrant further extraordinary proceedings. The existence of jurisdiction and the irreversible nature of execution do not independently establish temporary entitlement. No present execution event is found and no earlier Supreme Court stay is altered.

### Scalia

**Result and scope:** Adopt J, T, the textual direct-application reading in S, C and P. Deny original habeas and A-890.

**Own authority and ground:** His Sullivan opinion makes an actually defective burden structural, but his full Victor join supports rejecting this contextual challenge before that consequence arises. **Alternative paths and objections:** Section 2241's retained jurisdiction is the strongest objection to applying the new substantive limits, but jurisdiction to entertain an application is different from entitlement to grant it: (1)–(2) speak to successive state-custody claims while only the authorization paragraph is expressly district-specific. No newly announced Supreme Court-retroactive rule supplies (2)(A), and new professional opinions about available trial materials do not themselves establish (2)(B)'s due-diligence-unavailable predicate and constitutional-error innocence requirements. This is not a finding of purposeful withholding. Independently, Rule 20 permits denial; the medical disagreement does not transform his rejected freestanding-right theory into a constitutional trial violation.

**Join barriers/remedy:** Will join the narrower denial assuming an extraordinary innocence claim if it preserves his contrary view of that claim's existence. Refuse a mandatory direct-petition factual inquiry merely because affidavits dispute expert evidence, and refuse to treat original jurisdiction as an exception to every statutory habeas limit. No blanket ruling on every AEDPA provision or all possible Suspension Clause applications is necessary.

**Signed application ground:** Deny A-890: ordinary gatekeeping review is barred; retained original jurisdiction supplies no substantial ground for the presented claims under the successive standards and his stated constitutional position. Sullivan supplies no stay where its defective-charge predicate is absent. He adopts no inference about an execution date or prior order.

### Souter
