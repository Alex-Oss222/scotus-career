## Actual posture and supported alternatives

The First Amendment complaint was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6). Retaliatory purpose and removal at the mayor's direction are accepted allegations for this motion, not findings. The city maintained a longstanding rotation list, called providers in turn, and moved to the next if a provider could not respond. O'Hare alleges continued satisfactory-service assurances, refusal of a campaign contribution, support for the incumbent's opponent and display of opponent posters, followed by removal after the incumbent won. The city neither paid O'Hare nor made a direct municipal towing contract; vehicle owners paid. This is an existing government-controlled referral benefit, not an initial unsuccessful bid. Alleged service adequacy cannot be displaced by an invented neutral performance finding.

The separately rejected procedural-due-process/property claim is outside the submitted First Amendment review. No property entitlement is established, and Perry makes lack of entitlement different from lawful withdrawal for an unconstitutional reason. Reversal of the First Amendment dismissal establishes neither actual retaliation, municipal attribution, individual liability or immunity, damages, permanent referrals, nor a new property right.

**A — extend Elrod/Branti to the existing referral relationship.** Government may not terminate this continuing service benefit because the provider refuses partisan support or supports an opponent, unless political affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance of the service. Branti's exception is functional, not a label such as contractor, policymaker or confidential provider. No accepted allegation makes support of a mayor an appropriate towing qualification. Traditional patronage benefits and litigation costs are the principal contrary arguments; on A they do not justify selective coercion unrelated to the actual service. Rutan and Perry supply the employee/benefit analogy, extended here rather than treated as an existing contractor holding.

**N — narrower protection of the alleged electoral speech and contribution refusal.** Contractor/referral status does not categorically permit withdrawal of this established benefit to induce a contribution or punish public support for an opposing candidate. For the speech aspect, evaluate public-concern expression and genuine interests connected to the actual service relationship through a case-sensitive Pickering balance. The pleaded demand for electoral support has no service connection that can sustain dismissal. Ordinary neutral qualifications and proved same-decision grounds remain. N does not adopt every Elrod/Branti rule for affiliation-only contractor disputes or every employment benefit; it is a usable rule for the conduct pleaded here, rather than an unexplained instruction to provide some protection.

N's strongest objection is that campaign contribution, public support and affiliation are closely related, so limiting the holding may postpone an unavoidable patronage question. Its supporters nevertheless distinguish a present case of withdrawing an established outside business benefit for private electoral expression from deciding every affiliation-based governmental selection. They reject a merely relabeled universal patronage exemption and accept that the present facts cannot be dismissed under contractor status alone. A's supporters regard the functional Branti rule as the more direct and administrable account.

**B — retain the contractor/referral exclusion.** Decline to extend employment patronage and retaliation rules to this nonemployment business allocation; affirm the First Amendment dismissal without finding a neutral actual motive. The government retains authority to organize referrals and choose among independent businesses, and Rutan's dissent supplies the argument against expanding judicial supervision of patronage. B's strongest objection is Perry plus the pleaded use of valuable public leverage to exact electoral support: economic independence and absence of a property right do not by themselves make that reason permissible. Each B supporter confronts that objection below rather than changing the allegations.

**Proof and remedy limits:** A's and N's supporters reverse the First Amendment dismissal and remand for proceedings under their stated protection. Where disputed causation matters, protected conduct must be a substantial or motivating factor and the city may prove by a preponderance under Mt. Healthy that it would have acted the same way without it. Settle and the shared Waters principles preclude treating a hypothetical lawful explanation as established proof. Branti's particular affiliation inquiry must not silently become generic balancing; conversely, an affiliation rule does not decide every later mixed criticism/performance dispute. The appropriate speech balance remains available when that different issue actually arises. Neutral safety, responsiveness and service-qualification decisions remain lawful on their actual grounds. No compulsory hearing, trial or restoration order is selected now. B's supporters affirm the reviewed dismissal; all eight leave the separate due-process judgment outside this review.

## Eight independent commitments

### Stevens

**Result/ground:** A; reverse the First Amendment dismissal and remand. His own Rutan concurrence directly rejects the privilege/entitlement shortcut and explains why prohibition of an impermissible reason does not create tenure. It distinguishes public-service interests from partisan advantage and accepts affiliation requirements only where they actually serve effective performance. His actual NTEU opinion establishes that economic burdens can inhibit citizen expression, and his Waters actual-speech position preserves protection against retaliation for what a person actually said.

**Alternative and barrier:** B invokes discretionary referrals and less dependence than salaried employment; neither supplies a legitimate reason to demand a contribution or punish opposing support. N would dispose of the pleaded case but leaves the closely connected association claim unnecessarily uncertain; he adopts the full functional A rule for this established relationship. He does not adopt strict scrutiny for every mixed contractor speech dispute or infer a property right. No generic patronage-system benefit may replace Branti's service-specific exception. Remedy: reverse/remand only the submitted First Amendment claim, with actual proof, attribution and damages unresolved.

### O'Connor

**Result/ground:** N; reverse/remand the First Amendment dismissal, reserving the full affiliation-only contractor extension. Her own actual NTEU separate position protects unrelated citizen expression through a case-specific Pickering balance while recognizing genuine preventive and administrative interests. Her Waters plurality gives the government meaningful service latitude only within the proper speech rule and an adequate actual factual basis. Her adopted Rosenberger rule also distinguishes program-specific private expression from general control of a recipient's viewpoints.

**Alternative and barrier:** Her Rutan Parts II–III join includes the proposal to overrule Elrod/Branti and the independent refusal to expand them; she is not assigned Part I or treated as having silently withdrawn Part II-C. That is a substantial barrier to A's wholesale contractor affiliation rule. But B would allow the city's control of an established outside referral stream to punish private campaign expression and contribution refusal without any service connection. The city buys no speech or towing here, and the allegations do not concern selection of an internal political team. N protects the pleaded conduct while preserving a real service-interest inquiry and declining to settle abstract party-affiliation selection. She refuses an automatic NTEU heightened standard or full Waters investigation mandate. Remedy: N's limited reversal, preserving the separate property judgment and all unresolved factual defenses.

### Scalia

**Result/ground:** B; affirm the First Amendment dismissal. His Rutan dissent defends governmental latitude in patronage choices, disputes that every economic inducement is constitutionally forbidden coercion, and alternatively limits Elrod/Branti rather than expanding them beyond their employment setting. His actual NTEU dissent acknowledges financial speech burdens but gives administrative and predictive governmental interests weight; his genuine-ground Waters concurrence does not itself establish contractor coverage.

**Alternative and barrier:** A has the strongest constitutional-conditions analogy because O'Hare alleges loss of a longstanding benefit, not a new application. N emphasizes personal electoral speech and the refused donation. He regards both as extensions into the same public-benefit patronage choice rather than a distinction that avoids it. The no-property ruling alone is insufficient; his sufficient ground is refusal to extend the employment restriction to nonemployment referrals. His Rosenberger join concerns an opened private-publication program, not every nonexpressive service allocation. The nonpurchaser fact does not transform dispatch administration into a public speech forum, though it limits any characterization of the city as employer. He assumes the allegations, finds no independent neutral motive and creates no general power to regulate citizens' political expression. If protection is adopted, he preserves actual causal proof and lawful service choices, without adding a vote to A or N. Remedy: affirm the reviewed First Amendment dismissal.

### Kennedy

**Result/ground:** N; reverse/remand for the pleaded electoral-expression and contribution-refusal injury, without a full join in A's affiliation-only extension. His actual NTEU Part I join treats financial restraints on independent citizen expression as requiring an adequate governmental connection; his Rosenberger opinion protects private editorial choices within the actual program. His Waters genuine-ground concurrence permits substantial management discretion without permitting the asserted ground to be a fiction.

**Alternative and barrier:** His full Rutan dissent join includes all three contrary grounds: tradition, substantive objections to Elrod/Branti and refusal to extend those cases. Their continued force makes A's general contractor-patronage rule a material join barrier. B nevertheless goes too far on these allegations: the city uses an established, independently operated referral benefit to penalize the citizen's own opponent advocacy and refusal to finance the incumbent, not to select an internal official whose public work embodies an electoral program. N gives those later adopted speech/benefit principles effect without treating Rutan as reversed or every contractor preference as unconstitutional. He preserves an actual service-related balance for mixed speech disputes and lawful same-decision proof. Colorado's party-expression concurrence supports the importance of independent electoral choices but supplies no governing patronage holding. Remedy: N's bounded reversal; no permanent list entitlement or actual motive finding.

### Souter

**Result/ground:** A; reverse/remand. His adopted Waters objective-reasonableness position and NTEU Part I join attend to both the citizen's expression and the government's actual service interests. His Settle opinion insists on genuine motive and proper Mt. Healthy proof. Applied to pleaded partisan removal from a continuing list, those commitments support the functional affiliation rule rather than categorical contractor exclusion.

**Alternative and barrier:** B's litigation and independent-business concerns do not show why partisan support is an appropriate towing qualification. N protects much of the alleged conduct but separates intimately connected speech and association without a service-based reason; A states the relevant limit directly. The Branti exception remains operative where the function genuinely requires affiliation, and its absence here follows the pleading record, not a universal finding about all contractors. He refuses to infer actual liability, convert an unsupported efficiency argument into a finding, or reopen due process. Remedy: A's ordinary further proceedings with attribution, causal proof and relief unresolved.

### Thomas

**Result/ground:** B; affirm the First Amendment dismissal. His own adopted NTEU dissent permits meaningful administrative control despite a financial effect on expression, and his Waters concurrence resists enlarging procedural and substantive restraints beyond the justified employment rule. Rutan's dissent supplies a persuasive anti-extension argument, but he is not falsely assigned a prior Rutan vote or writing. His adopted Rosenberger holding remains confined to its private-publication program.

**Alternative and barrier:** Perry, the existing referral stream and the alleged support-based reason make A/N substantial alternatives; neither economic independence nor absence of a property entitlement alone answers them. He nevertheless distinguishes the city's organization of nonexpressive towing referrals from an opened program for private speech or a law forbidding political advocacy. He declines the proposed employment-to-business extension while accepting the allegations as pleaded. This does not find service inadequacy, prove a same-decision defense, approve a particular contribution as noncorrupt, or declare that government may impose any condition whatever. His Colorado party-expression join neither merges contributions with expenditures nor resolves public patronage. If A/N controls, ordinary proof and neutral service defenses remain necessary; that reservation is not a merits join. Remedy: affirm the reviewed dismissal; no new immunity/property adjudication.

### Ginsburg

**Result/ground:** A; reverse/remand. Her actual Waters plurality and NTEU Part I joins require attention to legitimate governmental interests while protecting economically burdened citizen expression. Her Settle join rejects substitution of a possible lawful reason for a proved causal account. O'Hare alleges a straightforward political-support condition unrelated to the service, sufficient to survive this categorical pleading dismissal.

**Alternative and barrier:** B invokes dependence and institutional discretion, but a longstanding referral benefit remains economically valuable and its loss can burden association without a salaried employment relation. N is a permissible narrower disposition, but she accepts the more direct A analogy for this existing relationship with Branti's genuine functional exception. Her Colorado contribution-channel dissent addressed limits protecting candidate-financing integrity; it supplies no defense to a city's alleged extraction of support for an incumbent. She rejects a finding of actual coercion or retaliation merely from the pleading result and preserves neutral service choices, separate liability elements and the unreviewed due-process disposition. Remedy: A.

### Breyer

**Result/ground:** A; reverse/remand. His actual NTEU Part I join recognizes the practical burden on citizen expression, and his Settle join preserves genuine service authority plus actual causal proof. The maintained rotation and existing participation make the employee-benefit analogy sufficiently close for the functional affiliation rule, without deciding the treatment of every initial applicant or contracting program.

**Alternative and barrier:** B's concern about turning commercial disappointments into constitutional cases is addressed by proving protected conduct, causation, actual responsibility and injury, with a same-decision defense. Here the pleaded cause is opposing support and refusal to contribute; no functional political qualification or neutral service decision is established. N would also remove the improper categorical bar, but a general Pickering balance must not obscure Branti's particular affiliation exception when partisan support itself is demanded. His A join permits a proper balance for materially different speech/performance disputes and does not import NTEU's prospective-policy burden wholesale. He had no Rutan or Waters participation, and none is invented. Remedy: A; no guaranteed restoration, trial or damages.
