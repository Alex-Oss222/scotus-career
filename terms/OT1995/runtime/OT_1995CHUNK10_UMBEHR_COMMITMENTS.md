## Supported paths and operative scope

**A — existing-relationship protection with a service-sensitive Pickering rule.** Contractor status alone does not remove protection against termination of this existing relationship for public-concern citizen speech. The claimant must establish protected speech and its substantial or motivating role. Assess content, form and context under Connick, and balance the citizen/public interest against legitimate governmental interests arising from the actual service relationship under Pickering. The county need not have directly bought the service to possess legitimate organizational interests, but its role also cannot be inflated into complete control of an employee. Independence, other customers, contact with officials, service responsibilities and supported operational concerns matter insofar as they bear on that balance; personal offense at criticism does not itself establish disruption. The government may prove by a preponderance under Mt. Healthy that it would have made the same decision without protected conduct.

The strongest objection is that choosing outside providers entails discretion and litigation costs unlike public employment. A answers through the actual governmental-interest balance, the claimant's causal burden, the same-decision defense and the existing-relationship limit. Perry precludes treating absence of tenure as a complete answer, while contractual termination authority remains available for lawful reasons. This is an extension of the employee-derived rule, not a claim that Pickering already decided contractor cases.

**B — decline the new contractor cause of action.** Preserve Pickering in public employment and refuse to extend it to termination/nonrenewal of this independently operated commercial relationship. Government must select and organize service arrangements, and converting disappointed contractors into constitutional litigants risks judicial supervision of ordinary business judgments. Rutan's extension dispute and the broader public-benefit distinction supply the comparison; the rule would sustain the categorical result, not find a neutral actual motive.

B's strongest objection is that the accepted record assumes economic punishment for public criticism and supplies an established valuable relationship, while Perry rejects the right/privilege shortcut. Contractor independence can strengthen, rather than diminish, the citizen's speech interest. The county's nonpurchaser role also weakens a claim of ordinary employer control. The individual commitments below state why their chosen path survives those objections; categorical nonprotection is not disguised as a factual same-decision finding.

**Other scope choices:** Automatic strict scrutiny or an absolute rule against every adverse response to contractor speech would disregard genuine service interests and the distinction between regulation of the public and management of a service relationship. Conversely, a possible lawful service reason does not prove the actual reason or the Mt. Healthy counterfactual. Initial bidders, all future contract awards and pure-affiliation patronage are reserved. Existing Waters holdings remain operative at their exact coalition levels; no universal investigation checklist or compulsory full-plurality framework is created. NTEU's special prospective-burden formulation is not imported into an individual termination.

**Remedy adopted by A's supporters:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit's rejection of categorical exclusion and its remand of the surviving official-capacity claims, with the stated standard. The courts below must address any unresolved public-concern/balance, causation, independent same-decision, entity-attribution and injury issues under the actual procedural posture. No actual retaliatory finding, automatic trial, renewal, reinstatement, damages award or property entitlement follows. Individual immunity and McClure's separate judgment remain undisturbed. B's supporters would reverse the reviewed appellate disposition and restore judgment against the official-capacity First Amendment claim on their categorical ground, leaving those other dispositions intact.

## Eight independent commitments

### Stevens

**Result/ground:** A; affirm and remand within its stated limits. His own Rutan concurrence explains that prohibiting an impermissible reason neither creates tenure nor installs a judicial civil-service code, and applies Perry to valuable benefits. His actual NTEU opinion treats economic burdens as speech burdens while retaining a public-service balance. His Waters actual-speech dissent rejects allowing a false account to erase actually protected expression.

**Alternative and barrier:** B's commercial discretion does not explain why an assumed retaliatory public-criticism reason becomes lawful merely because the speaker works independently. His Rutan distinction between partisan benefit and public service supports rejecting generalized patronage as a substitute for a genuine interest here. He accepts A's balance and proof requirements, not automatic liability or universal strict scrutiny. He will not join a newly expanded reasonable-mistake defense beyond controlling Waters or characterize his Waters dissent as assent to the seven-Justice safe harbor. That unresolved factual-account fork need not be decided on the present assumptions. Remedy: A, including preserved immunity/McClure judgments.

### O'Connor

**Result/ground:** A; affirm the limited official-capacity remand. Her actual Waters plurality protects citizen speech while accommodating genuine reasonable service judgments; her NTEU separate position invalidates an unjustified financial burden on unrelated expression through a case-specific Pickering balance. Those grounds support protection calibrated to this arrangement, not an automatic heightened standard for all contracting rules.

**Alternative and barrier:** Her Rutan join in Parts II–III is a serious counterweight: Part II-C favored overruling Elrod/Branti, and Part III independently resisted expansion. She is not assigned Part I's tradition-dispositive method. This case concerns termination for criticism of public administration, not an attempt to expand party-affiliation personnel rules or require hiring a new applicant. The county's contractual benefits and service role therefore support the Pickering analogy without retracting her patronage position. She refuses both categorical commercial immunity and a rule that treats every service concern as pretext. No complete Waters investigatory framework or NTEU prospective-rule presumption is required by her join. Remedy: A.

### Scalia

**Result/ground:** B; reverse the reviewed official-capacity ruling. His own Rutan dissent opposes extending judicial restrictions on governmental personnel choices, distinguishes government employment from regulation of private citizens, and rejects an unadministrable expansion justified by economic coercion alone. His actual NTEU dissent, joined by Thomas, gives weight to preventive and administrative governmental interests while acknowledging that compensation affects speech. His broader genuine-ground Waters concurrence operates within employment; it is not a prior adoption of contractor coverage.

**Alternative and barrier:** A correctly invokes Perry and an existing valuable benefit, and the county cannot claim to have purchased these services. Nonetheless, he treats the county's selection of a continuing commercial arrangement as governmental administration, rather than a private-expression forum or regulation forbidding criticism. His adopted Rosenberger rule concerned an opened private-publication program and expressly does not turn every public expenditure into a speech forum. Other clients and contractual remedies are relevant to the relationship, not proof of zero injury or a defense to an already established right. He declines this extension without overruling Pickering, Perry or Rutan, without blessing every conceivable unconstitutional condition, and without pretending a neutral actual reason has been proved. If A controls, its limited remand and ordinary causal defenses are preferable to an absolute contractor right, but that conditional preference adds no merits join. Remedy: B.

### Kennedy

**Result/ground:** A; affirm and remand. His actual NTEU Part I join protects employees' independent citizen expression against insufficiently related financial restraints; his Rosenberger opinion distinguishes private expression from governmental speech and preserves a program-specific viewpoint rule. His Waters concurrence with Scalia retains substantial managerial latitude but requires the genuine asserted ground. These positions support protection for public criticism by an independent business while respecting actual service interests.

**Alternative and barrier:** His complete Rutan dissent join includes tradition, systemic patronage interests, the proposed overruling of Elrod/Branti and the narrower anti-extension position. Those grounds weigh against treating every commercial selection as ordinary political-expression litigation. They do not make public-administration criticism identical to affiliation patronage, nor do they answer the actual NTEU principle that economic leverage must be assessed in relation to governmental work. He accepts A in this existing arrangement; he does not adopt universal equality between contractors and employees or decide pure affiliation here. Preserve his broader genuine-ground Waters position as noncontrolling where it exceeds the shared safe harbor; this record does not require choosing a new mistake rule. Remedy: A.

### Souter

**Result/ground:** A; affirm and remand. His adopted objective-reasonableness Waters position and NTEU Part I join protect citizen expression through attention to the government's actual role and the burden imposed. His unanimous Settle opinion requires genuine causal examination and preserves Mt. Healthy; a hypothesized permissible decision is not proof of the decision actually made.

**Alternative and barrier:** B's concern about routine contracting disputes is substantial but is addressed by the public-concern and service-interest balance, protected-conduct causation and the preponderance same-decision defense. The assumed retaliatory termination of an ongoing arrangement cannot be equated with rejecting a first bidder. He rejects an inference of no injury from five replacement city contracts and refuses to decide the truth of Umbehr's accusations. He adopts no automatic finding that all criticism prevails in the balance and no full investigatory rule beyond the issue presented. Remedy: A; preserve unrelated affirmed judgments.

### Thomas

**Result/ground:** B; reverse the reviewed official-capacity disposition. His own adopted NTEU dissent recognizes a speech burden but sustains latitude for government to administer relationships through preventive rules; his Waters concurrence accepts a genuine-ground approach and resists added procedural supervision. These support declining to create the proposed contractor extension rather than treating existing employee holdings as already controlling it. He had no Rutan participation and is assigned none.

**Alternative and barrier:** A has substantial support in Perry, the assumed critical-speech motive and the loss of an established benefit. His Rosenberger join also forbids viewpoint exclusion from the particular private-publication program. That rule expressly does not make every publicly controlled economic arrangement an expressive forum; this arrangement organizes refuse services. He considers extension of employment litigation into that business choice unwarranted even though the county is not a direct purchaser. This is a legal category conclusion, not a finding of efficient motive, negligible loss, no speech burden, or a general surrender of First Amendment rights. His Colorado join protecting party-authored political expression likewise supplies no employment/contractor equivalence. He reserves distinct direct regulation or expressive-program conditions and does not purport to overrule existing cases. If A controls, causation and genuine lawful defenses must remain; he supplies no affirmative A vote through that conditional position. Remedy: B.

### Ginsburg

**Result/ground:** A; affirm and remand. Her actual Waters plurality join requires genuine, objectively reasonable service grounds at that writing's stated scope; her NTEU Part I join recognizes financial restraints on citizen expression and demands a sufficient connection to public work. Her Settle join preserves actual motive and proof of the same-decision defense.

**Alternative and barrier:** B's lower-dependence and litigation concerns do not justify categorical power to withdraw an ongoing valuable arrangement for protected public criticism. They may bear on the real burden or operational balance; they do not negate it as a matter of law. Her Colorado dissent's institutional contribution-channel rationale does not validate non-financing retaliation for a citizen's viewpoint. She refuses both absolute contractor immunity from service decisions and a declaration that all assumed matters have been proved. The statutory/capacity limits are independent of the constitutional extension. Remedy: A, preserving immunity and McClure's judgment.

### Breyer

**Result/ground:** A; affirm and remand. His actual NTEU Part I join assesses the burden and the government's justified work-related interests, rather than making compensated citizen expression categorically unprotected. His Settle join demands actual causal analysis. He applies controlling Waters at its limited shared scope without an invented prior participation.

**Alternative and barrier:** Commercial flexibility and administrative costs are legitimate concerns supporting a realistic Pickering balance; they do not justify an automatic exemption for assumed retaliation against public criticism. The arrangement's nonpurchaser structure, ongoing benefits and contractor independence make direct importation of every employee detail inappropriate, but not the basic protection. He declines the contractor's stronger absolute viewpoint rule, a NTEU advance-policy presumption and any automatic hearing or trial. Subsequent private arrangements may affect proof of loss without eliminating the claim on this record. Remedy: A.
