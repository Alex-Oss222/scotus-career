## OT1995-047 — Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit and remand. Sustain Congress's clearly enacted direct federal action against Florida for prospective enforcement of IGRA's continuing negotiation duty. Independently permit a responsible officer action under Ex parte Young, confined to that same statutory sequence. This implements the user's attached position and supersedes the earlier preparation's dismissal of both defendants.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Constitutional power, a valid duty, a private cause of action, the defendant and the requested remedy are separate inquiries. Congress's Indian Commerce power and unmistakable IGRA action supply this prospective enforcement route. A State cannot disregard a valid ongoing duty merely because Congress allowed it to be named directly. Florida first receives the statutory opportunity to negotiate; only after the prescribed interval may a court decide good faith, require further negotiations, invoke mediation and allow the later federal process. This allocation preserves state political responsibility while making the federal obligation legally effective. Compliance costs do not by themselves convert prospective relief into accrued treasury compensation.

**Officer route:** Young supplies an independent route against an officer with a real connection to the ongoing violation. A detailed remedial scheme can constrain equitable relief; Stone reads IGRA's sequence as constraining both routes rather than silently extinguishing the officer route. A court cannot prescribe its preferred compact, bypass mediation, enlarge substantive obligations or award retrospective compensation by relabeling it an injunction. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not establish this official-capacity claim.

**Union Gas and precedent:** Do not overrule Union Gas. Its actual damages judgment remains precedent within its scope; a prospective case supplies no basis to erase that judgment while claiming to reserve it. Stone does not endorse every broader abrogation rationale or decide materially different retrospective remedies beyond settled holdings. He proposes the narrower affirmative conclusion necessary here: the clearly enacted prospective Indian Commerce action is lawful. His earlier personal preference against Article I abrogation is a position he now revises, not Court precedent being overruled. Reliance on Congress's express enforcement channel and the Tribe's continuing duty claim supports retaining a forum; a change in Court membership is not the reason.

**Remedy and reservations:** Remand for actual statutory predicates, good-faith proceedings and lawful prospective relief against the State and/or the responsible officer, avoiding double or inconsistent commands. Do not adjudge bad faith or dictate a compact here. Reserve new questions concerning accrued compensatory, punitive or restitutionary liability, compulsory state-court actions and materially different schemes, without describing a question already decided by Union Gas as wholly unsettled. No waiver or new Reconstruction holding is invented.

**Scope:** Preserve both the direct prospective State action and the responsible-officer route within IGRA’s statutory sequence. The availability of one route does not itself justify foreclosing the other.
