## OT1995-031 — Peacock v. Thomas

**Event:** 1996-02-21 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 349

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1453

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse and direct dismissal of the independent-liability action for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Preserve ordinary execution proceedings directed to the judgment debtor’s assets and any separate action against Peacock supported by an independent jurisdictional grant.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Decide subject-matter jurisdiction before veil piercing, fraudulent transfer, or the merits of personal liability.
- **Supporting Authorities:** Simulated *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375; 28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1332, and 1367; Fed. R. Civ. P. 69.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** Ancillary jurisdiction permits proceedings necessary to identify, preserve, and execute against the judgment debtor’s assets, but it does not authorize a new action imposing the debtor’s obligation on a nonparty without an independent basis of federal jurisdiction.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Execution of an existing judgment and creation of a new judgment debtor are legally different exercises of judicial power. The latter requires adjudication of new facts, duties, defenses, and personal liability against a new party, none of which is necessary merely to effectuate the original decree. Remedial need and factual relation cannot replace the jurisdictional source Congress requires.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not restrict contempt, asset discovery, fraudulent-transfer process directed to property of the judgment debtor, or a separately filed action independently within federal jurisdiction.

## OT1995-032 — Jones v. ABC-TV

**Event:** 1996-02-26 | **Category:** APPLICATION | **Action:** Motion for filing relief

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 363

**Docket(s):** No. 95-7186

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 2, 2026; the eligibility and Rule 39.8 branches below approved by the user on October 3, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. **Stone’s disposition:** Grant leave to proceed in forma pauperis if financial eligibility is established, because current frivolousness or malice has not been demonstrated on the available papers. Decline the proposed prospective noncriminal restriction. Consider the petition for certiorari separately. Additionally, future ordinary civil-certiorari submissions must receive individual consideration rather than automatic fee denial based on the filer’s identity and history.

**Approved branches (October 3, 2026):** If financial eligibility is established and current frivolousness or malice is not demonstrated, grant leave and consider the petition separately. If financial eligibility is genuinely not established, deny free filing for this submission only, preserving any lawful opportunity to correct the deficiency or pay; an affidavit missing from the packet is not itself proof that the litigant is financially ineligible. If Rule 39.8 applies, identify the present submission's actual defect and preserve the applicable payment opportunity. In every branch, impose no automatic restriction on future submissions based on identity or history. Fee denial and petition denial are different actions.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** The relevant question under Rule 39.8 concerns the particular submission. Prior unsuccessful or abusive filings may supply context, but cannot establish that a new paper is frivolous or malicious. The published Jones record describes history without supplying the present petition’s particular defect. A finding about that missing defect cannot be manufactured from ABC-TV’s identity or assumptions about the underlying claim. Financial eligibility must still be established; the absence of a demonstrated Rule 39.8 defect does not itself prove indigence.

Actual simulated Martin supports current-paper assessment: it allowed an arguable filing and denied the companion motion on identified defects while preserving a payment opportunity. Both applications remain law. The present record supports allowing filing once financial eligibility is established, not a finding that the petition warrants review or succeeds on its merits.

**Proposed rule for future filings:** Rule 39.8 permits action against a frivolous or malicious submission, not a predictive decision about the contents of an unwritten petition. Stone would require individual consideration of future ordinary civil-certiorari submissions by an otherwise eligible litigant. A blanket requirement to pay because this person previously abused the docket prevents that assessment and risks excluding a later legitimate claim solely because the claimant cannot afford the fee. Inherent docket authority should implement the Court’s filing rules without replacing their current-paper inquiry through a person-based prediction. The Court remains free to reject a defective current submission promptly; individual consideration does not require a merits opinion, hearing or elaborate explanation in every case.

This future-filing rule is a proposed development beyond Martin, Day and Sassower, which declined unsupported restrictions without categorically resolving their validity. Stone does not describe their reserved question as already decided. His rule concerns future ordinary civil-certiorari fee treatment. It does not decide every extraordinary-writ restriction, vacate unreviewed existing orders, establish a right to unlimited fee-free litigation in every court, or eliminate ordinary jurisdictional, timeliness, form and current-paper controls. McDonald and Sindram retain their distinct extraordinary-writ scope; an analogy from those restrictions to a blanket civil-certiorari restriction is rejected, rather than treated as a holding already compelled by Martin.

**Consequences and participation:** Financial leave permits the present petition to be filed without prepayment; it grants neither certiorari nor relief against the respondents. No payment deadline follows from the proposed grant. If a different actual disposition denies financial leave, any lawful payment opportunity belongs to that disposition, not a historical deadline automatically imposed here. Breyer does not participate. This is a Court-directed motion with no invented Circuit Justice presentation. Earlier prepared Attwood reasoning is law only if actually adopted and effective; later Shieh preparation has no backward force.

## OT1995-034 — Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson +

**Event:** 1996-02-26 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 60 F.3d 1411

**Docket(s):** Historical petition No. 95-673; no separate simulated Supreme Court docket assigned; Ninth Circuit No. 95-15449

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; the scope-of-affirmance line below approved by the user on October 3, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Affirm the permanent injunction requiring California's compliance with the NVRA within its enacted federal-election scope. Correct only an actually identified overbroad term; do not remand merely to create a new administrative process.

**Scope of affirmance (approved October 3, 2026):** Stone's affirmance covers the Ninth Circuit's whole judgment, including its implementation remand and retained jurisdiction.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Article I's Elections Clause authorizes Congress to displace state procedural choices for congressional elections. That express allocation differs from Congress commanding a State to administer a general Commerce program under New York. The Act's registration duties concern effective federal electoral participation, not a general revision of state voter qualifications. Federal presidential-election protection has its own supporting constitutional source, including Burroughs; the congressional Elections Clause should not be casually described as supplying every presidential power. The United States' enforcement role is independently material to immunity and cannot be erased by an analogy to a private damages action.

**Application and remedy:** This reviewed appeal concerns a permanent injunction, not provisional likelihood of success. Enforce the actual registration duties, leaving state-only electoral choices outside the Act unless an independent lawful source reaches them. California's preference for reimbursement or a uniform state system does not permit noncompliance with valid federal duties. A real injunction term extending beyond the statute can be narrowed on its own text; no hypothetical overbreadth justifies withholding the required access. Do not change the existing qualifications for federal voters under cover of regulating procedure.

**Limit:** Federal electoral obligations do not establish a general power to commandeer state government. The later prepared Seminole position does not govern this February event.
