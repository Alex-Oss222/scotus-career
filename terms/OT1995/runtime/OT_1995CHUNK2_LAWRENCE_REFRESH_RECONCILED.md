## North Carolina amendment: complete retained conditions

The [official North Carolina Session Law 1995-424](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/SessionLaws/HTML/1995-1996/SL1995-424.html), sections 1–3, agrees with the admitted-source Public Projection. It is effective background law from October 1, 1995, with the following distinct requirements:

- Under amended G.S.49-14(c), posthumous proceedings require an action commenced before the putative father's death; an action commenced within one year after death if no estate-administration proceeding began within that year; or, if estate administration began within that year, commencement within the claims period specified in G.S.28A-19-3(a). A judgment establishing paternity must take effect as of the day before the father's death.
- Under amended G.S.49-14(d), a contested action brought more than three years after the child's birth **or** after the putative father's death requires blood- or genetic-marker-test evidence to establish paternity. This separate proof requirement does not replace the filing conditions.
- The act becomes effective October 1, 1995; applies only to actions commenced on or after that date and before October 1, 1998, regardless of the father's death date; and expires October 1, 1998. Both the qualifying filing alternatives and this commencement window remain operative.

No qualifying paternity action, action date, estate-administration predicate, test evidence, or construction of the cross-referenced estate-claims period is established for Lawrence. A benefits application or federal petition is not deemed a state paternity action. Accordingly, none of the eight adopts the amendment as an independently sufficient GVR ground, a reason for reversal, or an entitlement to reopen or succeed in a paternity proceeding. Its possible relevance is left to lawful consideration on an adequate record, without an instruction that it applies. The October 24 letter's report of enactment does not establish applicability.
