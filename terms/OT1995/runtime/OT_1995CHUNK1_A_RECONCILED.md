## Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431

### Final components and law

Four Associates — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer — affirm denial of Lankford's immunity on the supported assumption of his own knowing, causal acquiescence. **Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal** (Johnson, June 12, 1995) confines review to separable legal immunity on the supported nonmovant assumptions. **Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue** (Elder, February 23, 1994) requires the full relevant conduct-date landscape, not retrospective notice. Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability and personal immunity. Collins separates a federal wrong from attribution, rejects an extra freestanding governmental-power-abuse element, and leaves deliberate bodily injury and different affirmative-deprivation claims outside its voluntary-workplace holding. Farmer supplies a current actual-awareness proof analogy, not a retroactive school duty. Albright has no controlling rationale that eliminates the independent bodily-integrity claim or establishes a universal state-remedy defense.

**D1's rule and limits:** Actual abuse of the teacher's school authority can infringe bodily integrity; off-campus location alone neither proves nor disproves that nexus. The asserted supervisor's own liability requires actual awareness of abuse or substantial abuse risk, deliberately unreasonable nonresponse despite authority and opportunity to act, and causation of injury or its continuation. Circumstantial proof may support awareness, but warning signs are not conclusive and what an official merely should have known is insufficient. Knowing causal acquiescence on Lankford's assumed pattern of dismissed or suppressed reports is personal conduct, not respondeat superior, a general guarantee of school safety, or liability from state law alone.

**D2's opposing ground:** The teacher's obvious wrong does not by itself clearly establish every supervisor's constitutional damages obligation. Rizzo's affirmative-link requirement and the contemporaneous Ford/Reimer/Vela/Chinchello line make the step from omission to personal responsibility materially uncertain. Wanger/Bowen and related cases supply the strongest contrary notice argument, but their actual facts and municipal/custodial distinctions matter. D2 grants immunity without announcing a comprehensive present school-duty rule or denying pupils bodily-integrity protection. A fact-identical precedent is not required; the dispute concerns the content of the then-established responsibility rule.

The January/February Caplinger notice discrepancy remains unresolved. His knowledge is not combined with the principal's undisclosed history. His inquiries, reprimand and warning are evaluated on the supported assumed record; the failure to hold the wider meeting and continued injury remain contrary facts. All eight's legal conclusion is that the particular additional federal duty asserted was not clearly established, not that his responses were necessarily prudent or that ineffective warnings always suffice.

The pre-May 1987 acts and later conduct must remain distinct. Jefferson and Lopez, issued in May, cannot establish earlier notice. D1 treats their actual later holdings as reinforcing an already established restricted knowing-acquiescence principle; its earlier denial does not depend on them. D2 considers them but finds that the direct teacher restraint in Jefferson and the bus-assault supervisory analysis/rejection in Lopez did not clearly resolve the particular noncustodial supervisory and postwarning-monitoring obligation alleged here. Neither group discards later-1987 conduct or treats Canton, Collins, Farmer or current appellate law as conduct-time notice.
