## OT1995-018 — Lawrence v. Chater

**Event:** 1996-01-08 | **Category:** CERTIORARI | **Action:** GVR consideration on certiorari petition

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 163

**Docket(s):** No. 94-9323

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Grant, vacate, and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Return the case for reconsideration without directing an award or adopting the agency’s new interpretation as controlling.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Exercise the Court’s procedural authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 without deciding the claimant’s entitlement or the deference owed to the agency’s new position.
- **Supporting Authorities:** 28 U.S.C. §2106; *Henry v. City of Rock Hill*, 376 U.S. 776; simulated *Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala*, 512 U.S. 504, and *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.*, 512 U.S. 218, solely as remand-side cautions.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** Section 2106 permits a GVR when a material intervening development creates a reasonable possibility that the lower court would reach a different result and reconsideration would conserve judicial resources; the disposition does not adopt the intervening agency position, whose legal force and any deference or persuasive weight remain for independent analysis on remand.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** The power to vacate and remand comes from §2106 and the Court’s supervisory practice, not from administrative-deference doctrine. The agency’s changed position is relevant because it may make the existing judgment obsolete, but vacatur expresses no view on entitlement. *Thomas Jefferson University* and *MCI* matter only after remand, where the lower court must test the position against statutory authority, procedure, consistency, and fair notice rather than accept it merely because the agency now advances it.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** A strategic litigation reversal, immaterial development, or position outside lawful agency authority does not justify a GVR, and a GVR may not be used to predetermine the merits.

## OT1995-020 — Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun

**Event:** 1996-01-09 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 199

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1387

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Affirm the Third Circuit.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Remand for adjudication of the parents’ maritime claims with state wrongful-death and survival remedies available to the extent they do not conflict with an applicable federal statute or a controlling uniform maritime rule.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Resolve the question on maritime statutory and federal-common-law grounds without reaching any unnecessary constitutional issue.
- **Supporting Authorities:** *Moragne v. States Marine Lines*, 398 U.S. 375; *Miles v. Apex Marine Corp.*, 498 U.S. 19; *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207; Death on the High Seas Act.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** State wrongful-death and survival remedies may supplement general maritime law for the territorial-water death of a nonseafarer when no federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule displaces them.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Congress supplied exclusive remedial rules for defined categories such as high-seas deaths, and *Miles* tied uniformity to the statutory treatment of seamen. Neither source establishes an exclusive federal damages code for a nonseafarer killed in territorial waters. Allowing state remedies in that residual category respects both the federal maritime cause recognized in *Moragne* and the state remedial role that federal law has not displaced.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not permit state law to enlarge remedies where an applicable federal maritime statute or a controlling uniform rule supplies an exclusive measure of recovery.

## OT1995-104 — Allridge v. Scott

**Event:** 1996-01-15 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Simulation-assigned merits opinion

**Citation:** 41 F.3d 213 (5th Cir. 1994)

**Docket(s):** Fifth Circuit No. 93-9137

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Reject the asserted entitlement to a favorable prediction about parole. Vacate the denial of the preserved, actually presented mitigation-instruction claim and remand for application of Graham and Johnson to the sentencing record. Leave guilt and matters outside the grant undisturbed.

**Reasoning.** The jury may not be told that a parole-eligible sentence legally excludes release. Simmons protects an accurate legal answer, not speculation about what parole authorities will do. A fixed statutory minimum, if properly presented as a distinct claim, is a legal fact rather than a prediction; that distinction does not establish that this habeas petitioner preserved such a claim or can obtain a new rule retroactively.

The mitigation ruling presents a different problem. The father's account cannot be dismissed merely because it fails to diagnose a uniquely severe permanent disability or conclusively link a diagnosis to the crime. Abuse and impaired functioning may bear on culpability independently of whether the defendant remains dangerous. Under Graham and Johnson the question is whether, considering the testimony, charge and proceeding together, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury could not give that supported mitigating force effect while answering the special issues truthfully. Weakness in the evidence affects what it establishes and the weight it may receive; it does not authorize the displaced categorical restriction.

**Relief and limits.** Remand that claim for the actual instruction, evidence, prefinality law and any properly preserved harmlessness issue. Stone would apply Chapman to a demonstrated, preserved denial of effective capital mitigation if a separate harmless-error inquiry is required, expressly extending the rationale of simulated Brecht/O'Neal rather than claiming their holdings already decide this category. Relief would be confined to punishment: adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence if the defect requires correction. No immediate release or automatic writ follows. Unintroduced medical evidence and a separate chilling claim require their own lawful basis; they are not assumed established by the remand.

The three roadmaps supply context only; this case presents no separate Second Amendment, Voting Rights Act or Commerce Clause question requiring an additional holding. Existing law, the presented claim and the actual record bound the disposition. Preserve applicable prior simulated law even where Stone would have preferred a different rationale.
