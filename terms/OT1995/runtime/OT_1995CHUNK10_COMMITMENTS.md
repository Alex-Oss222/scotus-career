## Participation, law and decisional components

The eight Associates are Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each participates at argument and decision; no case-specific exclusion is established. The full seated Court has nine participants and a five-vote threshold, but this handoff supplies only eight votes and never supplies Stone's ninth vote.

The material components are: **A**, threshold and the statutory/avoidance alternative; **B**, the First Amendment application to the particular independently prepared advertisement and the government's alternative contribution-channel argument; **C**, broader relief against Senate-party coordinated spending; and **D**, the enforcement mandate and declaratory-counterclaim remand. Different answers to these components are not merged into a general vote against or for campaign-finance regulation.

The current **Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association** standard, effective May 15, 1995 under Day Part III, preserves meaningful pooled advocacy and requires fit to an identified corruption/circumvention mechanism; it preserves realistic conduit controls and creates no dollar floor. Its reviewed recipient category excluded political parties and candidate principal committees. Application to this party is therefore an extension of its functional reasoning, not mechanical application of its judgment.

The current **Nonprofit political spending outside a literal safe harbor** standard, effective May 15, 1995 under Day Part IV, demands attention to actual political-resource, organizational and funding characteristics. Material commercial accumulation, economic claimants or corporate/union conduit financing remain different. Austin survives in that setting. This unincorporated party is not automatically an MCFL corporation or automatically an Austin business association.

The current **Anonymous individual political leaflets** standard, effective April 19, 1995 under McIntyre, requires exacting scrutiny in its individual-leaflet setting, but expressly reserves candidate finance, committees, organizational spending, coordination and materially different disclosure rules. It supports attention to actual speech burdens without deciding this case.

Day's responsive-financing judgment has no unified controlling rationale. Its four-Justice plurality is a published position of O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; its partial dissent is a published position of Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The dissent's premise that the independent speaker remains uncapped matters to distinguishing a direct ceiling. Neither side's financing explanation is Court law. The separate contribution and nonprofit holdings do control at their stated scopes. Day's Minnesota severability holding supplies no FECA severability result.

## Common issue definitions and bounded alternatives

### A. Review and statutory avoidance

All eight accept the supplied lawful merits vehicle and resolve the independent-expenditure question. None chooses DIG, jurisdictional dismissal or a factual remand merely because research was targeted. Doe preserves discretion and question/argument distinctions; it does not create a defect here.

All eight reject restoring the district judgment solely because this advertisement omitted the narrow express-advocacy words. The substantial competing route is MCFL's construction of the similarly broad phrase used in §441b. Its force is acknowledged: vague coverage can chill issue discussion and avoidance must precede an unnecessarily broad constitutional ruling. The decisive distinction is the election-centered party provision and this identified Senate-campaign advertisement, rather than an occasional issue organization's uncertain coverage. Neither Buckley nor MCFL already requires the party-specific subsection (d) to turn on a formulaic phrase. The Party's own acknowledged campaign function and the ad's candidate-and-election reference permit this application to be tested without deciding the outer reach of an electioneering-message standard. This is not approval of every vague issue-advertising regulation or a holding that party speech is always coordinated.

The categorical party presumption cannot receive deference on the constitutional question. No Justice supplies a new general administrative-deference holding. The selected positions assess the First Amendment application of the limit the court below actually sustained; they do not declare the whole of subsection (d) an affirmative independent-speech exemption or rewrite its terms to decide every statutory application.

### B. Independent expenditure versus contribution channels

The actual premise is an independently prepared $15,000 advertisement before either party selected its nominee. The Party's general institutional cooperation and its delegation of the entire $103,248 allowance are relevant, but do not establish candidate consultation or control over this communication. Callaway's incomplete recollection is not affirmative proof of coordination. No nominee-selection timing immunity is created: actual coordination could exist before nomination in a different record.

Subsection (a)(2)(A)'s $5,000 multicandidate contribution limit, (B)'s $15,000 annual national-party-committee limit and (C)'s $5,000 annual other-committee limit address different recipients. The multicandidate definition requires six months' registration and more than fifty contributors, plus contributions to five federal candidates except for a State party organization. Subsection (a)(4) separately exempts transfers among national, State, district and local committees of the same party from (a)(1)–(2). None of those provisions converts this payment for the Party's own advertisement into a direct recipient transfer simply by labeling it party spending.

The FEC's strongest statutory response is real: subsection (d)'s notwithstanding permission may supplement ordinary contribution allowances, so invalidating one limit does not itself erase a distinct applicable contribution restriction. The Party disputes that construction and also says the (a)(2) theory was newly raised. Neither advocate's characterization is adjudicated as a concession or forfeiture. For the seven Justices rejecting this application, the alternative theory fails at the operative classification: subsection (a)(7)(B)(i) requires cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion by the candidate, authorized committees or agents; the categorical presumption cannot supply that transaction predicate. Republication under (a)(7)(B)(ii) and earmarking/intermediary attribution under (a)(8) have different triggers, none established for this advertisement. These Justices do not invalidate those provisions or decide their uncertain application to another transaction.

Stevens's opposite constitutional route does not depend on declaring one of those factual triggers proved. He regards the specific Senate-party allowance as sustainable preventive regulation of the party's institutional candidate-financing role, even accepting actual independence of this ad. His position rests on subsection (d)'s own application and its constitutional justification; it does not enter an additional (a)(2) violation.

### C. Broad Senate-party challenge

The counterclaim and reply present broader Senate-election relief, including alleged chilled spending; the reply does not request invalidation of all subsection (d), including its distinct presidential allowance. A challenge to coordinated spending is therefore a genuine alternative, not invented or deemed abandoned. But the question supplied for decision concerns independent expenditure, and independent and candidate-directed spending invoke materially different constitutional mechanisms. No Associate commits here to invalidating or sustaining all coordinated Senate-party spending. This reservation rests on the issue and legal distinctions each Justice identifies below, not a fiction that archive documents were unavailable. None silently converts a remand into a substantive rejection of the counterclaim.

### D. Two precise remedial routes

**Relief route:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment requiring judgment for the FEC and a civil-penalty determination; remand consistently with the First Amendment protection of this independent advertisement. No penalty may be imposed on that advertisement under the unconstitutional application or an identical categorical-party reclassification. The lower courts must separately address the status and proper remaining scope of the declaratory counterclaim under the ordinary rules; no instruction requires a new factual trial, broader facial adjudication, or automatic mootness dismissal. The Court does not affirm the district court's express-advocacy reasoning merely by rejecting enforcement. Direct contributions and factually supported coordination, republication or earmarking in distinct transactions remain outside this judgment.

**Sustain route:** Affirm the adverse enforcement mandate on the constitutionality of subsection (d)'s application, leaving penalty determination to the district court under §437g(a)(6). No amount is fixed. Denial of the present as-applied constitutional claim does not adjudicate every application asserted in the broader Senate counterclaim; its remaining live grounds require separate treatment consistent with the narrow decision. No refund, damages or additional injunction is invented under either route.

## Independent Justice commitments and adversarial comparison

### Stevens

**Result by component:** A: Reach the merits; reject express-advocacy avoidance for this party advertisement. B: Sustain subsection (d)'s application despite actual independence; no separate finding of a subsection (a)(2) offense. C: Do not decide every coordinated Senate expenditure. D: Support the sustain route and existing penalty-assessment stage, not a present penalty amount.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** Congress may subject a formal party's spending in the specific candidate-election channel to a finite allowance serving corruption and circumvention interests where its institutional relationship to its candidates makes that channel materially different from an unaffiliated issue association's advocacy. His Austin concurrence, 494 U.S. 678–679, gives concrete weight to corruption and its appearance in candidate campaigns; his MCFL dissent join, 479 U.S. 266–271, accepts prophylactic regulation rather than requiring proof of a completed corrupt bargain. The analogy here rests on the party–candidate structure, not corporate status or commercial wealth. He regards the election-specific, inflation-adjusted allowance and the Party's own allocation of it as adequate accommodation of association on this record. $103,248 is neither a constitutional minimum nor proof that every higher ceiling is valid.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** Buckley, MCFL and his own Day Parts III–IV joins support relief because an independent message is expression, party affiliation is not transaction coordination, and no corrupt bargain or conduit is found. His Day financing dissent also rejects direct suppression merely to equalize. He nevertheless distinguishes Day's extraordinarily restrictive intermediary ceiling and nonprofit funding record from the institution dedicated to electing its affiliated candidates. The danger he accepts is evasion of candidate-financing safeguards through that continuing institution, not excessive persuasion or leveling voices. This is a constitutionally sufficient prophylactic inference in his assessment, not a newly found fact about Callaway or the advertisement.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** He can accept that genuinely independent spending occurs and that (a)(7) does not define it away; he will not join a rule requiring proof of actual quid pro quo in each enforcement action. He will not extend Austin's commercial-wealth rationale to every voluntary association, overrule Day, or sustain restrictions merely because Congress calls them contributions. He does not adopt a broad coordinated-spending holding unnecessary to sustaining this application. Any writing he supports must explain why the institutional channel supplies fit notwithstanding independence and must keep the separate contribution theory unresolved rather than use an unproved statutory trigger.

### O'Connor

**Result by component:** A: Reach; reject the proposed formulaic-advocacy disposition. B: Reject this application and the categorical alternative contribution theory. C: Reserve coordinated Senate-party spending. D: Support the relief route, including separate treatment of the counterclaim.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** A party's own independent political advertisement receives the expenditure protection Buckley supplies; the permanent party relationship alone cannot replace actual coordination or establish an adequately fitted corruption justification for this communication. Her MCFL concurrence, 479 U.S. 265–266, requires a demonstrated regulatory predicate for a direct organizational speech burden while preserving ordinary disclosure. Her join in Kennedy's Austin dissent, 494 U.S. 702–705, expressly differentiates independent expression from candidate resources. Day's Parts III–IV and her plurality join reinforce this result without creating one financing rule.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** She gives genuine weight to Congress's experience and the risk that party intermediaries could frustrate candidate limits; her 44 Liquormart concurrence does not demand conclusive empirical quantification before preventive regulation is possible. The FEC's supplemental-allowance theory also cannot be rejected just by deleting subsection (d). Yet this ad's actual independence leaves the crucial contribution-channel predicate missing, and the institutional generalization is insufficiently fitted to the direct speech restriction. Day's party exclusion prevents mechanical reliance on its judgment but does not nullify its required attention to actual burden and mechanism.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** She will join as-applied expenditure protection preserving supported conduit rules, direct contributions, ordinary disclosure and Austin's surviving scope. She will not join wholesale rejection of contribution/expenditure distinctions, automatic invalidity of all coordinated party limits, or a demand for a proved bribe. The counterclaim may receive proper consideration below; no broader judgment or factual retry follows automatically. Her reason for withholding broader assent is the greater regulatory latitude her MCFL concurrence and Austin join preserve for candidate contributions and materially different risks.

### Scalia

**Result by component:** A: Reach; no formulaic-advocacy-only disposition. B: Reject this application and party-status substitution for the statutory coordination predicate. C: No present coordinated-spending holding. D: Support the relief route.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** Paying to convey the association's own independent political message is protected expression; an expenditure limit cannot be justified by restricting a successful association's political influence. His Austin dissent, 494 U.S. 679–695, especially 682–685, treats independent advocacy differently from funds placed under a candidate's direction and rejects redefinition of distortion as sufficient corruption. MCFL III-A–C and Day's expressive-burden plurality and Parts III–IV are his own affirmative joins. His McIntyre dissent concerns a different identification requirement and supplies no ground for a ceiling here.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** A legislature can prevent disguised candidate contributions; parties are persistent electoral institutions, and apparent independence can be manufactured. His own Austin discussion recognizes the corruption danger of resources given directly to a candidate. That supports enforcement against actual circumvention, not a conclusive presumption that every party message is someone else's expenditure. Neither the ad's useful effect nor Congress's wish for an administrable line establishes the missing connection in this record. The (a)(2) argument cannot become sufficient simply by moving the same presumption between subsections.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** He supports a firm independent-expression rule rather than a constitutional dollar safe harbor. He does not demand a new evidentiary procedure or constitutionalize the absence of a nominee. His own recognition of candidate-directed funds and his express Day join preserving coordination controls bar attributing a general contribution-limit repudiation to him. Broader Senate-party coordination questions may be developed in the actual counterclaim; this record does not authorize changing his protected category from independent messages to every candidate-directed payment. No overruling of Austin is necessary to distinguish this unincorporated political association.

### Kennedy

**Result by component:** A: Reach; reject express-advocacy wording as sufficient statutory disposition. B: Reject this application and the categorical contribution-channel fallback. C: Reserve the broader coordinated-spending question. D: Support the relief route.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** The right to speak about a candidate does not diminish because citizens act through a political association. An actual independent expenditure must satisfy the protected-expenditure analysis, not be treated as a candidate contribution solely by identifying the speaker as a party. His Austin dissent, 494 U.S. 695–713, puts both speaker identity and the contribution/expenditure distinction at the center of the inquiry; 702–705 addresses why hypothetical official gratitude does not establish the necessary corruption interest. Day's plurality regards even the specified adverse financing trigger as an expressive burden; a legal ceiling is a direct one. His Parts III–IV joins preserve genuine conduit restrictions.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** The party's purpose is to help elect its candidates, and collective activity can provide reliable candidate benefits without a scripted consultation. A contribution scheme cannot survive if money directed to candidates merely takes an intermediate organizational route. He accepts that premise for an established route. It does not answer the materially different party-authored advertisement prepared independently before nomination, and no earmarking, candidate direction or corporate-wealth predicate is supplied. The complete counterargument is anti-circumvention, not simply equalization; he finds the categorical means insufficient even for that legitimate objective.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** He will support the common independent-expenditure ground with its express reservations. He will not join language implying party association is constitutionally suspect, that helpful speech alone is a contribution, or that a remand itself sustains coordinated limits. His Austin dissent itself distinguishes candidate payments from independent speech, so it cannot be cited as his prior adoption of immunity for every coordinated-party message. Reaching that broader claim would require a distinct treatment of association and the actual candidate-resource channel; it is not necessary to the present judgment. Austin remains undisturbed in the Court's law even though his earlier disagreement remains public.

### Souter

**Result by component:** A: Reach; no express-advocacy-only disposition. B: Reject this application and its categorical alternative contribution basis. C: Reserve broader coordination. D: Support the relief route.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** Buckley's independent-expenditure protection applies to the actual transaction; a continuing party relationship supplies a serious regulatory concern but not an irrebuttable factual conclusion that all of its speech is candidate-directed. His own Day Parts III–IV joins require an identified corruption mechanism and actual organizational characteristics. His Day financing dissent rests on leaving the independent speaker uncapped; the ceiling here removes that operative premise. McIntyre is supportive speech law with an express organizational-finance reservation. His 44 Liquormart advancement/fit joins reinforce attention to the record, without importing a commercial-speech test.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** Party organizations are plausible channels for evading valid contribution rules; treating each communication in isolation may miss that institutional reality. Day itself preserved realistic conduits, and a finding of personal corruption is not essential to all finance regulation. He nevertheless finds the record's general party descriptions insufficient to overcome the specific independent-expenditure premise. The written coordination and earmarking provisions offer different channels whose actual triggers cannot be assumed. The objection does not justify adopting a lower-court categorical classification as a factual finding.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** He can join an as-applied First Amendment ruling while reserving properly supported coordinated-spending regulation, higher contribution ceilings and business-corporation questions. He will not join abolition of preventive regulation or an assertion that the Court already invalidated party limits in Day. Nor will he join a broad heightened standard borrowed from a 44 Liquormart portion he did not join. His own Day finance dissent's limited premise explains why support for a public subsidy does not require support for this direct cap. Remand separates the present enforcement failure from the counterclaim's remaining proper reach.

### Thomas

**Result by component:** A: Reach; reject the proposed statutory magic-words resolution. B: Reject this application and its categorical contribution fallback. C: Do not adjudicate all coordinated Senate-party spending or reopen all contribution law. D: Support the relief route.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** The government's restriction directly limits the political association's own independent expression. His published Day plurality join supplies a strong independent-speech burden position, while his Day Parts III–IV joins require preservation of meaningful collective advocacy and functional treatment of nonprofit expression. These, together with Buckley's surviving independent-expenditure rule, support relief without an invented new opinion repudiating all of Buckley. His judgment-only McIntyre position supplies no rationale join, and his 44 Liquormart anti-ignorance rule does not classify corruption prevention as an illegitimate interest.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** Party organizations can be used to achieve what a candidate's donors cannot lawfully achieve directly. His own Day Part III join affirmatively preserves regulation of that kind of realistic conduit; this is more than an administrative-convenience claim. But the present justification presumes the transaction into that category rather than establishes it. The Party's decision to spend on its own independent message does not become candidate control because it benefits the eventual nominee or exhausts a finite statutory allowance.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** He will join the independent-spending holding at its exact scope, with actual coordination, republication, earmarking and direct contribution issues left distinct. He withholds assent to any rule treating the Party's rights as merely derivative of the candidate's. No source in this handoff supplies his adoption of a general contribution/expenditure merger or facial invalidation of all coordinated party limits, and his affirmative Day reservation points against assigning one without distinct supporting analysis. This is a legal scope decision on the supplied question, not a source-unavailability disposition.

### Ginsburg

**Result by component:** A: Reach; no express-advocacy-only resolution. B: Reject the application and the categorical contribution reclassification. C: Reserve the broader Senate-party challenge. D: Support the relief route.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** The institution's political purpose does not itself supply the missing corruption or conduit predicate needed to restrict its actual independent expression. Her own Day Parts III–IV joins protect meaningful association and demand evaluation of actual organizational characteristics. In the Day financing dissent she joined the explanation that the speaker remained uncapped and lost no resources; that affirmative premise is absent when enforcement punishes the speaker's expenditure itself. Her McIntyre and 44 Liquormart joins support meaningful scrutiny of a direct speech burden while retaining their subject-specific limits. This result does not rest on an attributed Austin or MCFL vote she never cast.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** Parties' continuing relationships with candidates distinguish them from issue organizations and can justify preventive regulation even when direct consultation is hard to prove. Day expressly excluded party recipients from the reviewed contribution ceiling and retained realistic conduit rules. This is the strongest basis for sustaining the application. She nevertheless concludes that the independent advertisement cannot be categorically reassigned to the candidate on that institutional generalization alone. The actual-characteristics requirement she joined in Day does not disappear because the organization is plainly political. A specific candidate-channel justification could support a different case without restricting this message.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** She will join the shared as-applied ground and rejection of the categorical presumption, but not a rule eliminating campaign-finance regulation, treating every anticipated burden as unconstitutional, or overruling Austin. Her prior finance dissent's protection of an uncapped speaker and her unanimous functional joins are expressly retained; neither is promoted into a majority responsive-financing rationale. Because her support depends on independence and the missing channel predicate, it cannot be counted for invalidating actual coordination restrictions. The counterclaim receives separate procedural consideration on remand.

### Breyer

**Result by component:** A: Reach; reject express-advocacy wording as the sole answer. B: Reject this application and categorical contribution treatment. C: Reserve coordinated Senate-party spending. D: Support the relief route.

**Sufficient rule and own authority:** Strong speech protection and legitimate campaign-integrity regulation can coexist by preserving the functional distinction between independent advocacy and candidate resources. His own Day Parts III–IV joins require adequate fit and examination of actual organization and funding; his Day financing dissent distinguishes facilitating additional speech from restricting the initial speaker. His join in O'Connor's 44 Liquormart concurrence recognizes that supported predictions can carry weight without dispensing with fit. Applied here, a general party–candidate relationship cannot alone justify the direct limit on the supported independent message.

**Opposite path and strongest objection:** Administrable prophylaxis may be necessary because formal nonconsultation can obscure indirect candidate financing, and legislatures possess relevant practical experience. He does not require numerical proof, a proved bribe or the least restrictive imaginable statute. Nevertheless, the candidate-contribution framework cannot be extended to this independent expenditure solely through an agency label. The actual coordination, republication and earmarking provisions remain available at their own scopes; no present factual trigger is established. The FEC's supplement-to-contribution theory does not cure that classification defect.

**Narrower/broader positions and join barriers:** He supports relief on the concrete independent-expenditure ground without condemning all party expenditure controls or voluntary public financing. He will not join a categorical assertion that every candidate-channel prediction is invalid, nor Thomas's different anti-ignorance theory as a campaign-finance standard. His stronger allowance for justified preventive rules makes the reservation of actual coordinated spending material, not boilerplate. The lower courts may address the remaining counterclaim under ordinary rules; the remand neither compels a broad invalidation nor forecloses a properly supported narrower challenge.

## Component definitions and independently adopted common positions

These common propositions are adopted by each of the eight Associates unless a stated limit identifies a smaller group. They are commitments for later compatibility testing, not a present Court opinion.

**J — ordinary review and original jurisdiction.** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars the ordinary certiorari petition directed to the Eleventh Circuit's authorization denial; dismiss that part for want of jurisdiction. Its express removal of appeals, rehearing petitions and certiorari petitions does not repeal the distinct original-application jurisdiction expressly conferred on this Court by §2241. Paragraph (3)(A) specifically governs filing in district court. A direct application seeking relief from custody is different from reviewing the circuit's authorization decision, and its Article III character is appellate despite its original filing form. Section 1651 supplies no freestanding enlargement of jurisdiction or right to evade the review bar.

The contrary reading that (3)(E) abolishes every Supreme Court habeas channel cannot account for the retained §2241 text and the statute's channel-specific language. Yerger supplies the separate-source and no-implied-repeal principle; McCardle permits withdrawal of the specified appellate route, without deciding Congress's power to eliminate all judicial remedies. The contrary claim that every restriction on Supreme Court review necessarily violates Article III fails on this narrower statutory construction. Klein does not prohibit Congress from establishing generally applicable habeas standards; no provision here commands the Court to decide a particular factual issue falsely or to apply an unchanged rule to a legislatively dictated result.

**T — temporal operation.** Apply (3)(E) to the requested review of the May 2, 1996 authorization ruling. This regulates a post-April 24 authorization proceeding and its requested appellate channel; it does not increase punishment for the underlying crime or reopen a completed judgment. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg use their adopted Landgraf/Rivers sequential inquiry. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas use their separate relevant-activity approach, with the post-enactment application/review as the pertinent activity. Breyer applies the controlling sequential rule without inventing an earlier join. This conclusion does not transfer chapter 154's express pending-case clause to all Title I provisions or decide every new consequence of §§2254(d) and (e).

**S — successive standards and constitutional reach.** Preserving jurisdiction does not guarantee its exercise or exempt a state-custody application from substantive habeas law. Paragraphs (b)(1) and (b)(2) concern distinct claim categories; paragraph (3)'s prima facie authorization is not a final merits adjudication, and paragraph (4) preserves a later district determination. A demand to announce a new constitutional rule now does not satisfy (2)(A)'s already announced, previously unavailable, Supreme-Court-retroactive-rule condition. Paragraph (2)(B) requires BOTH due-diligence unavailability and clear-and-convincing whole-evidence proof that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. Neither a credible innocence contention nor a Schlup showing automatically supplies all these predicates.

O'Connor, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reserve the full direct reach of (b)(1)–(2) to original Supreme Court applications: their original-application conclusions below do not require resolving that general question. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas read the substantive successive state-custody language to apply to this original application as well; the district-specific wording in (3) does not narrow (1)–(2). Their additional textual conclusion is not attributed to the other five. All retain Rule 20's separate exceptional-circumstances and no-adequate-other-relief requirements. None treats (3)(E), by itself, as a suspension of the writ while §2241 remains available. No group decides that the survival of an empty or legally inadequate remedy would necessarily satisfy the Suspension Clause. Broader challenges to state-merits deference, failed-development hearing restrictions, or an established innocent prisoner's exclusion are not resolved merely by approving this review-channel restriction.

**C — the challenged reasonable-doubt instruction.** Deny relief on this claim after applying actual Victor to the whole charge and the prosecutor's related voir-dire statements. The charge keeps every essential fact within the State's burden throughout; explicitly distinguishes criminal proof from civil preponderance; locates doubt in evidence, its absence or conflict; requires acquittal if the jurors remain wavering, unsettled and unsatisfied; requires wholly circumstantial proof to exclude every other reasonable theory; rejects suspicion, conjecture and possibility as a basis for conviction; and again requires acquittal for reasonable doubt on each count. Moral certainty does not stand as an alternative authorization to use personal ethical belief. The complete context supplies no reasonable likelihood of conviction below Winship's standard.

The strongest contrary path treats the antiquated terminology, prosecutorial repetition and truth-seeking language as a substitute for the criminal burden. That reading is rejected in this charge because the instructions actually define the required doubt through the evidence and direct acquittal when that doubt remains; the result does not follow simply from finding a correct sentence elsewhere. No new unpleaded instruction challenge is created from other language in the complete charge. Sullivan would foreclose an evidence-strength harmlessness rationale if the proof standard were vitiated, but that trigger is not met. The disputed date of the attempted first filing, formal docketing after Sullivan and amendment opportunity need not be converted into a new cause, retroactivity or default holding.

**P — independent trial-process claim.** Deny the proposed categorical due-process/Eighth Amendment claim that a nonphysician may not supply this expert evidence. Ake, Ford and Eddings require their actual protections and reinforce reliability; they do not establish the new physician-only evidentiary rule sought here. Tillman's qualifications were exposed to examination; the defense offered contrary medical testimony; Whitaker independently supported the earlier chronology; and the charge left expert weight to the jury and excluded opinions whose assumed facts were not proved. The later medical challenge does not by itself establish that admitting Tillman's testimony made this trial fundamentally unfair under Lisenba. No knowing false testimony, concealed credentials or denial of a requested constitutionally required expert is established by these papers. This is a rejection of the presented constitutional trial ground, not an adjudication that all forensic observations were correct.

The contrary path has force because Arden attacks the autopsy's observations and omissions, not merely professional credentials. Those matters remain material to innocence and the reliability of the chronology. They do not eliminate the distinction between a later expert disagreement and a demonstrated constitutional defect in the trial process. All eight reject relief on the automatic physician-only theory; Stevens and Souter's further original-application examination below concerns freestanding innocence, not adoption of that categorical trial rule.

**Evidence assessment common minimum.** Spitz and Arden give a medically reasoned later-death opinion, not simply an additional vote on professional qualifications. Arden addresses apparent rigor at recovery, temperature, limited skin slippage and marbling, organs, absence of purging or aquatic disturbance, changes during the ensuing twenty-four hours in air, inadequate sampling and the limited value of exhumation. He also contests the physical interpretation of alleged binding and sexual injury. Both experts reviewed Whitaker's testimony. These opinions strengthen the defense chronology; describing them as only cumulative credentials would fail to address their content.

The opposing evidence is also real: Whitaker favored death near disappearance while allowing three to five days; his medical qualifications and examination are independent of Tillman's credentials. The abandoned car, the victim's disappearance, the November 24 call from Felker's home, and the same clothing when the body was recovered supply circumstantial support for an earlier death. The lower opinion also recounts similar-crime evidence; its permitted trial use is not enlarged into an unrestricted propensity inference. None of these circumstances alone supplies a medical time of death. The experts' asserted certainty likewise is not a judicial finding. The difference below is the legal significance of this combined showing at the original-writ screening stage.

## Supported paths and operative scope

**A — existing-relationship protection with a service-sensitive Pickering rule.** Contractor status alone does not remove protection against termination of this existing relationship for public-concern citizen speech. The claimant must establish protected speech and its substantial or motivating role. Assess content, form and context under Connick, and balance the citizen/public interest against legitimate governmental interests arising from the actual service relationship under Pickering. The county need not have directly bought the service to possess legitimate organizational interests, but its role also cannot be inflated into complete control of an employee. Independence, other customers, contact with officials, service responsibilities and supported operational concerns matter insofar as they bear on that balance; personal offense at criticism does not itself establish disruption. The government may prove by a preponderance under Mt. Healthy that it would have made the same decision without protected conduct.

The strongest objection is that choosing outside providers entails discretion and litigation costs unlike public employment. A answers through the actual governmental-interest balance, the claimant's causal burden, the same-decision defense and the existing-relationship limit. ## Shared decisional premises and qualifications

### Reach, participation and operative text

All eight Associate Justices reach the challenged 1992 provisions and FCC rules through review of the June 6, 1995 en banc judgment, 56 F.3d 105, under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Each participates at argument and decision. No jurisdictional defect, litigant default, damages issue, or adjudicated removal of a particular program is supplied.

The provisions are distinct:

1. **Section 10(a):** An operator may prospectively enforce a written and published policy prohibiting programming it reasonably believes describes or depicts sexual or excretory activities or organs in a patently offensive manner under contemporary community standards. Permission is not a command to prohibit. The FCC's definition specifies standards for the cable medium. It is not Miller obscenity and carries no categorical serious-value exception; context still matters.
2. **Section 10(b):** When an operator has not voluntarily prohibited covered leased-access programming, the FCC must require provider identification, concentration on a single channel, blocking unless the subscriber requests access in writing, and programmer notification. The FCC's written request includes a statement that the requester is at least eighteen. Access must be supplied within thirty days, not after a mandatory thirty-day wait. The agency permits another blocked channel if the first lacks capacity. The described alternative for systems lacking premium/pay-per-view channels is an operator-controlled lockbox whose key or numeric code is retained until unblocking is requested. That party-reported accommodation is distinct from a parent-controlled lockbox; no numeric size threshold or universal feasibility finding is adopted. Neither accommodation eliminates the compulsory initial denial or written opt-in.
3. **Section 10(c):** Congress directs enabling regulations for operator prohibition of obscene material, sexually explicit conduct, or material soliciting/promoting unlawful conduct on PEG capacity. Its enacted categories differ from (a). The FCC construes sexually explicit conduct through its indecency definition and unlawful-conduct material as otherwise proscribed material, and permits operator certification requirements. Section 10(c) supplies neither (a)'s express prospective written-and-published-policy/reasonable-belief terms nor (b)'s mandatory blocking/request mechanism. Local selection of access speakers does not by itself classify every PEG channel as a constitutional public forum.
4. **Section 10(d):** The access-programming immunity exception concerns obscenity, not all protected indecency. It supplies no general threat of civil or criminal liability for transmitting protected indecent expression. No independent invalidation of (d) is proposed.

### Existing law actually applied

**Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions — present operation effective Turner, June 27, 1994:** Cable editorial selection and programmers' expression receive ordinary First Amendment protection; broadcast scarcity does not reduce cable review. Content classification examines selection and justification. The general content-based formulation has the eight joins identified in the supplied Turner entry, with Stevens's narrower position preserved. The five-Justice intermediate rule concerns the content-neutral full-power must-carry provisions. It is not the standard for (b)'s express indecency classification. The controlling limited predictive-review principle requires substantial consideration of supported legislative judgments together with independent constitutional review; neither a formal legislative trial record nor scientific certainty is indispensable. The four-Justice Turner conclusion that further evidence was needed is not an all-purpose remand requirement.

**ACT / Pacifica, March 4, 1996:** Protected nonobscene indecency, compelling child-protection and parental-supervision interests, and independent necessity/means review are governing law. The actual sixteen-hour broadcast schedule's invalidity does not decide any cable provision. Souter joined the protected-speech standard but proposed renewed review rather than final invalidity; Stevens, Scalia and Breyer would sustain that schedule on its actual contextual record. Those distinctions remain operative evidence of their positions.

**Private parade composition and compelled messages — present operation effective Hurley, June 19, 1995:** Souter's opinion for all nine protects the actual private expressive presentation against compelled insertion. It does not declare access-channel programs part of an operator's own presentation merely because the operator transmits them. Conversely, statutory access does not itself establish public ownership or a constitutional public forum. Rosenberger's actual independently edited publication program illustrates why attribution and the terms of an opened channel matter; its viewpoint holding is not a holding that every subject restriction is viewpoint discrimination. Lebron's special-creation/permanent-majority-appointment rule, in the supplied Speech Law slice, likewise does not make every regulated franchise governmental.

Predivergence Sable supplies the adult-access distinction and careful tailoring; Pacifica supplies its limited contextual broadcast principle. Blum/Jackson/Lugar and Reitman/Crawford frame the difference between review of legislation and attribution of a private editorial act. Perry/Cornelius require the actual forum arrangement. Alaska Airlines supplies independent operability and congressional-intent severability. These sources support the competing paths; no generic medium, ownership, or forum label resolves them all.

### Earlier June 26 law

Colorado adds no cable-specific test. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer's protection of a genuinely independent speaker cautions against equating institutional relationship with actual attribution. Stevens and Ginsburg's dissent permits a supported institution-specific contribution-channel judgment, not unexamined deference to any institutional label. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas's further party-expression position distinguishes a speaker's own message from another person's resources. Applying those distinctions here requires deciding whose editorial presentation and which governmental burden is involved. None of Colorado's positions supplies a new finding about PEG governance, cable technology or coercion. Reading the addendum therefore changes no statutory premise or operative cable standard; its Justice-specific implications are incorporated below.

### Alternatives, intervening law and shared remedial limits

The competing assessment of (b) considers the affirmative alternatives supplied: subscriber-requested devices under §544(d)(2), premium-preview notice and requested blocking under the separate §544(d)(3), program notice/schedules and parental control, and the 1996 section 504 no-charge requested full audio/video blocking duty. Section 504 has no sexual-content trigger in its text; section 505 separately concerns channels primarily dedicated to sexually oriented programming and carries its own blocking/time-rule conditions and thirty-day effective date. Neither enactment proves universal technical implementation. Future section 551 ratings/equipment is not an operating national alternative, and no future V-chip fact enters a commitment.

All eight reserve any independent facial challenge to **1996 section 506**. Its subsection (a) permits refusal of a public-access program or portion containing obscenity, indecency or nudity; public access is not silently expanded to every educational and governmental category. Subsection (b) supplies corresponding leased-access authority. The words and qualifications differ from the 1992 provisions. Those amendments do not repeal the challenged rules or automatically eliminate every continuing legal interest; they also prevent a judgment about §10 from guaranteeing carriage or erasing an independent source of authority. The 1996 Act's **section 561** requires the specified facial Title V challenges to proceed before a three-judge district court under §2284; direct appeal is supplied for the specified interlocutory or final holding of unconstitutionality and must be filed within twenty days. This is not the different provision codified at **47 U.S.C. §561**, and not an appeal from every district-court ruling.

The present review can decide the challenged 1992/FCC authority and order relief against it. Any remand must preserve the independent 1996 text, decide genuinely remaining remedial interactions within the issues properly presented, and avoid ordering a named program carried by force of invalidating §10 alone. No result below imposes a new FCC hearing, a universal franchise inquiry, a damages award, a broadcast timetable, a fixed compliance date, or an automatic interim injunction.

## Individual commitments and enhanced comparison

Each entry identifies a formal component position, sufficient grounds, competing path and strongest objection, permissible narrower reach, and the corresponding remedy. References to a Justice's prior view identify the supplied writing, not an inferred ideological position. Shared qualifications above apply to every entry.

### Stevens

**Own eligible anchors:** His Pacifica Part IV, especially the Court's contextual IV-C; his Sable Part IV join; his Turner concurrence accepting independent review but finding the existing structural record sufficient; his ACT dissent, joined by Scalia and Breyer; his full Hurley join. His Colorado dissent with Ginsburg supplies an institution-specific justification principle, not categorical power to restrict cable speech.

**Section 10(a) — affirm facial validity, independently of (b).** The limited restoration of an operator's own choice, constrained by a prospective written published policy and reasonable belief, may be sustained without treating all indecency as inferior speech or all cable as broadcasting. Hurley protects an actual private editor, and the compulsory-access setting does not erase that interest. The alternative is to treat selective repeal of access protection as government censorship regardless of the operator's independent choice. Its strongest point is Congress's express selection of sexual/excretory content and the integrated ban-or-block structure. Removing invalid (b) answers the compulsory-structure objection; a remaining prospective private-choice permission remains independently operable. The strongest objection to affirmance is that regulation cannot be insulated simply by using a private decision-maker. Stevens accepts that objection for genuine governmental inducement or control, but does not infer it from permission alone. He will not join a universal no-state-action rule or validate a particular franchise's ban.

**Section 10(b) — reverse approval and require removal of the compulsory segregation/blocking scheme.** Protected programs are transferred to a common restricted outlet, every household is initially denied them, and written subscriber action is required before adult access. These combined burdens materially differ from the actual broadcast timing structure he accepted in ACT. Here established cable channel-control mechanisms and requested blocking allow direct parental assistance without making every adult ask to restore access to the whole category. The strongest defense is that unpredictable mixed access schedules defeat ordinary parental scheduling and that written opt-in is less restrictive than a ban. Stevens credits both points but concludes they do not justify the cumulative mandatory restructuring, especially the all-or-nothing channel choice for mixed programming. This is a constitutional assessment of affirmative statutory features and supplied alternatives, not a finding that the government supplied no evidence or that every device is foolproof. He would not invalidate a narrowly drawn subscriber-control measure merely because some parental action is required.

**Section 10(c) — reverse the approval of the protected-indecency authorization and implementing rule at that scope.** Locally selected PEG programming is not established as an operator's own editorial presentation. Congress's broad permission to displace that selection, without (a)'s express prospective written-policy and reasonable-belief constraints, is not justified by merely invoking the operator's freedom. The competing private-owner approach has genuine force where an operator actually composes expression, but transmission ownership alone cannot answer this broader permission over access programming selected through local arrangements. The child-protection defense also lacks (b)'s promised adult restoration path; this is permission for complete denial, although no operator is commanded to ban. His conclusion does not classify all PEG as public forums and does not rest on the falsity of every conceivable operator interest. He would preserve ordinary obscenity and otherwise unlawful-material rules, reserve genuinely private expressive applications under their own law, and give no final §506 ruling.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join a contextual opinion sustaining the independent limited permission in (a) and rejecting the actual burdens in (b)/(c). Cannot join a speech-value hierarchy, universal public-forum theory, or categorical equation of all regulation with state action. Sever (b) from (a) under Alaska Airlines: the private permission functions without the rejected blocking command. For (c), remove the challenged authorization for protected indecency while preserving independently lawful unprotected-material authority and 1996-law questions.

### O'Connor

**Own eligible anchors:** Her Turner separate Parts I and III protect cable editorial autonomy and reject content-selected compulsory carriage; Part II separately rejects excessive burdens even assuming neutrality. Her Sable Part IV join, ACT Parts I–III join, Rosenberger concurrence on actual safeguards, Hurley join, and Colorado Part I and Parts II–III distinguish actual independent expression from categorical attribution.

**Section 10(a) — affirm on independent private-choice grounds.** The First Amendment ordinarily protects the operator's editorial refusal; Congress's limited relaxation of a statutory carriage constraint does not, without more, make the resulting private choice federal censorship. The strongest contrary path treats the content-defined alteration itself as a burden on the programmers' statutory access. She rejects the premise that every statutory entitlement to private editorial space becomes constitutionally irreversible as to its subject limits. Her Turner and Hurley positions supply the distinct operator interest. The counterargument that (b) effectively coerces prohibitions is answered by separately invalidating (b), not by presuming inevitable financial coercion the packet does not establish. She would reach an actual coercion or governmental-control application if supported, and would not sustain an operator's violation of an independently binding forum or access obligation simply by citing §10(a).

**Section 10(b) — reverse and set aside the compulsory scheme.** This is government content regulation, not protected operator choice. Her ACT join requires a real connection between child protection and the actual adult burden; her Turner alternative explains why broad statutory design may fail without a demand for endless factual development. The combined default block, written age request and forced regrouping of all covered programs burden willing adults beyond justified parental assistance. The strongest sustaining path relies on Sable's consideration of age screening and unpredictable programs. She distinguishes a tailored access-control method from compulsory segregation and continuing denial to every adult who has not affirmatively restored an entire channel. Existing parental controls and the new request-blocking obligation are affirmative, legally available alternatives, not assumed proof of flawless household compliance. She does not treat thirty days as a mandatory wait, declare all subscriber identification unconstitutional, or import Turner's intermediate standard.

**Section 10(c) — affirm against the presented facial challenge on private-choice grounds.** The statute enables, rather than requires, a private operator's refusal. There is no (b) compulsion on PEG. Neither a franchise nor a PEG label alone establishes that every refusal is governmental, and the facial theory cannot convert all private transmission capacity into a public forum. Her Rosenberger concurrence requires the actual institutional safeguards and attribution rather than a generalized analogy. The strongest invalidity argument is that locally selected access expression is not the operator's message and that (c) lacks (a)'s safeguards. She agrees those differences can matter to a properly established channel-specific governmental or contractual claim, but they do not establish universal constitutional attribution of an independent private refusal. The lack of a prescribed written policy does not make the private editor a governmental licensor. Her affirmance does not determine every PEG facility's status or decide §506.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join the same private-choice ground for (a)/(c), with actual state involvement and local obligations reserved. Can join a strict-tailoring invalidation of (b), including its integral identification/notification machinery, without an independent generic-vagueness holding. Cannot join final facial invalidation of (c), a blanket exemption from government-action review, or a compulsory-carriage order. Sever (b); affirm the distinct private permissions within these limits.

### Scalia

**Own eligible anchors:** His Sable concurrence at 131–133, including the distinction between a government ban and a utility's duty to carry; his joins in O'Connor's complete Turner separate opinion and Kennedy's protected-expression/content portions; his ACT dissent join; his full Hurley join; his Lebron constitutional-status opinion; and his Colorado joins in O'Connor's Part I and Kennedy's separate party-expression ground.

**Section 10(a) — affirm.** His Sable reservation and Turner/Hurley positions support recognizing an independent private refusal rather than creating a constitutional carriage entitlement from regulation. The content-based-restoration challenge correctly identifies governmental legislation but fails to establish that every resulting editorial refusal is governmental conduct. Lebron's actual governmental-creation/control conditions cannot be replaced with regulation and a franchise monopoly. The strongest objection is practical compulsion through (b). Scalia does not rely on a finding of no possible incentive, but the supported lower finding does not establish inevitable censoring and the facial statutory permission itself does not command refusal. If (b) falls, (a) remains independently operable. He would not approve a governmental official's use of an operator as a censor or immunize government-controlled channels under this private-choice rationale.

**Section 10(b) — affirm under the protected-indecency standard, separately from the private-choice ground.** The command is governmental and content selected; he does not deny either feature. Sable distinguished a wholesale ban from technological adult access controls, and his concurrence recognizes that the adequacy of protection must be judged in the actual setting without demanding a committee report. Here programmers may transmit, willing adult subscribers may obtain the segregated channel, and the default screen addresses the risk of unexpected child access to mixed leased programming. His ACT dissent accepts practical channeling where adults retain meaningful access; this cable measure uses household choice rather than making the speech unavailable throughout sixteen hours. The strongest contrary case combines written requests, regrouping, possible activation delay and less restrictive parent-operated controls. He regards the request as a confined age/access step and segregation as what makes the adult choice administrable; schedules and a parent-activated lockbox do not equally address unexpected access by children in households that have not activated a block. This conclusion accepts practical predictions, not guaranteed prevention, and does not assume the statutory section 504 duty has been universally implemented. He rejects neither protected status nor independent review.

**Section 10(c) — affirm.** The private refusal principle applies with no mandatory (b) mechanism. The strongest contrary argument is that PEG selection may be entrusted to other bodies and that Congress has selected a category for exclusion. In his view, neither circumstance proves governmental status for every private operator decision or confers an immutable constitutional entitlement to use private facilities. His Lebron rule preserves a different answer where the controlling institution is genuinely governmental; it does not establish such a status for all PEG. The FCC's construction of unlawful-conduct material as otherwise proscribed remains essential. He rejects a public-forum conclusion derived solely from Congress's channel designation, while reserving supported channel-specific governmental control and access claims.

**Joins/remedy:** Would affirm all three distinct components for their separately stated reasons, not a single no-state-action rationale. Will join rejection of the facial generic-vagueness claim under the context-sensitive definition and reasonable-belief terms where applicable: (a)'s reasonableness condition does not govern (c). Programmer notification/certification implementing the lawful screen is not automatically a governmental licensing board's prior restraint. No categorical serious-value exclusion or immunity for arbitrary governmental censorship is adopted. If another component is invalidated, he accepts independent severability of (a)/(c) and no expansion of relief to §506.

### Kennedy

**Own eligible anchors:** His Turner protected-expression/content-classification writing and its distinction between neutral source preservation and preference for messages; his Sable Part IV join; his ACT Parts I–III join; his Rosenberger Court opinion concerning independent expression in an opened channel; his Hurley join; and his Colorado concurrence protecting an institution's own speech while distinguishing actual transfers and conduits.

**Section 10(a) — reverse approval of the content-selected access exclusion.** Congress itself has selected protected sexual/excretory expression for removal from the otherwise established access protection. His ground addresses that legislative burden, not a declaration that every later private refusal is a state act. Turner protects programmers as well as operators and permits structural source regulation without authorizing government to select disfavored messages. The strongest sustaining path invokes the operator's private right not to speak, supported by Hurley and his own Colorado opinion. He distinguishes the program of an actual private speaker from a broad access obligation whose independently supplied messages are now singled out by Congress; a proven constitutional editorial claim remains available, but cannot justify the entire subject-selected withdrawal. Rosenberger informs that opened-channel distinction without converting this subject classification into viewpoint discrimination or every cable channel into a public forum. The child-protection purpose remains compelling, but allowing complete denial to adults is not carefully confined to that purpose. He can invalidate (a) independently of (b), without an unproved coercive-cost theory.

**Section 10(b) — reverse and remove the compulsory scheme.** It expressly burdens protected expression by content. The written-request condition is imposed on every adult subscriber, rather than on a demonstrated need to prevent a particular child's access; segregation then forces a single collective choice over otherwise different programs. Sable and his ACT join demand preservation of practical adult access through adequately tailored means. The strongest defense is that only initial screening is required and that mixed schedules impair parental supervision. He accepts the relevant risks but considers the actual combination unnecessarily restrictive given affirmative household blocking and notice alternatives. Sable's discussion of access codes supplies an example of a narrower adult-access mechanism, not approval of every written-request system in another medium. He does not rest on actual disclosure of subscriber lists, universal immediate blocking technology, or the absence of any legislative evidence. A more confined parental-control requirement remains open.

**Section 10(c) — reverse approval insofar as it authorizes exclusion of protected indecent PEG speech.** The case against content-selected legislative displacement is especially strong where speakers and purposes are selected through local access arrangements. Mere ownership of the cable plant does not establish that those messages constitute the operator's own editorial presentation. The contrary private-owner argument is substantial under Hurley, but its necessary expressive premise cannot be assumed for the statutory class. Neither the broad statutory category nor the agency's contextual definition supplies a child/adult distinction when the permission is exercised as complete exclusion. His ground reaches the governmental authorization for protected-content discrimination while preserving the potential of an actual private expressive defense. He does not announce that every PEG facility is a public forum or invalidate properly limited obscenity and otherwise-unlawful-material controls.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join the protected-content/tailoring grounds for all three provisions, with the distinct legislative-action and private-attribution questions expressly separated. Cannot join a no-state-action affirmance of (a)/(c), a broadcast-scarcity test, or relief requiring transmission notwithstanding independent rights and §506. Each challenged authorization must receive its own set-aside scope; no whole-Act judgment or forced program carriage follows.

### Souter

**Own eligible anchors:** His full Turner join, including its four-Justice record application; his ACT concurrence in part and dissent in part; his Hurley Court opinion; his Rosenberger dissent and the supplied contextual Chabad holding; and his Colorado joins in Part I and the separate narrower Parts II–III.

**Section 10(a) — affirm the independent limited permission.** Hurley gives concrete weight to the operator's private editorial interest. The written, published, prospective policy and reasonable-belief conditions confine the permission; neither regulation nor statutory access alone makes every choice governmental. The alternative legislative-discrimination theory correctly demands scrutiny of Congress's own acts, but does not eliminate the First Amendment interest in withdrawing a compulsory private carriage duty. He is willing to sustain this particular permission without deciding that every delegation or encouraged refusal escapes review. The strongest contrary point is that the ban and blocking branches were enacted together. Separating and invalidating the compulsory branch removes that structural objection while leaving an independently intelligible operator choice. He joins no finding of universally absent coercion or settled status for every access channel.

**Section 10(b) — reverse and require removal of the compulsory structure.** His ACT reservation was tied to renewed examination of an actual timetable's boundary and evidence; it does not make every unconstitutional design curable by additional research. This provision affirmatively groups diverse protected programs, blocks all households, and requires a written adult election for the whole channel. Even accepting the lower account of unpredictability and the feasibility of the prescribed blocking mechanism, those combined burdens exceed a properly tailored parental-assistance measure when requested household controls are available. The strongest alternative is a Turner-style remand for further comparative evidence, or affirmance because the adult can eventually opt in. He rejects remand here because the dispositive burden is the enacted combination, not an unresolved numerical uptake rate or disputed numeric exception. He does not find every alternative technically equivalent, infer actual privacy disclosure, or require an evidentiary hearing. A remand to implement removal differs from a remand leaving this structure's validity open.

**Section 10(c) — vacate approval and remand for the distinct constitutional inquiry; no final facial invalidity commitment.** The lower blanket private-choice explanation does not sufficiently distinguish PEG arrangements in which editorial selection belongs to local access bodies from a private expressive presentation. But the packet does not establish one constitutional actor/forum classification for the whole class. Hurley requires an actual private expressive burden; Rosenberger and Chabad preserve attention to the actual institution and attribution. The strongest invalidity path treats the enactment's content selection as enough for final relief. The strongest affirmance path treats ownership and discretion as enough to reject every facial theory. He accepts neither categorical shortcut. The court should address the governmental authorization and asserted operator expression in the actual regulatory/access arrangements, apply the protected-content standard where government burdens protected speech, and preserve the distinction between facial review and any established channel-specific claim. This is a necessary legal reexamination of the judgment's ground, not a finding that sources or litigant evidence are missing.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join (a)'s bounded private-choice affirmance and (b)'s final invalidity/removal, with no low-value speech premise. On (c), joins only vacatur for renewed review with no presumption that all PEG is a governmental forum, that all is private editorial expression, or that the provision is finally invalid. He prescribes no new universal franchise investigation, FCC hearing, automatic interim restraint or final carry order. The remand must address §506's actual independent text and legally available relief without adjudicating its constitutionality through this case.

### Thomas

**Own eligible anchors:** His joins in Kennedy's Turner protected-expression/content sections and O'Connor's Turner Parts I and III, with his nonjoin in her alternative intermediate ground preserved; his ACT Parts I–III join; his Hurley join; his Rosenberger concurrence and Court join; his Lebron join; and his Colorado joins in Part I and Kennedy's separate party-expression ground.

**Section 10(a) — affirm.** Turner and Hurley protect an actual private editor against compelled inclusion. A government choice to restore bounded discretion to that private speaker does not itself convert each exercise into a government suppression order. The strongest challenge emphasizes content selection and the statutory access entitlement. Thomas does not regard the entitlement as a constitutional transfer of all editorial ownership to access programmers; government regulation and franchise benefit alone are insufficient attribution under the supplied state-action authorities and Lebron. He reserves actual governmental control or a supported coercive application. The ban-or-block argument cannot establish universal compelled censorship merely because an operator has reasons to prefer one lawful route; if (b) is independently invalid, the permission survives without it.

**Section 10(b) — affirm under demanding content-based review.** He accepts ACT's protection of nonobscene indecency and independent review, and does not classify this command as private choice. The mechanism preserves an adult's ability to elect receipt rather than imposing ACT's broad hours of complete unavailability. Channel segregation lets households and operators identify the material that is screened; a written age declaration is connected to adult access. The lower explanation concerning unpredictable leased schedules supplies a concrete reason why ordinary parental timing controls differ. The strongest contrary path relies on default denial, the administrative request burden and household blocking alternatives. Thomas concludes those burdens remain sufficiently limited for the child-protection function: the statute requires an available adult access route, and the thirty-day provision is a compliance ceiling, not a mandatory delay. The possible adequacy of a different parent-choice scheme does not by itself establish that this arrangement violates the demanding standard. He does not adopt an intermediate test, an automatic child-protection defense, or a requirement that alternatives be foolproof; nor does he treat all serious programming as outside indecency.

**Section 10(c) — affirm the challenged private permission, reserving actual governmental facilities.** His Rosenberger position protects equality within the government's actual private-publication program, but establishes no universal constitutional right to use a privately owned cable system. His Lebron join requires genuine governmental institutional conditions rather than a label. The strongest invalidity path stresses local control of PEG selection and the lack of (a)'s safeguards. For Thomas, those matters can establish a different claim in a particular governmental arrangement; they do not convert all permissible private refusals into government censorship. The FCC's limited construction of unlawful-conduct material is preserved, and the statutory categories are not rewritten as (a)'s wording. He would not sustain a proven governmental actor's content restriction merely by calling its facility PEG or invoking an operator intermediary.

**Joins/remedy:** Would affirm (a), (b) and (c) on these separate grounds. Can join a context-sensitive rejection of facial vagueness and prior-licensing analogies while preserving legal constraints on actually governmental decisions. His prior strict ground does not authorize counting him for O'Connor's Turner intermediate-tailoring alternative. If another component falls, he accepts independent operability and severability of the remaining private permission; no independent §506 merits ruling or universal carriage entitlement follows.

### Ginsburg

**Own eligible anchors:** Her full O'Connor Turner separate-opinion join and her additional dissent concerning subject preference and inadequate justification; her ACT Court opinion; her Hurley and Lebron joins; her Rosenberger dissent join; her Chabad contextual-rule join; and her Colorado dissent join with Stevens.

**Section 10(a) — reverse approval of the governmental protected-content discrimination.** Her Turner position treats interwoven neutral and content-related purposes as content selection, not as neutralized by a benign justification. Congress has retained access protection while singling out protected sexual/excretory expression for loss of that protection. The strongest affirmance ground is an actual operator's editorial autonomy under Turner/Hurley and the difference between legislation and attribution. She preserves that difference: this disposition reviews Congress's subject-selected access rule, not every private refusal as a governmental act. A genuine independent editorial claim must be respected, but it does not justify the whole statutory permission merely from ownership of the transmission route. Her Colorado acceptance of institution-specific regulation requires the asserted institutional mechanism and fit; it does not establish a general deference rule or a cable-program attribution finding. The statutory child-protection rationale is too broad when the permitted result is complete denial to willing adults, and invalidity does not depend on proving coerced costs under (b).

**Section 10(b) — reverse and remove the compulsory scheme.** Her ACT opinion supplies the operative distinction between a compelling interest and adequate means. Here the direct burden consists of segregating programs, initial denial to every household and a written restoration request for an entire content-defined channel. Sable protects meaningful adult access, not merely theoretical availability on government-selected terms. The strongest defense is the unpredictability of programming and the practical effectiveness of a default screen. She accepts those considerations but finds the cumulative burden insufficiently confined when affirmative subscriber blocking and other parental controls address the asserted harm without presumptively withholding protected expression from all adults. The section 504 duty is relevant on its actual requested/no-charge terms, not as proof of universal efficacy. Her conclusion does not depend on actual subscriber-list disclosure, an invented serious-value safe harbor, or an assertion of no evidence in unreviewed materials.

**Section 10(c) — reverse the protected-indecency authorization.** Locally selected PEG expression and operator-authored programming are constitutionally distinct; the statutory grant of content-selected exclusion cannot be defended merely by invoking an owner's ordinary editorial choices elsewhere. Her Chabad join requires actual selection, control and attribution rather than a forum label, while her Hurley join protects an established private expressive presentation. The strongest contrary ground is that no operator is commanded to ban and that some access arrangements remain private. She concludes Congress's challenged governmental rule is nonetheless subject selected and overbroad in authorizing complete exclusion from independently selected PEG programming without a sufficient adult/child fit. She does not hold every PEG channel publicly owned, declare every private ban state action, or defeat a separately proved private expressive right. Lawful obscenity and otherwise-proscribed-material controls remain distinct.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join Kennedy at the stated legislative-content/tailoring level, but only if the opinion preserves real institutional distinctions, private editorial claims, and the actual scope of prior forum cases. Cannot join universal public-forum classification, automatic censorship attribution, or a broader §506 declaration. Relief removes the challenged protected-content rules rather than ordering publication. Her final invalidity grounds do not depend on another evidentiary search; a remand implements them and addresses independent surviving authority.

### Breyer

**Own eligible anchors:** His ACT dissent join with Stevens; his full Hurley and Lebron joins; his Rosenberger dissent join and Chabad contextual-rule join; and his Colorado joins in O'Connor's Part I and narrower Parts II–III. He was not a participant in Turner; its controlling rules bind him, but no historical or simulated Turner vote is assigned to him.

**Section 10(a) — affirm the limited independent permission.** The choice between an operator's expression and a programmer's access cannot be resolved by pretending that only one side speaks. A prospective written published policy governed by reasonable belief permits private editorial control within defined bounds. The competing path treats Congress's subject selection as dispositive. Breyer accepts that it requires constitutional attention but concludes this confined accommodation of the private operator's interest is permissible when detached from compulsory (b). Hurley's actual private-composition rule supports that interest; his Colorado position rejects categorical attribution based merely on institutional relationship. He will not join the claim that every statutory delegation is immune from review or that all access programming is necessarily the operator's own expression. A genuine governmental-control or coercive application remains open.

**Section 10(b) — reverse and require removal of the combined mandatory structure.** His ACT dissent accepted a practical contextual accommodation for broadcasting, not indiscriminate approval of every mechanism invoked to protect children. Cable's channel controls make parental choice more directly available, while the actual scheme restructures diverse programming and requires every adult to restore an entire segregated channel through a written request. The strongest defense is that default blocking addresses unpredictable encounters more reliably than parents' voluntary use of controls. He credits that advantage but finds the added practical benefit insufficient to justify the combined speech burden under the governing demanding review, given requested blocking and the other affirmative alternatives. This is no universal least-cost calculus and no newly invented multifactor test. His conclusion remains the same even if the operator-controlled lockbox accommodation works perfectly, because it implements rather than eliminates the default exclusion and request requirement. No technical finding about the accommodation's disputed eligibility is needed.

**Section 10(c) — reverse approval for protected indecent PEG expression.** The breadth of authority matters: unlike (a), the statute does not limit discretion through an express prospective published policy and reasonable-belief provision, and the affected speakers and uses come from local access arrangements rather than an established operator-composed presentation. Invoking the operator's property does not establish the necessary expressive interest at that breadth. The strongest affirmance path insists that private discretion cannot be equated with governmental censorship and that the record establishes no universal public forum. Breyer accepts both propositions but still finds the federal permission for complete content-selected exclusion inadequately justified in this statutory setting. The FCC's contextual definition is relevant but supplies no adult access path or missing statutory safeguards. He would decide the present protected-content authorization without classifying every PEG facility or adopting a comprehensive new cable test.

**Joins/remedy:** Can join Stevens's contextual (a)/(b)/(c) conclusions at the stated level and the controlling protected-speech/independent-review premises. Cannot join a speech-value hierarchy, a categorical no-state-action rule, or a universal PEG forum holding. Section (b) is independently removable while (a)'s limited choice remains operable. Section (c)'s protected-content authority is set aside at that scope; obscenity/otherwise-unlawful material, specific private expressive rights and 1996 §506 retain their separate status. No final transmission order follows.

## Proposition compatibility for reconciliation, without assembly

These are barriers and scopes, not a proposed Court opinion or an aggregate vote:

- An affirmance of (a) based on independent private choice is materially different from an affirmance assuming sufficient governmental involvement but finding a confined accommodation justified. Stevens, Souter and Breyer accept the latter bounded formulation; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas require preservation of their private-right/attribution ground. No join should be inferred merely from the common judgment position.

## Committed legal conclusions and opposing paths

### Actual contracts and what was promised

### Authority, sovereign power, and risk

Former section 1725(c)(3), the transaction-effective section 1729(f) assistance powers, and the actual Board resolutions authorize contracts and these merger-assistance transactions. Their applicable conditions matter. The packet supplies no actual failure of the statutory determinations or approval. The affirmative position recognizes monetary responsibility for an authorized contractual undertaking; it does not assume power to forbid Congress from legislating. Sutton's rule cannot be satisfied by labeling relief damages, but it also cannot be defeated by inventing a delegation defect.

Bowen, joined by Stevens and O'Connor, retains sovereign legislative power and enforces the express reservation in its statutory social-insurance arrangement. It also distinguishes genuine bargained debts and contractual rights. These thrifts claim particular negotiated consideration, not merely the right every participant received under a amendable welfare statute. The difference is material, although a money demand alone never establishes that a sovereign-power claim is absent. Tax-immunity and sovereign-surrender authorities remain available when enforcement would confer immunity rather than hold the sovereign to a financial undertaking.

Two affirmative routes must remain separate. One treats unmistakability as a protection against surrender of sovereign authority and holds it inapplicable to these particular monetary risk undertakings after establishing their actual scope. The other retains an exacting construction presumption and finds the incorporated transaction-specific accounting terms sufficiently definite to allocate the risk of their withdrawal. Agreement in the liability judgment does not combine those rationales into a single rule. Neither route should announce that every claim seeking money escapes unmistakability.

Horowitz recognizes protection for genuinely public and general sovereign acts and places the Government in the position of an ordinary contractor for that purpose. The source involved an embargo preventing shipment; it did not hold that a specifically allocated risk always disappears upon legislation. The adequate narrower affirmative rationale here is: even assuming the challenged measures qualify as public and general, ordinary impossibility does not discharge liability for the very regulatory-treatment risk these contracts allocated. A law's validity and compliance with it are different from contractual discharge. No conclusion about legislative bad faith or a predominant congressional purpose to evade contracts is needed or supplied. A broader theory treating FIRREA's particular impairments as non-general requires its own demonstrated basis and is not an additional committed holding here.

The strongest defense is that broad financial-system reform is public and general and lawful performance became impossible without any assumption of legislative-change risk. If the contract-scope premise fails, this defense gains force. Conversely, finding an actual promise does not itself answer which later risks were assumed. Scope must do the legal work; neither fairness nor the availability of money can substitute for it.

## Independent threshold and legal assessment

**Finality:** Both petitions invoke section 1257(a), but their assertions do not determine jurisdiction. Labron is a binding state-high-court pretrial suppression ruling; Kilgore reverses the adverse suppression ruling after conviction and requires further proceedings. Cox's **third** practical-finality category supplies the route: the federal issue is finally resolved and the Commonwealth ordinarily cannot obtain later review whether it wins a conviction or loses to an unappealable acquittal. The fourth category is not necessary merely because the issue is important. No broader jurisdiction over every interlocutory suppression ruling follows. The opinion dates differ from their January 1996 issuance/entry dates; no deadline ruling is inferred.

**State grounds:** Actual Evans retains Long's presumption for a substantially federal or interwoven decision without a clear adequate independent state ground. Sochor preserves an express adequate preservation ground even with alternative federal discussion, and Middlebrooks recognizes an actually independent state constitutional ground. Labron's state and federal constitutional references, Pennsylvania cases, and Commonwealth-jurisprudence formulation do not alone establish independence: its explanation invokes Carroll and Chambers as sources of the required exigency. Kilgore expressly finds a Fourth Amendment violation and is the clearer federal case. The committed Long application therefore permits review of both. Stevens and Ginsburg's actual Evans alternative presumption is relevant to their Labron petition assessment, but does not turn Kilgore's express federal conclusion into an invented state-law disposition. No preservation, waiver or state-law outcome is supplied by an operator-source limit.

**Ordinary automobile rule:** Johns, Michigan v. Thomas, Chambers and Carney reject a demand for an additional case-specific urgency solely because police could guard a vehicle, have taken custody, or previously could have sought a warrant, once the lawful automobile-exception predicates are established. Probable cause limits the object and extent of the search; Ross permits places and containers capable of holding that object. A sedan or pickup is not a motor home merely because parked at a residence, and private location is not automatically a separate-exigency requirement. Conversely, vehicle-search authority is not a license to enter every place where a vehicle sits. Probable cause alone is not a universal search authorization.

**Labron application:** The lower court accepted probable cause to search the Lincoln. The transcript affirmatively records failure to start when officers attempted impoundment, as well as four tires, glass, tag and apparently highway-legal exterior. The failure's time relative to trunk search and police knowledge before search are unresolved. The committed legal correction does not find that the engine worked, that the defect was permanent, or that officers knew it beforehand. A later failed start is not automatically proof that the constitutional vehicle predicate was absent at the time of search. The State's prior-opportunity dispute is separately not an added constitutional urgency requirement for a qualifying vehicle. The state majority's treatment of the alternative privacy-interest objection cannot be rewritten as a State victory on that objection.

**Kilgore application:** Probable cause was accepted below despite the officer's momentary loss of view and no plain-view contraband. Custody and officers' ability to guard the truck do not themselves defeat ordinary vehicle-search authority. The respondent actually presented the private setting and police control; those arguments are not waived by an operator's narrower extraction. The farmer's house/premises consent is not the truck owner's consent or established permission for every outdoor approach. Neither constitutional curtilage status nor lawful access can be found from driveway/rear-farm labels alone. The particular place, access route, protected interest, scope of permission and procedural presentation would matter to an independent access adjudication. A limited remand may reserve any properly available question without declaring it preserved or directing new factfinding. No search-incident rationale follows from rewriting custody as a preceding lawful arrest.

**Summary suitability:** The legal error is separable: requiring a distinct showing that there was no time for a warrant after the ordinary vehicle predicates are met. The petitions, oppositions and replies present that dispute. Summary correction can vacate the federal-rule basis and remand without a final search-validity or admission decision. Plenary review is the strongest competing route because actual mechanical immobility and residential access complicate application; denial is also lawful when the vehicle or independent-ground concern outweighs federal error correction. A limited legal correction answers the procedural objection only if its mandate actually preserves unresolved predicates. No unproved fact, waiver, default or lower-court reason can make summary action easier.
