## Components and supported alternatives

**A. Actually supported federal challenge.** The Does' original petition disputes the supposed federal necessity of Illinois' treatment of the father under Stanley and Lehr. Its Long argument is genuine. The majority's operative opinion, however, identifies a failure of proof under Illinois' statutory unfitness requirement: the father's efforts were frustrated or blocked, he had no opportunity to discharge the duty, his interest was improperly terminated, and the consent/unfitness prerequisite therefore prevented reaching best interests. It expressly describes the Illinois allocation of the adoption burden and notification duty. Those positive state grounds support reversal without a federal command requiring Illinois to adopt those protections. Their adequacy as substantive grounds is not disputed by a separate supported federal objection.

Heiple's later individual statement describes the reversal as correction of Illinois law and also invokes binding Stanley. The McMorrow concurrence cites federal parental-rights decisions while applying Illinois' statutory sequence and Syck's circumstance-sensitive evidentiary rule. Neither separate explanation replaces the original majority's sufficient state-law holding. They cannot be treated as an express courtwide federal holding, but neither can they be erased or converted into a courtwide declaration of independence.

The competing review path treats that federal discussion, the original petition and the absence of a formal independence statement as sufficient Long ambiguity, reaches the constitutional minimum and potentially remands for reconsideration. Its strongest objection to dismissal is that federal law may have influenced the state court's conception of parental priority. The response is the operative majority's concrete, sufficient application of Illinois law to efforts and obstruction, rather than a holding that federal law disables every thirty-day rule. A ruling that federal law permits a different state rule would not remove that actual Illinois ground. Review cannot become advisory correction of the explanatory writings.
