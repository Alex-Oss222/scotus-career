## Albright, Collins and Sandin: exact continuity test

The actual Albright judgment has **no controlling general rationale**. O'Connor, Scalia and Ginsburg joined the specific-source plurality; Kennedy and Thomas adopted a different rationale, and Souter's concurrence supplied neither a fifth plurality join nor a fifth state-remedy vote. This remains true after Love. No combination of the separate Albright opinions creates a rule foreclosing this substantive claim by arithmetic.

For **Souter**, the strongest argument for affirmance is his Albright reliance on Collins and reluctance to constitutionalize harms that do not constitute an additional substantial deprivation. The corrected record does not compel him to find such a deprivation in a delayed handgun purchase. It does, however, present an affirmative legal prohibition and claimed property or liberty injury that is not merely an injury ordinarily addressed by the abandoned Fourth Amendment theory in Albright. His recorded remand is confined to assessing that asserted independent deprivation under the proper standard. Preserving that examination is consistent with his reservation; a new definitive holding that Love already proves substantial constitutional injury would exceed it.

For **Ginsburg**, the strongest argument for affirmance is her full Albright plurality join and its insistence upon an identified constitutional wrong. The continuing-restraint concurrence did not itself establish substantive due process or make every official restriction constitutional injury. The plurality nevertheless expressly preserved independent protections under Soldal and James Daniel Good. Here no Fourth Amendment seizure rationale supplies a complete alternative source, and rejecting incorporation does not decide whether a distinct alleged affirmative arbitrary deprivation exists. Her bounded remand therefore has support in the preserved independent-protection limit and the corrected pleading posture. It is not a repudiation of her Albright join or an inference that all tangible restrictions satisfy substantive due process.

**Collins** governs the separation between state duties and federal constitutional wrongs. Its actual application concerns knowing protective omissions in voluntary employment, with different affirmative-deprivation claims left open. The five affirmers reason by that separation, not by pretending Collins directly adjudicated a licensing prohibition. The three remanders preserve the same separation but find the present affirmative settled-policy allegations require reconsideration without the lower court's erroneous shortcuts. A conclusion that the legal standard requires more than state illegality is common; their application to this complaint is not.

**Sandin** supports genuine substantive constraints in its prison liberty/process context. Its protected-interest analysis does not settle executive substantive arbitrariness, and its later-expungement discussion does not eliminate that separate requirement. The five affirmers may assume an interest and reject the constitutional-wrong element consistently with Sandin. The three remanders may insist upon examination of actual constraints without turning a prison procedural-liberty case into an automatic firearms damages rule. Neither the historical hardship rule nor the four-vote positive-interest application in the actual Sandin opinion is imported as controlling law.
