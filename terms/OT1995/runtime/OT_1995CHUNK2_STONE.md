## OT1995-012 — Klinger v. Department of Corrections +

**Event:** 1995-12-04 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 31 F.3d 727

**Docket(s):** Supreme Court docket not supplied; Eighth Circuit No. 93-2928

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Reverse the categorical rejection of comparability between the women's and men's prisons and remand the preserved program claims under ordinary equal-protection doctrine. Do not award universal program parity or constitutionalize identical prison administration.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** A sex classification does not become immune from review because the State houses women and men separately. Comparability depends on the opportunity being withheld, not whether every feature of two institutions is identical. Smaller population, shorter sentences or security needs may explain particular educational, vocational or recreational choices; they cannot answer every challenge at once. J.E.B. and Hogan require a genuine important objective and a substantial relationship for an actual sex classification. For a facially neutral decision, the required discriminatory purpose remains necessary. Stone applies those established rules without adding a novel prison-comparison test or presuming that expenditure totals determine equality.

**Record and relief:** This is a §1292(b) interlocutory appeal. The men's facility's much larger population, longer sentences and different security distribution matter when tied to the challenged service; they do not categorically make all women unlike all men. Restore the claims improperly foreclosed by wholesale noncomparability so the district court can decide the actual classifications and explanations. Preserve the existing pleading scope and the independent ruling denying a broad late amendment after extensive discovery unless a preserved error in that ruling is shown. No liability finding or systemwide injunction follows merely from this legal correction.

**Limit:** Apply existing sex-equality doctrine without a categorical prison-program exemption, while preserving legitimate operational differences.

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Reject the asserted entitlement to a favorable prediction about parole. Vacate the denial of the preserved, actually presented mitigation-instruction claim and remand for application of Graham and Johnson to the sentencing record. Leave guilt and matters outside the grant undisturbed.

**Reasoning.** The jury may not be told that a parole-eligible sentence legally excludes release. Simmons protects an accurate legal answer, not speculation about what parole authorities will do. A fixed statutory minimum, if properly presented as a distinct claim, is a legal fact rather than a prediction; that distinction does not establish that this habeas petitioner preserved such a claim or can obtain a new rule retroactively.

The mitigation ruling presents a different problem. The father's account cannot be dismissed merely because it fails to diagnose a uniquely severe permanent disability or conclusively link a diagnosis to the crime. Abuse and impaired functioning may bear on culpability independently of whether the defendant remains dangerous. Under Graham and Johnson the question is whether, considering the testimony, charge and proceeding together, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury could not give that supported mitigating force effect while answering the special issues truthfully. Weakness in the evidence affects what it establishes and the weight it may receive; it does not authorize the displaced categorical restriction.

**Relief and limits.** Remand that claim for the actual instruction, evidence, prefinality law and any properly preserved harmlessness issue. Stone would apply Chapman to a demonstrated, preserved denial of effective capital mitigation if a separate harmless-error inquiry is required, expressly extending the rationale of simulated Brecht/O'Neal rather than claiming their holdings already decide this category. Relief would be confined to punishment: adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence if the defect requires correction. No immediate release or automatic writ follows. Unintroduced medical evidence and a separate chilling claim require their own lawful basis; they are not assumed established by the remand.
