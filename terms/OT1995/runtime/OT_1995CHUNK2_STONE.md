## OT1995-015 — Love v. Pepersack +

**Event:** 1995-12-11 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 47 F.3d 120

**Docket(s):** Supreme Court docket not supplied; Fourth Circuit No. 94-1582

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026, applying only sources available by 1995; no Court vote or outcome entered.

Love v. Pepersack, Stone disposition

Judgment: Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded.

* Substantive due process: affirm dismissal.
* Claimed freedom or right to contract: affirm dismissal.
* Second Amendment: reverse dismissal and remand.
* Qualified immunity: not part of the principal holding unless the Court separately votes to take that question. If taken, Stone would replace ordinary clearly-established-law immunity with his legal-reliance defense and would not grant dismissal on the present record.

I. The claims must remain separate

Love challenged Maryland officials who denied her application to purchase a handgun because a computer record showed prior arrests. The officers did not investigate the dispositions. Maryland law did not itself make an arrest a ground for denial, and Love later obtained a state-court order requiring approval of the application. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/)

Stone does not merge those facts into one generalized liberty claim. His jurisprudence requires each constitutional source to do its own work and requires the court to distinguish merits, process, personal defenses, and relief. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

II. Substantive due process and contract

Stone would affirm dismissal of these claims.

Love may have had an entitlement under Maryland law to approval of a legally sufficient application, but an erroneous or even unauthorized state administrative decision does not thereby create a freestanding substantive due process violation. The State supplied administrative and judicial procedures, and Love successfully used them to establish that the police had misapplied Maryland law. The actual Fourth Circuit likewise noted that the State's process ultimately corrected the state-law deprivation. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/)

More importantly for Stone, the Constitution contains a more specific provision governing the asserted liberty to acquire and keep a firearm. Once the Second Amendment is held applicable, substantive due process does not become a duplicate route to the same protection.

The supposed "right to contract" fares no better. There is no separate federal constitutional right to complete every otherwise lawful private transaction. To the extent the attempted gun purchase implicates a constitutional right, that right comes from the Second Amendment. The actual Love opinion likewise treated the contract theory as part of the due process claim rather than as an independent constitutional guarantee. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/)

Those dismissals are affirmed.

III. The Second Amendment

A. The right belongs to individuals

Stone would reject the received Fourth Circuit rule that the Second Amendment protects only a collective governmental or militia interest.

He would be explicit that he is changing received law rather than pretending the result was already settled. That is required by his precedent doctrine. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

The text protects "the right of the people." The militia clause identifies an important constitutional purpose for protecting an armed citizenry, but it does not naturally transform "the people" into state governments or organized military units.

Miller does not require the collective-right theory. Miller asked whether the particular short-barreled shotgun before it had a demonstrated relationship to preservation or efficiency of the militia. In explaining the militia, the Court described it as civilians who ordinarily appeared for service carrying arms that they themselves supplied. [Justia Law](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/307/174/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) That is evidence about the kind of arms protected and the Amendment's militia purpose. It does not establish that no individual possesses the right.

Pre-Civil War state cases available long before 1995 support that distinction. Nunn v. State described the protected right as belonging to the people generally rather than only to organized militia members. [CounselStack Law](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/nunn-v-state-ga-1846?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Andrews v. State went further in distinguishing the political function of bearing arms for common defense from the private individual right to keep them, including the ordinary incidents of acquisition, maintenance, and use. [CounselStack Law](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/andrews-v-state-tenn-1871?utm_source=chatgpt.com) State v. Chandler likewise treated arms possession as serving personal defense as well as defense of the country. [CounselStack Law](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/state-v-chandler-la-1850?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Stone therefore rejects the collective-right statement adopted by the Fourth Circuit in United States v. Johnson and repeated in Love. The actual Love majority relied on Johnson to hold that Love had to identify how her handgun possession would preserve or insure militia effectiveness. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/) Stone would hold that requirement mistaken.

B. The core is keeping an ordinary firearm for lawful defense, especially in the home

Stone would not use Love to decide the entire scope of "bear Arms."

The narrower issue is acquisition for lawful possession. A right to keep an arm would be largely empty if government could prohibit a qualified citizen from acquiring one in the first place. Andrews, available in 1995, expressly recognized acquisition and transportation home as ordinary incidents of keeping arms. [vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/andrews-v-state-895669573?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Stone therefore holds that the Second Amendment centrally protects a law-abiding person's ability to acquire and keep a common firearm for lawful self-defense in the home. That is already the rule stated in his Arms domain. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

This case does not require him to decide concealed carriage, general public carriage, sensitive places, weapon categories, magazine limits, storage requirements, or the constitutionality of objective commercial licensing.

C. The right applies against the States

Cruikshank and Presser cannot simply be ignored. Both stated that the Second Amendment, as adopted in 1791, restricted the Federal Government rather than the States. [Justia Law](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/92/542/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Stone would say that proposition answered the original amendment's direct operation. It does not end the later Fourteenth Amendment inquiry.

By 1995, Duncan had established the selective-incorporation inquiry: a Bill of Rights guarantee fundamental to the American scheme of justice applies against the States through the Fourteenth Amendment. [Justia Law](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/391/145/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Benton made the doctrinal change especially clear, explaining that once such a guarantee is held fundamental, the same constitutional standard governs state and federal government, and overruling earlier nonincorporation precedent inconsistent with that approach. [Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/395/784?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The 1868 history strongly supports incorporation of this particular right.

The 1866 Freedmen's Bureau Act expressly referred to the "constitutional right to bear arms" among protections connected to personal liberty, personal security, and property, and required its equal protection without regard to race. [Equality Before the Law](https://teachinglegalhistory.unl.edu/s/oer/item/1844?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Senator Jacob Howard's introduction of the Fourteenth Amendment specifically included the right to keep and bear arms among the personal rights secured by the first eight amendments that the new amendment was intended to protect against state infringement. [Constitution Center](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/jacob-howard-speech-introducing-the-fourteenth-amendment-to-the-senate-1866?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Stone therefore holds the Second Amendment applicable to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause under the Court's received incorporation framework.

He does not need to reopen the entire Privileges or Immunities Clause to decide Love.

As to Cruikshank and Presser, Stone states the change candidly: their proposition that the 1791 amendment did not itself bind state governments remains historically correct. Their conclusion that the federal Constitution supplies no protection against state infringement cannot survive the later selective-incorporation doctrine as applied to a right shown to be fundamental. To the extent those decisions require the contrary result, Stone would overrule them.

IV. Who may be disarmed

Recognition of an individual right does not make the right absolute.

Stone's rule is status through adjudicated conduct, not status through accusation.

His Arms domain permits disqualification grounded in findings genuinely connected to danger: conviction of a serious violent offense, a judicial dangerousness determination entered with adequate process, and a domestic-violence protective order entered after notice and hearing. An unadjudicated allegation or uncontestable administrative notation is not enough. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

The same principle permits regulation where an adjudicated functional incapacity establishes that the person cannot safely possess a firearm. The category must concern actual capacity or dangerousness, not a bare diagnosis or administrative label.

Accordingly:

A conviction for murder, serious battery, armed robbery, violent domestic abuse, terrorism, an offense involving planned unlawful force against government, or comparable violent crime can constitutionally support dispossession.

An arrest for one of those crimes cannot, standing alone.

That distinction is essential. An arrest establishes that an accusation was made and that legal process began. It does not establish guilt.

Where a background check reveals a violent arrest whose disposition is genuinely unknown, the State may promptly investigate the disposition under authority its law actually gives it. But administrative uncertainty does not become proof of guilt. And a court may not manufacture an extra statutory waiting period merely because the legislature's existing deadline is inconvenient.

Stone's rule therefore is:

A State may verify an unresolved indication of potentially disqualifying conduct. It may not convert the absence of a disposition in its own records into an adjudication against the applicant.

V. Love is not disqualified

Love had arrests. She did not have the adjudications necessary to strip her of the right Stone recognizes.

The computer record showed several arrests, including battery charges, but their dispositions were not shown. The police conducted no further investigation and instead denied the application because an arrest record itself was their standard basis for denial. Maryland's statutory grounds did not include arrest alone. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/)

The only conviction identified in the published record was a Florida misdemeanor for participating in an obscene show, for which Love paid a fine. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/)

That conviction establishes neither violence nor dangerousness nor functional incapacity to possess a firearm.

The battery accusations potentially concerned conduct that could matter if adjudicated. But they remained accusations on the record before the officers.

Stone therefore holds that Maryland could not constitutionally deny Love acquisition of an ordinary handgun solely because those arrests appeared in its files.

Time pressure does not change that result. Public safety is unquestionably a substantial and, at the core, compelling governmental interest. But Stone's jurisprudence does not permit the State to satisfy a factual predicate by administrative convenience. The facts must satisfy the stated law. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

Love was therefore within the protected class for purposes of this case: a person with no adjudicated disqualification seeking lawful acquisition of a common firearm.

The dismissal of her Second Amendment claim is reversed.

VI. Disposition and limits

The judgment is:

Affirmed as to substantive due process.

Affirmed as to the asserted freedom-of-contract claim.

Reversed as to the Second Amendment claim.

Remanded for further proceedings consistent with the holding that the Second Amendment protects an individual right applicable to the States and that Love's arrests, without a qualifying adjudication, did not constitutionally disqualify her from acquiring the handgun.

Nothing in the holding decides:

* general public carriage or concealed carriage;
* sensitive-place restrictions;
* which particular weapons may be prohibited;
* ordinary dealer licensing, recordkeeping, or commercial regulation;
* storage regulation;
* the full range of constitutionally valid disqualifying convictions;
* the duration and restoration rules applicable after a valid disqualification;
* or any personal immunity defense not properly before the Court.

That restraint matches Stone's rule that doctrinal change goes only as far as the question presented requires. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

[Internal Stone request: take the qualified-immunity question]

Stone would ask the Court for a separate vote on whether to decide the officials' personal immunity in Love.

The proposed question would be:

When an official has violated a constitutional right, does individual-capacity liability turn on whether substantially identical precedent had previously announced that right, or is personal protection limited to demonstrated reasonable reliance on authoritative law that actually permitted the challenged conduct?

Stone votes to take the question.

If the Court declines, none of the following appears in the Love holding. The Second Amendment claim is simply remanded, and any properly available immunity issue proceeds below.

If the Court agrees to take it, Stone's position is as follows.

A. Stone rejects ordinary clearly-established-law qualified immunity

Stone would separate the constitutional merits from personal protection.

The constitutional question asks whether the official violated the right.

The personal-defense question asks whether the official should nevertheless be protected from damages because authoritative law affirmatively permitted the official's conduct and the official reasonably relied upon it.

Stone therefore rejects a rule under which liability disappears merely because no earlier case happened to contain materially identical facts. His Remedies domain expressly says that absence of such a case establishes no defense. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

He would replace that framework with the narrower legal-reliance defense already stated in his jurisprudence.

The official bears the burden to establish:

1. identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct;
2. genuine reliance on that authority;
3. and a reasonable basis for treating the authority as valid and applicable to the material facts.

Department policy, customary practice, a supervisor's instruction, legal silence, or a general power to perform the job is insufficient. MASTER_JURISPRUDENCE -

The defense is available when an officer genuinely followed authoritative law and Stone later changes that law unexpectedly. It is not retroactive punishment for obeying law that affirmatively instructed the officer that the action was lawful.

B. Presser and Johnson make the officers' argument serious, but they do not establish Stone's defense by themselves

The defendants would have a substantial argument.

In 1990, Presser said the Second Amendment did not directly constrain state government, and Fourth Circuit precedent as described in Love treated the Amendment as protecting a collective militia right. [Justia Law](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/116/252/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Stone is changing those rules.

That matters under his doctrine.

But it is not sufficient merely to place those cases in a brief after litigation begins.

Stone requires genuine reliance.

And the published Love record identifies a different reason for the officials' conduct. Pletcher and Pepersack testified that denying applications because of arrest records was the Maryland State Police's standard practice. The opinion does not say that they denied Love because they consulted Presser, Johnson, or any other constitutional precedent and believed it affirmatively authorized their conduct. [Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/120/472114/)

Custom is precisely what Stone's legal-reliance rule says is not enough.

C. Stone's vote on immunity in Love

On the record supporting the dismissal, Stone would not grant the defendants immunity.

That does not necessarily mean he would finally foreclose the defense after factual development.

It means the defendants cannot obtain dismissal merely by saying that Love's individual Second Amendment right was not clearly established in 1990.

They must satisfy Stone's own defense.

If they preserved the defense and can prove on remand that they actually relied upon identified binding authority they reasonably understood to permit precisely the relevant state action, they may attempt to establish the legal-reliance defense there.

If they acted simply because arrest-based denial was departmental custom, they lose the defense.

So Stone's qualified-immunity disposition, if the Court votes to take the question, is:

The ordinary clearly-established-law defense is rejected. The defendants have not established Stone's substitute legal-reliance defense on the present record. Dismissal on immunity grounds is therefore unavailable. The case is remanded, without prejudice to a properly preserved attempt to prove genuine reliance on authoritative law under the rule announced today.

That is where I would put Stone. It allows Love to be the case in which he attacks the existing qualified-immunity architecture without committing him to the opposite extreme of personal liability every time he changes constitutional law.

**Stone’s disposition:** Vacate and remand for application of §2's actual results inquiry and a lawful remedy analysis. Identify the legal errors affecting both the ultimate dilution finding and the separate remedy rationale; do not direct liability or impose subdistricts now.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** The appellate discussion asks whether voters supported a candidate because of that candidate's race and begins by emphasizing the absence of discriminatory enactment or maintenance. Incumbency, qualifications, partisanship and actual preference remain relevant contextual evidence, but cannot become a substitute legal prerequisite of racial motivation. The aggregate treatment must be applied to the actual challenged electoral units. **The independent remedy ground:** Alabama's linkage of electoral and judicial territorial jurisdiction and its qualified-candidate pool deserve concrete weight. They do not confer categorical immunity from §2 or make every racially aware remedial district constitutionally suspect. The lower opinion invokes historical Shaw's racial-message rationale; actual simulated Shaw and Miller do not make racial design alone an equal-protection violation. Holder requires an appropriate benchmark and workable remedy, not an automatic override of statutory opportunity whenever the State prefers its existing structure. Reject both automatic subdistricting and the claim that no adequate remedy can exist merely because judges are independent decisionmakers.
