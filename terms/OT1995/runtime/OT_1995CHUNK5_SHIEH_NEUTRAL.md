**Zatko**

**Holding:** When the Court is satisfied that pending submissions are frivolous and the applicants' repeated frivolous filings constitute extreme abuse of its process, Rule 39.8 permits denial of in-forma-pauperis status, meaning permission to file without paying fees. Denial may require payment of the docketing fee and compliance with ordinary petition form rules by a stated deadline; it does not decide petition merits or impose a broader prospective filing ban.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of the eight participating Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling is discretionary. It adopts no numerical threshold, does not make earlier denials alone conclusive, and decides no underlying claim or constitutional entitlement to unrestricted free filing.

**Martin v. District**

**Holding:** Rule 39.8 permits denial of leave to file without prepayment only upon a finding that the particular petition is frivolous or malicious; earlier unsuccessful filings cannot substitute for that finding. No. 92-5584's distinct general-rule and prospective statutory arguments clear the nonfrivolousness threshold on this record, so the Court grants leave without deciding that the arguments warrant certiorari or would succeed on the merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices; all nine participating Justices adopt this filing-specific application and its limits.

**Holding:** Where the current recusal petition identifies no concrete constitutionally disqualifying interest and develops no federal challenge to the independent state jurisdictional ground that defeats the action, those defects together establish frivolousness for this Rule 39.8 motion. In light of Martin's established extreme repetitive filing abuse, the Court exercises its discretion to deny this motion only, allowing payment of the Rule 38 fee and submission in Rule 33 form through November 23, 1992; the petition itself is not denied by this order.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six of nine control the motion, its stated ground and the compliance opportunity. Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Stevens dissent from this part.

**Holding:** The Court declines to impose a prospective fee-and-form condition on Martin's unidentified future civil-certiorari petitions because the established record does not show that the available treatment of particular filings is inadequate. This case-specific refusal decides neither that a prior warning is invariably required nor that prospective restrictions are categorically unlawful, and imposes no condition on future civil or criminal filings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices; all nine adopt the sufficiency of this narrower, record-specific ground without resolving the Court's power on a different record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No merits ruling on bar admission, ADA or Civil Rights Act coverage or retroactivity, immunity, the full complaint's jurisdictional status, or recusal follows. The two present defects in the companion operate together; neither professional affiliation, filing volume nor a state characterization alone establishes frivolousness. No universal warning prerequisite or categorical prohibition of prospective restrictions is adopted.

**Day v. Day**

**Holding:** The Court declines to impose a new requirement that Day pay the Rule 38 docketing fee and comply with Rule 33's printed-form requirements for unidentified future noncriminal certiorari petitions because the established record does not show that treatment of particular filings under existing rules is inadequate. This record-specific refusal neither requires a prior warning in every case nor makes prospective restrictions categorically unlawful, and it does not decide any current fee motion or displace existing authority over demonstrated abuse.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion of the Court, supported throughout by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine participating Justices adopt the same rule, application, and limited disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Filing volume establishes neither frivolousness of a particular petition nor inadequacy of particular-filing controls. No constitutional right to unlimited fee-free discretionary review or categorical rule about prospective restrictions is decided.

**In re Anderson**

**Holding:** Rule 39.8 permits denial of financial leave when the Court is satisfied that the particular submission is frivolous or malicious; prior unsuccessful filings do not by themselves supply that finding. Anderson’s affidavit supports inability to prepay, and the present petition’s specific, partly corroborated access-to-review contention supports granting this motion despite substantial independent obstacles to the requested extraordinary writ; the grant establishes no entitlement to that writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion and order, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices grant the motion on this bounded ground.

**Denton**

**Current rule:** A court may dismiss a claim proceeding without prepayment when its factual allegations are clearly baseless—irrational or wholly incredible—even without judicially noticeable contrary facts. The initial assessment must favor the plaintiff; mere improbability, extraordinary circumstances or ordinary disbelief is insufficient, and screening cannot decide genuine factual disputes. Review is for abuse of discretion, with legal errors independently correctable. The reviewing court must address relevant circumstances, including pro se status, resolution of genuine factual disputes, legal errors, adequacy of reasons, prejudice and whether more specific pleading could cure the defect. Such dismissal is not a merits adjudication barring a paid complaint with the same allegations; its possible effect on later fee-screening decisions makes prejudice and curability material.

**Authority by component:**
- Irrational-or-wholly-incredible threshold and plaintiff-favorable limit: [Denton v. Hernandez](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1991/records/Denton_v_Hernandez_merits_1992-05-04.md), May 4, 1992, “Factual frivolousness under section 1915(d).”
- Review, relevant circumstances and nonmerits character: the same record, “Appellate review and the consequences of dismissal.” O'Connor for Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas controls both propositions; the dissenters' agreement with the factual standard is not a whole-opinion join.

**Exceptions and limits:** Corroborating affidavits are not a pleading prerequisite. Rule 12(b)(6) merits pleading remains distinct from factual-frivolity screening.
