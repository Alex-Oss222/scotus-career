## Historical comparison: exact topology and limits

Historical Stevens Parts **I, II, III, V and VII** were joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Parts **IV and VI** were joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; Breyer expressly declined both. Breyer's opinion concurred in part and in the judgment. Historical O'Connor's concurrence in part and dissent in part was joined by Rehnquist, Scalia and Thomas. Rehnquist's historical participation has no substitute vote here, and historical authorship determines no simulated assignment.

Part V includes **three distinct grounds**: no preempting design command from this §510(k) clearance (pp. 492–494); preservation of genuinely parallel duties and a separate damages remedy (pp. 494–497); and survival of the challenged manufacturing/labeling claims because the asserted generic requirements do not supply the requisite comparison (pp. 497–502). Breyer joined all of Part V. It is therefore wrong to treat its manufacturing/labeling ground as located in an unjoined Part VI or to convert his concurrence into judgment-only agreement with all of Stevens's reasoning.

Part IV rejects Medtronic's sweeping attempt to displace common-law remedies through generic regulation. It does not itself establish that no common-law duty can ever be an MDA requirement. Part VI **reserves** that categorical question, predicts few or rare instances, and preserves independent conflict analysis. Breyer rejects the proposed significance of the Cipollone differences and the prediction of rarity; his own Parts I–II affirm that tort law can impose preemptible requirements, while rejecting preemption here after considering statutory ambiguity, the agency's role and regulation, and ordinary conflict/field principles. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas affirmatively include common-law duties, reject an added statutory specificity condition and inappropriate deference against clear text, preserve design and equivalent-duty claims, and sustain preemption of divergent manufacturing/warning requirements. Those grounds, not just historical outcomes, have been compared with the frozen model.

The official narrative at p. 484 contains an apparent direction-of-review slip in describing Medtronic's petition as challenging the portion affirming the District Court. The verified lower judgment, official Part IV's statement of Medtronic's actual challenge, and Part VII's disposition establish the mapping used below: Medtronic challenges the restored design claims; the Lohrs challenge the manufacturing/warning bars. No docket inversion is adopted from that sentence.

## Reconciliation result and changes to the provisional formulation

**Every Associate Justice's judgment commitment is retained.** No changed record, relief, preservation rule, or prior simulated premise supports moving an Associate Justice to a different component disposition. The stronger historical evidence does require the following precise rationale refinements. These qualify the final commitments below and supersede contrary common wording in the independent handoff; the original frozen file remains unchanged.

1. **Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg reserve the general express-preemption question.** The frozen handoff's shared sentence saying that common-law liability can impose an MDA requirement cannot be attributed to all eight as an affirmative, resolved proposition. Those four reject the company's blanket displacement argument and do not adopt categorical common-law immunity, but they need not decide whether §360k expressly preempts some other common-law claim. Their operative agreement here is the Part-V-level comparison and result. Their earlier simulated Cipollone positions do not establish a changed premise warranting elimination of the historical reservation: Cipollone construes its own clause, and these four historically distinguished the medical-device language even with the cigarette decision in place. The actual MCI, Sweet Home and Smiley rulings likewise do not compel that extra decision. This refinement changes the available proposition-level joins, not a judgment vote.

2. **O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas use the enacted comparison without an additional specificity prerequisite.** Their frozen references to a satisfiable regulatory specificity inquiry are retained only as an alternative explanation that these actual applicable commands do not become nonrequirements because they cover multiple devices. They do not join a holding that §360k requires an extra regulatory condition beyond its text, or that deference authorizes such a condition against otherwise clear text. The primary ground is that applicable GMP and warning commands are federal requirements and a materially different state conduct obligation is preempted. Their Sweet Home and Smiley positions respect reasonable delegated interpretation after statutory construction; they supply no changed premise for deferring on this question after finding the statute clear. No blanket rejection of agency interpretation in every case follows.

3. **Breyer's distinction remains material.** He affirmatively recognizes common-law requirements, joins the bounded absence-of-counterpart application, and gives substantial weight to the administering agency's reasonable specificity interpretation. He refuses both categorical tort immunity and a categorical rule that only a command naming an individual device model can preempt. He does not join a prediction that future common-law preemption must be rare. No circumstance has changed that warrants moving him to either categorical extreme.

4. **Genuine parallelism may carry narrower remedial elements.** The existing parallel-duty commitment includes the following express qualification confirmed at pp. 494–497: requiring negligence or an unreasonable hazard in addition to an actual violation of the same federal conduct requirement does not itself make the state duty broader; it may narrow when damages are available. This applies only insofar as the conduct obligation is genuinely the same. A changed substantive warning or production obligation does not become parallel through a pleading label. This states the operative limit of the already frozen duty/remedy distinction; it supplies no new claim, finding or federal cause of action.

## Questions, claims and common remedial boundaries

The 1982 substantial-equivalence clearance of the Model 4011 lead was not PMA or substantive FDA approval of its safety. The Activitrax pacemaker and lead allegedly failed after implantation; defect, breach and causation are not established. Classification, permission to market, required submissions, actual manufacturing controls and an approved substantive design are distinct.

No. **95-754** concerns Medtronic's challenge to restoration of negligent design and strict liability insofar as based on design. No. **95-886** concerns the Lohrs' challenge to preemption of negligent manufacture, negligent failure to warn, and manufacture/labeling-based strict liability. The consortium claim follows the surviving underlying claims without an independent finding of entitlement. The separate Florida-law warranty dismissal was not appealed and remains undisturbed.

The lower court expressly reached the parallel-duty argument despite its first appearance in reply. There is no procedural forfeiture commitment. It assumed, without deciding, that Florida recognizes such a duty and that the complaint could encompass it. Restoration of that possibility does not decide either question. The late FDA submission in Talbott was rejected below; its treatment does not mean that no FDA view existed. No unexamined present-case amicus position is attributed to the agency.

All eight Justices reach the submitted express-preemption questions. Each rejects preemption based merely on grandfathering, class III classification, §510(k) clearance, general oversight, or the submission of proposed labeling. Section 807.97 expressly separates substantial equivalence from official approval. Section 820.100's requirement that manufacture conform to an original design or approved changes is not itself FDA substantive adoption of that design. None decides preemption for an actual PMA approval, an unestablished device-specific standard, or a different device.

All eight preserve a genuinely equivalent, independently available state duty from **§360k(a) express preemption**. An additional damages remedy does not alone make the substantive requirement different. Equality must concern the actual obligation and application, not a pleading's use of the word parallel. A theory demanding additional warning content, different production conduct, or liability notwithstanding satisfaction of the identified federal duty cannot secure equivalence merely by invoking federal law. State elements, pleading, actual violation, causal connection and available relief remain for ordinary proceedings.

Section 337 does not create a private FDCA-enforcement action; its State-enforcement exception concerns enumerated food provisions, with its own notice and federal-enforcement limitations, not these private device claims. This preserves the distinction between an independent Florida duty and direct enforcement of the federal Act. No unpresented standalone §337 defense or later implied-preemption doctrine is decided. Freightliner leaves room for an independently established actual conflict; none is established merely by clearance or regulatory overlap here. No universal immunity from a proven conflict is declared.

## Statutory and regulatory qualifications integral to the commitments

These paragraphs retain the complete operative text qualifications and source boundaries from independent modeling. They do not attribute agreement on an added specificity prerequisite to O'Connor, Scalia or Thomas: their statutory interpretation and alternative regulatory argument are distinguished above and below.

1. **The enacted comparison.** Section 360k(a) requires a state requirement respecting a human-use device, an applicable federal requirement, a difference or addition, and the requisite relation to safety, effectiveness or another federally included matter. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer affirmatively treat common-law duties as potentially preemptible requirements. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg reserve that general express-preemption question while rejecting automatic displacement of these duties; they do not adopt categorical common-law immunity. Each compares the actual duty rather than relying on the negligence or strict-liability label. The Justices divide over the sufficiency and operation of the asserted federal manufacturing and warning requirements, as stated below.

2. **Separate exemption mechanisms.** Section 360k(b) permits, on State or local application and after notice and opportunity for an oral hearing, an exemption by regulation under prescribed conditions if the state requirement is more stringent, **or** if compelling local conditions require it and compliance would not violate an applicable federal requirement. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second alternative; it is not silently added to the first. Section 808.1(c) preserves federal requirements despite an exemption. No pertinent granted exemption is established. Nonpreempted equal duties do not first need an exemption. Section 360j(f)(2)'s manufacturer/device GMP exemption or variance is different: exemption requires that compliance be unnecessary to assure safety and effectiveness; a variance requires adequate proposed methods, facilities and controls, subject to necessary conditions and the statute's petition, decision and hearing provisions. No such exemption or variance is found here, and no agency-exhaustion requirement for the Lohrs is invented.

3. **Section 808.1 in full.** Subsection (b) includes force-of-law court decisions. Subsection (d) identifies specific counterpart regulations or other specific requirements applicable to a particular device before divergent state requirements are displaced. Its examples preserve general requirements whose purpose reaches other products as well as devices, including the stated commercial-law example, and equal or substantially identical requirements. Those provisions are not read without (d)(6)(ii): general adulteration/misbranding prohibitions may survive, but their application as a divergent substantive requirement for a specific device can be preempted; actual interpretation and enforcement, rather than identical wording alone, matters. The further exceptions remain intact: professional-practice licensing subject to conflicting federal §520(e)/(g) restrictions; state procurement contracts; Medicaid or similar payment criteria; general enforcement, inspection, registration and licensing subject to conflicting federal records, reporting and GMP requirements; administrative delegations; revenues and fees; and the identified atomic-energy/radiation measures until applicable specific FDA requirements intervene. None is factually applied to create an exemption here. Subsection (e)'s FDA responsibility, subject to judicial review, to determine equality or difference through the prescribed procedures does not establish an unpresented exhaustion bar. Subsection (f) preserves applicable federal duties whether or not a state rule is preempted or exempted.

4. **Manufacturing.** Original Part 820 imposes real obligations, not advice: quality systems, personnel and facilities, component acceptance, production controls and records, inspection, failures, complaints, labels, packaging and distribution have operative terms. Its scope concerns finished human-use devices; an accessory suitable for use can be a finished device, while the provision does not generally apply to component/part manufacturers and blood products have the separate Part 606 treatment. The everyday word component does not decide the lead's regulatory status. Additional critical-device duties require their own applicability basis; class III is not silently equated with every critical-device condition. Where compliance with two regulations is impossible the specific device regulation supersedes the general rule under §820.1; the exemption/variance route remains. No specific command, granted exception, violation, critical-device factual classification or substantive design approval is supplied by inference. Section 820.100's conformance requirement and device-master records do not themselves select the safe design for this lead.

5. **Warnings.** Section 801.109 conditionally exempts qualifying prescription devices from the consumer adequate-directions requirement, preserving its lawful possession and prescription/order conditions. Subsection (b)'s prescription caution and method-of-use label requirements have the surgical-instrument exception. Subsection (c)'s practitioner-use information includes indications, effects, routes, methods, frequency/duration and relevant hazards, contraindications, side effects and precautions; information **may** be omitted only if commonly known to licensed practitioners, with the written-request/Commissioner-opinion route retained. No qualifying omission is found for this lead. Subsection (d) separately governs labeling purporting to give use information, with its name-only reminder-piece exception. Subsection (e)'s issuance/revision-date requirement excludes labels and cartons. These are different exceptions, and permission is not obligation. Submission under §807.87(e) does not establish substantive FDA approval of particular warnings. No later amendment is treated as an earlier clearance condition.

6. **Savings and agency authority.** Section 360h(d) preserves liability despite compliance with an order **under that section**; it is not an unrestricted savings clause for every device suit. Section 371(a)'s efficient-enforcement rulemaking grant, together with the agency's device-regulation responsibilities and express §360k(b) task, supplies relevant authority for interpreting and administering this scheme, not an unlimited power to create or withdraw preemption. The formal §808.1 regulation receives consideration on its actual terms. Actual MCI requires statutory boundaries first; Sweet Home's six-Justice Chevron ground remains controlling; Smiley applies that sequence and assumes without deciding that courts always independently determine preemption's scope. Smiley therefore does not itself command deference to every direct preemption pronouncement. No separate litigating interpretation is automatically controlling, and neither its litigation setting nor age alone conclusively disqualifies a reasoned interpretation.

### Stevens — independent support and historical test

His actual Cipollone analysis identifies the liability duty and statutory boundary claim by claim. His Sweet Home and full Smiley joins support reasonable authorized interpretation after the Court determines the statutory boundary. Those premises support the frozen design, parallel and generic-regulation application commitments. Historical Parts III and V confirm them; Parts IV and VI show that rejection of wholesale displacement coexists with reservation of the categorical issue.

The strongest contrary argument is that an enforceable GMP or warning instruction already supplies the federal requirement, so a jury's additional conduct rule is preempted. Stevens agrees these are real federal obligations but concludes that enforceability and shared subject matter do not establish the requisite specific federal counterpart here. The alternative that every tort duty is exempt goes farther than his necessary conclusion; it remains reserved. He will join a bounded opinion retaining the actual comparison and regulatory support; he will not join automatic preemption from manufacturing/labeling overlap or findings of Florida entitlement. No changed historical premise warrants a different judgment.

### O'Connor — independent support and historical test

Her actual Cipollone join establishes the regulatory force of common-law warning duties. Her Easterwood and Freightliner positions require an operative federal predicate; this record supplies enforceable manufacturing and warning commands, unlike the absence of a substantive design requirement. Her Sweet Home and Smiley positions allow deference inside statutory boundaries, not against a clear enacted command. Her historical separate opinion at pp. 509–514 makes the last distinction decisive and confirms the component dispositions.

The strongest contrary reading relies on §808.1(d)'s specificity and general-applicability terms. Her answer is first statutory: Congress said applicable requirements, without an additional specificity limitation. Even a compatible reading of the regulation must account for court decisions and divergent substantive applications; it cannot erase a real different/additional duty simply because the generally framed federal instruction governs many devices. Conversely, preempting equal duties merely because damages add an incentive fails the statutory distinction. She withholds assent from both sweeping nonpreemption and total count dismissal where a genuinely parallel theory remains. Applicable exceptions, the actual conduct duty and Florida predicates remain to be determined without an invented compliance finding.
