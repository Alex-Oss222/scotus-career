## Wood v. Bartholomew, No.94-1419

### Question, record and shared legal floor

The reviewed September6,1994 Ninth Circuit judgment reversed a district habeas denial and directed a conditional premeditation retrial or reduction in degree. The January1995 implementing district order is part of the lower posture. This is a conviction-degree claim, not a penalty-only claim, and no finding of actual innocence is necessary. The petition-stage commitments support granting the stated Brady question. The following conclusions concern the permissible subsequent merits or reconsideration action.

Bagley and the simulated Kyles rule require collective lawful uses supported by the record, including preparation and investigation, to create a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence. Washington's guilt-stage exclusion of unstipulated polygraph results is a material limit on direct presentation, not a universal rule excluding every possible lawful fruit. The critical counterfactual is what supported lawful defense use disclosure would permit. The examiner's result is not proof of perjury, a substitute credibility verdict, or an admissible new eyewitness account. Rodney and Tracy's testimony supplies the identified direct premeditation evidence; the gun and surrounding circumstances still matter. Bell's penalty testimony does not enter this guilt-stage materiality assessment.

**Path W1 — bounded vacatur and renewed application:** Set aside the appellate command for relief and remand for supported, collective Kyles application. Its proposed admission, recantation and inconsistent statements cannot simply be presumed from further questioning; nor can direct inadmissibility alone end review. The strongest objection is that the habeas applicant has already litigated materiality and should not receive an opportunity to substitute an unproved theory. The remand therefore does not direct new discovery, another hearing, a new trial or another chance to invent facts. It requires application to the properly available record, permits denial, and preserves independent claims only as properly remaining. This path does not depend on finding counsel conceded everything the State attributes to him.

**Path W2 — reject the presented Brady theory:** Reverse the grant because the supported material contains no sufficient lawful change to the guilt evidence: a test result cannot itself be used, and an assumed confession or impeachment fruit does not follow from it. The strongest objection is that Kyles expressly protects investigative and preparation uses and does not demand a confession or admissible document already in hand. This path must therefore assess those uses collectively, assume counsel could have acted favorably to the defense, and rest on the failure of the identified counterfactual to establish materiality, not categorical inadmissibility, counsel's unverified concession, or mere sufficiency of the remaining evidence.

**Path W3 — sustain the existing conditional relief:** The strongest support is the centrality of the two related witnesses, deliberate withholding and the reported later inconsistency about examinations. Its barrier is that the examiner's adverse assessment cannot be treated as lawful impeachment itself, while the leap to a pretrial recantation or materially different admissible narrative remains unsupported in the described lower analysis. No provisional Justice below would affirm that analysis as it stands. This rejects the present justification, not all possible Brady relief after proper review.

## Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, Fifth Circuit No.90-8431

### Components and legal alternatives

The assigned merits review concerns individual legal immunity of Lankford and Caplinger. It does not concern a newly invented Supreme Court docket, teacher liability adjudication, district liability, damages award or TitleIX cause. Johnson controls the present interlocutory boundary; Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law. No Justice may decide what Caplinger actually learned in January, aggregate his knowledge with Lankford's, or convert a disputed inference into an appellate finding.

**D1 — legal denial for knowing, causal acquiescence:** Deliberately inflicted bodily injury through a teacher's actual school authority is a federal deprivation; a supervisor's own knowing and causally effective acquiescence can constitute personal participation rather than vicarious responsibility. The liability proposition requires actual awareness of the abuse or substantial abuse risk, deliberately unreasonable nonresponse, authority and opportunity to take relevant action, and causation of the injury or its continuation. Circumstantial proof may establish awareness; what an official merely should have discovered does not suffice. The theory is not a general constitutional guarantee of school safety or a duty created solely by state law. For notice, the pre-May1987 bodily-integrity and supervisory cases must supply the relevant principle without relying retrospectively on Canton, Farmer, Jefferson or Lopez.

The strongest objection is that Rizzo and the Ford/Reimer/Vela line, reinforced by Chinchello, distinguished affirmative responsibility or condonation from mere failure to act; municipal or custodial cases did not unmistakably settle an individual school supervisor's duties. A D1 denial is supportable only for the assumed knowing and causal conduct, including Lankford's repeated dismissal or suppression of complaints and relocation of the reporting student, not a broader negligent-inquiry rule. The lower court's phrase that facts pointed plainly toward abuse must not eliminate actual awareness.

**D2 — immunity for both on conduct-date uncertainty:** Even assuming a current constitutional theory, the 1986–1987 sources did not make this individual omission liability sufficiently clear. The strongest counterargument is that no fact-identical case is required and that knowing condonation of a state subordinate's deliberate bodily injury fits established §1983 principles. D2 therefore must distinguish the teacher's obvious wrong from each supervisor's particularized responsibility; it cannot declare that a minor lacks bodily-integrity protection, that state remedies defeat any substantive right, or that later Farmer supplies either retroactive notice or a new defense.

**Caplinger:** On the supported assumed account, his separated knowledge and actual responses do not establish a clearly defined 1987 federal obligation to take additional identified steps beyond his inquiries, reprimand and warning, before the later disclosure that prompted suspension. His failure to hold the broader meeting and the ensuing harm are the strongest contrary facts. The bounded legal immunity ground is that these acts and omissions, taking the nonmovant's supported version and preserving the January/February discrepancy, did not violate a clearly established particular federal duty. This is not a factual finding that he lacked knowledge, a conclusion that ineffective warnings are always reasonable, or a license to reject a richer properly supported assumed record.

**Later1987 omissions are included, not dropped:** The post-May continuation must be assessed with Jefferson and Lopez available. The D1 positions regard those decisions as additional notice supporting the already established restricted rule; their pre-May denial does not depend on them. The D2 positions acknowledge that Lopez speaks to school supervisory exposure but distinguish its bus-assault setting and actual rejection of supervisor liability, while Jefferson concerns the teacher's direct restraint and does not adjudicate supervisory responsibility. They therefore find that those later decisions do not clearly resolve the particular individual noncustodial supervision and postwarning monitoring obligation on this assumed record. D2 does not mean that the May decisions are ignored, nor that a newly identified affirmative approval or different preserved act would automatically be immune. The same distinction supports Caplinger's bounded legal protection without declaring his responses necessarily prudent or harmless.

## Hodge v. Jones, Fourth Circuit No.93-1182

### Live paths and operative limits

The preserved claim concerns confidential retention of the paper investigation report that recorded clearance. The separate erroneous AMF sexual-abuse code existed but was not added by an amended complaint according to the lower finding; no commitment denies its existence or adjudicates a new coding claim. The alleged acts occurred in 1989–1990. The later 120-day expunction rule does not supply retroactive notice, and the quoted five-year provision includes the no-further-incidents condition.

**H1 — affirm on immunity and prospective mootness, reserving federal merits:** The contemporaneous sources did not clearly establish a federal right to immediate disclosure or expunction of this cleared but retained confidential file under these circumstances. Whalen preserves questions of information privacy while accepting significant governmental collection protected by safeguards; Paul does not make stigma alone a status deprivation; Olim does not make mandatory procedure a substantive entitlement. Their combination does not clearly resolve this particular retention duty. The source record supplies no public dissemination, removal, interference with a particular parental choice, actual employment or benefit loss, or revoked clearance. The five-year period expired and plaintiffs did not claim required expunction failed; prospective relief is moot on that presented posture, without an independent destruction finding. Damages remain live but are subject to immunity.

The strongest counterargument is that state law may substantively limit discretion to maintain a registry of innocent parents, and that an informational injury need not await public publication or monetary harm. H1 expressly assumes or reserves those merits questions. The unclear statutory registry classification and unverified complete historical text prevent an expanded statutory construction, but do not supply an affirmative state entitlement or overcome the lack of settled federal notice.

**H2 — affirm a broader no-deprivation merits rule:** It is possible to conclude that the preserved confidential cleared report, without the additional concrete governmental interference alleged in other cases, does not establish the claimed deprivation. The strongest objection is that Whalen's unresolved privacy dimension and potentially substantive state limits cannot fairly be replaced with a categorical custody-or-money-loss requirement. With damages disposed of through contemporaneous immunity and prospective relief moot on this record, a broader constitutional decision has no necessary remedial work. None of the rows commits to adopting the lower court's full merits rationale.

**H3 — remand for statutory-entitlement construction or damages liability:** Mandatory limits could in a different properly established record support procedural protection. The present packet does not authenticate the full historical §5-715 terms or establish a clear contemporaneous federal entitlement for these acts. A new statutory construction cannot eliminate immunity retrospectively, and the claim does not become a preserved erroneous-code case through the footnote. Further source work is required only if that broader merits path is selected; H1 does not depend on declaring such an entitlement absent.

## Tuggle v. Netherland, No.95-6016

### Reach, predicates and adverse alternatives

The separate poll supports a limited grant by seven petition-stage votes; Scalia and Thomas favor denial but provide conditional merits commitments if the Court takes the case. No petition vote substitutes for a merits vote. The limited question assumes, without deciding, an Ake assistance violation that is otherwise available on habeas. The Commonwealth's 1995 opposition withdraws its earlier concession. Neither the prior concession nor the 1985 state ruling is silently treated as permanently binding or legally irrelevant.

**T1 — vacate the categorical surviving-aggravator disposition:** Zant's nonweighing rule concerned evidence properly presented, accurate and subject to defense explanation, and distinguished constitutional defects in the evidence or process. Ake protects meaningful psychiatric functions where its predicates are met, including relevant examination, preparation, testimony and rebuttal of State psychiatric future-danger evidence. The existence of a valid vileness factor preserves eligibility; it does not establish that the prosecution's psychiatric presentation, impaired rebuttal, unavailable preparation or lost mitigation could not have affected the jury's discretionary selection of death. The Fourth Circuit's deletion-of-factor rationale does not answer that independent injury. Vacate only the challenged sentence-relief disposition to the extent resting on that rationale, and remand for proper treatment of available threshold defenses and, if necessary, the legally appropriate error-effect inquiry.

The strongest counterargument is that Virginia is nonweighing and admissible future-danger evidence could still be considered with a valid factor, making Zant controlling. T1 accepts the nonweighing premise and continued eligibility but distinguishes ordinary evidence plus an invalid label from a proved deprivation of the means to test or answer that evidence. It does not transplant Stringer's weighing rule mechanically or assume that an Ake violation makes every prosecution psychiatric statement inadmissible.

**T2 — sustain the sentence solely because vileness survives:** This is the precise rationale under review. Its strength is Zant's rejection of automatic reversal from loss of one nonweighing eligibility factor. Its decisive limit is that an independent assistance violation is not erased by deleting the aggravator's legal label after the jury heard the evidence. No Justice below adopts that categorical cure. Each distinguishes this conclusion from a finding that the actual sentence was prejudiced under a particular standard.

**T3 — affirm on no Ake violation, default, waiver, Teague or harmlessness:** These may be lawful routes if established, but the packet does not support finally deciding the necessary requests, effective assistance, warnings/notice, preservation, finality or whole-sentencing record. The existing examinations are not automatically sufficient assistance; a requested preferred psychiatrist is not automatically constitutionally owed. The independent Estelle claims require their own predicates. Rejecting T2 neither invalidates T3 nor establishes the District Court's grounds. All eight preserve proper lower treatment instead of filling those source gaps.
