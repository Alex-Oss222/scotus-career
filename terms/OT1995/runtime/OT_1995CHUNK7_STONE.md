## OT1995-064 — Henderson v. United States

**Event:** 1996-05-20 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 654

**Docket(s):** No. 95-232

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Treat the action as timely commenced and adjudicate service compliance and the merits under the Federal Rules.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Distinguish the Act’s filing limitation from §742’s express direction to serve forthwith, and decide whether the latter procedural requirement survives the later Federal Rules and Rules Enabling Act supersession provisions.
- **Supporting Authorities:** Suits in Admiralty Act; Fed. R. Civ. P. 3 and 4; Rules Enabling Act.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** Timely filing commences this action. The Suits in Admiralty Act’s express forthwith-service direction is procedural rather than a condition defining the substantive waiver, and the applicable Federal Rules govern service through their authorized superseding operation.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Congress did enact a forthwith-service direction, so silence supplies no answer. Service regulates notice and the conduct of a timely suit, rather than the underlying class of claims for which Congress waived immunity. Apply the Rules Enabling Act and the Act’s own recognition of later inconsistent rules to this procedural direction. The 120-day period expired August 6, 1993; the court allowed fifteen further days on August 31, and the United States Attorney was served September 3. This position preserves the existing remand to adjudicate Rule 4 compliance; it does not newly decide the extension’s validity or deem the September service within the original 120 days.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not excuse failure to comply with Rule 4, displace an express statutory service condition without lawful superseding authority, or enlarge the United States’ substantive liability.

**Citation:** 54 F.3d 1169 (4th Cir. 1995) (en banc)

**Docket(s):** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm Johnson's qualified immunity on the March 1989 notice question, without adopting a categorical rule that private violence can never be constitutionally attributable to affirmative official conduct.

**Reasoning.** The alleged assurances were consequential, and the deaths are not rendered legally insignificant by calling the assailant a private person. Government may itself contribute to a known danger through an affirmative exercise of authority. But damages against this individual officer require more than identifying a plausible constitutional principle in hindsight. DeShaney had just rejected a broad protective duty, and the contemporaneous authorities did not sufficiently establish liability for the particular combination of assurances, charging decisions, third-party release and reliance alleged here. Later danger cases cannot fill that notice gap.

Stone would distinguish an affirmative use of authority that materially increases exposure to a known serious danger or obstructs available protection from a simple failure to prevent private crime. He need not convert every relevant fact into a mandatory element or announce a new multi-stage doctrine to decide this appeal. Nor would he require unlawful preventive detention as the constitutional solution.

**Relief and limits.** Enter judgment for Johnson on the individual damages claim within the reviewed immunity issue. Leave other defendants and legally distinct preserved claims to their proper disposition. The Court need not resolve the outer substantive boundary of state-created danger, and Stone's explanation of that possible boundary does not turn this immunity judgment into a merits holding that the assumed conduct was lawful.

**Character and precedent.** The updated Master Jurisprudence informs this individual position. The three roadmaps supply context only; this case presents no separate Second Amendment, Voting Rights Act or Commerce Clause question requiring an additional holding. Existing law, the presented claim and the actual record bound the disposition. Preserve applicable prior simulated law even where Stone would have preferred a different rationale. No vote or opinion assignment is predicted.

**Event:** 1996-06-03 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 706

**Docket(s):** No. 95-244

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** **Issue 1 — Appellate jurisdiction:** Affirm. **Issue 2 — Abstention disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit’s judgment vacating the District Court’s remand, on the narrower ground that this damages action cannot be relinquished through Burford abstention. A stay remains possible only if its independent requirements are met.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Permit a source-authorized stay if *Burford* abstention is otherwise warranted, but do not dismiss or remand the damages action merely to avoid federal adjudication.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** **Issue 1:** Determine whether the abstention-based remand conclusively surrendered the federal forum and is reviewable notwithstanding 28 U.S.C. §1447(d). **Issue 2:** Match the abstention remedy to the legal character of the relief sought.

- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** **Issue 1:** An abstention-based order conclusively surrendering the federal forum is immediately appealable when §1447(d) does not bar review. **Issue 2:** In an action seeking legal damages, *Burford* abstention may support a stay but ordinarily not dismissal or remand that relinquishes the jurisdiction Congress conferred.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** **Issue 1:** The remand conclusively ended federal adjudication, resolved a forum question separate from the merits, and could not be effectively reviewed after proceedings resumed in state court. **Issue 2:** Abstention coordinates the exercise of jurisdiction; it does not authorize a federal court to erase a damages action within its duty to decide. A stay protects state administrative interests while preserving the federal channel.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not govern a genuinely equitable action, apply where §1447(d) bars review, or foreclose another source-specific abstention doctrine.

The agency prevails under the surviving Chevron framework within the actual delegated field, not under independent earned-weight review.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not authorize the Comptroller to classify any charge as interest, validate an undisclosed or unconscionable fee, or displace generally applicable contract and consumer-protection rules that do not conflict with §85.

The Board’s rule remains within the permissible statutory range because it preserves timely representation challenges and later claims based on genuinely postacceptance events.

Allowing the first volunteer litigant to bind an entire public would convert representation from a legal relationship into a judicial fiction.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding preserves class judgments, genuine privity, actual control, source-authorized representative suits, and other historically established exceptions that satisfy due process.

* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No separate remedial instruction is required. Judgment for the defendants on the loss governed by the sustained sole-proximate-cause findings remains in place; no apportionment is ordered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Superseding cause under comparative maritime fault

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the governing causation rule and its application to the sustained findings. Do not review the excluded bifurcation question or recalculate navigational facts.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply comparative allocation and proximate cause as complementary doctrines rather than replace one with the other.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Reliable Transfer does not abolish superseding cause. Where supported findings establish that a later independent act was the sole proximate cause of the grounding, earlier negligence does not require comparative allocation of that loss.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The mooring failure explains the initial emergency, but the findings identify a later opportunity for safe navigation and a distinct extraordinary failure causing the grounding. Comparative fault apportions responsibility among actors legally causing the same loss; it does not decide whether each actor is such a cause. Contract and warranty theories likewise cannot recover this loss without their required causal connection. Exxon identifies no basis within the granted questions to disregard the sustained findings. [P1, 835–842]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ordinary foreseeable reactions to an emergency do not automatically supersede its creation. The holding does not excuse all suppliers whenever a captain is also negligent or approve every bifurcation practice.
