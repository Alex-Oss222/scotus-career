The controlling law and questions must remain separated:

- *Ward*, 448 U.S. 242, 248–254, asks first whether Congress intended a civil sanction and then whether the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect overrides that designation. *One Assortment of 89 Firearms*, 465 U.S. 354, 361–366, and *One Lot Emerald Cut Stones*, 409 U.S. 232, preserve genuinely civil forfeiture despite its relation to criminal conduct. Their particular weapons and customs rationales do not make every forfeiture remedial by definition.
- *Halper*, 490 U.S. 435, 442–452, remains law. A nominally civil exaction can, in its actual operation, become an additional punishment; the exceptional disparity inquiry permits rough remedial justice and does not make every deterrent incident or imperfect cost estimate dispositive. Kennedy's concurrence at452–453 insists upon objective character and circumstances. The actual *Kurth Ranch* holding recognizes a combined criminal-sanction operation, not a universal compensation ceiling or a rule that any partly punitive civil measure is a second criminal punishment.
- Actual *Austin* covers §881(a)(4) and (a)(7) under the Excessive Fines Clause. That holding survives fully, including its rejection of an in-rem-label exemption and its distinct excessiveness inquiry. It did not decide the Double Jeopardy Clause. A forfeiture can therefore require Eighth Amendment review without automatically producing a second prosecution or punishment for double-jeopardy purposes. Conversely, clause-specific analysis cannot become an irrebuttable civil-forfeiture immunity that extinguishes *Halper* or *Kurth Ranch*.
- Actual *Dixon* retains *Grady*. *Blockburger* and the retained essential-conduct inquiry, not the historical elements-only replacement, govern any necessary offense-identity issue. *Felix* still separates conspiracy from its substantive objects and rejects a same-evidence or same-episode rule. Actual *Witte* concerns the characterization of an authorized sentence for its offense of conviction; it neither immunizes an independent monetary penalty nor overrules *Grady*.
- Actual *Bennis* protects the separately owned interest of a claimant who proves nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention in the identified lawful automobile setting. It does not decide culpable-owner successive punishment. Actual *Libretti* preserves statutory and third-party limits despite agreement. Neither permits a consent judgment to enlarge the forfeiture statute. The June20 CF&I bankruptcy classification expressly decides no constitutional punishment question and is not a double-jeopardy holding.

## Supported record and remedy boundaries

Ursery's §881(a)(7) house proceeding ended in a May24, 1993 consent judgment and June17 payment of $13,250; his criminal jury trial began June30 after an earlier indictment. The Sixth Circuit's express rejection of the Rule12(f) objection is part of the vehicle. Consent or payment is not an established waiver of the constitutional objection. The majority's necessary-offense conclusion and the dissent's different-years processing/distribution allegations must not be collapsed: the latter identify a possible independent basis, not an adjudicated finding of a different forfeiture offense.

The companion civil complaint under §881(a)(6) and §981(a)(1)(A) preceded the criminal convictions but was deferred; the civil summary judgment followed them. The statutes cover more than proceeds. The record does not establish that every asset was proceeds, that every asset was lawful property, or that the corporate claimant had the individuals' personal criminal-jeopardy history. The Ninth Circuit left probable cause, excessive fines and a limited in-rem-jurisdiction objection unresolved. They survive removal of its double-jeopardy ground to the extent otherwise properly presented.

For both dockets, statutory civil design, the actual exaction, personal attachment of jeopardy, identity of offenses, and whether there were separate proceedings are different inquiries. A civil filing before an indictment does not itself attach criminal jeopardy. A stipulation to defer the civil case is not automatically consent to constitutionally forbidden successive punishment. The absence of a common factfinder and separate judgments cannot be cured merely by describing the prosecutors' efforts as coordinated. None of the individual positions below requires deciding a categorical rule for all coordinated civil and criminal litigation.

## Individual commitments

### Stevens

**Judgment and reach.** Vacate and remand in each docket, for different reasons. In Ursery, reject the proposition that this completed, owner-directed §881(a)(7) exaction is insulated from double-jeopardy scrutiny by civil form. Treat the paid settlement as completed punishment for purposes of the asserted successive-punishment objection; remand for the necessary offense-identity determination under actual *Dixon/Grady*, including the civil complaint's asserted conduct and the basis actually resolved by the settlement. Do not affirm a criminal bar solely because the same property and marijuana activity appear in both matters. In Currency, reject the Ninth Circuit's undifferentiated bar and remand for an asset- and claimant-specific distinction between recovery of proceeds or a genuinely remedial interest and a separate punitive forfeiture, followed, if necessary, by attachment, identity and sequence analysis. Preserve the other unresolved appellate grounds.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** His *Ward* dissent, 448 U.S.257–260, examines what an exaction actually does, including culpability and retribution. His joins in actual *Austin*'s historical/punitive explanation and *Kurth Ranch* reject formal immunity for property or tax sanctions. His actual *Witte* dissent treats an independently operative punishment as constitutionally significant even when its legal packaging obscures the additional burden. His actual *Dixon* position preserves *Grady* and supports scrutiny of what offense was actually prosecuted. The surrender of money to settle forfeiture of an ordinarily lawful home used in unlawful activity is sufficiently directed at the owner's offense to require the successive-punishment inquiry; government retention of the home is not the only possible punitive act. A settlement does not cease to impose the exaction because it avoided trial.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** *Firearms* and *Ward* support a civil classification notwithstanding deterrence, and actual *Austin* expressly reserved double jeopardy. That is the strongest route to reversal of both bars. Stevens would distinguish the owner's paid exaction in Ursery from removal of weapons from unregulated channels and refuse to make civil procedure dispositive of the additional burden. The opposite broad path—affirm both bars because any forfeiture has some punitive aspect—fails in Currency because proceeds recovery and the actual grounds for particular assets have not been separated. Nor is the corporation's own jeopardy established by convictions of individuals.

**Joins and limits.** Would join rejection of automatic waiver, preservation of *Grady*, separate treatment of each docket and retained appellate grounds. Would not join a holding that the Ursery exaction cannot be punishment, or that *Austin* is irrelevant rather than nonconclusive. Would not join automatic reinstatement of all companion forfeitures or an assertion that every asset is proceeds. His narrower remands do not require a constitutional innocent-owner rule beyond actual *Bennis*, a universal cost-accounting requirement, or a decision that all civil settlements attach criminal trial jeopardy. The commitment concerns completed punishment, with the offense predicate still to be established.

### O'Connor

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse the double-jeopardy grounds in both appellate judgments and remand for remaining properly presented matters. The statutory schemes and the grounds accepted below do not establish that these civil forfeitures became criminal proceedings or the exceptional additional punishment forbidden by retained law. Do not hold that an extraordinary, properly supported punitive application could never fall under *Halper*; neither *Austin* coverage nor the lack of a government accounting establishes that exception here.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** Her actual *Kurth Ranch* dissent places an initial burden on the challenger to show the claimed assessment lacks a rational relationship to nonpunitive objectives before demanding a governmental accounting. Her actual *Witte* opinion distinguishes an authorized measure's operative legal character from an argument that some underlying conduct has already influenced a burden. Her join in actual *Austin* requires full Excessive Fines review, not transplantation of its partly-punitive coverage test. Civil in-rem procedures, recovery of gains and forfeiture of property used to facilitate prohibited transactions support the ordinary civil classification; culpability exceptions and deterrent effects alone do not supply the clearest contrary proof. Apply *Ward* and *Firearms*, retaining *Halper*'s exceptional actual-operation inquiry.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** Stevens's treatment of the money settlement and the *Austin* reasoning offers a substantial punitive characterization. O'Connor would reject it as an automatic double-jeopardy inference without the objective showing her *Kurth Ranch* position requires. A narrower remand for a mandatory accounting in every case would reverse that threshold. Any distinct, properly preserved as-applied claim left unresolved may be addressed on its actual predicates; no such claim succeeds merely by repeating the categorical ground rejected here.

**Joins and limits.** Joins civil intent, the demanding punitive-effect inquiry, the clause-specific treatment of *Austin*, and these applications. Refuses categorical abandonment of *Halper* or *Grady*. Does not reach offense identity, consent-based waiver, attachment or coordination as independently sufficient grounds; they are unnecessary once the asserted prior punishment fails on the grounds adjudicated. No universal proceeds finding, automatic title transfer or resolution of excessive fines.

### Scalia

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse both double-jeopardy bars and remand. His principal ground is that these authorized civil in-rem proceedings are not successive criminal prosecutions; the Clause does not prohibit every legislatively authorized civil burden with a punitive incident. Preserve unadjudicated statutory, jurisdictional and Eighth Amendment issues.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** His actual *Kurth Ranch* dissent, joined by Thomas, would confine the Clause to successive criminal proceedings and reject *Halper*'s broader multiple-punishment treatment. His actual *Witte* judgment-only position likewise turns on the absence of an earlier prosecution for the later offense. His actual *Austin* separate writing accepts covered fines but treats forfeiture's historic property-offense relationship separately from a generalized owner-punishment account. These commitments support judgment-only agreement if the controlling rationale affirmatively rests on the continued validity of *Halper*'s broader theory.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** Retained *Halper* and actual *Kurth Ranch* foreclose pretending his preferred limitation is already law. Under those precedents, he alternatively agrees that *Ward/Firearms* civil classification has not been displaced by the grounds adopted below and that *Austin* does not resolve this Clause. The strongest contrary argument is that the paid settlement and owner-culpability exceptions supply an additional punishment notwithstanding form. He rejects the proposition that those incidents make the in-rem proceeding a criminal trial. His separate position must remain noncontrolling unless actually adopted; it supplies no silent overruling.

**Joins and limits.** May join statutory civil design and the distinction from *Austin*, but not a general affirmation of *Halper*'s independent civil-punishment doctrine. No join in an assertion that actual *Dixon* overruled *Grady*. His preferred rejection of *Grady* remains separate and is unnecessary to these judgments. Does not adopt consent waiver, automatic coordination or a conclusive proceeds finding. His *Austin* excessiveness view is not converted into the Court's exclusive test.

### Kennedy

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse the double-jeopardy grounds in both dockets; remand with other grounds preserved. Join a reasoned civil-classification holding under *Ward* that applies to these forfeitures and keeps an objectively demonstrated disguised criminal sanction outside its protection.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** His *Halper* concurrence, 490 U.S.452–453, directs attention to objective character and circumstances, not the abstract mixture of governmental motives. His join in actual *Kurth Ranch* recognizes that a nominal tax can have the combined operation of a criminal sanction. His join in actual *Witte*'s constitutional characterization separates an authorized consequence from a second independent punishment; he did not join its detailed sentence-coordination discussion. His actual *Austin* separate position with Thomas accepts the statutory fine coverage while withholding a universal historical owner-punishment inference. Taken together, these authorities support looking through labels when justified but do not equate every covered fine with a second criminal punishment.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** The house's ordinary lawful character and the money settlement make the punitive-application path serious. The missing link is objective transformation of the civil forfeiture into the exceptional independent criminal sanction, rather than its conceded deterrent and fine-related features. *Kurth Ranch*'s arrest-linked, confiscated-contraband tax combination is absent from the legal grounds established here. A categorical no-forfeiture-can-punish rule would be equally incompatible with his *Halper* concurrence. He supports the intermediate classification and preserves a distinct supported application rather than requiring an accounting by default.

**Joins and limits.** Joins civil intent, clearest proof, preservation of the actual *Halper/Kurth Ranch* exceptions, and the docket-specific judgments. Refuses an irrebuttable in-rem fiction or a statement that owners are never punished. His actual *James Daniel Good* opinion and *Bennis* join preserve independent property safeguards; neither supplies a successive-punishment bar here. Does not decide *Grady* identity, consent waiver or coordination without need.

### Souter

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse both categorical double-jeopardy bars and remand; preserve properly unresolved independent objections. Join the civil-classification application only with explicit preservation of retained substantive checks on disguised punishment.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** His joins in actual *Austin*, *Kurth Ranch* and *Witte* supply different clause-specific classifications rather than a single word-level definition of punishment. His actual *Dixon* support for *Grady* insists that the real offense predicate remain legally operative whenever prior jeopardy is established. His *Libretti* separate position with Ginsburg preserves a constitutional jury question despite an adequate actual waiver; it counsels against resolving an unnecessary constitutional issue from the civil label. His actual *Bennis* join does not create general innocent-owner or double-jeopardy immunity. Under *Ward* and *Firearms*, the grounds used below fail to establish a criminal proceeding or exceptional punitive application here.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** *Austin*'s punitive explanation and the settled monetary burden favor fuller scrutiny. He accepts that concern as the reason to retain *Halper* and the objective *Kurth Ranch* inquiry, but not as a reason to make the Eighth Amendment coverage threshold sufficient under the Fifth. A remand automatically requiring the Government to price every civil forfeiture against enforcement costs would give *Halper* a universal scope it lacks. A demonstrated, separately preserved punitive application remains different from that automatic accounting theory.

**Joins and limits.** Refuses language overruling *Grady*, collapsing conspiracy into its objects, abolishing *Halper*, or declaring all property forfeitures nonpunitive for every constitutional purpose. May join the common classification and applications with those limits. No conclusion that the corporation was personally tried, that all assets were proceeds, or that a stay stipulation waived successive-punishment protection. Does not resolve offense identity or settlement attachment unnecessarily.

### Thomas

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse both double-jeopardy bars; remand remaining issues. Primary agreement is in the judgments on the civil-versus-criminal-proceeding ground, not endorsement of a universal judicial recalculation of civil sanctions.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** His join in Scalia's actual *Kurth Ranch* dissent and his actual *Witte* judgment-only position support the limited criminal-prosecution account. His separate actual *Bennis* writing preserves attention to the historically grounded property nexus and refuses to make forfeiture of all remotely connected property self-justifying. His *Austin* position with Kennedy accepts these statutory fine protections while reserving an overbroad owner-punishment history. He therefore may accept civil in-rem proceedings without declaring every conceivable forfeiture historically authorized or free of other constitutional restrictions.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** Controlling *Halper* and actual *Kurth Ranch* remain contrary to part of his preferred doctrine. Applying them as existing law still does not establish the broad bars below from partial punitiveness alone; *Ward* requires much more before converting the legislative civil scheme. Stevens's substantive approach is strongest where lawful property bears a burden keyed to the owner's offense, but Thomas would test that burden under the appropriate forfeiture and fines limits rather than automatically attach criminal jeopardy. His historical skepticism about limitless forfeiture does not itself supply a same-offense bar.

**Joins and limits.** Would join civil statutory design, separate constitutional-clause treatment and the remands, while reserving his rejection of *Halper*'s theory. No silent change to current *Grady* or *Bennis*. No conclusive resolution of property nexus, innocence, proceeds, excessiveness or the unadjudicated jurisdictional objection. Does not require the Government's coordinated-proceeding theory to prevail.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse the double-jeopardy grounds in both dockets and remand. Join a bounded statutory and functional classification; do not decide every forfeiture statute or every punitive application from these cases.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** Her actual *Kurth Ranch* join establishes willingness to identify a civil-labeled measure's combined criminal-sanction operation. Her actual *Witte* join recognizes that a burden's relation to criminal conduct alone does not create a separate prosecution or independent punishment. Her actual *Bagwell* concurrence distinguishes coercion from completed-act punishment without joining every complexity-based explanation. Her *Bennis* dissent preserves historic entrustment/use rules in that record while recognizing the relevance of an actual equitable opportunity; it is not a declaration that ordinary owners can never be punished. Her *Libretti* separate position with Souter preserves questions not decided by actual informed waiver. These positions favor a source-specific classification under *Ward*, with the retained *Halper/Kurth Ranch* limits intact.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** The paid settlement and *Austin*'s analysis support the contrary argument that the owner has already been punished. She does not dismiss their significance; she distinguishes a fine's partly punitive character from transformation of a civil forfeiture into the separate punishment prohibited by this Clause. Broad automatic invalidation also disregards the distinct possible proceeds bases and the corporate claimant's position. Conversely, a declaration that a civil label always prevails would contradict her own *Kurth Ranch* vote.

**Joins and limits.** Joins the intermediate rule and applications, not a blanket forfeiture exemption, abolition of *Halper*, or an invented elements-only version of *Dixon*. Retains personal attachment and offense identity as separate questions when needed. Preserves other appellate issues; no automatic waiver from payment or postponement and no general coordination safe harbor.

### Breyer

**Judgment and reach.** Reverse both double-jeopardy grounds and remand, without resolving independent property, statutory, jurisdictional or Excessive Fines objections. Join the civil-classification application and preserve a genuinely exceptional punitive-application inquiry.

**Sufficient grounds and own authorities.** His actual *Witte* join is direct evidence: a sanction's relation to conduct does not itself make that conduct separately prosecuted or punished; the operative legal function matters. His actual *Bennis* join demonstrates that the functional and individual property interest still matters within the particular constitutional rule. He was not on the Court for *Austin*, *Kurth Ranch* or *Dixon* and is not assigned a fictional join in them; those are controlling law he must apply. *Ward*, *Firearms* and *Emerald* provide the civil forfeiture baseline. Actual *Halper* and *Kurth Ranch* prevent labels from defeating a proven contrary operation.

**Alternative and strongest objection.** An owner experiences a real loss, and the statutes deter crime; the strongest contrary path would regard that burden as already satisfying the punishment threshold. Breyer would reject the automatic inference because it erases the received distinction between civil consequences and separate criminal sanctions, and would also sweep proceeds recovery into a rule without examining its function. Mandatory accounting for every forfeiture similarly extends the exceptional *Halper* inquiry beyond its premise. A factually supported punitive application remains available under existing law, but the grounds used by these appellate courts do not establish one.

**Joins and limits.** Joins statutory intent, clearest proof, clause-specific treatment of *Austin*, the retained-law qualifications and the remands. Does not import CF&I's bankruptcy category, displace *Grady*, decide whether consent always attaches jeopardy, or accept mere coordination as a cure for otherwise forbidden successive punishment. No prediction of the remaining appellate results.
