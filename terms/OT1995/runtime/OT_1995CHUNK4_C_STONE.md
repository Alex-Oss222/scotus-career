## OT1995-039 — Bennis v. Michigan

**Event:** 1996-03-04 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 442

**Docket(s):** No. 94-8729

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Vacate and remand to determine whether Tina Bennis proves nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and that she took all precautions reasonably expected in the circumstances. If those conditions are satisfied, protect or restore her actual ownership interest through an appropriate property remedy. The State may still forfeit the culpable husband’s interest.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Ownership is individual. The husband’s misconduct does not automatically justify taking Tina’s share of their car. Joint ownership does not establish that she could monitor or prevent every use. Stone recognizes a due-process protection for an owner who neither participated in nor knew of the misuse and took the precautions reasonably expected of her in the actual circumstances. Ignorance alone is insufficient, but hypothetical legal control over a jointly owned vehicle does not establish a failure of reasonable care.

This adopts the protection suggested but left unresolved in Calero-Toledo. It is a development of the substantive due-process limit on forfeiture, not an assertion that Calero-Toledo already established the exception. Good supplies procedural safeguards, and Austin addresses excessive fines; neither independently establishes this innocent-owner rule.

**Mechanism and remedy:** Determine her actual ownership, knowledge, participation and reasonable precautions on the available record. Do not presume that joint title made constant supervision possible or that the conditions are already established. If she qualifies, preserve or restore her interest through release, division, sale accounting or another lawful property remedy that reflects the ownership and value actually established. Do not award the husband’s share to her or extinguish his forfeitable interest.

**Limits:** Knowingly tolerated misuse, culpable entrustment and a failure to take reasonably expected precautions fall outside this protection. The rule does not invalidate ordinary forfeiture or resolve materially different contraband and maritime settings. No compensation is required for an independently lawful forfeiture; the protected interest here cannot lawfully be taken on this ground. Protection of an innocent owner is distinct from the prohibition on successive punishment of a culpable owner.

## OT1995-107 — Action for Children's Television v. FCC (with Pacifica Foundation v. FCC)

**Event:** 1996-03-04 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Simulation-assigned merits opinion

**Citation:** 58 F.3d 654 (D.C. Cir. 1995) (en banc)

**Docket(s):** D.C. Circuit Nos. 93-1092 and 93-1100

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Preserve the rejection of the unjustified broadcaster distinction, but vacate approval of the substituted 6 a.m.–10 p.m. restriction. Remand with directions to set aside the challenged broad rule rather than select a new constitutional bedtime for the country.

**Reasoning.** The audience data establish that children sometimes watch or listen throughout the day and late evening. They do not by themselves establish that excluding every adult from the protected category during all those hours is a fitted response. Adults' access is not adequately answered by telling them to buy cable or stay awake after 10 p.m. Those alternatives change the audience's practical ability to receive lawful speech, especially for people who rely on free broadcasting.

Pacifica's contextual treatment of a particular broadcast leaves room to protect children. It does not require sustaining a general daily ban on all material within this broad indecency category. Sable's distinction between channeling and suppression requires a real examination of the burden on protected adult communication. Stone would accept the protective interest without requiring a new social-science study as a constitutional prerequisite. The legal defect is the sweep of this remedy and its inadequate treatment of adults and parental choice, not the mere absence of a favored form of evidence.

**Relief and limits.** The FCC may pursue a lawful, more confined approach under its remaining authority; ordinary obscenity enforcement and Pacifica's actual holding survive. The Court should neither prescribe station-by-station permission hearings nor invent an eight-factor broadcasting regime. A uniform 10 p.m. cutoff removes the unexplained classification but does not answer the independent breadth objection. This is Stone's proposed application of the existing speech principles, not a claim that Turner already invalidated the rule.

The three roadmaps supply context only; this case presents no separate Second Amendment, Voting Rights Act or Commerce Clause question requiring an additional holding. Existing law, the presented claim and the actual record bound the disposition. Preserve applicable prior simulated law even where Stone would have preferred a different rationale. ## OT1995-040 — SSC Corp. v. Town of Smithtown +

**Event:** 1996-03-11 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 66 F.3d 502

**Docket(s):** Historical petition 95-782 and Town cross-petition 95-1015; no separate simulated Supreme Court docket assigned

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** User-selected position; reasoning and disposition prepared for consideration. No Court decision is entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the Second Circuit's judgment: uphold the paid collection-and-disposal requirement and affirm invalidation of the flow-control ordinance. Leave the remaining payment and contract-liability dispute for ordinary proceedings on remand.

**Stone's reasoning.** A town may buy a complete public service and specify how that service is performed. Smithtown paid for disposal as well as collection; the designated tipping charges were part of SSC's bid. The destination requirement therefore governs a service the Town actually bought. Government need not own every facility or directly employ everyone performing its purchase. Using contractors does not strip it of the ability to specify the purchased performance.

That authority follows the service, not merely the formal boundary of the contract. *White* permits legitimate purchasing conditions to reach contractors' performance relationships. At the same time, the *South-Central Timber* plurality identifies the danger of treating participation in one market as permission to control another. Calling something a contract does not answer that objection. Here, however, disposal is part of the paid undertaking itself. Treating it as necessarily outside the purchase would disregard the agreement's substance.

The ordinance does different work. It directs independent private waste transactions to Ogden's privately owned facility through the Town's regulatory power. Simulated *Carbone* governs that discriminatory reservation of business. The Town's financing commitments and sanitation responsibilities do not make those private transactions its own purchases or establish that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives are inadequate. Its financial stake cannot by itself justify compulsory patronage of a favored private operator.

Criminal penalties are evidence of the ordinance's regulatory character, but are not the entire constitutional objection. Ordinary health rules can carry penalties. Likewise, direct contractual privity is not an absolute limit on a lawful purchase. The distinction here rests on the integrated service Smithtown actually bought and the ordinance's separate reservation of private commerce. It requires no new factor list or evidentiary procedure.

Simulated *Boston Harbor* reinforces the legitimacy of genuine government purchasing within its own labor-preemption setting. The dormant-Commerce result rests on the applicable market-participation authorities and simulated *Carbone*. It neither depends on a public-ownership requirement for ordinary procurement nor decides the treatment of a materially different public waste system.

**Relief.** The paired disposition decides constitutional authority, not whether SSC diverted waste, retained money it owed, or suffered an unlawful withholding of payment. Those disputes require the ordinary contract record. No damages or breach finding follows merely from sustaining the destination term.
