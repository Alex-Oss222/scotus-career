## Common petition-stage assessment

The petition presents an intelligible federal access concern: a requirement to obtain counsel's signature may operate differently from a fee-and-form requirement for an indigent incarcerated litigant. The Florida Supreme Court order requires a Florida Bar member's signature for future civil petitions and appeals; it does not supply the paid-filing alternative described in the Fourth District order. The distinct restrictions are not collapsed. The state court has made substantial abuse findings after a show-cause opportunity, but those findings do not automatically defeat review of the restriction itself.

That concern is enough for arguability under Rule 39.8, but the petition-stage case for plenary review is weaker. No developed conflict among federal courts of appeals or state high courts is identified in this petition. Its collection of allegations and numerous case numbers does not establish that one cleanly presented constitutional issue controlled each proceeding. The appended state order supplies the final denial of the current habeas petition and the future counsel condition, but not the underlying custody merits record, the full show-cause response, or a developed account of how the future civil restriction operates on a particular subsequent custody or mistreatment filing.

The Court therefore should not assume either that all custody-related routes remain open or that every such route has actually been closed. Nor should it find a preservation default from the absence of the full response: the state order records an objection to the attorney-signature condition, while the exact narrower federal formulation and its treatment are less clear. These are vehicle and scope limitations bearing on discretionary review, not adjudicated jurisdictional or constitutional bars.

The issue of access under filing restrictions is important and capable of recurrence. Its importance does not itself resolve the absence of an identified conflict and the entanglement of the proposed broader challenge with case-specific administration and incompletely developed allegations. The actual simulated Martin, Day, Sassower and Anderson rules govern this Court's own financial-leave and prospective controls; they do not, without another legal step, constitutionalize each of their docket-administration requirements for state courts. No new intervening merits authority or changed Government position supplies a supported basis for GVR. The present petition is not an appropriate vehicle for summary merits reversal on these materials.
