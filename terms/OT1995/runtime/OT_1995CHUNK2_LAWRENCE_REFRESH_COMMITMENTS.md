## Corrected propositions and their effect

1. **Appellate identity:** The judgment is the Fourth Circuit's. Correcting the circuit is necessary to an accurate decree but supplies no reason to change any Justice's position.
2. **Agency representation:** The agency now acknowledges examining the constitutionality of state intestacy/paternity requirements at least in some circumstances. The six supporting Justices rely on the possible effect of that changed scope of examination on a claim governed by those requirements, not on a new controlling merits interpretation, a concession of unconstitutionality, or an established entitlement. The two opposing Justices regard its qualified and untested application as insufficient to justify vacatur on this record. None finds this claimant within the agency's qualifying circumstances.
3. **State amendment:** The statute is effective entering law with the filing alternatives, test-evidence requirement, action-commencement window, expiration, and mandatory pre-death effective judgment date stated in the neutral addendum. ## Refreshed disposition and common supporting ground

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer support GVR; Scalia and Thomas would deny.** The numbers are unchanged, but the supporting ground is corrected to the agency's qualified constitutional-review position. Any formulation of the earlier handoff suggesting an already changed substantive eligibility rule must not carry forward.

The six supporting Justices find a sufficient procedural connection between the new acknowledgment and a benefits judgment applying state parentage/intestacy requirements to permit first-instance reconsideration. Section 2106 provides the remedial authority; Henry distinguishes reconsideration from deciding merits. Alvarado supplies a date-eligible example of remand without this Court resolving every possible alternative ground, while its dissent supplies the serious objection to surrendering judicial responsibility to a Government representation. The six make their own limited assessment of the position's potential relevance. They do not find the earlier judgment legally erroneous, that the agency must undertake constitutional review in this particular case, or that the benefits result must change. The remand permits examination of those questions within the lawful review channel.

The strongest argument against that GVR is now particularly clear: the agency's phrase is conditional and does not show that the condition is satisfied here. A newly effective state statute also may have no application. The six regard those uncertainties as compatible with the bounded agency-position remand, because the first-instance inquiry remains genuinely open and relevant; they do not count speculative statutory applicability as additional support. Scalia and Thomas instead conclude that possible relevance without an identified legal defect or demonstrated application does not justify vacating a judgment.

## Competing paths, authority, and remedy

**GVR versus denial:** Both remain lawful procedural paths on the supplied record. The six supporting Justices adopt the limited first-instance inquiry just stated; the two opposing Justices give greater force to the absence of demonstrated error or applicability. Their disagreement does not concern the correctness of any assumed constitutional or benefits holding. No comparative score or historical current-matter alignment is used.

**Possible statutory path:** A demonstrated qualifying action and the other operative requirements could make the admitted statute materially relevant, subject to its proper construction and the benefits statute. Those predicates are presently missing. The model therefore does not adopt this path as an additional sufficient ground. It also does not foreclose the lower court's ordinary authority to address a properly presented, genuinely applicable legal development. A direction to apply the amendment, treat a benefits filing as a paternity action, compute the unsupplied estate-claims period, or award benefits would exceed these commitments.

**Merits relief:** None of the eight supports a present declaration of constitutional invalidity, finding of parentage, award of survivor benefits, or retroactive entitlement. A plenary merits disposition would require source and issue development beyond this petition-stage handoff.

**Decree supported by six:** Grant certiorari, vacate the **Fourth Circuit's** judgment, and remand for consideration of the agency's qualified position concerning examination of the constitutional validity of state intestacy and paternity requirements. Preserve legally available threshold requirements and defenses, without reviving forfeited defenses or reopening independently closed issues. No agency or benefits outcome is prescribed; the judgment may remain the same after lawful consideration. The North Carolina amendment's mere effectiveness is not stated as a second, independently sufficient basis for this order.

Henry remains a distinction between procedural reconsideration and merits adjudication. Alvarado retains its own facts and competing judicial-responsibility positions. Actual Thomas Jefferson University remains confined to its qualified own-regulation doctrine; MCI retains its statutory limits. McCarthy and Darby retain their distinct exhaustion rules. A pure GVR supplies no substantive benefits holding. This refreshed Lawrence action does not enter Stutson's same-day baseline.

The two opposing commitments may support a common public objection to vacatur on conditional executive representations; no author or final joinder is assigned here. No new separate-writing function is needed from the state amendment.
