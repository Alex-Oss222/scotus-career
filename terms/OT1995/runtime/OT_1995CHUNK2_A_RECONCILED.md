### Common final result and rule

All eight **affirm the reversal of the existing equal-protection liability determination, vacate the direction to dismiss the reviewed equal-protection claims, and remand through the Eighth Circuit**. They reject both automatic liability from separate housing plus unequal programs and wholesale dismissal based on institution-wide dissimilarity. This is a mixed disposition, not an undifferentiated reversal or restoration of the district-court judgment.

For each challenged benefit, similarity must relate to that treatment. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery, and actual resource choices may matter; general differences between institutions cannot decide all particular claims without that relationship. An actual sex classification, whether expressed in the policy or its administration, requires an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship to it. For a genuinely neutral choice, disparity and awareness alone do not establish purpose: plaintiffs retain the burden of showing action taken at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex, including by circumstantial evidence. Housing segregation alone selects neither branch. Turner preserves attention to real prison administration; Craig, Hogan, Davis, Arlington Heights, and Feeney supply the classification/purpose distinction. Current Kelley confirms independent scrutiny of established sex-conscious treatment; its Title IX routes are not prison rules. Current J.E.B.'s jury-specific burden sequence is not imposed here.

### Common final rule and result

All eight affirm the Sixth Circuit's dismissal of the attempted appeal. A remand to the State court based on a timely raised procedural removal defect or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction falls within §1447(c), and §1447(d) bars review notwithstanding a claimed bankruptcy removal route under §1452. The two restrictions coexist: §1452(b) does not supply an affirmative entitlement to review forbidden by §1447(d). Thermtron identifies the covered-ground boundary; Rice applies the ordinary remand-review restriction across removal statutes absent contrary direction; Gravitt preserves the bar despite alleged error; current Germain supports coexistence where the statutes do not conflict. The remand's actual ground, not whether that ground was correct, governs the present jurisdictional inquiry. The applicable §1443 exception is preserved and not implicated; no modern exception is substituted.

### Supplemental positions preserved for assembly

**Kennedy and Ginsburg — Cohill reservation.** Thermtron held both that the particular unauthorized remand was improper and that §1447(d) did not bar review of a remand outside §1447(c). Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill subsequently recognized discretion to remand remaining state-law claims after elimination of the sole federal claim, notwithstanding the absence of express authorization, and did not decide whether §1447(d) bars review of such a remand. Kennedy and Ginsburg join the present covered-ground holding with the express understanding that it does not decide that distinct reviewability issue. They do not affirmatively establish reviewability or unreviewability of Cohill orders. This reservation may be reflected in a separate writing or answered expressly in the common opinion; the substantive position must survive either choice.

**Ginsburg and Stevens — independent §1452(b) ground.** They also conclude that §1452(b) independently bars the attempted appeal. Its authorization to remand on any equitable ground uses equity in the sense of fairness or appropriateness, not an obsolete law/equity pleading division; failure to remove promptly is within that sense. Treating statutory untimeliness as reviewable while equivalent delay labeled laches is unreviewable would make jurisdiction turn on a verbal label. The text, chapter structure, and the 1977 House report's stated parallel treatment of remand review support a supplementary bankruptcy remand regime, not an exclusive replacement for §1447. Hernandez v. Brakegate explains the broad modern sense of equity; Sykes v. Texas Air describes the incoherence of label-dependent review; Timbers supports reading the statute as a coherent whole.

They preserve the historical writing's alternative explanation: even if a procedural or jurisdictional defect were characterized as a legal rather than equitable reason not to hear a claim, equity may describe the choice to remand rather than dismiss. Section 1452(b) authorizes that fair disposition without requiring the impediment itself to fit an old equitable category. Their position concerns orders remanding claims related to bankruptcy; it does not purport to insulate absent subject-matter jurisdiction in a retained case. A decision not to remand may lack its own immediate appeal while jurisdiction remains subject to district-court reconsideration and eventual appellate examination in an otherwise proper review. Nor does their broad bankruptcy ground decide the separate Cohill question outside its statutory coverage.

These refinements change no judgment and displace no neutral fact. They refine the provisional statement that no Justice was yet committed to an independent §1452(b) ground: Ginsburg and Stevens now are, following examination of their exact historical reasoning and the absence of any materially changed premise. The other six are not counted toward that ground. The final handoff neither mechanically recreates historical authorship nor combines supplemental rationales into the common holding.

### Common final result and exact operative scope

All eight reverse the appellate judgments insofar as they sustain the firearm convictions on possession or storage as use, and remand both dockets. Section 924(c)(1)'s use prong requires actual, active employment of the firearm in the predicate crime. Mere possession, proximity, availability, protective storage, emboldening, or placement for possible later employment is insufficient. Use is not confined to firing or weapon use: current Smith's offered firearm in a drug exchange remains actual employment. The separate during-and-in-relation-to element must also be proved; potential facilitation cannot substitute for the conduct element. The use/carry distinction gives each statutory verb work. The limited rule does not require a new mens rea element or a free-standing lenity presumption regardless of text.
