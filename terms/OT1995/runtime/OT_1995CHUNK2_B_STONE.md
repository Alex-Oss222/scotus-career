## OT1995-103 — Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue

**Event:** 1995-12-11 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Simulation-assigned merits opinion

**Citation:** 28 F.3d 1501 (8th Cir. 1994)

**Docket(s):** Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the requirement of equal access on the established forum terms.

**Reasoning.** The district has opened the period to private activities concerned with children's development. It may define the forum by genuine subjects and ordinary operational needs, but it may not admit comparable moral or character instruction and exclude the religious perspective simply because it is religious. Lamb's Chapel supplies the governing distinction; calling every excluded religious perspective a different subject would defeat it.

The club remains private. Parental permission, voluntary attendance, parent leadership and clear attribution support that conclusion. The school need not lead a prayer, adopt a creed or persuade any student to join. Equal availability under those conditions does not turn the school into the religious speaker. The actual context matters, as Chabad explains; a sign would not cure real official control, but no such control is established here.

**Relief and limits.** Permit use on the same legitimate scheduling, supervision, safety and access terms applied to comparable groups. No special funding entitlement, school endorsement or compulsory attendance follows. Ordinary neutral forum changes remain possible, subject to the same prohibition on discriminatory exclusion. The ruling requires no new constitutional checklist beyond the existing speech and establishment inquiries.

The three roadmaps supply context only; this case presents no separate Second Amendment, Voting Rights Act or Commerce Clause question requiring an additional holding. Existing law, the presented claim and the actual record bound the disposition. Preserve applicable prior simulated law even where Stone would have preferred a different rationale. No vote or opinion assignment is predicted.

## OT1995-015 — Love v. Pepersack +

**Event:** 1995-12-11 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 47 F.3d 120

**Docket(s):** Supreme Court docket not supplied; Fourth Circuit No. 94-1582

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the retrospective §1983 damages claim on the actual pleaded theories and, where properly available, the absence of clearly established incorporated arms protection in September 1990. Preserve a separate Stone explanation of an individual ordinary-arms right and its Fourteenth Amendment implications; do not decide incorporation in a damages case that does not require it.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Love truthfully answered the application; an arrest without a disqualifying disposition was not the statutory ground officials treated it as. Her successful state review demonstrates that the administrative denial could be legally wrong. It does not by itself establish the substantive federal violation she pleaded, nor does it categorically cure every federal constitutional wrong. Albright supplies no controlling rule that adequate state review always defeats substantive relief. Her pleaded claims concern substantive due process, contract liberty and arms, not inadequate procedural due process. Hafer separates the officers' own conduct from the State's immunity; it does not abolish clearly-established-law immunity. On this 1990 conduct, a newly announced incorporated arms rule cannot simply be treated as already established.

**Stone’s separate substantive position:** The right belongs to persons and protects ordinary lawful possession, with meaningful lawful acquisition necessary to exercise it. A militia-related rationale is not equivalent to conditioning every personal protection on formal militia enrollment. Stone would examine incorporation through the Fourteenth Amendment's then-existing protection of fundamental liberties and candidly confront Cruikshank/Presser rather than cite later Heller, McDonald or Bruen. His proposed distinction permits objective, timely, reviewable licensing and disqualification based on legally sufficient dangerousness grounds, while rejecting an arrest-only administrative veto over an otherwise qualified applicant. That explanation advances the character's line without converting an unneeded broad conclusion into the Court's judgment. It does not define every weapon, carry location or disqualification.

**Remedy and limits:** Dismissal of these damages claims neither approves the arrest practice under state law nor bars relief on a properly presented different claim. Do not replace ordinary immunity with the Master’s proposed authoritative-reliance defense in this vehicle. Stone’s individual-right reasoning does not make incorporation an existing holding or enlarge this damages judgment.

## OT1995-016 — Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions +

**Event:** 1995-12-18 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 56 F.3d 1281

**Docket(s):** Historical petition No. 95-647; no separate simulated Supreme Court docket assigned

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Vacate and remand for application of §2's actual results inquiry and a lawful remedy analysis. Identify the legal errors affecting both the ultimate dilution finding and the separate remedy rationale; do not direct liability or impose subdistricts now.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** The appellate discussion asks whether voters supported a candidate because of that candidate's race and begins by emphasizing the absence of discriminatory enactment or maintenance. Section 2 instead protects minority voters' opportunity to elect their preferred candidates; it does not require that the preferred candidate be Black or that plaintiffs prove racially motivated individual ballots or discriminatory legislative intent. Incumbency, qualifications, partisanship and actual preference remain relevant contextual evidence, but cannot become a substitute legal prerequisite of racial motivation. The aggregate treatment must be applied to the actual challenged electoral units. Stone would correct that legal framing while leaving supported subsidiary historical facts intact; he would not simply choose the plaintiffs' expert over the State's expert.

**The independent remedy ground:** Alabama's linkage of electoral and judicial territorial jurisdiction and its qualified-candidate pool deserve concrete weight. They do not confer categorical immunity from §2 or make every racially aware remedial district constitutionally suspect. The lower opinion invokes historical Shaw's racial-message rationale; actual simulated Shaw and Miller do not make racial design alone an equal-protection violation. Holder requires an appropriate benchmark and workable remedy, not an automatic override of statutory opportunity whenever the State prefers its existing structure. Reject both automatic subdistricting and the claim that no adequate remedy can exist merely because judges are independent decisionmakers.

**Relief and limits:** On remand use the existing Gingles/Holder/De Grandy inquiry, matched to each challenged jurisdiction and actual minority preference, then determine whether the proved violation admits a lawful, practicable correction. The existing subsidiary findings are not erased. If those facts support no dilution under the corrected legal inquiry, judgment may again favor the State. The remand is warranted by the identified legal premises; it does not add a new statistical formula, mandate more expert studies or guarantee proportional results. No intent requirement is added to §2.

**Source and scope limits:** Apply actual Holder and Miller within their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes. Historical petition 95-647 identifies this same SCLC controversy; the corrected identifier changes neither the simulated merits assignment nor Stone’s disposition, reasoning or remedy. No separate simulated Supreme Court docket is assigned.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Seventh Circuit’s judgment. This is Stone's approved vote.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The disputed waiting is classified as the non-duty, non-rest return-transportation interval, subject to actual work being counted under the Act.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Post-assignment waiting within transportation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory question. Do not treat a disputed administrative interpretation as an independent source of power.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the enacted transportation category to waiting that is genuinely incident to returning from a completed assignment.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Waiting for transportation from a completed duty assignment to final release belongs to the statutory return-transportation interval, not to on-duty time; it also does not count as the off-duty rest the Act requires.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The general clauses for availability and other service cannot erase the provision specifically separating return transportation from duty and rest. The relevant travel cannot be performed without waiting for a vehicle, and treating that necessary interval differently would fracture Congress’s selected category. Fatigue is real but does not authorize converting all non-rest time into duty. Continued responsibility for the train or other assigned work would be different. [P1, 157–163]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No waiting interval that actually involves service is automatically excluded; no reduction of required rest or creation of a maximum permissible waiting duration follows.
