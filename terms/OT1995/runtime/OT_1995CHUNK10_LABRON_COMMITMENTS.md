## Independent threshold and legal assessment

**Finality:** Both petitions invoke section 1257(a), but their assertions do not determine jurisdiction. Labron is a binding state-high-court pretrial suppression ruling; Kilgore reverses the adverse suppression ruling after conviction and requires further proceedings. Cox's **third** practical-finality category supplies the route: the federal issue is finally resolved and the Commonwealth ordinarily cannot obtain later review whether it wins a conviction or loses to an unappealable acquittal. The fourth category is not necessary merely because the issue is important. No broader jurisdiction over every interlocutory suppression ruling follows. The opinion dates differ from their January 1996 issuance/entry dates; no deadline ruling is inferred.

**State grounds:** Actual Evans retains Long's presumption for a substantially federal or interwoven decision without a clear adequate independent state ground. Sochor preserves an express adequate preservation ground even with alternative federal discussion, and Middlebrooks recognizes an actually independent state constitutional ground. Labron's state and federal constitutional references, Pennsylvania cases, and Commonwealth-jurisprudence formulation do not alone establish independence: its explanation invokes Carroll and Chambers as sources of the required exigency. Kilgore expressly finds a Fourth Amendment violation and is the clearer federal case. The committed Long application therefore permits review of both. Stevens and Ginsburg's actual Evans alternative presumption is relevant to their Labron petition assessment, but does not turn Kilgore's express federal conclusion into an invented state-law disposition. No preservation, waiver or state-law outcome is supplied by an operator-source limit.

**Ordinary automobile rule:** Johns, Michigan v. Thomas, Chambers and Carney reject a demand for an additional case-specific urgency solely because police could guard a vehicle, have taken custody, or previously could have sought a warrant, once the lawful automobile-exception predicates are established. Probable cause limits the object and extent of the search; Ross permits places and containers capable of holding that object. A sedan or pickup is not a motor home merely because parked at a residence, and private location is not automatically a separate-exigency requirement. Conversely, vehicle-search authority is not a license to enter every place where a vehicle sits. Probable cause alone is not a universal search authorization.

**Labron application:** The lower court accepted probable cause to search the Lincoln. The transcript affirmatively records failure to start when officers attempted impoundment, as well as four tires, glass, tag and apparently highway-legal exterior. The failure's time relative to trunk search and police knowledge before search are unresolved. The committed legal correction does not find that the engine worked, that the defect was permanent, or that officers knew it beforehand. A later failed start is not automatically proof that the constitutional vehicle predicate was absent at the time of search. The State's prior-opportunity dispute is separately not an added constitutional urgency requirement for a qualifying vehicle. The state majority's treatment of the alternative privacy-interest objection cannot be rewritten as a State victory on that objection.

**Kilgore application:** Probable cause was accepted below despite the officer's momentary loss of view and no plain-view contraband. Custody and officers' ability to guard the truck do not themselves defeat ordinary vehicle-search authority. The respondent actually presented the private setting and police control; those arguments are not waived by an operator's narrower extraction. The farmer's house/premises consent is not the truck owner's consent or established permission for every outdoor approach. Neither constitutional curtilage status nor lawful access can be found from driveway/rear-farm labels alone. The particular place, access route, protected interest, scope of permission and procedural presentation would matter to an independent access adjudication. A limited remand may reserve any properly available question without declaring it preserved or directing new factfinding. No search-incident rationale follows from rewriting custody as a preceding lawful arrest.

**Summary suitability:** The legal error is separable: requiring a distinct showing that there was no time for a warrant after the ordinary vehicle predicates are met. The petitions, oppositions and replies present that dispute. Summary correction can vacate the federal-rule basis and remand without a final search-validity or admission decision. Plenary review is the strongest competing route because actual mechanical immobility and residential access complicate application; denial is also lawful when the vehicle or independent-ground concern outweighs federal error correction. A limited legal correction answers the procedural objection only if its mandate actually preserves unresolved predicates. No unproved fact, waiver, default or lower-court reason can make summary action easier.

## Scope supported by the six limited-summary commitments

The committed merits relief is a reasoned **vacatur and remand** in each case because the state judgment applied an added federal exigency requirement that cannot independently be demanded once ordinary automobile-exception predicates are met. It is not an unexplained GVR and not a declaration that every search here was valid. Labron's vehicle character and any outcome-material timing/knowledge issue, Kilgore's properly available access/location issues, and adequate independent state grounds remain subject to lawful state consideration. The Court neither finds those grounds established or preserved nor forecloses them through invented waiver. No evidence admission, reinstated conviction, new hearing, new trial or specified state outcome is automatically ordered. The two petitions must receive independent threshold and disposition treatment even if an eventual public opinion discusses them together.

A committed rule must retain probable cause and the ordinary vehicle predicate and must not describe police immobilization as equivalent to known preexisting mechanical incapacity. It must preserve Ross's object/scope limit. The Court's earlier Evans, Ornelas, Whren and Wilson remain in their own fields; no earlier controlling rule is overruled by this limited route. Stevens's and Ginsburg's petition/summary objections are not a shared affirmative merits holding on exigency.
