### Governing distinctions

1. **Capacity and scope:** Hafer preserves personal-capacity §1983 liability for official conduct and applicable personal defenses. It defeats the rejected official-capacity characterization; it does not establish a federal violation. There is no new State-immunity judgment component.
2. **Unasserted immunity:** Gomez v. Toledo, 446 U.S. 635, 640–641, assigns assertion of qualified immunity to the defendant and does not require the plaintiff to negate it in the complaint. Harlow, 457 U.S. 800, 815, preserves the affirmative-defense character while changing its substantive inquiry. Opposition p. 11 n. 6 expressly says the issue was not raised in the motion and would be alleged in an answer after remand. All eight decline to supply an immunity judgment on this record. This is not a rule that every new appellate legal argument is forbidden: Singleton v. Wulff, 428 U.S. 106, 120–121, recognizes limited appellate discretion. Here the defense was consciously reserved, no lower court adjudicated it, and the actual claim and defendant-specific application should not be replaced by an assumed defense. The five affirming Justices can decide the preserved merits without reaching immunity; the three remanding Justices leave it for proper assertion below. No permanent waiver is declared.
3. **State interest versus substantive wrong:** Roth and Olim distinguish a substantive entitlement from procedure alone. Authority to investigate does not necessarily confer authority to deny a complete truthful application. The five Justices rejecting the substantive claim assume a protected interest rather than adopt Gardner's absolute-no-discretion rule. The three seeking remand require attention to the actual statutory constraints but make no final entitlement finding. Simulated Sandin preserves real state-law constraints; its prison liberty/process holding does not turn every deliberate state-law violation into a substantive federal wrong.
4. **Actual Albright:** its judgment supplies no controlling general rationale. Its specific-source plurality, Kennedy/Thomas rationale and Souter additional-injury rationale remain distinct. No generalized state-remedy cure or tangible-loss requirement governs this case. Each Justice's own public position remains relevant as described below.
5. **Actual Collins:** the separation of a constitutional wrong from state duties is controlling; the voluntary-employment omissions application is not a categorical license for affirmative deprivation. Both sides accept its distinction. The dispute here concerns what the pleaded denial adds beyond a knowing violation of state licensing law.
6. **Second Amendment sources:** Cruikshank and Presser remain the inherited direct holdings that the Amendment does not itself bind the States. The modern selective-incorporation argument is presented and must be acknowledged; it is not resolved merely by calling the right collective. Miller addresses the weapon's relationship to militia effectiveness; it is not a holding that only a formally enrolled militia member has Article III standing. Verdugo-Urquidez's discussion of “the people” does not decide incorporation or all protected uses of firearms. No post-event arms decision is used.

## Enhanced Justice-specific comparison

### Stevens

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm rejection of the Second Amendment theory under the inherited nonincorporation holdings, while vacating the substantive-due-process dismissal. His own Daniels concurrence, 474 U.S. 327, 336–340, distinguishes incorporated protections, independently substantive rights and procedural fairness; remedial adequacy in the third category does not answer the first two. His actual Albright dissent permits development of deliberately arbitrary affirmative misuse and distinguishes Collins's protective omissions. His Collins opinion nevertheless requires a constitutional wrong beyond violation of a state duty. His Sandin concurrence with Ginsburg gives substantive constraints and the actual imposed deprivation legal significance, without making every mandatory procedure a right.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** Affirmance has a substantial argument: a screening policy related to potentially disqualifying convictions, followed by correction, differs from bodily coercion and may amount only to administrative illegality. Conversely, the complaint alleges years of knowing substitution of arrest for conviction, refusal to investigate and continued prohibition until the applicant disproves disqualification. Those allegations permit development of an affirmative arbitrary-deprivation claim; they cannot be rejected because state litigation was ultimately successful or because pressure to cut corners is inferred against the pleader. This is a remand for the legally sufficient federal claim and its elements, not a holding that malice plus state-law error always violates due process.

**Second Amendment alternative.** A modern incorporation analysis could question the reasoning of Cruikshank and Presser; Miller alone does not resolve that question. Stevens does not take that step here. His participation in Lewis, 445 U.S. 55, supports distinguishing the enforceability of regulatory restrictions from an absolute firearms entitlement, but its felon context is not a finding that Love is disqualified. Retaining the inherited state/non-state boundary supplies the judgment; no enrolled-militia pleading condition, collective-only right, or approval of all regulation is joined.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Remand only the substantive federal theory against the four personal defendants. Preserve lawful assertion of immunity, individual participation, causation, entitlement and damages questions. No joinder in a complete-merits liability ruling, a new procedural claim, an automatic constitutionalization of §442, or the theory that later state review cures an established substantive violation. Gomez, which he joined, bars treating Love's failure to anticipate an unraised defense as a defect in her cause of action.

### O'Connor

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm the preserved federal dismissal on the merits. Presser and Cruikshank retain their nonincorporation effect; on substantive due process, assume the asserted state-created interest but hold that the pleaded arrest-screening policy and burden of pursuing correction do not reach constitutional arbitrariness. Her joins in Daniels and Collins preserve the distinction between state tort/administrative duties and federal deprivation. Her own actual Albright plurality requires identification of the constitutional source and preserves genuinely independent rights; it does not make an unincorporated amendment silently displace all due process protection. Her Hodge opinion's availability of immunity does not compel reaching an unasserted defense here.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** The strongest remand argument is the pleaded, years-long knowing departure from a statute that denied discretion to prohibit this sale, together with a defendant-favorable inference about urgency. She does not resolve those factual disputes against Love or call the conduct negligent. The known illegality and malicious characterization still accompany a described policy directed to discovering whether serious arrests resulted in disqualifying convictions. The federal issue is the constitutional character of that conduct, not whether the administrative decision was lawful. Assuming the allegations, the choice is insufficiently extreme to establish the claimed substantive wrong. Neither actual budget pressure nor adequate postdeprivation remedies is an independently sufficient holding.

**Second Amendment alternative.** Her Verdugo-Urquidez join recognizes the textual “people” discussion but does not decide incorporation. The petition's selective-incorporation analogy is an argument for changing the inherited arms-specific result, not a change already accomplished by unrelated incorporation holdings. She retains that result without treating Miller as a collective-only holding. She would not join a broad rule that state officials may arbitrarily deny a right simply because firearms may be regulated.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Affirm the two preserved theories only. No State component, procedural-due-process decision, final Gardner entitlement rule, or immunity disposition. A formulation that all intentional state-law wrongs are necessarily outside due process exceeds her ground; so does one making state correction a universal federal defense. Her Hafer holding and personal-defense reservation remain intact.

### Scalia

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm. The inherited decisions foreclose the claimed application of the Second Amendment to this state licensing dispute unless reconsidered; this commitment retains them. His substantive ground follows his actual Albright concurrence and plurality join: §1983 requires an identified federal constitutional guarantee, and an intentionally incorrect administrative application does not itself create a freestanding substantive guarantee. He joins the bounded conclusion that the actual pleaded acts do not establish constitutional arbitrariness, assuming a protected interest. His Collins join supports separating violation of state duties from federal injury.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** The statutory constraint, direct prohibition of sale and alleged deliberate misconduct make the claim stronger than negligent nonperformance. His prior recognition of independent protections prevents a shortcut saying that every interest connected to an unincorporated amendment is automatically excluded from due process. But the complaint's independent theory still derives its demanded substantive outcome from state licensing rules; officials' departure and alleged malice do not identify the additional federal prohibition. He does not join a general rule that unconstitutional injury must be physical or economically measurable, and does not need to decide whether a different coercive or independently protected injury could support relief.

**Second Amendment alternative.** His full Verdugo-Urquidez join is an express barrier to reasoning that the words “the people” necessarily denote governments rather than persons. The competing textual and historical individual-right path is therefore material. It does not by itself establish application through the Fourteenth Amendment. This judgment retains nonincorporation without resolving the entire individual-right question, expanding Miller's weapon inquiry into a status requirement, or importing a later original-meaning conclusion. The source limitation is affirmative: no own date-eligible firearms-incorporation writing in the authorized record establishes a contrary commitment; no future writing is supplied in its place.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Affirm without reaching qualified immunity. His Hodge join supports properly presented immunity; it does not eliminate Gomez's allocation or authorize a silently constructed defense. Do not count his source-identification explanation as a join in any broader formula for executive substantive due process or in a categorical state-remedy cure.

### Kennedy

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm both preserved theories. Retain the nonincorporation disposition; assume the claimed property interest and reject a substantive federal right to legally correct application of this screening program on the pleaded facts. His Collins join supports a distinction between assumed state-created rights and conscience-shocking deprivation. His actual Albright concurrence, joined there by Thomas, rejects an asserted independent substantive guarantee and separately treats available remedies only in the particular random-unauthorized-deprivation alternative.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** The strongest remand path emphasizes affirmative misuse under a settled policy, not random unauthorized action, and the difference between eventual permission and damages for the preceding denial. That is a real limitation on his Albright alternative: he cannot simply extend Parratt/Hudson to cure every established substantive deprivation. His affirmance rests instead on the nature of the claimed substantive guarantee. Even accepting the policy and knowing state-law violation, the fact pattern concerns screening for disqualifying convictions and correction of a mistaken legal standard; it does not establish the additional constitutional arbitrariness required by Collins. The judge need not credit an actual emergency or find the policy reasonable under Maryland law.

**Second Amendment alternative.** His Verdugo-Urquidez concurrence, 494 U.S. 259, 275–278, expressly refuses to use “the people” as a simple exclusionary classification. He likewise will not use militia enrollment as a standing device here. Modern incorporation could alter the inherited result only through an affirmative constitutional holding; neither the textual argument nor the cited unrelated incorporation decisions compels that holding in this disposition. He retains the arms-specific precedent without adopting the Fourth Circuit's full account of the federal right.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Affirm with no immunity judgment and no final entitlement determination. No joinder in the proposition that administrative or judicial success categorically extinguishes substantive federal injury. No ruling that all nonphysical liberty lacks protection. The State and abandoned theories remain outside review.

### Souter

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm the independent Second Amendment rejection on inherited nonincorporation, but vacate and remand the substantive theory. His actual Albright concurrence expressly preserves an otherwise unprotected additional substantial deprivation. This complaint alleges direct loss of the legal ability to complete a purchase through official prohibition, not merely the prosecutorial consequences ordinarily examined under the Fourth Amendment. His Collins join leaves affirmative deprivation distinct from protective omissions. His Sandin concurrence with Breyer treats the complete statutory scheme and meaningful restraint as necessary to assessing protected interests.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** Affirmance is supported by the gap between violation of an assumed entitlement and constitutional arbitrariness; a safety-related investigation is not made conscience shocking simply by a state-law defect. But the Fourth Circuit also relied on successful correction and an asserted factual pressure that does not resolve the pleaded years-long burden-shifting policy. His bounded remand removes those categorical shortcuts and requires examination of the actual entitlement and allegedly deliberate deprivation. It does not find that the interim inability to purchase is constitutionally sufficient, establish malice, or demand a trial regardless of a legally adequate renewed motion.

**Second Amendment alternative.** Selective incorporation and an individual understanding could supply a different theory, and Miller's federal weapon inquiry does not foreclose that argument. Souter retains Presser's state boundary in this case; his recognition of overlapping protections under Soldal and James Daniel Good in Albright explains why rejection of this particular amendment theory does not decide the separate substantive claim. A no-Second-Amendment result is not a no-property-interest result.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Vacate only the substantive-due-process dismissal and return for further proceedings, including any properly asserted personal immunity defense. No automatic discovery or liability order, no revival of abandoned claims, and no required merits-first sequence after a proper immunity assertion. He refuses both a universal postdeprivation-cure rule and a universal federal remedy for knowing statutory departures. His Hodge join remains consistent: there immunity was the presented issue; here it has not been raised in the motion.

### Thomas

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm the two preserved federal theories. Retain nonincorporation without a categorical collective-right holding. On due process, his actual Albright join with Kennedy and his Collins join support requiring a substantive federal guarantee beyond state administrative legality, even assuming the state interest. The alleged arrests policy, supervisory knowledge and state-law error do not establish the independent substantive guarantee asserted here.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** An affirmative restriction pursuant to a continuing policy is not the voluntary-workplace omission in Collins, and cannot automatically be categorized as random unauthorized conduct under Parratt. His answer is the antecedent absence of the claimed independent substantive constitutional wrong on these facts, rather than the adequacy of Maryland review. He can accept intentional implementation and the alleged knowledge of statutory limits while rejecting the legal conclusion that those allegations alone cross the constitutional boundary. No hypothetical negligence premise supplies his vote.

**Second Amendment alternative.** The individual-right argument based on text and history remains a genuine legal alternative to the Fourth Circuit's collective-right reasoning. The authorized date-eligible materials do not establish an own-writing commitment that completes the separate Fourteenth Amendment incorporation inquiry. This model therefore neither fabricates such a prior position nor infers it from later jurisprudence. He retains the inherited result while withholding assent from a government-only right, an enrolled-militia standing requirement, or an asserted definitive reading of all protected weapons and uses. Retention of precedent here does not announce that constitutional precedents can never be reconsidered.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Affirm on merits, no immunity disposition or State component. His Hodge join supports the appropriate conduct-date inquiry when the defense is properly at issue, not plaintiff-borne anticipation of an unraised defense. No join in generalized substantive balancing, universal state-cure reasoning, or a finding that the officials complied with Maryland law.

### Ginsburg

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm the Second Amendment rejection under retained nonincorporation, but vacate and remand the substantive-due-process theory. Her actual Albright concurrence and full specific-source join retain the relevance of a concretely imposed legal restraint; they do not hold that every separate liberty or property deprivation disappears because a different amendment is mentioned. Her Sandin concurrence, joined by Stevens, distinguishes genuine deprivation and substantive constraints from drafting conventions and rejects treating later relief as retroactive erasure of the protected event. The analogy here concerns recognition and pleading of a restraint, not importation of prison procedure or a conclusion that firearm possession is fundamental.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** The strongest affirmance path is that state law may create an entitlement without making every malicious licensing error a substantive constitutional violation. She accepts that limit and Collins's source discipline. The strongest reason for remand is that this complaint alleges direct official prohibition under a knowingly unlawful, settled practice, accompanied by the actual need for litigation to remove the prohibition. The appellate disposition cannot simply substitute an exigency inference for those allegations or say the State cured every possible substantive injury. Further proceedings must test whether the pleaded facts supply federal arbitrariness, not merely recite the statutory breach.

**Second Amendment alternative.** The modern incorporation argument is presented, and its historical footing cannot be disposed of by misdescribing petitioner's authorities as nothing but law-review articles. She nonetheless retains the arms-specific nonincorporation result. Her Albright insistence upon the actual constitutional source supports identifying the distinct state-application question before deciding the federal right's full scope. No join in a collective-only declaration, an Article III defect based on absent militia membership, or a right of state officials to disregard an established incorporated guarantee.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Remand only the substantive federal theory, leaving entitlement, each officer's involvement, culpability, causation, damages and properly asserted immunity open. Her Hodge and Kimberlin joins permit appropriate immunity adjudication while respecting the actual claim and supported facts. No mandatory discovery, automatic success, new procedure claim or finding that statutory illegality itself establishes a federal violation.

### Breyer

**Sufficient commitment.** Affirm both preserved federal theories, retaining nonincorporation and rejecting substantive arbitrariness on the bounded pleaded facts. Amsden v. Moran, 904 F.2d 748, 753–758 (1st Cir. 1990), an opinion he joined, is particularly pertinent: license-related administrative illegality and even alleged bad faith do not necessarily establish an independently egregious constitutional wrong. Its judgment also rests on actually asserted immunity, so that part is not transported to Love. His actual Sandin opinion preserves substantive constraints and meaningful interests; it does not collapse the distinct question whether state action is substantively unconstitutional.

**Competing paths and decisive limit.** The remand argument identifies affirmative denial, alleged malice, a longstanding policy and an inference adverse to the plaintiff about time pressure. He accepts that these cannot be redescribed as proven good faith. Amsden's distinction nevertheless applies: an improper method of resolving uncertainty about disqualifying convictions remains a licensing-administration dispute absent additional facts establishing constitutional oppression. Alleging a deliberate departure from the prescribed method does not itself supply that additional element. His conclusion assumes the substantive interest and does not rest on whether the statute uses mandatory wording or whether a permit was eventually obtained.

**Second Amendment alternative.** The individual-right and incorporation arguments present separate questions; existing rights need not be absolute to be protected. He retains the arms-specific state-application precedents, without deriving a collective-only rule from Miller or treating Lewis's felon restriction as factually identical to Love's absence of a disqualifying conviction. His actual Sandin treatment of substantive constraints is a barrier to arguing that any administrative discretion necessarily eliminates every entitlement, not a commitment to incorporate the Second Amendment.

**Remedy and join barriers.** Affirm without an immunity judgment. No broader holding that state remedies invariably defeat substantive claims; no return to the historical Sandin replacement rule; no automatic invalidation of state-created interests because some investigative judgment remains. A materially different claim of independently oppressive affirmative action remains outside this bounded merits disposition.

## Proposition-level compatibility and downstream limits

- **Scope and defense:** Eight support individual-capacity treatment, exclusion of the unappealed/abandoned claims, and no present immunity adjudication. This is distinct from holding that the defense is forfeited forever or could never be addressed on an adequate appellate record. Harlow/Davis conduct-date standards remain untouched for later proper assertion.
- **Second Amendment:** Eight support only the retained nonincorporation ground and rejection of this state-application theory. None is committed to the Fourth Circuit's categorical collective-right proposition. No coalition is counted for an enrolled-militia standing rule, full federal individual-right rule, a new incorporation framework, or a conclusion that every firearm regulation is valid. Petitioner's modern incorporation argument has been rejected as a basis for changing the inherited disposition here, not omitted as unpresented.
- **Substantive due process:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support the common sufficient conclusion that, assuming entitlement and accepting the pleaded conduct, this arrests-screening policy and legally erroneous denial do not establish the additional constitutional arbitrariness necessary for the presented substantive damages claim. Scalia's and Kennedy/Thomas's source explanations remain distinct from a broad executive-action test; count only the bounded common application. No one joins the Fourth Circuit's general proposition that adequate state correction always defeats a substantive claim.
- **Remand position:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg share removal of the categorical state-cure premise and defendant-favorable factual inference, followed by renewed examination of the alleged affirmative deprivation. This common ground is not a finding of a protected interest, culpability or liability; their respective theories are not votes for immediate damages. If later assembly proposes a more definitive pleading holding, each must be tested against these recorded limits.
- **Entitlement:** No eight-Justice or five-Justice holding adopts Gardner's all-discretion test, resolves every §442 interpretation, or equates power to investigate with power to deny. The affirmers assume the interest; the remanding Justices preserve proper consideration. Sandin's controlling state-constraint rule remains intact and in its own context.
- **Remedy:** An affirmance leaves the personal federal dismissal in place on merits, with no separate new judgment concerning Maryland. The three proposed remands revive only the substantive federal theory and permit ordinary motions and defense assertion. Neither position orders a firearm transfer, an injunction, damages, a fee award or a state-law adjudication.
