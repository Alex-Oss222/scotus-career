**Department of Commerce v. Montana**

**Current rule:** Courts may review the constitutional population challenge to equal proportions; Congress's responsibility to choose a method and the availability of more than one constitutional method do not make that challenge a political question. When allocating whole Representatives within a statutorily fixed House while respecting state boundaries and each State's constitutional minimum, Congress may choose the population-based relative representational equality supplied by equal proportions. Improvement in selected absolute population disparities does not establish invalidity where the proposed change worsens other legitimate measures of equality. This permits equal proportions without uniquely requiring it, validating every population-related formula or creating a percentage tolerance. Intrastate district-equality requirements remain distinct and unchanged.

**Authority by component:**
- Constitutional reviewability: [United States Department of Commerce v. Montana](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_Department_of_Commerce_v_Montana_merits_1992-03-31.md), March 31, 1992, “Congress's apportionment choice remains subject to constitutional review,” Part II.
- Permissible relative-equality method and limits: the same record, “Equal proportions satisfies the presented interstate population challenge,” Part III. Stevens's unanimous opinion controls both components.

**Open question:** The separate Article I, §§2 and 7 challenge to automatic decennial apportionment remains undecided, including its standing, justiciability, merits and relief. This construction does not constitutionalize a House of 435 or decide counting-rule challenges.

**Franklin v. Massachusetts**

**Current rule:** The constitutional count may include federal personnel temporarily overseas whose enduring ties support residence in a particular State. Literal physical presence on census day is unnecessary; attribution without a supportable residence connection is not authorized. Interstate population counting does not import every intrastate district-equality rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Permissible residence connection: [Franklin v. Massachusetts, “Temporarily absent residents may be included in the constitutional count”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1991/records/Franklin_v_Massachusetts_merits_1992-06-26.md), June 26, 1992; Stone's Part III joined by all eight Associates.

**Exceptions and limits:** No dataset is universally required or approved. The record-specific APA explanation and methodology ruling adds no new standard of administrative review or tolerance for unequal districts within a State.

**Current rule:** The Secretary's completed census determination is final agency action under §704 despite the President's later statutory transmission of the resulting apportionment. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit counting entirely to unreviewable discretion: the obligation to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice. General supervision and possible correction do not make this completed decision tentative; the prescribed transmission differs from a statutorily independent presidential policy judgment.

**MCI Telecommunications**

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.
