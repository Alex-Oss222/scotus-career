## Reconciled rule, scope and remedy

For Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, retain Path A: affirm rejection of categorical contractor exclusion and remand the reviewed official-capacity claims. An existing independent contractor may establish a First Amendment violation from termination or nonrenewal because of protected public-concern speech. Connick's content, form and context inquiry and Pickering's balance apply, adjusted to the actual contractual relationship and with appropriate deference to legitimate service interests. Independence, other customers, actual working contacts and responsibility may affect the balance in either direction; none establishes universal contractor superiority or inferiority. Mere offense at criticism is not an operational justification. The claimant must prove protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the government may prove by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent that speech. A merely possible lawful reason is insufficient.

The summary-judgment assumptions do not establish retaliation, injury, the ultimate balance or municipal attribution. The remand requires neither trial nor automatic renewal, reinstatement or a damages award. It preserves personal immunity and McClure's separately affirmed causation disposition, and ordinary entity-liability and remedy requirements. Replacement contracts do not establish no injury. The comparator's discussion of possible later-acquired evidence and mitigation confirms that liability and remedy are distinct; this handoff neither finds such evidence nor creates a new remedial holding absent a developed issue. Initial bidders, every future award, pure affiliation and broader factual-mistake rules remain unadjudicated here. Do not import the historical petition-waiver statement as a newly found simulated preservation fact. Official-capacity claims do not become claims defeated by personal legislative immunity.

For Scalia and Thomas, retain Path B: reverse the reviewed rejection of the categorical exclusion and leave the official-capacity summary judgment in place on nonextension grounds. Their position does not find lawful actual motive or no injury, decide the truth of the allegations, overrule employee-speech precedent, or reopen the unreviewed dispositions. Direct sovereign regulation and separately protected expressive programs remain distinct.
