- **Citation / Docket:** 518 U.S. 37; No. 95-566
- **October Term:** 1995
- **Entry Stage / Event Type:** Granted merits review; decision pending in this simulation.
- **Question Presented:** Whether due process forbids a State from excluding voluntary-intoxication evidence when determining the mental state required for deliberate homicide.
- **Question Granted:** The stated question describes the submitted review scope; no additional question or plenary grant is inferred beyond the identified procedural stage.
- **Legal Area:** Criminal procedure — offense definition, mens rea, and defense evidence
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Supreme Court of Montana reversed Egelhoff’s homicide convictions, holding that the statutory exclusion violated due process by preventing consideration of relevant mens rea evidence.
- **Requested Supreme Court Disposition / Relief:** The party seeking review asks for relief from the adverse judgment identified below; the opposing party seeks its preservation. Any decision must stay within the presented issues and source-authorized remedy.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Egelhoff was heavily intoxicated when two people were killed in a vehicle. Montana law directed the factfinder not to consider voluntary intoxication in deciding the existence of a mental state that is an element. The dispute is whether that rule defines substantive culpability or leaves the element unchanged while barring evidence offered to rebut it.
- **Preservation / Threshold / Vehicle Issues:** Whether the exclusion reflects a State’s authority to define crimes or is an evidentiary rule excluding proof bearing on a retained element is contested.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument March 20, 1996; decision June 13, 1996.

**Victor v. Nebraska**

**Holding:** A reasonable-doubt instruction violates due process when, read with the charge as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that the jury understood it to permit conviction without proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every element. No particular verbal formula is required; if the complete proof standard is constitutionally vitiated, Sullivan requires relief without appellate reweighing of guilt evidence, while the question whether a court must give a definition when none is given remains open.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's shared-standard portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine adopt the standard. Sullivan supplies the existing remedial rule; no defective charge is found by the Court here.

**Holding:** Sandoval's complete charge creates no reasonable likelihood of conviction below the reasonable-doubt standard because its moral-certainty language requires an abiding conviction after consideration of all evidence and its other instructions confine findings to received evidence and exclude sentiment, passion, prejudice and public feeling. References to moral evidence and possible or imaginary doubt are sustained only in that context, not as detached approval of those expressions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Sandoval portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same instruction ruling.

**Holding:** Victor's complete charge creates no reasonable likelihood of conviction on insufficient proof because it requires an abiding conviction after full, fair and impartial consideration of the evidence, restricts doubt to evidence or its absence, and keeps the burden on the State throughout. Its moral-certainty terminology therefore does not detach conviction from proof; this holding concerns the complete instruction and does not make correct boilerplate an automatic cure for contradictory instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Victor evidentiary/abiding-conviction portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; these six Justices directly support this sufficient contextual ground.

**Holding:** In Victor's complete charge, actual and substantial doubt is contrasted with mere possibility, bare imagination and fanciful conjecture; the grave-transaction hesitation explanation further conveys a real doubt rather than an unusually large one. The strong-probabilities sentence is permissible here only because it expressly requires probabilities strong enough to exclude every reasonable doubt and operates with the charge's evidence and abiding-conviction requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's additional Victor contextual portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices support this additional explanation. Ginsburg does not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holdings sustain the two complete charges in context. No national script, isolated-word ban, general approval of moral-certainty language or evidence-strength cure is adopted. Ginsburg's Victor join extends to the six-Justice evidentiary ground, not the additional five-Justice hesitation/probabilities explanation.

**Staples**

**Current rule:** For §5861(d) possession of an unregistered rifle prosecuted as a whole-weapon machinegun under §5845(b), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics making it a machinegun: it shoots, is designed to shoot, or can readily be restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. Knowledge that the weapon is unregistered or that possession is illegal is not required. Ordinary possession of a rifle is not enough to presume knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge of the regulated weapon characteristics: [Staples v. United States — Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1993/records/Staples_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Open question:** The holding does not decide every separately defined machinegun part or combination, every other regulated weapon, or the sufficiency of proof in every conversion setting.

**Ratzlaf**

**Current rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowledge of the financial institution’s reporting duty and a purpose to evade it. Reasonable circumstantial inferences may establish knowledge; knowledge of a section number or having read the Code is unnecessary. If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity on that additional knowledge requirement, lenity independently requires this narrower criminal construction; conflicting history or general purpose cannot enlarge the crime beyond sufficiently clear enacted language.

**United States v. Gaudin**

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.
