**Hess**

**Holding:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

**Puerto Rico Aqueduct**

**Current rule:** A State or asserted arm of a State may immediately appeal under §1291 a conclusive denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity from federal suit when the issue is separate from the merits and the protection would be effectively lost by awaiting final judgment. A dispute over arm-of-State status does not itself require postponement until trial. Appealability establishes neither immunity nor entity status, waiver or abrogation; prospective officer-relief rules retain their separate limits.

**Authority by component:**

- Controlling holding: [Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc. — Controlling holding](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1992/records/puerto-rico-aqueduct-v-metcalf-eddy-merits-1993-01-12.md), 1993-01-12. **Hafer**

**Current rule:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority; sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by the officer personally. The plaintiff must prove the officer's own federal violation under color of law, and applicable personal immunities remain available. The capacity in which the officer is sued, rather than whether the conduct was official, distinguishes this action from a claim against the State or an official-capacity damages claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Personhood, capacity distinction, personal-payment immunity limit, and retained defenses: [Hafer v. Melo — Individual-capacity §1983 coverage of official conduct](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1991/records/Hafer_v_Melo_merits_1991-11-05.md), November 5, 1991; O'Connor's unanimous participating opinion.

**New York v. United States**

**Current rule:** Congress may not require an unwilling State to choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting ownership and possession of their waste with associated liability. Two unauthorized commands do not create a lawful option. State officials' advocacy for the federal statute neither enlarges congressional powers nor estops a noncompacting State from challenging the command. This differs from authorized monetary or access inducements leaving the State free to choose whether and how to govern, where consequences of refusal fall on private generators rather than transferring their responsibilities to the State.

**Authority by component:**
- Compulsory-ownership alternative and officials' assent: [New York v. United States, “5. Compulsory state ownership or regulation”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/87e62b7612c15409039d524fd66f743d0ad75e2a/terms/OT1991/records/New_York_v_United_States_merits_1992-06-19.md), June 19, 1992; O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five), extending Hodel's legislative-compulsion boundary.
- Distinct actual monetary and access inducements: New York, “3. Monetary incentives” and “4. Access incentives,” June 19, 1992; O'Connor Parts III-A and III-B, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author, applying the received spending and commerce doctrines without creating a numerical coercion threshold.

**Exceptions and limits:** Generally applicable regulation of state activity under Garcia and Baker remains distinct and undisturbed. Actual compact obligations, state market conduct, state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law are reserved. The decision establishes no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule or power to direct other States to accept waste.
