## OT1995-064 — Henderson v. United States

**Event:** 1996-05-20 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 654

**Docket(s):** No. 95-232

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome:** Reversed and remanded.
- **Historical Opinion Topology:** Ginsburg delivered an opinion of the historical Court. Consult the official report for part-specific joins and separate writings.
- **Historical Holding / Rule:** The Suits in Admiralty Act’s “forthwith” service instruction, 46 U.S.C. App. §742, has been superseded by Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4. Rule 4’s extendable 120-day period conflicts irreconcilably with that instruction; the instruction is a nonjurisdictional rule of procedure, not a condition of the waiver of sovereign immunity, so under 28 U.S.C. §2072(b) it is of no further force or effect. In actions arising under federal law and commenced within the governing limitations period, the manner and timing of service are generally nonjurisdictional matters of procedure controlled by the Federal Rules.
- **Historical Marks Status:** Only the actual historical component and joining pattern in the official report define its historical precedential scope; no historical component is entering simulated law.

## OT1995-067 — Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.

**Event:** 1996-06-03 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 706

**Docket(s):** No. 95-244

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome:** Affirmed, on grounds different from those given by the Ninth Circuit. The official report (517 U.S. at 706, 710) describes the Ninth Circuit as having ordered the case sent to arbitration; the Ninth Circuit’s own judgment (47 F.3d at 356) only vacated the remand order and remanded for proceedings consistent with its opinion.
- **Historical Opinion Topology:** O’Connor delivered an opinion of the historical Court. Consult the official report for part-specific joins and separate writings.
- **Historical Holding / Rule:** The abstention-based remand was appealable under §1291 as a collateral order, and §1447(d) did not bar review of a remand resting on no §1447(c) ground. Federal courts may dismiss or remand on abstention principles only where the relief sought is equitable or otherwise discretionary; in this damages action *Burford* could at most have supported a stay, not remand. The historical Court rejected the Ninth Circuit’s per se limitation of *Burford* to equitable cases and did not decide whether a stay would have been warranted.
- **Historical Marks Status:** Only the actual historical component and joining pattern in the official report define its historical precedential scope; no historical component is entering simulated law.

**Official source:** [517 U.S. 706](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-517/pdf/USREPORTS-517-706.pdf); [local source PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-517/pdf/USREPORTS-517-706.pdf). Historical same-matter reasoning remains in this section only.

## OT1995-070 — Auciello Iron Works, Inc. v. NLRB

**Event:** 1996-06-03 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 781

**Docket(s):** No. 95-668

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome:** Affirmed.
- **Historical Opinion Topology:** Souter delivered the opinion for a unanimous historical Court; the official report shows no separate writings.
- **Historical Holding / Rule:** The Board reasonably concluded that an employer commits an unfair labor practice when it disavows a collective-bargaining agreement because of a good-faith doubt about the union’s majority status at the time the contract was made, when the doubt arises from facts known to the employer before the union accepted its contract offer; the Board’s bright-line rule serves the Act’s interest in stable bargaining relationships. Accepting for purposes of decision the Board’s conclusion that a contract was formed, the Court declined to address, as not properly before it, Auciello’s suggestion that the contract was invalid ab initio because the Union actually lacked majority support at acceptance (517 U.S. at 784–785 n.2).
- **Historical Marks Status:** Only the actual historical component and joining pattern in the official report define its historical precedential scope; no historical component is entering simulated law.
