## OT1995-035 — Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein

**Event:** 1996-02-27 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 367

**Docket(s):** No. 94-1809

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Direct the lower court to give the Delaware judgment the preclusive effect required by §1738 unless respondents establish a fundamental defect in jurisdiction, notice, representation, or the settlement’s binding operation.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Apply the statutory full-faith-and-credit command before entertaining a collateral merits challenge to the released federal claims.
- **Supporting Authorities:** 28 U.S.C. §1738; *Kremer v. Chemical Construction Corp.*, 456 U.S. 461; *Hansberry v. Lee*, 311 U.S. 32; *Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts*, 472 U.S. 797.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** A final state class judgment releasing claims within exclusive federal jurisdiction receives the preclusive effect required by §1738 when state law would give it that effect, subject to collateral attack where constitutionally deficient notice, a disabling conflict, collusion, or representation tantamount to no representation deprived absent members of a genuine adversarial representative.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Exclusive federal jurisdiction prevents the state court from adjudicating the federal cause on its merits, but it does not prevent parties from settling and releasing that claim through a judgment the State was competent to enter. Section 1738 supplies the finality rule, while *Hansberry* and *Shutts* preserve a constitutional floor for absent members. Ordinary disagreement with litigation strategy, valuation, or settlement terms does not establish the structural failure necessary to avoid the judgment.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not bind an absent class member where jurisdiction, notice, collusion, a disabling conflict, or representative failure made the judgment fundamentally unfair.

## OT1995-036 — Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Hiles

**Event:** 1996-02-27 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 400

**Docket(s):** No. 95-6

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Reverse the automatic-liability ruling and remand consistently with the distinction between ordinary preparation and an established failure of the required coupling function. Expressly preserve existing statutory performance liability.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** The automatic-coupler requirement imposes an absolute duty that equipment perform as the statute requires. It does not make every injury sustained while preparing equipment proof that the equipment violated that duty. An ordinary off-center drawbar, which must pivot during operation, does not alone establish failure of the automatic-coupling function. Hiles’s injury while realigning the drawbar therefore does not support the directed statutory-liability ruling on that premise.

An established failure to perform remains actionable without proof of negligence or identification of a particular mechanical defect. Affolder’s distinction between required performance and equipment not properly set for coupling gives effect to both parts of the enacted duty. Stone does not require every preparatory adjustment to occur without entry between cars or substitute a judicially selected safer technology for the statutory command.

**Remedy and limits:** Reverse the ruling that this ordinary misalignment establishes liability as a matter of law. Preserve any properly presented statutory performance issue and its ordinary causation requirements. The complaint did not plead an independent FELA negligence claim; the disposition does not manufacture one. Neither the injury alone nor an employer’s general appeal to careful operation resolves an actual statutory failure.

## OT1995-038 — Hercules, Inc. v. United States

**Event:** 1996-03-04 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 417

**Docket(s):** No. 94-818

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the entering law and any material record or participation change at the event date; do not treat this position as a rendered decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit’s judgment. This is Stone's approved vote.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The warranty and implied-indemnity claims remain dismissed without creating an alternative equitable reimbursement action.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Warranty of specifications

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the claimed contractual undertaking under the Tucker Act. Do not decide the veterans’ underlying tort claims.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Spearin to the scope of the promise it recognizes, not as a general indemnity doctrine.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The implied warranty that compliance with supplied specifications permits satisfactory performance does not itself promise reimbursement for third-party tort defense and settlement costs. The asserted warranty does not support recovery here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant inference concerns what the purchaser undertook in specifying performance. It does not follow that the purchaser agreed to bear liabilities to strangers to the contract after performance was complete. Extending that promise would create an obligation implied by law rather than identify an agreement actually made. [P1, 422–425; Spearin]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An express indemnity or a demonstrated authorized implied-in-fact undertaking in another contract remains possible. No sovereign immunity from genuine contractual obligations is created.

##### Issue 2: Implied indemnity and compelled production

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach whether the specific circumstances establish an actual indemnity agreement. Do not turn an equitable claim into a Tucker Act contract.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the requirement of an authorized factual undertaking; give the statutory and appropriations context evidentiary and legal effect without declaring all indemnities forbidden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The asserted procurement compulsion and specification control do not establish an authorized implied-in-fact agreement to indemnify these third-party costs. The Defense Production Act’s liability language supplies no separate reimbursement promise.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A statutory command to manufacture can explain why a contractor acted without showing assent to an unlimited reimbursement obligation. The appropriations limits and specific authorized indemnity mechanisms make that inference especially unwarranted here. Equitable appeal cannot enlarge §1491 to obligations implied only in law. This conclusion leaves actual authorized indemnity agreements enforceable on their terms. [P1, 425–430]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on an independent taking, a different express indemnity, or the merits of veterans’ tort claims is necessary.
