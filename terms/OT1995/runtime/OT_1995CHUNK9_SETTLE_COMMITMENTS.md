## Independent disposition and operative level

The individual commitments select **vacatur of the Sixth Circuit's judgment and remand**, with the reviewed free-speech summary judgment set aside. Further proceedings may address genuine curricular reasons, actual causation, a properly supported same-decision defense and other actually available defenses and remedies. The result does not compel a trial on every issue or preclude a legally sufficient remaining ground under ordinary procedure.

The sufficient common ground is bounded: a teacher may impose genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements, but may not obtain summary judgment merely by identifying a possible pedagogical justification when the supported record permits a reasonable inference that the work was rejected because of its religious perspective rather than a legitimate application of those requirements. A sincere belief that law categorically forbids religious issues in class is not itself a legitimate educational rule. A student remains obliged to satisfy genuine assignment requirements; religious conviction supplies no exemption from them.

This does not impose viewpoint neutrality on every curricular selection or forbid schools to define the knowledge, method or perspective a particular lesson teaches. Objective study may be distinguished from personal testimony or devotional advocacy. The error is treating that distinction as established here despite the actual explanations and competing evidence, or treating topic-control authority as categorical immunity from the speech claim. The absence of publication narrows the available attribution justification but does not eliminate instructional authority.

The comparator evidence supports examination rather than a final equivalence finding. The district describes reincarnation, spiritualism and occult outlines containing religious figures, teachings and specific religious headings, while characterizing religion as background rather than focus. The petition disputes the significance of that distinction. The comparison is richer than topic names alone, but the recovered set does not establish identical student beliefs, outlines, source choices, timing or procedural compliance. The district's distinction and the concurrence's assessment are challenged summary-judgment reasoning, not trial findings. Likewise, the majority's no-motive-dispute conclusion cannot itself resolve the factual inference under review.

The best affirmance route relies on genuine curricular topic control and the admitted lack of advance approval, rather than endorsing all six explanations. It fails to sustain summary judgment here because the evidence supports a material dispute about the real academic versus religious basis, and the offered alternative, original-choice answer and mixed account prevent treating the procedural reason as a conclusively established same-decision defense. No personal hostility finding is necessary to recognize that dispute. A teacher can act without animus yet rely on an impermissible categorical exclusion; conversely, an unconvincing explanation alone does not establish final liability.
