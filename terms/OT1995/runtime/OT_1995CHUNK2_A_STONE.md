## OT1995-012 — Klinger v. Department of Corrections +

**Event:** 1995-12-04 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Expressly assigned simulated merits review

**Citation:** 31 F.3d 727

**Docket(s):** Supreme Court docket not supplied; Eighth Circuit No. 93-2928

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Reverse the categorical rejection of comparability between the women's and men's prisons and remand the preserved program claims under ordinary equal-protection doctrine. Do not award universal program parity or constitutionalize identical prison administration.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** A sex classification does not become immune from review because the State houses women and men separately. Comparability depends on the opportunity being withheld, not whether every feature of two institutions is identical. Smaller population, shorter sentences or security needs may explain particular educational, vocational or recreational choices; they cannot answer every challenge at once. J.E.B. and Hogan require a genuine important objective and a substantial relationship for an actual sex classification. For a facially neutral decision, the required discriminatory purpose remains necessary. Stone applies those established rules without adding a novel prison-comparison test or presuming that expenditure totals determine equality.

**Record and relief:** This is a §1292(b) interlocutory appeal. The men's facility's much larger population, longer sentences and different security distribution matter when tied to the challenged service; they do not categorically make all women unlike all men. Restore the claims improperly foreclosed by wholesale noncomparability so the district court can decide the actual classifications and explanations. Preserve the existing pleading scope and the independent ruling denying a broad late amendment after extensive discovery unless a preserved error in that ruling is shown. No liability finding or systemwide injunction follows merely from this legal correction.

**Limit:** Apply existing sex-equality doctrine without a categorical prison-program exemption, while preserving legitimate operational differences.

* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The remand remains undisturbed and the action proceeds in state court. No separate Supreme Court ruling on the underlying liability or removal merits is entered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Interaction of the two remand-review statutes

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide appellate jurisdiction first. Do not decide the rent claim, the bankruptcy merits, or whether the district court’s remand analysis was correct.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutes cumulatively where their conditions overlap, without extending §1447(d) to remands outside its source-defined scope.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A remand on a ground specified in §1447(c) is unreviewable under §1447(d) even when the case was removed under §1452 as well as ordinary removal authority. The Sixth Circuit correctly dismissed the attempted appeal.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bankruptcy removal changes which cases can be removed; it does not silently repeal the review bar for a covered remand ground. The equitable-remand provision performs additional work and need not be exclusive to do so. Because the grounds here fall within the ordinary bar, their substantive correctness cannot be reached through this appeal. [P1, 128–129]
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The decision does not declare every bankruptcy remand unreviewable or eliminate authorized review of orders falling outside the statutory bars.

## OT1995-014 — Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States

**Event:** 1995-12-06 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 137

**Docket(s):** Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse both §924(c) judgments.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Vacate the firearm convictions and remand for ordinary proceedings, including any legally available charge based on carriage or possession that was properly preserved.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Reach the enacted meaning of “use” and leave unpresented “carry,” possession, charging, and retrial questions to their ordinary channels.
- **Supporting Authorities:** 18 U.S.C. §924(c)(1); *Smith v. United States*, 508 U.S. 223 (the ordinary active meaning of “use,” including barter of a firearm for drugs); simulated *Staples v. United States*, 511 U.S. 600, and *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135, for source-bound criminal construction.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** A defendant “uses” a firearm under §924(c)(1) only by actively employing it as an operative instrument of the predicate offense; barter is use under *Smith v. United States*, while mere storage, possession, proximity, or accessibility is not use.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Congress selected an active verb and separately legislated other firearm relationships. *Smith* supplies an authoritative application because exchanging the firearm actively employs it as an item of barter; that holding does not convert passive possession into use. The statutory text may cover other conduct that actively brings a firearm into the offense, but the present records establish only storage and accessibility.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not define “carry,” disturb possession offenses, require discharge, or create a closed judicial list of qualifying conduct.
