### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as revised on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

**Stone’s disposition:** Affirm supported relief and unaffected findings in the reviewed university-desegregation decree, in its operative form as amended before this event. Vacate the portion of the judgment denying additional library relief, 879 F. Supp. at 1458, because its analysis makes white-student attraction a prerequisite to repairing an otherwise established educational injury, without adequately resolving the mission justification. Remand the library issue for reconsideration under the stated principle, using the existing findings and crediting the library improvements already underway; any additional library relief depends on what injury persists and what remedy addresses it. Leave the remaining supported relief undisturbed. Do not reverse every adverse ruling or declare every disparity unlawful.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** When an educational injury caused by the State’s segregated system remains unremedied, the absence of predicted increases in white enrollment cannot by itself justify refusing necessary, practicable, educationally sound repair. Students’ educational opportunities matter independently of their usefulness in attracting other students. Fordice requires dismantlement of traceable policies with continuing segregative effects and places the dismantlement burden on the State. Actual simulated Jenkins recognizes necessary educational repair as an independently sufficient remedial ground; improvement need not be justified solely by voluntary racial attraction. Applying that principle in this university setting remains tied to the established violation and educational injury, not to a general guarantee of educational quality or equal institutional budgets. Milliken v. Bradley (Milliken II), 433 U.S. 267, which simulated Jenkins and Fordice apply, sustains educational remedies beyond pupil assignment where the record connects them to the violation. Extending it to university library resources develops that doctrine; Milliken II did not decide the question.

**Equipment and libraries:** The 1995 findings identify unequal historical investment and continuing equipment and library conditions, and connect those policies to inherited missions to the stated extent. Preserve supported findings. Determine whether the challenged deficiency perpetuates an educational injury attributable to the unconstitutional system and whether the proposed expenditure would repair it practicably and consistently with sound education. A racial comparison of budgets alone does not answer that question. Where a laboratory deficiency has that established relationship, a necessary improvement may qualify as relief even if enrollment remains predominantly Black. This example illustrates the rule; it does not invent an additional laboratory finding in this record.

**Library ruling:** The district court declined to enlarge the historically Black institutions’ libraries on two grounds. It attributed the differences in volume holdings to historical mission assignments and found racial comparison across broadly disparate missions of little use; and, absent proof that the holdings precluded those institutions as viable choices for white students, it found enlargement beyond the level consistent with their missions not educationally sound. It also noted the library expansions underway at Jackson State and Alcorn. 879 F. Supp. at 1456–58. By conditioning enlargement on proof about white students’ choices, the second ground makes white-student attraction a prerequisite to repairing an educational injury the opinion otherwise connects to the dual system; under the stated principle, it cannot by itself support the denial. The first ground does not resolve the question on this record. Different missions can justify different collections, but an inherited mission restriction cannot conclusively justify limited resources if the restriction itself remains an unlawful feature of the former dual system. The same opinion found mission policies traceable to de jure segregation and still fostering separation, and library allocations traceable to the degree they follow those missions, 879 F. Supp. at 1477; simulated Fordice requires examination of inherited mission assignments with their combined segregative effects and educational justifications. The library ruling did not decide whether the mission limit it applied is justified apart from that history, what library resources the institutions’ missions require, including the program changes ordered in decree paragraphs 3–10, or whether the expansions underway repair the holdings and space conditions the opinion found. Had the findings established that the libraries adequately support lawful missions and that the improvements underway remedy any traceable injury, the principle would justify no additional relief; the analysis leaves both points unresolved.

**Programs and missions:** Preserve the supported Jackson State and Alcorn improvements reflected in decree paragraphs 3–10. Examine challenged mission limits and program arrangements for their continuation of the established violation. Historically Black institutions are not themselves the constitutional wrong. Equal opportunity does not require identical programs at every institution or justify discarding an effective educational institution merely because of its enrollment. Any separately challenged racial eligibility term retains its own governing equality requirements; support for institutional repair does not automatically decide such a term.

**Admissions and scholarships:** Apply Fordice directly to the identified barriers and their interaction, using the operative admissions provisions, including the fourth full-admission alternative the February 16, 1996 order made equally applicable to all institutions, rather than assuming the original March 1995 formulation remained unchanged. The State’s dismantlement burden, supported findings, educational justifications and practicable alternatives govern. AY-2 supplies no independent holding that a particular ACT threshold or scholarship criterion is unlawful. A policy with no established connection to the violation is not condemned merely because outcomes differ by race. Existing admissions reform is not discarded solely to substitute a preferred admissions formula.

**Partial affirmance and remand:** Preserve supported institutional improvements, including the supported Jackson State and Alcorn improvements reflected in decree paragraphs 3–10, effective admissions relief and unaffected findings, including the library and equipment findings at 879 F. Supp. at 1456–57 and 1477. The vacatur reaches only the portion of the judgment denying additional library relief. On remand, the court reconsiders additional library relief under the stated principle, using the existing findings and crediting the Jackson State and Alcorn expansions already underway: whether a library condition attributable to the dual system remains unremedied; what library resources each affected institution needs to carry out its legally justified mission, including the program changes already ordered in decree paragraphs 3–10; and what practicable, educationally sound repair, if any, remains necessary. The remand does not reopen the decree’s mission, program or admissions provisions or the equipment determinations, and does not require every university to acquire an equally large collection. Each challenged ruling needs a supported basis for relief, while connected policies may be evaluated together. The preparation does not assume any other denial rested on the enrollment premise, and the remand creates no new universal proof protocol. Preserve paragraph 12’s consideration of educationally sound consolidation alternatives without ordering a merger or closure. No automatic funding parity, fixed appropriation or withdrawal of supervision follows. Freeman and actual Jenkins govern any properly presented release question within their distinct scope.
