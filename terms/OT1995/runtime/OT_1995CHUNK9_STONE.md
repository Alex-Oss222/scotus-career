**Stone’s disposition:** In No. 95-345, sustain Ursery’s double-jeopardy objection and affirm the Sixth Circuit’s reversal of his criminal conviction. The completed punitive forfeiture settlement and the later criminal trial concern the same offense in separate proceedings. In No. 95-346, vacate the blanket Double Jeopardy bar and remand: established unlawful proceeds remain recoverable, but not every disputed asset may be presumed to constitute proceeds.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Recovery of established unlawful proceeds removes the gain of the offense. This offense-conditioned extraction from Ursery’s legitimate property instead imposes punishment on its owner. A civil label and an in rem caption do not determine constitutional character. The retained Halper rule and simulated Kurth Ranch require examination of the sanction’s actual operation; Austin’s excessive-fines coverage does not itself decide Double Jeopardy punishment. Stone reaches that conclusion here from the home-based exaction’s punitive operation, not from a rule that every civil forfeiture is punishment.

**Successive jeopardy and the same offense:** The Government obtained $13,250 to settle its forfeiture claim against Ursery’s home before the criminal trial. The indictment preceded completion of the settlement, but filing an indictment does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy, and overlapping commencement does not combine two independent proceedings. Stone treats the completed punitive settlement as the first jeopardy and the later trial as the second; the settlement resolved the property claim rather than consent to a second punishment for the same offense.

The forfeiture under §881(a)(7), as applied here, incorporated the marijuana offense as the predicate for taking the owner’s property. The later manufacturing charge prosecuted that predicate offense again. The additional property nexus does not make the incorporated drug offense distinct from itself. Under the governing Blockburger necessary-offense analysis and simulated Dixon’s preservation of Grady’s essential-conduct protection, this is a repeated prosecution of the underlying offense, not merely reuse of evidence or a common transaction. The first sanction’s punitive character, the separate proceedings and this offense identity together support the objection. None alone substitutes for the others.

**Companion remedy:** The companion complaint invokes proceeds and money-laundering forfeiture provisions covering different categories of property. Its caption and statutory labels do not establish the character of every disputed asset. Remove the categorical bar and permit recovery of assets established to be unlawful proceeds. For other contested property, determine the statutory basis and apply the appropriate punishment and successive-jeopardy rules on the record. No automatic recovery of every asset, blanket immunity for legitimate property or mandatory new accounting procedure follows.

**Related ownership principle:** Bennis concerns due-process protection for an innocent owner who satisfies the stated knowledge, participation and reasonable-precautions conditions. This case concerns successive punishment of the person responsible for the offense. The ownership protection does not itself answer the Double Jeopardy question. Stone’s Bennis position requires protection or restoration of Tina’s actual interest if its conditions are established, while leaving the husband’s interest subject to lawful forfeiture.
