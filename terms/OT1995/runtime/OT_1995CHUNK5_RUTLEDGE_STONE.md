## OT1995-049 — Rutledge v. United States

**Event:** 1996-03-27 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 292

**Docket(s):** No. 94-8769

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse in part and remand with instructions to vacate the §846 conspiracy conviction and its special assessment.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Leave the CCE conviction and sentence undisturbed; vacate the included conspiracy judgment and its separate assessment.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Compare the statutory elements and the offenses as charged and tried under *Blockburger* and *Ball* before considering any broader cumulative-punishment argument.
- **Supporting Authorities:** U.S. Const. amend. V; 21 U.S.C. §§846 and 848; *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299; *Ball v. United States*, 470 U.S. 856.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** When the concerted-action component of the CCE offense as charged and tried necessarily establishes every element of the §846 conspiracy, conspiracy is a lesser included offense and the two convictions may not both remain.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** The CCE verdict here necessarily incorporated the same agreement and concerted conduct supporting the conspiracy count. Double jeopardy protects against two judgments as well as additional imprisonment, and concurrent sentences do not eliminate the special assessment, stigma, or other collateral consequences of the second conviction. *Ball* therefore requires vacatur of the included judgment.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding is confined to the elements and trial structure presented and does not bar separate convictions for distinct conspiracies or offenses containing an element not necessarily included in the CCE count.
