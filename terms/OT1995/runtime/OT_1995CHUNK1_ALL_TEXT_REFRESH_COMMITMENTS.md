## Result and exact change in the factual premise

**All eight original component dispositions and sufficient legal grounds are retained after independent reconsideration.** The statutory descriptions are corrected, and the exception comparison now expressly addresses the complete bar on **both penalties and civil remedies under FACE**, including civil injunctive relief, when the actor is the minor's parent or legal guardian and the described activities are directed exclusively at that minor. No Justice may join a sentence suggesting civil FACE relief remains available for that excepted conduct. No Justice relies on 'grounds' or worship premises as additional words in §248(e)(1).

This is not a claim that the original shorthand was adequate or that civil remedial exclusion was already expressly analyzed. The original 'any penalties' description omitted a material statutory protection. On the corrected premise, the strongest underinclusion objection is more substantial: Congress withholds even FACE preventive injunctive relief for the qualifying parent/minor conduct while supplying it against the plaintiffs' alleged outsider obstruction. The fresh comparison below explains why that fact does not change the recorded judgments. The omission is corrected rather than minimized as a punctuation or formatting defect.

The corrected facility definition retains hospitals, clinics, physicians' offices and other facilities providing reproductive health services, **including the building or structure in which the facility is located**. It adds no grounds and does not incorporate places of worship by that definition. The Act separately protects passage to/from reproductive-service facilities and places of worship, with separate worship branches not presented here. Neither the earlier erroneous description nor its correction supplies a geographic buffer or an adjudication of outdoor conduct.

## Component-by-component reassessment

**A1 — Commerce and Tenth Amendment; eight retain the common ground.** The regulated class still directly concerns provision and receipt of reproductive medical services, their service facilities, and targeted force, threats, obstructive interference and destruction. The actual definition is tied to service provision; the theory never needed all surrounding grounds or a worship building to be treated as an economic facility. Lopez/Harris's bounded economic-class analysis therefore survives, with Robertson used only in its actual enterprise setting. Congress's complete remedial exception for a defined parent/minor relationship does not convert the remaining regulated activity into a remote productivity chain. This is no new general rule that exceptions never matter to commerce; the particular corrected definition and exception leave the sufficient direct service-market relationship intact. No individual commerce element is invented, no case is decided under the unnecessary alternative persons/things theory, and §5 remains reserved. Direct regulation of private acts still compels no state legislative or administrative program.

**A2 — Speech classification and fit; eight retain intermediate-scrutiny rejection of the presented facial claim.** The act/harm and both service-related motive branches remain as enacted; no protected message or religious belief is required for liability. The complete parent/minor exception is not ignored. Its eligibility turns on a specific relationship and exclusive target, not pro- or antiabortion viewpoint, secular belief or religious belief. It does not confer a comparable exemption on labor, environmental or other outsider obstruction directed at service-seekers generally. Its full civil scope weakens any claim that FACE pursues access by identical remedies in every relationship, but that universal claim was not the sufficient ground. The conduct-based classification and direct fit to the covered obstruction remain, under O'Brien/Ward rather than automatic use of Madsen's injunction test. Mitchell does not make every peaceful expressive obstruction unprotected; Turner/R.A.V. still require actual scrutiny of selection and fit. Section (d)(1)'s speech protection is a real limitation, not the sole answer.

The absence of the word 'grounds' does not defeat this fit: §248(e)(4) expressly addresses impassable or unreasonably difficult/hazardous ingress, egress and passage. The Court supplies no new radius, invitation requirement, property-line prohibition, compelled silence or finding that all outdoor advocacy is obstruction. Nonobstructive prayer, counseling, signs, leafleting and willing conversation remain distinct. Section (d)(2)'s separate outside-facility protection remains; it is neither erased nor turned into a blanket outdoor-conduct exemption. Worship branches remain outside the question presented.

**A3 — Vagueness and overbreadth; eight retain the bounded rejection.** The actual movement, reasonable bodily-harm apprehension, passage, intent and motive terms remain operative. The correctly narrower description of facility does not eliminate the separate passage definition, and no added geographical term supplies the ascertainable boundary. The full parent/minor exception excludes qualifying conduct from FACE remedies rather than enlarging prohibited protected expression. Its exact exclusive-target condition must be stated; no mixed-target or incidental-effect dispute is resolved from its shorthand. Abstract belief, ideological offense, protected persuasion and harsh political hyperbole are not converted into liability. No new universal true-threat mental-state rule or savings-clause-only rationale is adopted.

**A4 — Private statutory damages; eight retain only the categorical holding, with the corrected threshold exclusion express.** An otherwise eligible plaintiff may elect the statutory sum on the enacted conditions; the election is not mandatory and replaces actual compensatory damages, not every form of relief. But no FACE private damages, statutory sum, injunction or other civil remedy may be imposed for conduct actually within the parent/legal-guardian exception. The complete exception therefore qualifies the availability of this remedy at the outset. It does not undermine the abstract proposition that Congress may authorize violation-based damages without proof of the same actual loss for nonexcepted conduct. Claiborne's attribution and protected-activity limits remain; no award, amount reasonableness, violation count, punitive measure, private enforcement fact or qualifying-parent finding is made. A public formulation omitting the civil-remedy bar would exceed the refreshed join.

**A5 — Constitutional religion; the existing scope difference is retained.** All eight reject the presented facial-targeting claim. Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also reject the constitutional-exemption claim under the governing Smith/Lukumi rule; O'Connor and Souter reserve that broader question and join no categorical approval of Smith's full account. The complete parent/minor exception applies to religious and secular parents/guardians alike on its conditions; it is not a general secular-blockade permission. Lukumi requires scrutiny of comparable harm, not mere reliance on a neutral caption. The strongest contrary path now emphasizes identical denial of a minor's access with no FACE civil prevention, not merely lesser criminal exposure. The retained answer is the defined, exclusively parent/guardian-to-that-minor relation, which is materially different from collective outsiders directing obstruction at other service-seekers. As in the distinct shared-home comparison in Swanner's separate constitutional analysis, the limited relationship is not simply ignored because the same broad interest can be invoked.

This comparison does **not** hold all parent/minor conduct harmless, validate abuse, decide the constitutional allocation of parental power, or derive a universal parental veto. Nor is the possibility of other state/local remedies a substitute for FACE enforcement or proof that equivalent relief actually exists in a given case. Section (d)(3)'s nonpreemption confirms only that this is a federal remedial exception, not a grant of general immunity. The six reject the asserted constitutional exemption on the pleaded frame, with selective-enforcement and materially different comparable-exception applications reserved; the two retain their broader constitutional reservation.

**A6 — RFRA; five remand and three affirm on the original distinct grounds, reconsidered using the complete exception.** Clearwater and Swanner govern the separate burden-and-justification inquiry. A claimant must establish substantial burden; the government must produce evidence and persuade that the application to the person serves a compelling interest by the least restrictive means. Constitutional neutrality, intermediate scrutiny and medical-market participation cannot substitute for that showing. The original assumed-substantial-burden pleading posture remains; neither correction establishes sincerity, an exact proposed physical method, a particular statutory exception, a final burden or a completed evidentiary record.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas retain vacatur of the RFRA prospective-enforcement dismissal and remand. The complete civil exception strengthens the need to confront Congress's actual enforcement distinction rather than present access as an interest pursued without qualification. They require the actual person, threatened conduct and legally material comparison or alternative to be examined under Swanner, including the full exception if comparable. They do not declare every outsider blockade legally comparable to a parent/guardian's exclusively directed act, require a new evidentiary hearing in every case, or predetermine a religious exemption. The government may demonstrate that a proposed accommodation transfers the exact harm to another service-seeker. The corrected definition does not add a geographic accommodation, erase an actual access burden, or require patients to use another facility.

Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer retain affirmance on the bounded assumed-burden application. Their sufficient ground is the actual request, on this pleading assumption, for immunity to engage in the statutory physical obstruction of other persons' access. The complete remedial exception is now weighed directly. They still distinguish exclusively parent/guardian-to-that-minor conduct from the plaintiffs' asserted outsider obstruction: it grants no general right to direct a blockade at other patients/providers or an entrance for their access, and applies without religious classification. The relation, not supposed surviving FACE civil relief against a parent, supports the distinction. Thus the exception defeats any assertion of universal federal access enforcement, but does not supply the asserted exemption from protecting other service-seekers against these outsiders' defined obstruction.

The three do not substitute their Swanner dissent for the governing law. They apply Swanner's person/conduct inquiry on the stated pleading assumption and regard the direct, unavoidable access loss under the requested immunity as sufficient to justify this application. Referral, advance notice or requiring another route/facility does not preserve the same person's protected access in that encounter. The government retains production and persuasion; the conclusion does not place a universal burden on claimants to prove an alternative. A materially different nonobstructive practice, actual threatened enforcement against protected expression, or a supported application falling within the parental exception remains outside this assumed outsider-blockade determination. The correction supplies no finding that a plaintiff actually qualifies or does not qualify as a parent/guardian in a distinct proposed encounter.
