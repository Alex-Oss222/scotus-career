### Entering law and verified source expansion

Article I, §4 grants Congress authority to make or alter the times, places and manner of congressional elections. Article I, §2 and the Seventeenth Amendment retain their distinct voter-qualification provisions. Smiley, 285 U.S. 355, 366–367 (1932), includes registration and the duties of election officers within the manner power. Siebold, 100 U.S. 371, 383–393 (1880), permits partial congressional alteration, federal enforcement of state officers' election duties and the resulting shared administration; federal regulation does not require replacement of the entire state apparatus. Its express limitation to federal elections remains significant.

**“Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations,” effective New York, June 19, 1992**, bars the particular compulsory waste-regulation/take-title choice under ordinary commerce authority. Its allocation and accountability rationale must be addressed; its reservation of other constitutional settings prevents treating it as an already-decided answer to election administration. **“Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions,” effective Term Limits, May 22, 1995**, distinguishes genuine manner regulation from substantive congressional qualifications. It neither directly adjudicates voter qualifications nor automatically answers assignment of work to a particular state agency.

Presidential elections require a separate ground. Article I, §4 does not cover them by its text. Burroughs, 290 U.S. 534, 544–548 (1934), recognizes national power to safeguard presidential elections while preserving state authority to select the manner of appointing electors. Its actual holding concerns corrupt-practices regulation of political committees; applying its protective principle to the NVRA requires explanation and is not merely quoting a supposed rule that Article II and Article I powers are coextensive.

The statute is the complete 1993 enactment, Pub. L. 103-31, 107 Stat. 77–89, with the incorporated contemporary federal-office definition. Section 2's enacted findings and purposes address discriminatory/unfair registration, eligible participation, process integrity and accurate rolls. They are legislative findings, not adjudicated facts about particular California conduct. Sections 4–7 prescribe federal registration through motor-vehicle applications, mail and designated agencies; §§5(c), 8 and 9 preserve eligibility assessment while regulating its procedures; §10 requires a coordinating state official. Section 11(a) independently authorizes the Attorney General's declaratory/injunctive action. Its authority is not conditioned on private §11(b) notice. California's August 1994 executive direction conditioned implementation on federal funding; the resulting noncompliance, not an established $18 million cost, supplies the present enforcement controversy.
