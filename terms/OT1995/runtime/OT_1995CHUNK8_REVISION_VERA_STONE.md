### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as revised on October 2, 2026; the merits position below approved by the user on October 7, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** The primary position is VER-1: Governmental use of racial information to draw a shared territorial constituency does not, standing alone, establish concrete personal injury, even when a voter objects to assignment to that constituency. To the extent Miller recognized standing on that basis alone, it is overruled. Concrete unequal treatment remains an injury without requiring additional financial loss, electoral defeat or proof that the underlying constitutional claim will succeed. Applied to the reconstructed judgment, vacate the judgment invalidating Districts 18, 29 and 30 and the dependent redrawing relief, including the September 20, 1994 injunction against conducting the 1996 congressional elections under the Plan C657 districts, and remand with instructions to dismiss the design-only claims without prejudice for lack of Article III standing. Dismissal of the entire action is appropriate only if those are all the remaining claims. The disposition does not adjudicate the map’s constitutionality on the merits. The Miller-compatible approach below is the fallback; it does not displace the primary position.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Drawing a territorial constituency joins residents in a common political unit. Considering racial population data in making that decision does not necessarily confer different rights, opportunities or civic standing on any person within it. Residence establishes a relationship to the district; it does not by itself establish injury. The plaintiff must identify the personal treatment or impairment being challenged beyond disagreement with the government’s consideration of race. Stone accepts well-pleaded concrete facts at the appropriate litigation stage and applies ordinary proof requirements where facts must be established. Standing does not require proving the underlying violation.

The allegation that the State subjected the plaintiff to a racial assignment requires an answer. Stone’s proposed distinction is that a shared constituency does not, merely through its design, legally assign constituents different political identities or make the representative responsible only to one race. An inference of inferior status or unequal representation needs the alleged governmental operation that supports it; racial composition alone does not establish that the representative will neglect other constituents. This reasoning rejects neither nonmaterial equality injuries nor the possibility that geographical arrangements can impose personal unequal treatment. A plaintiff suffering actual unequal treatment need not identify a separate financial loss, electoral defeat or second governmental act. Unequal opportunity itself may be injury without proof of losing the ultimate benefit.

**Limited change to Miller:** In Part I-A of simulated Miller, for nine Justices, the Court held that voters personally subjected to the challenged electoral assignment may litigate their asserted unequal treatment when their own exposure, causation and redressability are established, even though their constitutional theory ultimately fails. The four Eleventh District plaintiffs there alleged that their assignment itself denied equality, and the completed Miller record supplies no campaign injury, constituent-service denial or other personal injury independent of that assignment. The five resident plaintiffs here allege the same kind of injury, so the absence of dilution or other personal inequality does not distinguish Miller. VER-1 therefore overrules Miller’s threshold holding to the extent it recognized standing on personal assignment to a race-conscious territorial constituency alone. This is an express change, not an unresolved question or an application already required by Miller. The completed Miller record remains unchanged.

Stone’s asserted legal reason is that Miller drew the wrong inference from personal exposure: exposure identifies the person affected by the governmental design, but does not automatically establish the concrete injury Article III separately requires. That argument must stand on its account of injury, not on the failure of the design-only merits theory. Miller is not internally contradictory merely because it recognizes standing and rejects the merits, and Stone does not assert intervening law, changed facts or demonstrated unworkability that the record does not supply. Miller’s substantive equal-membership and voluntary-inclusion holdings remain intact.

The prior access to a judicial forum carries weight under Stone’s precedent principles. Closing that route is a real cost, not an interest erased because the claim may lose. Stone concludes that the asserted Article III error justifies the limited correction despite that cost; fewer lawsuits or a preferred policy result would not suffice. Ordinary opportunities to identify an independently sufficient injury and the dismissal’s nonmerits character remain, but neither pretends that forum-access reliance is unaffected.

**Preserved claims and sources:** Unequal electoral treatment, exclusion, dilution, impaired competitive opportunity, inferior civic status or another concrete personal injury may establish standing under the applicable source without additional financial loss or electoral defeat. These examples are not exhaustive. Add no new constitutional prerequisite to statutory Voting Rights Act claims and preserve their existing elements, enforcement routes and ordinary standing requirements. Apply the same injury principle regardless of race. Allen’s rejection of abstract stigma does not itself settle a personally asserted assignment injury. Warth preserves the distinction between standing and success; Bell’s narrow insubstantial-federal-question doctrine is not a substitute ground for answering this Article III question.

**Fallback under unrevised Miller:** If Miller’s threshold holding remains controlling, reject a genuinely abstract objection lacking personal exposure and recognize standing for allegations within its personal-assignment holding: the five resident plaintiffs’ assignment allegations fall within it, while Chen, who alleges no assignment to Districts 18, 29 or 30, supplies no independent injury concerning them. Reject unsupported predictions of discriminatory representation where they are the only asserted injury, but do not eliminate an independent sufficient basis. Apply claimant-specific exposure and ordinary evidentiary rules only where the record warrants them; invent no expert-report, alternative-map or electoral-loss prerequisite. The fixed review concerns standing. Resolve that question and return remaining issues for the proceedings the actual judgment requires; decide no ungranted merits controversy.

**Merits once standing is recognized (approved by the user on October 7, 2026):** If the Court recognizes standing, Stone reaches the merits of the retained appeals and votes to reverse. Applying simulated Shaw and Miller, race-conscious district design, including predominant racial consideration that subordinates ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself establish unconstitutional unequal treatment. Reject the invalidation of Districts 18, 29 and 30 on that basis and reverse the dependent relief, including the September 20, 1994 injunction against conducting the 1996 congressional elections under the Plan C657 districts. Any independently cognizable deprivation, such as exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation or inferior civic status, retains its own elements and is not decided by this reversal.
