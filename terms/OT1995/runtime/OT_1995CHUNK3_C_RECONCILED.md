## Commissioner v. Lundy — January 17, 1996

### Historical comparison and effect on the provisional commitments

Historically, the Court reversed 7–2. Among these eight Associates, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer supported reversal; Stevens and Thomas supported affirmance. The full opinions, rather than the tally alone, are important. The majority used one hypothetical claim date both to choose the §6511(b)(2) window and to measure it. Thomas read §6512 as preserving an otherwise effective actual claim as well as supplying a protective hypothetical claim; Stevens joined that construction and emphasized the protective function of the provision. Neither dissent relied on free-standing equitable power to disregard an admitted statutory bar.

The provisional handoff instead placed Thomas in L1 and Breyer in L2. Further review does not reveal a concrete altered legal, factual, procedural, remedial or prior-position premise that supports either departure. Williams's actual simulated express-waiver rule retains §6511's applicable timing conditions, and its alternative taxpayer-definition rule concerns an eligible lien-backed payer, not the operation of the notice-date hypothetical. Reich protects a held-out state postpayment remedy, not a general freedom to change federal lookback limits. Bufferd identifies the return starting a different assessment period and does not resolve the effect of a later real refund claim under §6512. Their adoption in this timeline leaves this specific statutory disagreement open.

Thomas's provisional inference from Bufferd and his Williams position understates the statutory choice: giving effect to a real return that actually exists is not necessarily an equitable enlargement of consent, and the text does not expressly direct the court to disregard every later real claim. His comparator dissent is strong Justice-specific evidence on the unchanged issue. Breyer's general Williams/Reich remedial reasoning is insufficient to overcome his direct historical acceptance of the same hypothetical-claim mechanism where no changed premise is established. These two provisional rows are therefore revised after substantive source review, rather than retained merely to preserve independent-model output. The other six result commitments are retained.

### Reconciled rules and compatibility envelopes

**L1 — six Associates, reversal.** For the submitted §6512(b)(3)(B) refund question, use a hypothetical claim filed on the deficiency-notice date to select and measure the applicable §6511(b)(2) payment window. Because no return had been filed on September 26, 1990, that hypothetical claim was not within the return-based three-year period; the otherwise-applicable two-year window excludes withholding treated as paid on April 15, 1988. The actual December return does not retroactively supply a return at the date Congress selected. No actual refund claim is independently required merely to use the statutory hypothetical.

The strongest contrary construction preserves the real later claim, but L1 rejects separating the selected period from the statutory hypothetical's date. The hypothetical is a claim, not a claim plus a deemed return. The statutory provisions themselves contemplate a claim without a return. The practical advantage of a district-court route, assumed for argument, cannot change the enacted Tax Court mechanism; the Court need not decide every district-court filing question. Williams and Dalm retain their distinct claim-and-timing requirements without deciding this statutory choice in advance.

**L2R — two Associates, affirmance on a bounded real-or-hypothetical-claim construction.** Preserve a genuinely effective actual refund claim filed with the later return within three years of payment; treat §6512(b)(3)(B)'s hypothetical claim as an additional protective mechanism, not an instruction to erase that real claim. The incorporated lookback provisions and the actual return permit the refund inquiry here without an equitable exception. This does not replace the hypothetical with a universal actual-filing requirement: a timely filer who discovers an overpayment later may still rely on the hypothetical. It does not decide the broader case of a return filed more than three years after payment, manufacture an earlier return, or announce that every delinquent filer receives three years measured from notice.

L2R is the limited construction within the previously modeled preserved-window alternative that these two Justices support after reconciliation. It avoids attributing to them either an unqualified endorsement of every implication of the Fourth Circuit's wording or the broader universal three-year construction advanced separately by the taxpayer. Their objection is that Congress supplied a protective assumption without making it the exclusive mechanism; the majority's objection remains that the express incorporated hypothetical governs the whole calculation. Neither ground is subsumed in the other, and the two may not be combined into a broader majority rule.

**Shared limits.** The holding concerns the presented withholding and ordinary §6511(b)(2) alternatives. Section 6512(b)(3)(A) separately concerns payments after notice. Subparagraph (C) concerns a timely pre-notice actual claim that was not yet disallowed, whose disallowance still allowed a timely refund suit at notice, or on which a timely suit was already pending. Section 6511(c) concerns assessment-extension agreements and the specified time/payment limits; subsection (d) provides special periods for designated income-tax items, including carrybacks and foreign-tax-credit claims. No such alternative, qualifying filing extension, or other enlarged period is established here. These preserved statutory alternatives prevent any universal two-year rule for every nonfiler and every tax category.

The Tax Court's deficiency jurisdiction remains distinct from its power to refund the particular payment. The amount written on the return is not an adjudicated refund amount: the additional liability and addition to tax remain relevant. The conditional correspondence and settlement discussion do not establish a binding closing agreement, waiver, estoppel or a separate contractual award. No general classification of every tax deadline, damages, interest, fees, or extra hearing is adopted.
