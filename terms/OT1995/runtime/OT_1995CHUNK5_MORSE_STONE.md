## OT1995-048 — Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia

**Event:** 1996-03-27 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 186

**Docket(s):** No. 94-203

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Reverse the threshold dismissal. Hold that this state-integrated nominating convention's participation fee is subject to §5 preclearance and that the affected voters may pursue their pleaded §10 enforcement claim. Remand the unresolved statutory merits and appropriate relief.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** The convention selects a nominee receiving legal ballot advantages and functions as the State's nomination machinery in the respect at issue. The $45 fee changes access to that electoral participation. It is not merely a platform choice or an internal discussion among private members. Presley distinguishes changes in voting participation from unrelated internal allocations; Morris identifies the appropriate baseline without declaring every fee retrogressive. Allen's enforcement logic and the actual statutory design support an effective private §10 route. Recognizing that route does not prove that this charge is a prohibited poll tax.

**Relief and limits:** Require the appropriate preclearance treatment of the voting change and permit adjudication of the preserved §10 claim. Distinguish coverage, approval, retrogression and substantive illegality. Leave genuine private party speech, platforms and internal association outside this electoral-function holding; Hurley protects expressive composition but does not turn state-integrated nomination access into wholly private expression. No comprehensive new implied-cause-of-action doctrine or general regulation of all parties follows.

**Limit:** Statutory coverage and a private enforcement route do not establish that the particular fee is substantively unlawful. Resolve that question through the applicable statute and record.
