**Stone’s disposition:** Affirm (approved by the user on October 7, 2026). Montana retained the intent requirement, purposeful or knowing homicide; it cannot prevent the jury from considering evidence that could disprove intent. Preserve the ruling granting Egelhoff a new trial. Do not rewrite the offense for Montana.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Gaudin requires the jury to decide the enacted elements; it does not require every offense to retain subjective intent. Equally, a State cannot preserve an intent element while using an evidentiary label solely to prevent the jury from considering evidence that disproves it. Egelhoff's severe intoxication may bear on the purposeful or knowing state the charge otherwise required. Stone therefore treats the legislature's actual rule and authoritative state construction as decisive. A hypothetical saving rationale is not a substitute for state law. The sober-person formulation can matter only if it is truly the offense's substantive definition, not an appellate reconstruction invented to sustain this conviction.

**Mechanism and remedy:** Treat the Montana Supreme Court's construction, under which the offense retained purposeful or knowing mens rea, as the authoritative state-law premise. On that premise, apply the federal rule against this blanket exclusion of evidence that could disprove the retained element, and affirm the judgment ordering a new trial. A genuinely substantive legislative redefinition of culpability would be a different offense, analyzed on its own terms, including fair notice and retroactivity; no such redefinition is established here.

**Limits:** Preserve ordinary reliable evidentiary rules and offenses that genuinely define the prohibited conduct. Clarification of the actual state-law offense is distinct from categorical invalidation of every intoxication-evidence restriction.
