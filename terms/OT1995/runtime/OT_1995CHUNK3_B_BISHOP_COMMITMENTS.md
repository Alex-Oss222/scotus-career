## Completed components

**B1 — Bishop's threshold and Commerce claim:** Reach only the claim that, even accepting the charge and facts admitted by his plea, the Government lacks constitutional power to prosecute original §2119. Menna and Blackledge, as limited by Broce, preserve that face-of-the-record governmental-power claim; they do not authorize a new factual challenge, an attack on the vehicle-history admission or every constitutional objection after a plea. Reject the surviving claim and affirm Bishop's judgment within the submitted scope.

**B2 — Stokes's Commerce claim:** Reject it under Harris. Congress may regulate the economic class of armed vehicle takings within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme, with the statute's actual vehicle-history element remaining necessary. Neither remote interstate movement alone, a currently interstate journey, individual interstate-resale intent nor generalized insurance/crime/productivity costs supplies or replaces this rationale. No independent vehicle-history sufficiency issue is decided.

**B3 — Stokes's cumulative punishment:** Reject the same-prosecution Double Jeopardy objection and uphold the offense-date §§2119/924(c) cumulative authorization under Harris and Missouri v. Hunter. The additional, consecutive-punishment direction is sufficient even assuming Blockburger overlap; there is no residual ambiguity on this pairing for lenity. This does not govern successive prosecution, convert possession into use/carrying, or decide an unpresented instruction or sufficiency issue. Bailey requires active employment for use. The reported gun-to-head act is active employment, not storage, but the Court does not manufacture a separate Bailey claim, carrying finding or harmless-error ruling.

**B4 — Stokes's identification claim:** Reject the asserted due-process ground for excluding these identifications. Apply Manson/Biggers reliability against the corrupting effect of suggestion, not automatic admission whenever a witness claims independence. The two witnesses observed the gunman during the taking; the separate identifications followed within ninety minutes; the district court found their identifications rested on their own recollections, and the appellate court sustained that finding and reliability conclusion. The police's caught-the-thief comment and station showup are weighed as suggestive. On the supported findings and the presented challenge, they do not establish the substantial likelihood of misidentification necessary for exclusion. Do not supply an absent detailed description, duration, certainty quotation or lighting finding. Do not use capture near the vehicle or other guilt evidence as a substitute for reliability of the identification itself.
