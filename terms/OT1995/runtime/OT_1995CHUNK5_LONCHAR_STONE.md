## OT1995-051 — Lonchar v. Thomas

**Event:** 1996-04-01 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 517 U.S. 314

**Docket(s):** No. 95-5015

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Require adjudication under the habeas statutes and Rules Governing §2254 Cases. Permit source-authorized scheduling control and any Rule 9 disposition supported by Rule 9’s own predicates, but do not extinguish the first petition through free-floating equity.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Determine first whether an enacted statute or Habeas Rule authorizes dismissal for the asserted delay; proximity to execution is not an independent terminal bar.
- **Supporting Authorities:** 28 U.S.C. §§2241–2254; Rule 9, Rules Governing §2254 Cases; simulated *McFarland v. Scott*, 512 U.S. 849; simulated *Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** A first federal habeas petition satisfying the governing jurisdictional and pleading requirements may not be dismissed merely because it was filed near an execution date; delay may support dismissal only through Rule 9 or another source supplying and satisfying its own conditions.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Congress and the Habeas Rules already allocate authority over exhaustion, default, successive petitions, prejudicial delay, and meritless claims. A free-form last-minute exception would add a terminal bar without a legal source and make first review turn on unguided equitable reaction. Courts retain control over schedules, stays, sanctions, and Rule 9, but those powers must operate within their stated predicates.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not excuse deliberate misconduct, guarantee a stay, prevent dismissal under Rule 9 on the required showing, or displace statutory default, exhaustion, and successive-petition rules.
