## 1. The parent/legal-guardian exception reaches penalties and civil remedies

The concluding clause of §248(a), across printed pages 694–695, states:

> shall be subject to the penalties provided in subsection (b) and the civil remedies provided in subsection (c), except that a parent or legal guardian of a minor shall not be subject to any penalties or civil remedies under this section for such activities insofar as they are directed exclusively at that minor.

The shortened phrase **'any penalties under the section' is incomplete**. The corrected neutral statement is: **A parent or legal guardian of a minor is not subject to any penalties or civil remedies under §248 for the described activities insofar as they are directed exclusively at that minor.** Preserve both the relational condition and the exclusively-at-that-minor condition. This is not a qualification confined to criminal punishment or to civil money penalties.

Section (c) supplies private civil actions and governmental civil actions. Its relief includes, on the conditions stated in the respective paragraphs, injunctions and other civil remedies. The exception is therefore not a premise that federal FACE civil injunctions or private damages remain available for the very conduct within the exception. This correction supplies no factual finding that any current plaintiff is a parent/guardian, that a proposed act meets the exclusive-direction condition, or that an actual remedy was denied.

The statute's separate rules remain material. Section (d)(3) says FACE does not provide exclusive penalties or remedies and does not preempt state or local laws providing them. Section (d)(4) preserves enforcement of state/local laws regulating reproductive services. The exception is an exclusion of remedies **under this section**, not an affirmative grant of a parental power to use force, a constitutional parental veto, a repeal of other law, or a general immunity from every legal remedy. The source does not itself decide every mixed-target or incidental-effect application of 'exclusively.'

## 2. The facility definition does not add grounds or redefine places of worship

Section 248(e)(1), printed page 696, states:

> The term 'facility' includes a hospital, clinic, physician's office, or other facility that provides reproductive health services, and includes the building or structure in which the facility is located.

The corrected neutral description uses **includes**, retains the reproductive-service condition and the building/structure language, and does not add **grounds**. A place-of-worship building is not separately included in this definition merely because it is a place of worship. This is a correction of the quoted definition, not a new adjudication of its outer applications.

Places of religious worship appear in different provisions. Section (a)(2) addresses intentional force/threat/obstruction interference with lawful religious exercise at such a place. Section (a)(3) separately addresses damage or destruction to the property of a place of religious worship. Section (e)(4) separately defines physical obstruction:

> The term 'physical obstruction' means rendering impassable ingress to or egress from a facility that provides reproductive health services or to or from a place of religious worship, or rendering passage to or from such a facility or place of religious worship unreasonably difficult or hazardous.

Those separate references do not expand §248(e)(1)'s definition. Conversely, removing the invented 'grounds' language does not delete the actual ingress/egress/passage language, immunize all conduct outdoors, or convert §248(a)(1)'s person-directed force/threat/obstruction prohibition into a rule confined to the interior of a building. No buffer distance, categorical public-sidewalk prohibition, property-boundary holding or new trespass rule is supplied.

Section (d)(2) also remains distinct: FACE does not create new remedies for interference with First Amendment-protected speech or free exercise occurring outside a facility, regardless of viewpoint, or limit existing remedies for that interference. Section (d)(1) preserves expressive conduct protected from prohibition by the First Amendment. Neither provision substitutes for examination of the actual prohibited act, statutory harm and motive requirements.

## Other retained limits and handoff

The reproductive-services definition in §248(e)(5) includes medical, surgical, counselling and referral services relating to the human reproductive system, including pregnancy and termination, provided in the listed service settings. The two §248(a)(1) motive branches, intentional-act requirement, actual movement/bodily-apprehension definitions, distinct private/government remedy schedules, permissive pre-final-judgment election of $5,000 per violation in place of actual compensatory damages, severability and May 26, 1994 prospective-conduct effective date remain unchanged. The source does not decide the pleaded constitutional or RFRA questions, factual sincerity, substantial burden, exact threatened conduct, an exemption, or any damages award. The unchallenged worship components do not become new questions presented through this correction.
