## OT1995-094 — Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission

**Event:** 1996-06-26 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 518 U.S. 604

**Docket(s):** No. 95-489

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** User-selected or delegated position; procedural and legal implementation authorized October 1, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered. The user's selections control; the three roadmaps provide context rather than predetermined outcomes.

**Stone’s disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining application of the party-expenditure limit and reject the requested facial invalidation. The basis is the party’s statutory financing relationship and circumvention of lawful contribution limits, notwithstanding the independently arranged expenditure.

**Stone’s reasoning and application:** Accept the record's absence of candidate coordination; do not relabel an independent advertisement as factually coordinated to obtain the desired result. Stone instead would sustain Congress's treatment of the official party's candidate-election financing as a distinctive contribution channel. The party selects, supports and helps govern through its nominees. Donors can give more to the party than directly to a candidate, creating a concrete route around candidate limits when party election spending is wholly uncapped. The fact that this advertisement preceded nomination does not erase that institutional channel. Buckley reserved the particular party-expenditure question, so Stone would apply its contribution and anticircumvention principles here rather than announce a general power to cap everyone's independent speech.

**Statutory and constitutional justification:** Congress adopted the party category in §441a(d); Stone need not invent a new factor test. The existing calibrated, election-related ceiling leaves meaningful collective advocacy while restricting the contribution conduit and the associated risk of influence over officeholders. Ordinary issue discussion outside the relevant statutory candidate-election coverage is not automatically swept in. Simulated Day v. Holahan invalidated a poorly fitted $100 intermediary limit, while expressly preserving demonstrated conduit regulation and meaningful collective association. This different ceiling must be defended on its own scope; Day is not silently overruled. Stone accepts Stevens's corruption and circumvention grounds, but does not adopt equalization of political influence or dislike of expensive speech as an independent justification contrary to Buckley and the Master's protection of natural persons.

**Tension and limits:** This is a deliberate departure from Stone's earlier position that actual independence resolves this particular party case. The reason is the statutory party/candidate financing relationship and evasion of valid contribution ceilings, not a factual finding of a corrupt bargain that the record lacks. The opposing view has force: the spending was independently arranged, donors are already limited, and a voluntary political association has protected speech. Stone answers by distinguishing the official nominating institution from an ordinary independent association and by defending the particular contribution-channel limit. He does not extend that answer to every PAC, individual, press organization or voluntary nonprofit, or claim the record establishes every form of party corruption. No new public-financing mandate, general expenditure ban or future case holding is imported.

**Scope:** Sustain the selected party limit on contribution-channel and circumvention grounds, not leveling. Preserve the distinct treatment of coordinated contributions and lawful anti-evasion regulation without treating every independent speaker as the party’s nominating institution.

## OT1995-095 — Felker v. Turpin

**Event:** 1996-06-28 | **Category:** ORIGINAL / CERTIORARI | **Action:** Certiorari-jurisdiction and original-habeas disposition

**Docket(s):** No. 95-8836 (associated application A-890); original habeas petition considered within the listed event

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Dismiss the petition for certiorari for want of jurisdiction and deny Felker’s separate original habeas petition.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Enter no affirmance of the Eleventh Circuit’s authorization ruling. Dismiss the certiorari proceeding because AEDPA withdraws ordinary certiorari review of that gatekeeping determination, and separately deny original habeas relief while recognizing that this Court retains original habeas authority subject to lawful statutory and traditional limitations.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Address jurisdiction first: ordinary certiorari review of the court of appeals’ gatekeeping determination is unavailable. Then consider the separately invoked original-habeas petition under the governing statutory and traditional extraordinary standards.
- **Supporting Authorities:** U.S. Const. art. I, §9, cl. 2; 28 U.S.C. §§2244 and 2254 as amended; simulated *McFarland v. Scott*, 512 U.S. 849; simulated *Lonchar v. Thomas*, 517 U.S. 314.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** AEDPA withdraws this Court’s ordinary certiorari jurisdiction over a court of appeals’ successive-application gatekeeping determination but does not repeal this Court’s original habeas jurisdiction; Congress may impose substantive and procedural limits on successive applications so long as a meaningful constitutional habeas core remains, and original habeas relief remains extraordinary.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** The jurisdictional provisions foreclose ordinary certiorari review of the Eleventh Circuit’s gatekeeping decision, so the Court cannot affirm that ruling and must dismiss the certiorari petition. The Suspension Clause protects the writ’s constitutional core, not freedom from every source-bound rule governing repeated applications, and AEDPA leaves this Court’s original habeas jurisdiction intact. The separate original application requires assessment under the law governing that channel and the actual claims. The proposed denial must rest on that assessment; failure to obtain authorization for a district-court filing does not by itself establish failure of this Court’s original petition.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not approve elimination of every meaningful forum for testing unlawful custody, apply AEDPA backward to petitions governed by prior law, or foreclose a claim falling within the constitutional habeas core.

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Affirm the recognition of Umbehr’s First Amendment claim and remand for application of the governing balance and causation rules.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Permit factfinding on protected speech, operational disruption, motivating cause, same-decision proof, and legally available relief on the reviewed official-capacity claim. Preserve the unreviewed individual-capacity immunity judgment.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Limit the decision to retaliation affecting an existing government-contractor relationship; do not decide the position of an unsuccessful bidder with no prior relationship.
- **Supporting Authorities:** Simulated *Waters v. Churchill*, 511 U.S. 661; *Pickering v. Board of Education*, 391 U.S. 563; *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274; *Perry v. Sindermann*, 408 U.S. 593.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** Government may not terminate an existing independent-contractor relationship in retaliation for the contractor’s protected speech; the court must balance the speech and public interests against the government’s legitimate contracting and operational interests and apply the *Mt. Healthy* causation sequence.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Government should not evade the First Amendment by purchasing a speaker’s services rather than hiring the speaker as an employee. An existing contractual relationship supplies a concrete governmental benefit and retaliation risk, while contract performance, trust, efficiency, cost, and disruption remain legitimate interests within the native balance. Extending the functional speech protection does not convert contractors into employees for unrelated statutory or constitutional purposes.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not govern bidders lacking an existing relationship, prevent termination for deficient performance or genuine operational reasons, or reopen the final individual-capacity immunity disposition; causation and source-bound remedial requirements remain.

## OT1995-098 — Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC; Alliance for Community Media v. FCC

**Event:** 1996-06-28 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 518 U.S. 727

**Docket(s):** Nos. 95-124 and 95-227

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Issue 1 — affirm as to §10(a). Issue 2 — reverse as to §10(b). Issue 3 — reverse as to §10(c).
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Permit cable operators to exercise the limited leased-access editorial choice in §10(a); enjoin enforcement of the segregation-and-written-request mechanism and the public-access censorship authorization.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Keep the three statutory provisions separate because their speakers, channel structures, burdens, and asserted interests materially differ.
- **Supporting Authorities:** Simulated *Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC*, 512 U.S. 622; *FCC v. Pacifica Foundation*, 438 U.S. 726; *Sable Communications of California, Inc. v. FCC*, 492 U.S. 115; *Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association*, 460 U.S. 37.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** (1) Congress may permit a cable operator to decline patently offensive leased-access programming when the operator retains a genuine editorial role; (2) it may not impose a segregation and advance written-request regime that predictably suppresses protected adult access more broadly than necessary; and (3) it may not transfer content-censorship power over public-access channels to operators when those channels function as government-created forums for private speakers.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** The three provisions do materially different work. Section 10(a) respects an operator’s own editorial choice in a channel it is otherwise compelled to lease. Section 10(b) burdens speakers and willing adult viewers through stigma, delay, and default blocking far beyond the child-protection interest. Section 10(c) is different again: the operator does not author the public-access message, so delegated suppression is censorship of a channel structured for private speakers rather than protection of the operator’s own expression.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not bar precise channeling, notice, parental-control technology, or enforcement of obscenity law, and it does not convert every cable channel into a public forum or create a medium-specific First Amendment tier.

## OT1995-099 — United States v. Winstar Corp.

**Event:** 1996-07-01 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Argued merits decision

**Citation:** 518 U.S. 839

**Docket(s):** No. 95-865

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Affirm the Federal Circuit’s liability judgment.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Remand for contract-specific causation and damages without restraining Congress or invalidating the later statute.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Decide contractual liability without questioning Congress’s authority to enact the later regulatory change.
- **Supporting Authorities:** *Lynch v. United States*, 292 U.S. 571; *Horowitz v. United States*, 267 U.S. 458; *Bowen v. Public Agencies Opposed to Social Security Entrapment*, 477 U.S. 41; traditional contract principles.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** The Government remains free to legislate, but when it expressly contracts for specified regulatory treatment and assumes the risk of legal change, later legislation that prevents performance may create ordinary damages liability without surrendering sovereign power or invalidating the statute.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** Sovereignty answers whether Congress may change the law; contract answers who bears the promised transaction’s financial consequences. The acquisition agreements used specified regulatory treatment as bargained consideration and allocated the risk that the promised treatment would become unavailable. The sovereign-acts and unmistakability doctrines protect legislative authority from implied surrender, but they do not erase an express contractual allocation of financial risk or permit the Government to retain the acquisitions without the ordinary consequence of breach.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not infer a promise from regulation alone, require compensation whenever law changes, surrender legislative power, or authorize specific performance against a valid statute.

## OT1995-100 — Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore

**Event:** 1996-07-01 | **Category:** MERITS | **Action:** Summary review on certiorari petition

**Citation:** 518 U.S. 938

**Docket(s):** Nos. 95-1691 and 95-1692

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Position status:** Approved by the user as written on October 2, 2026; no Court vote or outcome entered.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment:** Reverse both Pennsylvania judgments and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy / Remand Position:** Apply the automobile exception if probable cause and ready mobility are established; address search scope and any adequate independent state-law ground on remand.
- **Stone’s Threshold Position:** Resolve the federal automobile-exception question while preserving any independent state constitutional ground not properly before the Court.
- **Supporting Authorities:** *Carroll v. United States*, 267 U.S. 132; *California v. Carney*, 471 U.S. 386; simulated *Ornelas v. United States*, 517 U.S. 690; simulated *Whren v. United States*, 517 U.S. 806.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding for This Case:** Police may search a readily mobile vehicle without a warrant when they have probable cause to believe it contains evidence or contraband; the Fourth Amendment does not require an additional case-specific showing that obtaining a warrant was impracticable.
- **Stone’s Reasoning:** The automobile exception rests on ready mobility and the regulated, public character of vehicles, not on proof of a fresh emergency in every case. Probable cause remains indispensable and limits the search to places capable of containing the object sought. Pennsylvania’s added exigency requirement contradicts the inherited exception and improperly converts its established predicates into a warrant-impracticability inquiry.
- **Stone’s Operative Limit:** The holding does not authorize entry into a home or protected curtilage, treat an immobile structure as a vehicle, dispense with probable cause, or exceed the scope supported by the object sought.
