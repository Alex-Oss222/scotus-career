## Decisions and Dispositions

### Brown v. Pro Football, Inc., No. 95-388

Decision, June 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

518 U.S. 231. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit. Argued March 27, 1996; decided June 20, 1996. The question is whether the nonstatutory labor exemption protects NFL member clubs' agreement to implement the last proposed developmental-player wage after bona fide collective bargaining reached lawful impasse. The lower court rejected the players' antitrust recovery on that exemption ground.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens.

The exemption defeats the antitrust recovery for the bargaining conduct presented. The Court does not reinstate the district court's treble-damages award, injunction or attorney's fees, adjudicate a new unfair labor practice, or confer immunity on other league restraints. Stevens would reverse the exemption ruling and remand for the defendants' unresolved appellate challenges; his proposed remedy would not itself reinstate every component of the district court's relief.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Justice Stevens dissents alone. The Court opinion's complete rule, explanation and remedy command eight votes.

#### Holdings

##### Joint implementation within an ongoing bargaining process

**Holding and operative rule.** The nonstatutory labor exemption protects members of an existing consensual multiemployer bargaining group when they jointly implement their last lawful wage proposal during or immediately after bona fide bargaining reaches impasse, the conduct directly arises from that bargaining, and it concerns mandatory employment terms and the bargaining participants. Contract expiration, impasse and the union's rejection of the particular proposed wage do not by themselves end that protection; it does not cover product-market collusion, agreements with nonlabor parties, sham bargaining, or restraints remote in time or purpose from the labor relationship.

**Authority.** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology, controls this proposition and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation.** Federal labor law permits joint bargaining by an employer group and leaves lawful bargaining responses available when negotiations reach impasse. Connell recognizes the need to accommodate that process with antitrust law while denying protection to restraints that reach beyond it. Jewel Tea supplies the collective-bargaining foundation of the exemption; its different opinions do not furnish one undifferentiated majority explanation. Bonanno establishes that impasse alone does not dissolve the bargaining relationship, and Buffalo Linen recognizes the legitimate role of coordinated employer bargaining responses.

The clubs' implementation concerns the represented players' wages, follows genuine bargaining, and directly continues that process. Applying antitrust liability at this point would subject its lawful operation to treble-damages judgments about the permissible bargaining response. The players correctly observe that an agreement fixing compensation would ordinarily raise antitrust concerns. But requiring union acceptance of this particular term before recognizing the exemption would let the rejection of a proposal transform an ongoing labor dispute into antitrust liability even though the bargaining relationship persists. The exemption protects the bounded process, not the desirability of the wage or the identity of the employer. The Court leaves open the precise point at which later conduct becomes sufficiently detached from bargaining to lose protection. It decides neither the legality of unrelated restraints nor any labor-law violation outside the submitted premises.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Jewel Tea, 381 U.S. 676:** Applied for the exemption's collective-bargaining foundation, preserving the distinction between White's and Goldberg's different explanations rather than treating either as a six-Justice rationale.
- **Connell, 421 U.S. 616:** Applied for the accommodation of labor and antitrust law; its refusal to exempt exclusion of unrelated subcontractors from the product market remains in force and marks the boundary against restraints on outsiders.
- **Bonanno, 454 U.S. 404:** Applied for the proposition that impasse alone does not dissolve consensual multiemployer bargaining; the precise later endpoint of this exemption remains undecided.
- **Buffalo Linen, 353 U.S. 87:** Applied for the recognized legitimacy of coordinated employer bargaining responses within the statutory labor relationship; it does not create immunity for every employer agreement.

#### Law After Decision

The Sherman Act remains applicable outside the protected bargaining conduct. Within the continuing consensual multiemployer relationship, immediate implementation of the last lawful mandatory-term proposal after genuine impasse can retain the established exemption even without agreement on that term. The decision does not create a sports exception, a comprehensive labor-market immunity, or protection after conduct becomes remote from bargaining. Labor-law obligations and remedies retain their independent force.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens distinguishes the protection of collective employee demands and consensual group negotiation from immunity for an employer-only agreement imposing a wage the union never accepted. Jewel Tea's negotiated working condition does not establish that the employer restraint here receives the same protection. Connell shows why lawfulness under labor law cannot alone eliminate an otherwise independent antitrust constraint. Stevens accepts that impasse need not end the bargaining relationship under Bonanno; continued bargaining duties nevertheless do not establish an affirmative license to suppress wage competition through concerted enforcement of the rejected wage. His position preserves lawful multiemployer negotiation and permits ordinary antitrust defenses to be considered. He would reverse the exemption ground and remand for the unresolved appellate issues, without automatically restoring the entire monetary and injunctive judgment.

#### Source Notes

The bargaining setting and judgments below are reported in [50 F.3d 1041](https://static.case.law/f3d/50/html/1041-01.html). The operative premise is genuine bargaining to lawful impasse; no disputed bargaining motive is adopted as an additional finding. The principal earlier authorities are [Jewel Tea](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep381/usrep381676/usrep381676.pdf), [Connell](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep421/usrep421616/usrep421616.pdf), and [Bonanno](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep454/usrep454404/usrep454404.pdf). Their different doctrinal functions and the limits of the exemption are stated above.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The affirmed judgment returns through the ordinary appellate process. No new trial, monetary award or injunction is ordered. The Court sets no further proceeding or date.

**End of entry: Brown v. Pro Football, Inc., Decision, June 20, 1996.**

### Lane v. Peña, No. 95-365

Decision, June 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 15, 1996; decided June 20, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit. The question is whether the Rehabilitation Act unequivocally waives federal sovereign immunity from compensatory damages for discrimination in this federally conducted academy program. The District Court found a violation and ordered reinstatement, then rejected damages after Dorsey; the Court of Appeals summarily affirmed. The Government did not contest the injunction.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer.

The discrimination determination and reinstatement remain undisturbed. No damages calculation or new trial is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Justice Stevens dissented, joined in full by Justice Breyer.

#### Holdings

**Federal program coverage does not supply the missing consent to compensatory damages.** Sections 504(a) and 505 of the Rehabilitation Act, read with §1003 of the Rehabilitation Act Amendments of 1986, do not unequivocally waive the United States' immunity from Lane's claim for compensatory damages arising from this federally conducted academy program. The damages judgment is affirmed; the existing discrimination determination and reinstatement remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices adopt the rule, explanation and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress imposed §504(a)'s nondiscrimination duty on programs conducted by Executive agencies and the Postal Service. Section 505(a)(2), however, supplies Title VI remedies to persons aggrieved by recipients or federal providers of financial assistance. That assistance-related formulation does not unequivocally expose every federal program operator to compensatory damages. Nordic Village requires enacted consent to the particular money judgment; Ohio requires an incorporated remedial provision's limits to receive independent effect. A substantive prohibition and an affirmative obligation to pay from the Treasury are distinct.

The combined-enactment argument does not cure that difference. Section 505(a)(1) separately supplies Title VII remedies for §501 federal employment complaints, while §505(b) permits a reasonable attorney's fee as costs, in the court's discretion, to a prevailing party other than the United States. Neither channel converts this academy enrollment claim into a federal employment case or a general damages waiver. Section 1003 removes specified State immunity and compares remedies against States with remedies against other public or private entities. Its public-entity reference can encompass nonfederal recipients; its comparison does not independently grant every remedy against every sovereign. The Court need not choose among all possible readings of that State-directed comparison to find the claimed federal consent unclear.

Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional discrimination in the established Title IX action, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss; it did not supply consent to this federal Treasury award. Their satisfaction is not decided because waiver fails. Meyer likewise keeps waiver, cause of action and entitlement to recovery distinct. Legislative purpose and the desirability of compensation cannot supply missing enacted clarity. This conclusion neither licenses discrimination nor removes independently authorized prospective relief. It decides no differently situated federal assistance-provider claim, fee application, new officer action or implied damages cause.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992):** Applies the requirement of unequivocal enacted consent to the particular affirmative monetary remedy; a plausible narrower statutory operation defeats the asserted waiver. The original §106(c) holding and its limits remain unchanged.
- **United States Department of Energy v. Ohio, 503 U.S. 607 (1992):** Applies the distinction between federal substantive or adjudicatory coverage and the limits of incorporated monetary-remedy provisions; no pollution-remedy question is reopened.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992):** Preserves appropriate relief in the established Title IX action and distinguishes that remedial inquiry from federal sovereign consent. Compensation still requires proved intentional discrimination, recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss; their satisfaction is not decided here.
- **FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994):** Preserves the separate inquiries into waiver, substantive cause and recovery, including the effect of a genuinely broad sue-and-be-sued clause. No such clause supplies this academy claim's waiver; no agency Bivens question is decided.

#### Law After Decision

The existing requirement of unequivocal consent to the particular federal monetary remedy remains unchanged. These Rehabilitation Act provisions do not authorize Lane's compensatory claim against the United States for this federally conducted academy program. Section 504's substantive duty, independently authorized prospective relief, the separate discretionary fee provision and differently worded waivers retain their own force.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justice Breyer. In their view, Congress's express extension of §504 to federal programs carries the remedial setting in which that prohibition already operated. Section 505's differentiated enforcement channels codify particular means of enforcement without withdrawing ordinary damages for the newly express federal obligation; the 1986 recognition of legal and equitable remedies reinforces that reading. They accept that consent must come from enacted law and that Franklin alone cannot waive immunity, but find the combined provisions sufficient and distinguish Nordic Village's ambiguous bankruptcy formulation. Treating §505's assistance language as a restriction on the separate federal-program obligation gives the remedial scheme an unwarranted gap. They would reverse the categorical damages bar and remand for litigation of entitlement, amount and any remaining lawful defense. They would neither award damages automatically nor reopen the injunction.

#### Source Notes

The applicable text is Rehabilitation Act §§504 and 505, 29 U.S.C. §§794 and 794a, in the 1994 Code, and §1003 of the Rehabilitation Act Amendments of 1986, 100 Stat. 1845, codified at 42 U.S.C. §2000d-7. Section 1003 applies to violations occurring in whole or part after October 21, 1986. The lower proceedings include 867 F. Supp. 1050 and the D.C. Circuit's summary affirmance following Dorsey, 41 F.3d 1551. The academy's separation decision supplies the damages controversy; the unchallenged reinstatement is outside the reviewed dispute.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The ordinary mandate returns the case with the damages rejection affirmed and the reinstatement order undisturbed. This decision adds no new claim and resolves no fee application.

**End of entry: Lane v. Peña, Decision, June 20, 1996.**

### United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., No. 95-325

Decision, June 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 25, 1996; decided June 20, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 53 F.3d 1155. The questions are whether the allowed 1989 section 4971(a) assessment receives excise-tax priority under pre-1994 section 507(a)(7)(E), and whether section 510(c) permits its subordination on the grounds used below.

CF&I failed to make approximately $12.4 million in required pension contributions by September 15, 1990 and filed Chapter 11 on November 7, 1990. The approximately $1.24 million ten-percent assessment was allowed, but denied tax priority and subordinated. Basic liability and plan qualification are undisputed. Other disallowed assessments are outside review. The plan's zero-payment Class 13 has an alternative Class 12 general-unsecured classification. The lower courts expressly found no inequitable IRS conduct and did not decide the independent plan-law alternatives.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Excise-tax priority | Denial preserved, 8-1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Thomas | The allowed assessment receives no section 507(a)(7)(E) priority. |
| Equitable subordination | Stated ground rejected, 9-0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | | The subordinate ranking is set aside and the case remanded; lawful independent grounds remain open. |

The overall judgment is vacated and remanded. No other disallowed claim is reinstated and no final payment amount is ordered. Thomas would also reverse the denial of priority.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Preserves denial of tax priority | Complete statutory text-and-operation rationale and priority disposition |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Rejects stated equitable subordination and requires remand | Complete equitable-subordination rule and remand on that ground; Thomas does not join Part I's tax treatment |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Thomas | | Agrees with Part II; opposes Part I | Federal excise-tax classification and its priority consequence |

#### Holdings

##### I. This funding-default assessment does not receive excise-tax priority

**Controlling proposition:** The allowed 1989 section 4971(a) assessment is a noncompensatory penalty, not an excise tax entitled to priority under the governing pre-1994 section 507(a)(7)(E). When the Bankruptcy Code does not direct adoption of an external classification, the exaction's actual statutory operation controls its bankruptcy tax characterization; neither a tax label nor a deterrent purpose alone resolves that inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the rule and complete explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Feiring requires federal bankruptcy characterization by statutory operation. United States v. New York applies that inquiry to federal exactions and recognizes that a revenue tax can encourage conduct without becoming a penalty. Sotelo looks beyond a penalty label where the assessment represents taxes already withheld and not paid. Those decisions make the governing legal operation decisive, rather than importing another statute's terminology or treating every regulatory purpose as disqualifying.

Section 507(a)(7)(E) contains no direction adopting the Internal Revenue Code's classification. This assessment arises from failure to perform an independent pension-funding obligation and measures the sanction by the accumulated deficiency. Payment neither satisfies that obligation nor replaces the independently due pension/PBGC liability. It therefore punishes the funding default rather than collecting an underlying tax or compensating that loss. Congress's use of the word tax and the provision's excise-tax setting do not overcome that operation. Congress remains free expressly to give a specified assessment priority.

The ten-percent assessment reviewed here is distinct from the additional hundred-percent assessment under section 4971(b). Minimum-funding waivers under section 412(d) and 29 U.S.C. section 1083(a) can remove the relevant deficiency and prevent either assessment; the distinction does not depend on treating only the additional assessment as avoidable. Section 507(a)(7)(G) separately requires a penalty related to a qualifying claim and compensation for actual pecuniary loss, which this assessment does not provide.

The Court adopts no general Lorber checklist, rule against regulatory taxation, or constitutional punishment classification. It leaves the allowed claim intact. Denial of priority does not itself permit subordination, decide dischargeability or proportionality, or restore any other disallowed assessment.

##### II. The stated equitable ground cannot place this claim below ordinary unsecured claims

**Controlling proposition:** Section 510(c) does not authorize distributional subordination of this noncompensatory penalty merely because its payment reduces innocent creditors' recoveries. Its equitable authority remains available on a legally sufficient, supported claim-specific ground, but the stated class-based reasons are inadequate even if described as individual balancing; the challenged subordinate ranking must be set aside. Rejection of that equitable ground does not itself confer tax priority; the Part I majority separately resolves priority.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other participating Justices, controls the equitable-subordination rule and the remand required by that rule. Thomas's join does not extend to Part I's denial of tax priority.

**Controlling explanation:** Noland preserves section 510(c)'s real statutory power while rejecting a judicial replacement of Congress's distribution choices. After notice and a hearing, the provision permits subordination for distribution of all or part of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, under principles of equitable subordination. Priority status does not confer immunity from that power; lack of priority does not establish a sufficient ground for exercising it.

Here the lower courts relied on the assessment's noncompensatory character, the estate's inability to pay ordinary creditors fully, and the reduction in those creditors' recoveries. They expressly found no inequitable IRS conduct. The stated reasons recur whenever claims of this class compete with compensatory creditors in an insolvent estate. They supply a general preference for compensation, not a distinct equitable circumstance. Calling the process balancing does not change that deficiency.

This prepetition assessment differs from Noland's postpetition administrative penalties. That difference matters to statutory priority, but it does not permit the same inadequate equitable reason to operate within the general-unsecured class. Pepper preserves a misconduct-based equitable application; neither it nor this case establishes misconduct as indispensable to every possible section 510(c) application. The Court announces no frozen catalogue of equitable grounds and no entitlement to relief without misconduct.

The plan's ordinary-unsecured fallback remains available if no independently lawful basis supports different treatment. Properly presented arguments under sections 1122(a) and 1129(a)(7), including the claimed relevance of section 726(a)(4), remain open; the Court neither endorses them nor decides their merits. Any further equitable ground requires lawful presentation and support. No new misconduct hearing, lien transfer, final payment amount, or reopening of every factual issue is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **City of New York v. Feiring, 313 U.S. 283 (1941):** Applies the federal bankruptcy inquiry into the exaction's legal operation rather than the creating statute's label.
- **United States v. New York, 315 U.S. 510 (1942):** Applies that inquiry to a federal exaction and preserves the distinction between a revenue tax that creates incentives and a sanction for violating an independent duty.
- **United States v. Sotelo, 436 U.S. 268 (1978):** Applies the rule that a penalty label does not change the character of liability for already-withheld taxes; distinguishes this independent funding-default sanction.
- **United States v. Noland, May 13, 1996:** Applies the limit on class-based equitable reprioritization, including recurring reasons described as balancing. Preserves statutory equitable susceptibility even for priority claims. Its distinct administrative classification of postpetition tax penalties is not extended to this prepetition assessment.
- **Pepper v. Litton, 308 U.S. 295 (1939):** Preserves its misconduct-based equitable application without converting misconduct into a necessary condition for every section 510(c) case. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The allowed 1989 section 4971(a) assessment lacks excise-tax priority because of its funding-default sanction function. That characterization neither disallows it nor independently establishes subordinate rank. Noland's restriction on class-based equity applies within the general-unsecured setting while preserving lawful section 510(c) power. This decision establishes no constitutional punishment rule, universal misconduct prerequisite, automatic distribution, or ruling on the independent Chapter 11 classification and Chapter 7 comparison arguments.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Thomas concurs in Part II and dissents from Part I. Congress enacted this federal assessment as a tax properly treated as an excise, and its regulatory or deterrent object does not authorize withdrawing the priority Congress gave excise taxes. He distinguishes the need to prevent a State's label from controlling federal bankruptcy law from the treatment owed Congress's own federal tax choice. The bankruptcy tax inquiry does not, in his view, license the Court to reclassify this federal excise on the majority's functional ground. He would reverse the penalty-based priority ruling and remand consistently with tax treatment, while retaining express Bankruptcy Code limitations; he does not claim that every IRS charge receives priority. He fully agrees that Noland forecloses the stated equitable ground, even for a claim he would classify as a priority tax. His disagreement therefore does not extend to rejection of the challenged subordination, preservation of legally supported statutory equity, or leaving independent plan issues open.

#### Source Notes

The governing priority text is pre-1994 11 U.S.C. section 507(a)(7)(E), with its distinct actual-pecuniary-loss penalty provision in (G); the later subsection (a)(8) numbering does not govern. Section 510(c)'s notice-and-hearing, distribution and allowed-claim/interest terms remain operative. Section 4971(a) specifies ten percent, or five percent for a multiemployer plan; the actual ten-percent assessment and qualifying plan status are undisputed. The reviewed liability is separate from section 4971(b)'s additional assessment and the underlying pension obligations. The lower record is reported at 53 F.3d 1155. No final plan distribution or independently sufficient alternative ground was adjudicated below.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Tenth Circuit for proceedings consistent with both holdings. The stated recurring class reasons cannot sustain subordination under a different label. The plan's Class 12 alternative remains available if no independently lawful basis supports different treatment. Properly presented sections 1122(a) and 1129(a)(7) arguments, the asserted relevance of section 726(a)(4), and any legally sufficient supported equitable ground remain undecided. The Court orders no new misconduct hearing, factual reopening, lien transfer or fixed distribution.

**End of entry: United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., Decision, June 20, 1996.**

### Baby Richard, by his guardian ad litem, v. Kirchner (In re Petition of Kirchner)

Decision, June 21, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided June 21, 1996, on review under 28 U.S.C. section 1257 of the Illinois Supreme Court's turnover judgment, No. 78101, 164 Ill. 2d 468, 649 N.E.2d 324. The order issued January 25, 1995; the explanatory opinion issued February 28, 1995.

The question is whether the child has an independent due-process interest requiring consideration of serious avoidable harm from the manner of implementing a lawful custody transfer. Richard entered the Does' home four days after birth and has lived there for years. Kirchner was deceived into believing his child had died, discovered the truth after fifty-seven days and promptly asserted his claim. The adoption was invalidated without a finding that he was unfit. His lawful custody entitlement remains fixed. Richard remains with the Does and initial transfer is pending.

The state judgment rejected the child's federal-interest claim and required immediate turnover. The narrower implementation argument falls within that claim. Federal review of implementation does not reopen the state-law entitlement to custody; the domestic-relations exception to diversity jurisdiction does not bar this appellate review.

#### Judgment

**Vacated in part and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm the reviewed federal judgment.

The vacatur removes the categorical rejection of the child's implementation claim and the mandatory forthwith requirement only as necessary to permit meaningful, prompt consideration before compulsory transfer. The adoption's invalidation and Kirchner's lawful custody entitlement remain intact. The Court orders no new custody comparison, fixed stay, period of residence, compulsory long-term contact or return transfer. It finds neither serious injury nor a need for delay.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their joins cover the protected-interest rule, procedure, limited vacatur and remand. O'Connor dissents, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; they would affirm on the grounds explained below.

#### Holdings

##### I. The child's own interest limits the manner of compulsory implementation

**Operative rule:** When the State abruptly severs an established caregiving relationship to implement an otherwise lawful custody transfer, the child has an independent liberty interest against serious, avoidable injury from the manner of that compulsory implementation. That interest permits a claim directed to concrete transition harm and practicable means of avoiding it; it confers no right to retain an invalid adoption, remain indefinitely with nonparents or defeat a fit father's custody entitlement through a best-interests comparison.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins support the entire rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The protected interest concerns the child's personal security during affirmative government enforcement. It does not arise merely because a loss is grievous or because the child prefers an existing home. Years of caregiving identify the relationship whose abrupt state-compelled severance may cause the serious injury asserted; they do not themselves prove injury or create permanent custody rights.

Smith recognizes the significance of caregiving ties but assumes the asserted interest and emphasizes the natural family's claim to restoration. The Court respects that limit by preserving Kirchner's entitlement. Parham establishes that children possess liberty interests of their own even when parental authority is legitimate, although its institutional-confinement holding does not decide this case. These principles support a bounded development: the child's own security is not exhausted by deciding which adult is legally entitled to custody. Neither lawful entitlement nor state action alone establishes a constitutional injury; the claim must concern serious avoidable harm attributable to the chosen manner of enforcement.

Stanley and Lehr protect a parent's opportunity to assume responsibility. Deception did not establish Kirchner's abandonment, and protecting the child during implementation cannot confiscate that relationship by delay. Santosky supplies principles concerning family deprivation, not a right to preserve this invalid adoption. Michael H. did not decide every independent interest of a child.

The contrary argument correctly distinguishes parental restoration from institutional confinement. It does not justify treating evidence of serious avoidable injury from compulsory implementation as categorically irrelevant. Flores's distinct custody scheme remains intact. DeShaney and Collins reject general protection guarantees; Pinder preserves the distinction between private nonprotection and attributable affirmative deprivation without deciding this claim. The Court neither guarantees an ideal placement nor constitutionalizes ordinary disappointment. Recognition of this limited interest precedes, and is not created by, the procedural inquiry.

##### II. The required opportunity and relief must fit the actual transition claim

**Operative rule:** Before compulsory implementation in these circumstances, the child's representative must have a meaningful, prompt opportunity to present concrete evidence of serious transition harm and practicable measures to avoid it, and the father must have an opportunity to respond; the state court must consider that issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. Procedures and any protective measures must fit the actual showing and preserve the father's entitlement: unsupported requests may be rejected, immediate implementation may follow adequate consideration, and no automatic evidentiary hearing, fixed delay or renewed custody contest is required.

**Authority:** Part II of the same Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete procedure and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews weighs the identified private interest, the risk of erroneous deprivation and value of additional procedures, and the government's interests and burdens. Here categorical exclusion prevents the child from showing whether the State's chosen implementation threatens serious avoidable injury. A focused opportunity to present evidence can expose that risk and identify a lawful means of avoiding it. The father must be able to contest the showing; his right and the child's need for a settled home make expedition essential.

The required inquiry concerns implementation, not which household is better. It must address competent evidence of the child's actual circumstances without presuming constitutionally decisive trauma from length of residence. Parham supports procedures fitted to the actual decision rather than an invariant trial. A. St. P. C. illustrates attention to a particular family deprivation, but its clear-and-convincing abuse predicate does not govern this claim. Heller and Foucha likewise supply no universal family-hearing or proof requirement.

The state court may use procedures sufficient to the concrete proffer and actual disputes. The Court prescribes no full trial, appointed clinician, recurring professional approval, number of visits or particular burden of proof. A supported brief transition may be permissible when necessary to avoid the serious implementation harm shown; it is not mandatory. Unsupported requests may be rejected, and adequate consideration may support immediate transfer. Independently lawful emergency protection remains available; no emergency is found here.

The categorical federal rejection and inconsistent forthwith requirement must therefore be vacated to permit the opportunity before compulsory implementation. That limited relief supplies no fixed Supreme Court stay or residence period. It leaves the adoption's invalidation and lawful parental entitlement untouched, and determines neither actual injury nor a necessary transition plan.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Smith v. Organization of Foster Families for Equality & Reform, 431 U.S. 816:** Preserves its assumed-interest analysis and natural-family qualification. Its recognition of caregiving ties informs this new implementation rule but establishes no right to defeat the fit father.
- **Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584:** Applies the principle that the child has an independent liberty interest and that process fits the actual decision; distinguishes its institutional-admission interest and procedures.
- **Stanley v. Illinois, 405 U.S. 645, and Lehr v. Robertson, 463 U.S. 248:** Preserve parental relationship and responsibility protections; the implementation inquiry cannot reopen Kirchner's lawful entitlement.
- **Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745:** Preserves its termination and error-allocation principles without importing its proof burden or establishing a right to retain this adoption.
- **Michael H. v. Gerald D., 491 U.S. 110:** Preserves its reservation concerning a child's independent filial claim; its rejection of two legal fathers does not resolve these implementation safeguards.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applies only after the bounded liberty interest is identified; requires consideration of that interest, error risk and procedural value, and governmental burdens.
- **Reno v. Flores, March 23, 1993:** Distinguishes the sustained temporary immigration-custody scheme and its actual review avenues. Its facial holdings establish neither this transition rule nor categorical immunity from individual implementation claims.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C., November 20, 1995:** Uses its attention to the specific deprivation while preserving its distinct clear-and-convincing rule for Louisiana's complete-contact cutoff.
- **Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992, and Heller v. Doe, June 24, 1993:** Preserve their separate confinement and family-participation rules; neither provides a child-transition proof burden or hearing schedule.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services, 489 U.S. 189, and Collins v. Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Preserve the absence of a general protective-services or safe-environment guarantee and distinguish the present challenge to affirmative compelled implementation.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Preserves the distinction between private nonprotection and attributable affirmative deprivation and its reserved constitutional merits. Its immunity judgment does not decide this prospective implementation claim.
- **Ankenbrandt v. Richards, June 15, 1992:** Preserves its limited exception to diversity jurisdiction; it does not bar section 1257 review of this decided federal claim.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The child now has the bounded liberty interest and opportunity described in Parts I-II when challenging serious avoidable injury from the State's manner of severing an established caregiving relationship through compulsory transfer. The rule develops existing principles; no earlier case had already imposed this particular transition inquiry.

The lawful parental entitlement remains controlling as to permanent custody. Ordinary grief, a preferable-home claim or an unsupported demand for delay supplies no right to renewed custody litigation. Protective measures require support in the actual circumstances and must remain necessary to lawful implementation. The earlier termination, institutional-custody, abuse-proof and individual-immunity rules retain their own scopes.

#### Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor dissents, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The dissent accepts the distinction between implementation and permanent custody but concludes that the established relationship and possible trauma do not supply the new constitutional liberty predicate. Smith did not establish the asserted foster-family interest and gave special weight to restoration of the natural family. Parham and Flores address institutional restraint, a materially different deprivation from placing a child with his fit father.

Mathews selects process for an established interest; the practicality of a modest inquiry cannot establish that interest. Neither state compulsion, the seriousness of possible loss nor a carefully limited remedy is enough. A. St. P. C.'s particular proof requirement and Heller and Foucha's commitment rules cannot be transferred to this setting. DeShaney and Collins caution against turning concern for safety into a general due-process guarantee. Pinder did not decide the outer affirmative-injury merits and supplies no rule resolving them against every claimant.

The dissent would affirm the reviewed federal judgment while leaving humane state-law implementation measures available. It does not infer that Richard will be unharmed, endorse every abrupt transfer, require a factually identical historical precedent, or foreclose a different independently supported constitutional injury. Its common rationale does not adopt Michael H.'s most-specific-tradition method. The disagreement is with the protected-interest predicate here, not with the father's obligation to comply with otherwise lawful child-protection measures.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Illinois Supreme Court for prompt proceedings confined to implementation. Before compulsory transfer, the child's representative must receive the required opportunity and the father an opportunity to respond; the court must consider the actual showing and explain its implementation decision. It may reject an unsupported proffer or direct immediate transfer after adequate consideration. ### Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., No. 95-719

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 16, 1996; decided June 24, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit's September 12, 1995 judgment, 66 F.3d 427, in a diversity action in the Southern District of New York.

The Center conceded liability for 300 lost original photographic transparencies. A June 1994 jury awarded Gasperini $450,000, reflecting $1,500 per transparency. The district judge denied the Rule 59 new-trial motion without explanation. Applying New York's material-deviation standard, the Second Circuit ordered a new trial unless Gasperini accepted $100,000. No acceptance is established.

The questions are whether the state compensation standard governs, how Rule 59 operates with it, and what appellate review the Seventh Amendment permits. The appellate order is conditional, not an unconditional replacement judgment. The separate ruling allowing industry-standard evidence with other proof does not make $1,500 per transparency conclusive and is not overturned here.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens would affirm the existing conditional new-trial/$100,000 order; Scalia and Thomas would reverse that order and restore the district judgment against the reviewed excessiveness challenge.

The district judge must apply the state standard through Rule 59; appellate review is for abuse of discretion within the limits below. The Court selects neither $450,000 nor $100,000, orders no automatic new trial, and makes no independent findings about uniqueness or earning prospects.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | State measure governs; Stevens does not join the remand | State compensation standard and coexistence with Rule 59 |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Souter | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacatur and remand for district reconsideration | Federal trial responsibility, bounded appellate review, vacatur and remand |
| Concurrence in Part I and dissent from Part II and the judgment | Stevens |  | Would affirm the conditional new-trial/$100,000 order | State measure accepted; existing appellate order would be affirmed on legal-boundary review |

#### Holdings

##### I. New York's compensation standard applies through the federal new-trial function

**Operative rule:** In this diversity action governed by New York damages law, the federal district court must assess excessiveness under CPLR section 5501(c)'s material-deviation measure through Rule 59. The state compensation constraint applies without importing the State's appellate hierarchy, displacing federal new-trial procedure, creating a fixed damages cap or authorizing federal additur.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Stevens does not join Part II's constitutional allocation or remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Erie and Guaranty Trust require respect for the State's substantive recovery limits in diversity. Allowing materially different compensation merely because federal jurisdiction is available would invite forum selection for a larger award and unequal administration of the same state claim. This is more than a preference for a particular reviewing court.

Hanna first requires asking whether the Federal Rule actually governs the disputed point. Rule 59 permits new trials for reasons historically recognized in federal actions at law. Excessiveness is such a reason; that text does not prescribe an immutable substantive degree of excessiveness independent of the law governing compensation. Applying New York's measure therefore preserves the federal motion and trial function rather than overriding a conflicting Rule.

Section 5501(c) directs the Appellate Division, in its specified review of a money judgment following an itemized verdict, to find excessiveness or inadequacy when the award materially deviates from reasonable compensation; the context is a request for a new trial unless a different award is stipulated. Although the enacted direction names the appellate court, Inya, Cochetti and Shurgan establish trial application of the same measure under New York law. Those decisions preserve sparingly exercised trial discretion. They do not compel one remedy for every claimed deviation or establish a photographic price schedule.

The contrary view treats the measure as a standard of judicial reassessment already occupied by Rule 59. But the traditional federal ground and the State's compensation constraint can perform their separate work. Browning-Ferris recognizes state damages confines alongside federal new-trial procedure; its reserved appellate constitutional question requires the separate analysis in Part II. Byrd preserves federal allocation of functions. The Court does not transplant every outcome-affecting state practice, the State's reviewing hierarchy or its power of conditional increases.

##### II. The district judge applies that measure; appellate review must remain genuinely deferential

**Operative rule:** The district judge must exercise the federal new-trial function using the state measure, while the court of appeals reviews that determination for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment. The Seventh Amendment permits that review of trial-court discretion but does not permit the appellate court to substitute its own first-instance valuation of the jury's compensatory award; this judgment must be vacated for district reconsideration under the proper allocation.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Reexamination Clause protects jury-tried facts while preserving the traditional judicial power to grant a new trial. The district judge's trial experience and recognized new-trial responsibility place the state-standard assessment there. Byrd requires preserving the federal division of functions even when state procedure affects outcomes.

Appellate examination of whether that authority was lawfully exercised can coexist with the protected jury function when it genuinely reviews discretion. It must correct an erroneous legal measure, but may not use the abuse label to perform a fresh valuation from the cold record. This resolves, within that limit, the constitutional appellate question Browning-Ferris expressly reserved. The contrary historical objection properly demands more than a change of labels; the required restraint preserves the distinction between reviewing the district court's exercise of new-trial power and independently retrying compensation.

The district denial supplies no demonstrated application of New York's measure. That absence is not a finding that the judge actually used a particular wrong formula. The Second Circuit's first operative valuation cannot substitute for the assigned district assessment. Its use of favorable inferences and a conditional remittitur does not remove that allocation problem. Another district consideration therefore remains necessary.

On remand the judge must examine the record under the state standard, informed by pertinent New York decisions, and select lawful relief. Comparable cases are guidance, not fixed prices. Neither the jury's $450,000 nor the appellate $100,000 is declared correct. Donovan's accepted-remittitur bar does not apply because no acceptance is established. If the district court orders remittitur, Gasperini must have the choice of accepting the reduced award or a new jury trial; the court may not simply substitute a lower judgment. This differs from the additur barred by Dimick. No automatic retrial or imported state conditional increase follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64, and Guaranty Trust Co. v. York, 326 U.S. 99:** Apply respect for substantive state recovery limits in diversity without making every outcome-affecting procedural rule binding.
- **Hanna v. Plumer, 380 U.S. 460:** Applies the Federal Rule coverage inquiry before an unguided choice of law. Rule 59's traditional new-trial ground coexists here with the state compensation measure.
- **Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Electric Cooperative, Inc., 356 U.S. 525:** Preserves federal institutional allocation rather than requiring reproduction of the state reviewing hierarchy.
- **Browning-Ferris Industries of Vermont, Inc. v. Kelco Disposal, Inc., 492 U.S. 257:** Applies its distinction between state damages constraints and federal new-trial procedure. Its express reservation of the Seventh Amendment appellate question is resolved here only by the bounded abuse-of-discretion rule.
- **Donovan v. Penn Shipping Co., 429 U.S. 648:** Preserves federal procedural control and the bar on appealing an accepted remittitur. The absence of acceptance here distinguishes that bar.
- **Dimick v. Schiedt, 293 U.S. 474:** Preserves traditional remittitur and the prohibition on federal additur. State conditional increases do not alter that distinction.
- **Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, June 24, 1994, and BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, May 20, 1996:** Preserve their distinct punitive-damages protections. Neither supplies a compensatory formula, mandatory number of reviewing courts or answer to this federal reexamination question.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., April 23, 1996:** Preserves function-specific jury allocation and its reservation of subsidiary appellate review; patent construction does not dictate this compensation review.
- **Thompson v. Keohane, November 29, 1995; Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996; and Koon v. United States, June 13, 1996:** Retain their particular legal/factual review standards. No universal independent review of mixed issues or separate Seventh Amendment holding is inferred from them.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

New York's material-deviation measure governs compensation review in this diversity action through the district judge's Rule 59 authority. The court of appeals may correct legal error and review for abuse of discretion, but must respect the district judge's case-specific assessment and may not make its own first operative valuation.

The state substantive constraint and federal allocation both retain force. There is no universal rule importing state appellate procedure, no automatic damages cap, and no change to federal review of an independently presented constitutional limit on damages. The state measure does not itself decide which amount is reasonable here or compel a particular form of relief.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Stevens joins Part I but dissents from Part II and the judgment. He agrees that the New York compensation constraint applies and that Rule 59 does not displace it. He would affirm the Second Circuit's existing order because, in his view, it enforced a legal outer boundary while drawing reasonable factual inferences for Gasperini. A court can correct compensation beyond the legal limit without impermissibly retrying a fact.

Stevens rejects a Seventh Amendment requirement of the Court's particular appellate deference. His common-law account preserves the essentials of jury protection without fixing every historical institutional form; practical respect for the district judge does not create a constitutional bar to this legal correction. The district court already had an opportunity to review the award, and he identifies no permissible different result requiring another pass. His affirmance would retain Gasperini's choice between a new trial and accepting $100,000, not impose an unconditional replacement judgment. He does not convert Markman into a general appellate-review holding or BMW into compensatory-damages law.

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Thomas. Their first independent objection is that Rule 59 governs the federal degree of judicial excessiveness review through its reference to historical federal practice. Under Hanna, Erie policies cannot displace a Federal Rule that governs the point. They acknowledge the State's trial-court application of section 5501(c), but classify that measure as judicial reassessment rather than a substantive cap. Genuine state damages elements, categories and actual statutory ceilings remain binding; the dissent does not insist upon a single verbal federal excessiveness formula.

Their second independent objection is historical: appellate correction of legal error differs from reassessing compensation facts or evidentiary weight. Neither an abuse-of-discretion label nor a conditional new-trial option makes prohibited appellate factual reexamination permissible. Browning-Ferris expressly reserved that constitutional issue; Honda's punitive procedure and the other modern allocations do not decide it.

Scalia and Thomas retain traditional trial-court new-trial power and conditional remittitur, distinguish additur, and preserve ordinary correction of genuine legal insufficiency. They would reverse the appellate conditional-remittitur order and restore the district judgment against this excessiveness challenge, without computing a different award. No independent reversible industry-evidence error is established.

Its assessments of uniqueness and earning prospects are the appellate analysis under review, not findings newly made by this Court.

[Inya v. Ide Hyundai, 209 A.D.2d 1015](https://static.case.law/ad2d/209/html/1015-01.html), [Cochetti v. Gralow, 192 A.D.2d 974](https://static.case.law/ad2d/192/html/0974-01.html), and [Shurgan v. Tedesco, 179 A.D.2d 805](https://static.case.law/ad2d/179/html/0805-02.html), establish state trial application of the measure with sparingly exercised discretion. #### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Court of Appeals with directions for district reconsideration of the Rule 59 motion under New York's material-deviation standard. The district court must perform that assessment and select any legally available relief; a later appeal is subject to the bounded abuse-of-discretion rule.

The Court neither directs a new jury trial now nor reinstates the $450,000 as substantively correct. It does not approve the $100,000 limit, find how many transparencies were unique, or infer acceptance of remittitur. The distinct industry-evidence ruling remains undisturbed. No new liability issue, waiver, damages tariff or further Supreme Court event is prescribed.

### Lewis v. Casey, No. 94-1511

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 29, 1995; decided June 24, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's December 27, 1994 judgment, 43 F.3d 1261, reviewing the Arizona access-to-courts injunction entered October 13, 1993.

The certified class comprises adults in Arizona Department of Corrections custody. The district court found deficient assistance causing dismissal with prejudice, supported by named plaintiff Bartholic's evidence, and inability to file, supported by witness Harris's evidence. Recurring resource and assistance barriers, differences between institutions and improvements after filing are also in the record. These findings establish real obstruction at their supported scope, not an identical violation throughout every prison.

The State conceded initial standing while disputing proof warranting relief. That concession does not resolve Article III or admit statewide liability. The Ninth Circuit stated that the identified general actual-injury issue was not before it; that statement is not a blanket forfeiture ruling. The represented reach of the wrong and the statewide decree remain at issue.

The decree under review is already modified: the $46 eligibility threshold and typewriter requirement were vacated below; copying-price justification and master-fee objections were remanded. Other claims litigated in the class action are outside this access appeal. The original PLRA, enacted April 26, 1996, governs the prospective-relief questions at the scope stated below.

#### Holdings

##### I. Effective access cannot be measured by books alone

**Operative rule:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. It guarantees neither a preferred library program, physical browsing in every case, professional counsel for every prisoner nor the decree's numerical requirements.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join.

**Controlling explanation:** Bounds identifies effective access as the protected object and leaves administrators a choice of adequate methods. Murray preserves that choice without making books an automatic safe harbor. The district findings concerning Bartholic's dismissed action and Harris's inability to file are concrete consequences of inadequate assistance. They cannot be rewritten as an absence of injury simply because a library existed.

An efficient paging system with an inventory and prompt delivery may work for prisoners capable of independent use. Respondents' conditional acknowledgment does not establish that Arizona's actual system worked or that it serves prisoners with different literacy or language needs. The complete arrangement and actual obstacle matter.

The evidence distinguishes findings from testimony and common practices from statewide conclusions. Bartholic is a named representative; Harris supplies witness evidence. No fixed total of injured prisoners follows. Nor must a prisoner prove eventual victory in a case that inadequate access prevented him from pursuing. The opinions differ on the additional threshold described in Part II, not on the reality of these preserved findings.

##### II. The access violation requires a real connection to a qualifying claim

**Operative rule:** A Bounds access-liability claim requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying, nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to the validity of restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing, but a deficient inventory, preferred staffing arrangement or abstract wish for better research does not alone establish the violation.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt this substantive access-liability rule. The additional preliminary Article III merits screen and categorical restrictions in Scalia's concurrence have only four votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Libraries and assistance are means of enabling protected legal claims, rather than independent constitutional ends. The required connection identifies what a deficient arrangement actually prevents. A technical dismissal caused by inability to meet a filing requirement, an inability to file a contemplated qualifying claim, or sufficiently concrete threatened obstruction can establish that connection. A procedural deficiency does not itself make the underlying claim substantively frivolous.

The rule is not a demand for ultimate success or a rigid sequence of preliminary prison tests. Evidence and reasonable inferences may establish the real claim and obstacle before an avoidable filing failure occurs. Lujan's ordinary requirements of personal injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain; today's five-Justice proposition does not additionally require preliminary proof of legal merits as an Article III screening rule.

Direct and collateral sentence challenges and prison-conditions claims fall within the governing doctrine. Genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin's actual substantive-constraint rule. The Court does not adopt a categorical exclusion of all other important retained rights, declare that every affirmative assistance duty ends on filing, or provide a general subsidy for private civil litigation. Properly presented independent communications or petition claims retain their own predicates. Those reservations do not turn every resource request into an access violation.

##### III. The scope of the wrong and the needs of repair control relief

**Operative rule:** Common relief must correspond to a demonstrated common barrier and an identified affected group; individual obstruction or a resource deficiency alone does not establish an unrestricted statewide violation. Relief must respect restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests and give the State a genuine opportunity to devise an effective cure, while permitting measures actually necessary to repair the proved wrong.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this rule and the broader remand. Stevens supports a narrower remedial remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower courts' core-Bounds shortcut allowed resource deficiencies to do the work of proving the full violation. The findings preserve actual obstruction but do not establish the same denial at every institution, or separate broad entitlements for all non-English-speaking or lockdown prisoners. Bartholic's location in a lockdown unit does not itself show injury caused by lockdown status rather than inadequate assistance. This evidentiary limit is not a finding that only two inmates were ever injured.

Common evidence may prove a common wrong; every class member need not already have lost a lawsuit. Geographic and functional reach nevertheless require support. Jenkins permits necessary implementation of a demonstrated violation without making each salary, hour or program measure an independent constitutional right. It does not justify general prison improvement or indefinite control.

Turner sustains restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests, with substantial deference especially relevant to dangerous or specially restricted prisoners. Delay caused by a valid restriction does not become unconstitutional merely because an access injury results. Conversely, asserting security does not prove that every restriction is valid; the actual relationship must be examined.

Arizona had participated and objected, but an opportunity merely to rebut a presumptive statewide Gluth template did not provide the required genuine initial design role. Earlier resistance elsewhere does not establish obstruction here or forfeit that role. The State must now receive that opportunity within enforceable constitutional limits. These conclusions warrant setting aside the unsupported approval while retaining findings, common evidence and lawful targeted relief.

##### IV. The original PLRA governs existing prospective relief and any replacement

**Operative rule:** Section 802 applies amended section 3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996; relief here requires findings that it is narrowly drawn, extends no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation of the particular plaintiffs, and is the least intrusive means necessary, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Relief exceeding official state-law authority or otherwise violating state or local law additionally requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation, and absence of any other corrective relief.

**Authority:** Part IV of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices. The Chief Justice joins the statutory remand disposition without adding a ground. The same eight-Justice authority governs the distinct statutory conditions in Holdings V and VI.

**Controlling explanation:** The enactment expressly reaches existing prospective relief, including this federal prison-conditions decree. Prospective relief includes relief other than compensatory monetary damages. The habeas exclusion does not remove a prison-conditions action merely because the access services may assist habeas litigation.

The existing findings may establish necessary predicates at their actual scope; the statute requires substance, not ritual wording. But reasonableness, modest cost or absence of demonstrated hardship does not replace need, narrowness and least intrusiveness. Effective alternatives and public safety require actual evaluation. Necessary implementation can qualify even when each measure is not itself a freestanding constitutional entitlement.

Section 3626(a)(1)(B)'s three conditions operate separately from (A). Subsection (C) confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable limits; it does not create a new prohibition on every otherwise lawful means. No state-law conflict or prisoner-release remedy is found here.

Any new preliminary relief must be otherwise authorized, narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary for the harm requiring it, and least intrusive, with substantial weight to safety and criminal-system effects and respect for the separate state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the required prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. The old permanent decree is not automatically converted into a new preliminary order.

No constitutional challenge to section 802 is decided. The decree must be reconsidered under these actual terms without erasing established access wrongs or presuming that every old measure must survive or disappear.

##### V. Termination and stays depend on their own statutory predicates

**Operative rule:** The original section 3626 termination provisions do not dissolve relief merely upon enactment: the immediate-termination entitlement for relief entered without the required findings remains subject to written, record-based findings that relief is necessary to correct a current OR ongoing federal-right violation and remains narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary, and least intrusive. The automatic stay requires an actual pending qualifying motion and runs only for the original statutory period described below.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice Part IV coalition identified above; the Chief Justice agrees with the statutory disposition without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Under (b)(1), any party or intervener may seek termination at the applicable two-year period after relief was granted or approved, one year after denial of a termination motion under that paragraph, or, for preenactment relief, two years after enactment. Earlier agreement is permitted. Under (b)(2), a defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination when the necessary findings were absent, subject to (b)(3)'s written findings on the record. The original condition is current OR ongoing, not a requirement that both words separately be satisfied. Section (b)(4) preserves earlier modification or termination otherwise legally available.

Courts must promptly decide modification or termination motions. Under original (e), relief subject to a pending (b)(1) or (b)(2) motion is automatically stayed beginning on the thirtieth day after filing; for a motion under other law, the period begins on the 180th day. It ends when the court enters the final order deciding the motion. No qualifying filing is established here, and the Court creates none.

Consent decrees remain subject to subsection (a). Private agreements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct, and available state-law remedies for breach remain. The statute does not govern relief entered by a state court solely on state-law claims. These distinctions authorize no new settlement or termination in this case.

The present remand follows review of the decree and intervening law. It is not an automatic stay, a ruling on any termination motion or a finding that current necessary relief is unavailable.

##### VI. Continued use of a special master must satisfy the original statute

**Operative rule:** A special master in this prison-conditions remedy must be disinterested and objective, give due regard to public safety, and be warranted by a finding of sufficient remedial complexity; appointments, compensation, work and continued need must comply with section 3626(f)'s separate conditions below. Those requirements apply to the master's statutory role regardless of title and do not themselves establish misconduct or invalidate every prior act or accrued fee.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice Part IV coalition identified above; the Chief Justice agrees with the statutory disposition without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** When an appointment under (f) is necessary, the institution and plaintiffs each submit up to five names. Each side may strike up to three from the opposing list, and the court selects from the remaining names. A party may bring the prescribed interlocutory appeal on partiality. Compensation may not exceed the section 3006A hourly rate for appointed counsel, plus reasonably incurred costs; Judiciary appropriations supply payment.

The court must review continuing need every six months. Authorized hearings and proposed findings must be on the record; ex parte findings and communications are forbidden. A master may assist development of remedial plans and may be removed at any time, but service must end when the relief terminates. The statute is not evaded by calling a person exercising a master's powers something else.

The already ordered opportunity to challenge fees survives. Actual questions about accrued charges and temporal application require determination on remand; the Court orders no repayment and assumes no retrospective invalidity of all work. The district court may retain necessary lawful assistance only on the statutory conditions. No automatic new appointment follows from the complexity of the former decree.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817, and Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539:** Preserve meaningful access and effective methods rather than a constitutional library specification. Bounds is not overruled; four proposed categorical limits do not become Court law.
- **Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1:** Preserves choice among effective assistance arrangements without a general right to appointed counsel for every prisoner.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, and City of Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95:** Preserve personal injury, causation, appropriate prospective exposure and proof at the litigation stage. The Court does not adopt the four-Justice preliminary merits screen or decide standing in gross from a party concession.
- **Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78:** Applies reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, with actual examination and proper deference rather than automatic acceptance of a security assertion.
- **Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995:** Applies violation-linked repair while preserving implementation measures necessary to correct a proved wrong. Each means need not independently violate the Constitution.
- **Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367:** Preserves lawful responsive modification; the new statute's distinct mandatory requirements must also be applied.
- **Sandin v. Conner, June 19, 1995:** Preserves its actual rule for meaningful state-created prison liberty under genuine substantive constraints. No categorical hardship replacement is imported to exclude access to those claims.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc., June 30, 1994:** Preserves connection between remedial terms and their predicate; its speech-specific standard is not a universal least-restrictive-means rule or a substitute for section 3626.

No precedent is overruled. Gluth's earlier remedy supplies background and a proposed template, not a finding of statewide injury in this record.

#### Law After Decision

The controlling access-liability rule requires actual or evidence-supported imminent obstruction of a qualifying nonfrivolous claim, without requiring ultimate success or an impossible completed filing. Five Justices adopt that rule; only four would additionally make preliminary arguable-merits proof a jurisdictional screen, impose their subgroup-standing rule, or categorically confine affirmative Bounds capability to sentence/conditions claims and initial presentation. Those additional propositions are not law.

Effective assistance, security, represented scope and violation-linked relief remain distinct. Supported common proof may justify corresponding common relief, but the existing findings do not sustain this unrestricted statewide decree. The demonstrated access wrong survives. The original PLRA independently controls prospective relief, termination procedures, conditional stays and masters at the exact scopes above; enactment creates no motion or automatic dissolution.

The former decree's operational numbers are not constitutional minima. Its library-placement rule had a 150-person population-or-capacity trigger and exceptions for Aspen DWI, Flamenco, Papago DWI, Maricopa and Picacho; exempt facilities still owed access opportunities. Documented safety/security inability and medical determinations permitted postponement. Requested use was ten actual hours weekly with extra deadline time; opening hours varied between fifty without advance scheduling and sixty, seventy or eighty under population-based scheduling bands. Assistance was needs-based, and librarian qualifications included library-science, law or paralegal degrees.

The phone provision concerned three twenty-minute weekly calls to attorneys, designated representatives or legal organizations, with extra emergency calls, private unrecorded communications, payment or collect calling, exclusions for uncompleted calls and a documented-abuse qualification. These terms, copying and other asserted communications protections require their own supported remedial connection or properly presented independent legal basis. None is uniformly adopted or categorically prohibited today.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Scalia concurs, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. They would require actual or sufficiently imminent obstruction of a nonfrivolous claim as an Article III prerequisite, not merely as the substantive access rule adopted by five Justices. After trial a named plaintiff must prove personal injury from the type of administrative deficiency challenged; standing is not conferred in gross. ### Lewis v. United States, No. 95-6465

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 23, 1996; decided June 24, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 65 F.3d 252, affirming the Eastern District of New York's affirmance of two bench convictions.

Lewis faced two counts of obstructing the mail under 18 U.S.C. section 1701. The applicable punishment identified below for each count was up to six months' imprisonment, a $100 fine and a $10 assessment. He requested a jury. Before trial, the magistrate announced without qualification that imprisonment would not exceed six months under any circumstances and granted the Government's request for a bench trial. Lewis was convicted on February 25, 1994 and received concurrent three-year probation terms. The district court affirmed December 7, 1994; the Second Circuit affirmed September 5, 1995.

The questions are whether the joined charges' aggregate authorized imprisonment requires a jury and whether the advance limitation permits this nonjury adjudication. The jury demand was preserved; participation after its denial was not a waiver.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Ginsburg would reverse and remand for proceedings respecting the jury right.

The convictions and concurrent three-year probation terms remain in place. No retrial, acquittal, resentencing or remand is ordered. The completed sentence determination neither moots the jury question nor retroactively cures a jury right that existed at trial.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion announcing the judgment; plurality rationale | O'Connor | Scalia, Souter, Thomas | Affirms both convictions and concurrent probation; four-Justice rationale | Offense-specific nonaggregation; cap question reserved |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Kennedy | Breyer | Affirms the judgment on aggregate-liberty grounds | Aggregate-liberty protection; constitutional ceiling permits this bench trial |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stone-Zsela |  | Affirms the judgment based on the irrevocable advance cap | Aggregate exposure assessed first; actual irrevocable advance cap supports affirmance |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg | Would reverse and remand for jury-compliant proceedings | Jury right attaches to aggregate statutory exposure at prosecution; the judicial cap cannot remove it |

No rationale for affirmance commands five votes. A distinct, exact sentencing prohibition is supported by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer across their writings.

#### Holdings

##### The common rule forbids aggregate imprisonment above six months after this form of nonjury prosecution

**Operative rule:** In a single prosecution of joined petty statutory offenses, when the defendant's timely jury demand has not been waived, aggregate imprisonment exceeding six months may not be imposed after a nonjury trial. This prohibition does not establish majority agreement about when the jury right attaches, whether an advance sentencing cap permits trial without a jury, or what mechanism makes such a cap valid.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's concurrence, Kennedy's concurrence joined by Breyer, and Stevens's dissent joined by Ginsburg directly support this exact prohibition. These five votes establish the proposition, although they do not share a disposition or a complete theory of jury entitlement. The rule is not an aggregation of the seven votes to affirm or a synthesis of supposed narrower affirmance grounds under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Five Justices agree that dividing a prosecution into individually petty counts cannot permit aggregate imprisonment above six months following a nonjury trial over an unwaived timely jury demand. Their disagreement concerns the earlier consequences of authorized exposure and whether this advance limitation permits the convictions.

Kennedy and Breyer directly prohibit the greater aggregate term as the constitutional limit of such a nonjury trial. Stone requires an irrevocable limit before trial and forbids lifting it through disposition. Stevens and Ginsburg would require a jury from the commencement of this prosecution; their broader rule necessarily and expressly forbids imposing the same greater term following its nonjury adjudication. No supporter treats eventual leniency as a retrospective cure.

Codispoti's protection against aggregate serious confinement extends to this minimum prohibition for joined statutory petty counts. The writings do not agree that every feature of contempt procedure or its actual-sentence measure transfers to statutory offenses. Baldwin, Blanton and Nachtigal retain the independent rule for an individually serious offense and the complete additional-penalty inquiry. The present six-month imprisonment prohibition cannot be used to declare an otherwise serious offense petty.

Lewis received concurrent probation, so the judgment requires no enforcement remedy for a sentence exceeding this limit. Whether his jury entitlement nevertheless arose before trial divides the Justices. The Court's affirmance resolves his case without establishing a common rule validating caps or categorical nonaggregation.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Duncan v. Louisiana, 391 U.S. 145, and Baldwin v. New York, 399 U.S. 66:** Preserve the jury guarantee for serious offenses and the rule that a single offense authorizing more than six months' imprisonment is serious. The present fracture does not permit reducing an independently serious offense merely through promised leniency.
- **Blanton v. North Las Vegas, 489 U.S. 538, and United States v. Nachtigal, February 22, 1993:** Preserve the rebuttable petty-offense presumption. Additional authorized penalties, considered with maximum imprisonment, can rebut it when sufficiently severe to clearly reflect a legislative judgment of seriousness. Government-imposed penalties, including authorized fines and probation conditions, remain relevant; speculative private consequences do not supply the statutory seriousness inquiry. Neither case had decided this statutory aggregation question.
- **Codispoti v. Pennsylvania, 418 U.S. 506:** Its protection against aggregate serious confinement informs the five-Justice prohibition here. The four-Justice plurality would confine its aggregate actual-sentence analysis to the distinct contempt setting; that limitation does not command a majority.
- **Taylor v. Hayes, 418 U.S. 488:** Preserves the distinct contempt setting in which actual punishment can supply the measure absent a legislative maximum. Its treatment does not produce a majority statutory-cap doctrine here.
- **International Union, United Mine Workers of America v. Bagwell, June 30, 1994:** Preserves jury safeguards for serious criminal contempt and its limits concerning petty and direct contempt. It supplies no universal statutory aggregation or sentencing-cap rule.
- **United States v. Gaudin, June 19, 1995:** Preserves jury resolution of every element when the jury right applies. Its allocation holding does not settle the petty-offense threshold.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The exact five-Justice aggregate-imprisonment prohibition controls future cases within its stated conditions. The seven-Justice affirmance does not establish that joined petty offenses can never be aggregated. Nor does it establish a general rule that an advance cap removes the jury right or that the right always attaches to aggregate statutory exposure at the commencement of prosecution.

Each offense's six-month maximum remains presumptively, not conclusively, petty. The complete additional-penalty inquiry remains necessary; fines and probation are not categorically disregarded. The supported package here does not independently make either count serious. No rule about every collateral consequence, separately tried charge, charging strategy or contempt procedure is decided.

#### Separate Writings

**O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Souter and Thomas.**

The plurality would affirm because legislative seriousness is measured offense by offense. Each mail-obstruction count is petty under Blanton's full test, and joinder alone does not alter Congress's classification. The additional fine, assessment and probation package supplies no independent ground for treating either offense as serious.

Codispoti's aggregate actual-sentence analysis concerns contempt without a legislative maximum and the concentration of judicial power in that setting. Statutory crimes supply Congress's own seriousness measure. The plurality would therefore reject aggregation here without deciding whether the magistrate's cap was effective. The concurrent-sentence default does not establish that consecutive sentences were legally unavailable. This four-Justice rationale does not become Court law, and it announces no universal approval of prosecutorial evasion, new waiver or harmlessness rule.

**Kennedy's concurrence, joined by Breyer.**

Kennedy and Breyer reject a rule allowing unlimited cumulative imprisonment in one nonjury prosecution simply because each count is petty. In their view, a bench trial of joined petty charges constitutionally cannot yield aggregate imprisonment above six months. The magistrate's advance ruling made clear that this trial would remain within that limit; the constitutional analysis itself supplies the ceiling, without relying on an independent statutory cap.

They distinguish an individually serious offense, which carries legislative stigma and a jury right that a short-sentence promise cannot remove, from the aggregate liberty loss created by multiple petty counts. Codispoti and Taylor are not irrelevant merely because they involved contempt. Here the advance announcement supports affirmance; eventual probation is not its retrospective justification. Their reasoning supplies no finding of charging abuse or rule for every sequence of separate prosecutions.

**Stone-Zsela's concurrence.**

The Chief Justice first aggregates the exposure and then asks whether a lawful, irrevocable limitation actually removes serious punishment from the prosecution. The jury guarantee concerns the punishment the accused legally faces, not the labels on the counts.

Here the magistrate's unqualified announcement before trial operates with the constitutional prohibition on greater aggregate nonjury imprisonment. It binds the court through disposition and cannot be lifted after the jury demand is denied. That constitutional limit supplies enforceability; no separate statutory reduction, sentencing order or waiver is assumed. The other applicable penalties do not independently establish seriousness under Blanton.

An informal promise, revocable prediction, limitation imposed only after trial or cap omitting independently serious punishment would fail this analysis. Stone does not join categorical nonaggregation or rely on the probation actually imposed to cure a prior denial. His requirement of an effective advance cap remains distinct from Kennedy and Breyer's complete rationale.

**Stevens's dissent, joined by Ginsburg.**

Stevens and Ginsburg would reverse and remand because the joined prosecution authorized a year of imprisonment. The legislative measure remains controlling, but it must be applied to the prosecution as a whole. Blanton's single-offense facts did not require choosing between the offense and prosecution as the relevant unit.

Codispoti supports aggregation of serious confinement. Where contempt lacks a legislative maximum, actual punishment supplies a proxy; that feature does not confine the jury's protection to contempt. The right here attached when prosecution began. A judge's promise of leniency cannot alter the statutory exposure or remove that attached right, and the eventual probation terms do not cure its denial.

The dissent rejects the proposed distinction between the stigma of one serious offense and the serious liberty exposure produced by several petty counts. Its broader entitlement necessarily prohibits aggregate imprisonment above six months following this nonjury trial, but its proposed remedy is more extensive: reversal for lawful jury-compliant proceedings. It orders no acquittal and makes no finding about guilt.

Its discussion of the cap was unnecessary to its nonaggregation ground. The announcement is a fact; its legal force is the question on which the writings differ.

[Blanton, 489 U.S. 538](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep489/usrep489538/usrep489538.pdf), supplies the complete petty-offense presumption and additional-penalty qualifications. No separately enacted cap, independent sentencing order, new waiver or legislative reduction is established by the record.

The judgment sustaining both convictions and concurrent probation stands, subject to ordinary mandate procedures. No further lower-court proceeding or new sentence is directed. No waiver, completed probation, future violation or later cap modification is found.

### United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, Nos. 95-345 and 95-346

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 17, 1996; decided June 24, 1996. The Court reviews the Sixth Circuit's reversal of Ursery's criminal conviction and the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the companion civil forfeiture judgment. The question is whether the forfeitures impose a second criminal punishment forbidden by the Double Jeopardy Clause.

Ursery paid $13,250 to settle the claim against his home under 21 U.S.C. section 881(a)(7). The consent judgment entered May 24, 1993, payment followed June 17, and his jury trial began June 30. He was convicted of manufacturing marijuana and sentenced to 63 months. The indictment preceded completion of the settlement; the trial followed it. The preserved double-jeopardy objection concerns that sequence, not an appeal from the settlement itself.

The companion has the opposite sequence: criminal convictions preceded civil summary judgment on a previously filed complaint under sections 881(a)(6) and 981(a)(1)(A). That complaint covers overlapping property categories; not every disputed asset is established to be unlawful proceeds.

Section 881(a)(6) reaches things furnished or intended in exchange for controlled substances in violation of the federal drug subchapter, proceeds traceable to such an exchange, and money, negotiable instruments and securities used or intended to facilitate a violation of that subchapter. Section 881(a)(7) reaches real property used or intended to commit or facilitate a violation of that subchapter punishable by more than one year. Each protects an owner's interest where that owner establishes the relevant act or omission occurred without the owner's knowledge or consent. Section 981(a)(1)(A) reaches property involved in, or traceable to property involved in, violations of sections 5313(a) or 5324(a) of title 31, or sections 1956 or 1957 of title 18. Its separate section 5313(a) exception bars seizure or forfeiture for that violation by a domestic financial institution examined by a federal bank supervisory agency, a financial institution regulated by the Securities and Exchange Commission, or a partner, director or employee of either. Section 981(a)(2) protects the interest of an owner or lienholder who establishes the relevant act or omission occurred without that person's knowledge. These distinct exceptions remain intact. Both statutes incorporate customs procedures only insofar as applicable and consistent with their own terms.

#### Holdings

##### I. These civil forfeiture proceedings do not constitute a second criminal punishment

**Operative rule:** For forfeiture under sections 881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A), the court first determines whether Congress intended civil proceedings; that intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. These schemes and the grounds established here do not meet that demanding contrary-proof standard, so the challenged forfeitures supply no double-jeopardy bar to the respective later proceeding; neither a civil label nor an action against property creates an irrebuttable exemption.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt both holdings and their complete explanations; additional judgment votes do not enlarge their rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Ward and 89 Firearms require attention first to legislative design and then to genuinely contrary punitive operation. Congress's incorporation of customs forfeiture procedures, civil notice and claim mechanisms, and statutory proof arrangements establishes civil intent. The qualified incorporation and different owner defenses retain the terms described above; none must be treated as an admission that the proceeding prosecutes the claimant for a crime.

The statutes serve substantial nonpunitive functions: removing property used to facilitate unlawful activity, discouraging its unlawful use, and recovering actual illicit proceeds. Forfeiture may burden an owner and deter wrongdoing. Those features, a connection to a criminal violation, and an owner defense do not by themselves supply the clearest proof that the proceeding is criminal. A predicate showing criminal use of property is not necessarily a charge that this claimant personally committed the offense.

The contrary argument treats the real loss of a lawful home and the settlement's amount as conclusive punishment. That ignores the statutory operation and the demanding second inquiry. The exception remains real, but Halper's comparison of a fixed personal civil penalty with governmental loss is not the method for assigning a compensatory dollar value to every forfeiture. Forfeiture's permissible functions extend beyond reimbursement of enforcement costs. Halper remains effective in its own setting; Kurth Ranch retains its examination of the extraordinary tax's combined punitive operation. Neither makes every civil burden connected with crime a second criminal punishment.

This classification removes the grounds on which the two appellate courts disposed of the cases. It does not establish each asset's statutory connection, provenance, ownership or constitutional excessiveness. Because criminal punishment has not been established under this test, the Court need not decide whether a punitive civil proceeding itself places a person in jeopardy, whether the offenses coincide under Blockburger and retained Grady, or whether coordinated proceedings may be treated as one. Settlement waiver likewise supplies no holding.

##### II. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy punishment

**Operative rule:** A forfeiture's coverage by the Excessive Fines Clause does not, without the separate civil-intent and clearest-proof inquiry, establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy purposes. Austin's protection and any properly presented excessiveness objection remain effective; today's classification decides neither excessiveness nor an exclusive test for measuring it.

**Controlling explanation:** Austin holds that the covered statutory forfeitures are sufficiently punitive to require review under the Excessive Fines Clause despite their civil form and concurrent remedial aims. That clause guards against excessive exactions; it does not require the identical classification used to decide whether the Government has imposed a second criminal punishment. Applying each protection to its own question preserves both.

The argument that Austin automatically establishes a double-jeopardy violation collapses those distinct inquiries. Ward and 89 Firearms supply the criminal-classification test; Austin supplies fines coverage and leaves excessiveness for its own examination. The Court therefore does not withdraw Austin, impose an instrumentality-only rule or declare the companion forfeiture nonexcessive.

James Daniel Good's requirement of meaningful prior process for real-property seizure, subject to its demonstrated exigency exception, likewise addresses a different protection. Bennis protects the separately held interest of the innocent automobile owner who proves nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention; it does not decide these culpable-owner successive-punishment claims. Libretti's statutory and third-party limits also survive. The Government must still establish lawful forfeiture under the applicable provisions and answer independently available objections.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242:** Applies legislative-intent and demanding contrary-proof analysis; civil designation is relevant but not conclusive.
- **United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354:** Applies the civil-forfeiture classification test, including the clearest-proof exception, rather than categorical immunity for proceedings against property.
- **Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez, 372 U.S. 144:** Preserves consideration of punitive purpose and effect; no individual indicator is conclusive and no new mandatory exhaustive checklist is announced.
- **United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435:** Preserves its exceptional fixed-personal-civil-penalty accounting rule, including reasonable remedial approximation, while distinguishing forfeiture's broader nonpunitive functions. It is not overruled or converted into universal asset-value accounting.
- **Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, June 6, 1994:** Preserves the combined-operation punishment holding for the extraordinary arrest-linked assessment on confiscated contraband; no universal compensation ceiling follows.
- **Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993:** Preserves Excessive Fines coverage and separate excessiveness review. Its partly punitive coverage rule does not automatically answer the Double Jeopardy question.
- **Witte v. United States, June 14, 1995:** Preserves the distinction between consideration of conduct and an independent punishment, and its separate coordination limits; coordination cannot cure an otherwise established successive-prosecution bar.
- **Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299; Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508; United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992; and United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993:** Preserve necessary-offense and essential-conduct rules and the distinct-agreement limitation. The Court does not reach their application here and does not replace retained Grady with an elements-only rule.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, December 13, 1993:** Preserves notice and meaningful opportunity before civil seizure of real property; exigency requires showing that less restrictive measures would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use, with appropriate subsequent process.
- **Bennis v. Michigan, March 4, 1996:** Preserves its stated innocent-owner protection; no qualifying innocent-owner finding is established here.
- **Libretti v. United States, November 7, 1995:** Preserves statutory authority and third-party limits despite agreement; it supplies no settlement waiver in these cases.

No precedent is overruled. The separate writings' broader or contrary theories are not the Court's treatment of precedent.

#### Law After Decision

The five-Justice civil-intent/clearest-proof rule governs classification under the three forfeiture provisions. The two categorical double-jeopardy barriers are removed, while the statutory requirements and separate constitutional safeguards continue to govern the remands.

Halper remains effective for its fixed personal civil penalty setting, including reasonable remedial approximation; that accounting inquiry does not govern these forfeitures. Austin remains controlling on its fines questions. Existing innocent-owner protection, prior seizure process, retained Grady and the prohibition on using coordination to cure a genuine successive-prosecution bar are unchanged. #### Separate Writings

Justice Kennedy concurs and joins the Court opinion in full. His Halper concurrence calls for objective examination rather than a label. He acknowledges that an owner can suffer punishment while distinguishing property-use accountability from a second personal punishment for the owner's criminal offense. The property may have been used unlawfully by someone else; the civil claim need not charge this owner with that crime. The distinct statutory defenses remain important. His explanation supplements the Court's classification and does not turn its reserved Grady or Blockburger questions into categorical rulings.

Justice Scalia concurs in both judgments, joined by Justice Thomas, and joins no part of the Court opinion. Their Kurth Ranch and Witte positions understand the Double Jeopardy Clause as limiting successive criminal prosecution, rather than every authorized civil consequence with a punitive effect. These proceedings are civil even under the Ward and Mendoza-Martinez approach. Their criticism of the broader punishment account does not itself overrule Halper or Kurth Ranch. They leave property-specific statutory and Excessive Fines objections to their own rules.

Justice Stevens concurs in reversal in Currency in Part I of his separate opinion. Taking genuine illicit proceeds removes unlawfully derived gain, unlike extracting an ordinarily lawful home from its owner. This principle defeats a categorical bar that would protect genuine proceeds. It does not establish that every disputed asset is proceeds, validate each statutory claim or resolve the other appellate objections.

In Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Justice Stevens would affirm in Ursery. Ward's dissenting analysis, Halper and Kurth Ranch support examining what this offense-conditioned $13,250 exaction from legitimate property did to its owner. Austin reinforces attention to real operation but its fines coverage is not alone dispositive. The completed punitive settlement was first jeopardy; the indictment's earlier filing was not trial jeopardy. Under the necessary-offense reasoning illustrated by Harris and Vitale, the manufacturing offense actually incorporated in the forfeiture predicate cannot be prosecuted again merely because the forfeiture also required a property connection. Retained Dixon and Grady reinforce that conclusion; other possible statutory predicates do not change the one actually punished. Parallel commencement did not merge separate proceedings and judgments. Consent resolved the property claim, not the legality of a second punishment. The dissent does not classify every civil settlement as jeopardy or every regulatory sanction as punishment.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela separately dissents from the precise disposition and scope of remand in Currency. The complaint's proceeds and money-laundering labels cannot prove the character of every asset. His proposed inquiry concerns punishment, attachment, offense identity and separate proceedings where they become material; none substitutes for another. He would neither order automatic recovery nor confer blanket immunity on legitimate property, and prescribes no mandatory new accounting procedure. He otherwise joins Stevens's Ursery dissent as stated above.

### Settle v. Dickson County School Board

Decision, June 25, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided June 25, 1996, on review under 28 U.S.C. section 1254(1) of the Sixth Circuit's May 8, 1995 judgment, No. 93-6207, 53 F.3d 152, affirming defendants' summary judgment on the student's free-speech claim.

During March 1991, ninth-grade teacher Dana Ramsey assigned a paper teaching research, synthesis and writing. Students could choose or change subjects on a list through March 22; later changes required permission, and outlines were due March 26. Brittney Settle first listed Drama, changed over the weekend, and tendered The Life of Jesus Christ without prior discussion or permission. Ramsey rejected it and the later April 3 proposal, A Scientific and Historical Approach to Jesus Christ. Settle received zero after declining a different assignment. The petition additionally reports a later completed paper tendered without being read; its full contents are not reproduced in the cited materials.

The six reported explanations remain distinct:

1. Settle had not obtained advance permission for the change.
2. Her strong belief might prevent dispassionate research or make ordinary criticism of grammar and organization seem criticism of religion.
3. Personal religious beliefs were considered inappropriate classroom dialogue.
4. Her existing knowledge might permit a quick outline without new research.
5. Ramsey believed law prohibited religious issues in class.
6. She believed sources about Jesus ultimately derived from the Bible and failed the four-source requirement.

The district memorandum reports a following-day opportunity to submit another topic without penalty and reproduces Ramsey's statement that procedure and topic both contributed. The petition reproduces her answer that she would also have rejected the topic as an original choice, adding the same answer for Buddha or Muhammed. Actual source permissions included two books and two encyclopedias or resources, magazine or pamphlet substitutes, and outside materials producible on request.

The question is whether those grounds and the contrary evidence permit summary judgment under genuine pedagogical limits and ordinary causation rules. No independent religion-clause or equal-protection claim is decided.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Further proceedings must apply the genuine-pedagogy and causation rules below. The Court does not require acceptance of a topic, academic credit, reading or grading of a paper, removal of the zero, damages, a trial or a particular discovery process. Properly presented capacity, immunity, municipal-responsibility and remedial questions remain open.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the curricular-speech rule, summary-judgment application, causation allocation and bounded remand. No separate opinion is filed.

#### Holdings

##### I. Curricular authority must rest on genuine, legitimate pedagogy

**Operative rule:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the rule, explanation and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazelwood preserves substantial authority over the lesson taught, maturity, quality, research and method. Tinker does not entitle a student to replace a genuine assignment with expression of her own choosing. The Court applies that pedagogical principle here without assuming that a paper submitted only to a teacher carries public school endorsement or creates an open forum.

Lamb's Chapel, Rosenberger and Good News distinguish legitimate program boundaries from exclusion of an otherwise admitted religious perspective. Their forums and benefits differ from this graded assignment; they inform the distinction without requiring acceptance of every religious topic. A school may select the knowledge, method or perspective taught and distinguish objective inquiry from personal testimony or devotional advocacy. Today's rule does not impose universal viewpoint neutrality on every curricular selection.

Epperson preserves educational authority while denying constitutional immunity to curriculum decisions and distinguishing academic study from religious instruction. Lee and Sherman likewise do not make all consideration of religion unlawful. The asserted blanket legal prohibition therefore cannot be justified simply as an honest academic mistake. Ordinary genuine factual or evaluative errors may remain within legitimate pedagogical discretion; a legally impermissible categorical rule is different.

The complete board policy permits objective, respectful academic treatment, including religious literature, music, drama and art intrinsic to learning, with balanced, comprehensive thematic treatment that neither promotes tenets nor demeans belief. It also accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief in responses and assignments. Both parts matter. Neither establishes automatic topic approval or substitutes a policy dispute for the presented federal claim.

##### II. The affirmative record leaves a material causal dispute

**Operative rule:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The approval violation is supported, and no religious exemption excuses it. But the documented next-day alternative without penalty, the mixed explanation and the original-choice answer permit dispute over whether that violation independently governed the result. The answer concerning Buddha and Muhammed matters in full; it does not establish exclusively anti-Christian hostility.

The concern about dispassionate research and ordinary criticism can be academic, but the student's faith alone does not conclusively prove inability to research or accept evaluation. The classroom-dialogue explanation may distinguish objective work from testimony, or may exclude religious perspective as such. Familiarity can justify requiring new inquiry, but does not itself establish that this project involved none. These applications and their causal roles remain disputed.

The asserted legal ban is not governing law. The Bible-source explanation must be assessed against actual permission for secondary sources, substitutes and producible outside materials. A primary-source-only or school-library-only rule cannot be added to sustain judgment. Other accepted religiously inflected subjects provide relevant context, but their described content does not establish identical method, source, belief or approval circumstances.

Waters preserves the distinction between a genuine permissible account and an explanation merely available after the fact. Its full employment balance and investigation framework do not govern school assignments. Mt. Healthy likewise distinguishes actual motivating cause from a proved same-decision defense: identifying authority to make a lawful decision does not prove that decision would have occurred, while an impermissible factor does not defeat a properly proved defense automatically.

The strongest affirmance argument preserves genuine objective-research topic control. The Court accepts that authority but cannot treat its disputed application as established here. The affirmative testimony and permissions warrant vacatur; incomplete source reproduction is not evidence of litigant default or an independent reason for relief. The Court finds neither pretext nor final liability and leaves ordinary adjudication of remaining grounds available.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The legitimate-pedagogy principle now has this bounded application to a teacher-only research assignment: instructional authority remains substantial, but it does not insulate a categorical religious reason or conclusively resolve a supported dispute about actual cause. Neither content selection nor a student's religious commitment decides the claim by itself.

Courts must preserve genuine academic requirements, apply ordinary summary-judgment inferences and maintain Mt. Healthy's separate burdens. They do not manage every research judgment, require perfect factual accuracy or impose an independent constitutional duty to investigate each proposed topic. The decision establishes no general religious-topic entitlement, classroom forum, curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule, compelled grade or final liability.

### Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc., Nos. 95-754 and 95-886

Decision, June 26, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 23, 1996; decided June 26, 1996. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 56 F.3d 1335, reviewing dismissal of a Florida action removed on diversity grounds.

The question is whether the Medical Device Amendments displace the Lohrs' negligent-design, negligent-manufacture, failure-to-warn and strict-liability claims concerning a pacemaker lead cleared through substantial equivalence. The Court separately considers what counts as a state requirement, whether a relevant federal requirement applies, whether the state obligation differs from it or adds to it, and whether a genuinely equal duty becomes different because state law supplies damages.

The Model 4011 lead received substantial-equivalence clearance in 1982, without premarket approval. The FDA's November 30 letter permitted marketing subject to general controls and did not endorse safety. Lora Lohr received the pacemaker in 1987; failure in December 1990 required emergency surgery. Her physician identified a likely lead defect. The alleged design, manufacture and warning failures, and their causal consequences, have not been adjudicated.

The Eleventh Circuit restored negligent design and strict liability based on dangerous design, while sustaining preemption of negligent manufacture, failure to warn and strict-liability contentions based on manufacture or labeling. Medtronic challenges the restoration in No. 95-754; the Lohrs challenge the adverse preemption rulings in No. 95-886. Michael Lohr's consortium claim follows the underlying claims. The separate warranty dismissal was not appealed.

#### Holdings

##### I. Substantial-equivalence clearance does not supply the asserted design approval

**Operative rule:** Section 360k(a) requires an applicable federal requirement and a state requirement respecting a human-use device that differs from or adds to it and concerns safety, effectiveness or another matter included in the federal requirement, subject to subsection (b). This lead's substantial-equivalence clearance does not establish substantive federal approval of its design and therefore does not preempt the restored negligent-design or design-based strict-liability claims on that asserted ground.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by all nine Justices named in the topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Permission to market is not the substantive safety determination Medtronic's design argument requires. Section 510(k), codified at section 360(k), requires advance notification and information about classification and compliance with applicable standards or approval requirements. It does not itself declare the notified design safe. Section 807.97 expressly separates a substantial-equivalence determination from official approval. The 1982 letter likewise did not endorse safety.

The separate premarket-approval system examines safety and effectiveness under its own statutory predicates. Class III status does not establish that this device underwent that process. Nor does grandfathering supply a federal judgment selecting its design. Part 820's requirement that manufacture conform to an original design or authorized changes ensures manufacturing conformance; it does not itself make that design the FDA's substantive choice.

Easterwood requires the actual federal predicate, not regulatory overlap. Freightliner likewise rejects inventing a protected federal choice where the asserted operative standard is absent. Their distinct statutory language remains intact. Here, the presence of real federal controls does not fill the missing design comparator.

The Court therefore affirms the design restoration. It decides no defect, breach or causal issue, no claim involving an actual premarket approval, and no hypothetical device-specific standard. No independently established actual conflict arises merely from this clearance. The surviving claims remain subject to their state-law elements.

##### II. An equal conduct duty is not made additional merely by a damages remedy

**Operative rule:** Section 360k(a) does not expressly preempt an independently available state duty that genuinely requires the same conduct as the applicable federal duty merely because state law adds damages. Requiring negligence or an unreasonable hazard in addition to violation of that same conduct obligation can narrow recovery without making the substantive duty different; a claim requiring different warnings or production conduct is not parallel merely because its pleading invokes federal law.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress required a difference or addition in the obligation that the State imposes. A remedy for violating the same obligation does not supply that difference. The lower court therefore erred in treating the prospect of damages as sufficient to bar an equal-duty theory. Additional state-law conditions for recovery, such as negligence or an unreasonable hazard, do not broaden the conduct requirement when a violation of the same federal obligation remains necessary.

Section 808.1(d)(2) expressly preserves equal or substantially identical requirements. The comparison concerns how the duty actually operates, not identical words alone. A theory permitting liability despite satisfaction of the identified federal conduct obligation, or demanding additional warning content or changed production conduct, cannot establish equality by calling itself parallel. Cipollone's claim-specific approach similarly requires attention to the duty supporting liability rather than the pleading label.

The Eleventh Circuit reached this argument despite its first appearance in reply, so no forfeiture bars review. It assumed Florida recognition and adequate pleading without deciding them. Those questions, actual violation, causal connection and available relief remain for ordinary proceedings.

This holding creates no private action directly enforcing the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. Section 337's enforcement allocation and any independently presented question under it remain distinct from the express-preemption issue decided here. Freightliner also preserves inquiry into an independently established actual conflict; no such conflict is proved merely by regulatory overlap or an additional remedy. The categorical parallel-duty barrier is reversed.

##### III. The asserted manufacturing and warning rules do not displace these claims

**Operative rule:** The general manufacturing and prescription-information requirements invoked here do not establish a federal counterpart that makes the presented ordinary manufacturing and warning duties different or additional within section 360k(a). The challenged preemption barriers are therefore reversed for negligent manufacture, failure to warn and corresponding strict-liability theories, without exempting all tort law or all claims involving generally applicable regulations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Part 820 and section 801.109 impose enforceable obligations. Describing them as general does not make them advice or establish that no federal law governs this lead. But legal force and common subject matter do not by themselves identify the substantive federal choice that these state duties would alter.

The manufacturing provisions govern quality assurance, component acceptance, production controls, inspection, records, labeling and related operations. They leave substantial choices to manufacturers; maintaining a device master record and conforming production to the original design do not establish substantive FDA approval of that design. The warning rules require practitioner information with their own qualifications. Submission of proposed labels under section 807.87 does not establish substantive approval of their precise contents.

The contrary argument would treat these applicable commands as necessarily sufficient to displace any further state assessment of manufacture or warning. It omits the required comparison of actual obligations. Neither the negligence label nor the strict-liability label resolves that comparison. Freightliner supplies the need to identify the operative federal choice; Travelers reinforces examination of the particular statutory connection rather than unlimited effects from shared subject matter.

The result is confined to the asserted federal predicates and the duties presented. A generally framed rule can contain a precise applicable command; the Court imposes no universal condition that a regulation name one manufacturer or model. An actually ambiguous pleaded duty may require identification in ordinary proceedings, but every products case need not begin with a new evidentiary hearing. Regulatory compliance, liability and a different approval pathway remain undecided.

##### IV. A common-law damages duty can be a preemptible requirement

**Operative rule:** A state obligation enforced through common-law damages can constitute a requirement under section 360k(a); common law has no categorical exemption from the express clause. Inclusion alone does not establish preemption: the applicable federal obligation, actual difference or addition, subject connection and statutory exceptions must still be satisfied.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer. These are five direct votes on this proposition despite their different positions on Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** A damages judgment can require a manufacturer to change its conduct as effectively as an enacted command. The statutory word requirement does not cease to apply because a court enforces the obligation. Cipollone's covered common-law warning duties illustrate that distinction, while its different statutory clauses and separate surviving categories caution against treating inclusion as universal displacement.

Section 808.1(b) also recognizes requirements having legal force through court decisions. That provision is consistent with the enacted term. It does not eliminate the rest of the statutory comparison or turn every state tort rule into a forbidden device requirement.

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would leave the general question open because these claims survive on narrower grounds. The Court decides the limited inclusion question but no hypothetical future application. Part IV does not itself preempt a surviving Lohr claim, establish a new balancing test, or make the dissenters' manufacturing/warning application the Court's rule. The direct majority for inclusion and the direct majority rejecting preemption on this record answer different questions.

##### V. The FDA's formal interpretation independently supports the bounded manufacturing and warning result

**Operative rule:** Within the statutory comparison, the FDA reasonably interprets section 360k through section 808.1(d) to require specific counterpart regulations or other specific applicable requirements before divergent state requirements are displaced. Giving that formal construction substantial weight independently supports survival of these manufacturing and warning claims, while preserving its general-applicability, equal-duty and divergent substantive-application qualifications.

**Authority:** Part V, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This independent ground has five direct supporters; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** The FDA administers the device scheme, exercises the express exemption responsibility in section 360k(b), and has section 371(a)'s authority to issue regulations for efficient enforcement. Those responsibilities support consideration of its formal interpretation of the relation between federal and state requirements. MCI first requires adherence to statutory boundaries; Sweet Home and Smiley then give force to reasonable interpretation within actual delegated responsibility. No agency receives authority to contradict Congress.

Section 808.1 reads the federal and state requirements together. Its specificity provision identifies a meaningful counterpart; its examples preserve general requirements reaching products beyond devices and genuinely equal obligations. These terms reasonably support the conclusion that the asserted generic controls do not displace the ordinary duties here. They are read with subsection (d)(6)(ii), which permits preemption when a generally worded adulteration or misbranding prohibition actually imposes a divergent substantive requirement on a specific device. Labels alone therefore protect neither side.

The objection that this construction adds an impermissible condition gives insufficient weight to the ambiguity in the required federal/state comparison and the agency's responsible administration of that boundary. But the construction is not a universal individual-model requirement. Nor does it establish automatic deference to every litigating position or agency pronouncement about preemption. Smiley assumed without deciding that courts always independently determine statutory preemption; its substantive banking interpretation supplies no free-standing FDA power to preempt. The Court decides this reasonable formal construction and application, not every possible device regulation or state duty.

##### Operative statutory and regulatory qualifications

These qualifications govern the foregoing holdings and remand; the descriptions of regulatory text do not enlarge a Justice's joins to Parts IV or V.

- **Separate state-requirement exemption:** Section 360k(b) permits the Secretary, on a State or political subdivision's application, after notice and opportunity for an oral hearing, to exempt a requirement by regulation on prescribed conditions if it is more stringent than the otherwise applicable federal requirement, or if compelling local conditions require it and compliance would not violate an applicable federal requirement. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second alternative. An exemption does not remove federal obligations. No pertinent exemption is established; an equal, nonpreempted duty does not first require one.
- **Section 808.1's complete scope:** Subsection (b) includes force-of-law court decisions. Subsection (d)(1) preserves general requirements whose purpose reaches other products as well as devices, including general electrical codes and the Uniform Commercial Code's fitness warranty, and unfair-trade rules not limited to devices. Subsection (d)(2) preserves equal or substantially identical requirements. Subsection (d)(6)(ii) preserves general adulteration/misbranding prohibitions but displaces an actual divergent substantive device requirement; interpretation and enforcement, rather than literal wording alone, control that comparison.
- **Other section 808.1 qualifications:** Professional-practice permits, licensing, registration, certification and related approval remain subject to conflicting federal restrictions under sections 520(e) or (g), which prevail. State procurement specifications and Medicaid or similar payment criteria are preserved. General enforcement, inspections, registration and licensing remain subject to conflicting federal records, reporting and good-manufacturing-practice rules under sections 519 and 520(f), which prevail. Administrative delegations and related matters, measures solely raising revenue or charging fees, and the identified atomic-energy and radiation measures remain outside preemption on their stated terms; the last category lasts until applicable specific FDA requirements intervene. Subsection (e) assigns the FDA a determination role through prescribed procedures subject to judicial review, without establishing an exhaustion bar here. Subsections (c) and (f) preserve applicable federal duties whether or not a state requirement is exempt or preempted. No unestablished factual exception is applied.
- **Original manufacturing regime:** Part 820 applies to finished human-use devices and defines a finished device to include an accessory suitable for use whether packaged or labeled. It is not generally directed at manufacturers of components or parts; blood and blood components have Part 606's separate treatment. The everyday description of this lead as a component does not settle that classification. Additional critical-device duties retain their own applicability: the definition addresses an implant or life-supporting device whose failure when properly used can reasonably be expected to cause significant injury, with identification by the Commissioner after advisory consultation. Class III status alone is not a substitute finding. Where simultaneous compliance with applicable regulations is impossible, the specific device regulation supersedes the general regulation. No exemption, variance or violation is found here.
- **Real manufacturing obligations:** The original provisions require appropriate quality assurance, trained personnel, suitable premises and equipment, controlled component acceptance and handling, written manufacturing specifications and processing controls, inspection and failure investigation, labeling integrity, protective packaging, distribution controls and records. Section 820.100 requires manufacturing conformance to the original design or approved changes, with controlled and documented specification and process changes; it does not itself approve the design's safety. Critical-device additions, including component sampling, operation verification, labeling, distribution and history records, depend on their actual applicability. The Court decides no breach of any particular provision.
- **Distinct manufacturing exemption or variance:** Section 360j(f)(2) permits a subject person to petition in the prescribed form with required information. An exemption requires a determination that compliance is unnecessary to assure safety, effectiveness and statutory compliance. A variance requires a determination that the proposed replacement methods, facilities and controls for manufacture, packing, storage and installation are sufficient to assure safety, effectiveness and compliance with the Act; an approval order must impose conditions necessary to assure those ends. Optional advisory referral requires recommendations within sixty days; the Secretary must approve or deny within sixty days after submission or, if referred, after expiration of the committee's sixty-day period, whichever is later. An informal hearing is available after the order. These provisions differ from a State's section 360k(b) exemption, and no granted exception is inferred.
- **Prescription information:** Section 801.109 conditionally exempts a device unsafe without licensed-practitioner supervision from consumer adequate-directions requirements. The device must be in the possession of a person, or that person's agents or employees, regularly and lawfully engaged in its manufacture, transportation, storage or wholesale or retail distribution, or in the possession of a practitioner licensed by law to use or order it; it must be sold only to or on the prescription or other order of such a practitioner for professional use. Subsection (b)'s prescription caution and method-of-use label requirements exclude surgical instruments. Subsection (c) requires dispensing-package information about indications, effects, routes, methods, frequency and duration, relevant hazards, contraindications, side effects and precautions for intended and advertised uses. That information may be omitted only when commonly known to licensed practitioners; on written request stating reasonable grounds, the Commissioner will offer an opinion on proposed omission. No omission qualification is found here.
- **Separate labeling exceptions:** Section 801.109(d) independently requires adequate use information on manufacturer, packer or distributor labeling that furnishes or purports to furnish use information, whether or not within the dispensing package. Its exception covers a reminder piece calling attention only to the device name without indications or other use information. Subsection (e)'s issuance-or-revision-date duty excludes labels and cartons. These exceptions do not merge with subsection (c)'s permissive omission or subsection (b)'s surgical-instrument exception. Section 807.87(e)'s submission of proposed labels, labeling and advertisements is not substantive approval of their contents.
- **Clearance, classification and approval:** Section 360(k)'s report ordinarily precedes intended interstate commercial introduction by at least ninety days. Classification, substantial equivalence and premarket approval remain distinct. Section 360e(a) requires approval for a class III device subject to a subsection (b) regulation or classified under section 360c(f), unless exempt under section 360j(g). Section 360e(b) supplies the rulemaking route for pre-1976 devices and their substantially equivalent types; no such approval is found for this lead. PMA's distinct evaluation and application requirements do not become clearance requirements by inference. The Court decides neither a different approval pathway nor a particular investigational exemption.
- **Temporal limits:** The 1994 Code and April 1, 1996 CFR are text sources, not findings that every amendment applied to the 1982 clearance, 1987 implantation or 1990 injury. The 1990 codification requires the same intended use and a Secretary's order finding either the same technological characteristics or, for different characteristics, submitted information—including clinical data if the Secretary deems it necessary—demonstrating that the device is as safe and effective as a legally marketed device and raises no different questions of safety and efficacy than the predicate. Different technological characteristics means a significant change in materials, design, energy source or other device features. A predicate removed from the market at the Secretary's initiative or judicially determined adulterated or misbranded cannot establish substantial equivalence. That later text is not backdated; the lower court assumed that it described earlier practice. Later submission amendments and design-validation language create no earlier approval or breach. The original Part 820 regime governs the comparison, and no later quality-system revision enters the decision. An actual conduct-date duty remains to be identified for liability.
- **Savings and federal enforcement:** Section 360h(d) preserves liability despite compliance with an order under that section; it is not an unrestricted saving clause for every device action. When damages for economic loss enforce such liability, the value of a remedy supplied under the order must be taken into account. No such order or offset is adjudicated. Section 337 reserves direct enforcement to the United States except for its State food-enforcement provision. A State may bring proceedings in its own name and jurisdiction to civilly enforce, or restrain violations of, sections 341 and 343(b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (k), (q) and (r), only for food located in the State. Proceedings may not begin until thirty days after notice to the Secretary, or until ninety days after notice if the Secretary begins an informal or formal enforcement action concerning the food within the first thirty days. Proceedings are barred while the Secretary diligently prosecutes a court proceeding concerning the food or after settlement of that proceeding or the related informal or formal enforcement action; the State may intervene as of right in the described court proceeding. This exception creates no private device action. No unpresented standalone enforcement defense or later implied-preemption rule is decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992:** Its actual covered common-law warning proposition supports identifying the duty imposed by liability. Its distinct statutory clauses and category-specific surviving grounds remain intact; no whole amended-Act opinion or blanket tort exemption is imported. Part IV applies the inclusion principle to section 360k through its own text.
- **CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood, April 21, 1993:** Supplies the requirement of the actual applicable federal predicate and subject comparison. Its railroad-specific substantial-subsumption language, warning predicates, local-hazard qualification and speed holding are not substituted for the Medical Device Amendments.
- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick, April 18, 1995:** Applies the distinction between an operative federal requirement and regulatory absence or overlap, and preserves independently established actual-conflict analysis. Its reserved common-law question under another statute and its motor-vehicle saving clause do not decide this clause.
- **New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co., April 26, 1995:** Supports examining the connection required by the particular statute instead of unlimited displacement from indirect effects. Its ERISA holding becomes no general exemption for state health or tort law.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T, June 17, 1994:** Requires independent adherence to statutory limits before agency deference. The FDA may not dispense with Congress's comparison or create unlimited preemption authority.
- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837; Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Preserve reasonable agency interpretation within enacted ambiguity and actual delegated responsibility. Part V applies that bounded principle to the formal FDA construction without adopting every agency view or enlarging the statutory exemption power.
- **Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., June 3, 1996:** Preserves statutory construction before reasonable delegated interpretation, and distinguishes substantive agency responsibility from free-standing power to preempt. Its specific banking permission and qualified use of interpretation for antecedent transactions do not supply substantive approval or retroactive duties for this device.

#### Law After Decision

Section 360k(a) can reach common-law damages duties, but it does not categorically displace them. Courts must identify the actual state obligation, an applicable federal requirement, the difference or addition and the required subject connection, preserving statutory exceptions. A separate remedy and additional elements that narrow recovery do not themselves make an otherwise equal conduct duty different. State-law recognition and all liability elements remain necessary.

The design claims here survive because substantial-equivalence clearance did not establish the asserted substantive design approval. The manufacturing and warning claims survive the asserted generic federal predicates on the six-Justice comparison in Part III and the independent five-Justice formal-regulation ground in Part V. General regulatory form alone neither guarantees survival nor establishes displacement. The decision creates no private FDCA action, no universal model-specificity test, no all-tort exemption and no rule for an actual PMA approval or another future device pathway. Independently established actual conflict remains distinct.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join the design, parallel-duty and manufacturing/warning holdings and the formal FDA interpretation. They would leave open whether some other common-law duty may be expressly preempted by section 360k. Rejecting Medtronic's sweeping displacement argument does not require categorical immunity for tort law, but neither does it require affirmative inclusion beyond the duties actually resolved. Cipollone construed different statutory language and does not compel that additional step here. Their reserved position is not the Court's rule because five Justices directly adopt Part IV. Their agreement with the present application does not depend on a prediction that future common-law preemption must be rare.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I, II and IV. Substantial-equivalence clearance supplies no substantive design approval; genuinely equal state duties are not different merely because damages are available. But actual federal manufacturing and warning commands apply to this device even though they cover other devices. In their view, the statute requires applicability, difference or addition and subject connection, not an extra specificity prerequisite. The FDA cannot impose that extra condition against text they find clear. Section 808.1's recognition of court decisions and divergent substantive applications also defeats a categorical exemption based on general state-law form. They would affirm preemption of genuinely divergent manufacturing/warning duties, reverse the parallel-duty bar and remand to identify the actual obligations. They make no finding of compliance, breach or Florida entitlement and do not demand dismissal of an entire count that may contain a viable equal-duty theory.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I through IV and the judgment. Congress's express comparison controls: a damages duty can be a requirement, but clearance and the agency's presence do not supply a missing substantive federal comparator. Equal conduct does not become different merely because damages are available. His conclusion rests on that statutory ground, so he does not join Part V's independent reliance on agency interpretation. He neither rejects that ground as unlawful nor adopts a general tort exemption. The unresolved content of a particular pleaded duty can be identified through ordinary proceedings without requiring a new preliminary hearing in every products case; breach, causation and relief remain unproved.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The cases return for ordinary proceedings consistent with the affirmed design restoration and reversed manufacturing/warning and parallel-duty barriers. ### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107

Decision, June 26, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

518 U.S. 515. Argued January 17, 1996; decided June 26, 1996. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit.

The United States sued to challenge VMI's male-only admission policy. The District Court initially rejected liability, 766 F. Supp. 1407; the Fourth Circuit vacated that judgment and required a constitutional remedy, 976 F.2d 890. Virginia challenges that liability ruling in No. 94-2107. After remand, the District Court approved VWIL, 852 F. Supp. 471, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed with monitoring directions, 44 F.3d 1229. The United States challenges the remedy approval in No. 94-1941. The questions are whether the categorical exclusion violates equal protection and whether the separately created program adequately remedies it.

Justice Thomas took no part in the consideration or decision.

#### Judgment

| Docket and component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. 94-2107 — constitutional liability | Affirmed, 7–1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia | The Fourth Circuit's liability judgment stands. |
| No. 94-1941 — approval of VWIL | Reversed and remanded, 7–1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia | The approved plan cannot serve as an adequate cure; further proceedings must secure prompt effective relief within the Court's stated limits. |

Scalia's primary remedy position is consequential vacatur after reversal of liability, not a vote for the Court's remedy reversal on established liability. His independent conditional position would affirm the VWIL approval if liability remains the governing premise. Virginia retains lawful remedial choices; if it continues the same public VMI without an established adequate alternative, the categorical bar to consideration of qualified women must cease.

#### Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part I sustains liability under intermediate scrutiny; Part II rejects the actual remedial design; Part III states the operative relief and preserved State choice. Scalia filed a dissent, joined by no other Justice, addressing both dockets and separately stating his conditional remedy position. There is no partial join, judgment-only concurrence or other separate writing.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified above controls each holding, its explanation and its qualifications. Scalia joins no part.

##### Part I — Justification for the exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the existing demanding intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test. Craig v. Boren supplies the important-objective and substantial-relationship requirements; Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan places the burden on the State and requires justification for the discriminatory allocation itself. Hogan's exceedingly persuasive justification describes that burden. J.E.B. rejects sex as a conclusive proxy for individual capacity, while Kelley permits genuinely justified sex-conscious educational choices. Klinger requires comparison of the particular benefit and gives neither immunity nor automatic liability merely because whole institutions differ.

VMI's integrated adversative method has educational value. The accepted findings and predictions that coeducation would change privacy arrangements, physical training and interpersonal methods are not discarded. Their constitutional sufficiency remains a legal question. Preserving an effective program and supplying institutional diversity do not, on this record, adequately explain why its distinctive public opportunity must be reserved to men. Some women can meet the existing physical standards and seek the experience; preferences attributed to most women cannot settle those individuals' entitlement to consideration. The presence of an exceptional individual does not by itself invalidate every sex classification, but these accepted general benefits do not establish sufficient fit for this complete exclusion.

A genuine present educational objective need not have been articulated in 1839 or set out in a contemporaneous written declaration. Litigation timing alone does not establish pretext, and this holding finds neither deceit nor hostility. The failure is the insufficiency of the justification for the actual allocation, not the absence of all pedagogical benefit. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant. The Court does not decide that every single-sex institution or program is unconstitutional.

##### Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Controlling explanation:** Sweatt v. Painter supplies the insight that educational opportunity includes the training, resources, standing and connections an institution actually provides. Its racial setting does not establish a universal prohibition on separate education by sex. Klinger directs the inquiry to the benefit denied; neither each isolated disparity nor every institutional difference is an independent constitutional wrong.

VWIL offers real education: leadership courses, seminars, service, an externship, physical education and four years of ROTC at Mary Baldwin. Its cooperative method is a legitimate educational choice. The promised annual State payment equals VMI's per-student appropriation, ordinary student cost is no greater, and a separate endowment supports the program. The VMI Alumni Association promises recruitment assistance and the same access to its placement network, externships and advancement opportunities. The Court credits those promises fully and assumes faithful performance.

That assumption does not supply VMI's integrated military and adversative experience, its degree, its institutional history and standing, or its total resources. VWIL lacks VMI's on-campus engineering option; the alternative requires three years at Mary Baldwin and two years at Washington University, with private tuition and no proposed public discount for the final two years. The cumulative differences in training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources and credential leave the particular opportunity materially unequal. Network access is not absent, but equal access to a network does not erase the other differences or assure identical outcomes.

Missouri v. Jenkins distinguishes installing and funding a program from effectively repairing an established injury. Monitoring faithful implementation cannot make this substantively inadequate design sufficient. The Court finds no breach of a promise or unreported implementation failure. It prescribes no endowment ratio, identical campus, expenditure minimum or guarantee of equal careers, and it does not question Mary Baldwin's independent educational value.

##### Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice

**Holding and operative rule:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Controlling explanation:** The initial liability remand gave Virginia a choice of lawful responses, not advance approval of any separate program it might propose. Reversal of the present plan does not forfeit the State's responsibility to design an effective cure. Missouri v. Jenkins requires relief connected to the proved injury and distinguishes effective repair from general educational improvement. Lewis v. Casey likewise preserves government choice among effective means and permits necessary implementation without enlarging relief beyond the established wrong; its prison-conditions statutory restrictions supply no additional test here.

Appropriate privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations may preserve legitimate educational needs while providing effective opportunity. The Constitution does not require abandonment of genuine qualifications or admission of a named applicant regardless of them. Nor does this judgment order closure, identical public programs, a quota, equal career outcomes or a particular physical standard. An actual lawful change in State involvement remains different from retaining the same public institution under another label; no proposed withdrawal arrangement is preapproved. The lower courts must secure effective correction and assess any genuinely material remedial proposal consistently with this decision, without a newly required hearing, special master or additional procedural framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the general validity of private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate arrangement, every sex-conscious opportunity measure, or any independent Title IX claim. Ordinary respect for underlying factual findings remains intact. No damages, individual admission award, exact admissions date, prescribed training adjustment or finding about unreported 1995–1996 implementation is entered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 26, 1996, the settled intermediate-scrutiny standard governs this public admissions classification, with a controlling application invalidating VMI's complete exclusion of women. An alternative selected to repair that exclusion must provide an effective cure of the material opportunity denied; this VWIL design is inadequate even on full performance of every stated commitment. The decision adds these admissions and remedial applications without raising the scrutiny tier, requiring institutional identity, prohibiting all single-sex education, or displacing supported sex-conscious measures. State design discretion survives within the requirement of prompt and effective correction. The dissent supplies no rule of decision.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting; no joins.** Scalia applies the existing important-objective and substantial-relationship standard but reaches the opposite conclusion on the accepted record. In his view, preserving VMI's integrated method and a real option within a diverse educational system is an important actual objective. The findings about the value of single-sex education and the changes coeducation would cause establish substantial fit. Intermediate scrutiny permits an imperfect fit; some women's ability to meet physical standards does not alone defeat it. He distinguishes Hogan because its permission for men to audit classes undermined the claimed need for exclusion, whereas VMI's residential and educational methods operate together. His position does not treat tradition as immunity, replace heightened scrutiny with rational-basis review, or exempt unlike institutions from comparison.

His primary disposition would reverse liability in No. 94-2107 and, consequently, vacate the compulsory remedy in No. 94-1941 insofar as it rests on that rejected liability, remanding to remove those dependent obligations. That would not prohibit Virginia from voluntarily continuing VWIL.

Separately, assuming liability remains established, he would affirm the existing VWIL approval with its monitoring requirements. He considers the subsidized leadership education, four-year ROTC, endowed support and fully credited alumni assistance meaningfully comparable despite different methods, engineering costs, credentials, resources and prestige. In his view, rejecting the whole alternative gives those differences more constitutional weight than intermediate scrutiny permits. His conditional approval requires faithful funding and alumni performance, the District Court's retained jurisdiction and six-month reports, and the appellate directions for qualified leadership, recruitment and promotion, near-term funding and continuing professional review; promises are not findings of completed performance. This conditional ground neither accepts liability nor replaces his primary cross-docket disposition.

#### Source Notes

The first appellate opinion, [976 F.2d 890](https://static.case.law/f2d/976/html/0890-01.html), supplies the initial trial disposition, accepted pedagogical findings, capable-women evidence and initial remedial alternatives. The remand findings in [852 F. Supp. 471, Appendix III.H–I, IV and VI](https://static.case.law/f-supp/852/html/0471-01.html), and the later appellate opinion, [44 F.3d 1229](https://static.case.law/f3d/44/html/1229-01.html), supply the program, engineering, financing, facilities, alumni promises and monitoring terms. The comparison concerns the approved design and assumes faithful performance; it does not determine unreported implementation results. Promised alumni access is fully credited and is distinct from a VMI degree, institutional history or guaranteed outcomes. The findings report Mary Baldwin's endowment at approximately $19 million in one comparison and $20.5 million in another; the separate $5.4625 million VWIL commitment is a different fund. No exact common-date resource ratio is adopted.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment at 976 F.2d 890 in No. 94-2107 is affirmed. The judgment at 44 F.3d 1229 in No. 94-1941 is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with Parts I–III. Liability remains established; the existing plan's approval cannot authorize continued unequal exclusion. The lower courts must secure a prompt effective cure while permitting Virginia to design lawful implementation and consider any genuinely material adequate alternative. No fixed admissions date, immediate individual admission, special mandate date or retained Supreme Court proceeding is ordered.

**End of entry: United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, Decision, June 26, 1996.**
