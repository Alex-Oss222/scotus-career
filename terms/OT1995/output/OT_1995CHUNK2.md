### Klinger v. Department of Corrections, Eighth Circuit No. 93-2928

Merits decision, December 4, 1995

The Court reviews the Eighth Circuit's interlocutory judgment, 31 F.3d 727, reversing the district court's equal-protection liability determination and directing dismissal of the reviewed claims. The appeal below arose under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b); the appellate decision issued August 10, 1994, and rehearing was denied October 7, 1994. The questions concern whether institution-wide differences categorically prevent comparison of prison programs and what classification or discriminatory-purpose findings are necessary for liability. The review is confined to that interlocutory program controversy.

#### Judgment

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing equal-protection liability determination | Reversal affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | The district court's existing liability determination is not restored. |
| Direction to dismiss the reviewed program claims categorically | Vacated, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Remand through the Eighth Circuit for program-specific comparison and the applicable classification or purpose inquiry. |

The denied late amendment adding other male institutions remains undisturbed; no preserved error in that distinct ruling is established. Existing findings may be used if legally adequate. Whether further evidence or proceedings are necessary belongs to the lower courts under ordinary procedure. No liability, damages, funding formula, particular program, systemwide injunction or automatic evidentiary reopening is ordered.

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Similarity must concern the challenged opportunity

**Operative rule:** In an equal-protection challenge to prison programs, whether prisoners are similarly situated must be assessed in relation to the treatment challenged. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resource choices may materially distinguish a particular program; general differences between entire institutions cannot categorically defeat every comparison of the benefits provided.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices identified above, controls this and the following two holdings unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Equality does not require identical institutions before any particular deprivation may be examined. The women's institution is smaller than the Nebraska State Penitentiary and houses prisoners with different sentence lengths and security characteristics. Those circumstances may explain particular choices; they do not establish that sex is irrelevant to every challenged opportunity. Hogan requires scrutiny of the actual sex distinction rather than a broad institutional label. Turner preserves attention to legitimate prison administration and resources; it does not exempt an actual sex classification from the governing equality rule.

The State correctly rejects a demand to duplicate the larger prison's entire program mix. But dismissal based on whole-institution dissimilarity is broader than that premise supports. The prisoners likewise cannot prove a constitutional injury merely by showing that another institution spends more or offers more programs. The Court vacates categorical dismissal while leaving the significance of concrete operational differences for the individual claims.

##### Actual sex classifications and neutral decisions require distinct inquiries

**Operative rule:** If a challenged program decision actually classifies by sex, in its terms or administration, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the sex distinction. If the choice is genuinely sex neutral, plaintiffs must prove that it was made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and awareness of disparity alone do not establish that discriminatory purpose, though circumstantial evidence may do so.

**Authority:** The unanimous whole-opinion coalition identified above directly adopts both branches and their qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Craig and Hogan supply the scrutiny of actual sex classifications. Davis and Arlington Heights distinguish disparate effects from purposeful discrimination while permitting proof from circumstances. Feeney distinguishes a decision made because of its adverse consequences for a sex from one made despite them. Segregated housing does not itself decide which description fits each program choice; nor must plaintiffs produce a written admission before they may establish discriminatory administration.

Kelley confirms that a sex-conscious allocation remains subject to an actual important objective and substantial connection even when real program constraints exist. Its Title IX athletic routes are not prison rules. J.E.B. similarly rejects sex as a proxy for individual opportunity, but its jury-specific sequence of proof is not a new prison-program procedure. The Court preserves ordinary plaintiff burdens for neutral decisions and the State's justification burden for established classifications. It announces no impact presumption, least-restrictive-means requirement or right to a selected program.

##### The legal error requires further adjudication, not restored liability

**Operative rule:** The existing liability determination cannot stand merely on sex-separated housing and unequal programs without the required classification or purpose findings, but the absence of adequate findings under that mistaken approach does not justify dismissal through an overbroad comparator rule. The liability reversal is affirmed and the dismissal direction vacated so the preserved claims can be adjudicated under the proper standards.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices directly adopt this mixed disposition and its bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate majority and dissent differ over what the trial findings establish. The Court does not turn that disagreement into its own finding either of discrimination or of its universal absence. It instead separates whether a particular comparison is meaningful, whether the State used sex or acted with discriminatory purpose, whether the applicable justification is sufficient, and what relief follows. The district court's disparity premise is insufficient for immediate restoration; the appellate court's categorical dissimilarity premise is insufficient for complete dismissal.

The reviewed controversy remains limited to the pleaded program comparisons. Title IX, independent access-to-courts claims, other male institutions excluded by the denied amendment, unpleaded discriminatory funding, and any later final judgment are outside it. Gunter's individual responsibility and immunity, Clarke's official-capacity responsibility, damages and equitable relief remain separate and undecided. Judgments concerning other defendants are not reopened. Legally adequate existing findings may be used; additional proceedings are neither prohibited nor automatically required.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190; Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718:** Applied to actual sex classifications: genuine important objective and substantial relationship remain required.
- **Washington v. Davis, 426 U.S. 229:** Applied to preserve the distinction between unequal effects and purposeful discrimination.
- **Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp., 429 U.S. 252:** Applied to permit circumstantial proof of discriminatory purpose without treating disparity as conclusive.
- **Personnel Administrator v. Feeney, 442 U.S. 256:** Applied to require action because of sex or its adverse consequences, not merely awareness of them, for genuinely neutral choices.
- **Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78:** Applied for real prison-administration constraints; it creates no categorical exemption for sex-based program allocation.
- **Kelley v. Board of Trustees, May 22, 1995:** Applied for independent constitutional scrutiny of sex-conscious allocation. Its separate Title IX accommodation alternatives are not imported.
- **J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., April 19, 1994:** Applied at the sex-classification principle's scope; its peremptory-strike burden-production sequence remains confined to its own setting.

These classification and purpose authorities govern further adjudication; the Court substitutes no new substantive liability or remedial test.

#### Law After Decision

Prisoners' program-equality claims require comparison tied to the challenged benefit and the ordinary distinction between actual sex classifications and purposeful discrimination in neutral choices. Institution-wide differences create no automatic dismissal rule, and institutional separation plus unequal offerings creates no automatic liability. Legitimate program, security and resource explanations remain available under the appropriate inquiry.

[Hogan](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/458/718), [Turner](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/482/78) and [Feeney](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/442/256) supply the relevant preexisting distinctions. The review concerns the identified interlocutory judgment; no later final disposition or broader grant limitation is established by the available record.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Eighth Circuit for further proceedings on the preserved program equal-protection claims under the stated standards. The existing liability determination stays reversed; the direction to dismiss those claims is vacated. No subsequent final judgment, denied amendment, other defendant judgment or distinct claim is reopened, and no finding of ultimate liability or right to a particular remedy is made.

### Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, No. 94-1530

Merits decision, December 5, 1995

Argued October 2, 1995, on writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, whose dismissal is reported at 65 F.3d 169. A state lease-guaranty action was removed under ordinary and bankruptcy removal provisions. The district court's order required the bankruptcy court to return the case to state court on untimeliness and jurisdictional grounds; the Sixth Circuit dismissed the attempted appeal. The question is whether §1447(d) applies despite the claimed bankruptcy removal route under §1452, not whether the remand was correct or rent is owed.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The state-court remand remains effective. The Court determines no lease liability, bankruptcy merits or correctness of the district court's remand grounds.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance | Entire §1447 covered-ground and statutory-coexistence holding |
| Concurrence | Kennedy | Ginsburg | Joins the Court fully | Preserves the separate question of reviewability of Cohill remands |
| Concurrence | Ginsburg | Stevens | Joins the Court fully | Adds an independent §1452(b) equitable-remand ground, supported by these two Justices |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The ordinary covered-remand bar applies in bankruptcy removals

**Operative rule:** When a removed case is remanded to the State court from which it came because of a timely raised defect in removal procedure or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, §1447(d), read with §1447(c), bars appellate review even if removal also invoked §1452. An order remanding a case removed pursuant to §1443 may be reviewed on appeal or otherwise; that then-applicable exception is not implicated here. The rule does not declare every bankruptcy remand unreviewable.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices identified above, supplies nine direct votes for this holding and its application. The two concurrences add no limitation to those joins and no independent majority ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Thermtron requires reading the review restriction with the grounds listed in §1447(c). A procedural-remand motion must be made within thirty days after removal; lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is a distinct covered ground. The district court remanded this action for untimely removal, and the remand request was timely raised. The actual stated ground thus falls within the ordinary bar. Gravitt makes clear that an asserted error in a covered remand ground does not open an appeal to decide its correctness.

Rice applies the ordinary remand-review restriction across removal statutes absent contrary congressional direction. Section 1452(b) separately permits equitable remand and restricts review of the decisions it covers. It grants no entitlement to review forbidden by §1447(d). Germain requires giving overlapping statutes effect where they do not positively conflict. The specialized bankruptcy provision can perform its additional work without displacing the ordinary restriction. Petitioner's exclusivity theory mistakes an additional statutory regime for a repeal of a compatible rule.

The Sixth Circuit therefore properly dismissed the attempted appeal. The Court does not determine the ultimate scope of equitable grounds under §1452(b), the merits of bankruptcy jurisdiction or removal, the rent claim, or reviewability of remands outside the present statutory grounds.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Thermtron Products, Inc. v. Hermansdorfer, 423 U.S. 336:** Applied to read §§1447(c) and (d) together; no general ban on review of every kind of remand is announced.
- **United States v. Rice, 327 U.S. 742:** Applied to the ordinary restriction's operation across removal statutes absent contrary direction.
- **Gravitt v. Southwestern Bell Telephone Co., 430 U.S. 723:** Applied to prevent review of a covered remand merely because it is alleged to be erroneous.
- **Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249:** Applied for coexistence of nonconflicting jurisdictional provisions; its preservation of qualifying appellate routes creates no appeal where another provision expressly bars it.
- **Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill, 484 U.S. 343:** No holding on reviewability of its discretionary state-law remands is adopted. Kennedy and Ginsburg expressly preserve that question in their concurrence.

#### Law After Decision

The §1447 covered-ground review restriction applies cumulatively with the bankruptcy-remand regime where their conditions overlap. Classification of the stated remand ground permits the appellate-jurisdiction decision; it does not permit review of that ground's correctness. Ginsburg and Stevens's additional §1452(b) construction remains noncontrolling, and no universal rule for all remands results.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, concurring.** They join the Court's opinion with the express understanding that the present covered-ground holding does not decide reviewability of a remand under Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill. Thermtron held both that a particular unauthorized remand was improper and that the ordinary review bar did not cover remands outside §1447(c). Cohill later recognized discretion to remand remaining state-law claims after elimination of the sole federal claim, despite the absence of express statutory authorization. Cohill did not decide whether §1447(d) bars review of that distinct remand. Neither today's affirmance nor these two Justices' reservation resolves that issue in either direction.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurring.** They also find an independent review bar in §1452(b). Equity there means fairness or appropriateness, not the former division between legal and equitable pleading. Failure to remove promptly fits that meaning. Making a statutory time bar reviewable while equivalent delay labeled laches is immune would make appellate jurisdiction depend on a label. Hernandez v. Brakegate supplies the modern sense of equity; Sykes v. Texas Air explains the incoherence of label-dependent review. The chapter's structure and the 1977 House report's parallel treatment of remand review support a supplementary bankruptcy-remand regime, while Timbers directs attention to the coherent statutory scheme.

They give an alternative explanation as well: even if a procedural or jurisdictional impediment were called legal rather than equitable, equity may describe the choice to remand rather than dismiss. Section 1452(b) permits that fair disposition without requiring the impediment itself to fit an old equitable category. Their position concerns remands of claims related to bankruptcy. It does not insulate lack of subject-matter jurisdiction in a retained case: a decision not to remand may lack a separate immediate appeal while jurisdiction remains open to district-court reconsideration and eventual review through an otherwise proper channel. Their additional ground neither answers the separate Cohill question outside its statutory coverage nor commands a majority of the Court.

#### Source Notes

[The published opinion and syllabus, 516 U.S. 124](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-124.pdf), supply the chronology, district-order effect, timely-remand premise, term-specific statutory language and public-source support for the identified doctrinal questions. [Thermtron](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/423/336), [Rice](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/327/742), [Gravitt](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/430/723), and [Cohill](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/484/343) supply their respective earlier rules. The applicable §1447(d) exception concerns §1443; no later statutory exception is applied.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit's appellate-jurisdiction dismissal is affirmed. The action proceeds in state court under the undisturbed remand; no new federal merits adjudication, payment order or reconsideration of the remand's correctness is directed.

**End of entry: Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, merits decision, December 5, 1995.**

### Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492

Merits decision, December 6, 1995

Argued October 30, 1995, on writs of certiorari to the District of Columbia Circuit. The consolidated question is whether firearm use under the applicable 18 U.S.C. §924(c)(1) includes possession or storage without active employment. The court of appeals sustained the firearm convictions on accessibility and protective-availability grounds. Both submitted counts included use or carrying; Robinson's instructions also submitted aiding and abetting. The distinct drug and felon-possession judgments are outside the disposition.

#### Judgment

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bailey, No. 94-7448: appellate affirmance based on possession or storage as use | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | The use rationale cannot sustain the firearm conviction. |
| Robinson, No. 94-7492: appellate affirmance based on possession or storage as use | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | The use rationale cannot sustain the firearm conviction. |
| Further disposition of both firearm convictions | Bounded carrying remand, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | The D.C. Circuit may consider a legally sufficient, properly available carrying ground; no immediate Supreme Court vacatur of the trial convictions. Stone-Zsela would vacate those convictions and remand for ordinary proceedings on any legally available preserved alternative. |

No carrying conviction, comprehensive carrying definition, finding of harmless error, automatic retrial or acquittal is ordered. Any aiding-and-abetting route in Robinson requires its own lawful substantive-offense and intentional-assistance predicates. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments are not disturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal of both use-based appellate affirmances | Active-employment construction, separate relation element, and preserved Smith rule |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Bounded carrying remand without immediate vacatur of the trial convictions | No determination of carrying sufficiency or automatic remedy |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | — | Joins Part I and the appellate reversals; would also vacate the firearm convictions | Would remit any properly preserved lawful alternative to ordinary proceedings |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Use requires active employment of the firearm

**Operative rule:** Under the applicable §924(c)(1), a defendant uses a firearm only by actually and actively employing it as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment does not by itself satisfy use; firing or employment as a weapon is not required, and Smith's actual firearm barter remains use.

**Authority:** The unanimous coalition joining O'Connor's Part I, identified above, directly adopts this rule, its application and reversal of the use-based appellate affirmances.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress used a conduct word and separately referred to carrying. Reading use as every way a gun's availability may support a criminal enterprise would erase the difference between actual employment and possession. Smith holds that offering a firearm in exchange for drugs employs it in the transaction even though as merchandise. That example broadens the purposes for which a gun may be actively used; it does not eliminate the act of using it.

The Government's protective-availability and emboldening account addresses a real danger, but that danger cannot replace the conduct Congress specified. Bailey's weapon was stored in a locked automobile trunk; Robinson's was kept in her bedroom near drug proceeds. The trial theories and instructions permitted conviction through possession and availability rather than active employment. Those grounds cannot sustain the appellate judgments. Staples and Ratzlaf reinforce respect for the distinct enacted elements; they do not create an extra knowledge-of-illegality requirement or a universal presumption that every criminal ambiguity must be resolved before ordinary interpretation.

The Court provides no closed list of qualifying conduct and no comprehensive definition of carrying. It decides no retroactivity question and leaves distinct possession offenses untouched.

##### Connection to the offense cannot substitute for using or carrying

**Operative rule:** The Government must establish the use or carrying conduct independently of the requirement that it occur during and in relation to the predicate offense. A firearm's actual or potential facilitation may satisfy the required relationship under Smith, but it does not establish active employment under the use prong merely because the firearm is present or available.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Part I coalition directly adopts this distinct-elements proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith gives the relation requirement work by excluding an accidental or coincidental connection and requiring a purpose or effect concerning trafficking. Its actual exchange also supplies the separate act of employment. The two showings therefore coexist; one does not do the other's work. Harris likewise requires independent proof of §924(c)'s use or carrying even where the qualifying predicate itself includes a firearm. The Court applies that separation here without enlarging Harris's carjacking holding or deciding the outer boundary of sufficient facilitation.

##### The carrying alternative remains for proper consideration on remand

**Operative rule:** Both use-based appellate affirmances are reversed, and the D.C. Circuit may consider whether a legally sufficient and properly available carrying basis supports either reviewed firearm conviction. The presence of carrying language in the charges and instructions establishes no finding that every carrying element was lawfully determined, that the error was harmless, or that any particular remedial course is permissible.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice coalition joining O'Connor's Part II, identified above, controls this holding. Stone-Zsela supports remand but dissents from leaving the trial convictions available for that consideration without first vacating them.

**Controlling explanation:** The instructions submitted carrying in both cases, but they also treated possession and intended availability as sufficient without preserving the necessary conduct distinction. The Court decides the presented use issue; it does not decide an unexamined carrying theory from a charge label. The lower court must address the independent lawful requirements and procedural availability before relying on that alternative. Robinson's aiding-and-abetting submission likewise proves neither a substantive firearm offense nor intentional assistance by itself. Harris preserves the distinct requirement of intentional assistance; mere presence, association or awareness is insufficient.

The remand accordingly directs no carrying conviction, immediate acquittal or retrial. It neither determines waiver nor resolves sufficiency or harmlessness from instruction excerpts. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments remain undisturbed. The Court's refusal to order immediate trial-conviction vacatur is not a holding that either conviction is valid on carrying grounds.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Smith v. United States, 508 U.S. 223:** Applied to active firearm barter and the independent trafficking connection. Mere storage is excluded from use; the earlier five-Justice coalition and actual holding are not retroactively changed by today's unanimous application.
- **Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600:** Applied for respect for the characteristics and elements Congress chose; no new §924(c) knowledge-of-illegality element follows.
- **Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135:** Applied for distinct enacted elements and source-bound criminal construction; its structuring knowledge rule and residual-lenity ground are not transformed into new elements here.
- **United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995:** Applied to preserve independent proof of §924(c) conduct and, in the eight-Justice remand explanation, intentional assistance for aiding and abetting. Its carjacking elements and cumulative-punishment result are unchanged.

#### Law After Decision

Section 924(c) use requires active employment; passive possession and protective availability cannot supply that element. Smith's firearm-barter holding and separate relation requirement remain binding. Carrying retains independent statutory work, but its comprehensive definition and sufficiency in either case remain undecided. No general possession offense, retroactivity rule or automatic remedial consequence is created.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the active-employment rule, its preservation of Smith's barter holding, the separate relation requirement, and reversal of both use-based appellate affirmances. Because the present convictions rest on the storage-and-accessibility use theory that the Court rejects, he would also vacate the firearm convictions now. He would then remand for ordinary proceedings, including any legally available charge based on carrying or possession that was properly preserved. That alternative does not convert possession into an offense under the applicable §924(c), disturb separate possession judgments, or decide an unpresented charging or retrial question. He requires neither automatic acquittal nor automatic retrial. No other Justice joins this different remedial position.

### Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148

Merits decision, December 11, 1995

On review of the Eighth Circuit's July 12, 1994 judgment, 28 F.3d 1501, Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148. The district court entered judgment for the school district after a bench trial; the Court of Appeals reversed and remanded for determination of appropriate relief. The questions are whether the district may exclude this otherwise eligible private religious Club from the 3–6 p.m. period available to comparable youth activities and whether equal access would establish religion. The viewpoint claim was presented after trial and decided by the district court; the Court of Appeals rejected waiver.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justice Stevens.

The affirmed judgment is the Eighth Circuit's reversal of the district court and remand for an appropriate remedy. The Club must receive nondiscriminatory access on the legitimate scheduling, supervision, safety and other access terms governing comparable groups. Proceedings below may provide the appropriate declaratory or injunctive relief. No damages, immediate Supreme Court injunction, reopening of instructional hours, or required restoration of every term of the former 1986 policy is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and III | Souter | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance | Actual-forum access, concrete rejection of the establishment defense, and remedy |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Souter | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance | Contextual purpose, effect and entanglement explanation |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Kennedy | Scalia, Thomas | Affirmance; joins Parts I and III | Private-expression ground; withholds Part II's full methodology |
| Dissent | Stevens | No other Justice | Reversal | Restricted-forum and actual-activity distinction |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Religious instruction on an admitted subject receives equal access

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school that opens an after-school period to comparable private instruction and activity concerning children's moral and character development may not exclude an otherwise eligible club because its approach to that subject is religious. The rule applies to this actual forum even if it is nonpublic; genuine subject, activity, scheduling, supervision, safety and access limits remain available when applied without viewpoint discrimination.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice coalition joining Souter's Part I, identified above, directly adopts this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the district has excluded a religious perspective from a subject it admits. Lamb's Chapel supplies the distinction between a permissible subject limit and an impermissible religious-viewpoint exclusion. The district court found that both the Scouts and this Club concern moral development. The Scouts' practical and secular activities and the Club's Christian instruction differ, but the district cannot make religious treatment of the admitted subject a disqualifying subject of its own. The policy's express exclusion of religious speech even within the Scout program confirms the viewpoint restriction. Prayer and Bible lessons do not erase the relevant comparison on this record. Perry and Cornelius preserve genuine forum limits; they do not permit that reclassification of an admitted perspective. Access after 6 p.m. does not cure discrimination in the 3–6 p.m. period available to comparable activities. The students' ages and the continuing extracurricular day require consideration of the actual arrangements, but do not make private religious instruction school speech. The parent-led, voluntary meetings, parental permission and express notice of private sponsorship establish the concrete private-use basis on which establishment concerns cannot justify this exclusion. The Court does not require every school to open every facility to every religious activity.

##### 2. These actual private-use arrangements do not establish religion

**Holding and operative rule:** Allowing this Club equal access on the established forum terms does not violate the Establishment Clause: the forum serves a secular purpose, the Club's expression remains private in the actual context, and equal access creates neither governmental religious advancement nor excessive entanglement. Private sponsorship must be real; school selection, control, adoption, preferential treatment or obscured attribution could change the constitutional assessment, and neither neutral availability nor a disclaimer alone would cure official religious activity.

**Authority:** The five-Justice coalition joining Souter's Part II, identified above, directly adopts the contextual explanation under existing establishment law. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join Part I's concrete rejection of the establishment defense but do not join Part II's full explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the existing inquiry into purpose, effect and entanglement; it creates no new constitutional checklist. Lemon supplies that inquiry, and Lamb's Chapel applies it to equal access for private expression in a school. Chabad requires examination of actual governmental affiliation rather than reliance on a forum label. Here parents lead the meetings, students attend voluntarily with parental permission, and announcements disclaim school sponsorship. No current official direction of the Club's worship is established. The former board member's earlier involvement does not establish present control. Students are 11–15 and meetings occur while other school activities continue, but those contextual facts do not outweigh the established separation between the school and this private program. The available late bus adds convenience, not compulsory attendance. The secular purpose is to make facilities available for the admitted youth activities; permitting the religious perspective does not make the school its speaker. Ordinary neutral supervision and administration do not require school officials to direct or endorse the Club's religious instruction. Pinette supplies no categorical exemption for religious speech in a forum, and Chabad supplies no universal sign requirement. Rosenberger's distinct printing-benefit program does not convert this access decision into an entitlement to devotional funding.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The independent religion-clause claims need not be decided. The Court does not decide the eligibility of standalone worship in a different forum, the facial validity of every religious-use restriction, or any public financing claim. It imposes no school endorsement, official prayer, compulsory attendance, fixed disclaimer wording, additional evidentiary hearing, or universal monitoring procedure. The district may make genuinely neutral prospective forum changes and need not operate this forum permanently.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Applied to religious instruction concerning the moral-development subject actually admitted; its limits on genuine forum definition and official devotional programs remain.
- **Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association:** Applied to preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral management of a nonpublic forum; it does not authorize exclusion of an admitted religious perspective.
- **Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund:** Its distinction between subject or speaker limits and viewpoint discrimination is applied to the actual programs and policy.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Its purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains governing and is applied by the five-Justice Part II majority; it is neither replaced nor expanded into a new checklist.
- **Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller:** Its contextual distinction between clearly private speech and actual governmental affiliation is applied in this school setting, with the same rejection of automatic attribution by location or label.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its bounded protection against a categorical establishment defense is applied; no comprehensive forum exemption or mandatory disclaimer is extracted.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its private-viewpoint protection is consistent with the access holding; the printing-payment program remains distinct and creates no general devotional-funding entitlement here.
- **Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens:** Its distinction between private religious expression and school sponsorship informs the discussion, but no Equal Access Act holding is made and the statutory decision does not define every school forum.

#### Law After Decision

Lamb's Chapel's existing viewpoint rule applies to this after-school moral-development forum despite the Club's prayer and Bible instruction. The decision adds the concrete application to voluntary parent-led participation by students aged 11–15; it does not announce that religious instruction is always private or eligible. Existing establishment law continues, with the actual arrangements determining attribution. Lawful neutral forum management and prospective changes remain available. No new funding right, constitutional test or administrative procedure takes effect.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** All three join Parts I and III, including the actual-forum viewpoint rule, the concrete conclusion that the establishment defense fails on these private-use facts, and the remedial limits. They withhold assent from Part II's comprehensive contextual Lemon explanation. Their ground is the distinction between genuinely private religious advocacy and governmental religious action, reflected in Kennedy's Mergens opinion and in the access reasoning of Rosenberger and Chabad. Voluntary attendance, parental choice and private leadership support access here; a religious message or possible subjective impression of endorsement does not alone permit suppressing private expression. They propose no unlimited forum, general financing entitlement or alteration of existing establishment law through this concurrence.

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the Eighth Circuit and leave the judgment for the school district in place. Perry and Cornelius permit a genuinely restricted forum, and his Mergens analysis treats the activity the school actually admitted as decisive. The Scout and athletic programs are secular activities related to the school's extracurricular program; the fact that both those programs and the adult-led Club concern moral development does not make them the same admitted activity. He acknowledges the policy's religious restriction and Lamb's Chapel's prohibition on viewpoint exclusion within an admitted subject, but finds the activity distinction sufficient for this Club's denial. The younger audience and continued extracurricular day reinforce the restricted character of the forum. His disagreement concerns that comparison, not a general exemption allowing schools to suppress religious viewpoints, a necessary finding that every Club meeting establishes religion, or rejection of Lamb's Chapel's governing rule.

### Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions

Merits decision, December 18, 1995

On review of the en banc Eleventh Circuit judgment of June 14, 1995, 56 F.3d 1281, affirming the rejection of the Section 2 claim after a bench trial. The questions concern the application of Section 2 to the challenged at-large trial-judge elections, including a proper comparison, actual minority electoral opportunity, and the linkage between judges' electoral and territorial jurisdictions. The challenge covers ten identified judicial circuits and district-judge elections in four counties, not all Alabama judicial elections.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The Court of Appeals must reconsider the independent no-dilution ground under the correct legal standards, with any properly necessary proceedings in the district court, before imposing relief. Supported subsidiary historical findings remain. If a violation is proved, the lower courts must consider lawful, practical correction and genuine judicial-administration interests without categorical racial-partiality assumptions. The Court imposes no present liability decree, map, number of judgeships, injunction, election delay, deadline or guaranteed election of Black candidates.

Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor dissents, joined by Kennedy, on the trial-record failure-of-proof ground. Thomas dissents, joined by Scalia, on statutory noncoverage. The four dissenters seek the same affirmance but do not share a substantive rationale or join one another's writings.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** The five-Justice coalition joining Souter's opinion, identified above, directly adopts each holding and the remand. The dissenting grounds supply no additional support for these holdings.

##### 1. The opportunity determination must use the statutory results inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2 reaches these at-large judicial elections and requires proof of unequal opportunity for minority voters to elect their actual preferred candidates under the applicable Gingles conditions and the totality of circumstances; it requires neither a Black preferred candidate nor proof that voters or legislators acted from racial motives. The challenged jurisdictions must be evaluated on their actual electoral opportunity, with contextual treatment of candidate preference, incumbency, appointments, partisanship, qualifications and electoral success; neither raw success elsewhere nor the absence of discriminatory enactment resolves the claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judgment's independent no-dilution ground requires reconsideration because its legal framing affects the opportunity inquiry itself. Chisom and Houston Lawyers establish that judicial elections fall within Section 2. Gingles supplies the required causal conditions: a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, with special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate considered. Those conditions do not alone establish liability. De Grandy requires the further totality inquiry and distinguishes meaningful opportunity from candidate race, numerical success and a duty to create every feasible district. The appellate discussion of voting because of candidate race and nondiscriminatory enactment cannot substitute a racial-motive prerequisite for the statutory inquiry. Nor can an aggregate account obscure the actual challenged electoral units. The State correctly invokes the trial court's consideration of both experts, varied elections, party affiliation and incumbency. Supported subsidiary findings remain, but deference to those findings does not settle whether the ultimate conclusion uses the correct legal criteria. The Court neither chooses the plaintiffs' expert nor categorically excludes white-only or statewide contests. Such evidence may inform local preference and opportunity in context. The remand corrects the governing premises; it does not find dilution or presume that the necessary conditions establish it.

##### 2. Judicial structure warrants concrete consideration, not categorical remedial immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 comparison and remedy in judicial elections must be legally grounded, practicable and fitted to the governmental task, with genuine weight given to the linkage between the electorate and the jurisdiction served, the qualified-candidate pool and judicial administration. Those interests do not categorically defeat a proved violation or every possible correction, and racial awareness in territorial remedial design alone does not establish unconstitutional racial partiality; any independently cognizable constitutional injury remains subject to its own rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Houston Lawyers treats the State's jurisdiction-electorate linkage as legitimate and relevant, not an exemption from Section 2. Holder requires a reasonable comparison with independent legal and practical roots, rather than a size or structure selected solely to produce desired racial winners; it does not demand a uniquely ideal arrangement. Comparison, proof of dilution and relief remain separate. A possible subdistrict is not enough to establish liability, and the statute does not authorize automatic restructuring simply because another electoral arrangement can be imagined. But the lower court's alternative remedy ground cannot stand insofar as it treats independent judges, jurisdictional linkage or race-aware boundaries as necessarily incompatible with relief. Shaw and Miller require an independently cognizable deprivation beyond territorial racial design alone. They do not presume that voters' preferred judges would dispense racially partial justice or serve only members of one race. Those decisions leave actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation and inferior civic status fully reviewable under their own standards. The court below must evaluate concrete administration and candidate-pool concerns and lawful alternatives if a violation is proved. The Court selects no plan and decides neither the constitutionality nor practical adequacy of any particular new district.

##### 3. Reconsideration preserves findings and the plaintiffs' burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the challenged jurisdictions under the correct opportunity, comparison and remedy standards, preserving supported subsidiary historical findings and the plaintiffs' burden to prove each required condition and ultimate unequal opportunity. Relief may be ordered only for an established violation and must be lawful and practicable; if the preserved facts do not establish dilution under the corrected inquiry, judgment may again be entered for the State.

**Controlling explanation:** The judgment rested on two independently stated grounds, so a flaw confined to remedies would not justify vacating the separate no-dilution determination. The Court identifies legal error affecting that determination as well as the categorical remedial premises. Reconsideration must therefore begin with the independent opportunity ground, not with an assumed liability finding. Gingles and De Grandy preserve the plaintiffs' causal and totality burdens, while Holder keeps a grounded comparison distinct from both liability and relief. The remand returns through the Eleventh Circuit for reconsideration and any properly necessary district-court proceedings. It requires no new statistical formula, additional expert studies, automatic liability trial, particular judicial structure or guaranteed electoral result.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The decision concerns circuit-judge elections in the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Tenth, Thirteenth, Fifteenth, Twentieth, Twenty-Third and Twenty-Sixth Circuits and district-judge elections in Jefferson, Mobile, Montgomery and Russell Counties. A statewide plaintiff class does not expand the challenge to all forty Alabama circuits or every judicial office. The constitutional-purpose claims are not revived. No proportional-results entitlement, maximization duty, universal evidentiary exclusion, additional constitutional-injury element for Section 2, particular population denominator, new statistical method or burden shift is adopted. Cumulative voting was mentioned in the lower dissent, not proposed as a preserved remedy; no entitlement to its adoption is decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chisom v. Roemer, 501 U.S. 380:** Applied to preserve Section 2 coverage of judicial elections; the two-Justice contrary view does not alter that rule.
- **Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas, 501 U.S. 419:** Applied to independent trial judges and the legitimate but nonconclusive linkage interest; coverage, proof and remedy remain distinct.
- **Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30:** Its causal conditions, special-circumstance qualification and totality inquiry remain required; no racial-motive condition, automatic-liability rule or universal statistical method is added.
- **Holder v. Hall:** Applied in its controlling form to a legally and practically grounded comparison and a remedy fitted to a proved violation; its rules do not require a uniquely ideal structure or grant categorical immunity to existing arrangements.
- **Johnson v. De Grandy:** Its local-opportunity, candidate-preference and totality rules are applied, including the rejection of maximization, proportional-results entitlement and conclusive numerical safe harbors; meaningful success remains evidence.
- **Shaw v. Reno:** Its rejection of a territorial-design-only constitutional claim is applied; actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation and legally inferior civic status remain independently cognizable.
- **Miller and consolidated cases:** Applied to reject racial predominance alone as a constitutional defect or proof that an officeholder represents only one race; the decision adds no constitutional-injury gate to Section 2 and decides no particular remedial map here.

#### Law After Decision

Section 2's existing judicial-election coverage and results inquiry remain governing. This decision requires reconsideration of these challenged elections under actual candidate preference and jurisdiction-matched opportunity, and it rejects the categorical remedial premises used below. It does not establish dilution in any jurisdiction, prescribe a new proof method or adopt the lower dissent's liability findings. Legitimate linkage, candidate-pool and administrative interests retain concrete weight; territorial racial design alone supplies neither constitutional liability nor an additional statutory element. The separate noncoverage view remains noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Kennedy.** They would affirm on the independently sufficient failure-of-proof ground under the governing Section 2 results inquiry. Houston Lawyers permits judicial-election coverage while retaining legitimate institutional considerations, and De Grandy requires proof of ultimate unequal opportunity rather than automatic liability from polarization or a feasible additional district. The trial court evaluated both experts, several kinds of elections, actual candidate preferences, party affiliation and incumbency. In their view the Court of Appeals upheld permissible contextual factfinding rather than applying a racial-intent requirement or numerical safe harbor. They agree that legal error would defeat factual deference, but find no error warranting reconsideration of this independent ground. They do not defend presumed racial partiality, categorical structural immunity, or the proposition that all proposed remedies are unconstitutional. Kennedy's earlier broader objection to judicial vote-dilution coverage is not the ground he selects here. Neither joins Thomas's noncoverage rationale.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** They would affirm on their separate statutory view that Section 2 does not reach this judicial vote-dilution claim. Their preserved Holder and De Grandy positions reject dilution coverage; Scalia's Chisom and Houston Lawyers dissent supplies earlier judicial-election-specific support. They acknowledge that the Court's contrary coverage holdings govern unless changed, but continue to urge their construction rather than join the majority's comparison and totality framework. They do not join O'Connor's trial-record application merely because they seek the same decree. Their opinion supplies no additional votes for an evidentiary proposition, categorical remedial rule or present change in the Court's coverage law.

### Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railroad Co., No. 94-1592

Merits decision, January 8, 1996

516 U.S. 152. Argued October 30, 1995. On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, sitting en banc. The question is whether waiting for return transportation after a duty assignment has ended is on-duty time or the statutory transportation interval that counts as neither duty nor rest. The judgment below treated the waiting as part of that interval.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

No additional remedial direction, employee-specific award, or penalty determination issues.

Justice Kennedy delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Waiting incident to completed-assignment return is neither duty nor rest

**Operative rule:** Waiting genuinely incident to transportation from a completed duty assignment to final release falls within 49 U.S.C. §21103(b)(4)'s return-transportation interval and counts as neither on-duty time nor required off-duty rest. Time engaged in or connected with movement of a train remains duty time under §21103(b)(2), as does any other service for the carrier during a twenty-four-hour period in which the employee is engaged in or connected with train movement under §21103(b)(3); transportation to a new duty assignment remains on duty.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices identified above. All nine adopt the rule, its limits, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Transportation describes the undertaking of returning the crew, including awaiting the vehicle that carries it. Nothing in the specific return provision confines that undertaking to minutes of physical movement. The general reporting-to-final-release rule in §21103(b)(1) must leave room for the express return category in §21103(b)(4). Otherwise the general language would defeat the exception Congress placed beside it.

The unions emphasize that waiting crews remain subject to the railroad's direction and may become fatigued. Those circumstances do not distinguish the wait from the return journey Congress expressly excluded from duty. Nor does the exclusion declare the time restorative: Congress also withheld off-duty credit. The separate provisions counting actual service and certain interim rest as duty continue to govern their own predicates. An employer's transportation label cannot remove work from those provisions.

MCI Telecommunications requires an independent determination of the statutory boundary; Town & Country permits agency construction only within the statutory range. The enacted categories resolve this question without reliance on an agency's litigation position or administrative history. No general deference doctrine is changed.

The question concerns waiting after assignment completion without further train-movement responsibility or other service. It does not require a judicial maximum waiting duration, a finding that waiting causes no fatigue, or a decision about a particular worker's compensation, penalty, or rest entitlement. The Seventh Circuit's classification is affirmed.

**Operative qualifications:** Section 21103(a)'s ordinary eight-consecutive-hours-off requirement within the preceding twenty-four hours, and its ten-consecutive-hours-off requirement after twelve consecutive hours on duty, remain. Section (c)'s distinct emergency permission for a wreck or relief crew allows up to four additional hours in twenty-four for emergency-related work and ends when the track is cleared and the line reopened; that permission is not invoked here. Sections (b)(5)–(6) count interim rest outside a designated terminal, or shorter than four hours at a designated terminal, as duty. Section (b)(7) separately excludes an interim period available for at least four hours of rest at a place with suitable food and lodging facilities when a casualty, track obstruction, act of God, or derailment or major equipment failure prevents the employee from reaching the designated terminal; for derailment or major equipment failure, the cause must have been unknown and unforeseeable to the carrier or its officer or agent in charge of that employee when the employee left the designated terminal. Those predicates are neither found nor excused by this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T, 512 U.S. 218:** Its independent statutory-boundary inquiry is preserved; no agency may replace the enacted categories.
- **NLRB v. Town & Country Electric, Inc., 516 U.S. 85:** Its allowance for permissible agency construction within the statute remains; the present classification rests directly on the statute and decides no new deference question.

#### Law After Decision

Completed-assignment waiting genuinely connected with return transportation is excluded from both duty and required rest. Actual service, transportation to duty, statutory interim-rest classifications, and ordinary rest obligations retain their independent force. No maximum permissible delay or general carrier immunity is created.

#### Source Notes

[The 1994 text of 49 U.S.C. §21103](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title49/pdf/USCODE-1994-title49-subtitleV-partA-chap211-sec21103.pdf) supplies the classifications and distinct rest and emergency conditions. The [reported record and posture](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/94-1592.ZS.html) identify the argument date, court below, and submitted waiting interval. The ruling does not resolve disputed facts about a particular employee's continuing service or delay.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Seventh Circuit's judgment stands. No further Supreme Court proceeding or special remand condition is ordered.

**End of entry: Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railroad Co., merits decision, January 8, 1996.**

##### A material agency development can justify reconsideration without a merits decision

**Operative rule:** Under 28 U.S.C. §2106, an intervening or sufficiently recent development makes a grant, vacatur, and remand potentially appropriate when there is a reasonable probability that the decision below rests on a premise the lower court would reject upon reconsideration and that reconsideration may affect the ultimate outcome. The Court must also independently assess the equities, including finality, lower-court independence, delay, cost, and manipulative litigation; a Government request alone does not justify vacatur.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining the per curiam opinion, identified above, directly adopt the procedural rule and its application. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on his separately stated ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Henry v. City of Rock Hill distinguishes reconsideration from adjudication of the merits. Alvarado v. United States illustrates remand for judicial examination of a changed Government position; the Court must nevertheless supply its own reason for vacating a judgment. Section 2106 permits that limited remedy without a present determination that the claimant must prevail.

The agency now acknowledges examining the constitutional validity of state intestacy and paternity requirements at least in some circumstances. That qualified position bears sufficiently on the state-law premise of this benefits claim to warrant examination below. The lower court has not considered it. Its possible effect and the value of an initial lawful inquiry justify reconsideration even though this Court cannot determine whether the acknowledgment applies to this claimant or changes the ultimate result.

The objection that conditional executive advocacy proves no legal error is substantial, but the order makes no such finding and delegates no judicial judgment to the Government. The receiving court remains responsible for the position's relevance, legal authority, and effect. It may reach the same result. Neither constitutional invalidity, parentage, benefits entitlement, mandatory agency review of this claimant, nor automatic deference is established.

The North Carolina amendment is effective background law, but no claimant-specific qualifying action or other statutory predicate establishes it as an independent reason for vacatur. The order rests on the qualified agency development, preserves lawfully available threshold requirements and defenses, and reopens no independently closed issue.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Henry v. City of Rock Hill, 376 U.S. 776:** Applied to distinguish reconsideration from a present merits decision.
- **Alvarado v. United States, 497 U.S. 543:** Applied as remand practice while preserving independent judicial assessment of a Government representation.
- **Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, 512 U.S. 504:** Its qualified own-regulation deference rule remains; it supplies no automatic statutory or constitutional deference here.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T, 512 U.S. 218:** Preserves independently enforced statutory limits; no agency position becomes lawful merely by being asserted.
- **McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140, and Darby v. Cisneros, 509 U.S. 137:** Their distinct statutory, non-APA, and APA exhaustion rules remain undisturbed; this procedural disposition creates no exception.

#### Law After Decision

The Court adopts the probability-and-equities procedural rule stated above, without establishing benefits eligibility or the agency position's validity, applicability, or deferential weight. The Chief Justice's reasonable-possibility formulation is separate and noncontrolling. North Carolina Session Law 1995-424 remains effective subject to its terms; the Court neither applies it to this claimant nor treats it as an independently sufficient GVR ground.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court's opinion and adds that certiorari administration properly permits a case-specific assessment of the totality of the circumstances. The value of obtaining the lower court's judgment about a potentially consequential development need not depend on first proving that the claimant will win. That discretion remains judicial and supplies no automatic rule accepting Government requests. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice would use §2106 when a material intervening development creates a reasonable possibility of a different result and reconsideration would conserve judicial resources. He does not adopt the Court's distinct reasonable-probability formulation. Henry separates this procedural return from a benefits award. The agency's changed position matters because it may unsettle the existing judgment, not because administrative deference supplies power to vacate. A strategic litigation reversal, an immaterial development, or a position outside lawful agency authority would not suffice, and remand may not predetermine the merits. Thomas Jefferson University and MCI supply cautions for the receiving court's independent examination of statutory authority, procedure, consistency, fair notice, and the position's lawful weight; they do not make the new position controlling. No other Justice joins this opinion.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny the petition. An intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment can justify vacatur without a merits determination. An established defect can also support a justified error-correcting disposition; plenary review is not foreclosed. Conditional executive advocacy of uncertain relevance supplies no sufficient ground here. The agency's qualified acknowledgment establishes neither its legal force nor its application to this claimant. A clearly cognizable agency change could qualify, but a desire to reassess an arguably valid decision or obtain better reasoning does not. The unproved predicates of the state amendment do not cure that defect. The dissent concerns judicial justification for this remedy, not a ruling that the agency can never examine constitutional validity.

### Stutson v. United States, No. 94-8988

Petition-stage grant, vacatur, and remand, January 8, 1996

516 U.S. 193. On petition for certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit's unexplained affirmance of the refusal to excuse a late criminal notice of appeal. Counsel misdirected the filing, which arrived one working day late. The question is whether Pioneer and the Government's changed position warrant reconsideration of excusable neglect under Rule 4(b). The criminal merits are not before the Court.

#### Public Action

The Court grants leave to proceed in forma pauperis, grants certiorari, vacates the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, and remands for reconsideration in light of Pioneer. Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. No petition vote tally is announced.

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated. That court must determine the proper effect of Pioneer under Rule 4(b) and arrange any necessary District Court findings. The order does not accept the late notice, require a merits appeal, find excusable neglect, reverse the conviction, or alter the sentence.

The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stevens also files a concurrence, without an additional join. Scalia files a dissent, joined by Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The unresolved excusable-neglect premise warrants reconsideration

**Operative rule:** Section 2106 permits a limited return for reconsideration where the combination of a potentially important governing formulation, the Government's changed legal position, a one-working-day misdirected filing, and an unexplained appellate affirmance leaves a substantial question whether the correct excusable-neglect inquiry was performed. These circumstances warrant reconsideration of Rule 4(b) in light of Pioneer; no one circumstance, including a Government concession, makes vacatur automatic.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining the per curiam opinion, identified above, directly adopt this case-specific procedural ground and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Pioneer Investment Services construed excusable neglect in Bankruptcy Rule 9006(b)(1) to include mistake and carelessness, while requiring an assessment of all relevant circumstances and attributing freely chosen counsel's conduct to the client. Its possible relevance matters because the Government originally opposed using that formulation for criminal appeals and now accepts its relevance. The unexplained affirmance leaves uncertain whether the appellate court considered it.

The short delay and changed position do not prove an erroneous judgment. Pioneer was already available; the court below may have considered it and denied relief on a lawful ground. But that possibility does not remove the concrete value of reconsidering a legal premise that can determine whether appellate review is available. Section 2106 permits that procedural step while leaving actual excusability to the receiving court. Neither administrative convenience nor Government agreement alone supplies the reason.

The Eleventh Circuit must determine Pioneer's proper effect under Rule 4(b) and arrange any necessary District Court findings. Relevant circumstances include prejudice, delay and its effects, the reason for delay and its reasonable controllability, and good faith; no factor is invariably decisive under Pioneer. Counsel attribution remains. The Court does not decide that all Rule 4(b) applications are identical to bankruptcy enlargement, transplant Rule 9006(b)(2)–(3)'s separate restrictions, find excusable neglect, accept the late appeal, or adjudicate conviction or sentence.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates Limited Partnership, 507 U.S. 380:** Applied as the potentially relevant excusable-neglect formulation, preserving its Chapter 11 scope, all-circumstances inquiry, counsel attribution, and rule-specific enlargement limits. No complete Rule 4(b) merits extension is decided.
- **Henry v. City of Rock Hill, 376 U.S. 776:** Its distinction between reconsideration and final merits adjudication is applied.
- **Alvarado v. United States, 497 U.S. 543:** Independent judicial justification remains necessary; changed Government advocacy does not dictate the disposition.

#### Law After Decision

This record requires renewed consideration of the appellate-timeliness ruling. A short delay is not automatically excusable, a Government confession creates no automatic GVR, and an unexplained judgment is not conclusively presumed erroneous. The lower court retains responsibility for Rule 4(b), actual neglect, and the availability of appellate review.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court and adds that case-specific, totality-of-the-circumstances discretion is a proper part of certiorari administration. The combination of an uncertain review premise and the potential loss of appellate consideration supports obtaining a fresh lower-court judgment without deciding excusability. This position creates no entitlement for every short delay and no exception to attribution of counsel's conduct. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny. Pioneer was available when the lower courts acted, and an unexplained affirmance may have applied its approach and still rejected the excuse. The Government concedes a point of law concerning relevance, not error in the judgment. Its changed argument therefore establishes neither new intervening law nor a defective judicial premise. The dissent accepts vacatur without a merits determination for an intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment. It does not foreclose plenary review or a justified error-correcting disposition. None of those grounds is established here. Uncertainty about which ground sustained a judgment, or a desire for better reasoning, does not itself justify vacatur; the proper exercise of §2106 requires more than the possibility that reconsideration could change the result.

#### Source Notes

The [reported procedural record, 516 U.S. 193–198](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-193.pdf) supplies the misdirected filing, one-working-day delay, unexplained appellate disposition, and changed Government position. The actual lower-court treatment of Pioneer remains uncertain; silence is not a finding that it was ignored.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Proceedings resume through the Eleventh Circuit under the limited remand. A lawful renewed denial remains available; no merits appeal or criminal relief is guaranteed.

**End of entry: Stutson v. United States, petition-stage grant, vacatur, and remand, January 8, 1996.**

### Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun, No. 94-1387

Merits decision, January 9, 1996

516 U.S. 199. Argued October 31, 1995. On certiorari to the Third Circuit, 40 F.3d 622. A child died in a jet-ski accident in Puerto Rico's territorial waters. The parents invoked admiralty and diversity and alleged negligence, strict liability, and implied warranties. The District Court's certified partial-summary-judgment order treated federal maritime law as displacing state death remedies; both sides obtained permitted interlocutory appeals. The questions are the scope of review of that order and whether state wrongful-death and survival remedies remain available to this nonseafarer.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Review follows the certified order, including its anterior displacement question

**Operative rule:** When a court of appeals permits an interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b), its jurisdiction reaches issues fairly included within the certified order, rather than only the particular question formulated for certification. Here the maritime displacement question is fairly included because it is the premise of the appealed partial-summary-judgment order; this authority reaches no unrelated order in the lawsuit.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice coalition joining Part I of O'Connor's opinion, identified above, directly adopts the rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition on this component and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written certification that an order involves a controlling question of law, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that immediate review may materially advance ultimate termination. A timely application within ten days allows the court of appeals to permit review in its discretion; an application alone does not stay proceedings without an authorized stay order. The certified object of review is the order.

The District Court relied on federal displacement when it granted Yamaha partial summary judgment against future earnings and punitive damages but denied it against loss of society and support or services. Both parties sought certification and obtained permitted appeals. The Third Circuit could examine the anterior displacement issue, which both sides briefed and which underlies the damages rulings, even though the certified question described recoverable damages under a federal maritime cause.

Swint v. Chambers County Commission rejected adding a separate, otherwise nonappealable county defense to the officers' collateral-order appeal for convenience. It preserved §1292(b)'s distinct authorized route. Here that route was actually followed. The Court neither enlarges collateral-order jurisdiction nor grants appellate power over every issue or order associated with the case.

##### State death remedies survive in this nonseafarer territorial-waters case

**Operative rule:** Moragne's federal maritime wrongful-death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state wrongful-death and survival remedies for a nonseafarer killed in territorial waters when no applicable federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule supplies an exclusive recovery regime. This rule concerns persons outside the Jones Act seaman and pertinent federal maritime-employment classes; it does not enlarge remedies within Congress's occupied seaman or high-seas fields.

**Authority:** The unanimous coalition joining Part II of O'Connor's opinion, identified above, directly adopts the remedies rule, its limits, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Moragne v. States Marine Lines supplied a federal wrongful-death action to remove a gap in maritime protection. Adding that remedy did not require withdrawal of state death remedies traditionally available in territorial waters. The Court assumes without deciding that Moragne extends to this nonseafarer and includes a survival action; even on those assumptions, the federal remedy is not an exclusive damages code here.

Miles v. Apex Marine Corp. aligned the remedy for a seaman with Congress's employment-related compensation choices. Offshore Logistics v. Tallentire preserves the Death on the High Seas Act's exclusive regime within its geographical field. Neither source extends that occupied field to a child who was neither a seaman nor a covered longshore worker and who died in Puerto Rico's territorial waters.

Yamaha's appeal to uniformity cannot replace those statutes' coverage conditions. Damages are substantive, so American Dredging's separate protection of state forum administration does not alone decide the issue. The relevant ground is the established coexistence of territorial-water death remedies outside a conflicting federal regime, not a general permission for state law to alter characteristic maritime rights.

The Third Circuit therefore correctly rejected displacement as the basis for Yamaha's partial-summary-judgment motion. The Court does not select Pennsylvania or Puerto Rico law, determine what either wrongful-death or survival law permits, or award any category or amount of damages. Liability standards, defect, negligence, causation, and punitive liability remain unresolved.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Moragne v. States Marine Lines, 398 U.S. 375:** Its federal death remedy does not automatically oust state remedies here. Extension to nonseafarers and inclusion of a survival action are assumed, not decided.
- **Miles v. Apex Marine Corp., 498 U.S. 19:** Its congruence with congressionally prescribed seaman remedies remains; that limitation is not transferred to this uncovered territorial-water death.
- **Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207:** Its protection of DOHSA's exclusive high-seas regime is preserved and distinguished geographically.
- **American Dredging Co. v. Miller, 510 U.S. 443:** Its state-forum practice holding remains distinct; substantive damages do not survive merely because a State labels them procedural.

#### Law After Decision

A permitted §1292(b) appeal includes issues fairly within its certified order, not unrelated orders. State death and survival remedies remain available in this nonseafarer territorial-waters category subject to actual federal displacement. No general rule validating every state maritime remedy or liability standard is announced.

### Allridge v. Scott, Fifth Circuit No. 93-9137

Merits decision, January 15, 1996

Judgment below: 41 F.3d 213 (1994). On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Fifth Circuit's December 15, 1994 judgment affirming denial of federal habeas relief. Allridge remains alive in custody. The submitted questions concern parole information and effective use of mitigation under Texas's capital special issues. His father offered lay testimony about prior prison abuse and alleged mental illness; the defense requested a supplemental mitigation instruction. Unintroduced medical evidence and a separate asserted deterrent effect of the special issues are distinct from that admitted proof.

#### Judgment

| Parole-prediction entitlement | Affirm rejection, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer | No opposing Justice | No relief on this claim |
| Actually admitted mitigation | Vacate and remand, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer | No opposing Justice | Apply Graham and Johnson to the actual sentencing record without the categorical disability/crime-linkage requirement |
| Independent instruction based on unoffered medical evidence and strategic deterrence alone | Affirm rejection, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer | No opposing Justice | No instruction entitlement on assumed evidence; distinct supported legal-impediment claim not decided |

| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | — | Joins Parts I-A, II, and IV; agrees only in the judgment on unoffered evidence | Fixed-legal-fact distinction and proposed Chapman extension; no join to Parts I-B or III |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Ordinary parole availability defeats the asserted release-prediction entitlement

**Operative rule:** Simmons's right to explain legal parole ineligibility does not require a favorable prediction about eventual release when the noncapital alternative permits ordinary parole. The rejection of that asserted entitlement is affirmed; this conclusion neither categorically bars truthful parole evidence nor authorizes a misleading description of the lawful sentencing alternative.

**Authority:** The unanimous coalition joining Part I-A of Souter's opinion, identified above, adopts this eligibility-versus-prediction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Simmons v. South Carolina protects an effective opportunity to tell the jury that the only lawful noncapital sentence excludes ordinary parole when the State raises future dangerousness. That legal fact answers the State's sentencing case; speculative clemency does not change it. Here ordinary parole was legally available. A prediction that release is improbable therefore cannot be substituted for Simmons's absent predicate. The Court does not adopt the lower court's suggestion that danger to inmates universally defeats Simmons. The claim fails because of ordinary parole availability, without reaching harmlessness.

##### The proposed forecast-instruction extension is independently unavailable under Teague

**Operative rule:** Federal habeas relief on a proposed constitutional entitlement to a favorable parole-improbability instruction is unavailable here because no basis establishes that this different rule was dictated when Allridge's conviction became final before Penry in 1989, or that either then-existing Teague exception applies: rules excluding conduct, classes of persons, or punishments from the State's substantive power, or watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. This is an independent ground for rejecting the proposed extension, not a new rule governing accurate information about a fixed statutory minimum.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice coalition joining Part I-B of Souter's opinion, identified above, directly adopts this alternative ground. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Teague requires claim-specific identification of law dictated at finality. Simmons's separate legal-ineligibility premise cannot establish a broader forecast right where ordinary parole remains available. The record supplies no prefinality requirement for that proposed prediction instruction. As Gilmore v. Taylor and Caspari v. Bohlen explain, the then-existing exceptions concern substantive limits on the State's power to punish and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. The requested forecast instruction regulates the jury's information; it does not remove Allridge's conduct, a class to which he belongs, or his punishment from the State's substantive power. Nothing establishes it as a watershed accuracy-and-fairness procedure; asserted usefulness in answering future-danger evidence does not itself meet that demanding category. Neither exception supplies a route to this requested extension. A properly presented claim about a fixed legal minimum would require its own preservation and retroactivity inquiry; this decision does not resolve such a claim.

##### Admitted mitigation must be assessed by its possible independent culpability significance

**Operative rule:** A court may not reject a preserved claim for effective consideration of admitted capital mitigation merely because the evidence fails to establish a uniquely severe permanent disability to which the crime is conclusively attributable. When the actual evidence supports material mitigation of culpability independent of deliberateness or future danger, the court must determine whether, considering the charge, evidence, and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that jurors could not give it effect while answering the special issues truthfully; mere possibility is insufficient.

**Authority:** The unanimous coalition joining Part II of Souter's opinion, identified above, adopts this correction of the reviewing rule and bounded remand, without finding an actual instructional violation.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham v. Collins requires an effective sentencing route for record-supported mitigation with independent culpability significance and rejects a uniquely severe permanent-handicap restriction. Its background holding does not require that the evidence lack all relevance to future danger. Johnson v. Texas applies the whole-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry: admission and partial future-danger relevance do not suffice if truthful special-issue answers prevent the supported mitigating use. Those rules preserve Jurek's conditional approval of the Texas scheme while enforcing Lockett and Eddings's requirement that relevant mitigation receive lawful effect.

The father testified about alleged mental illness and prior prison abuse. The lower court rejected the claim because his nonexpert account failed to establish the crime-attribution and severe-disability linkage. That categorical requirement is no longer a lawful reviewing premise. His lack of diagnostic expertise limits what the testimony proves; it does not erase admitted lay evidence or establish that no abuse occurred.

The competing contention—that the testimony may still be weak, unsupported, or adequately usable under the actual charge—remains for proper review. The Court has not found psychiatric illness, credibility, causation, material independent mitigating force, or the absence of an effective sentencing route. The jury need not assign favorable weight. No categorical additional special issue, facial invalidity, or new general mitigation category follows. Because the stated lower ground is legally insufficient, the rejection of this admitted-evidence claim is vacated and remanded.

##### Unoffered medical evidence supplies no independent instruction entitlement

**Operative rule:** A defendant cannot obtain a mitigation instruction on the assumed content of medical evidence never presented merely because presenting it might also support future dangerousness. The independent instruction claim based on that unoffered evidence fails, while a supported challenge to an actual unconstitutional exclusion or legal impediment remains distinct and is not decided here.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice coalition joining Part III of Souter's opinion, identified above, adopts the rationale; Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of this component in the judgment only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Delo v. Lashley permits a requirement of some evidence supporting a requested mitigating circumstance while protecting meaningful use of mitigation actually offered. Evidence carrying both mitigating and aggravating implications can create a difficult strategic choice. That concern alone does not establish the missing diagnosis, its content, admissibility, or mitigating significance, and does not require jurors to act on assumed proof. The separate admitted-evidence inquiry continues under Part II. This ruling does not compel a defendant to present evidence formally excluded by an unconstitutional rule, foreclose a supported challenge to an actual legal barrier, or adjudicate a new ineffective-assistance claim.

##### Correcting the reviewing standard does not itself require a sentencing writ

**Operative rule:** The admitted-evidence mitigation component returns for review of the actual evidence, charge, prefinality law, and any properly available defenses and error-effect inquiry; vacatur alone establishes neither an available violation nor entitlement to relief. If a defect ultimately requires correction, relief concerns punishment through adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt untouched; no present writ, hearing, release, stay, deadline, or execution authorization issues.

**Authority:** The unanimous coalition joining Part IV of Souter's opinion, identified above, adopts the bounded remand and reservations.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham's prefinality analysis bars treating its established functional rule as newly created merely because another defendant invokes it. A materially new extension nevertheless requires its own Teague inquiry. The documented instruction request proves that a request was made, not exhaustion or preservation of every reformulated theory. Applicable factual presumptions and genuinely available exhaustion, default, and other habeas defenses remain; no forfeited defense is revived and no settled issue reopened.

Tuggle corrects a discrete legally insufficient review ground without resolving the underlying violation or issuing a writ. That distinction governs this remand too. Brecht governs preserved Doyle error and O'Neal preserved personal-intent instructional error. Neither already selects a universal collateral harmlessness rule for capital mitigation. The Court reserves whether a separate error-effect inquiry is required here, its applicable standard, the actual effect, and ultimate relief. The complete charge, relevant testimony, claim-specific procedural rulings, and any properly preserved error-effect submissions are needed before further relief. Unreviewed Brady and lesser-offense claims are untouched.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154:** Distinguished because ordinary parole was legally available; its legal-ineligibility trigger remains, without a new prison-danger exception.
- **Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288:** Applied separately to the proposed forecast extension. Its claim-specific prefinality inquiry and exceptions for substantive limits on punishment and watershed accuracy-and-fairness procedures remain; neither exception is established here.
- **Gilmore v. Taylor, 508 U.S. 333, and Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383:** Their application of the two then-existing Teague exceptions is followed; an important procedural protection does not by that importance alone satisfy the demanding watershed category.
- **Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461:** Applied to reject the severe-permanent-disability and exclusive-future-danger restrictions. Its independent background and youth holdings remain; its fractured positive-character component supplies no general rule.
- **Johnson v. Texas, 509 U.S. 350, and Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370:** Applied through the complete-charge reasonable-likelihood standard, preserving the distinction between admission and effective mitigating use.
- **Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262:** Its conditional approval survives; Texas's special issues are not facially invalidated.
- **Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104:** Their effective-mitigation requirement remains, without compelled favorable weight.
- **Delo v. Lashley, 507 U.S. 272:** Its supporting-evidence requirement is applied to the unoffered medical material; it neither excludes admitted lay mitigation nor bars a supported unconstitutional-exclusion challenge. It creates no expert-only rule, adverse inference from silence, or reduction of the State's aggravation burden.
- **Tuggle v. Netherland, October 30, 1995:** Its distinction between correction of an insufficient review ground and actual relief governs this remand; its separate Ake subject is not transferred.
- **Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O'Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995:** Their category-specific Chapman rules remain, without a controlling extension to capital mitigation.
- **Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18:** No new collateral application is adopted by the Court; the Chief Justice proposes one separately.

#### Law After Decision

Ordinary parole availability does not become legal ineligibility through a prediction of unlikely release. Admitted mitigation must receive the functional inquiry required by Graham and Johnson; an expert diagnosis or uniquely severe disability is not a categorical entrance requirement. Unoffered medical material establishes no independent instruction entitlement merely because its introduction would present strategic risks. The actual instructional violation, available habeas gates, any error-effect standard and finding, and relief remain unresolved. The proposed Chapman extension has no controlling force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins the parole-prediction holding, the admitted-mitigation ruling, and the bounded remand. A jury may not be told that a parole-eligible sentence legally excludes release. A fixed statutory minimum, if properly presented as a distinct claim, is a legal fact rather than a favorable prediction; that distinction establishes neither preservation nor retroactive entitlement here. He does not join the Court's independent Teague rationale or its additional rationale concerning unoffered medical evidence.

If a demonstrated, preserved denial of effective capital mitigation requires a separate harmless-error inquiry, he would extend the rationale of Brecht and O'Neal and require the State to establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the whole relevant record that the denial did not contribute to the death sentence. This is a proposed extension to a distinct category; those decisions do not already decide it. He does not find a violation, harmlessness, or a present right to a writ. Correction, if required, would be confined to punishment through adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. The unintroduced medical evidence and separate chilling contention require their own lawful basis. No other Justice joins this proposal or opinion.

Applicable factual presumptions remain. No forfeited defense is revived and no settled ruling reopened.
