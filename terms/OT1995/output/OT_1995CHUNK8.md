### Lockheed Corp. v. Spink, No. 95-809

Merits decision, June 10, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**517 U.S. 882.** Argued April 22, 1996, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 60 F.3d 616. The questions concern fiduciary status and prohibited transactions under an amended pension plan offering enhanced retirement benefits for employment-related releases, and OBRA's application to earlier uncredited service.

Spink returned to Lockheed in 1979 at age 61 and was then lawfully excluded from its plan. He entered on December 25, 1988, the first day of the relevant plan year. He declined a 1990 enhanced-retirement offer because he would not release his claims. The complaint distinguishes sponsor adoption from Retirement Committee implementation; its allegations are accepted at the dismissal stage, not found after trial. The Ninth Circuit reinstated Counts I–III while leaving independent loyalty and anti-inurement grounds unaddressed and awarding appellate fees.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0 overall.** The component rulings differ as follows.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fiduciary liability based solely on sponsor design or adoption | Rejected, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Dismiss the theory resting solely on amendment. |
| Section 406(a)(1)(D) release-payment classification | Reviewed per se theory rejected; seven support deciding it | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg | Souter, Breyer | Souter and Breyer would remand without a merits answer. |
| Counts I–II: OBRA credit for earlier lawful nonparticipation | Reinstatement reversed and dismissal restored, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without adding a ground. |
| Preserved independent Count III alternatives | Returned for consideration; all nine support | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Consider their own elements consistently with the decided holdings; no unconditional dismissal of Count III or finding of breach. |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. Plan design and plan administration are different functions

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** An employer acts as a plan sponsor, rather than an ERISA fiduciary, when designing and adopting the pension-plan amendments at issue. Subject to section 408's statutory exceptions, section 406(a)(1) requires a fiduciary to cause a transaction that the fiduciary knows or should know is of a prohibited kind; employer party-in-interest status alone does not satisfy that requirement, and actual implementation must be examined as a separate function.

**Controlling explanation:** Curtiss-Wright recognizes the sponsor's plan-amendment role. Section 3(21) attaches fiduciary status to the functions performed, not to every employer act affecting future expenditures. Pension funding and vesting requirements do not transform design itself into fiduciary administration. Varity requires a separate inquiry when the employer actually performs an administrative function; John Hancock likewise separates assets, control and fiduciary duties. Thus sponsor status cannot dispose of the distinct allegation that the Committee controlled plan distributions. The asset-disposition branch does not acquire an extra discretion requirement. The holding leaves amendment procedures and ERISA's other substantive commands in force, and does not establish authorized adoption or lawful implementation merely from amendment power.

##### II. Employer benefit from the release does not alone prohibit the payment

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the seven identified in Opinion Topology.

**Operative rule:** Payment of the described genuine enhanced retirement benefits under an otherwise lawful plan is not the transaction prohibited by section 406(a)(1)(D) merely because the required employment-related releases benefit the employer. This rule assumes fiduciary implementation for the legal inquiry and does not immunize a sham asset transfer, kickback, independently unlawful plan or unenforceable release.

**Controlling explanation:** The prohibited-transaction provision addresses dealings with parties in interest of a different character from these plan benefits. Employers obtain recruitment, retention and retirement advantages from ordinary benefit programs; that advantage alone cannot turn payment to participants into prohibited diversion. The release has value, but Keystone's exchange of property to discharge an enforceable funding obligation is a distinct transaction. Surplus does not become employer property, and the Court creates no section 408 exemption or general net-benefit defense.

The rule rejects the asserted per se section 406 theory on the accepted allegations. Independent participation, funding, vesting, loyalty and anti-inurement requirements remain. Section 406(b)'s different prohibitions are not merged into the presented question. Whether a particular waiver is enforceable under the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act or other law is undecided. No sham or kickback is alleged in the issue decided; no finding of loss, deception or hidden diversion is made.

##### III. OBRA does not require the claimed credit for earlier lawful exclusion

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the eight Associate Justices. The Chief Justice agrees with the disposition only.

**Operative rule:** OBRA sections 9201 and 9202 do not require pension credit for Spink's service during his earlier lawful exclusion from participation. Section 9204(a)(1)'s express direction applies their new accrual protections to plan years beginning on or after January 1, 1988 and employees with an hour of service in a covered year; a smaller total benefit from earlier uncredited service is not itself a reduced accrual rate during a covered year.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf requires the Court first to apply Congress's enacted temporal direction. That direction resolves this claim. Applying the new protection to a participant in a covered plan year does not convert all prior lawful nonparticipation into credited service. The later date of benefit calculation and the statute's general antidiscrimination purpose supply no contrary command.

Section 9204(b) separately applies section 9203's participation amendments to covered plan years and service performed on or after January 1, 1988. Its additional service-date language is not inserted into subsection (a). The provisions perform distinct work. The plan year began on December 25, 1988; this ruling establishes no independent violation during the preceding part of that calendar year.

For a plan maintained under one or more collective-bargaining agreements between employee representatives and employers ratified before March 1, 1986, section 9204(a)(2) applies subsection (a)(1) to benefits pursuant to, and individuals covered by, those agreements using a substituted date: the commencement of the first plan year beginning on or after the earlier of January 1, 1990 or the later of January 1, 1988 and termination of the last qualifying agreement, disregarding extensions after February 28, 1986. No allegation invokes that transition. Section 9204(c) permits delayed formal amendment until the first plan year beginning on or after January 1, 1989 only if interim operation complies and the amendment covers that interval retroactively. A pension plan is not treated as failing to provide definitely determinable benefits or contributions, or to operate according to its provisions, merely because it operates under that subsection. This does not postpone substantive compliance. No general rule forbidding every use of a past fact, or new default presumption of temporal reach, is adopted.

##### IV. Independent claims and fees follow their own grounds

**Authority:** Part IV-A has nine joins; Part IV-B has eight Associate joins and the Chief Justice's agreement in the disposition.

**Operative directions and explanation:** The sponsor-capacity holding forecloses section 404 liability resting solely on design or adoption. Actually preserved independent fiduciary implementation and section 403(c)(1) anti-inurement grounds remain for ordinary lower-court consideration; no new allegation or claim is supplied. Varity, Mertens and Peacock preserve the distinct statutory predicate and remedy requirements rather than guaranteeing relief. The appellate fee award is vacated because its stated grounds included the substantive conclusions rejected here; reconsideration must rest on the remaining litigation and applicable fee law.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen:** Applied to sponsor amendment of a pension plan; its amendment-procedure requirements remain separate from actual authorized adoption.
- **Varity Corp. v. Howe:** Applied to distinguish sponsorship from administration and preserve its qualified equitable-remedy rule. No general immunity for misleading or disloyal fiduciary conduct follows.
- **John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank:** Applied to keep asset status, fiduciary function, breach and remedy distinct.
- **Commissioner v. Keystone Consolidated Industries, Inc.:** Distinguished by its property-for-funding-obligation exchange; valuable consideration alone does not equate that transaction with these participant benefits.
- **Mertens v. Hewitt Associates:** Its remedial-category requirements remain in force on any properly preserved claim.
- **Peacock v. Thomas:** Its predicate requirements remain in force on any properly preserved claim.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Its enacted-direction-first inquiry is applied. The Court does not reach a new default temporal rule.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Its enacted-direction-first inquiry is applied. The Court does not reach a new default temporal rule.

#### Law After Decision

Sponsor amendment alone is not fiduciary conduct. Genuine release-conditioned benefits do not become prohibited section 406(a)(1)(D) transfers merely through employer advantage. Separate administration, anti-inurement and release-validity requirements survive. OBRA's distinct effective-date provisions foreclose the claimed credit for this earlier lawful exclusion without deciding a different covered-period discrimination claim.

#### Separate Writings

**Breyer, joined by Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree with the sponsor-capacity and OBRA rulings and with reversal and remand, but would not decide the implementation-based prohibited-transaction question. Whether the release-conditioned distribution falls within section 406 is a difficult technical issue that would benefit from further lower-court consideration and informed submissions. Assuming a fiduciary function and preserving independent statutory duties does not eliminate their objection to deciding that classification now. They would return it without holding either that the payment is prohibited or that every benefit payment is immune.

The complaint posture, mixed decree and preserved alternatives appear in the [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/60/cases/0616-01.json). The controlling text is ERISA's separate fiduciary, duty and transaction provisions and [OBRA section 9204, 100 Stat. 1979–1980](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-100/pdf/STATUTE-100-Pg1874.pdf). No unreported release term, actual loss or fee amount is established by this decision.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the component rulings and reconsideration of appellate fees. The Court orders no damages amount, benefit payment, invalidation of a particular release or finding of an independent breach.

### United States v. International Business Machines Corp., No. 95-591

Merits decision, June 10, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**517 U.S. 843.** Argued March 18, 1996, on certiorari to the Federal Circuit. The question is whether the Export Clause permits application of the general foreign-insurance premium tax in 26 U.S.C. §4371 to insurance of goods in export transit.

For 1975–1984, IBM paid assessments concerning foreign policies covering its exported products and sought refunds. The Federal Circuit, 59 F.3d 1234, affirmed the Court of Federal Claims' ruling that this application was unconstitutional. The policies attach to identified shipments; their premiums reflect both cargo value and shipment-specific risks. Section 4371 is not confined to exports.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Kennedy, Ginsburg.

**Not participating:** Stevens, who takes no part in consideration or decision.

The judgment requiring refund of the challenged unconstitutional assessments stands within the claim and amount properly preserved. The Court neither determines a new refund amount nor invalidates section 4371 in all its applications.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer. Justice Kennedy dissents, joined by Justice Ginsburg.

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, with the six direct joins identified above, controls the rule, application and limits below.

##### The general tax cannot reach this export-transit insurance

**Operative rule:** A federal tax on goods in export transit does not escape the Export Clause merely because it applies without discrimination to other transactions. Thames & Mersey remains controlling for premiums on insurance of goods during that transit, so section 4371 cannot be applied to the insurance at issue here; the rule does not exempt every activity or expense that indirectly affects exporting.

**Controlling explanation:** The Constitution expressly forbids federal taxes or duties on articles exported from a State. Fairbank protects export bills of lading, and Hvoslef protects charter arrangements for export carriage; those cases prevent taxation of the protected export transaction through its integral documents. Thames & Mersey directly applies that protection to insurance of goods during the export voyage.

The Government's contrary argument rests on nondiscrimination and changes in other tax doctrines. Those doctrines answer different constitutional questions. Michelin concerns the States' Import-Export restriction and goods no longer in transit. Washington Stevedoring concerns a service tax and reserves direct taxation of goods in transit. Neither converts the federal Export Clause into a prohibition only of discriminatory taxes. Itel preserves the direct-export question while sustaining a distinct local container transaction; Jefferson Lines concerns a local retail sale under the Commerce Clause. Fulton requires comparison of actual taxable events within that Clause, rather than transferring its standard to different constitutional text.

The insurance here covers the identified outward shipments. Risk as well as cargo value affects the premium, but that does not distinguish the export-insurance category already decided in Thames & Mersey. The Government's asserted interclause developments do not justify overruling that directly applicable precedent. Its decision to press nondiscrimination, while declining to advance a separate goods-versus-services challenge, also counsels against undertaking a comprehensive reconsideration of that boundary here. An included legal argument may be considered; the Court is not required to decide it.

The Court retains the distinction between taxing exports and imposing ordinary burdens before exportation or on genuinely distinct or tangential activities. It leaves fuller classification of other services open. Applying the existing insurance rule is sufficient to preserve this refund judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Thames & Mersey Marine Insurance Co. v. United States, 237 U.S. 19:** Retained and applied to export-transit insurance; its directly governing rule is not displaced by nondiscrimination alone.
- **Fairbank v. United States, 181 U.S. 283:** Applied as the export-document precedent supporting the Clause's protection of the export transaction.
- **United States v. Hvoslef, 237 U.S. 1:** Applied as the export-carriage precedent supporting the Clause's protection of the export transaction.
- **Michelin Tire Corp. v. Wages, 423 U.S. 276:** Distinguished by its state-tax setting and goods outside export transit; it does not replace the federal Clause's rule.
- **Department of Revenue v. Association of Washington Stevedoring Cos., 435 U.S. 734:** Distinguished as a service-tax decision reserving direct goods-in-transit taxation.
- **Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston:** Distinguished by the separate local container transaction and its reserved direct-export question.
- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc.:** Its transaction-specific Commerce Clause rule remains effective in its own setting and supplies no federal Export Clause transformation.
- **Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner:** Its transaction-specific Commerce Clause rule remains effective in its own setting and supplies no federal Export Clause transformation.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Its permission to consider an included argument remains discretionary. The Court announces no new review-scope restriction.

#### Law After Decision

The Export Clause continues to protect the export-insurance application governed by Thames & Mersey even under a generally applicable, nondiscriminatory federal tax. No comprehensive immunity for export-related services or incidental exporter costs is adopted. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would reach the narrower argument that the tax falls on a purchased indemnity service rather than the exported articles. Itel and Jefferson Lines illustrate the need to identify the actual taxable transaction. The stipulated premium reflects shipment risks as well as cargo value; that relationship does not make the service identical to the goods. The dissent would expressly overrule Thames & Mersey to the extent its commercial-integration rationale treats this genuine insurance-premium tax as a tax on exports.

The dissent regards the narrower issue as fairly included in the question presented and sufficiently supported by the stipulated record despite the Government's emphasis on nondiscrimination. It would reverse and remand without validating all export-associated taxes, creating a nondiscrimination safe harbor for an actual goods tax, or determining a new assessment amount. Its service classification does not command a majority.

#### Source Notes

The record and litigation positions are supported by the [Federal Circuit opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/59/cases/1234-01.json) and the [petition and party briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0659). The applicable insurance-tax terms are those governing the 1975–1984 assessments; later statutory exemptions are not substituted. Reported argument statements establish the described presentation limits without resolving any unpresented exemption or computation question.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The refund judgment remains in force subject to ordinary mandate procedures. No new trial, tax computation or further Supreme Court proceeding is ordered.

**End of entry: United States v. International Business Machines Corp., merits decision, June 10, 1996.**

### United States v. Myers

Merits decision, June 11, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Seventh Circuit's judgment, 46 F.3d 668, in No. 94-1912. The questions are whether the targeted thermal examination of Myers's home was a Fourth Amendment search and what follows for his preserved suppression claim.

Police used an Agema 210 on March 12, 1992, to record comparative heat from the residence. The passive instrument emitted no rays and did not penetrate the viewed object; the reported pattern indicated concealed high-intensity cultivation lights. Police used the scan and other evidence to obtain the April 22 entry warrant. After suppression was denied, Myers entered a conditional guilty plea under §841(a)(1), preserving review of that denial, including his contention that the later warrant rested on the unlawful scan. The Seventh Circuit affirmed on the ground that the scan was not a search.

#### Judgment

**Vacated in the stated part and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Kennedy, Breyer.

The Court vacates the Seventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains denial of Myers's preserved suppression claim and the dependent conviction affirmance on the no-search premise. The lower courts must dispose of that claim under Pinson and the preservation and remedy limits below. The Court orders neither final suppression nor final admission of the evidence, and does not order acquittal, release, retrial, a replacement sentence or immediate withdrawal of the conditional plea.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Vacatur and remand | Parts I–III in full |
| Opinion of the Court, partial join | Scalia | Stone-Zsela | Vacatur and remand | Parts I and III; not Part II |
| Dissent | Kennedy | Breyer | Would affirm | In full |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

**Authority:** Parts I and III of Scalia's opinion control with seven direct joins: the six-Justice whole-opinion coalition identified above and Stone-Zsela. Part II controls with the six whole-opinion joins. No dissenting or judgment-only position is needed to establish any holding.

##### I. This targeted acquisition of concealed domestic information is a search

**Operative rule:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home's heat patterns is a search when it reveals otherwise unobservable information about concealed domestic operation. External reception, passive equipment and the absence of a recognizable interior image do not defeat that protection; equipment use or investigative purpose alone does not establish a search.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinson supplies the precise rule for this acquisition. Its application of Katz protects concealed domestic information, and Karo establishes that technology can obtain protected information about a home without a physical entry. The Agema 210's heat pattern disclosed the operation of indoor cultivation lights that ordinary outside observation did not reveal. The Court need not find that the instrument identified a person, produced an intimate silhouette or penetrated a wall.

The contrary argument confuses the physical location of heat with the information acquired. Roof vents and failure to conceal emissions do not establish that Myers exposed the operation of his home to this examination. Greenwood concerns garbage delivered for collection; the underlying domestic information here was not similarly discarded. Place concerns detection confined to contraband; thermal patterns can reveal lawful domestic activity as well. The absence of curbside garbage supplied an investigative inference, not exposure of the scanned activity. No different record involving unaided observation of visible smoke is before the Court.

Pinson therefore forecloses the lower court's no-search ground. This application adopts no general rule for all instruments, emissions or uses of technology, and no new test based on market availability or predicted future capabilities.

##### II. The later entry warrant did not authorize the earlier scan

**Operative rule:** This search required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied. No such exception is established on this record, and the April 22 entry warrant could not retroactively authorize the March 12 scan.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinson's warrant holding applies to the same protected acquisition. Investigation supported by other suspicious circumstances does not itself establish an exception to the warrant requirement. Whren's requirement of authority for the particular intrusion reinforces that distinction; its rule for an objectively justified ordinary traffic stop supplies no authorization to examine a home thermally. The Court finds no established exception here, while reserving the distinct admissibility of evidence obtained under the later warrant.

##### III. The preserved suppression claim returns for a separate remedy determination

**Operative rule:** When rejection of a preserved suppression claim rests on this erroneous no-search ground, the affected judgment must be vacated and the claim decided under the correct search rule. Remand permits consideration of the actual preserved derivative-warrant contention and independently supported, procedurally available alternatives; it neither presumes exclusion nor establishes a new defense or revives a waived claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinson separates a search violation from the remedy. Karo permits a distinct inquiry into whether untainted information supports a later warrant. Unlike Pinson's litigant, Myers expressly challenged the warrant's dependence on the scan and preserved suppression review. That reservation does not preserve every newly formulated objection.

The published record does not establish the full affidavit's independent sufficiency, objective reliance, independent source or inevitable discovery. Other investigative information and the fact that a warrant issued cannot substitute for those determinations. Leon is available only if independently supported and procedurally open. It does not protect knowing or reckless deception of the magistrate, abandonment of judicial neutrality, reliance on an affidavit so lacking probable-cause indicia that reliance is wholly unreasonable, or reliance on a facially deficient warrant precluding reasonable reliance. Its inquiry includes officers obtaining or supplying information and assumes execution within the warrant's scope. Evans concerns qualifying non-law-enforcement court-employee clerical error and objectively reasonable reliance; it creates no exception for this deliberate police scan.

The mandate requires no new hearing, discovery or automatic affidavit-excision proceeding. It decides no absent factual predicate. A genuinely forfeited additional issue would remain subject to Olano's requirements of error, plainness and an effect on substantial rights, ordinarily outcome prejudice, followed by the separate discretionary requirement of serious effect on fairness, integrity or public reputation. Intentional waiver extinguishes the relinquished error. Those requirements are not imposed on the claim Myers preserved. Rule 11(a)(2) permits withdrawal of a conditional plea if the defendant prevails on appeal; this limited correction, leaving the suppression outcome unresolved, does not itself order withdrawal. Ordinary proceedings must resolve the available grounds before any final admission or exclusion determination.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Pinson, May 8, 1995:** Applied to the demonstrated thermal acquisition, its warrant requirement and its separate remedy inquiry. Its individual affidavit nonchallenge is distinguished from Myers's preserved derivative contention; its rules remain intact.
- **Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347:** Applied through Pinson to protection of concealed domestic information; physical penetration is not indispensable.
- **United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705:** Applied to technological acquisition of protected home information and retained for the distinct possibility that untainted information sustains a later warrant.
- **California v. Greenwood, 486 U.S. 35:** Distinguished because deliberate disposal of garbage does not establish exposure of concealed domestic operation through heat emissions.
- **United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696:** Distinguished because this examination can reveal lawful domestic activity and is not confined to contraband.
- **Whren v. United States, June 10, 1996:** Its particular-intrusion requirement is retained; objectively justified traffic enforcement supplies no home-scan exception.
- **United States v. Leon, 468 U.S. 897:** Retained with its factual, procedural and reliance limits. No qualifying reliance finding or new police-technology exception is made.
- **Arizona v. Evans, March 1, 1995:** Confined to its qualifying court-clerical-error setting; it does not excuse the deliberate thermal examination.
- **United States v. Olano:** Retained for genuinely forfeited additional issues and distinguished from the suppression claim actually preserved here; intentional waiver remains distinct from forfeiture.
- **Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996:** Its legal and factual review allocation remains intact. It neither shields a categorical legal error nor orders a new hearing or displaces the distinct review of an issuing magistrate's warrant determination.

#### Law After Decision

Pinson continues to govern targeted thermal examinations that reveal otherwise unobservable concealed domestic operation. Neither passive external reception nor the lack of a recognizable interior image removes this examination from the Fourth Amendment. Its warrant and exclusionary inquiries remain distinct. Myers's actual reservation preserves the derivative-warrant contention without enlarging it to every possible objection. No earlier decision is overruled, and no final rule admitting or excluding the later seized evidence is adopted.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent maintains the content-specific position it took in Pinson: a comparative measurement of exterior warmth, followed by an inference about its cause, does not itself acquire the concealed interior fact protected in Karo. Katz requires attention to the information actually obtained; neither concern about more revealing future equipment nor the fact that police used a device resolves that question. The dissent would reject Pinson's application to this kind of exterior measurement and affirm. It does not claim that a lower court may disregard existing precedent or that all externally received information is unprotected.

The dissent does not adopt a general rule that heat is abandoned property, treat the scanner as confined to contraband, or sustain admission on an unproved reliance or warrant-sufficiency ground. If the Court retains Pinson, the distinction between violation and remedy and the actual preservation limits still apply. That conditional agreement supplies no vote for the Court's vacatur or for its search holding.

#### Source Notes

The [Seventh Circuit opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/46/cases/0668-01.json) and [OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/46/f3d/668) support the investigative facts, device description, conditional plea and preserved derivative-warrant claim. It does not supply the full affidavit or resolve the alternative exclusionary grounds; this does not establish that the underlying materials are absent from the litigation record. The reported docket is the appellate docket, and no separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is reported.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the lower courts for lawful disposition of the preserved suppression claim and the judgment dependent on it, subject to ordinary mandate procedures. The search determination binds those proceedings. Available remedial grounds remain for decision within their actual factual and procedural predicates; the Court schedules no further event.

**End of entry: United States v. Myers, merits decision, June 11, 1996.**

### Bush v. Vera; Lawson v. Vera; United States v. Vera, Nos. 94-805, 94-806 and 94-988

Merits decision, June 13, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

These direct appeals under 28 U.S.C. §1253 arise from the three-judge Southern District of Texas court's declaration and injunction concerning congressional Districts 18, 29 and 30. The Court decides the residents' standing and whether the racial design of those shared constituencies establishes the asserted equal-protection violation under Shaw and Miller.

Blum and Powers reside in District 18, Thomas and Vera in District 29, Orcutt in District 30, and Chen in District 25. The five residents challenge their own race-based assignments; Chen alleges no personal connection to the assignments in the three districts. No plaintiff asserts dilution, exclusion, unequal participation or another personal injury supporting this decree. The court below invalidated these districts and rejected challenges to 21 others. The state defendants, Lawson and the private defendant-intervenors, and the United States as defendant-intervenor appeal, respectively; no plaintiff appeal is before the Court.

The August 17, 1994 opinion was followed by a September 2 relief order and September 20 amended injunction. The lower court found racial predominance and subordination of ordinary districting considerations amid disputed incumbent, political and community explanations. The Court assumes the strongest predominance premise for deciding the legal sufficiency of the claims; it makes no conclusive determination of the disputed Dallas purpose findings.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

**Threshold authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices identified above, controls Holdings I–III by eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela supplies the ninth threshold judgment vote without joining that reasoning.

##### I. The five residents' own assignment claims satisfy standing

**Operative rule:** Under Miller, voters personally subjected to the challenged electoral assignment may litigate their asserted unequal treatment when their own exposure, causation and likely redress are established, even if their substantive theory fails. Residence alone or disagreement with racial districting is insufficient, but these five plaintiffs' own assignments supply that personal relationship without an additional financial loss, electoral defeat or denial of service.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller separates personal-assignment standing from success on the design-only claim. Blum and Powers challenge their assignment to District 18, Thomas and Vera theirs to District 29, and Orcutt his to District 30. The plan directly causes that treatment, and relief against its use would redress the claimed wrong if their theory were valid. Northeastern Florida likewise separates exposure to challenged unequal treatment from proof of winning an ultimate benefit; Warth keeps standing distinct from merits success.

Lujan's litigation-stage factual-support requirement remains. The established lower adjudication supplies the residents' actual relationship here; unsupported allegations do not invariably suffice after trial. Neither a new residence dispute nor a need for an expert report, alternative map or changed-election proof is established. Configuration testimony, misdirected-ballot accounts and Blum's campaign are not rewritten as independent injuries.

The Court declines to replace Miller's threshold rule. The absence of an additional downstream injury cannot distinguish a holding that recognized this same personal-assignment relationship. That threshold conclusion does not establish a constitutional violation or entitlement to an injunction.

##### II. Chen cannot borrow another resident's stake

**Operative rule:** Without a personal connection to the challenged assignments, a plaintiff cannot obtain standing for their relief from another plaintiff's exposure or a generalized concern about the plan. Chen supplies no independent basis for relief concerning Districts 18, 29 and 30, but his deficiency does not defeat their personally affected residents' claims.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a personal stake rather than an undifferentiated interest in lawful government. Chen lives in District 25 and alleges neither assignment to the three districts nor another personal connection to their challenged classifications. Each district nevertheless has at least one personally affected plaintiff. No broader nonresident or cross-district theory needs decision. The defendants' appeals do not reopen Chen's unsuccessful District 25 claim or the other rejected district challenges.

##### III. The three merits injunction appeals are directly reviewable

**Operative rule:** The required three-judge court's merits grant of injunctive relief in this substantial constitutional apportionment controversy supports direct review under §§2284 and 1253. Standing having been sustained, the Court reaches the presented constitutional merits and corresponding decree in all three appeals.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller distinguishes a merits grant or denial of an injunction from a nonmerits dismissal requiring its own review-channel analysis. These appeals concern the former. A serious constitutional controversy does not become jurisdictionally frivolous merely because governing precedent supplies a strong merits answer. The declaration and injunction are therefore reviewed through the established direct channel, without inventing a new appeal, additional party or collateral preclearance action.

**Merits authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls Holdings IV–V with five direct joins. No dissenting rationale or Marks combination supplies their authority.

##### IV. Racial predominance alone does not establish the asserted unequal treatment

**Operative rule:** Race-conscious design of a shared territorial constituency, including predominant racial consideration that subordinates ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself establish this equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. Even assuming the challenged predominance and subordination in Districts 18, 29 and 30, the sole presented design-only theory therefore cannot sustain their invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw supplies the substantive distinction between territorial design and an independently cognizable deprivation. Miller applied it even when race predominated and ordinary criteria were subordinated; moving from assumed purpose to findings does not distinguish its legal rule. SCLC applies the same boundary while preserving real statutory opportunity claims and lawful remedies.

The contrary argument identifies the assignment itself as the unconstitutional classification. Miller rejected that inference for a shared electorate: composition alone imposes no racial eligibility condition, political belief, inferior civic membership or legal duty for a representative to serve only one race. Croson and Adarand concern operative allocation of opportunities; their standards are not replaced by treating every consideration of race in district design as that same act.

Actual exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior civic status or another recognized deprivation can be complete in government's own conduct. Gomillion, Anderson, White and McLaurin preserve those protections without requiring a changed election winner, monetary loss or second governmental act. None is pleaded or found as an independent basis for this decree. The Court neither imposes a universal downstream-loss requirement nor treats formal access as a complete equality guarantee.

The trial findings and competing political explanations therefore need no conclusive appellate resolution to decide these claims. Shape remains evidence, not an automatic wrong. The Court does not determine whether these districts satisfy strict scrutiny on a hypothesis it rejects. Nor does it require a federal command for otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions: Miller and Voinovich preserve that distinction. Sections 2 and 5 retain their separate proof, justification and enforcement requirements; no extra constitutional-injury element is added to a statutory claim.

##### V. Relief dependent solely on the rejected claim must end

**Operative rule:** Because the sole adjudicated basis for the three-district decree fails on the merits, each appeal requires reversal and remand to enter judgment for defendants on those claims and terminate their dependent declaration, injunction and compulsory-redraw relief. The mandate does not revive dismissed counts, disturb the 21 unappealed district rulings or authorize a new inquiry to manufacture a different injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw and Miller distinguish a failing substantive theory from jurisdictional dismissal. The five residents have standing, but their claim supplies no legal basis for the challenged decree. Leaving that decree in force would preserve relief for the very violation the Court holds unestablished. Reversal therefore reaches the September 20 injunction and the compelled plan/remedial proceedings founded solely on it.

No independent pleaded claim sustains those remedies: the statutory, state-constitutional and Fifteenth Amendment counts were dismissed, and the complaint's Fifth Amendment reference supplied no separate relief. The general confusion and campaign evidence is not a newly adjudicated personal injury. The appropriate return is implementation of judgment for defendants, not continued merits retention or a new liability trial.

The fee-application direction is not an award. It cannot supply entitlement derived from the reversed merits judgment, but the Court decides no amount or distinct collateral fee issue. Any properly presented collateral request retains its own requirements. No map is approved or prescribed, and no federal preclearance decision, unappealed district judgment or hypothetical future claim is adjudicated by this mandate.

#### Law After Decision

Personal exposure to the challenged assignment permits these residents to litigate, but the sole asserted territorial-design theory fails under Shaw and Miller even on the strongest predominance premise. Independent constitutional and statutory wrongs retain their own elements and remedies; they require neither an invariably separate later loss nor a new constitutional-injury prerequisite for §2. #### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the standing judgment and joining the merits opinion.** Stone would overrule Miller's threshold holding only insofar as personal assignment to a race-conscious shared constituency alone supplies injury. Exposure identifies the affected person but does not necessarily establish concrete unequal treatment. A common constituency does not by design alone assign different political identities or make its representative responsible to one race. Predicted neglect or inferior status requires a supporting governmental operation.

Concrete exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, impaired competitive opportunity, inferior civic status or another supported personal injury may itself suffice; these examples are not exhaustive and require no additional financial loss, electoral defeat or second governmental act. Northeastern Florida preserves unequal opportunity as injury, while Warth distinguishes standing from success. Allen's rejection of abstract stigma does not settle personal assignment, and Bell's narrow insubstantial-federal-question doctrine supplies no substitute answer. Ordinary pleading and proof remain, with no new injury prerequisite for statutory Voting Rights Act claims and no race-dependent distinction.

Stone acknowledges that the same assignment allegations supported Miller, so his proposal changes that rule. He claims no intervening law, new fact or demonstrated unworkability. Losing access to a judicial forum is a real reliance cost; he considers the asserted Article III error sufficient despite that cost, while fewer suits or a preferred policy outcome would not be enough. His proposed jurisdictional disposition would vacate the dependent decree and dismiss the design-only claims without prejudice, reaching the whole action only if no independently supported claim remained. That proposal does not prevail. Under unrevised Miller he recognizes the residents' standing and Chen's separate deficiency; he then fully joins the Court's merits reversal under Shaw and Miller. Today's operative disposition is on the merits, not his proposed jurisdictional dismissal.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissenting from the merits disposition.** These Justices join the standing opinion but would affirm the three-district invalidation. They expressly propose replacing Shaw and Miller's contrary boundary: official assignment predominantly by race, with ordinary criteria subordinated, should trigger a compelling-interest and fitted-justification inquiry. This is a sufficient trigger, not a common rejection of every broader trigger. Mere awareness, a percentage or an unusual shape does not establish automatic invalidity. They acknowledge the recent contrary decisions and governmental reliance but maintain their previously stated individual-classification objection; the 1991 enactment itself preceded Shaw, while later reliance remains a real consideration.

They accept the supported district-specific purpose findings under ordinary clear-error review. District 30's population objective and neighborhood choices, District 18's block-level changes to preserve opportunity, and District 29's target and interwoven boundaries show predominance despite genuine political and community explanations. They distinguish compact minority populations from district geometry; legitimate nonracial noncompactness is not independently unlawful. They would not infer that political and racial motives are always identical, and Kennedy leaves open whether foreordaining a racial majority itself establishes the trigger.

Assuming that compliance with valid Voting Rights Act obligations can be compelling, they allow reasonable preventive action without a prior judgment commanding an exact map. Section 5 nonretrogression does not require new Districts 29 and 30 merely because population grows. Preserving District 18 presents a stronger concern, but does not itself require a fixed increased racial share regardless of alternatives. Section 2's applicable Gingles conditions and totality inquiry remain distinct: cohesion, actual preferences and ordinarily defeating majority bloc voting cannot be assumed by combining Black and Hispanic populations. Beer, Pleasant Grove, Growe, Holder and De Grandy retain their distinct requirements. Discrimination history and contested polarization make the compliance defense serious but do not alone establish this particular fit.

For District 30, the lower findings about C500, C606 and preserving neighborhoods show a more ordinary route to minority opportunity, with the selected departures substantially tied to incumbents. For Districts 18 and 29, C606, C676 and witness acknowledgments similarly bear on alternatives respecting ordinary criteria. C676's joint influence district does not by itself establish an equivalent Hispanic-majority opportunity district; C606 and the other findings do separate work. The dissent certifies no alternative's complete legality or precise electoral equivalence. Its conclusion is deficient connection between these racial departures and statutory need, even allowing latitude.

The United States' separate fit defense is considered; the State's reported unwillingness to defend precise shapes is no default or waiver binding that intervenor. The alternatives' asserted opportunity costs are weighed against the lower findings, not ignored. The dissent rejects the lower court's least-irregular-map formula, universal compactness metrics and an exact-map-compulsion demand. It finds the remaining district-specific findings sufficient and thus rejects a fit-only remand. It would preserve the declaration and prospective injunction, leaving the State an initial lawful planning opportunity and any necessary bounded implementation below. It would prescribe no map, racial quota, special election or new deadline, presume no compliance or contempt, and convert no fee application into an award. The Court adopts none of this dissent's proposed trigger, factual conclusions or fit holding.

**O'Connor, dissenting separately.** O'Connor joins Kennedy's opinion and would additionally hold that compliance with valid §2 obligations is a compelling interest. Her Voinovich and Miller positions preserve a State's practical room to address actual racial electoral conditions and avoid statutory violations; judicial insistence on one ideal district or a prior exact-map command would defeat that latitude. Her SCLC position likewise insists on genuine statutory proof rather than categorical noncoverage. She distinguishes racial awareness from predominance subordinating ordinary criteria. She nevertheless agrees that the supported departures here lack sufficient connection to the asserted duty under a standard allowing reasonable choice. Her affirmative compelling-interest conclusion is hers alone; the common dissent assumes it for the fit analysis.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting separately.** Both join Kennedy's sufficient predominance ground while preserving a broader objection to intentional creation of a majority-minority constituency as racial classification. Their Miller writings supply that objection. Their separate statutory reservations, maintained in SCLC, remain distinct from the common dissent's analysis assuming existing statutory law. They adopt no new statutory noncoverage holding, proportional-officeholding entitlement or separate decree. Their additional trigger and statutory views receive no controlling support.

### Jaffee v. Redmond, No. 95-266

Merits decision, June 13, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**518 U.S. 1.** Argued February 26, 1996, on certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 51 F.3d 1346. The questions concern a federal privilege for confidential psychotherapy, its application to a licensed clinical social worker, and case-specific balancing of confidentiality against evidentiary need.

After the shooting, Redmond received psychotherapy from licensed clinical social worker Karen Beyer. In the ensuing federal excessive-force and Illinois wrongful-death action, the jury was permitted to infer that withheld counseling notes were unfavorable and was told that withholding lacked legal justification. The earlier exclusion of Redmond's shooting testimony had been withdrawn. The Seventh Circuit reversed the civil judgment for a new trial, recognizing a privilege but allowing case-specific balancing.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia.

The Seventh Circuit's reversal and new-trial remand remain in force. Further proceedings must protect privileged treatment communications and may not impose an adverse inference from their valid withholding. The Court does not reinstate the $45,000 federal and $500,000 state verdicts, decide that the shooting was lawful, or reopen the separately rejected deadly-force instruction challenge.

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion controls on each rule below, joined by all eight judgment supporters. The future-contours illustration is not necessary to any holding.

##### I. Confidential psychotherapy communications are privileged

**Operative rule:** Federal common law protects confidential communications between a patient and a licensed psychotherapist in the course of diagnosis or treatment, including confidential psychotherapy provided by a licensed clinical social worker. The protection covers the treatment conversations and counseling notes at issue; a professional title alone does not protect administrative conversations, ordinary workplace counseling or communications outside a confidential treatment relationship.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 501 directs courts to interpret common-law privilege through reason and experience, subject to the Constitution, Acts of Congress and rules prescribed under statutory authority. Trammel supplies the responsibility to examine a privilege's present justification; University of Pennsylvania requires a defensible benefit and boundary rather than an assumption that secrecy is always useful. Effective psychotherapy depends on frank disclosure, which predictable confidentiality makes possible. Its benefit extends beyond the patient to the treatment of mental illness.

Widespread state recognition corroborates that judgment, without proving that every State uses identical definitions or exceptions. Proposed Rule 504 reflects considered professional judgment but was not enacted and does not govern. The loss of relevant evidence is serious, especially in a dispute about a killing. Rule 501 nevertheless permits a defined relationship privilege where its public function justifies that cost.

Beyer's license, training and actual psychotherapy bring this treatment within the rule. Clinical social workers perform the same protected function and provide access for patients unable to afford other providers; the record supplies no confidentiality distinction warranting exclusion of this relationship. The holding creates no general physician-patient privilege or privilege for every conversation with a social worker.

Rule 501's separate state-law sentence remains operative for an element of a civil claim or defense governed by state law. Recognition here makes it unnecessary to decide which privilege law would govern evidence relevant to both federal and state claims if the two laws conflict. The Court adopts no general federal-preemption rule for that situation.

##### II. Protection does not depend on routine balancing in each lawsuit

**Operative rule:** A communication within this privilege does not lose protection merely because a judge finds the opposing party's evidentiary need more important in the particular lawsuit. A valid assertion of the privilege cannot support the adverse inference and lack-of-legal-justification instruction given here.

**Controlling explanation:** A patient must know the protection's character when speaking. If disclosure turns on an unpredictable later assessment of usefulness, the uncertainty can deter the very communication that makes treatment effective. The Seventh Circuit's reliance on the availability of other eyewitnesses cannot establish a general balancing test within the privilege.

United States v. Nixon concerns a presumptive executive privilege and demonstrated criminal-trial need; it does not require routine balancing inside every recognized confidential relationship. Daubert and Williamson concern admissibility and reliability under different evidence rules, not this relationship's protection under Rule 501.

Rejecting general balancing does not make the privilege exceptionless. A patient may waive it. The account of partial responses and three redacted pages supplies no adjudicated waiver of all treatment communications; whether any waiver occurred remains undecided. The Court decides no waiver rule, court-ordered-examination rule, litigation-use exception, criminal constitutional override or serious-threat dispute. No adverse inference may rest on a valid assertion of the protected core. Because the actual instruction penalized withholding protected treatment material, the appellate new-trial judgment stands without its balancing rationale.

**Reserved contours in the Court's opinion.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer observe that the privilege must yield in a hypothetical situation where a serious threat to the patient or others can be averted only by therapist disclosure. No such threat is found here; the illustration decides no operative exception, procedure or ordinary evidentiary-need claim. Stone-Zsela does not join that affirmative illustration. All eight leave the actual boundaries for a case presenting them.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Trammel v. United States, 445 U.S. 40:** Applied for reason-and-experience development of privilege with attention to present function and evidentiary cost; its distinct marital rule is unchanged.
- **University of Pennsylvania v. EEOC, 493 U.S. 182:** Distinguished on the proposed relationship and demonstrated justification. Its rejection of an unsupported peer-review privilege does not foreclose this defined treatment privilege.
- **United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683:** Distinguished as executive privilege in criminal process; it supplies no universal case-by-case balancing requirement for confidential treatment.
- **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals:** Its admissibility and reliability rules remain intact and do not decide Rule 501 privilege.
- **Williamson v. United States:** Its admissibility and reliability rules remain intact and do not decide Rule 501 privilege.

#### Law After Decision

Federal common law now protects the defined confidential psychotherapy relationship, including licensed clinical social-work psychotherapy. Evidentiary usefulness alone does not defeat the privilege, and valid withholding cannot be penalized by the instruction used here. State-law privilege obligations under Rule 501 remain distinct. Waiver and other particular limits require their own supported questions; this decision supplies no comprehensive code of exceptions and overrules no earlier decision.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting.** Rule 501 authorizes common-law development, but the dissent finds the asserted benefit insufficient to justify withholding reliable evidence about disputed conduct. The possibility that confidentiality assists treatment does not by itself establish how much this evidentiary privilege adds to ordinary professional secrecy, while lost evidence directly impairs the adjudicative task. University of Pennsylvania supplies the need for a supported justification, not a categorical prohibition on creating privileges.

The dissent also disputes the professional boundary. Proposed Rule 504 did not include social workers and was never enacted; differences in training, functions and state exceptions caution against treating all such treatment as one established category. Scalia would reverse the federal-privilege basis for the new-trial judgment, leaving unresolved matters in their proper channels. He does not find wholesale waiver or hold that every note must be disclosed, and his objections create no controlling balancing alternative.

#### Source Notes

The [Seventh Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/51/f3d/1346/jaffee-v-redmond) supplies the treatment relationship, disputed accounts, actual sanction, verdicts and new-trial judgment. The [original Rule 501 enactment](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-88/pdf/STATUTE-88-Pg1926-2.pdf) supplies both its federal common-law direction and its state-law qualification. State experience establishes widespread recognition, not uniform scope or exceptions; the Court leaves whether any waiver occurred undecided.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for the new trial ordered by the Seventh Circuit, subject to ordinary mandate procedures and the privilege announced here. Independent, properly presented issues retain their own requirements. No new disclosure, damages amount or finding about the shooting is ordered.

Stone-Zsela joins its abuse-of-discretion rule and permission to correct legal error and unsupported grounds; those propositions have nine votes. The additional factor-classification, degree, unmentioned-factor and commentary methodology has eight direct joins.

**Operative rule:** A departure decision receives review for abuse of discretion: legal interpretation and permission are independently reviewed, historical findings are protected unless clearly erroneous, and supported judgments about case-specific unusualness and degree receive substantial deference. Section 3553(b) requires the applicable range unless a circumstance of a kind or degree inadequately considered by the Commission should result in a different sentence; adequate consideration is assessed from the Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3742(e) expressly requires due regard for credibility, protection of factual findings and due deference in applying the Guidelines. Cooter & Gell shows that deferential review still corrects erroneous law; Pierce explains why an experienced trial judge may better assess a concrete, poorly generalizable judgment. Uniformity does not require appellate substitution on every such assessment. Ornelas supplies an issue-specific allocation, not a universal rule for mixed questions.

The Commission's categories matter. A forbidden factor cannot support departure. An encouraged factor can support it when the applicable guideline has not already taken that factor into account. A discouraged factor, or an encouraged factor already accounted for, requires exceptional degree or another supported feature distinguishing the case from its ordinary form. Where the applicable guideline and adjustments account for the factor, §5K2.0 requires a degree substantially exceeding that ordinarily involved. An unmentioned factor requires examination of the relevant guideline's structure and theory and the Guidelines as a whole; unguided departures should be highly infrequent. Rarity alone does not establish that a different sentence is warranted.

Stinson makes interpretive commentary authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to statute, inconsistent with the guideline or plainly erroneous; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Rivera illustrates the useful categories but imposes no compulsory sequence. Sentencing purposes—just punishment, deterrence, protection of the public and needed training, medical care or correctional treatment—remain relevant to the particular sentence, without authorizing additional categorical bans beyond the Commission's scheme. Errors of law or calculation receive no deference.

##### II. The separate five-level victim-misconduct departure stands

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this component's judgment and joins no reasoning about application or extent.

**Operative rule:** Under §5K2.10, wrongful victim conduct that significantly contributes to provoking the offense behavior may support a reduced sentence, with the extent governed by the provision's considerations and qualifications. The sentencing court's complete findings support both use of that ground and the five-level reduction here, despite the end of resistance and danger before the force became criminal.

**Controlling explanation:** The relevant guideline covers aggravated assaults under color of law, including deliberate unprovoked assaults; it is not confined to arrests in which initially lawful force becomes excessive. Its six-level official-conduct addition applies to provoked and unprovoked conduct alike. The judge found significant earlier provocation and a transition from lawful to unlawful force within seconds. That supported comparison with the guideline's typical cases is more than a statement that the arrest happened first.

Section 5K2.10 directs attention to comparative size, strength and other relevant physical characteristics; persistence of the victim's conduct and efforts to avoid confrontation; reasonably perceived danger, including reputation for violence; actual danger; and other victim conduct substantially contributing to danger. The end of resistance and danger remains material under those considerations, but the provision does not require provocation to cease having relevance at the precise instant force becomes criminal. The judge's complete account supports the five-level extent within the governing discretion. The holding excuses no part of the criminal assault and supplies no finding that danger continued.

The policy's limits remain: victim misconduct ordinarily does not justify its application to criminal sexual-abuse offenses and usually does not make it relevant to nonviolent offenses. Unusual substantial misconduct may nevertheless justify mitigation in a nonviolent case, as with extended provocation and harassment leading to property retaliation. No later-added factor is applied.

##### III-A. These employment consequences do not justify departure

**Authority:** Part III-A, joined by eight; Stevens does not join.

**Operative rule:** Loss of public employment and future law-enforcement prospects following these official civil-rights offenses does not justify departure because the applicable guideline adequately contemplates those consequences. The Court does not categorically exclude every unusual employment consequence or equate job loss with socioeconomic status.

**Controlling explanation:** Loss of the position whose authority the offender willfully abused is a foreseeable consequence of this class of conviction. The unusual-circumstances requirement does not turn that ordinary consequence into additional mitigation here. Section 5H1.5 makes employment record not ordinarily relevant to a sentence outside the range; its separate permission to consider employment in setting probation or supervised-release conditions does not authorize a prison-range reduction. Section 5H1.11 similarly treats military, civic, charitable or public service, employment-related contributions and prior good works as not ordinarily relevant. Those are discouraged considerations, distinct from §5H1.10's exclusion of race, sex, national origin, creed, religion and socioeconomic status. This application preserves those distinctions while rejecting the ground offered in these cases.

##### III-B. Category I already includes the lowest recidivism risk

**Authority:** Part III-B, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** A first offender already in criminal-history category I cannot receive a departure below that category's lower range on the ground that his risk of recidivism is exceptionally low. Section 4A1.3 expressly sets that lower bound for the first offender with the lowest risk.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an actual Commission limit, not a merely discouraged factor open to another unusual-degree assessment. Individualized predictions of particularly good future conduct cannot evade that express treatment. The rule concerns this criminal-history ground and does not foreclose independently valid circumstances belonging to another departure inquiry.

##### III-C. The supported extraordinary vulnerability may be considered

**Authority:** Part III-C, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Operative rule:** The sentencing court may consider the extraordinary prison vulnerability it found from the combination of these defendants' police status and exceptional public notoriety. Police status alone creates no departure entitlement, and this ground does not independently establish three levels or any particular sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Commission has not made every circumstance of confinement categorically irrelevant. The inquiry is whether the supported burden differs sufficiently from the ordinary case to warrant consideration under §3553(b) and §5K2.0. The judge did not rely on occupational prestige alone; the extraordinary publicity materially distinguished these defendants' position in prison. The Court respects that case-specific assessment without finding that continuous solitary confinement is inevitable.

The objection that the crime's notoriety contributed to the risk does not establish a categorical prohibition absent from the Commission's scheme. The majority permits the actual vulnerability to be considered; it does not announce mitigation for every notorious crime, presume greater desert based on professional standing, or decide the amount attributable to this factor. The ultimate reduction remains subject to the separate valid-ground and reasonable-extent inquiry.

##### III-D. The significant burden of these successive proceedings may be considered

**Authority:** Part III-D, joined by the same five Justices as Part III-C.

**Operative rule:** The District Court may consider its finding of a significant burden from these lengthy state proceedings followed by federal prosecution for the same underlying conduct. Lawful succession alone creates neither an entitlement to departure nor a categorical bar to considering a genuinely unusual, inadequately considered burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Separate sovereign authority explains why the federal prosecution was lawful; it does not erase the actual burden the sentencing judge assessed. The Commission's treatment of unmentioned circumstances allows examination of that burden under the relevant guideline and the Guidelines as a whole. The Court sustains this particular ground without finding a double-jeopardy violation, prosecutorial misconduct or an automatic entitlement for defendants tried by two sovereigns. Nor does the holding establish that the ground warrants all three combined levels. Its permissible extent remains to be determined on valid reasons under Williams.

##### IV. Invalid factors require further sentencing under Williams

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** Once reliance on an invalid departure factor is established, the sentence defender must show from the whole record that the sentencing judge would have imposed the same sentence without that factor, and any departure extent must independently be reasonable on valid reasons the judge actually identified. That showing is absent here; both cases must return to the District Court for further sentencing while retaining the separately sustained five-level departure.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams requires actual harmlessness of the invalid reliance, not merely the possibility that a lawful sentence of the same length could be imposed. All nine reject low recidivism as used here, and the Court also rejects the employment ground. The judge combined four considerations, stated that none alone justified three levels, and supplied no determination that the surviving valid combination would produce the same sentences. Petitioners, who defend those sentences, have not carried the required burden. The challenger need not prove that the invalid factor changed the result.

The appellate court may not invent replacement reasons. Even where valid circumstances can collectively justify departure although individually insufficient, their combination cannot preserve invalid considerations or substitute for a reasoned and independently reasonable extent. Upholding the separate five levels therefore does not restore either 30-month sentence. The District Court must sentence again within these holdings; the Court selects no final number of months and reopens neither guilt nor the bodily-injury adjustment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Williams v. United States, March 9, 1992:** Applied without alteration. The sentence defender bears the whole-record same-sentence burden after invalid reliance; reasonable extent separately requires actual valid sentencing reasons.
- **Stinson v. United States, May 3, 1993:** Applied to the Commission's commentary with its constitutional, statutory and textual-consistency limits; commentary does not amend a contrary guideline.
- **Cooter & Gell v. Hartmarx Corp., 496 U.S. 384:** Applied for deferential review that still corrects erroneous legal premises and clearly erroneous factual assessments.
- **Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552:** Applied for the relevance of institutional competence and limited generalizability to the particular review allocation.
- **Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996:** Distinguished by its particular Fourth Amendment task; it does not compel independent review of every sentencing application.
- **United States v. Rivera, 994 F.2d 942:** Its distinction among forbidden, discouraged, encouraged and unmentioned considerations informs the framework. Its suggested sequence is not mandatory, and its discussion of Williams cannot displace this Court's controlling rule.

#### Law After Decision

Departure review protects supported sentencing judgments while independently correcting legal error. The Commission's actual treatment of a factor controls whether and on what terms it may be used. The five-level victim-misconduct departure stands; employment consequences and lowest category-I recidivism cannot support these sentences, while the particular vulnerability and successive-prosecution findings may be considered. Their permissible use does not validate the combined three-level amount. Williams requires new sentencing because the invalid reliance has not been shown harmless and departure extent needs an independent valid-ground explanation.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stevens would also permit the exceptionally severe combination of employment and professional consequences described by the sentencing judge. Sections 5H1.5 and 5H1.11 discourage ordinary consequences but do not forbid unusually substantial ones. His position rests on the judge's case-specific assessment, not preference for police officers or a right to mitigation for ordinary job loss. Valid circumstances that do not separately justify departure may sometimes do so in combination. That principle cannot rescue prohibited considerations, and the invalid recidivism ground still requires the Williams remand. He joins the Court's remaining holdings.

**Souter, joined by Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** A departure requires an unusual difference that rationally warrants mitigation within the sentencing scheme, not unusualness alone. On this record, notoriety generated by the brutality cannot make more conspicuous misconduct a reason for less punishment. Nor does the relationship between the state and federal outcomes warrant an additional benefit because the state system apparently failed to vindicate the same underlying wrong. This is an assessment of the claimed mitigation, not a finding of misconduct by a state official. Souter would reject both grounds here while leaving genuinely different extraordinary physical vulnerability or a materially different comparison of verdicts open. He accepts the review framework, all five victim-misconduct levels, rejection of employment and recidivism, and resentencing.

**Breyer, joined by Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The relevant civil-rights guideline contemplates a federal corrective proceeding when state enforcement has been inadequate; ordinary succession does not put these offenses outside that scheme. Rivera's distinction between legal permission, Commission consideration and entrusted application preserves that limit. Prison treatment also varies among convicted officers. Even with due deference, this record does not establish vulnerability sufficiently unusual relative to that relevant class. Breyer agrees with Souter's conclusions and pertinent considerations but does not join his separate writing in full. He would reject both factors on this record, while joining the other Court holdings and the Williams remand.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Being a convicted officer does not itself distinguish these defendants from other officers entering prison, and publicity is not a substitute for evidence of exceptional risk. Genuinely extraordinary vulnerability may matter, but the offered justification is legally insufficient here. The successive prosecutions exercised distinct sovereign responsibilities; this record identifies no individual mitigating circumstance showing why authorized federal civil-rights enforcement makes punishment less deserved. Additional factfinding cannot replace deciding the insufficiency of those two grounds as offered. Stone would exclude them, as well as career loss and ordinary low recidivism, and require resentencing on independently valid grounds. Under Williams the combined explanation establishes neither the same sentence without invalid factors nor independently reasonable extent. He agrees only with the judgment sustaining the separate five-level victim-conduct component and adds no ground concerning its application or amount.

#### Source Notes

The [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/34/f3d/1416) and the sentencing explanation reported at 833 F. Supp. 769 support the findings, separate departures and limits of review. The operative text is the [1992 Guidelines Manual](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/archive/1992-federal-sentencing-guidelines-manual), including its complete policy qualifications. Later revisions to §5K2.10 or other provisions are not applied. The finding that danger had ended remains distinct from the finding of significant earlier provocation; extraordinary publicity does not establish inevitable continuous solitary confinement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Court of Appeals must return both cases to the District Court for further sentencing under these holdings, subject to ordinary mandate procedures. The five-level victim-misconduct determination remains sustained; the final sentences and mixed three-level departure require renewed consideration. No replacement sentence, future hearing date or further Supreme Court event is fixed.

### Montana v. Egelhoff, No. 95-566

Merits decision, June 13, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 20, 1996, on certiorari to the Supreme Court of Montana, 272 Mont. 114, 900 P.2d 260. The question is whether Montana may forbid the jury to consider intoxication evidence when deciding purpose or knowledge while retaining that mental-state requirement for deliberate homicide.

The Montana court reversed Egelhoff's two deliberate-homicide convictions and ordered a new trial. The jury heard intoxication evidence for memory and physical-capacity issues, but the challenged instruction prohibited using it to determine the mental-state element. Egelhoff preserved that objection in settling the instructions and in seeking a new trial. His claim concerns rebuttal of an element, not a constitutional entitlement to an affirmative intoxication defense.

Mont. Code Ann. §45-2-203 states:

> A person who is in an intoxicated condition is criminally responsible for his conduct and an intoxicated condition is not a defense to any offense and may not be taken into consideration in determining the existence of a mental state which is an element of the offense unless the defendant proves that he did not know that it was an intoxicating substance when he consumed, smoked, sniffed, injected, or otherwise ingested the substance causing the condition.

This unnumbered paragraph's exception requires proof that the defendant did not know the substance was intoxicating when he ingested it. It does not encompass every instance of involuntary intoxication, unexpectedly severe impairment or lost memory. The Court neither finds that Egelhoff met it nor decides a separate challenge to its burden of persuasion.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg.

The new-trial judgment stands on both deliberate-homicide counts. The State may not repeat the challenged prohibition on using otherwise admissible intoxication evidence to contest the retained purpose-or-knowledge requirement. The Court does not acquit Egelhoff, determine his actual mental state or decide the unresolved state assignments concerning lay opinion, evidentiary sufficiency and dangerous-offender classification.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter and Breyer. Justice Souter concurs separately. Justice Breyer concurs separately, joined by Justice Stevens. Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas. Justice Ginsburg dissents separately.

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer, controls each proposition, explanation, application and limit below by five direct joins. The separate writings add no controlling ground.

##### I. The actual state-law element governs the constitutional inquiry

**Operative rule:** When a State retains purpose or knowledge as an offense element, the constitutional inquiry must address that actual requirement rather than an appellate substitute that treats the defendant as sober. Montana's highest court treated deliberate homicide as retaining purpose or knowledge and §45-2-203 as restricting evidence bearing on that element; this Court accepts that state-law premise.

**Controlling explanation:** Patterson permits a State to define offenses and allocate a genuinely separate affirmative defense; it does not authorize this Court to replace the offense the State has retained. Gaudin requires the jury to determine enacted elements under the proper burden, while leaving the legislature free to define crimes within constitutional limits. Neither makes subjective intent indispensable to every offense.

Here the charge required purposely or knowingly causing death. The Montana Supreme Court treated intoxication as potentially relevant to those mental facts and the instruction as restricting their proof. Giving §45-2-203 substantive effect cannot mean disregarding that authoritative construction. A rule defining culpability without the exculpatory effect of voluntary intoxication presents a different question only when it is actually the State's rule. A possible saving construction is insufficient to supply it here.

The Court does not decide the validity of every genuinely different culpability definition, including a law expressly equating intoxication with knowledge, or any fair-notice or retroactivity issue such a law might present. Ratzlaf and Staples remain interpretations of their particular federal crimes, not constitutional commands fixing Montana's elements.

##### II. This blanket restriction denies a fair opportunity to contest the retained element

**Operative rule:** Due process forbids applying §45-2-203 to prevent consideration of otherwise admissible intoxication evidence bearing on the actual purpose-or-knowledge element retained here, without a sufficient justification for that restriction. The ruling preserves ordinary evidence rules addressing relevance, reliability, privilege and fair trial management; it creates neither an absolute right to present all evidence nor an affirmative intoxication excuse.

**Controlling explanation:** Winship requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every actual element. Martin permits a separate affirmative-defense burden because the defense evidence remains available to create doubt about the prosecution's elements; withholding that use presents a different constitutional problem. Chambers and Rock protect a meaningful opportunity to defend while allowing legitimate evidentiary restrictions. Together these principles require attention to the function of this instruction.

Montana did not simply decline to excuse conduct committed while intoxicated. It retained a mental-state requirement and directed the jury to disregard a category capable of rebutting it. Admission for memory or physical capacity did not permit the forbidden use. Under Victor's whole-charge approach, a general reasonable-doubt instruction does not neutralize that express limitation.

Historical limits on voluntary intoxication deserve consideration, but do not establish a sufficient justification for this restriction on proof of the retained element. Responsibility for self-induced impairment and deterrence may inform an actual substantive culpability choice; they do not make this contrary evidence incapable of bearing on the element Montana kept. The unknown-intoxicant exception itself permits intoxication evidence when the defendant proves lack of knowledge that the substance was intoxicating when ingested. That feature defeats a categorical claim of inherent evidentiary incapacity, without enlarging the exception.

The reported blackout and high intoxication may bear on purpose or knowledge; they do not establish its absence. Evidence that Egelhoff walked, spoke or acted in a directed manner may support the State, but physical functioning does not conclusively establish the required mental state. The jury must evaluate the actual evidence under lawful instructions. The Court decides neither its precise effect nor a universal method for all criminal due-process questions.

##### III. The constitutional ruling sustains the state new-trial judgment

**Operative rule:** The unconstitutional restriction in the challenged instruction supplies the ground for affirming the Montana Supreme Court's existing new-trial judgment on both deliberate-homicide counts. This disposition decides no unresolved state assignment and makes no new finding of exact impairment, evidentiary insufficiency or harmlessness.

**Controlling explanation:** The preserved federal question challenges the instruction that led the state court to order a new trial. Because the Court sustains that constitutional ruling, Montana has shown no basis on the question reviewed to set aside the resulting new-trial judgment. The separate lay-opinion, sufficiency and dangerous-offender assignments remain for appropriate state treatment, without first-instance resolution here.

Sullivan addresses a charge that vitiates the reasonable-doubt standard; the Court does not extend its structural-error rule to every restriction on defense evidence. Nor does it weigh the prosecution's evidence to create an alternative harmlessness holding. The existing new-trial relief follows the adjudicated instructional error within the judgment under review, not a new appellate determination of Egelhoff's mental condition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **In re Winship:** Applied to preserve proof beyond a reasonable doubt of the elements Montana actually retained; it does not fix every offense's substantive mental state.
- **Patterson v. New York:** Preserved for genuine offense definitions and separately allocated affirmative defenses; it does not sustain replacing authoritative state law with a hypothesized culpability rule.
- **Martin v. Ohio:** Applied for its distinction between an affirmative-defense burden and the continuing opportunity to use defense evidence against the prosecution's actual elements.
- **Chambers v. Mississippi:** Applied for the bounded right to present a meaningful defense; ordinary legitimate evidentiary rules remain available.
- **Rock v. Arkansas:** Applied against an unjustified categorical restriction on relevant defense evidence, without requiring admission of unreliable evidence.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied to actual enacted elements and the jury's decision; it creates no universal subjective-mental-state requirement.
- **Victor v. Nebraska and Sandoval v. California:** Applied to the complete charge; correct general burden language does not automatically cure a contrary instruction.
- **Medina v. California:** Its California-specific competency allocation remains; no exclusive general due-process methodology is inferred from it.
- **Cooper v. Oklahoma:** Its protection against the higher incompetence burden remains distinct from the offense-definition and rebuttal question decided here.
- **Ratzlaf v. United States; Staples v. United States:** Distinguished as constructions of particular federal criminal enactments; their rules are not converted into a constitutional definition of every state offense.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Its structural-error rule is preserved at its stated scope and is not extended to every evidentiary restriction.

#### Law After Decision

Montana must permit otherwise admissible intoxication evidence to bear on the purpose or knowledge its homicide law retains. The decision does not excuse intoxication, direct the jury's factual answer, abolish ordinary evidentiary limits or prescribe the mental state for every crime. The State's authority to enact genuine culpability definitions remains subject to their own constitutional limits; no such different definition is adopted for these convictions. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring alone.** Souter joins the Court's opinion and emphasizes the decisive state-law premise. Absent the Montana Supreme Court's construction, he would regard an implicit substantive redefinition as a plausible reading of the enactment. But the Court cannot reject the state court's definition merely because another construction would avoid the constitutional problem. Gaudin protects the element actually required; Patterson preserves a legislature's genuine definition authority. He leaves open whether a convincing evidentiary justification could sustain a restriction on relevant rebuttal. Possible confusion between an incapacity excuse and the required mental state does not adequately justify this restriction, even if it could support a different substantive rule. His additional interpretive observation is not a new Court holding.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, concurring.** Both join the Court's opinion. Breyer adds that the proposed constructive-knowledge formulation may make criminal liability turn on contrasting external appearances despite identical intoxication, replacing inquiry into the retained mental fact with an uncertain proxy. Physical behavior can be circumstantial evidence; it is not automatically the culpability the State has chosen to require. Gaudin's actual-element principle and the practical protection reflected in Cooper support careful separation of the required finding from a procedure that prevents it from being fairly tested. He does not decide whether an express law equating voluntary intoxication with knowledge or purpose would be constitutional, infer lack of purpose from blackout, or require subjective intent in every offense. His additional objection, joined only by Stevens, remains noncontrolling.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent would reverse the federal constitutional ruling and remand for the unresolved state assignments. First, it reads the express intoxication provision together with the homicide statute as a substantive culpability choice: known-origin intoxication cannot negate the culpability otherwise established. Patterson leaves that choice to the State within constitutional limits; Gaudin requires a jury decision on the resulting actual elements rather than prescribing them. The rule neither makes drinking proof of guilt nor shifts the State's burden.

Second, these three Justices would sustain this particular restriction even if classified as evidentiary. The older tradition restricting voluntary-intoxication claims, the incomplete and nonuniform contrary practice, responsibility for self-induced impairment and deterrence provide sufficient justification in their view. They distinguish Martin's actual self-defense holding from a supposed universal right to use all relevant evidence for every purpose. Their own historically attentive approach remains consistent with Medina's limited controlling rule and Cooper's distinct protection against incompetent trial. They do not authorize arbitrary relabeling of every exclusion, unlimited legislative power, an exclusive Courtwide historical method or automatic harmlessness. Their proposed remand would not itself reinstate both convictions or the sentence.

**Ginsburg, dissenting alone.** Ginsburg would reverse and remand solely because she regards §45-2-203 as defining culpability. Its location among general liability principles and its operation, considered with the homicide provision, qualify the mental-state inquiry. The State must establish actual purpose or knowledge, or circumstances establishing them apart from the exculpatory effect of voluntary intoxication. The rule does not make every act by an intoxicated person purposeful or abolish all mental-state requirements. Her Ratzlaf and Staples approach attends to the precise culpability the enactment chooses. She regards the Montana court's contrary treatment as resting on an unduly restrictive federal-law premise. She does not join Scalia's additional evidentiary rationale or approve arbitrary exclusion of concededly relevant rebuttal while retaining the same element. Admission for memory alone would not cure such a defect. The remaining state assignments require appropriate state disposition on her proposed remand.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Montana Supreme Court with its new-trial judgment on both deliberate-homicide counts affirmed. Further proceedings must permit otherwise admissible intoxication evidence to be considered on the retained purpose-or-knowledge element, subject to lawful evidence rules. The separate lay-opinion, evidentiary-sufficiency and dangerous-offender assignments receive only such further state treatment as remains appropriate; this Court decides none of them. ### Rise v. Oregon

Merits decision, June 14, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Ninth Circuit's judgment, 59 F.3d 1556, in No. 93-35521. The §1983 action concerns Oregon's compulsory convicted-offender blood collection and identification program, the general demand for a precollection hearing, retrospective punishment, and Milligan's separate claim against Knox and Pearce.

Rise, Durham and Rhodes had qualifying convictions before enactment. Milligan's attempted-murder and unlawful-weapon-use convictions were outside the statute. The Ninth Circuit affirmed summary judgment, sustaining collection even on the assumption that ordinary law enforcement was its only purpose. Its Milligan ruling rested on the absence of evidence connecting the remaining defendants' own acts to the mistaken demands and discipline.

**The statutory scheme.** ORS 137.076 covers the listed rape, sodomy, unlawful sexual penetration, sexual-abuse, public-indecency, incest and child-display offenses; burglary intended to commit a listed sexual offense; promoting or compelling prostitution; attempt or conspiracy to the felonies in those groups; and murder or aggravated murder. The attempt provision does not extend to murder. It does not cover every prior offender or only violent sex crimes.

An appropriate-agency request creates the sample duty even if the judgment omits a separate order. The court must enter the order, require reimbursement unless the person lacks ability to pay, and impose the sample condition when probation applies. DOC handles persons in its legal and physical custody; otherwise the law-enforcement agency attending the court acts. Collection is as soon as practicable and, for incarceration, before release. State Police notice of inadequacy requires another sample; notice of an adequate previously received sample under §137.076, §161.325 or §419C.473 excuses another draw. A court finding of substantial and unreasonable health risk also excuses it. The same conditions apply to the designated persons incarcerated as a sentence or probation condition on September 29, 1991. There is no absolute one-draw limit or permission for arbitrary repeats.

Collection must be medically acceptable and performed by a licensed professional or practical nurse, qualified medical technician, licensed physician, or someone under physician direction or control, with prescribed procedures. The civil-liability protection concerns authorized medically acceptable collection. A procedural deviation not affecting reliability does not alone exclude a test result or opinion.

ORS 181.085 permits retention of samples and derived physical material, genetic-profile or identity analysis by the State or qualified contracted laboratories, identification databases, and population-frequency databases not identifying particular persons. Accurate and reliable identification techniques may be used. Transfer or disclosure is limited to specified law-enforcement agencies, district attorneys or the Justice Department's Criminal Justice Division for identification in criminal investigations or proceedings; parties receiving independently required criminal or juvenile discovery; and lawful court or grand-jury process for otherwise unprivileged evidence. Receiving public agencies remain subject to those limits.

A person may inspect the database information on request, unless a reasonable likelihood of prejudice to a pending investigation justifies denial. The Department need not permit testing of physical material. Procedures must address inspection and accuracy challenges while preserving material from contamination and destruction. A written destruction request and certified reversal of the predicate conviction, judgment or order generally require destruction of the person's sample, derived evidence and identification records. Exceptions preserve a separate qualifying obligation arising from another conviction, juvenile adjudication or guilty-except-for-insanity finding, and physical evidence whose destruction would also destroy evidence relating to another covered person. A §137.225 set-aside does not itself affect the material; release alone creates no destruction right. These terms establish neither unrestricted use nor universally permanent retention, and no actual unauthorized genetic analysis is found.

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. The offered identification rationale does not justify this suspicionless bodily search

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Operative rule:** A qualifying conviction and an ordinary future-crime identification objective do not, on this record, justify compulsory suspicionless blood extraction and protected examination of the resulting material under this program. The State's reduced-privacy, identification and medical-safeguard arguments do not establish an exception authorizing this bodily acquisition; the Fourth Amendment summary judgment must be reversed.

**Controlling explanation:** Schmerber distinguishes lawful grounds for entering the body from medically acceptable means of doing so; Winston confirms that safe means alone do not resolve bodily privacy. Skinner and Von Raab address their particular safety or supervisory needs. Vernonia sustains a documented athletic-safety program with confidential, nonpolice use and limited consequences. Bell's institutional-security rationale cannot simply be transferred to an ordinary investigatory database. Sitz permits a defined brief highway intervention, but does not authorize extracting and retaining internal biological material.

The contrary identification argument is substantial. Conviction reduces privacy, the State legitimately identifies offenders, selection is objective, the draw is medically modest, and the statutory sample, health, use and retention protections constrain the program. Evidence concerning certain offenders' recidivism and useful biological evidence supports its practical value. Those facts distinguish this scheme from arbitrary testing of the public, but do not eliminate the body's separate protection or justify this extension for unidentified future crimes. Fingerprinting remains a useful analogy, not an equivalence that erases compulsory extraction and retention.

The holding assumes neither misuse nor unavoidable medical harm. Whren's ordinary traffic-probable-cause rule supplies no missing bodily-search authority; Myers's home-information holding decides no offender exception. A different security or health program, consent, a supported warrant or exception, materially different noninvasive identification, and hypothetical secondary misuse remain undecided. Collection, analysis, disclosure and retention remain distinct when relief is framed; this ruling itself orders no destruction or damages.

##### II. The general hearing claim requires the correct legal premise, if still live

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Operative rule:** The preserved general-process claim cannot be resolved on the categorical premises that medically safe extraction presents no procedural liberty concern and an adjudicated conviction exhausts every material collection condition. Its summary judgment is vacated for consideration under ordinary due-process principles only if an independent issue within that existing claim remains live after the Fourth Amendment relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Bodily integrity is a direct liberty interest. The statute also makes correct coverage, adequate-prior-sample notice and a court-determined substantial and unreasonable health risk material. Sandin preserves inquiry into meaningful substantive constraints and significant deprivation without treating every statutory command as a constitutional entitlement to procedure. Mathews requires examination of the private interest, risk of error and probable value of added safeguards, and governmental burdens. A final conviction is not retried.

The strongest contrary argument is that no covered plaintiff establishes an individual sample or health exception dispute and therefore no universal additional hearing is owed. The Court agrees that there is no automatic formal-hearing entitlement. It nevertheless declines to sustain the existing judgment on the false conviction-only premise or an assumed account of available procedures. The statute's conditions bear on the general claim actually decided; considering them creates no new individual exception claim and finds no exception denied.

The lower court must consider actual lawful opportunities to invoke material conditions. Ordinary record verification and existing agency or court access may suffice. The mandate requires no new evidentiary hearing, discovery, prescribed notice period, added proof burden or finding of deficient procedure. Existing preservation and remedial rules govern; no new claim or amendment is authorized. Missing detail supplies no adverse inference. If search relief leaves no live independent process issue, no further process determination is needed. Procedure cannot cure an unconstitutional search.

##### III. The presented obligation is not additional punishment for earlier crimes

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** Applying this regulated identification obligation to persons whose qualifying convictions predated enactment does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause on the presented record. Its design and demonstrated operation do not establish additional punishment, independently of the Fourth Amendment defect.

**Controlling explanation:** Collins and Morales preserve the distinction between added punishment and every later burden associated with a conviction. Compelled blood collection, retained material, probation conditions and possible deterrent effects require examination; a civil label does not settle the question. Here the controlled identification purpose, limited medical procedure and reimbursement exception for inability to pay do not establish added penal severity. No enlarged sentence, lost release opportunity, newly criminalized conduct or eliminated defense is shown.

Morales's protection of a meaningful opportunity for earlier release does not make every new regulation punishment or transfer its particular substantiation rule to this scheme. The Court approves no unspecified sanction, added imprisonment or extension beyond a lawful release date. Milligan's unauthorized demands and discipline are separate, rather than lawful statutory applications proving general punitive operation. An unreasonable search need not be punishment, and a nonpunitive purpose does not make the search reasonable.

##### IV. Milligan has not connected Knox or Pearce personally to the mistake

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** Individual-capacity liability under §1983 requires evidence of the defendant's own causal involvement in the federal violation; supervisory title or general implementation responsibility does not suffice. Summary judgment for Knox and Pearce stands because the record does not connect their own acts to Milligan's mistaken collection demands or ensuing discipline.

**Controlling explanation:** Hafer requires each officer's own violation. Milligan's attempted-murder and weapon convictions were not statutory predicates. Tuttle's uncontroverted affidavit identifies his erroneous extension of the attempt provision to murder, but neither Tuttle nor the disciplinarians is a defendant. No blood draw from Milligan is established.

Repeated unauthorized demands, a fine and segregation may present serious liberty questions. Sandin preserves those questions in a properly supported claim; it supplies no missing causal act by these two defendants. The Court does not adopt the District Court's negligence characterization as a universal answer under Daniels, adjudicate unsued actors, or infer waiver from Milligan's failure to raise coverage at his two hearings. Segregation duration and conditions, responsible-actor liability, damages and defenses remain undecided. The unappealed dismissals of DOC, State Police, Madsen and the John Doe defendants remain undisturbed.

- **Winston v. Lee, 470 U.S. 753:** Applied to the independent bodily-privacy inquiry; safe medical means do not settle search reasonableness.

The challenged program cannot be sustained against the covered plaintiffs on the offered ordinary-investigation justification for suspicionless bodily acquisition. The decision creates neither an all-context DNA rule nor automatic destruction or damages. The general process claim returns only if independently live and under its existing scope, with no universal hearing entitlement or finding of inadequate procedures. Nonpunitive classification remains distinct from search validity. Milligan's noncoverage does not establish Knox or Pearce's personal responsibility. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Thomas and Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices would sustain the actual conviction-defined identification scheme through contextual reasonableness. Skinner and Von Raab support attention to the setting, intrusion and safeguards; Sitz shows that an ordinary enforcement connection does not invariably prohibit structured assessment. Qualifying convictions and the lawful correctional relationship reduce privacy, objective selection limits discretion, the medical burden is modest, and identification has demonstrated value. The actual sample, health, authorized-use, disclosure and retention protections are essential to this conclusion, including anonymous population-frequency use rather than an identity-matching-only description.

The dissent accepts that blood extraction and protected examination are searches and that future-crime investigation and retained biological material make this a substantial extension. It proposes that extension for this defined program, rather than calling it an existing DNA or special-needs rule. All three join the ex post facto and Milligan holdings; Breyer also joins the Court's process remand, while Kennedy and Thomas join the separate process dissent.

**Scalia, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices would affirm only rejection of a categorical entitlement to another hearing before every otherwise lawful draw. An undisputed final predicate conviction has already been adjudicated; another hearing cannot change it. The statute's actual court and agency exceptions remain enforceable, and a concrete material coverage error, inability to invoke an exception or inadequacy of available procedures remains open under ordinary due-process law.

This narrower affirmance would not endorse the lower court's conviction-only description, deny the importance of bodily integrity or infer adequate procedures from silence. No individual exception dispute or denial has been established for the covered plaintiffs, so these Justices would not vacate the generalized hearing ruling merely to investigate possible inadequacy. Milligan's error establishes neither another plaintiff's injury nor these defendants' liability. Scalia and Stone-Zsela's search votes independently reject the offered collection authority; their process position cannot make that search lawful. Kennedy and Thomas separately would sustain the defined search program.

### Calderon v. Moore, No. 95-1612

Summary disposition, June 17, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**518 U.S. 149.** On the warden's petition for certiorari to review the Ninth Circuit's dismissal of the habeas appeals as moot. The question is whether the State's steps toward a new trial under an unstayed conditional order ended appellate review even though retrial had not begun and a favorable decision could remove that obligation. Moore also seeks leave to proceed in forma pauperis and file his opposition under Rule 33.2.

The District Court's March 31, 1995 order required release after sixty days unless California granted a new trial. The State reported granting a new trial on May 30, pursued trial-setting proceedings and maintained its appeal. Moore disputes adequate compliance. The December 28 mootness dismissal, amended January 10, 1996, covered Nos. 95-99009 and 95-99013, including the cross-appeal. Retrial had not begun. The reported stay proceeding, 56 F.3d 39, is distinct from that final dismissal.

#### Judgment

Moore's financial-leave and Rule 33.2 motion is granted. Certiorari is granted on the stated mootness question.

**Mootness judgment reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The amended dismissal of appellate Nos. 95-99009 and 95-99013 is removed, and the actual appeals return for ordinary consideration consistent with the availability of effectual relief. This does not decide every cross-appeal claim's independent jurisdictional basis or its merits. The Court neither reinstates the original conviction or sentence nor vacates the conditional writ on its merits. It orders no execution, retrial, release, new deadline or stay, and does not preclude otherwise lawful interim relief.

#### Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion joined in full by all nine participating Justices. The effective-relief holding and independent Rule 39 application each have nine direct joins. No separate writing or partial join is filed.

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### Effective relief remains because the retrial obligation is unperformed

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Operative rule:** A State's steps toward a new trial in response to an unstayed conditional habeas order do not moot its maintained appeal when reversal could still remove the obligation to conduct the trial. Because retrial has not begun and that effective relief remains possible, the appellate mootness dismissal cannot stand.

**Controlling explanation:** Mills requires dismissal when a court can no longer grant effectual relief. Church of Scientology preserves appellate review after compliance when meaningful partial relief remains; complete restoration of the earlier position is unnecessary. Here a favorable appellate decision could remove a concrete future obligation. Responding to the conditional order while maintaining an appeal does not itself concede the habeas claim or abandon review.

Moore argues that granting a new trial spent the conditional order and also invokes prudential restraint. The supported record establishes neither a completed retrial nor legal irrevocability that eliminates relief. Whether the State complied sufficiently with the sixty-day order is a distinct disputed issue, not decided by the existence of review. W.T. Grant's discretion over equitable relief does not make an ongoing obligation constitutionally moot. The two-line dismissal identifies no independently adjudicated prudential ground, and the Court will not invent one to sustain it. Lonchar preserves source-specific habeas and stay discretion, not an unrestricted substitute for the effectual-relief inquiry.

The State's ability to seek relief establishes no right to prevail on Faretta or the underlying judgment. Properly available independent questions may be considered below. The Court announces no rule for a completed retrial, a superseding judgment or an appeal offering no legally effective relief, and relies on no exception for the importance of capital litigation. No AEDPA temporal-merits question is necessary to this decision.

##### Moore's statutory appointment supplies the financial-leave exception

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion and independent filing order.

**Operative rule:** Under the governing Rule 39.1, appointment of counsel by a federal district court or court of appeals under the Criminal Justice Act, 18 U.S.C. §3006A, or another applicable federal statute excuses the ordinary affidavit or declaration, but the motion must cite the appointment statute and state any earlier leave requests and outcomes. Moore's unchallenged motion identifies the District Court's CJA appointment and absence of earlier requests, so leave is granted without requiring that affidavit.

**Controlling explanation:** The signed motion invokes the rule's actual appointment route. Financial leave rests on that route, not on success or failure of the habeas appeal. The Court accepts the unchallenged representation of statutory appointment and applies the rule's affidavit exception.

Other filing rules remain. The motion must accompany the document and comply with Rule 21, with a copy preceding and attached to each document copy. A represented party ordinarily files an original and ten copies; only a confined unrepresented inmate may submit the original alone. Rule 33.2 preparation applies unless impossible, except where the Rules expressly require Rule 33.1. Legibility, substantive compliance, jurisdictional timeliness and Rule 29 service remain necessary. No defect or challenge is established here. Rule 39.8's screening of certiorari petitions, jurisdictional statements and extraordinary-writ petitions supplies no finding that this represented respondent's opposition is frivolous or malicious. The grant neither dispenses with every filing requirement nor declares the habeas merits resolved.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Mills v. Green, 159 U.S. 651:** Applied for the requirement that no effectual relief remain before a case is dismissed as moot.
- **Church of Scientology of California v. United States, November 16, 1992:** Applied to meaningful partial relief after compliance; its rule preserves review without establishing entitlement to the requested remedy.
- **United States v. W.T. Grant Co., 345 U.S. 629:** Distinguished because discretion over equitable relief does not itself establish constitutional mootness of the unperformed obligation here.
- **Lonchar v. Thomas, April 1, 1996:** Its source-specific habeas and stay principles remain; no unrestricted equitable dismissal power substitutes for the supported threshold inquiry.

#### Law After Decision

Compliance steps under a conditional habeas order do not necessarily exhaust appellate relief. The still-unperformed retrial obligation keeps these appeals live because reversal could remove it; the merits and independent questions remain undecided. Rule 39's statutory-appointment exception operates independently of that result. No earlier decision is overruled and no public-importance exception to mootness is created.

### Leavitt v. Jane L., No. 95-1242

Decision on petition; partial summary vacatur and remand, June 17, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

On petition for certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 61 F.3d 1493. No oral argument was held in this Court.

The District Court invalidated Utah Code §76-7-302(2) but sustained subsection (3). The Court of Appeals held subsection (3) inseverable without deciding its federal constitutional validity. The State did not appeal invalidation of subsection (2). The petition challenges that severability ruling, framed in Eleventh Amendment terms, and separately seeks review of the fetal-survival method duties in §§76-7-307 and 308.

Section 302(3) applies after twenty weeks from conception and incorporates only the exceptions for abortion necessary to save the woman's life, to prevent grave damage to her medical health, or to prevent birth of a child with grave defects, in the attending physician's professional judgment. Subsection (2) also contains rape, rape-of-a-child and incest exceptions conditioned on reporting to law enforcement before abortion; subsection (3) does not incorporate those as separate exceptions. Twenty weeks is the enacted trigger, not a finding that every pregnancy is viable at that point.

Section 307 applies when the unborn child is sufficiently developed to have any reasonable possibility of survival outside the womb. It requires the procedure offering the best survival chance in the physician's best medical judgment and forbids a procedure designed to kill or injure unless necessary, in the woman's physician's opinion, to prevent grave damage to her medical health. Section 308 requires all the performing physician's medical skills to attempt to promote, preserve and maintain the life of a child sufficiently developed for that survival possibility, consistently with saving the woman's life or preventing grave damage to her medical health.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

Certiorari is granted limited to whether the appealed judgment setting aside §76-7-302(3) on inseverability grounds should stand, and is otherwise denied. The grant does not reopen the unappealed invalidation of subsection (2) or accept a separate categorical Eleventh Amendment question. The question concerning §§307–308 is not granted.

The Court summarily vacates the judgment insofar as it eliminates independently lawful applications of §302(3) solely on the rejected inseverability ground and remands. The severability correction is definitive; the Court does not decide the remaining provision's independent federal validity.

#### Judgment

**Partial summary vacatur and remand, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The count concerns the summary merits disposition. The Court makes no new finding that a particular §302(3) application has been proved unconstitutional. The unappealed invalidation of §302(2), the unreviewed §§307–308 judgment and other unreviewed rulings remain untouched. Denial of review concerning §§307–308 supplies no opinion on their validity.

#### Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join both parts: Part I decides statutory severability; Part II states the confined remedy and federal reservations. Each controlling holding has five direct joins. Stone-Zsela concurs separately without an additional constitutional judgment. Stevens dissents, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; they join neither part of the per curiam and oppose the present summary state-law ruling on institutional grounds.

On the separate §§307–308 question, Stone-Zsela agrees that review should be denied, without stating a separate ground or any view on those provisions' validity.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The remaining prohibition is not inseverable merely because subsection (2) is invalid

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 317's express preservation of provisions and applications, including its declaration that each would have been enacted irrespective of invalidity elsewhere, permits §302(3) to operate independently of §302(2)'s invalid prohibition. The Court of Appeals therefore may not eliminate every application of subsection (3) solely because it was part of the same original scheme or incorporates subsection (2)'s exceptions; independent constitutional objections remain separate.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Five direct joins support the rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Utah's stated legislative choice must govern this severability question. Section 317 addresses the very contingency that occurred: invalidity elsewhere in the enactment. It preserves each provision and application and states that the legislature would have enacted each independently. The relevant choice is between the surviving provision and none after invalidation, not whether the legislature originally preferred its full enactment.

Section 302(3) supplies an independent prohibition after twenty weeks from conception. Its reference to subsection (2)(a), (d) and (e) identifies the life-saving, grave-medical-health and grave-fetal-defect circumstances, assessed in the attending physician's professional judgment, in which abortion remains permitted. Those conditions remain intelligible without enforcement of subsection (2)'s general prohibition. The reference does not retain rape or incest as independent exceptions or turn the fixed week into individualized viability.

Salt Lake City v. International Ass'n of Firefighters, State v. Salt Lake City and Carter preserve Utah's inquiry into interdependent provisions and inseparable legislative compromises. They do not make the original comprehensive design conclusive when this unusually explicit text and the remainder's independent operation point the other way. Section 317.2 does different work: it conditionally reenacts the 1974 §302 if this Court holds unconstitutional the provision as amended by 1991 Senate Bill 23. It does not withdraw §317's ordinary severability instruction.

The serious federal objections do not justify retaining an erroneous state-law premise. They must receive their own lawful consideration. The Court neither treats every severability clause as conclusive nor holds every surviving application constitutional.

##### II. Correcting severability does not authorize automatic enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Removal of the appealed inseverability ground requires a remedy confined to that error, while preserving necessary protection supported by independently established federal grounds. The courts below must determine the lawful scope of the declaration and injunction, retain protection against any demonstrated unconstitutional previability application, and address genuinely dependent reporting, certification and sanction consequences without automatically validating or invalidating them.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam, joined by the same five Justices. This is a direct majority remedial holding; it does not adjudicate an independent federal claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Dalton v. Little Rock Family Planning Services supplies the distinction between correcting excessive relief and extinguishing the federal protection properly supporting it. Here the Court of Appeals expressly left §302(3)'s federal validity undecided. State-law severability cannot answer that remaining question. This judgment therefore removes the inseverability rationale and remands for the proper treatment of preserved federal grounds and corresponding relief; it grants no blanket permission to enforce.

Respondents may seek to sustain their favorable judgment on a preserved alternative ground without enlarging the relief they obtained. That principle supplies neither a missing finding nor an enlarged grant. The Court does not decide preservation, proof or constitutional validity in the first instance. Existing protection must have a lawful basis and scope; this remand orders neither its automatic dissolution nor indefinite retention of every provision of the decree.

The unappealed invalidation of §302(2) and the unreviewed §§307–308 ruling remain untouched. H.B. 206's possible effect remains available insofar as properly relevant below, but its existence establishes neither repeal nor mootness. Its new viability method is expressly confined to §310.5; its amended penalty clause continues to identify §§307–308. Section 315's serious-emergency exception applies only when insufficient time prevents compliance with §§302, 304(2), 305(2) or 310.5, and does not include §§307–308. No assumed saving construction of their different survival and grave-health terms can supply a constitutional judgment outside this grant. No direct H.B. 206 challenge, new clinical finding or general funding duty is decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Salt Lake City v. International Ass'n of Firefighters, 563 P.2d 786:** Distinguishes its Utah interdependence setting; the doctrine retains force, but does not overcome this enactment's express independent-enactment direction and separately operable command.
- **State v. Salt Lake City, 445 P.2d 691:** Distinguishes its severability application for the same concrete textual and functional reasons; the decision is not displaced as Utah authority.
- **Carter, 399 P.2d 440:** Distinguishes the inseparable-compromise reasoning relied on below; no such necessary dependence follows from incorporating the remaining exceptions here.
- **Dalton v. Little Rock Family Planning Services, March 18, 1996:** Applies its requirement that correction of excessive relief preserve protection supported by an independent federal ground; it supplies no conclusion about Utah's substantive validity.
- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992:** Leaves its entire protected-choice, six-step regulatory and remedial framework unchanged. The state-law correction neither narrows that authority nor constitutes new agreement with its rationale.
- **Fargo Women's Health Organization v. Schafer, February 13, 1995:** Leaves intact the distinction between adequate notice or clinical clarity and the substantive sufficiency of a health exception; neither is decided by this severability holding.
- **Planned Parenthood, Sioux Falls Clinic v. Miller, April 15, 1996:** Preserves its relief against the proved indivisible compulsory condition. That holding does not establish a defect in every application of this different statute.

#### Law After Decision

The appealed inseverability rationale can no longer support eliminating §302(3)'s independently lawful applications. Its independent federal validity remains undecided by this Court, and no fixed gestational week has been approved as a substitute for individualized viability. The retained lower judgments concerning §302(2) and §§307–308 do not become Supreme Court holdings through the limits of this review.

Casey's complete framework continues to govern any properly reached constitutional challenge: protected previability choice and timely necessary physical and mental health care; examination of the actual duty, function and objectively established purpose; proof of attributable interference using a stable affected population and usable alternatives; independent invalidity for a baseline violation, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle; compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference; substantiated justification and fit for remaining meaningful interference; clinical and truthful-communication safeguards; and relief corresponding to the proved violation. Challengers first establish causation and triggering interference. A finding of no substantial obstacle alone does not end review of demonstrated meaningful interference. Neither a universal no-valid-applications requirement nor a funding entitlement is added.

H.B. 206 remains subject to its own terms. Its §310.5 prohibition concerns the defined dilation-and-extraction and saline procedures after its section-specific physician determination of viability. That determination uses the physician's best clinical judgment, the pregnancy's known particular facts and reasonably available medical information and technology to decide whether life can realistically be maintained and nourished outside the womb, with or without temporary artificial support. The first procedure definition excludes dismemberment-before-removal dilation and evacuation, suction curettage and suction aspiration. Its exception applies when **all other available abortion procedures** would pose a risk to the woman's life or health; its own felony clause requires intentional, knowing and willful violation. Amended §314 separately retains intentional unauthorized-abortion liability, exempts the woman seeking or obtaining her own abortion from criminal liability, preserves willful-violation felony penalties for §§307, 308, 310, 310.5, 311 and 312, and treats other violations as class A misdemeanors. The Court decides neither the enactment's independent validity nor its alleged repeal or mootness effects.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone joins the statutory correction because Utah's severability clause preserves independently lawful applications; it cannot make an unconstitutional application enforceable. Under Casey, twenty weeks is not individualized viability. A demonstrated prohibition of protected previability choice violates the constitutional baseline and cannot be justified by a compelling interest or narrow medical exceptions. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration; postviability protection also requires individualized physical and mental health considerations.

He would invalidate demonstrated violations while preserving independent lawful applications. Remaining regulations must satisfy Casey's separate obstructive-purpose and substantial-obstacle prohibitions, its scrutiny of remaining severe interference, and its justification-and-fit requirement for remaining meaningful interference, with usable exceptions and actual effects assessed. A label describing later pregnancy cannot do the constitutional work. He agrees with deciding severability now because the mandate retains necessary lawful protection and returns the injunction and dependent reporting, certification and sanction consequences for proper definition. Respondents' ability to defend a favorable judgment on a preserved alternative ground does not cure missing notice, review scope or proof. His concurrence declares no unpresented application proved and supplies no general funding duty.

**Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** These Justices oppose using summary review to correct a disputed question of state law when the remaining provision's federal validity has not been decided. The express severability language supplies a substantial argument, but an arguable state-law error does not itself establish a sufficient institutional reason for this Court's intervention. Federal interpretations of state law do not conclusively bind the State's own courts; this Court's national role ordinarily favors leaving such disputes to the usual judicial process.

The alternative federal grounds, the actual fixed-week and grave-health limitations, and the intervening enactment remain unresolved after today's correction. If review is undertaken, ordinary briefing would better address the interaction of those matters within the granted scope. The dissent does not hold every restriction constitutional, conclusively endorse the inseverability ruling, find H.B. 206 repealing or mooting the disputed provisions, or deny federal power to consider state severability when defining relief for a federal violation. Its objection is to this discretionary summary intervention, not to the continuing force of independently required federal protection.

### Melendez v. United States, No. 95-5661

Merits decision, June 17, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**518 U.S. 120.** Argued February 27, 1996, on certiorari to the Third Circuit, 55 F.3d 130. The question is whether a Government substantial-assistance motion limited to departure below the Guidelines range also authorizes a sentence below the statutory minimum.

The Guidelines range was 135–168 months; the separate statutory floor was 120 months. The Government's October 7 letter requested a §5K1.1 departure below the Guidelines range, consistent with a conditional cooperation promise in the May 10 plea agreement. It did not request below-minimum relief. The sentencing judge imposed 120 months and five years of supervised release on November 23, 1993. The Third Circuit affirmed.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Breyer.

The 120-month sentence and five-year supervised-release term remain unchanged. The Court orders no resentencing or departure below the statutory floor. Independent statutory, constitutional or plea-enforcement claims are not adjudicated by implication and remain subject to their own lawful procedures.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Affirmance | Parts I–II in full |
| Partial joins to the Court opinion | Kennedy | O'Connor, Breyer | Agree with the general statutory limit but oppose affirmance | Part I only |
| Concurrence | Souter | No other Justice | Full agreement with the Court's operative conclusions | Explains his commentary-based interpretive route |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | No other Justice | Affirmance on Congress's statutory condition | Does not join the Court's opinion or its Commission-intent construction |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Breyer | O'Connor | Would remand under the coordinated-Guidelines reading | Part I join; opposition to Part II and the judgment |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. Statutory-minimum relief requires Government authorization of that relief

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all participating Justices except Stevens: eight direct joins.

**Operative rule:** Section 3553(e) requires the Government to request or consent to a sentence below the statutory minimum to reflect substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another person who committed an offense; useful assistance alone and Commission policy cannot supply that authorization. The request may express the necessary authorization in substance without a separate document, prescribed formula or citation to §3553(e).

**Controlling explanation:** The motion condition attaches to the statutory relief Congress makes available. Reading any acknowledgment of assistance as authorization to disregard the statutory floor would detach the Government's request from that relief. The court decides the appropriate reduction only after the relevant authority exists; this does not give the prosecutor power to dictate the precise extent of an authorized statutory departure.

Section 3553(e) also requires the authorized sentence to accord with Commission guidelines and policy statements under §994. Section 994(n) directs the Commission to reflect the general appropriateness of lower sentences for substantial assistance, including sentences below statutory minima. Neither provision empowers the Commission to replace Congress's motion condition. Wade preserves the motion prerequisite, while reserving the independent operation now at issue. Stinson makes explanatory commentary authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to a federal statute, inconsistent with the Guideline or a plainly erroneous reading of it; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Neal likewise preserves distinct statutory and Guidelines measures within their respective fields.

The requirement concerns the substance of authorization, not the number of papers. Nor is it declared an Article III jurisdictional limit. Wade's existing review for unconstitutional purpose, including race or religion, and refusal unrelated to any legitimate governmental end remains; useful assistance alone establishes neither. Discovery or a hearing still requires a substantial threshold showing of improper motive; useful assistance, with or without generalized accusations, is insufficient. Its particular corrective remedies remain unresolved. No such refusal claim is decided here.

##### II. The present Guidelines do not convert this limited request into statutory permission

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six direct joins.

**Operative rule:** The existing §5K1.1 provisions permit a Guidelines assistance departure without making every such departure conditional on authorization to sentence below an applicable statutory minimum. The motion actually made here requested Guidelines relief alone, so it did not authorize a sentence below 120 months and the sentence must be affirmed.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5K1.1 permits departure from the Guidelines on the Government's appropriate assistance request. Application note 1 addresses below-statute sentencing under the circumstances of §§3553(e) and 994(n); it preserves the statutory condition rather than treating a limited request as authorization for different relief. The Government's letter requested reduction from the Guidelines minimum, and the plea agreement promised no particular sentence. Neither assistance's value nor the letter's mere existence supplies an unexpressed below-floor request.

The contrary reading combines two different proposals. A Guidelines-only request cannot be converted into statutory authority Congress conditions on a request for that relief. A system conditioning Guidelines departures on the kind of motion required for statutory relief presents a different question. The Court concludes that the existing provisions do not impose that condition; whether the Commission could lawfully adopt such a system remains open. Text and structure resolve this case, so Granderson's residual lenity does not change the result merely because interpretations differ.

Once proper authority exists, the court determines the appropriate reduction for stated reasons. The nonexclusive assistance considerations include significance and usefulness with attention to the Government's evaluation; truthfulness, completeness and reliability; nature and extent; resulting injury, danger or risk to the defendant or family; and timeliness. Not all five must be proved. Assistance remains distinct from acceptance of responsibility, and the Government's assessment receives substantial weight, especially when value is difficult to assess. Those standards inform lawful reductions without requiring the Court to assign different direct referents to §5K1.1's two sentences. They do not independently authorize crossing the statutory floor.

The Court does not decide a particular reduction, safety-valve entitlement, drug-quantity issue or plea withdrawal. It also declines the distinct plea-interpretation theory: the statutory question is resolvable without new contract findings, and no basis for exceptional reach is established. This is nonreach, not a merits rejection or finding of intentional waiver.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Wade v. United States, May 18, 1992:** Its Government-motion prerequisite and constitutional refusal-review rules remain. The Court resolves the reserved relationship between the actual statutory and Guidelines requests without selecting a refusal remedy or changing Wade's discovery threshold.
- **Stinson v. United States, May 3, 1993:** Applied to authoritative commentary within constitutional, statutory and Guideline-consistency limits; commentary cannot supply authority contrary to Congress's condition.
- **Neal v. United States, January 22, 1996:** Applied for the distinction between enacted statutory rules and the Commission's delegated measures; its different LSD question did not previously decide this motion issue.
- **United States v. Granderson:** Residual lenity remains available after ordinary interpretation leaves the required ambiguity; disagreement alone does not establish it here.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp., February 21, 1995:** Permission to consider an included legal argument does not require adjudicating a distinct factual plea-promise theory. No new categorical bar to late arguments is adopted.
- **Santobello v. New York, 404 U.S. 257:** Enforcement of an established plea promise remains available on its own requirements; no promise breach or remedy is decided here.

#### Law After Decision

Under the present provisions, Guidelines assistance relief and statutory-minimum relief retain distinct authorization requirements. Substance controls whether the Government has authorized the latter; a mandatory second paper is unnecessary. This motion left the statutory floor in force. The Commission's possible adoption of a different coordinated-request system, independently preserved plea claims and constitutional refusal review remain within their stated reservations. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring.** Souter joins the Court's opinion because it does not make a sentence-by-sentence division of §5K1.1 necessary to the result. He reads both sentences as addressing Guidelines departures. The Application Notes, given policy-statement force by §1B1.7, extend the assistance standards to authorized statutory departures and fulfill §994(n)'s direction. That route preserves Congress's separate authorization requirement and the Court's reading of the existing Guidelines. It gives no support to automatic statutory permission, a jurisdictional label or assistance-only entitlement to discovery.

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** Stevens believes the Commission intended a unitary assistance system, and therefore does not join the Court's construction of its intent. Congress nevertheless placed the statutory-minimum exception behind the Government's authorization of that relief. The Commission cannot convert this Guidelines-only request into permission to disregard the floor. His objections to mandatory minima do not change that statutory constraint, so he affirms. His ground neither supplies a join to Part II nor combines with the dissent's different conditioning theory to establish another controlling rule.

**Breyer, joined by O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The dissent accepts Congress's statutory condition and joins Part I. It reads the Commission's Guidelines as requiring the same kind of motion for a Guidelines assistance departure as would authorize a statutory departure. The identical assistance language, cross-referencing commentary, §994(n)'s delegation and the analogous Rule 35(b) structure support that coordination. On this reading, the lower courts cannot accept the motion as sufficient to reduce 135 months to the statutory floor while treating it conclusively as insufficient for statutory relief without examining the coordinated requirement.

The dissent accepts the letter's actual terms and does not claim the Commission can override Congress. It would remand to determine whether this motion sufficed for both departures or for neither, not declare that it necessarily authorizes a sentence below 120 months. It directs no amount and adjudicates no unpresented Government request to increase the existing sentence. Actual preservation and procedural limits would govern any ensuing relief; no new cross-appeal or breach finding is supplied.

The [Third Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/55/f3d/130/united-states-v-melendez) supplies the affirmed ruling. The reported reply-stage characterization of the distinct plea theory does not itself decide waiver or breach; neither is adjudicated. No unexpressed authorization or eligibility for a separate statutory exception is assumed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The affirmed sentence stands subject to ordinary mandate procedures. No new sentencing hearing, reduction, supervised-release modification or inquiry into an unspecified contract or refusal claim is ordered.

### Gray v. Netherland, No. 95-6510

Merits decision, June 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**518 U.S. 152.** Argued April 15, 1996. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit's June 28, 1995 judgment in Gray v. Thompson, 58 F.3d 59, Nos. 94-4009 and 94-4011, reversing a federal grant of sentencing relief and directing dismissal.

The questions concern the capital defendant's opportunity to meet the Sorrell-murder evidence, whether the asserted protection was established when direct review ended on October 5, 1987, and the separate presentation and procedural treatment of meaningful opportunity, affirmative misrepresentation, and Brady suppression. The independent vileness-instruction question is outside this review.

The District Court's May 26, 1993 partial amendment reopened the identified Sorrell due-process claim and left the other dismissals, including the independent Brady ground, unchanged. After an April 21-22, 1994 evidentiary hearing, its August 25 decision found that the prosecutor's statements-only assurance and materially different last-minute presentation impaired preparation and created a substantial probability of a different sentencing result. The Fourth Circuit treated the notice claim as a new rule. Hall concurred in its judgment but withheld assent to that rationale, relying instead on a factual-development bar. The defendant seeks relief from the death sentence; the guilt and firearm convictions are not before the Court for reversal.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reopened meaningful-opportunity claim | Reverse and remand for conditional sentencing relief, subject to the statutory reservation below, **5-4** | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Conditional sentencing mandate; genuinely applicable unadjudicated statutory requirements must be resolved before an operative writ. |
| Independently dismissed Sorrell Brady ground | Leave dismissal undisturbed and exclude general reconsideration of its availability or merits, **9-0** | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | No general reconsideration of this independent ground. |

Six Justices adopt the old-rule holding: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. O'Connor would remand for lawful review of the actual findings and necessary claim, merits, and effect questions without presently requiring a writ. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would retain the ordinary-notice Teague bar and vacate only for bounded consideration of distinct misrepresentation, its proper presentation, and any preserved procedural defense. Their agreement that global dismissal cannot stand is not agreement with the five-Justice sentencing mandate.

The constitutional violation and prejudicial effect of the reopened claim are resolved. Before entering an operative writ, the receiving court must address any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory requirement material to relief. Once those requirements are met or determined inapplicable, the Commonwealth must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable prospective period fixed by the District Court, or replace death with a lawful noncapital disposition. The old 180-day calendar is not automatically reinstated or declared expired. No guilt retrial, acquittal, particular replacement sentence, or immediate release from independent lawful custody follows.

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. The independent Brady dismissal remains separate

**Operative rule:** Reopening the identified due-process challenge to the Sorrell evidence did not reopen the independently dismissed Brady ground when the amendment expressly preserved the remaining dismissals. The independent dismissal remains undisturbed in this review and is excluded from a general availability or merits remand; evidence relevant to the properly available due-process injury may still be considered for that distinct purpose.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on this component. The eight direct joins supply the controlling explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The amendment identified the claim it reopened and preserved the other dismissals. Federal Ground A's suppression allegations and Gray's continued-withholding argument do not undo that procedural distinction. Kyles describes the collective lawful uses of favorable evidence on an available Brady claim; it does not itself reopen a dismissal. The Court therefore grants no independent suppression relief and makes no new suppression or materiality finding.

The opposite conclusion, that every use of related evidence is barred, would also disregard the amendment. Picard and Anderson require the substance of the constitutional claim, while Vasquez distinguishes supplementation from fundamental alteration. Evidence bearing on the inability to investigate and answer the penalty case may establish the injury or effect of the live due-process claim without reviving the independent suppression ground. Shared evidence alone neither merges the claims nor establishes their separate procedural predicates.

The initial order's nonpresentation and state-habeas factual-availability determinations were reported as uncontested in this Court. That description does not resolve the parties' dispute over counsel's knowledge at the 1985 trial. The Court does not adopt an additional appellate-abandonment finding or decide every asserted predicate of a Virginia adequate-and-independent-ground defense. Those additional propositions are unnecessary to the limited disposition of this independent ground.

##### II. A meaningful opportunity requires more than eventual disclosure

**Operative rule:** The established right to deny or explain material information used to select a capital sentence is denied when the State's supported assurances and materially different last-minute presentation deprive the defendant of a real opportunity to investigate and answer that case. Applied to this supported loss of the protected function, rather than to a fixed advance-disclosure interval, the claim invokes law dictated before Gray's October 5, 1987 finality and is not barred as a new rule under Teague.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer: six direct joins. O'Connor's join does not extend to the final merits application or remedy in Parts III-IV.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner protects an opportunity to meet the information used to impose death. Its protection would be empty if disclosure immediately before use always sufficed, regardless of an established inability to investigate, prepare objections, or answer the proof. Weatherford denies a general constitutional discovery entitlement; it does not establish that State-created impairment of this protected opportunity is permissible whenever the evidence eventually appears in open court.

The Commonwealth emphasizes that Gardner involved undisclosed information and that Gray could cross-examine and testify. Those facts matter, but the issue is whether those formal opportunities could perform their adversarial function after the supported assurance and last-minute change. Graham requires that functional comparison without dispensing with Teague's dictated-at-finality requirement. Caspari likewise demands the actual rule and finality date, not a general appeal to fairness. Gardner supplies the pre-1987 protection; the later cases do not insert new law into that date.

The Court adopts no universal notice period, automatic exclusion remedy, or right to more preparation merely because counsel encounters surprise. It requires a supported deprivation of the existing opportunity. No Teague exception is invoked, and neither contemporary exception is abolished. A separately reformulated affirmative-deception claim still requires its own proper presentation and legal predicates; this old-rule holding does not automatically decide every such claim.

##### III. This presented injury and its effect are established

**Operative rule:** Gray's reopened due-process claim fairly encompasses the preparation injury adjudicated here, and the accepted existing findings establish denial of the protected opportunity and a prejudicial effect on selection of death. Neither the lack of a formal continuance motion nor the surviving vileness finding defeats relief on this record; the constitutional entitlement to sentencing relief does not depend on prevailing on a newly recast deception claim or the dismissed Brady ground.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer: five direct joins. The holding determines this claim's availability, violation, and effect without declaring all related theories exhausted or all due-process errors structural.

**Controlling explanation:** Claim IX and the state appeal presented delayed disclosure, impaired preparation, and fundamental unfairness; federal Ground A carried that complaint forward. The amendment expressly reopened that consistently presented due-process claim. Picard and Anderson require its substance, not identical wording; under Vasquez, the evidence accepted here develops the same preparation injury rather than fundamentally altering it. Claim IX's previously-adjudicated treatment is not converted into another claim's omission-based default. Keeney retains Townsend's hearing requirement when a properly presented claim turns on material facts inadequately developed in state proceedings, unless the prisoner deliberately bypassed the available state process. Through Fay, that qualification concerns the prisoner's knowing, considered choice to forgo the state opportunity; ordinary counsel negligence, or counsel's choice without the prisoner's participation, does not automatically establish bypass. Deliberate bypass permits refusal of the otherwise required hearing rather than compelling denial, and does not replace Coleman's distinct claim-default rule. Hall's negligence-based substitution therefore cannot sustain dismissal on this existing hearing record.

The District Court found an unequivocal statements-only assurance, reliance, and concrete impairment of investigation, impeachment, objections, and response when photographs and police and medical-examiner evidence were introduced. The preparation objection and renewed exclusion request were real, although no formal continuance motion was made. That omission does not establish a feasible cure or an adequate procedural bar here. The Court accepts the findings of the protected injury and a substantial probability of a different sentence.

Other violent-crime evidence and lawful eligibility do not resolve the effect of an effectively unanswered graphic presentation on the choice of death. Tuggle distinguishes eligibility from that choice. The existing findings and whole penalty record establish substantial injurious influence and also preclude a conclusion beyond reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. Brecht and O'Neal retain their category-specific holdings; the Court need not choose a general standard for this different category because relief follows under either competing approach. This conclusion neither identifies another perpetrator nor prohibits all uncharged-crime evidence.

##### IV. Conditional sentencing relief requires lawful prospective implementation

**Operative rule:** The reopened claim's established constitutional violation and prejudicial effect require reversal of the adverse appellate disposition and restoration and implementation of conditional relief from the death sentence. Before an operative writ is entered, any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory requirement material to that relief must be resolved; once met or held inapplicable, the Commonwealth must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable prospective District Court period or replace death with a lawful noncapital disposition.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer: five direct joins to this mandate and all its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The defect concerns the penalty proceeding. A new constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding, or lawful noncapital disposition, corrects that defect without retrying guilt or disturbing independent custody. Graham and Simmons preserve the distinction between sentencing relief and the unaffected conviction. The existing constitutional violation and effect are decided, rather than returned for unrestricted reconsideration.

The 1996 habeas amendments nevertheless cannot be bypassed by a constitutional conclusion if a statutory restriction actually governs. An applicable amended section 2254(d) restriction on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court requires its own temporal and predicate analysis. An applicable state-fact presumption or chapter 154 requirement likewise retains its force. Section 107(c)'s pending-case clause neither establishes Virginia's qualifying counsel system nor settles chapter 153's transition. No express exhaustion waiver, statutory exception, or satisfaction of an unadjudicated condition is presumed. Landgraf requires attention to the provision's enacted direction and actual operation, rather than resolution by the bare fact that litigation is pending.

This is a reservation for an actual applicable legal requirement, not a direction to reopen settled issues or search for hypothetical impediments. The federal evidentiary hearing occurred in 1994; no new hearing is authorized, and no exemption from section 2254(e)(2) is assumed. The receiving court must settle prospective implementation terms without treating the old 180-day calendar as presently running, automatically restored, or expired. The mandate fixes no immediate hearing date and orders no acquittal, guilt retrial, particular noncapital sentence, or release from independent lawful custody.

- **Weatherford v. Bursey, 429 U.S. 545:** Distinguished from the supported denial of an existing opportunity; its rejection of a general discovery entitlement remains.
- **Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288:** Applied using October 5, 1987 finality and dictated law. - **Caspari v. Bohlen, February 23, 1994:** Applied for finality-specific identification of the actual rule; no broad fairness exception replaces that inquiry.
- **Picard v. Connor, 404 U.S. 270:** Applied to the substance of the federal claim presented to the State.
- **Anderson v. Harless, 459 U.S. 4:** Applied to distinguish a presented federal claim from facts alone or a merely similar complaint.
- **Vasquez v. Hillery, 474 U.S. 254:** Applied to supplementation that does not fundamentally alter a presented claim; its deliberate-withholding reservation remains.
- **Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992:** Its deliberate-bypass qualification continues to govern the identified factual-development objection; ordinary negligence is insufficient, while claim defaults and distinct factual-presumption rules remain separate.
- **Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293:** Its fifth mandatory-hearing circumstance and deliberate-bypass qualification remain operative through Keeney for inadequate factual development of a properly presented claim.

#### Law After Decision

The existing capital opportunity-to-answer protection reaches a supported assurance and last-minute evidentiary change that actually prevent meaningful preparation and response; Teague does not bar this dictated application. The rule creates no fixed discovery deadline or automatic relief for surprise. This presented claim's violation and prejudicial effect warrant conditional sentencing relief on the existing record, subject to genuinely applicable unadjudicated statutory requirements before a writ operates.

The independently dismissed Brady ground remains outside the remand, while related evidence may support the distinct available due-process injury. Claim presentation, independent defaults, factual-development limits, and statutory restrictions retain their separate predicates. No universal collateral harmlessness standard, structural-error category, broad uncharged-evidence exclusion, new hearing entitlement, or AEDPA temporal exemption is established. The separate writings' broader reliability objection and alternative remand positions are not controlling law.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring alone.** Stevens joins all four parts of the Court's opinion. He would also find this presentation fundamentally unreliable even if adequately announced: weakly supported allegations of separate murders, a graphic account, and insufficiently tested linkage supplied the jury an aggravating case whose reliability counsel could not meaningfully test. Gardner's concern with a trustworthy capital determination supports his additional objection; his Lankford approach likewise attends to the defense's real ability to answer the case. His conclusion concerns this record, not a categorical prohibition of every uncharged act, and it does not identify Timothy Sorrell as the perpetrator. He accepts the established prejudicial effect under demanding whole-record substantial-influence review, without requiring a universal Chapman rule. No other Justice joins his independently sufficient reliability ground.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** O'Connor joins Parts I-II. Graham's functional treatment of an established capital protection, Gardner's opportunity to answer, and Caspari's finality-specific discipline support treating the supported meaningful-opportunity claim as an old-rule application rather than an automatic discovery timetable. Her Lankford and Simmons positions recognize the legal significance of misleading assurances. But correcting that legal premise does not, in her view, settle the present claim boundaries, findings, violation, and error effect sufficiently to require a writ. She would vacate and remand for lawful review of those actual questions, applying Keeney's factual-development rule and the appropriate independent claim requirements. Like her reserved remedial position in Simmons, that disposition leaves sentencing relief dependent on the proper application below. She preserves the separate Brady dismissal, rejects an exploratory search for hypothetical injury, and orders neither a new federal hearing nor a present penalty proceeding.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Part I but reject the ordinary-notice old-rule holding and the present sentencing relief. Gardner concerned information the defendant could not see; Weatherford supplies no general constitutional entitlement to earlier evidentiary disclosure. Under Teague and Caspari, those precedents did not dictate a preparation-time entitlement for evidence disclosed before use merely because additional time would help. Neither contemporary Teague exception permits relief on that newly formulated notice rule. Graham's distinct effective-mitigation holding does not eliminate the finality inquiry.

Affirmative misrepresentation is a distinct allegation requiring an actual assurance, reasonable reliance, a supported deprivation, and proper claim presentation. They would vacate the global dismissal only for that bounded inquiry, including whether the Commonwealth preserved a procedural-default defense; an unpreserved defense is not automatically revived. They decide neither that formulation's novelty nor its constitutional merits, effect, or eventual remedy. Keeney governs a properly available factual-development question, without curing an independent omitted claim. Their remand preserves the independent Brady dismissal, authorizes no new hearing or open-ended investigation, and supplies no current writ. Scalia's Lankford insistence on supported reliance and Kennedy's Lankford and Simmons distinctions between notice and misleading assurance remain consistent with that confined route.
