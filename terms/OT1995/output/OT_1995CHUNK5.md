## Decisions and Dispositions

### Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc., No. 95-83

Merits decision, March 19, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc., No. 95-83, 516 U.S. 479. Argued January 10, 1996; decided March 19, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 49 F.3d 518, reviewing its reversal of a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal. The questions are whether RCRA permits this private cleanup-cost reimbursement action and whether §6972(a)(1)(B) requires endangerment when suit begins.

KFC alleges that it bought the property in 1975, discovered petroleum-contaminated soil during October 1988 construction, and completed government-required cleanup in 1989. Its May 1992 suit seeks at least $211,296.23 and related expenses. The amended complaint alleges that the waste may have presented a substantial danger; it alleges none remaining at commencement. The allegations are accepted favorably to KFC for this review, without findings of contribution, liability, unjust enrichment or reasonable cost.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The pleaded claim fails independently under the filing-time endangerment requirement and the statute's remedial limits. No reimbursement, liability finding, discovery or trial is directed. Independently pleaded or otherwise lawfully available claims remain governed by their own requirements; another statute or state law is not held necessarily to authorize recovery.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer throughout; Stone-Zsela as specified below | Part I's present-threat holding has nine joins. Part II's full remedial construction and two precise reserved categories have eight joins; Stone-Zsela additionally joins the severable holding and necessary reasoning rejecting this action solely for completed-cleanup reimbursement, with costs arising during a properly commenced abatement action reserved. |

Stone-Zsela does not join Part II's additional rejection of general reimbursement for earlier expenses merely because endangerment remains when a proper action begins, or its separate treatment of costs paid after statutory-process invocation. His limited join states no contrary rule on those questions. No separate opinion is filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A qualifying threat must exist when the citizen action begins

**Holding:** Section 6972(a)(1)(B) requires waste that may present an imminent and substantial endangerment when suit begins. Past contribution may create a present threat, and the threat's physical effects may occur later; an allegation only of danger completely eliminated before filing does not satisfy the requirement.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight other participating Justices. This ground independently requires dismissal of KFC's pleaded RCRA claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute distinguishes the contributor's past conduct from the danger the waste may presently pose. Giving each phrase effect permits an action against a former contributor whose waste still threatens health or the environment; it does not convert a past danger into a current one. Imminence does not require existing injury or immediate physical consequences. Partial cleanup therefore does not defeat prospective relief if a qualifying threat remains and the other statutory prerequisites are met.

KFC alleges completed cleanup years before commencement and no remaining endangerment. Continuing off-site disposal expenses do not themselves allege a continuing threat. The Ninth Circuit's view that the language merely identifies a category of formerly dangerous waste removes the present-tense condition. The value of prompt cleanup cannot supply that missing condition. Gwaltney's ongoing-violation rule arose under different Clean Water Act language; this result rests on RCRA's own distinction between past contribution and present endangerment. The defect is statutory. It neither denies KFC's alleged financial injury nor imports summary-judgment proof requirements into pleading review.

##### The citizen-suit remedy does not create the claimed past-cleanup reimbursement action

**Holding:** Section 6972(a)'s restraint and necessary-action powers authorize prohibitory and affirmative abatement relief, not a private reimbursement action for a past cleanup effort merely labeled damages or equitable restitution. Remaining endangerment when a proper action begins does not itself confer general authority to reimburse earlier expenses, including expenses labeled emergency cleanup; the following two categories remain unresolved.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II, joined throughout by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins the narrower, independently sufficient holding and necessary reasoning that this action solely for a completed cleanup's costs is unavailable, reserving expense allocation arising during a properly commenced abatement action.

**Controlling explanation:** Necessary action serves removal of the qualifying threat. It does not establish retrospective allocation of a completed cleanup's cost. CERCLA expressly provides private cost recovery and contribution; RCRA does not create the same remedy through general abatement language. Porter, Hecht and Franklin preserve equitable relief subject to congressional limits. Sea Clammers and Transamerica support respecting the remedies of a specified enforcement scheme. An asserted restitutionary character cannot itself overcome that statutory limit.

The enforcement arrangements address threats while abatement remains possible. Section 6972(b)(2) ordinarily requires ninety days after notice to EPA, the affected State and alleged contributors; an action respecting a subchapter-III violation may begin immediately after notification, without a general emergency waiver. Government-action bars require restraint or abatement of acts or conditions that may have contributed or are contributing to waste activities that may present the alleged endangerment. The federal bar requires a commenced and diligently prosecuted §6973 or CERCLA §106 action; actual §104 removal; costs to initiate a §104 remedial investigation and feasibility study plus diligent remedial action; or a court order, including a consent decree, or §106/§6973 administrative order under which a responsible party diligently performs removal, a remedial investigation and feasibility study or remedial action. The administrative-order bar lasts only within that order's scope and duration. The state bar requires a commenced and diligently prosecuted §6972(a)(1)(B) action, actual §104 removal, or costs incurred to initiate a §104 remedial investigation and feasibility study plus diligent CERCLA remedial action. These arrangements serve abatement, not an unrestricted later cost action. No bar is found satisfied here.

KFC's asserted discharge of another's duty and the government's emergency-cost hypothesis do not add the omitted remedy. Similar language in EPA's separate §6973 powers does not decide private reimbursement. Prompt-cleanup policy cannot replace enacted authority. The Court rejects this pleaded demand without classifying all restitution as damages, determining liability or inventing a RCRA limitations period, contingency-plan burden or rule against laches.

**Exact reservations:** The Court does not decide whether a private plaintiff may obtain an injunction requiring another party to pay cleanup costs arising after a citizen action has properly commenced, **or otherwise recover cleanup costs paid after invocation of RCRA's statutory process**. Neither category is affirmatively authorized. Statutory-process invocation is not equated with filing; classification of a particular notice-stage expense remains undecided. Remaining danger alone does not reserve every earlier emergency expense.

**Additional limits:** EPA reimbursement under §6973 is not decided. Section 6972(f) preserves existing statutory and common-law rights without creating an alternative cause or guaranteeing recovery. Section 6972(e) permits litigation costs, including reasonable attorney and expert-witness fees, when appropriate in a final order for a prevailing or substantially prevailing party; it creates no cleanup-cost entitlement, and no fee award is made. CERCLA's petroleum exclusion is not imported into RCRA, and no universal petroleum-mixture classification is decided. The government's proposed Restatement restitution conditions are not adopted as new RCRA elements. Notice compliance supplies no alternative dismissal ground in this appeal. The state order and pending voluntary-dismissal request establish no new preclusion or mootness disposition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Gwaltney of Smithfield, Ltd. v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation: its ongoing-violation requirement concerns different Clean Water Act wording; it does not eliminate RCRA's distinct coverage of past contributors causing current danger.
- Hallstrom v. Tillamook County: the applicable notice and waiting requirements retain their force, including the actual statutory immediate-after-notification exception; cleanup policy creates no general waiver.
- Porter v. Warner Holding Co.: traditional equitable powers remain subject to statutory limits and do not supply the additional RCRA cost remedy.
- Hecht Co. v. Bowles: equitable discretion remains within the authority Congress grants; it does not enlarge this abatement scheme into general reimbursement.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools: appropriate relief in an existing statutory action remains available absent congressional limitation; RCRA's affirmative remedial structure supplies the material distinction.
- Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association: its enforcement-structure reasoning supports declining to add the asserted private remedy.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis: its caution against additional remedies in a specified statutory scheme supports the same construction.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: its statutory-category rule remains intact; this decision does not treat all monetary relief as legal damages or reject all restitution.

No earlier Supreme Court holding is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 19, 1996, private endangerment actions under §6972(a)(1)(B) require a qualifying present threat at commencement. Past conduct and delayed harm remain covered within that condition. The citizen-suit remedy provides prospective restraint and affirmative abatement, without a general past-cleanup reimbursement action. The two post-commencement and post-invocation cost categories remain unresolved on their precise terms.

The decision adds these statutory limits without modifying ordinary equitable principles across other statutes, CERCLA's separate cost regime or EPA's distinct §6973 authority. It creates no general prohibition on monetary equity and no guarantee of an alternative remedy. Both actual-claim grounds independently require the present dismissal; eight Justices directly support the full remedial construction.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The complaint's expenses are allegations, not proof of responsibility or a recoverable amount. The later California summary-judgment order concerns a different evidentiary proceeding and establishes neither federal pleading facts nor every preclusion predicate. The petition appendix's signed November 9, 1992 dismissal says with prejudice; the November 10 district-docket description says without prejudice. That discrepancy supplies no additional disposition. The voluntary-dismissal request was taken off calendar pending review, with no completed dismissal shown. The agency order, technical soil record, invoices and state evidentiary submissions support no additional findings on this review. The [1994 Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title42/html/USCODE-1994-title42.htm), §§6972 and 6973, contains the distinct citizen and governmental provisions; §6972(b)(2) adds no filed-earlier/120-day/90-day override.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit must implement reversal and remand for dismissal of the RCRA claim as pleaded. Its order allowing further proceedings on that reimbursement theory cannot stand. No special mandate date, payment, discovery, trial or adjudication of independent claims is directed.

**End of entry: Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc., Merits decision, March 19, 1996.**

### Varity Corp. v. Howe, No. 94-1471

Merits decision, March 19, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Varity Corp. v. Howe, No. 94-1471, 516 U.S. 489. Argued November 1, 1995; decided March 19, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit, 36 F.3d 746, as clarified at 41 F.3d 1263. The questions concern individual equitable relief for fiduciary breach under ERISA §502(a)(3) and liability for the challenged benefit communications despite reserved amendment and termination powers.

The 83-member Retired Class transferred employment to MCC in reliance on an integrated benefits presentation and retired before MCC's receivership. Ten other respondents had already retired from M-F when their benefit obligations were assigned without their knowledge or consent. The district court's unchallenged findings establish deliberate deception and reliance for the class; the ten retirees' independent claim concerns unauthorized assignment. MCC stopped paying benefits, while the M-F plan remained in existence.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retired Class | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas | Reviewed fiduciary liability and restoration sustained under the clarified decree. |
| Ten individual retirees | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas | Independent lower-court assignment-breach determination preserved; categorical objection to individual equitable recovery rejected. |

Stone-Zsela joins the judgment on the ten retirees' distinct assignment component without adding a ground.

The clarified decree restores membership with only the rights enjoyed by members of the M-F plan as it now exists. The rejected $7.6 million and $712,332 compensatory awards remain rejected. Past-benefit entitlement may be adjusted for intervening events, and the district court may hear relevant additional evidence and enter a conforming decree. The Court enters no new amount and does not classify each substituted monetary item as equitable. Future lawful changes remain possible, subject to applicable law and preserved retaliation or discrimination claims; reinstatement grants no immutable lifetime benefits.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The benefit presentation was fiduciary administration

**Controlling proposition:** Under ERISA §3(21)(A), fiduciary responsibility attaches to the particular exercise of discretionary plan administration, including plan information communicated in that capacity. Varity performed that function when it used its administrative authority, benefit comparisons and linked assurances of benefit security to induce the class's transfer; general employer status, corporate forecasting or participant perception alone would not establish it.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices directly support this rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court identifies what Varity was doing, rather than treating every act of a plan administrator as fiduciary. John Hancock requires attention to the particular statutory function, separately from breach and remedy. Here, administrators used their authority over benefit information to explain the proposed transfer's consequences for participants' existing protection. The descriptions of current benefits and the assurances of continued security formed one administrative presentation. Accurate statements that MCC would initially maintain the same terms do not require treating the deliberately linked assurances as unrelated corporate speech. The speakers' authority, the occasion and the communication's content identify actual administration; employees' understanding is relevant context, not an independent statutory test. The record also includes warnings about MCC's success and possible future changes. The unchallenged findings establish that the combined presentation was nonetheless knowingly misleading. Curtiss-Wright preserves sponsor amendment powers but distinguishes possession of a power from its authorized exercise. Neither that power nor the employer's ability to act in two capacities supplies immunity for this different administrative act. The holding does not classify every employment statement or prediction of corporate solvency as fiduciary conduct.

##### II. Deliberate disloyal deception breaches §404

**Controlling proposition:** An administrator violates §404(a)(1)'s loyalty duty when, acting in its fiduciary capacity, it knowingly and significantly misleads participants about benefit security to induce surrender of existing plan interests for the employer's benefit. Varity's found deception and the class's reliance satisfy that rule notwithstanding the reservation of lawful amendment or termination powers.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by the same six Justices as Part I; direct majority support.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory duty requires administration solely in participants' and beneficiaries' interests. Firestone's use of trust principles helps explain why deliberate deception in that relationship violates loyalty; it does not replace the statutory function requirement established in Part I. The findings show that Varity used benefit explanations and assurances to procure transfers while knowing MCC's poor prospects and seeking to remove benefit costs. The Court therefore decides intentional disloyal conduct, not whether the plan promised permanent benefits. A power to make a lawful future change does not authorize knowingly misleading participants into surrendering present membership. The warnings and reservation language remain part of the evidence but do not overturn the accepted findings about the full communication. No general duty to volunteer information, answer every inquiry, disclose every forecast or avoid negligent misstatement is decided. This communication holding supplies no new rule governing the ten retirees' separate assignment claim.

##### III. Section 502(a)(3) permits appropriate individual equitable redress

**Controlling proposition:** Sections 409 and 502(a)(2) do not impose a categorical plan-only bar on §502(a)(3)'s express authorization of appropriate individual equitable relief for a §404 violation. Such relief must remain within traditionally equitable categories; where another ERISA provision supplies adequate relief for the injury, further equitable relief normally is unnecessary and therefore inappropriate, without an absolute rule governing every alternative pleading.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this proposition and its application. Stone-Zsela supports the class's judgment on the distinct, stricter condition explained in his concurrence, without joining this Part.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 502(a)(2) provides the plan-centered remedy specified by §409. Section 502(a)(3) separately permits participants and beneficiaries to redress statutory and plan violations through appropriate equity. Giving that express grant work for personal injury does not change the recipient of §409 recovery or imply an omitted damages remedy. Russell confined the latter provision and expressly left §502(a)(3) open. Mertens likewise reserved its disclaimed antecedent action question and instead enforced the equitable-category limit. That limit remains binding here; trust law does not authorize general compensation. Peacock requires an actual statutory or plan violation, which the class's proved §404 breach supplies, rather than equitable relief at large.

The class seeks restoration after administrative deception procured its surrender of membership. Failure of its different lifetime-benefits theory does not adjudicate an adequate remedy for that injury under §502(a)(1)(B). The specific benefits remedy remains effective on its own terms, but its existence does not itself defeat this restoration. Adequate statutory redress ordinarily makes further equity unnecessary; the Court does not decide every overlap or pleading combination. The equitable character of the particular monetary items is outside the questions presented, so affirmance neither reinstates the rejected compensatory awards nor classifies replacement-insurance premiums or all unpaid benefits as restitution. The clarified decree restores the existing relationship without creating additional vested rights.

##### IV. The ten retirees' distinct breach remains undisturbed

**Controlling proposition:** The common categorical plan-only objection does not defeat the ten retirees' reviewed individual equitable recovery, while their distinct lower-court assignment-breach determination remains undisturbed because the petition did not sufficiently present a challenge to that ground. Affirmance preserves the clarified decree without deciding a new substantive assignment rule, the precise monetary classifications, or whether §502(a)(1)(B) is categorically unavailable to these retirees.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the presentation ground and its remedial limits. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without adding a ground on the distinct assignment component.

**Controlling explanation:** These respondents had retired before the transfer and did not consent to assignment of their benefit obligations. Their claim does not depend on receiving the class's presentation or voluntarily leaving the M-F plan. Petitioner's reply contests recovery, but its reliance on absence of misrepresentation and lifetime contractual rights does not identify error in the different unauthorized-administration ground. The petition's communications question does not adequately put that ground before the Court. The individual-action question does apply to both groups, and Part III rejects the categorical statutory obstacle. The Court thus preserves the lower determination rather than deciding assignment liability anew. Curtiss-Wright does not convert an amendment reservation into proof of an authorized assignment, while reinstatement creates no permanent vested entitlement. The rejected contract theory proves neither an adequate alternative remedy nor the categorical absence of one. No separate monetary-category ruling is required to affirm the clarified judgment. Section 510 and equitable estoppel remain unadopted alternatives; the Court revives no terminated-employee, punitive, lifetime-contract, settled disability or pension claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

* **Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell, 473 U.S. 134:** Its plan-centered §409 construction is preserved. The Court answers the distinct §502(a)(3) individual-action question Russell expressly reserved.
* **Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248:** Its traditionally equitable-category limit is applied without converting the reserved antecedent action issue into a prior rejection. Its unchallenged restitution disposition and reservation of other monetary equity remain intact.
* **John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 510 U.S. 86:** Its particular-function inquiry is applied to administration. The insurance-asset holding and the reservation concerning agency litigation positions remain separate; no deference ruling is needed here.
* **Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen, 514 U.S. 73:** Its distinction between retained amendment power and authorized exercise is preserved. The decision supplies neither an immunity for administrative deception nor lifetime vesting.
* **Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch, 489 U.S. 101:** Trust-law guidance informs fiduciary loyalty within ERISA's actual text. The Court does not replace ordinary benefits-review rules with a general fiduciary remedy.
* **Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349:** Its requirement of a statutory or plan predicate is satisfied by the identified §404 breach; its later new-debtor jurisdictional limit does not govern this original fiduciary action. Its broader monetary reservations remain open.

No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

ERISA fiduciary status remains tied to the function performed. The Court now applies that requirement and the loyalty duty to the deliberate use of plan administration to mislead participants into surrendering membership. It resolves the previously open categorical individual-action question in favor of appropriate equitable redress under §502(a)(3), preserving the distinct plan remedy, benefits remedy and equitable-category limit. The controlling adequacy principle is qualified: adequate statutory relief normally makes further equity unnecessary. Stone-Zsela's stricter condition does not become the Court's rule.

The ten retirees' lower breach determination remains effective in this case without establishing a new general assignment rule. The decision establishes no universal disclosure duty, categorical overlap prohibition, new financial-remedy classification or immutable benefits entitlement.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the holdings on fiduciary administration and deliberate loyalty breach. Varity used its administrative role to explain existing benefit security and induce surrender through materially false assurances; employer restructuring powers do not excuse that separate function. He would permit individual equitable redress only when no other ERISA provision adequately remedies the same injury. Sections 502(a)(1)(B)–(3) must retain their separate tasks, and Russell's reserved question permits the express equitable action without weakening Mertens's prohibition on converting it into general legal damages. He therefore supports the class's restoration within the clarified decree while excluding legal or punitive damages and duplicate relief through another adequate statutory channel. His condition is stronger than the Court's statement that adequate statutory relief normally makes further equity inappropriate; he does not join Part III. He concurs in affirmance of the ten retirees' distinct assignment component without adding a ground and does not join Part IV. He supplies no finding that they voluntarily left their plan, no categorical denial of a benefits action and no new classification of the monetary items.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by O'Connor and Scalia.** The dissent reads §§409 and 502(a)(2) as the specific statutory route for fiduciary-breach relief, with recovery directed to the plan. In its view, §502(a)(3)'s residual language cannot remove that deliberate recipient limitation for the same violation; the provision still applies to other statutory and plan obligations. Russell expressly reserved this question, but its respect for ERISA's differentiated enforcement structure supports that construction. Mertens decided the remedy category and reserved its antecedent action question, so it supplies no prior action bar. The dissent reaches its construction independently rather than treating either precedent as already dispositive.

The dissent also rejects the class's fiduciary-capacity theory. Accurate descriptions of current benefit terms do not, in its view, turn corporate viability forecasts into discretionary administration merely because the employer also administers a plan. It accepts the findings of deliberate deception but disputes their ERISA classification; participant understanding and general fiduciary status cannot replace the statutory function. This ground does not dispose of the ten retirees' different assignment claim. Assuming their undisturbed breach, the dissent applies its individual-action objection to both groups. Reserved amendment power does not establish a valid assignment or erase the findings. It would reverse the reviewed fiduciary-breach recovery and remand for any preserved §510 or estoppel ground, without guaranteeing recovery, dismissing the entire action, reviving closed claims or deciding the unpresented monetary classifications.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The [Eighth Circuit judgment, 36 F.3d 746](https://static.case.law/f3d/36/cases/0746-01.json), and its December 8, 1994 clarification, 41 F.3d 1263, govern the affirmed decree. The class past-benefit figure below is $696,195. The individual items total $82,812, although one appellate passage states $81,812. Affirmance does not settle that discrepancy or enter a new award. The selected appendix does not contain the entire trial record; no later amendment, payment or enforcement result is established.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The affirmed clarified judgment returns to the lower courts for the existing adjustment of past entitlement and entry of a conforming decree. Reinstatement carries the rights of the M-F plan as it now exists, with lawful future changes subject to applicable law and preserved retaliation or discrimination objections. No new payment amount, payment deadline, mandate date or subsequent amendment is directed.

**End of entry: Varity Corp. v. Howe, Merits decision, March 19, 1996.**

### Wisconsin v. City of New York; Oklahoma v. City of New York; Department of Commerce v. City of New York, Nos. 94-1614, 94-1631 and 94-1985

Merits decision, March 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Wisconsin v. City of New York, No. 94-1614; Oklahoma v. City of New York, No. 94-1631; Department of Commerce v. City of New York, No. 94-1985; 517 U.S. 1. October Term 1995. Argued January 10, 1996; decided March 20, 1996. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, 34 F.3d 1114.

It did not direct adoption of adjusted numbers. The questions concern that constitutional standard and this methodological choice; the categorical sampling argument presents a separate, disputed question of scope.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The individual voters' claim is reviewable; APA availability does not reopen a distinct claim

**Controlling proposition:** The identified California voter appellants have standing to pursue their own continuing representation injury where the challenged census determination has a concrete connection to the asserted loss of a California seat and authoritative review is substantially likely to provide redress through the statutory allocation process. Their claim does not depend on California's own appeal or require certainty that reconsideration will produce a chosen dataset or an injunction against the President.

**Authority:** Part I, supported by Stevens and the seven Associates named in the topology, controls with eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Mack and Morales are identified California voters and individual appellants. Both sides' accounts of the later corrected adjustment identify a potential Wisconsin-to-California seat transfer. That connection supports a personal representational injury, rather than a general interest in census accuracy. Montana recognizes continuing allocation injury and likely declaratory redress despite completed statutory transmission; Franklin applies that rule to review of the responsible census determination. A favorable legal ruling could remove the asserted obstacle to corrective consideration without guaranteeing the agency's ultimate lawful methodological choice. The State's decision whether to appeal cannot surrender these individuals' separate voting claim. The Court therefore need not resolve the discrepancy concerning California's own appellate designation, rely on Arizona's earlier projected gain or decide every other plaintiff's standing. This conclusion neither orders a seat transfer nor finds independently proved unchanged personal residence through the decision date.

**Distinct APA proposition:** Franklin's rule that the Secretary's completed census determination is final agency action under §704 remains governing law despite later presidential transmission. Availability of that review does not reopen the district court's ordinary APA disposition in this constitutional appeal. The Court decides no new APA merits challenge and does not equate constitutional reasonableness with every requirement of APA review.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Part I coalition directly adopts this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin preserves substantial methodological choice while requiring a contemporaneous, reasonably discernible explanation grounded in the agency's actual reasons. Its completed-determination holding leaves the President's own APA status and coercive relief undecided. The appellate presentation here concerns the independent constitutional challenge; it supplies no basis for a fresh disposition of ordinary APA merits. The Court therefore preserves the surviving APA disposition without adopting the district court's colloquial review shorthand or holding that every possible statutory claim was forfeited. Reviewability, preservation and the constitutional merits remain separate inquiries.

##### II. This nonadjustment decision satisfies the constitutional standard

**Controlling proposition:** The choice whether to supplement this nationwide enumeration with the proposed post-enumeration survey must bear a reasonable relationship to enumerating the population and securing population-based representation. Courts enforce that boundary while respecting the methodological judgment entrusted to the Secretary; a genuine enumeration effort and an explained choice addressing geographic accuracy and comparative uncertainty do not become unconstitutional merely because another method improves some accuracy measures. This nonadjustment decision satisfies that standard, and the panel's legitimate-objective-and-essentiality requirement is rejected.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices' direct support and controls both the standard and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Constitution requires an actual enumeration directed toward population-based representation. It does not identify a statistical technique that courts must select whenever one measure improves. Wesberry, Kirkpatrick and Karcher concern avoidable population differences in drawing districts within a State. Choosing among estimates of the population itself presents a different problem. Montana permits competing legitimate measures of interstate equality, and Franklin sustains a supported population-counting choice; neither makes census administration immune from constitutional review.

The Secretary undertook extensive enumeration and coverage efforts and considered the acknowledged differential undercount. He accepted likely improvement in the national total but explained uncertainty about geographic shares, heterogeneity within statistical groups and sensitivity to reasonable statistical choices. The district court's finding of improvement for most purposes does not resolve every relevant geographic comparison. Respondents correctly distinguish a better alternative adjustment from proof that the proposed adjustment is worse than no adjustment. The Court does not confuse those propositions: the Secretary's actual explanation supports doubt that this proposal reliably improves the distribution of the count.

The delegation of the census task warrants latitude over that methodological judgment; the Secretary's title, adviser headcount and technical disagreement alone do not supply a reason. The rebuttable administrative guideline favoring the enumeration operates within the explained comparison, not as a constitutionally mandated presumption. The stipulated duty of de novo agency reconsideration did not authorize de novo judicial selection. On this record no additional factual inquiry is needed under the proper constitutional standard. Differential undercount is serious, but does not itself establish discriminatory intent or the claimed constitutional duty to adjust. Davis and Arlington Heights retain their purpose requirement for ordinary racial-discrimination claims; the distinct representation claim fails on the analysis just given, not merely for lack of intent.

**Operative limits:** The Court creates no numerical tolerance, presumption of perfect accuracy or inevitable superiority of enumeration, automatic extension of intrastate burdens, or census exemption from judicial review. Proven discrimination, unauthorized methods and actual unsupported reasons remain subject to their governing legal requirements. The decision does not find actual PES manipulation, endorse every future-participation prediction, treat disruption as an independent cure for an unsupported count, or find a missed production deadline. Later statistical corrections bear on injury and relief, not on the information available in July 1991.

##### III. Sampling authority remains open; relief follows the constitutional holding

**Controlling proposition:** Rejection of a constitutional duty to make this adjustment decides neither whether the Constitution prohibits this type of adjustment nor whether §195 categorically bars sampling for congressional apportionment. Reversal ends the panel's constitutional justification inquiry and requires implementation of judgment for petitioners on the reviewed challenge, without judicial selection of replacement numbers or disposition of unrelated matters.

**Authority:** Part III has all nine Justices' direct support. Its reservations endorse neither contested statutory construction.

**Controlling explanation:** Permission to use a method would not establish a constitutional duty to select it. Section 195's apportionment exception may be argued to prohibit that sampling or to remove it from the provision's otherwise mandatory direction conditioned on feasibility. Neither construction is necessary to decide this constitutional challenge. Section 141(a)'s methodological language does not alone resolve their interaction, and the Court decides neither respondents' Rule 14.1(a) objection nor the panel's statutory construction. The federal petitioners reject a categorical ban; the States assert it. The Secretary expressly disclaimed legal considerations as the basis of his decision, so their appellate theory cannot replace his actual reasons. The constitutional holding itself supplies the bounded judgment.

**Relief and reservations:** No adjustment, dataset, seat transfer, presidential injunction, funding redistribution or new redistricting is ordered. The separate tape-disclosure and protective-order ruling remains outside the judgment. No new merits hearing, renewed APA proceeding, special mandate date or continuing Supreme Court supervision is prescribed.

- Franklin v. Massachusetts: applied for supported constitutional counting choices and for the completed Secretary determination's finality and actual-reasons requirements. Its constitutional holding did not already decide PES adjustment; its reservations concerning the President remain intact.

### Ayers v. Fordice

Dismissal as improvidently granted, March 25, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Ayers v. Fordice. Decided March 25, 1996; October Term 1995. On certiorari before judgment in the Fifth Circuit, limited to the denial of additional library relief in the Northern District of Mississippi's March 7, 1995 decision, 879 F. Supp. 1419, 1458, No. 4:75CV009-B-O.

The challenge concerns the relationship among inherited university missions, library allocations and the repair of segregation-caused educational injury, including whether necessary repair may depend on predicted additional white enrollment. The library denial remains operative and the challenge is properly presented in the pending appeal. Every other decree provision and appellate challenge is outside this review.

#### Public Action

The writ of certiorari before judgment is dismissed as improvidently granted. The Court leaves the library denial and the remaining decree undisturbed without affirming their merits.

#### Disposition and Relief

**Dismissed as improvidently granted, 8–1. Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The Court neither affirms nor vacates the library denial and orders no merits remand, library expansion, expenditure or release from supervision. The pending appeal and the district court's continuing remedial jurisdiction remain available. Stone-Zsela would reach the limited question, vacate only the denial of additional library relief and remand for reconsideration, leaving all other provisions and challenges undisturbed without merits affirmance.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela files a dissenting opinion, joined by no other Justice, and joins no portion of the Court's opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The limited library dispute does not warrant continued review before appellate judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 11 permits certiorari before appellate judgment only when imperative public importance justifies departure from normal appellate practice and requires immediate determination in this Court. On this limited library challenge, the Court dismisses the writ as improvidently granted because the existing appeal can examine the asserted error under established law and the circumstances do not sufficiently justify immediate Supreme Court determination; dismissal decides no merits question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All eight adopt the procedural rule, its application and dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1254(1) and 2101(e) authorize review before appellate judgment, but statutory availability does not establish that exceptional review should continue. Rule 11 requires both sufficient public importance to depart from ordinary appellate practice and a need for immediate determination here.

The unfinished statewide dismantlement obligation is important. A legal restriction on necessary educational repair could matter while remedial work proceeds. Fordice already establishes the State's continuing dismantlement duty, and Jenkins recognizes violation-linked educational repair on sufficient findings. The pending appeal can examine whether this particular denial respects those principles, its mission rationale and the supported factual findings. A potentially consequential error in their application does not alone establish the need to bypass appellate judgment.

The district opinion's account of ongoing Jackson State and Alcorn library projects does not establish that awaiting the appellate judgment will foreclose effective relief or that an impending decision requires immediate clarification by this Court. Rule 11 does not impose a separate emergency, imminent-expenditure or fixed-deadline requirement; those are possible reasons for immediacy, not mandatory categories. The Court makes a case-specific judgment under the Rule, not a categorical bar to early review of desegregation remedies. It decides neither the correctness of the library denial nor the necessity of any additional relief.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Fordice and Ayers v. Fordice, June 26, 1992:** The continuing dismantlement duty, State's burden and examination of interacting inherited policies remain governing. Their availability on ordinary appeal supports the procedural disposition; their application to this library denial is reserved.
- **Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995:** Its permission for violation-linked educational repair on sufficient findings remains unchanged. The Court neither imports its injury findings nor decides its application to additional university library relief.
- **Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992:** Its complete conditions for partial withdrawal of supervision remain unchanged. This dismissal grants no release and decides no request for one.
- **Milliken v. Bradley (Milliken II), 433 U.S. 267:** Its connection between educational remedies and the established violation remains intact. The library application proposed in the dissent is not adopted by the Court.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 11's exceptional-review criterion is applied without adding a new emergency prerequisite. The dismissal establishes no library merits holding, validates no inherited mission limit and imposes no funding obligation. Fordice's dismantlement duty and burden, Jenkins's requirement of a sufficient violation-linked repair basis and Freeman's distinct release standard remain unchanged. The denial remains subject to the pending appeal; neither it nor any other decree provision receives merits affirmance.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The Chief Justice would vacate only the additional-library-relief denial. When an established segregation-caused educational injury remains unremedied, the absence of predicted additional white enrollment cannot by itself justify refusing necessary, practicable and educationally sound repair. Fordice places the dismantlement burden on the State and requires examination of interacting inherited policies. Jenkins recognizes educational repair as an independently sufficient ground; Milliken II connects educational remedies beyond pupil assignment to the violation. Its application to university libraries develops the doctrine; Milliken II did not decide this question.

The district court attributed collection differences to historical missions and denied enlargement beyond them because the holdings were not shown to preclude the institutions as viable choices for white students. It also credited Jackson State and Alcorn expansions. The dissent preserves supported findings, including those about volume counts and student choice, but finds the educational-repair inquiry unresolved. Different lawful missions may require different collections. Inherited missions cannot conclusively justify resource limits when mission policies continue fostering separation and library allocations are traceable to the extent they follow those missions.

Reconsideration would ask whether a library condition attributable to the dual system remains unremedied, what resources are needed for each institution's legally justified mission—including the program changes already ordered in decree paragraphs 3–10—and what practicable, educationally sound repair remains necessary after crediting the existing improvements. Neither traceability, historical inadequacy nor a collection disparity itself establishes a present injury or a necessary expenditure. If the libraries adequately support lawful missions and existing improvements remedy any attributable injury, no additional relief follows.

The proposed remand preserves existing findings and leaves all other decree provisions and appellate challenges undisturbed without merits affirmance. It reopens no mission, program, admissions or equipment determination and adjudicates no scholarship or endowment eligibility, committee procedure, maintenance control or reporting deadline. It requires neither identical collections nor funding parity, a fixed appropriation, new hearing, universal proof protocol or withdrawal of supervision. Historically black institutions are not themselves the constitutional wrong. Any release question retains Freeman's and Jenkins's distinct requirements.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The [district opinion, 879 F. Supp. 1419](https://static.case.law/f-supp/879/html/1419-01.html), distinguishes numerical collection differences from the poorer physical condition of library space, and mission-linked allocations from capital-improvement and repair allocations found not traceable to segregation. Its account of projects underway in March 1995 establishes no finding of completion in March 1996. This Court makes no new finding of present library injury, necessary enlargement, cost or feasibility. [Rule 11 of the 1995 Rules](https://www.supremecourt.gov/ctrules/rules/Rules_1995.pdf), 515 U.S. 1204, supplies the exceptional-review standard.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This review before appellate judgment ends. The Fifth Circuit appeal remains pending, and the district court retains its existing remedial jurisdiction. No decree provision is vacated, affirmed on its merits or newly imposed; no new Supreme Court event or special mandate deadline is set.

**End of entry: Ayers v. Fordice, Dismissal as improvidently granted, March 25, 1996.**

### Barnett Bank of Marion County, N.A. v. Nelson, No. 94-1837

Merits decision, March 26, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Barnett Bank of Marion County, N.A. v. Nelson, No. 94-1837; 517 U.S. 25. October Term 1995. Argued January 16, 1996; decided March 26, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 43 F.3d 631.

The bank seeks prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against Florida's prohibition on insurance-agency activity conducted through a financial-institution affiliation. The questions concern conflict with 12 U.S.C. §92, whether Florida's law was enacted to regulate the business of insurance, and whether §92 specifically relates to that business within McCarran-Ferguson's federal exception. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed denial of relief on McCarran-Ferguson grounds without deciding the bank's qualifying-location and customer-geography cross-appeals.

Barnett Marion is a holding-company subsidiary headquartered in Ocala with a branch in Belleview, whose population was fewer than 5,000 under the last decennial census. It bought an agency operated by a Florida-licensed agent who became its employee. The bank sued on the purchase date; four days later the Department prohibited the agency activity except credit life and credit disability sales. Belleview's population alone does not resolve the association's statutory eligibility.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

McCarran-Ferguson does not preserve the challenged prohibition against qualifying §92 activity, and the ordinary-preemption defense of the judgment is rejected. The Eleventh Circuit must resolve the preserved questions whether the Ocala-headquartered association qualifies through its Belleview branch and whether a qualifying association may serve customers outside its small locality. The lower courts must then provide prospective relief against application of the conflicting prohibition to activity within the bank's established §92 entitlement. No unconditional injunction or nationwide sales entitlement is awarded now.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I-A | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | Eight Justices adopt the general national-bank prevention/significant-interference principle, including its statutory and compatible-regulation limits. |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I-B, II and III | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | All nine adopt the specific §92 conflict holding in Part I-B, the insurance-specific federal exception in Part II, and the conditions, reservation and bounded remand in Part III. |

Stone-Zsela joins Parts I-B, II and III and the judgment. No separate opinion is filed. Each controlling proposition has direct majority support.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I-A. State law may not prevent or significantly interfere with federally conferred national-bank powers

**Controlling proposition:** A congressional grant of national-bank power ordinarily protects its exercise against state laws that prevent or significantly interfere with that exercise. This protection includes lawfully conferred enumerated and incidental powers, whose existence and limits depend on the particular federal scheme, including any congressional incorporation of state restrictions. States retain authority under this ground where their laws do not prevent or significantly interfere with the federal power.

**Authority:** Part I-A, supported by Ginsburg and the seven Associates named in the topology, controls with eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin National Bank identifies affirmative federal banking authority as a protected power, rather than a capacity available only with an additional state veto. De la Cuesta establishes that a protected option need not be compulsory to prevail over a conflicting prohibition. McClellan preserves state rules compatible with the granted power. The inquiry therefore concerns what Congress conferred and how the state law operates upon it. Where Congress expressly incorporates state limitations, those limitations remain part of the federal scheme.

This rule reaches significant interference short of an outright ban. It does not rest on an assumption that national banks occupy a field free of state regulation. Gade rejects displacement from overlap alone, and Freightliner requires an operative federal obligation or demonstrated protected objective, rather than mere federal silence. Those distinctions remain intact. A law's ordinary cost, general applicability or regulatory label does not mechanically decide whether it significantly interferes with an actual federal power. The Court creates no numerical threshold, balancing formula, special evidentiary procedure or categorical licensing safe harbor. No state requirement beyond the challenged prohibition is adjudicated here.

##### I-B. Florida's categorical affiliation prohibition conflicts with §92

**Controlling proposition:** Section 92 affirmatively authorizes the specified insurance-agency activity for a national banking association satisfying its conditions. Florida may not apply the challenged affiliation prohibition to prevent that association from exercising the authority §92 confers; the ability to comply with both laws by abandoning the federally authorized activity does not remove the conflict.

**Authority:** Part I-B has all nine Justices' direct support. This specific statutory holding does not depend on unanimous adoption of Part I-A's broader rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 92 adds insurance-agency activity to national-bank powers and expressly defines the eligible associations, permitted services and conditions. Congress did more than leave an existing practice undisturbed. Franklin National Bank supports giving that affirmative banking grant operative effect, and de la Cuesta confirms that a protected federal option is not defeated merely because exercising it is voluntary.

Florida prohibits the activity because the agent is affiliated with the bank. Respondents read §92 as removing only a federal corporate-capacity disability, leaving national banks free to compete wherever state banks may sell insurance. But §92 contains no corresponding state-veto or competitive-parity condition. Its express requirement of state authorization for the insurer remains effective; it does not say that the State may negate the bank's agency power because of its affiliation. Congress's express adoption of state branching restrictions in another statutory setting does not insert that condition here. Equal treatment of state and national banks likewise does not reconcile the prohibition with the grant.

The conclusion follows from this particular authorization and its setting, not the word may alone or a rule that every federal permission is preemptive. Compatible supervision of sales remains possible. No abstract ruling on ordinary licensing, consumer-protection or contract requirements is needed, and no independent national-bank power beyond §92 supplies this specific holding.

##### II. Section 92 falls within McCarran-Ferguson's insurance-specific federal exception

**Controlling proposition:** Section 92 specifically relates to the business of insurance within 15 U.S.C. §1012(b). McCarran-Ferguson therefore does not preserve Florida's conflicting prohibition, even assuming it was enacted for the purpose of regulating that business. The federal exception removes the obstacle to ordinary preemption; the actual conflict established in Part I-B remains independently necessary.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices' direct support. The Court does not decide Florida's disputed statutory purpose as an alternative ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 92 expressly addresses acting for fire, life and other insurers by soliciting and selling policies, collecting premiums and receiving agency compensation. It also limits particular insurance undertakings. Those operative provisions directly concern bringing insurer and policyholder into the insurance transaction, not an incidental reference to an insurance company.

A provision can promote banking income and specifically relate to insurance at the same time. Its location within a broader banking enactment does not remove its insurance subject matter, and the words Act of Congress do not require every provision of the containing enactment to concern insurance. The federal exception asks whether the enactment specifically relates to the business; it does not repeat the different state-law purpose requirement. Nor does it require an additional express-preemption statement. Replacing specifically relates with specifically preempts would change the enacted exception.

Fabe protects policyholder performance and necessary administration against a federal priority statute lacking the required insurance relationship; it does not eliminate this express exception. John Hancock likewise recognizes insurance-specific federal duties within a broader enactment while preserving compatible state regulation. Section 92's 1916 date changes neither its subject nor Congress's authority over national banks. McCarran-Ferguson imposes no post-1944 enactment condition, and National Bank of Oregon establishes that §92 remains in force.

**Reservation:** Florida's asserted concerns about coercion, concentration and insurer solvency are assumed in its favor without a final purpose classification. The Court finds neither protectionist purpose nor bank misconduct and imposes no least-restrictive-means test or requirement for a particular legislative record. McCarran-Ferguson's protection against federal enactments that do not specifically relate to insurance remains intact.

##### III. Federal conditions and the unresolved cross-appeals govern relief

**Controlling proposition:** Relief must prevent application of the conflicting affiliation prohibition to the bank's qualifying §92 activity, after resolution of the preserved association-location and customer-geography issues. A favorable preemption ruling does not itself establish this bank's eligibility, authorize unrestricted nationwide sales or dispense with the statute's conditions.

**Authority:** Part III has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** The Eleventh Circuit left two independent cross-appeal questions undecided after sustaining the McCarran defense. A branch in a small town does not, without resolving the statutory issue, establish that an association headquartered elsewhere is located and doing business in a qualifying place. Nor does qualification itself answer the separate customer-geography question. National Bank of Oregon reserved geographic reach, and NationsBank's conditioned annuity-brokerage holding reserved genuine-insurance geography and preemption. Neither supplies the missing answers. The district court's favorable conclusions therefore cannot become conclusive merely because the court of appeals' preemption premise fails. The lower courts must resolve the preserved matters and conform relief to the resulting entitlement, without reconsidering the statutory conflict and federal exception decided here.

**Federal conditions retained:** Section 92 applies to an association located and doing business in a place with **no more than 5,000 inhabitants under the last preceding decennial census**. The insurer must be authorized by the authorities of the State where the bank is located to do business there, and the activity remains subject to the Comptroller's rules. The bank may solicit and sell insurance, collect premiums and receive agreed fees or commissions. It may neither assume nor guarantee payment of a premium on a policy issued through its agency, and may not guarantee the truth of an insured's application statements. Agency authority supplies no underwriting power or guarantee of insurer obligations.

**Distinct Florida limits:** The independent-bank exclusion uses **less than 5,000 inhabitants under the last preceding census** and requires a bank that is not a holding-company subsidiary or affiliate; Barnett's affiliation defeats it. The financial-institution definition also excludes an institution with a Federal Reserve Board exemption under Bank Holding Company Act §4(d), and an institution that neither owns more than 10 percent of the capital stock nor exercises effective control of the specified bank, savings-and-loan or chapter 494 entity licensed or authorized in Florida. Neither other exclusion is found applicable. Credit life and credit disability are separately excluded from the restricted agency activities.

**Further limits:** Compatible state regulation survives. No complete invalidation of §626.988, independent agent-licensing disposition, damages, required new hearing, agency proceeding or special mandate date follows. No rule of deference to the Comptroller on preemption or McCarran-Ferguson is adopted. Other state requirements must be considered on their own terms and operation; no categorical immunity or safe harbor is supplied.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Franklin National Bank v. New York: applied for protection of affirmative national-bank authority against state obstruction. The protected-power principle supports Part I-A's prevention/significant-interference rule and the specific §92 result.
- Fidelity Federal Savings & Loan Assn. v. de la Cuesta: applied because a protected federal option can conflict with a state prohibition although federal law does not compel its exercise; it does not make every federal permission preemptive.
- McClellan v. Chipman: preserves state regulation compatible with the actual federal banking power; no banking-field immunity results.
- United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America: §92's survival remains settled; geographic reach and the Comptroller's construction remain outside that prior holding.
- United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe: its function-specific protection of policyholder performance and necessary administration against nonspecific federal legislation remains intact; it does not convert the federal exception into an express-preemption requirement.
- John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank: applied for giving insurance-specific federal directions effect within a broader enactment while retaining compatible state regulation; its separate ERISA asset and fiduciary rules are unchanged.
- NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.: its conditioned brokerage and classification holding remains effective; genuine-insurance geography, distinct state classifications and preemption remain reserved by that decision. No new deference rule follows here.
- Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association: its rejection of automatic overlap preemption is preserved; its physical-compliance-only plurality theory is not treated as the Court's rule.
- Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick: applied to require an operative federal basis for conflict; §92 supplies affirmative authority rather than the regulatory silence insufficient there.
- New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co.: its statute-specific approach remains intact; neither ordinary economic influence nor a universal definition of relation decides this different enactment.

No earlier Supreme Court holding is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 26, 1996, eight Justices adopt the national-bank rule against state prevention of or significant interference with lawfully conferred enumerated and incidental powers, subject to the governing federal scheme and incorporated state restrictions. All nine hold that the specific §92 authorization defeats the categorical affiliation prohibition and that §92 falls within McCarran-Ferguson's insurance-specific federal exception. These cumulative conclusions permit the preemption result even assuming Florida's insurance-regulatory purpose.

The rule reaches significant interference short of a ban without immunizing banks from compatible state regulation. Section 92's conditions and its unresolved association-location and customer-geography questions remain operative. Fabe's state-law protection against nonspecific federal enactments survives. The decision supplies neither a broader licensing disposition nor a new agency-deference holding.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The [Eleventh Circuit opinion, 43 F.3d 631](https://openjurist.org/43/f3d/631), leaves both cross-appeals unresolved. The [1994 text of §92](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title12/html/USCODE-1994-title12-chap2-subchapIV-sec92.htm) supplies the federal conditions. Florida's reproduced operative provisions do not establish proposed continuation, grandfathering or independent-officer exceptions appearing only in the 1974 proposal; the judgment does not dispose of an unestablished exception.

Section 1012(a) subjects the business of insurance and persons engaged in it to state regulatory and tax laws. Section 1012(b)'s first sentence protects state laws enacted to regulate that business, or impose a fee or tax on it, unless the federal enactment specifically relates to that business. Its distinct proviso applies the Sherman, Clayton and Federal Trade Commission Acts after **June 30, 1948**, to the extent the insurance business is not regulated by state law. Section 1013(b) separately preserves Sherman Act application to agreements or acts of boycott, coercion or intimidation. That qualification is not a general exception defeating every state law aimed at coercion. No antitrust claim or expired §1013(a) suspension affects this judgment.

### Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia, No. 94-203

Merits decision, March 27, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia, No. 94-203; 517 U.S. 186. October Term 1995. Argued October 2, 1995; decided March 27, 1996. On direct appeal from the three-judge United States District Court for the Western District of Virginia, 853 F. Supp. 212.

The district court dismissed with prejudice Count III, seeking §5 preclearance of a convention-participation fee, and Count IV, asserting a private §10 poll-tax claim. It denied statutory preliminary relief and expedited discovery without deciding whether this fee was an unlawful poll tax or had discriminatory purpose or effect. The separate constitutional counts and the reported voluntary dismissal of the campaign-related §11 count are outside this appeal.

RPV's June 3–4, 1994 convention selected its Senate nominee. Virginia gives a qualifying party's certified nominee preferential general-ballot access and placement. #### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The identified payment keeps the statutory controversy live

**Controlling proposition:** The end of the convention does not moot this statutory appeal where Morse seeks return of his identified $45 payment on a substantial statutory restoration theory that the judgment has not conclusively eliminated. That live personal controversy permits decision of the coverage and private-action questions without establishing repayment, the proper refund defendant or every plaintiff's prospective standing.

**Authority:** Part I has the eight Associates' direct support, as stated in the topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Morse paid the charge, repaid the campaign reimbursement and expressly requested return of $45 from RPV or Albemarle. The payment, alleged retention and requested restoration identify injury, a causal connection and potential redress. Whether the statutes ultimately support relief is a merits question that cannot simply be assumed against this substantial pleaded theory to make the controversy disappear. Franklin distinguishes relief within an existing action from creation of an action; Meghrig requires statutory authority for monetary restoration without making every restitution request unavailable. The Court awards neither repayment nor general §5 damages. The separate requests concerning future participation require their own live basis. The statutory three-judge judgment and direct-review provisions supply this appeal's channel; no general election-recurrence rule is necessary to the common threshold conclusion.

##### II. This pleaded primary-like convention fee is subject to §5

**Controlling proposition:** On the accepted dismissal-stage allegations, a changed financial prerequisite to participation in this qualifying party's primary-like convention selecting its preferential general-ballot nominee is subject to §5. The combination of that electoral function, the pleaded participation practice and Virginia's statutory nomination relationship supplies coverage; the convention label creates no categorical exemption.

**Authority:** Part II has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They share this application, not every broader delegation or party-office proposition in the concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5 protects against unreviewed changes in voting qualifications and prerequisites. Section 14(c)(1) includes action necessary to make a vote effective in primary, special and general elections, including the stated public- and party-office activities. Allen's functional enforcement approach and Smith and Terry's treatment of nomination devices prevent an exclusion merely because an electoral step takes a party form.

Here the pleaded convention closely resembles a primary: otherwise qualified voters professing party loyalty could participate subject to payment, and the selected nominee receives Virginia's statutory ballot advantages. The fee directly changes access to choosing that nominee. Presley distinguishes such voting participation from unrelated reallocations of internal governing authority. This combination is sufficient without declaring every party a covered government or holding that ballot preference alone resolves every constitutional state-action question.

The application satisfies §51.7's requirements of a change affecting voting, a public electoral function and explicit or implicit authority from a covered jurisdiction. Its primary-election examples are not an exhaustive exemption for convention substitutes; its exclusions for recruitment, campaigns and platform drafting remain effective. An appropriate party official may submit the change under §51.23(b).

The fee-submission duty is sufficiently removed from compelled messages, platforms and expressive composition to fall within the Act here. Hurley protects material expressive choice; it does not make this state-integrated voting prerequisite wholly private. The exact constitutional boundaries for other convention rules remain open. The Court neither requires a party message or candidate nor adjudicates every possible prior-approval burden. It rejects the categorical dismissal while preserving those boundaries.

**Record and scope limits:** The written delegate-selection, slating and instruction rules and the affidavit describing earlier fees remain for their proper treatment; the complaint's contrary allegations are not final findings. The controlling application does not establish an unprecleared switch from primary to convention. Different Senate seats, the unheld 1990 primary and disputed preservation prevent treating that additional theory as already proved or decided. No racial purpose, effect or substantive illegality is required merely to obtain this coverage determination or is found by it.

##### III. Congress intended the private §10 action asserted here

**Controlling proposition:** Affected voters may pursue the asserted private action under §10 to enforce its voting protection against an alleged unlawful poll tax or qualifying substitute. Dismissal solely because §10 expressly directs the Attorney General to sue is erroneous. Recognition of this action does not decide that the convention charge is a prohibited poll tax, establish a refund remedy or create a general implied-action rule.

**Authority:** Part III has the same six Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Congressional intent must be determined for this provision within this Act. Section 10(a) supplies findings and a constitutional declaration concerning voting poll taxes; subsection (b) authorizes and directs the Attorney General to bring necessary declaratory and injunctive actions, and subsection (c) provides the specified judicial channel. The absence of an express private-plaintiff clause is material but does not end the inquiry.

Allen's explanation of private enforcement under §5 applies with similar force to this particular voting protection. Section 12 identifies rights secured by §10 and separately addresses official preventive enforcement and jurisdiction for persons asserting statutory rights. Congress's 1975 treatment retained and amended §10 while recognizing private voting enforcement, including §3's aggrieved-person language and §14(e)'s fee provision. Together with the Act's established enforcement setting, these features support complementary private enforcement. Neither a jurisdiction provision nor a fees provision alone creates the action.

The Attorney General's express route therefore is not exclusive merely because it is explicit. Suter requires provision-specific intent, which this VRA design supplies; remedial usefulness alone would not suffice. Central Bank's respect for established enforcement is a limited analogy, not a universal instruction to imply actions. Allen did not itself decide §10, and the Court does not substitute an unpresented §1983 theory. Varity's express equitable grant and Meghrig's distinct remedial limitations remain separate.

**Merits and remedy limits:** RPV may maintain its argument that the charge is not a poll tax or substitute, but unresolved predicates on this dismissal record do not establish an alternative ground for affirmance. No refund, proper repayment defendant, racial discrimination or particular monetary entitlement is found. The effects of lawful §5 proceedings on further §10 relief remain for appropriate treatment rather than an automatic bar to the action.

##### IV. Coverage, preclearance merits and relief remain separate

**Controlling proposition:** On remand the local court must enforce §5's submission requirement for a legally relevant, unprecleared fee change from the operative baseline, leaving preclearance purpose and effect to the designated D.D.C./Attorney General process. The private §10 claim proceeds under its statutory requirements and remaining defenses; neither reversal itself supplies substantive invalidity or a monetary award.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same six Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5 requires identification of the legally operative baseline; it does not make the complaint's no-1964-fee allegation conclusive or declare every fee increase retrogressive. Evidence of an earlier fee does not itself preclear a later change. Shaw preserves the designated preclearance channel, and Morris restricts review of federal preclearance determinations, so the local court cannot replace their purpose-and-effect process. Section 10's separate action likewise requires adjudication of the fee's character and lawful relief. Expiration of temporary convention requests does not revive those requests on reversal, while the substantial payment controversy establishes no entitlement to prevail. The remand must address the remaining questions under their own sources rather than convert coverage into permanent invalidation or a refund.

**Exact statutory conditions:** Virginia's applicable baseline is **November 1, 1964**; the Act's other coverage dates do not replace it. Section 5 permits the prescribed D.D.C. declaration that the change lacks prohibited purpose and effect, or Attorney General submission followed by sixty-day nonobjection. Good cause permits expedited affirmative nonobjection within that period, with reserved reexamination upon additional information during the remaining sixty days. Neither nonobjection nor a declaratory judgment bars a later enforcement challenge. Coverage and approval are distinct from retrogression and substantive illegality.

Section 10(b)'s voting-poll-tax language separately includes a substitute enacted after November 1, 1964; the statute's findings, constitutional declaration, official-enforcement direction and judicial procedures retain their distinct work. Former subsection (d)'s late-payment provision was repealed in 1975 and supplies no current exception. No unpresented §2 cause, §1983 action, classwide repayment, general damages rule or attorney-fee amount is decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring, joined throughout by Ginsburg.** They join the Court's limited application and both reversals but also adopt a broader functional explanation. Virginia recognizes the qualifying party's choice and certification as the means of obtaining preferential general-ballot access. That authority concerns an official stage of electoral choice; a monetary prerequisite to exercising it affects the effectiveness of voting under §§5 and 14. Allen, Sheffield and the White Primary Cases support coverage through delegated electoral authority, including the party-office/delegate route, without requiring that the nominee be virtually certain to win. The regulation's primary example is not exhaustive, and the statute itself supplies authority without administrative deference creating a missing power.

Their rule still requires an electoral function and a change affecting voting; it does not cover all party activity. They reject the asserted association obstacle to submission of this fee without approving every hypothetical application. Hurley preserves actual expressive choice, while the fee duty compels neither a message nor a candidate. They retain the pleading-versus-proof and nomination-method reservations and adopt the Court's Act-specific §10 ground without a poll-tax or refund finding.

They additionally conclude that the recurring fee practice, the Party's continued defense without disavowal and the unreturned payment preserve this controversy, including as capable of repetition yet evading review. That case-specific ground does not establish that every election dispute remains live or that every prospective request here is available. Their broader statutory and recurrence explanations are noncontrolling.

**Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia throughout and Kennedy only in Part II.** He joins the Court's payment-based threshold discussion but would affirm both statutory dismissals. Part I first reads State or political subdivision as the covered governments and their organs, rather than every association whose conduct might count as constitutional state action. Sheffield's governmental-body holding does not erase that distinction; Congress used broader person/under-color-of-law language elsewhere. The regulation cannot extend the statute, and a litigant's legal concession about it cannot determine the meaning of the presented coverage question.

Alternatively, he finds Virginia's recognition, ballot benefits and limited requirements insufficient to establish the governmental delegation or effective substitution for an election that he regards as necessary. Smith and Terry retain their constitutional force, but do not make this private nomination a state election on these facts. He independently reads §14(c)(1)'s primary, special and general elections as excluding conventions: party-office and effective-vote language does not erase that list, and §2's later amendment did not amend these provisions. His actual Holder ballot-access position remains noncontrolling and is not substituted for the Court's §2 law.

His association/avoidance ground also incorporates Scalia's overbreadth, uncertainty, prior-restraint and avoidance objections. Party funding, membership and organization are substantial choices; denial of preclearance could require admission of those unwilling or unable to pay. The asserted financing justification remains advocacy. These grounds support his statutory construction, not a facial decree invalidating §5 or a final disposition of the separate constitutional counts. An appropriate challenge to a State's own discriminatory electoral practice remains distinct. The disputed additional rationale attributed to Williams supplies no independent ground.

Part II denies a private §10 action. In his view, the provision's declaration and express direction to the Attorney General lack the private statutory command found in Allen's §5 setting. General private-enforcement references in §§3, 12 and 14 and the 1975 amendment do not establish this additional action. Congressional intent, not usefulness, controls. Central Bank preserves its confined existing securities action; his contrary position there supplies no contrary Court holding. He would affirm without deciding whether the charge is an unlawful poll tax, awarding money or denying all constitutional voting remedies.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined throughout by Thomas.** He joins Part I of the Court opinion and Thomas's entire separate opinion. His additional explanation treats candidate selection as central expressive association. Preclearance requires prior federal permission even for lawful changes, imposes the burden of establishing nondiscriminatory purpose and effect, and subjects convention timing and choices to governmental control. That is the concrete prior-restraint concern; it cannot be dismissed as mere regulation of customer identity under Hurley.

He also objects to overbreadth and chilling uncertainty. Even if this isolated fee could constitutionally require submission, the interpretation's reach across protected party activity must be addressed. Leaving the covered class indefinite can chill current conduct; reserving every difficult application for later supplies neither the limiting construction nor the overbreadth treatment he considers necessary. The substantial constitutional doubt favors the statute's express reference to States and political subdivisions rather than imposing this restraint on parties.

Congress could address some discriminatory practices through other provisions without extending §5 to every party procedure. References to party office elsewhere do not prove the distinct preclearance duty. Those statutory and avoidance grounds, together with Thomas's §10 analysis, support affirmance. The opinion declares neither facial invalidity of the Act or regulation nor immunity from constitutional law, and decides no fee merits or refund.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Part I of the Court opinion and only Part II of Thomas's separate opinion. His independent §5 ground distinguishes a statutory covered government from every person whose conduct may be attributed to the State under constitutional doctrine. Congress named governments here while using broader private-person language elsewhere; ordinary ballot recognition and preserved party autonomy do not impose the asserted direct submission duty on the Party.

A State's own change making party practices legally operative or facilitating discriminatory nomination can remain subject to §5 as a state practice. That governmental-responsibility inquiry differs from requiring the Party to submit every procedure because its nominee receives ballot benefits. He does not decide whether these appellants would prevail under constitutional state-action principles, the full reach of Congress's Reconstruction Amendment powers or Thomas's separate convention-definition theory. Smith and Terry remain available for the constitutional claims in their existing posture.

He also identifies serious costs in placing party procedure under prior control of a politically accountable federal officer, without alleging misconduct. That concern would support avoidance if ambiguity existed; his statutory entity reading is an independent ground. His §10 objection is the specific congressional-intent analysis in Thomas's Part II, consistent with distinguishing development within an established action from creation of a new one. He would affirm both statutory dismissals without deciding fee legality or monetary relief.

### Rutledge v. United States, No. 94-8769

Merits decision, March 27, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**Rutledge v. United States**, No. 94-8769; 517 U.S. 292. October Term 1995. Argued November 27, 1995; decided March 27, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 40 F.3d 879.

The question is whether the §846 narcotics conspiracy is a lesser included offense of §848 continuing criminal enterprise and whether separate convictions and concurrent life sentences may stand. Both counts concern the same agreement. The District Court entered six convictions and imposed a $300 special assessment. Review concerns the CCE and conspiracy judgments, Counts 1 and 2, and their consequences.

#### Judgment

| Sustaining both Counts 1 and 2 judgments | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | One conviction, its entire corresponding sentence and its $50 assessment must be removed. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The CCE concert requirement includes agreement

**Holding:** Section 848(c)'s requirement of action in concert with five or more other persons requires mutual agreement in a common criminal plan or enterprise. In the narcotics setting presented, §846 conspiracy is therefore a lesser included offense of CCE; the same agreement supports both challenged counts.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the application only at the level of these offenses as charged and tried, where the CCE verdict necessarily establishes every conspiracy element.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court compares statutory elements, not the mere overlap of evidence. Shabani requires a knowing narcotics agreement proved beyond a reasonable doubt, without an additional overt act. CCE requires the agreement and adds the continuing series, five-other-person, managerial and substantial-income-or-resource requirements. The concert language requires mutual commitment to the unlawful plan. An organizer cannot satisfy it merely by using people wholly unaware of that plan; individual agreements with the necessary participants can supply the requisite conspiracy without a universally prescribed organizational form.

Jeffers supplied the textual reasoning but assumed the included-offense conclusion. The Court now resolves it. The Government's unwitting-participant hypothetical does not give §846 an element absent from the statutory concert requirement. Here the same agreement supplied Count 2 and the concert element of Count 1. This construction does not place a subchapter-II-only enterprise within §846's distinct subchapter-I coverage. A distinct agreement, different offense or separate substantive predicate requires its own analysis; successive-prosecution questions remain separate.

##### II. Congress did not authorize both judgments for this agreement

**Holding:** Congress did not authorize cumulative convictions and punishment under §§846 and 848 for the included agreement at issue. Concurrent imprisonment does not cure the unauthorized second conviction.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela additionally joins Ball's second-conviction and concurrency propositions and the conclusion that these two judgments cannot remain, on his charged-and-tried ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Under Hunter, the single-prosecution inquiry concerns the punishment Congress authorized. Blockburger's elements comparison and Whalen's presumption weigh against cumulative punishment for an included offense. Jeffers's rejection of cumulative punishment and Garrett's distinction between conspiracy and substantive predicates confirm the absence of authorization here. Separate section numbers, CCE's additional elements and severe punishment, and the usefulness of a backup conviction do not overcome that conclusion.

Jeffers's retention of judgments does not decide this question otherwise. Only the later CCE judgment was under review; the plurality relied on the defendant's separate-trial election, while Justice White treated the offenses differently. Silence about an unreviewed judgment creates no controlling authorization for two present convictions.

Ball establishes that a second conviction remains a sanction despite concurrent imprisonment. Potential collateral consequences cannot be erased by combining labels, and the actual assessment supplies a concrete additional penalty. Harris and Bishop/Stokes apply §924(c)'s express additional-and-consecutive direction to their different statutory pairing; §§846 and 848 contain no corresponding authorization here. Garrett's separate substantive-predicate rule and Felix's distinction between conspiracy and its substantive objects remain intact. This holding requires no constitutional punishment rule independent of congressional authorization.

##### III. The second assessment is a present consequence of the challenged judgment

**Holding:** The mandatory $50 assessment attached to the second felony conviction is actual additional punishment and may be considered in resolving the preserved challenge to the dual judgments. Vacatur must remove the selected conviction's assessment.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the actual-sanction and assessment-removal propositions, without joining the separate presentation ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3013 imposes a $50 assessment for each individual felony conviction. Unlike concurrent prison time, these separate monetary sanctions accumulate. Considering that automatic consequence answers the Government's assertion that the second judgment imposes no additional punishment; it does not introduce an unrelated monetary claim. The Government's objection that the assessment was not separately challenged below therefore does not require ignoring it for this purpose. The Court adopts no general exception to preservation rules.

Ball also forecloses treating the second conviction as nonexistent. No conclusive prediction about parole, future recidivist treatment, impeachment or marginal stigma is necessary given the actual assessment. Removing one $50 assessment reduces the existing six-count total from $300 to $250. The Court makes no finding concerning payment, collection, a refund amount or an expired obligation, and decides no freestanding refund claim or assessment on another count.

##### IV. One conviction must be vacated, with the initial choice left to the District Court

**Holding:** The District Court must vacate either Count 1 or Count 2, its entire corresponding life sentence and its $50 assessment, choosing initially within lawful discretion. Retaining both convictions while vacating only one sentence, staying execution or applying a combined label is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the requirement to remove one judgment, sentence and assessment without reopening Counts 3–6; he dissents from permission to select CCE for vacatur.

**Controlling explanation:** Ball requires removal of an unauthorized conviction and leaves selection to the sentencing court. Rutledge's own requested relief permits either count; the Government's narrower preference to preserve CCE does not bind him or require a conspiracy-only mandate. The Court therefore corrects the dual-judgment error while preserving the District Court's lawful initial selection.

A second conviction does not become permissible through a different label or a stay of its execution. The selected judgment, its sentence and assessment must be vacated. The surviving challenged conviction and Counts 3–6 remain undisturbed, including their existing sentences and assessments and Count 3's independent life term. The order does not erase the fact that the jury returned the verdicts, require a prospective stay-of-entry system, reopen unpresented objections or direct general resentencing. It orders neither release nor a new trial.

##### V. Qualified appellate correction answers the asserted need for a backup conviction

**Holding:** An appellate court may, in an appropriate case, direct entry of judgment on a valid lesser included offense when reversal rests on a defect affecting only the greater offense. That existing power does not require two present convictions and does not make later reinstatement automatic.

**Authority:** Part V, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government's backup concern does not justify retaining an unauthorized second judgment now. Section 2106 supplies corrective appellate authority. Tinder illustrates that authority through correction of a sentence after felony treatment failed, while retaining the underlying conviction; it did not decide revival of a vacated conspiracy conviction. Ward provides the closer example: the CCE conviction failed for insufficient five-person supervisory proof, the conspiracy conviction had been vacated, and the defendant conceded reinstatement before remand. That concession is part of the example, not a rule that consent is always required or irrelevant.

A valid conspiracy verdict may thus support appropriate lesser-offense relief after a CCE-specific failure. But a ground invalidating the agreement or otherwise tainting that verdict cannot be ignored. The precise limits, procedural vehicle and relief in a future contested application remain open. The Court orders no restoration now, adopts no automatic-revival or general finality exception, and decides no entitlement to retrial, compelled lesser judgment or harmlessness of a future error. Present vacatur for duplication is not an evidentiary acquittal. An earlier separate conspiracy prosecution, previously unknown CCE facts and a noncoterminous enterprise present questions this case does not decide.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299:** Applies the statutory-elements inquiry to the included agreement; does not replace it with an evidentiary same-conduct test.
- **Whalen v. United States, 445 U.S. 684, and Missouri v. Hunter, 459 U.S. 359:** Apply the presumption against unauthorized cumulation and Congress's authority over punishment imposed in one prosecution. Their distinct roles remain intact.
- **Jeffers v. United States, 432 U.S. 137:** Resolves its assumed included-offense premise and applies its no-cumulative-punishment conclusion; its procedural disposition supplies no contrary dual-judgment holding.
- **Ball v. United States, 470 U.S. 856:** Applies its recognition of a second conviction's consequences and its requirement to vacate one conviction, with initial selection left to the sentencing court. No new multiple-charging prohibition is adopted.
- **Garrett v. United States, 471 U.S. 773:** Preserves its substantive-predicate rule and distinguishes the shared conspiracy agreement; its successive-prosecution questions are not resolved here.
- **United States v. Shabani, 513 U.S. 10:** Applies its knowing-agreement requirement and absence of an overt-act element under §846.
- **United States v. Harris and United States v. Bishop and Stokes:** Preserve their statute-specific express-authorization holdings. Their §924(c) direction does not authorize this statutory pairing.
- **United States v. Felix, 503 U.S. 378, and United States v. Dixon, 509 U.S. 688:** Preserve the agreement/substantive-offense distinction and the retained Grady rule within successive-prosecution law. This single-prosecution disposition changes neither.
- **Witte v. United States, 515 U.S. 389:** Distinguishes authorized relevant-conduct sentencing from entry of an additional conviction; leaves its separate conditional sentence-coordination rule unchanged.
- **Tinder v. United States, 345 U.S. 565:** Uses its bounded §2106 corrective authority without treating its sentence correction as a prior decision reinstating a vacated conspiracy conviction.

#### Law After Decision

The statutory concert requirement now receives a definite agreement construction, and the included §846 narcotics agreement cannot support both challenged judgments absent congressional authorization. Existing single-prosecution authorization and second-conviction principles remain governing; a second $50 assessment is a concrete sanction despite concurrent prison terms. One conviction, its sentence and assessment must be removed, with lawful initial count selection left to the District Court.

Qualified appellate entry of a valid lesser conviction may answer a later greater-only reversal; it is not automatic restoration. The exact future procedure, defects affecting the lesser verdict, distinct agreements, subchapter-II-only offenses, separate substantive predicates and successive prosecutions retain the limits stated above. The eight-Justice general holdings have direct support; the additional narrower joins do not reduce their scope.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part. He begins with Blockburger's element comparison and the offenses as charged and tried. Here the CCE verdict necessarily incorporated the same agreement and concerted conduct supporting conspiracy, so every element of the §846 count was necessarily established. His conclusion is confined to that elements-and-trial structure; it does not bar convictions for distinct conspiracies or offenses requiring an element not necessarily included in the CCE count.

Ball makes the second judgment consequential even though imprisonment runs concurrently. The separate assessment, stigma and other collateral effects prevent treating the included conviction as an empty entry. On that basis, the Double Jeopardy Clause requires removal of the included judgment. His partial joins adopt the corresponding case-specific application, second-conviction consequences and assessment correction; they do not adopt the Court's broader statutory construction, fuller congressional-intent analysis, preservation ruling or future appellate-entry proposition.

His remedial disagreement is concrete. Because conspiracy is the included offense established by this CCE verdict, he would direct vacatur of Count 2 and its special assessment and leave the CCE conviction and sentence undisturbed. He therefore agrees that the dual-conviction ruling must be reversed but dissents from allowing the District Court to vacate CCE instead. No other Justice joins his separate writing.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The judgment below is 40 F.3d 879; the indictment and criminal judgment appear at Joint Appendix 2–12. Section 3013(a)(2)(A) prescribes the $50 assessment. Tinder, 345 U.S. 565, 570, concerns sentence correction while retaining the conviction; United States v. Ward, 37 F.3d 243, 251, records the CCE proof defect and the defendant's reinstatement concession. Neither supports automatic restoration after every later reversal.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for the District Court to choose and vacate one of Counts 1 and 2, its corresponding sentence and its $50 assessment. The other challenged conviction and Counts 3–6 remain undisturbed. No later challenge or restoration is ordered. The precise procedure and relief in any future contested lesser-offense application remain open, subject to the limits stated in Part V.

### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, No. 94-12

Merits decision, March 27, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, No. 94-12; 517 U.S. 44. October Term 1995. Argued October 11, 1995; decided March 27, 1996. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing its January 18, 1994 immunity judgment, 11 F.3d 1016.

The Tribe sued Florida and Governor Lawton Chiles in his official capacity under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. The district court denied immunity dismissal on June 18, 1992; the court of appeals reversed and directed dismissal. The questions concern congressional authorization of a prospective direct-State action under the Indian Commerce Clause and a prospective officer action under Ex parte Young. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The statutory judicial decision is not categorically advisory

**Controlling proposition:** Adjudicating IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function where the judgment conclusively determines the parties' legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. The prospect of later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make the court's decision merely advisory.

**Authority:** Part I has seven direct supporters: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens's serious reservation is not an eighth join; Stone-Zsela supplies no independent join.

**Controlling explanation:** A court decides an existing dispute over a federal duty and, upon the requisite finding, changes the parties' legal obligations and access to the next statutory stage. That supplies effective partial relief even though neither the desired compact nor every desired game is assured. The decision's legal operation matters; the answer does not rest solely on whether the Secretary can edit individual findings.

Aetna and Nashville reject a universal requirement that judicial relief take the form of an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial adjudication in aid of administration, while its actual enforceable-order setting prevents treating it as an answer to every intermediate proceeding. Ferreira and the relevant Gordon and Hayburn materials require attention to whether the purported judgment has legal effect or remains subject to executive revision. IGRA's binding determination and prescribed consequences satisfy that concern here.

Wisconsin's partial-redress reasoning offers a limited analogy, not a prior adjudication of this judicial-function question. Morgan Stanley/Plaut's protection of completed judgments likewise supplies no universal answer. The Court neither determines every later preclusion question nor manufactures coercive remedies or a new cause of action to make the statutory decision judicial.

##### II. IGRA permits this connected officer action within its own limits

**Controlling proposition:** Ex parte Young permits a prospective official-capacity action against Governor Chiles to enforce an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiating duty, subject to the statute's full sequence and remedial limitations. IGRA's State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case.

**Authority:** Part II has five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The common rule is specific to IGRA; it does not adopt the three Justices' additional general clear-indication theory.

**Controlling explanation:** Young provides the established route against a responsible officer for an ongoing federal violation. The Governor's representatives participated in negotiations, supplying the required connection to the alleged failure. Rejecting categorical dismissal does not require proof that he alone can bind Florida to every compact term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over those terms.

Congress imposed a federal negotiating duty and prescribed how its enforcement progresses. References to the State do not, in this arrangement, exclude the official through whom its obligations are performed. The scheme limits the officer action rather than authorizing a free-standing injunction with greater remedies. This construction answers the objection that an officer could evade Congress's chosen restraints: both sixty-day periods, mediation, selection, consent and Secretarial conditions remain effective. It does not treat remedial detail as irrelevant or establish that every statutory right carries officer relief.

The State's involvement in negotiations establishes neither waiver nor unlimited compacting authority. Incorporation of state gaming law into the federal obligation does not itself convert the claim into a Pennhurst demand to enforce state law. Hafer's individual-capacity damages rule supplies no basis for this action, and Edelman's retrospective-relief boundary remains.

The actual request for a compact and declaration does not establish entitlement to either. A court cannot prescribe its preferred compact, enlarge the good-faith duty, bypass mediation or recast accrued compensation as an injunction. The statutory progression, rather than an added coercive order compelling agreement, governs failure to reach agreement. The categorical officer dismissal must therefore be reversed while the remaining merits and lawful relief receive their proper treatment.

##### III. Removing the officer bar does not establish the predicates for compacting relief

**Controlling proposition:** The remand permits only relief authorized by IGRA upon the actual statutory predicates. It neither resolves good faith or gaming scope nor disturbs the distinct merits judgment and appeal. The following conditions govern any relief through the recognized officer route.

**Authority:** Part III has the same five direct supporters. The four opposing the route preserve the same enacted conditions in their dissent; recognizing those conditions does not imply their agreement to reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** A valid duty, an available enforcement defendant and entitlement to a particular remedy are different questions. IGRA makes adverse findings, successive waiting periods and specified mediation and administrative steps do separate work. Removing immunity cannot substitute for any of them. Nor does the actual prayer enlarge the relief Congress supplied. The September 1993 merits judgment and its stayed appeal must receive their own lawful disposition; this immunity mandate decides neither. The Court accordingly preserves the statutory progression and independent gaming conditions below without ordering that progression to begin as though bad faith were already established.

**Complete statutory conditions and limits:**

- Under 25 U.S.C. §2710(d)(3)(A), a qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the relevant Indian lands triggers the State's duty to negotiate in good faith, not a duty to accept preferred terms. A failure-to-negotiate suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary's enforcement action under (A)(iii) remain distinct.
- Under (B)(ii), tribal evidence that no compact exists and that the State failed to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's burden to prove good faith. Nonagreement alone does not establish bad faith. An actual adverse finding triggers (B)(iii)'s order to the State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty-day period. The court **may** consider public interest, public safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming; it **shall** consider a demand for direct taxation of the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith.
- If no agreement follows during that period, (B)(iv) requires each side's last-best proposal to a court-appointed mediator. The mediator selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law, and the court's findings and order. Submission to both parties under (B)(v) begins a **second sixty-day period** for State consent. Consent makes the proposal an agreed compact under (B)(vi).
- Nonconsent during that second period triggers notice to the Secretary under (B)(vii). The Secretary **shall** prescribe procedures, consulting the Tribe and acting consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The express findings/order selection criterion appears in (iv), not verbatim in (vii). The Secretary lacks unrestricted discretion to withhold all action after the enacted predicates occur.
- State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. The government's qualified prediction supplies no textual default. The adopted construction adds neither contempt nor an order compelling agreement merely because agreement or participation during the ordered period fails; statutory progression is the consequence. No rule concerning every separate obstruction or disobedience sanction is decided.
- Section (d)(4) grants no new state taxing power while preserving regulatory-cost assessments agreed under (d)(3)(C)(iii). That exception is distinct from the mandatory treatment of direct-tax demands as bad-faith evidence. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.
- Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain separate. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA violations, other federal law **not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands**, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction produces approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA; Federal Register publication remains necessary.
- Section 2721's severability provision preserves qualifying remaining law but does not satisfy a finding, waiting period, mediation, selection or nonconsent condition and creates no alternative program. The distinct asserted Secretary substitute remedy remains unadjudicated. The Court neither endorses an automatic bypass nor reverses the lower court's separate substitute-remedy determination. Immunity dismissal alone satisfies none of the enacted predicates.

##### IV. Direct-State intent is agreed upon, but State amenability is unresolved

**Majority-supported statutory proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes a direct action naming the State. Its specified State-party action, State evidentiary burden, order to State and Tribe and successive State-directed steps satisfy the clear-statement requirement. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered references to States alone would not suffice.

**Authority and force:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and the four dissenters actually adopt this statutory construction across their writings. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept it only if their alternative State route must be reached. The six-position agreement establishes only the exact statutory-authorization proposition stated above; it creates no common State-amenability opinion. Statutory intent and constitutional authority remain separate: no controlling holding sustains or rejects Florida's amenability, and no majority opinion decides the constitutional question. It also supplies no sequencing rule requiring abrogation to be decided before officer relief.

**Common judgment, without common State rationale:** Five support setting aside the immunity-dismissal direction and remand. Two support affirmative direct-State relief; three support nonmerits vacatur; four oppose either change. Avoidance is not a logical subset of the two Justices' affirmative constitutional grounds. No narrower controlling constitutional rationale can be extracted by treating their different reasons as interchangeable.

**Other limits:** No express waiver, automatic reciprocal tribal/state consent, constructive waiver by negotiating, or delegation of the United States' immunity exemption establishes this suit; the United States is an amicus. The Court does not reach the distinct Tenth Amendment defense first raised on appeal and not decided below. The question-scope limitation is prudential: X-Citement Video permits an appropriate respondent defense without requiring resolution of this distinct constitutional issue. No universal forfeiture rule, jurisdictional incapacity or independent mediator-appointments invalidation is announced.

- **Ex parte Young:** Applied to this connected officer and ongoing federal statutory duty. The recognized route carries IGRA's limits and establishes no universal rule that detailed statutes preserve officer equity.
- **Edelman:** Its prospective/retrospective boundary remains. An injunction cannot be used to obtain accrued treasury compensation; prospective compliance costs alone do not decide that classification.
- **Pennhurst:** Its restriction on federal orders enforcing state law remains. Incorporation of state gaming law as part of the federal IGRA obligation does not itself turn this action into a state-law enforcement claim.
- **Union Gas:** Not overruled. Its actual damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain precedent within their decided scope. The fractured rationale neither erases that conclusion nor makes every plurality sentence binding. Four Justices would overrule the constitutional conclusion; their dissent changes no law. Three unused constitutional alternatives cannot be aggregated with two affirmative State votes to create a present holding.
- **Hans:** Not overruled. The separate common-law accounts of its nontextual immunity do not become a Court holding, and the State set-aside does not abolish its rule.
- **Fitzpatrick:** Its distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unaffected; the decision establishes no new Reconstruction ground.
- **Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227:** Applied for the judicial character of specific, conclusive declaratory relief in an actual dispute over present rights. The declaratory statute is procedural; it creates neither a constitutional controversy nor an otherwise missing substantive action. The absence of a damages or injunction award is not alone disqualifying.
- **Nashville, Chattanooga & St. Louis Railway v. Wallace, 288 U.S. 249:** Applied for the proposition that an adverse, conclusive declaratory adjudication can be judicial without execution or coercive process. Its actual state-tax controversy does not establish that a declaratory label alone supplies legal effect or an IGRA remedy.
- **ICC v. Brimson, 154 U.S. 447:** Applied as support for judicial adjudication assisting administration, while preserving its ordinary adversarial proceeding, conclusive judgment and judicially enforceable order. Contempt there followed disobedience of the court's order, not merely the agency's request. It neither establishes an IGRA contempt remedy nor alone answers whether a differently structured intermediate judgment is judicial.
- **United States v. Ferreira, 54 U.S. 40:** Distinguished. The judge acted as a claims commissioner in a process lacking ordinary suit, parties, process and court record; no debt arose without Treasury approval, and the Secretary could revise the award. IGRA's conclusive adjudication and prescribed legal consequences differ. Ferreira's reserved appointments question supplies no mediator ruling; the appended Yale Todd account is a later inferential report, not a recorded reasoned holding.
- **Gordon v. United States, 69 U.S. 561, and the later appendix at 117 U.S. 697:** The actual disposition dismissed for want of appellate jurisdiction without a contemporaneous reasoned opinion. Taney's prepared paper, considered after his death and later printed, was not a delivered majority opinion; the contemplated Court opinion was never prepared. Its execution language informs the historical concern about legal effect contingent on another branch, but is not an unqualified holding requiring execution in every judicial case.
- **Hayburn's Case, 2 U.S. 409:** The reported Supreme Court proceeding produced no merits decision. The circuit judges' communications printed in the note explain objections to executive suspension and legislative revision of purported judicial decisions. They remain relevant historical materials, not a reasoned Supreme Court holding deciding IGRA's constrained later process.
- **Wisconsin v. City of New York:** Its concrete partial-redress holding supplies a limited analogy. It did not decide whether IGRA assigns a judicial function, eliminate Stevens's doubt or guarantee the claimant's preferred ultimate result.
- **Morgan Stanley/Plaut:** Its protection of the completed, unappealed private securities judgment remains confined to that actual holding. It supplies no universal answer to judicial character, executive implementation or finality under this different statute.
- **Puerto Rico Aqueduct:** Applied to the immediate appeal of the conclusive immunity denial. Appealability resolves neither immunity nor waiver, abrogation or the officer route's merits.
- **Hess:** Its entity-specific arm-of-State analysis does not resolve Florida's own immunity or congressional abrogation.
- **Hafer:** Its personal-capacity damages holding remains separate from the official-capacity prospective action recognized here.
- **New York v. United States:** Its compulsory regulation-or-ownership rule and distinct permitted inducements remain effective. It does not resolve the separate Tenth Amendment defense left unadjudicated here.
- **Voting Rights Coalition:** Its election-power grounds and United States enforcement posture supply no general Indian Commerce abrogation or state-command rule.
- **X-Citement Video:** Its allowance for an appropriate prevailing-respondent defense is preserved without compelling adjudication of this distinct Tenth Amendment issue. The Court's question-scope limit remains prudential.

#### Law After Decision

From March 27, 1996, the controlling rules reject the categorical advisory-function objection to this legally effective IGRA adjudication and permit the connected prospective officer action within the complete statutory sequence. No general rule that every detailed scheme preserves or excludes Young is adopted. Six actual writers agree that IGRA clearly authorizes a State action, but the judgment creates no controlling rule on Congress's power to subject Florida to that action.

The State-removal judgment binds the parties without a controlling State-amenability rationale. Union Gas remains precedent; the decision neither adopts a general Article I abrogation power nor establishes the dissent's general Article I prohibition. Statutory duty, cause, defendant, constitutional power, merits and remedy remain separate. The judgment supplies no direct-State jurisdiction by default, Secretary bypass, new damages action or gaming authorization.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the officer holding and statutory limits and supports removal of Florida's dismissal direction, but would affirmatively sustain Congress's clearly enacted prospective direct-State action. Indian Commerce authority, the ongoing federal duty and unmistakable authorization support that route. Prospective compliance costs do not become accrued treasury compensation merely because payment is needed. Congress's express enforcement channel and the Tribe's continuing-duty claim warrant retaining a forum. Young independently reaches the connected officer, with the same statutory restraints. Neither route permits a preferred compact, bypassed mediation, enlarged duty or disguised retrospective damages; lawful relief should avoid double or inconsistent commands.

He expressly refuses to treat officer availability as sufficient reason to stop short of sustaining the State action. He retains Union Gas's actual damages judgment without adopting every broader abrogation rationale. He reserves materially new compensatory, punitive or restitutionary liability, compulsory state-court actions and different statutory schemes, while recognizing that already-decided Union Gas questions remain settled within its scope. He supplies no new waiver or Reconstruction ground.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts II–III and would sustain both routes. Outside the Eleventh Amendment's actual textual restriction, Hans immunity is judicial doctrine subject to displacement by valid congressional legislation. IGRA supplies the affirmative direction absent from Hans; Congress cannot amend the Constitution or exceed Article III. His rationale is not confined to prospective Indian Commerce litigation and does not require overruling Hans's actual result. He retains Union Gas's constitutional conclusion and leaves the textual issue concerning out-of-state or foreign-citizen plaintiffs open; the Tribe is not treated as such a plaintiff. His own remedial-intent analysis supports the constrained officer action, and he accepts lawful officer-only resolution as a narrower sufficient alternative without abandoning his affirmative State position.

He nevertheless retains serious doubt whether this intermediate judicial step, particularly if separate executive relief could make it dispensable, is fully judicial under Article III. That is a reservation, not a vote to dismiss independently. Wisconsin's partial-redress holding does not settle this different judicial-function issue. He does not formally join Souter's entire separate theory.

**Souter, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, respecting the State claim in Part IV.** They prefer the established officer route and vacatur of Florida's immunity-dismissal direction without deciding abrogation. Intricate remedies and State wording alone do not clearly exclude established prospective equity; Congress may limit statutory enforcement, but they find no sufficient indication of exclusion here. This is distinct from inventing a personal damages action. They would address whether State adjudication is necessary in light of lawful officer relief while preserving lawful jurisdiction before State merits adjudication or a proper nonmerits resolution. The Tribe might forgo its State claim or adequate officer relief might support denying duplicative relief, but neither fact is found. They prescribe no automatic abandonment, mootness, dismissal or indefinite stay. Their additional sequence is not the Court's mandate.

If Young were rejected and Florida's still-live immunity claim adjudicated, they would recognize the direct-State clear statement and uphold congressional displacement of Hans immunity under valid Article I power. They regard that nontextual immunity as federal common law, retained by stare decisis absent deliberate legislative displacement, rather than constitutional immunity Congress can never alter. Constitutional text, limited prior holdings and legislative control of common law independently support that account; it is not a wholesale adoption of Brennan's Union Gas reasoning. Their view is not confined to prospective relief or commerce, but decides no damages or unrelated statute. Because Young succeeds here, these constitutional views remain conditional alternatives, not actual joins in a State-amenability holding.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join the judicial-character ruling and find unmistakable direct-State intent, but would affirm both dismissals. Their constitutional rule is broad: original Article I powers cannot overcome the constitutional immunity limiting federal judicial power over a nonconsenting State. Prospective relief does not cure the defect; neither Indian/interstate commerce nor proprietary/governmental distinctions justifies retaining Union Gas. They would expressly overrule its constitutional conclusion while leaving its statutory construction and Fitzpatrick's later Fourteenth Amendment authority intact.

They acknowledge Union Gas's five-vote constitutional conclusion rather than declaring it never precedent. In their view its conflict with the constitutional immunity structure, fractured rationale and deficient integration with prior immunity decisions supply the special justification for correction. They reject the opposing distinction between a common-law default and congressional displacement, without assuming zero reliance or proof that clear-statement rules are empirically unworkable. Their premise does not adjudicate every other statute or remedy, grant immunity to every public entity, foreclose United States enforcement or eliminate ordinary Young relief.

Independently, they read IGRA as selecting the State-party action, reciprocal State/Tribe order and limited subsequent progression. An ordinary officer injunction risks judicial coercion Congress withheld, but their objection is not limited to unlimited contempt: even an officer action copying the deadlines changes Congress's selected defendant and enforcement form. The sovereign-compacting arrangement reinforces that inference. They recognize that Congress can authorize constrained officer remedies; they conclude it did not do so here. They establish no universal detailed-scheme bar, no categorical affirmative-duty exception to Young, and no power to rewrite the Act merely because enforcement otherwise has a gap.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The immunity decisions are 11 F.3d 1016 and 801 F. Supp. 655. The separate merits judgment and stayed appeal establish no finding of good faith in this review. The differing filing-date accounts establish no amendment history. The record does not resolve every game-scope issue or the Governor's complete state-law authority to bind Florida; a party's account of a possible administrative substitute is not enacted authority.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for proceedings consistent with the officer holding, shared State set-aside and preserved statutory conditions. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 require their own lawful treatment; this decision supplies neither reversal of that judgment nor a finding of bad faith. The State claim remains unterminated by the immunity disposition, with no majority determination of State amenability and no mandatory avoidance sequence imposed by the Court.

No immediate compacting order, mediator appointment, default proposal, Secretarial prescription, damages, game-scope declaration or license issues from this decision. The distinct asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting authority, Tenth Amendment question and mediator-appointments issue remain unadjudicated. No special mandate date or continuing Supreme Court supervision is ordered.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Delay does not supply the asserted additional dismissal sanction

**Controlling proposition:** A first personal federal habeas petition satisfying the governing jurisdictional and pleading requirements may not be terminated solely for the reported delay and litigation purpose through an additional equitable sanction that dispenses with the conditions of the habeas statutes and Rules. Delay may support dismissal through Rule 9 or another lawful source only when that source's own predicates are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress and the Habeas Rules allocate authority over initial examination, exhaustion, default, repeated applications, prejudicial delay and claims plainly lacking entitlement. Section 2243's direction to dispose of a matter as law and justice require operates within that arrangement; it does not authorize an additional terminal bar whenever a court regards a prisoner's timing or purpose as offensive.

Rule 9(a) specifically addresses delay prejudicing the State's ability to respond. Its reasonable-diligence qualification and Congress's deletion of a proposed five-year presumption would lose their work if generalized delay alone could replace them. The different predicates for repeated or abusive applications likewise cannot be supplied by counting unsuccessful next-friend captions.

The appellate opinion identifies serious conduct, not merely proximity to execution. Those findings retain significance under applicable law. But neither their seriousness nor an appeal to equity establishes a Rule 9 condition, claim insufficiency or another lawful ground to extinguish this petition. The district court's refusal to employ that additional dismissal sanction was not the error the court of appeals supposed.

This conclusion preserves structured judicial judgment and legally authorized control of schedules, stays and sanctions. It excuses no deliberate misconduct, decides no unpresented fraud or inherent-power issue, and eliminates no established exhaustion, default or other claim-specific defense. It does not find that any undisclosed claim warrants relief.

**Statutory qualifications:**

- Pre-AEDPA §2254(a) requires custody violating federal law. Sections (b)–(c) retain exhaustion, including the absence or ineffectiveness of available state corrective process; an available state procedure cannot simply be disregarded. The then-effective factual-presumption provisions remain distinct from any later statutory deference regime.
- Section 2243's initial writ-or-show-cause command has an exception when the application shows nonentitlement. Its law-and-justice direction does not guarantee a writ or evidentiary hearing. Rule 4 requires prompt examination, summary dismissal and notice if lack of entitlement plainly appears from the petition and exhibits, and otherwise an answer or other appropriate judicial action, with service on the respondent and state attorney general. Lawful expedition remains available.
- Rule 9(a) permits dismissal for filing delay that prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless the petitioner shows that reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before the prejudice arose. Losing an execution date or bearing generalized finality costs does not alone establish that response prejudice. The enacted Rule contains **no five-year presumption**; the deleted proposal in older advisory discussion is not law.
- Rule 9(b) separately concerns second or successive petitions: no new or different grounds after a prior merits determination, or new or different grounds whose earlier omission constituted abuse. Prior proceedings must be examined for their actual disposition, not merely counted.
- Section 2244(b)'s then-effective subsequent-application permission follows a federal denial of habeas relief after an evidentiary hearing on a material factual merits issue or a hearing on a legal merits issue. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of withholding does not eliminate every other abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The State must plead abuse clearly and particularly before the prisoner's burden arises. No such subsequent-petition predicate is established here.

##### II. Independent equitable stay discretion survives the dismissal ruling

**Controlling proposition:** First-petition status does not exclude lawful independent consideration of the State's enforcement interest, unjustified delay or proved obstruction in deciding an execution stay. But the existence of that distinct discretion does not sustain this appellate order, whose stated premise was the supposed authority to dismiss through the sanction rejected in Part I.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Gomez permits equitable assessment independently of a habeas procedural bar, even on an assumed open procedural route. Its four earlier petitions are facts of that case, not a minimum number needed before equity may operate. The State therefore presents a genuine stay argument, and its reported conduct findings cannot be dismissed as irrelevant merely because this is Lonchar's first personal petition.

McFarland likewise preserves denial for proved inexcusable disregard of a reasonable statutory opportunity and obstruction, while protecting that opportunity. The seriousness of a supported constitutional claim and execution's irreversibility matter without alone compelling last-minute relief. Neither case licenses an unsourced permanent dismissal or establishes what examination this actual petition has received.

The appellate order treats supposed dismissal authority as the reason the district court erred in granting a stay. Once that legal premise is removed, the reported conduct does not itself supply a separate determination that all required process occurred or that a lawful claim-specific disposition is available. Corrective remand allows the courts below to evaluate those questions and genuine independent stay grounds on the proper basis. It does not forbid sustaining a stay refusal on an adequately established independent ground.

**Section 2251 limits:** A federal justice or judge before whom a habeas proceeding is pending **may** stay covered State proceedings before final judgment, after final judgment **of discharge**, or pending appeal. The covered matter must involve the detained person and a matter involved in the habeas proceeding. Proceedings within a granted stay are void; without a stay, they remain as valid as if no habeas proceeding or appeal were pending. Filing alone therefore supplies no suspension, and the statute does not invalidate unrelated State action. Its discretionary text does not by itself answer whether protection is necessary for an otherwise required judicial opportunity.

##### III. Protection must preserve genuinely required process when lawful expedition cannot

**Controlling proposition:** If execution would prevent completion of process actually required by the governing habeas statute and Rules, and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time, a court must provide the interim protection necessary to make that identified opportunity meaningful. Mere filing, first-petition status or a capital sentence establishes neither condition.

**Authority:** Part III has five direct supporters: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy joins only the necessary-opportunity formulation, without resolving the opportunity's extent on this record. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reserve the general proposition; Stone-Zsela supplies no additional join.

**Controlling explanation:** Barefoot protects meaningful review in its specified initial-appeal setting. Its reasoning supports, by analogy, protection for whatever examination the first-petition statute and Rules actually require. This is an extension of that meaningful-review principle to a different stage, not direct application of a certificate-of-probable-cause rule whose trigger has not been established here. McFarland reinforces protection of a real statutory opportunity while retaining individualized limits for established disregard and obstruction.

The court must identify what process remains due. Rule 4 screening, lawful procedural dismissal, appropriate expedition and the absence of an automatic evidentiary hearing remain effective. If those means permit a lawful disposition in time, this conditional rule supplies no further stay entitlement. Nor does it erase the actual opportunity and obstruction inquiry in McFarland or the independent equitable considerations preserved in Gomez.

Execution cannot replace process that governing law still requires merely because the court lacks time to perform it. Necessary protection makes that legal obligation effective; it does not guarantee every requested merits step. The present decision finds neither that a particular undisclosed claim requires further review nor that a particular period is necessary. Those are matters for the directed examination and reasoned stay redetermination.

**Barefoot's distinct settings remain intact:**

- A first district petition receives no automatic suspension upon filing. The rule above identifies an analogy and requires its own actual-process and timing predicates.
- On an **initial habeas appeal with a certificate of probable cause**, meaningful opportunity to address the merits and an appellate merits decision are required. If execution would moot review and time prevents a considered disposition, a stay must issue. Adequate notice and opportunity to address the merits permit expedited or simultaneous merits/stay decision. Certification requires more than nonfrivolity: a substantial federal showing, including reasonably debatable issues, possible different resolution or questions deserving further consideration; neither ultimate success nor sentence severity alone is the test. No final denial and certified appeal are established here.
- Second or successive petitions retain Rule 9(b)'s actual conditions, lawful expedition and the requirement that a stay reflect substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Failed next-friend proceedings do not alone put this petition in that setting.
- Pending certiorari after appellate habeas denial, a stay is not automatic. There must be a reasonable probability that four Members would find the issue sufficiently meritorious for certiorari or notation of probable jurisdiction, a significant possibility of reversal and likely irreparable harm. The application must supply materials for careful assessment; relief should first be sought in the court of appeals, whose action generally receives considerable weight in that setting. These are not the merits criteria for this already-argued challenge.

##### IV. This legal error requires corrective remand and prompt stay redetermination

**Controlling proposition:** The appellate stay-vacatur judgment cannot stand on its erroneous dismissal premise. Remand proceeds through the Eleventh Circuit for prompt district examination of the actual petition and proper State objections and redetermination of the need and terms of §2251 protection. Removing the appellate order restores the displaced district stay pending that decision, subject to lawful modification or dissolution.

**Authority:** All nine support this operative correction. The eight Associates formally vacate and remand; Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand. All support the limited restoration independently of any additional Part III join.

**Controlling explanation:** The order under review displaced an existing district stay on a mistaken legal understanding. Setting it aside restores the prior protection so the directed examination can occur, while requiring the courts below promptly to apply the correct law. That consequence differs from issuing a new Supreme Court stay or declaring every first petitioner entitled to continued protection.

The reported conduct findings remain available for lawful assessment. They do not establish Rule 9 prejudice, procedural default, nonexhaustion, abuse, claim insubstantiality or completion of all required examination. The Court supplies none of those missing determinations. Nor does it direct a hearing or full constitutional-merits adjudication despite lawful barriers. Once the required examination permits lawful disposition or establishes that protection is unnecessary, this mandate supplies no entitlement to further delay. The existing order may be modified or dissolved consistently with the governing law; indefinite continuation is not approved.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Gomez:** Retained as an independent equitable stay rule, not reduced to successive-abuse doctrine. Its assumed open procedural route and record-specific balancing remain material; four earlier petitions are not a minimum trigger. It creates neither the asserted additional first-petition dismissal ground nor an automatic late-claim bar.
- **McFarland:** Applied for a meaningful statutory opportunity and individualized stay discretion, including denial for proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction. Its qualified counsel/preparation proceeding and preapplication pendency holding do not establish Lonchar's preparation facts or an unconditional stay. An actual application is present here.
- **Barefoot:** Part III extends its meaningful-review reasoning by analogy to genuinely required district process. Its initial-appeal/CPC, successive-petition and certiorari formulations remain confined to their respective triggers and qualifications stated above. No certified appeal is established, and lawful expedition is preserved.
- **Schlup:** Its legal-error remand supports correcting the asserted mistaken premise without predetermining further proceedings or relief. Its crime-innocence gateway still requires reliable evidence and the probative record as a whole to show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. Reliability, likely credibility, delay and inconsistency matter; mere possibility, ordinary merits prejudice or a reviewing judge's doubt does not suffice. No gateway is found satisfied here, and innocence is not an entry requirement for every first petition.
- **Sawyer:** Its separate death-eligibility gateway requires clear and convincing proof that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find eligibility under applicable state law. Additional discretionary mitigation does not satisfy it. It supplies no finding or universal first-petition condition here.
- **McCleskey:** Its abuse rules and clear, particular State pleading requirement remain within their actual habeas setting. The then-effective §2244(b) conditions are preserved rather than replaced by a generalized delay sanction.
- **Delo v. Stokes:** Its abuse-of-discretion review and successive-petition application retain their established scope. They do not by themselves establish an abuse ground or the merits-screening standard for this first personal petition.

No precedent is overruled. The judgment preserves legally authorized equity while rejecting the additional terminal sanction asserted below.

#### Law After Decision

From April 1, 1996, a first personal federal petition cannot be extinguished solely by the described delay and litigation purpose through an additional equitable dismissal rule dispensing with statutory and Habeas Rules conditions. Independent stay equity survives. Five Justices also establish the conditional protection needed for genuinely required process when execution would defeat it and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time.

These rules do not guarantee a stay, hearing, full merits proceedings or relief. Exhaustion, default, Rule 4, Rule 9, the then-effective subsequent-application rules, lawful expedition and legally authorized sanctions remain. No five-year delay presumption, later habeas regime, new innocence gateway or numerical timetable is created. The required corrective remand establishes no claim-specific factual predicate.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The published opinions at 58 F.3d 590 and 588 concern different personal-petition and next-friend proceedings; the factual findings remain those of the lower courts. The federal petition, State submissions, actual district orders, pertinent competence and preparation evidence, merits papers, joint appendix and exact grant instrument are unavailable in the materials supporting this decision. That limitation prevents additional claim-specific findings or an enlarged review scope. The statutory grounds are the then-effective text of §2254 and the Habeas Rules and the 1994 main-edition §§2243 and 2251, stating laws effective January 4, 1995. No independent statutory ground under §§2253 or 1651 is established. The lower report's distinct header and body dates establish no new execution date.

The displaced district stay is restored pending prompt redetermination under the correct legal framework, subject to lawful modification or dissolution. The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit to the District Court for examination of the actual petition and proper State objections, and a reasoned determination of any further protection needed under §2251.

### O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., No. 95-354

Merits decision, April 1, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.**, No. 95-354; 517 U.S. 308. October Term 1995. Argued February 27, 1996; decided April 1, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 56 F.3d 542.

The question is whether replacement by a person within the ADEA's protected class precludes a prima facie claim of age discrimination. O'Connor was 56; Finnell, treated below as his effective replacement, was 40. The Fourth Circuit affirmed summary judgment under a formulation requiring replacement outside the protected group and also gave performance, reduction-in-force and ordinary-proof grounds. This review addresses the protected-class replacement condition; it does not separately review discovery or postjudgment relief.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0, as to the reviewed ADEA merits affirmance.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The discovery and Rule 59/60 postjudgment rulings remain undisturbed. The remand directs no trial, evidentiary hearing, affidavit admission, liability finding or particular relief. A sufficient remaining ground may support summary judgment after consideration under the corrected rule and ordinary evidentiary standards.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal of the reviewed merits affirmance and bounded remand | Entire opinion. |
| Partial join in the Court opinion | — | Stone-Zsela | Same judgment and remedy | Parts I and III; Part II except its categorical proposition that an insignificant replacement-age difference, standing alone, does not support the necessary inference. His assent preserves that a minor difference or materially different circumstances may fail to support an inference. |

The general insignificance-alone proposition has eight direct votes. The other holdings and bounded judgment have nine at their stated scope. No separate opinion is filed, and no fractured-opinion synthesis is needed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Replacement within the protected group does not preclude the claim

**Holding:** An ADEA plaintiff need not show replacement by a person under 40 to establish the initial inference of age discrimination. The replacement's membership in the protected group does not itself defeat that inference.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 631(a) identifies the individuals protected by the Act: those at least 40. Section 623(a)(1) identifies the prohibited reason for adverse treatment: the individual's age. The statute protects a 56-year-old against discrimination because of age even when the worker replacing him is also protected. Crossing the age-40 boundary has no independent significance that makes it a necessary element of the initial inference.

Hazen Paper requires attention to age as the actual cause, rather than an analytically distinct characteristic. The prima facie framework must serve that causal inquiry. An administratively simple cutoff is not a sound evidentiary screen when it disqualifies a 40-year-old replacement but accepts an otherwise comparable 39-year-old. The availability of ordinary direct or circumstantial proof does not justify an irrelevant restriction on the indirect route.

The parties proceed under McDonnell Douglas, and the Court assumes its applicability here without deciding its wholesale application to every ADEA claim. Review likewise takes the case as a non-reduction-in-force matter; the Court does not settle every alternative prima facie formulation. Finnell was exactly 40, so he was protected, but that fact cannot defeat O'Connor's claim.

##### II. Relative age must support an inference of the prohibited discrimination

**Holding:** Within the assumed framework, the prima facie evidence must adequately support an inference of age discrimination, and each required element must have a logical connection to that inquiry. A substantially younger replacement may support the inference even if at least 40; an insignificant age difference, standing alone, does not supply it.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associates' complete joins. Stone-Zsela joins the inference requirement, positive relative-age proposition, unchanged burdens and reservations, but not the categorical insignificant-gap-alone proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** McDonnell Douglas and Teamsters give the initial showing an evidentiary function: facts that support the inference call for an explanation. The replacement's relative age serves that function more reliably than membership in the protected group. An insignificant difference alone does not supply the required inference. This is not a ten-year threshold or any other numerical rule; nor does it resolve the force of an entire record containing additional age-related evidence.

A substantially younger replacement is not indispensable where other evidence supplies the inference or no replacement can sensibly be identified. Conversely, neither every younger replacement nor protected status, qualification, discharge and continued need for the job necessarily suffices without circumstances supporting an age inference. Material differences in the circumstances may weaken or defeat the suggested comparison. The sixteen-year difference here cannot be excluded because Finnell was 40, but it does not establish every initial element, satisfactory performance or actual motive.

Burdine keeps employer production distinct from employee persuasion. The employer remains free to articulate a lawful reason; the plaintiff retains the ultimate burden of proving that age caused the action. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a special additional category of direct or independent evidence, but, after nondiscriminatory reasons are produced, rejection of those reasons does not compel liability. The Court decides neither pretext nor ultimate discrimination here.

##### III. Corrected consideration is required; the remaining grounds and proof are not finally resolved

**Holding:** The reviewed ADEA merits affirmance is reversed, and the lower courts must reconsider the claim under the corrected replacement rule and ordinary proof and summary-judgment standards. This disposition neither invalidates every remaining ground nor requires trial or recovery; it leaves the unreviewed discovery and postjudgment rulings intact.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Respondent may defend the existing judgment on an appropriate alternative ground. A missing cross-petition does not itself bar its performance and ordinary-proof arguments. But the separately stated grounds do not make the replacement error immaterial: the lower court's performance and comparison analysis also informed its ordinary-evidence conclusion, while the timely record contains competing accounts of the territorial reduction, a contemporaneous volume memorandum, comparative material and disputed age remarks.

The earlier review and bonus do not conclusively answer later performance concerns, and the larger-district explanation remains available. Those considerations also do not authorize this Court to choose among disputed accounts. Anderson and Celotex require a genuine material dispute and favorable reasonable inferences for the nonmovant, not acceptance of speculation or judicial credibility findings. Reconsideration below permits those standards to operate with the corrected inference rule.

As McKennon demonstrates, reversal of an ADEA categorical bar does not establish liability or relief. A genuinely sufficient remaining ground may still support summary judgment. The Court does not adjudicate the alternative reduction-in-force formulation, find all timely evidence sufficient, or reopen the diligence and likely-effect rulings concerning Dennis's later affidavit. That affidavit remains outside the timely record. No new discovery, compulsory hearing, willfulness finding, damages, backpay or reinstatement is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792; Furnco Construction Corp. v. Waters, 438 U.S. 567; International Brotherhood of Teamsters v. United States, 431 U.S. 324:** Preserve the prima facie showing's function of supporting an inference; their factual formulations do not impose an under-40 replacement condition. General ADEA applicability remains assumed for this question.
- **Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine, 450 U.S. 248, and Postal Service Board of Governors v. Aikens, 460 U.S. 711:** Preserve the distinction between an initial inference, employer production and ultimate employee persuasion concerning actual discrimination.
- **Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604:** Applies its actual-age-motivation principle without requiring proof of the entire ultimate case at the initial stage. Its remaining causation and willfulness rules are unchanged.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502:** Preserves its rejection of compulsory liability after employer explanations are produced and disbelieved, and its rejection of a mandatory additional category of proof. No new persuasion allocation follows.
- **McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., 513 U.S. 352:** Applies its separation of correction of an ADEA legal bar from proof of liability and lawful relief; its distinct after-acquired-evidence remedy is not extended here.
- **Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, and Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317:** Preserve ordinary summary-judgment requirements and constrain further consideration without deciding the present evidence's ultimate sufficiency.

#### Law After Decision

The replacement's protected status is no longer a permissible categorical condition of the assumed ADEA prima facie formulation. Relative age must be assessed for its connection to prohibited discrimination; a substantially younger replacement may support the inference, while an insignificant difference alone is insufficient under the eight-Justice general rule. No numerical minimum or universally indispensable replacement element is created.

The plaintiff's ultimate persuasion obligation and the employer's opportunity to produce a lawful explanation remain. Correction of the age-40 condition guarantees neither trial nor recovery and does not reopen distinct unreviewed rulings. Specific small-gap applications, the effect of additional evidence and alternative formulations remain unresolved within the stated limits.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The lower opinions and appendix, including 56 F.3d 542, distinguish the replacement condition, alternative merits grounds, discovery ruling and postjudgment affidavit ruling. Disputed accounts remain disputed. Sections 631(a) and 623(a)(1) supply the coverage and prohibited-reason rules. Review makes no complete new determination of the district evidentiary record and does not admit Dennis's affidavit.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Fourth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the corrected rule. The lower courts must consider the properly remaining merits grounds under ordinary evidentiary standards; the Court prescribes no trial or further factfinding procedure. The discovery and postjudgment dispositions remain intact, and any later relief requires its own liability and remedial predicates.

**End of entry: O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., Merits decision, April 1, 1996.**

##### I. The identified grounds do not sustain this successive-petition stay

**Controlling proposition:** A stay pending a second or successive federal habeas petition requires substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. ##### II. The reported penalty-cause account supplies no substantial basis for overturning the no-causation determination

**Controlling proposition:** A preference for death that leads a prisoner to withhold permission for a sentence challenge does not, by itself, establish an objective factor external to the defense that prevented presentation. ##### III. Prior disagreement about the first-degree instruction does not establish a substantial federal ground

**Controlling proposition:** Where genuine, evidence-supported noncapital verdict choices are supplied, Beck does not require every supported lesser-offense instruction. **Controlling explanation:** Schad v. Arizona, 501 U.S. 624, Part III, supplies this limit while expressly preserving the requirement that an alternative be genuine and supported by evidence. Both trial parties requested the first-degree felony-murder instruction. Morgan's testimony is not made conclusive. The issue was previously adjudicated on its merits. Sanders v. ##### IV. The distinct second-degree constitutional theories do not sustain the stay on the bounded assumptions

**Controlling propositions:** For this assessment the Court assumes the omitted second-degree felony-murder instruction was available under then-applicable state rules, was refused differently from materially similar cases, and presents a procedurally available claim. **Authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, with the same five-Justice coalition, separately adopting the due-process and equal-protection conclusions.

**Controlling explanation:** Schad answers the Beck demand for an additional option, but does not alone resolve the other constitutional theories. Hicks v. The bounded omission here leaves supported lawful noncapital homicide choices and supplies no separate defect in that decision process. **Authority:** O'Connor's Part V, with the same five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Pulley v. Harris, 465 U.S. 37, rejects an invariable federal right to comparative proportionality review. Even accepting the missing-files allegation, its number alone does not establish deprivation of that opportunity. The Court finds neither that every file was irrelevant nor that stated review invariably satisfies due process.

Habeas Rule 11 permits civil rules only when appropriate and consistent with the habeas Rules. The new subject and completed adjudicative work together supply the apparent justification here. No bad faith, specific prejudice, early discoverability or actual district rationale is attributed. Ultimate relief remains undecided; he requires no new hearing, gateway or protection through every appellate step and need not decide the other claims.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would deny the application. The available second-degree claim presents a substantial question whether Missouri arbitrarily withheld a state-created jury opportunity by refusing an available instruction differently from materially similar cases. The intent and kidnapping dispute gives that question potential consequence. Hicks protects against arbitrary deprivation of that opportunity; Schad's rule that genuine noncapital verdict options satisfy Beck's minimum safeguard does not conclusively resolve the distinct due-process issue. Availability alone creates no mandatory instruction duty, and the assumed inconsistency proves no constitutional violation. The question warrants the existing appeal although ultimate relief remains undecided. Denial would leave the appellate stay governed by its own terms and subject to lawful modification. He proposes no new Supreme Court stay, guarantees no duration and adopts no independent ground on the other claims.
