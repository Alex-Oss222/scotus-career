### Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc., No. 94-2003

Merits decision, January 16, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 233. Argued January 8, 1996. On certiorari to the First Circuit, 49 F.3d 807. Borland's Emulation Interface reproduced Lotus's command hierarchy; its Key Reader used that hierarchy and initial command letters to execute existing user macros. The questions concern the operating-method exclusion in 17 U.S.C. §102(b) and the properly preserved statutory defenses. Source and object code were independently written; screen displays and long prompts are outside this appeal.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded on both the Emulation Interface and Key Reader, 5–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Souter, and Breyer.

**Not participating:** Stevens.

The First Circuit must address Borland's properly preserved fair-use defenses and remaining statutory issues as necessary to resolve both claims. The judgment determines neither copyrightability nor fair use and does not direct reinstatement of the district court's injunction or final liability judgment. Factual development is required only if lawfully necessary for the remaining issues.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Announcement of the Court's judgment; plurality opinion | O'Connor | Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg | Supports vacatur and remand on both implementations | Four Justices reject categorical exclusion of the arrangement and support bounded vacatur; their copyright rationale is not an Opinion of the Court. |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Supplies the fifth vote for the same decree | Distinct fair-use-remand ground; does not join the plurality's copyrightability rationale. |
| Dissent | Souter | Scalia, Breyer | Would affirm both components | Operating-method exclusion for both implementations. |

#### Holdings

##### A binding remand, with no majority substantive copyright rule

**Operative effect:** For both implementations, the appellate judgment is vacated and the preserved fair-use defenses and remaining statutory issues must receive further consideration as necessary to resolve the claims. No majority determines that the hierarchy is copyrightable, that either use is fair, or that liability or an injunction must be restored.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg support this decree. Their reasons do not combine into a substantive majority rule: four reject the categorical §102(b) exclusion, while Stone-Zsela leaves that question open and seeks the unresolved §107 inquiry.

**Explanation of controlling effect:** The remand preserves the ordinary burdens, actual record, and scope of the grant. It orders no universal sequence requiring fair use to precede copyrightability and no advisory decision of an unnecessary question. The two rationales supporting the decree answer different questions; neither is a logical subset of the other. The judgment therefore supplies no narrower substantive holding derived by combining their votes.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Baker v. Selden, 101 U.S. 99:** Remains the distinction between using a system and copying protected expression. The plurality and dissent disagree about its application to this hierarchy; neither application commands five votes.
- **Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co., 499 U.S. 340:** Retains protection for original selection and arrangement without ownership of unprotected constituents. The Court announces no majority resolution of how that rule interacts with this operating-method claim.
- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569:** Retains its integrated four-factor fair-use inquiry and case-sensitive treatment of commercial purpose and market substitution. The remand establishes no automatic compatibility defense or infringement conclusion.

#### Law After Decision

The First Circuit's judgment is set aside, but no substantive copyright rationale receives a majority. The decree binds the parties to further consideration within its limits. Copyrightability, infringement, fair use, and ultimate relief remain undecided by the Court.

#### Separate Writings

**O'Connor's plurality, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg.** Section 102(b) leaves functions, procedures, and methods free, but an original selection and arrangement does not necessarily lose all protection merely because users employ it to operate software. Feist protects original arrangement while withholding ownership of the underlying material. Baker permits use of a system without establishing that every particular presentation of it is excluded. The plurality would reject the First Circuit's categorical treatment and distinguish any original expression from the unowned operation.

That view covers both implementations without declaring every command or all 469 choices protected. Compatibility matters to purpose and defenses, but does not automatically answer classification or fair use. Campbell requires the actual defense inquiry; Kennedy's caution against categorical borrowing permission remains consistent with the plurality's limits. The plurality supports remand, not automatic restoration of liability or an injunction, and does not reach code or screen claims outside the appeal.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** He would protect the command compatibility necessary to operate users' existing macros through Campbell's full §107 inquiry. Such use has a concrete functional purpose beyond copying expression for its own sake; the commands' utilitarian character matters. The amount copied must be connected to actual interoperability rather than assumed necessary. Legally cognizable substitution and licensing markets count, while loss from lawful competition alone is not protected market harm. Commercial purpose is relevant but not decisive, and user investment creates no general property right in a learned interface.

The First Circuit did not resolve the fact-sensitive defenses. He would have it address them and obtain necessary findings, while allowing an alternative statutory ground within the grant without demanding an advisory copyrightability decision. He finds neither all compatibility copying fair nor all software structure unprotectable, and reserves source code, screen expression, and unnecessary copying. No other Justice joins this rationale.

**Souter, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Breyer.** The particular hierarchy is the method by which users issue executable instructions and existing macros operate. Section 102(b) excludes a method in whatever expressive form it is embodied; the availability of different original terms or arrangements does not remove that exclusion. Baker protects use of the system, and Feist distinguishes a copyright in the overall work from rights in excluded constituent material.

These are user commands, not the independent implementing code or separable visual presentation. That boundary answers the objection that treating all software as operational would swallow program protection. Because the copied subject matter is excluded, no fair-use decision is needed. The dissent would affirm both components without adopting a general interoperability license, Boudin's possible new privilege, or a rule denying protection to all interfaces.

#### Source Notes

The [First Circuit opinion, 49 F.3d 807](https://openjurist.org/49/f3d/807/lotus-development-corporation-v-borland-international-inc) supplies the two implementations, independent code, excluded screen issues, and unresolved defenses. The [official reported chronology](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-233.pdf) supplies argument and decision dates and Stevens's nonparticipation. No finding establishes that every copied command was necessary for compatibility or fixes a relevant market.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the First Circuit for the required further consideration. Ordinary appellate authority determines whether additional trial-court findings are needed. No automatic new trial, defense victory, infringement judgment, damages award, or injunction follows from vacatur.

**End of entry: Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc., Merits decision, January 16, 1996.**

### Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.; Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. v. Zicherman, Nos. 94-1361 and 94-1477

Merits decision, January 16, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 217. Argued November 7, 1995. On certiorari to the Second Circuit, 43 F.3d 18. Following a passenger's death over the high seas, the court below excluded her mother's loss-of-society award but permitted her sister's claim to depend on further proof of dependency. The question is whether the Warsaw Convention permits that nonpecuniary recovery when United States law supplies the damages measure.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded.**

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mother's loss-of-society claim | Exclusion affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer | Loss-of-society damages remain excluded. |
| Sister's loss-of-society claim | Dependency-based remand reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer | Loss-of-society damages are excluded regardless of dependency. |

Neither claimant may recover loss-of-society damages. Remand is for a conforming damages judgment and properly remaining matters; liability and unrelated award components are not vacated.

#### Opinions and Holdings

O'Connor writes for a unanimous Court. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer join the entire opinion and decree. This unanimous opinion controls both holdings and their explanations below.

##### The Convention leaves compensable loss to applicable domestic law

**Operative rule:** The Warsaw Convention's reference to damage does not supply an independent, comprehensive code identifying every compensable loss or beneficiary. Domestic law supplies those matters, subject to the Convention's own conditions and limits; the parties' accepted use of United States law permits decision here without choosing a general international conflicts rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Article 17 identifies the accident and passenger injury giving rise to carrier liability. Article 24(2) leaves questions concerning the persons entitled to sue and their respective rights without prejudice. Neither provision establishes a worldwide measure of damages. Air France v. Saks requires attention to the treaty's actual terms and their shared legal meaning; Eastern Airlines v. Floyd likewise enforces the injury conditions the treaty contains rather than adding recovery from a general compensatory purpose.

The relatives argue that a treaty action requires a uniform federal common-law damages measure. That argument confuses uniform conditions of carrier liability with a comprehensive rule governing every resulting loss. A different domestic measure in a different location does not authorize the Court to write a missing treaty provision. Nor does removal of a Convention liability cap create a category of loss that applicable domestic law excludes. The Court decides neither the treaty's exclusivity in every setting nor which country's law would govern a disputed choice-of-law question.

##### DOHSA excludes loss-of-society damages for both relatives

**Operative rule:** When United States law governs damages for a death on the high seas beyond a marine league from a United States shore, within the Death on the High Seas Act's geographical field, the Act's restriction to pecuniary loss excludes loss-of-society recovery. Dependency cannot revive that excluded category, and general maritime law cannot supplement the Act to permit it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Death on the High Seas Act, or DOHSA, governs the accepted high-seas location and limits recovery to pecuniary loss. Loss of society compensates the absence of companionship, a nonpecuniary loss. Mobil Oil Corp. v. Higginbotham holds that courts may not supplement DOHSA where Congress has spoken to the damages question. Offshore Logistics v. Tallentire likewise preserves the Act's exclusive high-seas regime against a contrary state-law recovery.

Miles v. Apex Marine Corp. requires respect for Congress's occupied maritime remedial fields. Yamaha Motor Corp. v. Calhoun preserves supplementary state remedies for an uncovered nonseafarer in territorial waters precisely because no governing federal statute occupied that field. It therefore does not authorize supplementation here. Sea-Land Services v. Gaudet's territorial-water society remedy cannot supersede DOHSA. American Dredging's treatment of forum administration supplies no different substantive damages measure.

The mother's exclusion is correct. Further findings about the sister's dependency cannot affect the result because the category itself is unavailable. The Court leaves other properly preserved damage components undisturbed and decides no general dependency rule, survival claim, predeath suffering claim, mental-distress claim, beneficiary-standing issue, or jury-trial issue.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S. 392:** Applied for interpretation of the Convention's actual conditions and shared meaning; it supplies no general damages code.
- **Eastern Airlines, Inc. v. Floyd, 499 U.S. 530:** Applied for the same insistence on the treaty's enacted injury conditions; its limits are not enlarged by a compensatory purpose.
- **Mobil Oil Corp. v. Higginbotham, 436 U.S. 618:** Applied to forbid judicial addition of nonpecuniary damages where DOHSA provides the measure.
- **Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207:** Applied to preserve the exclusive high-seas statutory recovery regime.
- **Miles v. Apex Marine Corp., 498 U.S. 19:** Applied for respect for Congress's occupied maritime remedial choices, without extending a different employment statute's coverage.
- **Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun, 516 U.S. 199:** Its territorial-water coexistence rule remains; the present high-seas death lies within the statutory field Yamaha preserved.
- **Sea-Land Services, Inc. v. Gaudet, 414 U.S. 573:** Its territorial-water society remedy does not override DOHSA.
- **American Dredging Co. v. Miller, 510 U.S. 443:** Distinguished as governing forum administration, not authority to add substantive damages.

#### Law After Decision

The Convention does not itself make loss of society compensable. Under the accepted United States-law premise, DOHSA excludes that category for this high-seas death regardless of dependency. The Court announces no rule eliminating territorial-water remedies or resolving other damages categories.

#### Source Notes

The [Second Circuit opinion, 43 F.3d 18](https://openjurist.org/43/f3d/18/zicherman-v-korean-air-lines-co-ltd) and the [official reported case](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-217.pdf) supply the accident, award components, accepted domestic-law premise, and judgment under review. The [reported chronology](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/94-1361.ZS.html) supplies the argument and decision dates. Dependency and unreviewed damage questions are not newly found or adjudicated.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for a damages judgment excluding both society claims and for properly remaining proceedings. No new trial of carrier liability or unrelated damages is ordered.

**End of entry: Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.; Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. v. Zicherman, Merits decision, January 16, 1996.**

### Commissioner v. Lundy, No. 94-1785

Merits decision, January 17, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 235. Argued October 31, 1995. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 45 F.3d 856. Lundy had filed no return when the Commissioner mailed a deficiency notice on September 26, 1990. His withholding was deemed paid April 15, 1988; he filed his return later that December. The question is whether the Tax Court may refund that withholding under the hypothetical-claim provision of 26 U.S.C. §6512(b)(3)(B).

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Thomas.

Section 6512(b)(3)(B) does not authorize refund of these withholding payments. The Tax Court may adjudicate the deficiency and remaining computations consistently with that limit; the Court fixes no refund amount or separate remedy.

#### Opinions and Holdings

O'Connor writes for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Thomas dissents, joined by Stevens. The seven-Justice Court opinion controls the rule and explanation below.

##### The notice-date hypothetical selects and measures the payment window

**Operative rule:** Under §6512(b)(3)(B), a hypothetical refund claim filed on the deficiency-notice date both selects and measures the applicable §6511(b)(2) payment lookback. For the ordinary withholding claim here, no return existed at that date, so the two-year window applies; a later return cannot bring earlier payments into that window.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 6511(a) ordinarily requires a claim within three years after filing the return or two years after payment, whichever expires later; if no return is filed, it uses two years after payment. Section 6511(b)(2)(A) limits a claim filed within the three-year period to payments within three years, plus any filing-extension period, before the claim. Subparagraph (B) uses two years for a claim outside that period. Section 6512(b)(3)(B) directs the Tax Court to apply those payment limits as if a claim stating the overpayment ground had been filed at the notice date, whether or not an actual claim was filed.

The hypothetical supplies a claim, not a return. On September 26, 1990, Lundy had filed no return; the hypothetical claim therefore did not fall within a return-based three-year period. His April 15, 1988 payments lie outside the resulting two-year window. Treating December's return as already present in September would change the counterfactual Congress selected.

The contrary construction would preserve a later effective real claim as an alternative. But it separates the choice of period from the notice-date instruction governing the incorporated calculation. A possible advantage in another refund forum does not authorize rewriting this mechanism. United States v. Dalm preserves the connection between refund authority and statutory conditions; United States v. Williams does not remove applicable timing limits when recognizing an eligible payer. Reich's protection of a promised state postpayment remedy supplies no different federal lookback.

This conclusion concerns the ordinary subparagraph (B) route presented. No filing extension, assessment-extension agreement, special-period item, or other statutory route is established. The Court decides neither every nonfiler's refund rights nor whether every tax deadline has the same jurisdictional character.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Dalm, 494 U.S. 596:** Applied for the connection between refund authority and its statutory conditions, without classifying every tax limit alike.
- **United States v. Williams, 514 U.S. 527:** Its express-remedy and payer-eligibility holdings leave applicable timing requirements intact; it does not decide the notice-date hypothetical.
- **Reich v. Collins, 513 U.S. 106:** Its protection against withdrawal of a clearly held-out state postpayment remedy remains; it supplies no power to change this federal payment window.

#### Law After Decision

For this ordinary §6512(b)(3)(B) claim, the notice-date hypothetical governs both selection and measurement; the later return does not cure the payment-window defect. Deficiency jurisdiction remains distinct from refund authority. Subparagraph (A)'s treatment of payments after notice and subparagraph (C)'s treatment of qualifying actual claims filed before notice remain separate. No qualifying alternative is established here, and other special periods are not adjudicated.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Stevens.** They would affirm consideration of the refund under the actual claim filed with the later return within three years of payment. In their reading, Congress created a hypothetical claim to protect taxpayers who had not made one; it did not direct courts to disregard a real, otherwise effective claim. The incorporated refund provisions can therefore operate through either the actual claim or the protective hypothetical. The majority's notice-date reading turns a benefit into an exclusive restriction without express language requiring that result.

This is a statutory disagreement, not an equitable exception. Dalm's timing conditions remain enforceable, and Williams supports using a remedy Congress actually provided. They would not require an actual claim in every Tax Court case, manufacture a return at the notice date, or give every delinquent filer a three-year window. Their ground concerns the real claim actually filed within three years of payment; the amount recoverable remains subject to ordinary computation.

#### Source Notes

The [official report, 516 U.S. 235–263](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-235.pdf) and the [Fourth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/45/f3d/856/lundy-v-commissioner-of-internal-revenue) supply the filings, payment dates, and judgment below. The [1994 United States Code, title 26](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title26/html/USCODE-1994-title26.htm), §§6511–6513, supplies the governing text. The return's requested amount and the correspondence do not establish a final refund entitlement or binding settlement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fourth Circuit must proceed consistently with the Tax Court's inability to refund these identified withholding payments under §6512(b)(3)(B). Remaining deficiency and tax computations are not displaced. No fixed refund, damages, interest, fees, or additional hearing is ordered.

**End of entry: Commissioner v. Lundy, Merits decision, January 17, 1996.**

### Attwood v. Singletary, No. 95-6710

Financial leave and certiorari action, January 22, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 297. The Court considers financial leave for the present certiorari petition, a proposed prospective noncriminal filing condition, and disposition of the petition. Attwood challenges Florida counsel-signature restrictions while alleging inability to obtain counsel. The attached state order records extensive abusive litigation; his claims about the restrictions' effects on custody and mistreatment remedies remain allegations.

#### Public Action

The motion for leave to proceed without prepayment of fees is granted. The petition for a writ of certiorari is denied. No new prospective filing condition is imposed. The Court announces no petition poll.

The present petition is accepted without prepayment and denied. No payment interval is necessary, no future noncriminal fee-and-form condition issues, and no judgment on the underlying federal or state claims is entered. Denial of certiorari does not affirm the state court's reasoning or approve its counsel-signature requirement.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to action | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Financial leave granted; proposed prospective control declined | Present-application and prospective-control grounds. |
| Concurrence in the result | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Agrees with the result | Separate explanation; does not join the Court's reasoning. |

The certiorari denial supplies no merits opinion or disclosed poll.

#### Holdings

##### This particular application supports financial leave

**Operative rule:** Supported inability to prepay and an arguable present petition warrant financial leave on this record; prior volume and state abuse do not themselves establish the particular frivolousness or malice needed for the proposed Rule 39.8 denial. Granting leave does not establish the truth of the allegations, entitlement to certiorari, or future financial eligibility.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. The same eight Justices adopt the following prospective-control holding. Stone-Zsela agrees with the result on his separately stated grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The signed poverty statement and accompanying financial answers support this application despite partly illegible account details. The Court adopts no precise asset balance. The petition raises a concrete concern that mandatory counsel signatures, coupled with an alleged inability to obtain counsel, impede access including claimed custody-related relief.

Martin distinguishes an arguable current submission from one actually established as frivolous. Anderson likewise separates financial leave for an access claim from obstacles to obtaining the requested writ. The state's findings of extensive abuse remain relevant, but do not decide this distinct challenge to the restriction's practical operation. Zatko continues to permit denial of a particular filing upon the required showing. The Court accepts no general right to unlimited fee-free litigation and decides no claim of constitutional invalidity here.

##### The proposed future condition lacks its separate supporting showing

**Operative rule:** A prospective fee-and-form condition on a category of future filings requires support tied to the applicant, that category, and the inadequacy of treating individual submissions. Extensive state litigation alone does not establish that showing for the proposed condition on future noncriminal certiorari petitions in this Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The restriction would preserve paid and criminal routes. That limited scope matters, but does not prove its necessity or fit. The established state abuse does not itself demonstrate abuse of this Court's relevant category or why particular-filing treatment is inadequate. Martin, Day, Sassower, and Anderson require those distinctions rather than a prediction that every unwritten claim will be frivolous.

The Court declines the condition on this record. It establishes no universal warning prerequisite, numerical threshold, or obligation to exhaust every conceivable lesser measure. McDonald and Sindram retain their authority for properly supported targeted controls. Ordinary neutral filing rules and sanctions for proved misconduct remain available.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Zatko v. California:** Preserves supported denial of a particular frivolous or malicious filing; prior losses alone do not make this challenge frivolous.
- **Martin v. District of Columbia:** Applied to separate the current petition's arguability from the independent justification for prospective control.
- **In re Anderson:** Applied to distinguish supported financial leave and actual access concerns from entitlement to a writ or future control.
- **Day and Sassower:** Their individualized, category-specific prospective-control requirements remain and are applied here.
- **In re McDonald, 489 U.S. 180, and In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177:** Targeted protection of the judicial process remains available on an adequate record; neither dictates the proposed order without its supporting predicates.

#### Law After Decision

The filing ruling applies the existing current-submission and prospective-control requirements. It creates no categorical immunity from supported controls and no right to discretionary Supreme Court review. The certiorari denial decides none of the access, habeas, counsel-availability, or mistreatment questions.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the result.** He favors granting leave upon the established financial showing and promptly disposing of the present petition without attaching a disability to future litigation. In his view, equal access to federal process cannot be traded away merely for docket convenience; volume warrants efficient scrutiny of each paper, not a presumption that every unwritten claim is frivolous. Rule 39.8 permits a fee denial when justified but does not require that intermediate step before ordinary certiorari disposition.

His constitutional position seeks stronger protection against predictive, identity-based exclusion than the Court's record-specific ground establishes. His preference for direct screening is an institutional judgment, not an empirical finding about administrative cost. He preserves ordinary neutral rules, jurisdiction, sanctions for proved misconduct, and valid finality doctrines. No other Justice joins this explanation, and it does not turn denial of the petition into a ruling on the underlying claims.

#### Source Notes

The [actual petition, affidavit, and attached Florida order](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0788) supply the present financial showing, access allegations, and state abuse findings. The [reported docket identification](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-297.pdf) supplies the case, docket, and date. The partly illegible account explanation is not an exact financial finding. The Florida Supreme Court counsel-signature condition is distinct from the paid-filing alternative described for another state appellate court.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This petition is concluded. No payment deadline, additional Court warning, counsel appointment, release order, or new prospective restriction follows. Any different later filing remains subject to the ordinary rules and governing law.

**End of entry: Attwood v. Singletary, Financial leave and certiorari action, January 22, 1996.**

### Neal v. United States, No. 94-9088

Merits decision, January 22, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 284. Argued December 4, 1995. On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 46 F.3d 1405. Neal seeks use of the Sentencing Commission's amended weight per dose of LSD when calculating the statutory mandatory minimum. The question is whether that Guidelines amendment displaces Chapman's construction of 21 U.S.C. §841.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The statutory-minimum judgment remains in force; no new sentence or calculation is ordered.

#### Opinions and Holdings

Kennedy writes for a unanimous Court. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer join the entire opinion. This unanimous opinion controls the rule and explanation below.

##### The statute and Guidelines retain their separate LSD measures

**Operative rule:** For LSD absorbed into the blotter-paper carrier at issue, §841(b)(1)'s statutory mixture-or-substance weight includes the actual carrier weight under Chapman. Amendment 488's 0.4-milligram-per-dose calculation governs the Guidelines Drug Quantity Table within its lawful scope and does not replace that statutory measure; when the statutory minimum exceeds the maximum of the applicable Guidelines range, §5G1.1(b) makes the statutory minimum the Guidelines sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman v. United States construed the statute to count the LSD and its carrier together. The Commission's later amendment selects a constructive weight to reduce disparities among carrier media. Its commentary expressly preserves statutory mandatory minima. Courts must give both rules their proper operation rather than turn the Guidelines change into a statutory amendment.

Stinson v. United States makes valid interpretive commentary authoritative within the Guidelines system, while excluding commentary that conflicts with a federal statute. It does not empower the Commission to overrule a judicial construction of §841. Patterson v. McLean Credit Union gives particular force to statutory stare decisis because Congress can amend the enacted rule. The amendment's policy criticism of carrier disparities is not itself an adequate ground to overrule Chapman here. The Court does not hold that statutory precedent can never be reconsidered, or that every later agency interpretation is foreclosed by every earlier judicial statement.

The amendment's qualifications remain within their own field. For unmarked blotter paper, a quarter-inch by quarter-inch section is presumed one dose. For liquid LSD not yet placed on a carrier, pure-drug weight may inadequately reflect seriousness and may warrant an upward departure. Neither provision creates an exception to §841's minimum or requires a departure in this case.

The Court leaves lawful Commission policymaking intact. It decides no unpresented sentencing exemption, Guidelines temporal question, or distinct constitutional challenge involving an unusually heavy carrier with very little LSD.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453:** Followed for actual LSD/carrier weight under §841; the Commission's constructive Guidelines measure does not displace it.
- **Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36:** Applied to preserve valid commentary within the Guidelines system and its limitation against conflict with a federal statute.
- **Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164:** Applied for the special force of statutory stare decisis while Congress retains authority to amend the rule.

#### Law After Decision

Statutory and Guidelines quantities may differ. Chapman controls the statutory mandatory minimum; Amendment 488 controls its delegated Guidelines calculation subject to that minimum. Neither source is silently rewritten to match the other.

#### Source Notes

The [Seventh Circuit opinion, 46 F.3d 1405](https://openjurist.org/46/f3d/1405/united-states-v-neal) and the [official reported record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-284.pdf) supply the reviewed statutory sentencing question. The [Commission's original Amendment 488](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/amendment/488) supplies the calculation, qualifications, and express preservation of mandatory minima. No new carrier weight, dose count, statutory tier, or replacement sentence is found.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Seventh Circuit's judgment stands. The Court orders no resentencing, alternative calculation, or inquiry into an unspecified exception.

**End of entry: Neal v. United States, Merits decision, January 22, 1996.**

### Beadle v. City of Tampa, Eleventh Circuit No. 93-3271

Merits decision, February 5, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits review of the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, 42 F.3d 633. Beadle's Sabbath observance conflicted with the police field-training schedule. The question is whether the City's neutral rotation and asserted training burdens satisfied Title VII's distinct religious-accommodation duty.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The lower courts must apply the proper accommodation and hardship inquiry. No particular schedule, placement, reinstatement, damages award, or new trial is ordered. The separate release determination remains undisturbed.

#### Opinions and Holdings

Stevens writes for the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents. The eight-Justice Court opinion controls both holdings and their remedy limits below.

##### Neutral scheduling does not alone discharge the accommodation duty

**Operative rule:** Under 42 U.S.C. §2000e(j), an employer must reasonably accommodate religious observance or practice unless it demonstrates inability to do so without undue hardship on its business. Under Hardison's governing rule, the employer need not incur more than a minimal cost, including concrete lost efficiency, deficient training, or consequent safety burdens; once an effective reasonable accommodation resolves the conflict, Ansonia does not require the employee's preferred alternative or proof against every alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII's accommodation duty does work separate from its prohibition on intentional discrimination. A neutral rule and absence of hostility do not themselves answer whether a religious conflict can reasonably be accommodated. Here the ordinary rotating schedule and one isolated Saturday leave did not resolve the continuing conflict.

Trans World Airlines v. Hardison permits an employer to refuse accommodation imposing more than the governing modest cost, including interference with protected seniority rights and concrete operational burdens. It does not exempt police work from the statute. Ansonia Board of Education v. Philbrook ends the alternative inquiry when the employer has supplied a reasonable accommodation. It does not turn an unchanged, still-conflicting schedule into one.

The City may rely on professional evidence and reasonable operational inferences about training and safety. It need not await an accident or reduce every burden to dollars. The Court imposes no independent negotiation duty when an effective accommodation or sufficient hardship defense exists, and imports neither a compelling-interest test nor a later hardship standard.

##### A concession about one trainer does not resolve materially different proposals

**Operative rule:** When the work-religion conflict remains unresolved, a hardship justification must address the supported, materially distinct accommodations and actual operations at issue. A concession that using one trainer would impair training does not establish that every proposed arrangement preserving multiple trainers would cause undue hardship; the accommodation judgment must be reconsidered where that mismatch underlies the decision.

**Controlling explanation:** The trial court relied on the neutral system and general police responsibilities while finding no demonstrated deprivation of another trainee's scheduling benefit. The appellate court relied on Beadle's concession that assignment to one trainer throughout the program would reduce educational variety. The record also presents multiple qualified trainers while retaining Dawson's squad, qualified trainers on other Sabbath-compatible nontraining squads, and a four-to-eight-week extension to address rotation deficits. The concession does not decide those different proposals.

Hardison allows actual training deterioration and operational cost to establish hardship. The City may prove those consequences here. But Ansonia's protection against employee selection among effective accommodations does not remove the unresolved statutory inquiry. The Court finds an error in that inquiry, not that any proposal must succeed.

Later practice does not prove an arrangement was available during Beadle's training. Extensions given to other recruits are relevant without establishing that this extension would be costless. Section 11.3 makes classification seniority a factor when skill, performance, and ability are equal and the supervisor finds the task unaffected, but removes seniority from consideration when training affects assignments. That training exception and the absence of a required-swap finding prevent assuming a seniority conflict, without eliminating legitimate coworker interests. A possible downtown posting is not a guaranteed vacancy. The existing record may support a properly reasoned decision; further proceedings are required only as lawfully necessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison, 432 U.S. 63:** Preserved and applied; concrete noncash operational burdens and protected seniority interests may establish hardship under its more-than-minimal-cost rule.
- **Ansonia Board of Education v. Philbrook, 479 U.S. 60:** Preserved; a sufficient reasonable accommodation ends the alternative inquiry, but the continuing conflict here has not been resolved by one isolated leave day or the ordinary schedule.

#### Law After Decision

Police departments remain subject to Title VII's actual accommodation duty and governing hardship defense. Neutrality and general safety concerns are insufficient substitutes for the required application. This decision establishes neither the feasibility of Beadle's proposals nor a right to an exemption regardless of cost, training, seniority, or safety.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would affirm on the established training and assignment burdens while leaving the rejected release defense untouched. He agrees that neutral scheduling alone is insufficient and that Hardison requires accommodation unless the employer establishes hardship. In his view, the trial findings concern actual training exposure, work conditions, assignments, and the commitment to a later posting. Those operational burdens satisfy Hardison's existing threshold without a categorical public-safety exception.

He would consider an available arrangement that genuinely preserves the training objectives, but finds none established as one the City was legally required to adopt on this record. His position permits neither gratuitous refusal of workable alternatives nor automatic compliance through neutrality, and sets no universal staffing ratio or religious priority. His disagreement is with the majority's assessment of the existing hardship showing and resulting remand.

#### Source Notes

The [Eleventh Circuit judicial opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/42/html/0633-01.html) and its [reported text](https://openjurist.org/42/f3d/633) supply the judgment, training rationale, and release treatment. The [petition, appended findings, opposition, appendix, and reply](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386012_1595) supply the fuller dispute about alternatives. Assertions in briefs are not findings of feasibility. The reply's reference to pages 25–26 points to the petition's printed discussion, not an identical finding on appendix pages 25a–26a. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is specified.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit for proper application of Title VII, with any further district-court proceedings that are lawfully necessary. The factfinder's role and ordinary review standards remain. The City may again prevail upon a sufficient actual hardship showing. The Court orders no particular accommodation or award.

### Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21 of Wheeling Township, Seventh Circuit No. 91-1684

Merits decision, February 12, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Seventh Circuit's November 20, 1992 judgment, 980 F.2d 437. The question is whether a public elementary school may organize daily recitation of the Pledge containing “under God” when pupils may remain silent and decline participation. The ongoing school-program claim presents a live prospective controversy.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Establishment challenge to the school-directed Pledge | Affirmed, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Judgment for the school defendants stands; no program injunction or damages award. |
| Separate Attorney General dismissal | Undisturbed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | No reopening of the official-capacity damages or prospective claim against that officer. |

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition preserving the Attorney General dismissal and adopts no additional ground on that component. He would reverse the school-program judgment and remand for prospective relief against the challenged official religious component to the extent severable and necessary to redress these plaintiffs' injury. The unappealed Society claim is outside review.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and III | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | School-program affirmance | Concrete civic-acknowledgment ground, protected abstention, judgment and remedial limits |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | O'Connor | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | School-program affirmance | Contextual purpose, effect and entanglement explanation |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Kennedy | Scalia, Thomas | School-program affirmance; joins Parts I and III | Concrete acknowledgment ground; withholds Part II's comprehensive methodology |
| Dissent | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse the school-program judgment | Official religious sponsorship and limited prospective remedy; agrees that the Attorney General dismissal remains undisturbed |

#### Holdings

##### 1. This voluntary civic Pledge is distinct from an official worship program

**Holding and operative rule:** A public elementary school may conduct the daily civic Pledge containing the brief acknowledgment “under God” on this record, where the exercise expresses allegiance to the flag and Republic rather than conducting prayer, devotional instruction or a required religious profession, and pupils may decline words and gestures without punishment or forced exit. This permission does not authorize school-directed worship or compulsory assent merely because it appears within a patriotic program.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Eight directly adopt the concrete civic classification and its limits; their agreement does not establish eight joins to Part II's full methodology.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this exercise is a civic acknowledgment rather than a school worship service. Engel and Schempp prohibit official prayer and devotional exercises even when a pupil may abstain; their rule remains intact. Lee likewise forbids the official graduation worship it addressed, independently of a separate showing of punishment, and also protects against the substantial religious participation pressure identified there. Those holdings do not decide that every religious phrase within a civic declaration constitutes worship. Lynch and Allegheny recognize a distinction between religious advancement and civic acknowledgment; their discussions do not create an exemption for every historical practice. Here the declaration concerns allegiance to the flag and Republic, with a brief religious reference and no separate prayer, devotional lesson or worship leader. The words retain religious meaning for those who understand them that way; the result does not require believers to regard them as empty. The school's selection of the exercise makes it governmental conduct, so Good News and Pinette's protection of genuinely private expression does not supply an answer. The Court examines what the government is doing. The pupils' protected ability to abstain matters within this civic exercise; it would not cure actual school worship. The Court rejects the proposed religious-program classification on the presented record, while leaving officials no authority to add devotional instruction, prayer or compelled belief.

##### 2. The program satisfies the existing contextual establishment inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged Pledge program serves a civic educational and ceremonial purpose, has no primary effect of governmental religious advancement in its actual use, and creates no religious-institutional entanglement. Its validity rests on that actual civic function and context, not on a universal historical exemption or voluntariness alone.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Five Justices directly adopt this explanation under the existing purpose, effect and entanglement principles; no combination of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Lemon's existing inquiry, as informed by Lynch's examination of an acknowledgment in its actual setting. The school conducts a declaration of national allegiance. The short reference within that declaration does not, in this bounded use, turn the civic exercise into instruction in religious doctrine or a school profession of faith. The school neither delegates its program to a religious institution nor undertakes the religious direction present in Lee. School authorship and the elementary pupils' ages require direct scrutiny; they cannot be answered by labeling the exercise private. Nor does possible disagreement or a sense of exclusion alone establish that the program's primary effect is religious advancement. At the same time, the absence of admissible proof of hazing is not a finding that every child feels no pressure. Pupils may remain seated and silent, decline the hand-over-heart gesture and stay in the classroom without punishment. These circumstances support the actual civic function; they do not excuse a devotional program if one exists. Good News's contextual inquiry distinguishes actual private sponsorship from government action and supplies no forum immunity here. The Court preserves Lee's official-worship and independent religious-pressure rules and decides only the practice presented.

##### 3. Permission to conduct the program does not permit compelled affirmation

**Holding and operative rule:** School officials may not require a pupil to recite the Pledge or make gestures affirming it, punish refusal, or require the pupil to leave as the price of declining participation. Because the established practice permits silence, remaining seated, declining the gesture and remaining in the room without penalty, the record supplies no compelled-affirmation ground for relief.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I is joined by the eight Associate Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela independently accepts that silence answers the compelled-personal-recitation claim, while rejecting the separate establishment conclusion; his dissent is not a join to the Court's establishment rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Barnette supplies the protection against compelled affirmation. The record establishes an actual opportunity to decline the words and gestures without being removed or punished. The teacher prohibited hazing and reported observing none; the father's contrary assertions were not supported by personal-knowledge evidence. The Court therefore does not decide a case of proved compulsory speech, exit or harassment. Barnette's protection remains fully operative, and the present affirmance confers no power to compel assent later.

##### Limits and questions not reached

The decision does not resolve every public motto, adult legislative prayer, historical document used in instruction, teacher-employment dispute or unproved harassment claim. The school-program controversy does not reopen the separate Attorney General dismissal: official-capacity damages were barred and no enforcement connection supported prospective relief against that officer. The Court adds no new holding on those distinct grounds. The unappealed Society claim remains outside review.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette:** Applied to preserve protection against compelled words and gestures; the program's permission does not reduce that protection.
- **Engel v. Vitale and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp:** Their prohibition of official school prayer and devotional exercises remains controlling; the present civic exercise is distinguished, and an opt-out is not made a cure for worship.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Both the official-sponsorship and independently sufficient religious-pressure holdings remain in force. The Court distinguishes this civic acknowledgment from the school-organized worship and pressure Lee decided.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** The five-Justice Part II majority applies purpose, effect and entanglement; the test is not replaced or converted into an automatic historical exemption.
- **Lynch v. Donnelly and County of Allegheny v. ACLU:** Their contextual treatment of civic acknowledgment informs this application; no general permission for government religious speech or rule of religious meaninglessness is extracted.
- **Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue and Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Their private-expression holdings remain distinct. The school-directed Pledge is governmental action, and neither decision supplies categorical forum immunity.

#### Law After Decision

The decision adds a bounded application permitting this civic Pledge program with effective freedom to abstain. Existing law continues to forbid official school worship, the substantial religious participation pressure identified in Lee, and compelled affirmation. The five-Justice contextual explanation controls at its stated scope; the three-Justice methodological reservation does not displace it. No rule about private religious access, public funding or every historical religious reference changes.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the concrete conclusion and limits in Parts I and III, but not Part II's comprehensive purpose, effect and entanglement explanation. Kennedy's Allegheny approach distinguishes a civic acknowledgment from governmental worship without requiring religious words to lose all meaning. The Pledge's actual civic character and protected right to decline assent support this result. The school remains the speaker; Good News's private-expression rationale therefore cannot itself sustain the program. Their agreement does not repudiate Lee, require a formal penalty before religious coercion can exist, or permit officials to add prayer merely because the activity is patriotic. They offer no broader holding through this concurrence.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would reverse the establishment ruling and remand for prospective relief against the official religious component, limited to what is severable and necessary to redress these plaintiffs' injury. A school-selected declaration that the nation stands under God states a religious proposition for children's present affirmation. Its civic setting, brevity and history do not change who prescribes it or what it says. Lee's official-sponsorship principle therefore supplies the governing reason, although applying it here extends the rule beyond the clergy-led graduation prayer Lee decided. Silence answers compelled personal recitation; it does not answer sponsorship. Passing discussions of ceremonial references in earlier decisions do not settle this recurring elementary-school practice. Historical instruction may explain the Pledge and religious beliefs in earlier documents without requiring a current profession of them. His proposed relief preserves lawful patriotic exercises, genuinely private religious expression and references to religion in teaching; it would neither rewrite federal statutory wording nor depend on invented punishment, compulsory exit or individual psychological injury.

#### Source Notes

The [Seventh Circuit opinion, 980 F.2d 437](https://static.case.law/f2d/980/html/0437-01.html), including its concurrence and footnotes, supplies the challenged phrase, actual abstention arrangements, evidence concerning hazing, lower-court judgment and separate Attorney General dismissal. The [OpenJurist primary opinion text](https://openjurist.org/980/f2d/437) supplies the same public record. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is specified. Absence of a supported harassment finding is not a finding that no child could experience social pressure.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment for the school defendants stands, and the separate Attorney General dismissal remains undisturbed. No injunction against the program, damages award, new hearing or further Supreme Court event is ordered. Officials remain bound by the protected right to abstain and the limits stated in the Court's holdings.

**End of entry: Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21 of Wheeling Township, Merits decision, February 12, 1996.**

### United States v. Hale, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830

Merits decision, February 12, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below reported at 978 F.2d 1016.

On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment affirming thirteen machinegun-possession convictions under 18 U.S.C. §922(o) and three unregistered-firearm convictions under 26 U.S.C. §5861(d). The appellate decision issued October 20, 1992; rehearing was denied November 20, 1992. The questions concern congressional power, the asserted Second Amendment protection based on military usefulness, and the preserved registry-proof objections. Hale seeks reversal; the United States seeks affirmance. No companion case is consolidated with this matter.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The thirteen §922(o) convictions survive the commerce and Second Amendment challenges; the three §5861(d) convictions survive the presented constitutional-source challenge. Admission of the two ATF certifications, exclusion of the 1979 Senate material and refusal to take judicial notice of disputed registry unreliability are affirmed. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition only on the registration-authority and two evidentiary components. No conviction is vacated, and no new trial, resentencing or evidentiary hearing is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer throughout; Stone-Zsela as specified | Supports the complete affirmance | All five holdings have eight Associate joins; Stone-Zsela additionally joins holdings 1 and 3 |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Concurs in the judgment; also joins holdings 1 and 3 of the Court opinion | States a distinct individual-arms premise and functional automatic-fire distinction; adds no ground on the registration-authority, certification, or excluded-material components |

#### Holdings

##### 1. Possession controls enforce the actual machinegun-market restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit possession as an integral enforcement component of §922(o)'s restriction on the machinegun transfer and supply market. This authority rests on the actual commercial scheme, including its exceptions for a transfer to or by, or possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies, or political subdivisions, and for lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; it does not depend on adding an individual commerce element or on generalized crime costs.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined on this holding by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that local possession cannot be detached from the commercial prohibition it enforces. Restricting the machinegun supply and transfers would be undermined if newly made or unlawfully transferred weapons could be retained through possession outside that restriction. The grandfather exception helps define the lawful existing supply; the governmental-authority exception remains intact. Harris permits regulation of an economic class within its actual interstate scheme. Lopez rejects a different theory: remote consequences of stand-alone school-zone possession through crime, education and productivity. Hale's reliance on the local character of his possession therefore does not defeat this prohibition, and the absence of a separately enacted nexus element does not require the Court to insert one. The holding approves this integrated restriction, not every firearm prohibition or every regulation of an object that once crossed a state line.

##### 2. The registration requirement retains its independent tax foundation

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5861(d)'s registration requirement remains constitutionally supported insofar as it serves the National Firearms Act's genuine making-and-transfer tax system. The separate prohibition on new machinegun transfers does not, by itself, establish that the entire tax-registration system has lost its constitutional foundation.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with the affirmance of these counts without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Sonzinsky supplies the taxing foundation for a genuine firearms tax despite its regulatory effects; Freed recognizes the accompanying registration system. Those authorities require examination of that system's actual tax function rather than reliance on a federal police power. Hale's contention that prohibited weapons cannot be newly registered does not establish that the making-and-transfer tax system ceased to exist. The three registration counts therefore survive the constitutional-source objection presented here. The Court does not find that every prohibited transaction is registrable or taxable. An independently preserved claim that compliance was impossible for a particular weapon would require its own record and analysis; no rejected application, count-specific impossibility finding or separate due-process claim of that kind is established here.

##### 3. Military usefulness does not establish the asserted immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A weapon's military utility alone does not establish the claimed Second Amendment immunity from these federal restrictions. Miller does not make that characteristic sufficient to invalidate Hale's convictions.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined on this holding by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller examined the relationship between the weapon and preservation or efficiency of a well-regulated militia; it reversed a dismissal and remanded rather than affirming a conviction. Its inquiry does not yield the converse proposition that every weapon useful in war is immune from regulation. Hale supplies no evidence connecting this possession with preservation or efficiency of such a militia, and military usefulness alone does not make the necessary case. The Court rejects that asserted exemption without adopting the lower court's broad exclusively collective-right characterization. It does not settle the full scope of an individual right or the treatment of every rifle, semiautomatic firearm, suppressor or other regulated item.

##### 4. The certifications satisfy the governing confrontation inquiry on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The two authenticated ATF certifications reporting that a diligent search disclosed no application by Hale to register the weapons are admissible on the conceded firmly rooted negative-record exception and this administrative-record setting. The governing Confrontation Clause framework requires no additional showing of the certifying witnesses' unavailability on this record.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition of this objection.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts supplies the reliability inquiry; a genuinely firmly rooted exception can establish the required reliability. Wright preserves a constitutional inquiry distinct from a rule's label, so counsel's concessions concerning Rule 803(10) and authentication under Rule 902(1) do not waive every confrontation objection. Here those conceded predicates concern administrative negative-record proof, not the unsupported residual-exception reasoning rejected in Wright. Dutton and Inadi also prevent treating Roberts's former-testimony discussion as an unavailability requirement for every hearsay category. The certificates satisfy the applicable inquiry on this record. The Court does not approve every affidavit prepared for a prosecution or hold that all evidence admissible under a hearsay rule necessarily satisfies confrontation.

##### 5. Disputed registry criticism is not a judicially noticeable fact

**Holding and operative rule:** The trial court did not abuse its discretion by excluding the offered 1979 Senate material as stale hearsay or by refusing to notice its disputed assertions of registry unreliability. Rule 201 does not make contested factual conclusions indisputable merely because they appeared in a governmental hearing.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition of these objections.

**Controlling explanation:** Hale offered the earlier material for the truth of contested claims about the registry's reliability. The occurrence of the hearing does not establish the truth of every assertion made there. Ordinary hearsay and relevance requirements therefore remain applicable, and the age and character of this submission support its exclusion. The Court does not make registry accuracy conclusive or forbid competent, relevant proof of error. It holds only that this submission required neither admission nor judicial notice and thus establishes no ground to disturb these convictions.

**Questions not reached:** No preserved Staples instructional error, suppression claim, count-specific registration-impossibility claim or additional unspecified claim presented without counsel is decided. The absence of a proved error makes a harmless-error determination unnecessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: its bar on the stand-alone school-possession/downstream-effects theory and on inserting an absent commerce element remains intact; this actual market restriction is distinguished.
- United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995: its economic-class approach is applied to the machinegun supply and transfer scheme; its vehicle-specific statutory nexus is not added to §922(o).
- Sonzinsky v. United States, 300 U.S. 506: its genuine-tax foundation is applied; regulatory effects alone do not eliminate the tax power.
- United States v. Freed, 401 U.S. 601: its tax-registration framework is applied without deciding an unpresented impossible-compliance claim.
- United States v. Miller, 307 U.S. 174: its limited militia-related inquiry is applied; it does not establish automatic immunity for weapons with military utility or require the lower court's exclusively collective-right formulation.
- Ohio v. Roberts, 448 U.S. 56: its firmly rooted-exception reliability approach is applied to the conceded administrative negative-record proof.
- Dutton v. Evans, 400 U.S. 74: its rejection of an indiscriminate unavailability requirement is applied within the inherited confrontation framework.
- United States v. Inadi, 475 U.S. 387: its distinction between former testimony and other hearsay categories prevents a universal unavailability prerequisite here.
- Idaho v. Wright, 497 U.S. 805: its independent constitutional reliability requirement remains; the conceded firmly rooted category distinguishes this proof from its residual-exception setting.
- Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994: its knowledge-of-weapon-characteristics rule remains unchanged; its availability does not establish an instructional defect absent from the presented record.

#### Law After Decision

Section 922(o)'s possession control is sustained as part of its actual machinegun-market restriction, while the NFA's tax-registration ground remains independent. The arms holding rejects military usefulness as automatic immunity without resolving the complete individual-right question. The evidence holdings apply the existing confrontation and evidence rules to this conceded category and submission; they establish neither universal affidavit admissibility nor conclusive registry accuracy. The Court changes no rule governing the required mental state and adopts no new harmless-error test.

#### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs. He joins the market holding and the conclusion that Miller does not give military usefulness automatic immunity. He also would recognize a personal right to ordinary lawful arms while distinguishing these weapons' sustained automatic fire by function and relation to ordinary lawful civilian defense. Appearance or military ancestry alone would not draw his line. An ordinary handgun and these automatic weapons need not receive identical constitutional treatment. His explanation does not declare every rifle, semiautomatic firearm, suppressor or specially regulated item unprotected, and this federal prosecution presents no incorporation question. No other Justice joins that additional ground; it does not enlarge the Court's holding.

#### Source Notes

The [Eighth Circuit opinion, 978 F.2d 1016](https://static.case.law/f2d/978/html/1016-01.html), including its concurrence and footnotes, supplies the counts, posture, arguments, certifications, concessions and challenged evidentiary rulings; the [same judicial text at OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/978/f2d/1016) provides an additional public source. [Miller, 307 U.S. 174](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/307/174), supplies its actual disposition and limited arms inquiry. No separate Supreme Court docket, grant text or argument date is specified.

The [1994 Code, 18 U.S.C. §922(o)(2) and effective-date note](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title18/html/USCODE-1994-title18-partI-chap44-sec922.htm), supplies the complete governmental and pre-May 19, 1986 exceptions preserved in the market-scheme holding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit's affirmance stands. All thirteen machinegun-possession counts and all three registration counts remain undisturbed. The mandate returns through the ordinary appellate process and orders no new trial, resentencing, registration proceeding or evidentiary hearing. No further Supreme Court event is set.

### Behrens v. Pelletier, No. 94-1244

Merits decision, February 21, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 7, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's November 17, 1994 dismissal in No. 94-56507. The questions are whether an earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal bars a later appeal following denial of summary judgment, and which issues may receive immediate review. The first appeal did not adjudicate the job-termination claim because that claim then stood dismissed as time-barred. The District Court later reinstated it and denied summary judgment, stating that material factual issues remained. The Court of Appeals dismissed the second appeal under its categorical one-appeal rule.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Justices Stevens and Breyer.

Reversal reaches the Court of Appeals' categorical dismissal, not the District Court's summary-judgment denial. On remand the Ninth Circuit must determine whether a separable legal immunity issue is actually presented under the otherwise applicable collateral-order requirements. It must use the District Court's properly assumed facts favorable to the party opposing summary judgment and dismiss factual-sufficiency disputes and other unavailable issues. The decree neither awards immunity nor requires the entire appeal, or any particular asserted issue, to receive merits review. No new disposition of the separate United States negligence claim or earlier substitution ruling is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and IV | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | Reversal and remand | Rejection of numerical exhaustion; exact bounded decree |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Conditional legal/factual rule | Johnson's boundary within an otherwise authorized appeal; this part does not independently authorize successive review |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Conditional claim-specific rule | A different surviving claim does not itself defeat an otherwise authorized legal-immunity appeal; Stone-Zsela does not join |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Breyer | Stevens | Would affirm; joins Parts II and III | Importance and finality permit only one ordinary pretrial immunity appeal as of right; conditional agreement does not extend to Parts I and IV |

#### Holdings

##### 1. A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding and operative rule:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Seven directly adopt the rule and reversal of the categorical dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the character of the later order, not a numerical limit, determines whether §1291 permits immediate review. Cohen requires a conclusive, sufficiently important issue separate from the merits that would be effectively unreviewable after final judgment. Mitchell recognizes the qualified-immunity category for a legal question about immunity from suit. A complaint and a developed summary-judgment record can present different legal questions; a legal defense to a reinstated claim may not have been available on the first appeal. Here the first appellate decision did not adjudicate the job-termination claim because it then stood dismissed. That circumstance exposes the defect in automatic exhaustion without establishing Behrens's immunity. Concerns about duplication and delay are substantial, but they do not justify withholding review solely because an earlier appeal occurred when the later issue independently meets the existing category. Digital Equipment requires disciplined examination of the category and every collateral-order condition; it does not equate public immunity with any asserted right to avoid litigation. Johnson preserves the factual boundary, while the first mandate and ordinary restrictions continue to govern repetitive arguments. The Court therefore rejects the categorical rule and remands for examination of the actual issues. It does not hold that every second denial is appealable or determine that every issue Behrens presents is legal.

##### 2. An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding and operative rule:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the party opposing summary judgment in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this conditional legal/factual rule. Stevens and Breyer do not regard the condition of authorized successive review as satisfied here.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson distinguishes a legal question about the consequences of assumed conduct from a dispute about what the evidence establishes. Its boundary governs the second appeal just as it governs the first. The District Court's statement that material factual disputes remain does not itself demonstrate that every legal formulation is unavailable; conversely, calling a contention legal does not establish jurisdiction if it depends on selecting the official's disputed version. The Ninth Circuit must identify the proper assumptions and the actual legal issue without deciding the underlying employment conditions or regulatory facts. Kimberlin preserves review of a separable legal rule while excluding factual selection. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date precedent on an authorized immunity question, but supplies no independent jurisdiction or later notice. Swint forbids enlarging appellate authority for convenience; a legal issue does not carry unrelated factual disputes with it. The Court leaves actual issue sorting to the Court of Appeals. Its instructions do not resolve the factual merits, establish any property interest or constitutional violation, or award immunity.

##### 3. A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Eight directly adopt the conditional principle; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this part. Stevens and Breyer continue to reject a second ordinary appeal as of right.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell's protection concerns immunity from defending the claim to which immunity applies. Having to litigate a different claim does not erase that interest. Respondent's proposed categorical objection would deny legal-immunity review whenever any other claim survives, regardless of the separate defense and the distinct litigation burden. The Court rejects that inference while preserving every other jurisdictional requirement. Swint confirms the complementary limit: one proper appeal cannot justify review of a separate nonappealable matter merely because deciding both would save litigation. The present rule therefore gives no jurisdiction over the United States negligence claim, the earlier substitution ruling or unrelated issues concerning continuing interference. It also does not decide whether the actual claims and assumed facts here produce an appealable legal question; that inquiry remains for the Ninth Circuit under the Court's other holdings.

##### Limits and questions not reached

No immunity entitlement, constitutional violation, property interest, disputed regulatory condition, evidentiary sufficiency, damages or trial outcome is determined. No blanket jurisdiction over mixed orders or new answer to Swint's reserved question of genuinely necessary connected review is adopted. The holding does not authorize relitigation of an unchanged ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate; it does not announce a new absolute rule governing every conceivable repetitive-appeal circumstance. The existing restrictions continue to apply. No stay, certification, appellate permission or mandamus relief is granted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp.:** Applied as the source of the cumulative collateral-order conditions; rejection of numerical exhaustion does not remove importance, conclusiveness, separateness or effective-unreviewability requirements.
- **Mitchell v. Forsyth:** Applied to legal qualified-immunity review and immunity from suit. The decision makes clear that the category is not exhausted merely by an earlier pleading-stage appeal, while leaving each later issue to satisfy its requirements.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Applied without weakening its factual-sufficiency exclusion or its rules for properly assumed facts; it does not impose a numerical one-appeal ceiling.
- **Kimberlin v. Quinlan:** Its distinction between a separable legal rule and factual selection is retained; no general mixed-order jurisdiction is created.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its independent consideration of relevant conduct-date law governs an authorized legal issue; it creates no jurisdiction and permits no retrospective notice from later law.
- **Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct:** Its category and cumulative-condition discipline remains controlling; the public qualified-immunity category remains distinct from ordinary private settlement rights.
- **Swint v. Chambers County Commission:** Its prohibition on convenience-based expansion is applied. Review of a separate claim or factual dispute does not follow from one proper immunity appeal; the reserved connected-review question remains reserved.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District:** Its exclusion of purely factual-sufficiency appeals remains unchanged; the present decision concerns successive-stage legal review, not the supervisory-liability merits decided there.

#### Law After Decision

The existing legal-immunity appeal category cannot be restricted by an automatic rule allowing only one pretrial appeal. A later order must receive the ordinary jurisdictional analysis, on properly assumed facts and without evidentiary reweighing. Surviving different claims neither destroy otherwise qualifying claim-specific review nor become reviewable through it. The final-judgment rule, independent collateral-order conditions, prior mandate and ordinary preservation limits remain in force. No new substantive immunity or general interlocutory-appeal entitlement results.

#### Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Stevens.** They would affirm the jurisdictional dismissal. They join Parts II and III's conditional legal/factual and claim-specific rules but find no authorization for a second ordinary pretrial appeal as of right. Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require attention to importance and practical finality, not merely identification of another separable legal question. One pretrial immunity appeal adequately protects the sufficiently important interest in avoiding trial; repeated appeals can duplicate legal adjudication and delay resolution, while avoidance of additional discovery does not necessarily warrant another interruption. The reinstated termination claim presents a substantial argument for additional review, but does not justify expanding judge-made collateral review in this case. Mitchell's immunity-from-suit rationale does not require an appeal at each successive stage.

They would leave an exceptional later legal question to a separately authorized route. Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written statement that the order involves a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that immediate appeal may materially advance the litigation's termination. Application must be made within ten days, and the Court of Appeals may permit review in its discretion; applying alone creates no stay. No such certification or permission is established here. Sections 1292(e) and 2072(c) authorize rulemaking within §2072's substantive-rights limit; they are not themselves an operative rule permitting this appeal. The dissent favors that institutional route over another appeal as of right. It neither determines that every issue Behrens raises is factual nor decides his immunity.

#### Source Notes

The [petition and appended lower-court decisions](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385013_0579/micro_IA40385013_0579%2002.%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari_djvu.txt) supply the appellate dismissal, District Court reinstatement and summary-judgment order, later one-appeal certification, and the first appellate decision. The [petitioner's merits brief](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385013_0579/micro_IA40385013_0579%2006.%20Petitioners%20Brief_djvu.txt), [respondent's merits brief](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385013_0579/micro_IA40385013_0579%2007.%20Respondents%20Brief_djvu.txt), and [United States amicus brief](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385013_0579/micro_IA40385013_0579%2009.%20Amicus%20Curiae%20Brief_djvu.txt) state the competing legal/factual and surviving-claim arguments. Disputed employment and regulatory assumptions remain disputed; the decision does not resolve the materiality of every asserted fact or definitively classify every appellate issue. [Mitchell, 472 U.S. 511](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/511), supplies the inherited legal-immunity appeal rule.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit to identify the District Court's relevant assumptions and determine which, if any, asserted issues present an otherwise reviewable legal immunity question. The court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the party opposing summary judgment where necessary under Johnson, but may not reweigh evidence or choose the official's disputed account. It must dismiss factual-sufficiency disputes and other unavailable issues. The summary-judgment denial is not itself reversed, no immunity is awarded, and unrelated claims and the first mandate retain their lawful force. No further Supreme Court event or new stay is set.

**End of entry: Behrens v. Pelletier, Merits decision, February 21, 1996.**

### Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner, No. 94-1239

Merits decision, February 21, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 325. Argued October 31, 1995.

On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of North Carolina. The question is whether North Carolina may tax residents' corporate shares more heavily as the issuing corporation's North Carolina income-tax exposure decreases, and whether a compensatory-tax defense sustains that distinction. Fulton seeks reversal and appropriate tax relief; the State defends the differential as compensation for its corporate income tax.

The intangibles levy is twenty-five cents per $100 of share value, reduced according to the issuer's income apportioned to North Carolina. For a corporate shareholder the linked dividend-deduction proportion determines the reduction without the ordinary $15,000 dividend-deduction cap. The intermediate appellate court invalidated the discrimination, severed the deduction and denied a refund. Both parties sought review. The state Supreme Court sustained the tax on December 9, 1994, leaving refunds and fees undecided. The claim concerns 1990 taxes. No companion matter is consolidated here.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The State's compensatory-tax defense fails. State courts must resolve severability and constitutionally adequate tax relief. The Court orders no fixed refund, blanket stock exemption, extension of the deduction, retrospective assessment or fee award. The intermediate court's earlier severability and no-refund ruling is not prescribed as the remedy.

#### Opinions and Holdings

Justice Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This unanimous opinion controls all five holdings and their explanations below.

##### 1. The issuer-based deduction discriminates against interstate investment

**Holding and operative rule:** A State discriminates against interstate commerce when the tax on a resident's otherwise comparable corporate shares increases as the issuer's local income-tax exposure decreases. Neither the shareholder's local residence nor drafting the geographic advantage as a deduction removes that discrimination, and proof that a particular issuer already lost an investment is unnecessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the tax classification itself creates a geographic investment preference. Two otherwise comparable holdings receive different treatment because their issuers conduct different proportions of activity taxable in North Carolina. The local shareholder is the person assessed, but the classification rewards investment connected to local corporate activity and burdens the competing interstate choice. Oregon Waste supplies the rule that an express geographic differential is discriminatory despite the government's fiscal objective. West Lynn directs attention to the linked operation rather than the label attached to its favorable side. The State cannot answer that objection merely by noting that securities movement or a lost purchaser has not been proved. Its compensatory defense must meet the distinct requirements of the governing tax cases.

##### 2. Corporate income and share ownership are not shown to be equivalent taxable events

**Holding and operative rule:** A compensatory-tax defense requires the State to identify the actual intrastate burden being offset, show a roughly equivalent and nonexcessive interstate charge, and establish substantially equivalent taxable events reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies. North Carolina's corporate-income and shareholder-property taxes do not satisfy those requirements merely because corporate profits may influence share value; the failure to establish equivalent events independently defeats the defense.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste states the cumulative requirements; Maryland v. Louisiana requires identification of the actual local burden; Armco makes the absence of equivalent events independently fatal. The State's strongest argument treats both levels of taxation together to avoid an added burden on corporations already contributing locally. But the income tax concerns the corporation's generation of income and the shareholder tax concerns ownership of a valuable asset. A relationship between profits and asset price does not make those events interchangeable proxies. Henneford preserves genuine complementary taxation of equivalent events, not every proposed fiscal offset. The Court does not hold that different nominal taxpayers always make compensation impossible, or that income and property taxes can never coexist. It holds that this defense has not established the required equivalence.

##### 3. Typical ratios and aggregate contributions do not prove the matching burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A State does not establish the compensatory defense's actual-burden and approximate-equivalence requirements merely by invoking typical price/earnings ratios or aggregate corporate tax contributions. North Carolina's comparison fails to demonstrate the required matching burden on this record, an additional ground for rejecting its defense.

**Controlling explanation:** Associated Industries requires examination of the relevant local counterpart rather than compensation attributed to transactions elsewhere or to broader totals. The same discipline applies here: an ordinary relationship between annual income and stock value cannot establish that the relevant corporate burden roughly matches, and is not less than, the differential placed on the competing investment. The state court's comparison explains its reasoning; it is neither a statutory command nor a finding of equivalence for every taxpayer. Its discussion of a 7.75% corporate rate also notes the earlier 7% rate applicable before 1991, and this claim concerns 1990. The Court supplies no new universal numerical finding. It rejects the sufficiency of the State's demonstration under the existing test, independently of the mismatch between taxable events.

##### 4. Darnell's contrary Commerce Clause permission is overruled

**Holding and operative rule:** Darnell v. Indiana is overruled to the extent it permits discriminatory taxation of residents' shares in out-of-state corporations under the Commerce Clause merely by reference to a different corporate-level local tax and an asserted substantial equality. Its distinct equal-protection and standing determinations are not erased.

**Controlling explanation:** Darnell's substantial-equality shortcut cannot be reconciled with the intervening requirements of actual matching burdens and substantially equivalent events. The present Court must state that conflict openly because the State and the court below relied on the earlier decision. Oregon Waste and Associated Industries give the developed compensatory doctrine its operative content; retaining Darnell's contrary permission would deny those requirements independent force. Approval under a rational-basis inquiry does not substitute for the Commerce Clause test. Lower courts were not required to anticipate this Court's express action. Concrete reliance and administration concerns receive their proper treatment through lawful remedial choices; they do not justify continued discrimination. The overruling reaches only the incompatible Commerce Clause ground.

##### 5. Invalidity requires lawful remedial proceedings, not a predetermined refund

**Holding and operative rule:** Reversal of the tax's constitutional justification requires remand for state-law severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When a State compelled payment before a meaningful opportunity to contest the tax, McKesson requires adequate backward-looking correction; independently, Reich forbids withdrawing a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after payment and substituting an unpursued prepayment route.

**Controlling explanation:** McKesson permits lawful refunds, constitutionally permissible equalizing retrospective treatment, or a lawful combination where backward-looking relief is required. It does not select one remedy for every invalid tax. Reich protects a clear postpayment remedy against withdrawal after collection, including where the payment was voluntary; reasonable previously announced requirements and prospective procedural changes with fair notice remain permissible. The lower courts must determine the relevant payments, preserved claims, applicable procedures and lawful state choices. The state Supreme Court expressly left refunds and fees undecided. Its reversal therefore neither fixes a sum nor makes the intermediate court's earlier deduction severance and denial of refund this Court's decree. The Court orders no automatic extension or elimination of the deduction, assessment against others, interest or fee award. Other constitutional theories not properly presented remain undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality, April 4, 1994: its geographic-discrimination rule and three cumulative compensatory-tax requirements are applied to this share-investment preference.
- Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman, May 23, 1994: its requirement of an actual matching burden, rather than aggregate offsets, is applied; its distinction between invalidity and lawful relief remains.
- West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy, June 17, 1994: its examination of a linked fiscal arrangement informs the classification, without making every tax relationship compensatory or deciding every subsidy.
- Maryland v. Louisiana, 451 U.S. 725: its requirement to identify the actual intrastate burden remains a necessary part of the compensatory defense.
- Armco Inc. v. Hardesty, 467 U.S. 638: its equivalent-events requirement supplies an independently sufficient reason to reject the defense.
- Henneford v. Silas Mason Co., 300 U.S. 577: genuinely complementary taxation of equivalent events remains permissible; the decision does not support this unsupported income/ownership offset.
- Darnell v. Indiana, 226 U.S. 390 (1912): its contrary Commerce Clause permission based on a different corporate tax and substantial equality is expressly overruled; its distinct standing and equal-protection treatment is left intact.
- McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco, 496 U.S. 18: its conditional requirement of adequate backward-looking correction governs remedial proceedings while preserving lawful alternatives.
- Reich v. Collins, December 6, 1994: its prohibition on withdrawing a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after collection governs any applicable remedial route; it does not establish a fixed refund or bar lawful prospective change.

#### Law After Decision

The compensatory-tax requirements now apply to North Carolina's issuer-based intangibles-tax preference: a resident investor's location does not shield discrimination directed at the interstate investment choice. Corporate income and share ownership have not been shown to be equivalent events, and general fiscal contribution does not establish the necessary actual offset. Darnell no longer authorizes the contrary Commerce Clause treatment. Ordinary nondiscriminatory corporate and property taxation remains available. Severability and tax relief remain governed by state law subject to federal nondiscrimination and due process; no universal refund formula or new tax is imposed.

#### Source Notes

The [North Carolina Supreme Court opinion, 338 N.C. 472](https://static.case.law/nc/338/html/0472-01.html), supplies the tax mechanism, 1990 claim, rate qualification, lower-court proceedings and reservation of refunds and fees. [Darnell, 226 U.S. 390](https://static.case.law/us/226/html/0390-01.html), decided December 23, 1912, supplies the earlier tax comparison and distinct standing limits. The [official report at 516 U.S. 325](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-325.pdf) supplies the public caption, docket and argument and decision dates. The state court's generalized price/earnings comparison is not a finding that every application imposes equal burdens.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the North Carolina Supreme Court for proceedings consistent with the invalidity of the discriminatory tax. State courts must determine severability and the appropriate constitutionally adequate remedy, including the applicable payment, preservation and procedural conditions. No refund amount, retrospective assessment, deduction change or fee disposition is fixed by this mandate, and the intermediate appellate remedy is not automatically reinstated as this Court's command. No further Supreme Court event is set.

**End of entry: Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner, Merits decision, February 21, 1996.**
