## Facts and judgment below

Bank loaned $5,068.75 under a November28,1989 unsecured consumer note payable in twenty-four installments, with default acceleration, collection fees, and contractual setoff. Strumpf filed Chapter13 January25,1991, with $11,279.86 in the checking account and loan in default. JA18a–19a,32a; Fourth Circuit37F.3d155,156. The plan supplied $100 monthly wages for forty-eight months, attorney/trustee classes, and pro rata unsecured claims; it did not name Citizens or expressly address its setoff right. JA3a–4a. The confirmation hearing was scheduled March20; the **written confirmation order was entered April8**. JA7a,10a–11a. Distinguish hearing/payment commencement and order entry dates.

Bank filed its stay-relief/setoff motion October7,1991. JA1a,15a–22a. A **bank counsel letter dated October2**, JA26a, says an administrative hold up to $3,500 has been placed with filing of the enclosed motion, pending its outcome. Debtor's October9 motion expressly alleges an October2 hold, JA24a. Lower opinions and bank's merits statement describe the hold and October7 filing as simultaneous; debtor's merits brief pp3,16 insists the motion came five days after the hold. The actual documentary distinction should be retained; don't turn an advocate's timing description into an undisputed finding. The hold was limited to $3,500, and the bank says the then-total balance was $7,206.59, leaving the remainder withdrawable (Bank Br.4, citing JA26a, whose letter itself supplies only the cap).

Motion requests $2,877.10 plus $382.58 attorney fees, totaling **$3,259.68**, and $1.15 daily interest from October15 through order date. JA16a. Lower opinions instead state **$3,250.48** due at hold date. JA32a n.1/37F.3d156. Keep these source attributions; don't silently equate them. Debtor raises excess hold and future-accrual objections (Br.16–17 n.6). Bank's proof-of-claim amount is described by both merits statements as $2,555.50, filedJune13. JA13a trustee's September notice lists Citizens 'not filed'/unsecured; don't infer that this notice adjudicated the actual proof of claim invalid.

Debtor moved October9 for contempt and October31 for release/turnover. The court heard the bank's motion November4 and reserved ruling, then heard debtor's motion November13. The written sanctions order datedNovember20, enteredNovember21, awards $500 counsel fees, $375 punitive damages and $25 nominal damages under then-§362(h). JA27a–28a. The separate order **datedNovember25, enteredDecember2** grants stay relief and authorizes the requested setoff with per-diem interest/fees. JA29a–30a. District and circuit opinions report the bank lifted the hold following the adverse ruling and Strumpf removed all funds before it could use that authorization. Bank states the setoff authorization was not appealed (Br.6; Reply4).

District court reversed the stay-violation finding and sanctions: 138B.R.792 (decisionMarch27, filedMarch30,1992). Fourth Circuit, 37F.3d155 (October13,1994), reversed and remanded to reinstate the bankruptcy decision **except any contempt determination**, which Strumpf had not appealed; it treated contempt language as surplusage to §362(h). It held the hold tantamount to setoff under(a)(7), relying on Reynolds, and upheld §362(h) sanctions using intentional conduct with knowledge of the stay as its willfulness rule. These are the challenged lower holdings, not this Court's law. JA39a–46a/AppendixA complete text. The present case concerns the sanctions judgment, not a fresh determination that Citizens may now recover depleted deposits.

## Every live statutory branch

1. **§362(a)(7)** stays setoff of prepetition debt owing to the debtor against a claim against the debtor. The bank distinguishes temporary refusal to pay pending judicial determination from completed setoff; it invokes decision, accomplishing action, and recordation as setoff elements under Baker and cites Maryland debtor-creditor principles. The debtor and circuit focus on loss of access and the automatic stay. Bank Br.8–11; Reply6–8; debtor Br.12.
2. **§362(a)(3)** separately prohibits obtaining possession of estate property or exercising control over it. Debtor acknowledges deposited money belongs to the bank but contends the right of withdrawal is estate property and the hold controls that right. Bank answers that the estate inherits the contractual claim with existing setoff limits, and §542(b) permits withholding to that extent. Debtor Br.12–14; Reply8–10. This cannot disappear merely because the(a)(7) issue is answered.
3. **§362(a)(6)** separately prohibits acts to collect, assess, or recover a prepetition claim. Debtor contends hold is collection; bank argues limited preservation without coercive collection and distinguishes account freezes coupled with reaffirmation pressure or postpetition funds. Debtor Br.14–16; Reply10–13. No coercion beyond this hold should be invented.
4. **§542(b)** requires payment of matured/on-demand/on-order estate debts, except to the extent they may be offset under§553, subject also to(c)/(d). The parties dispute whether this exception operates pending decision or requires prior relief. Those exceptions concern good-faith payment without bankruptcy notice and automatic life-insurance transfers; neither is this bank's asserted route. The subsection concerns a debt, distinct from turnover of tangible estate property under(a).
5. **§553(a)** preserves existing nonbankruptcy mutual prepetition setoff rights subject to its own exceptions and§§362/363. It creates no general new setoff entitlement. Both briefs address interplay with§506(a) secured treatment and§363 cash collateral/adequate protection. Stay relief under§362(d), emergency relief under(f), and the then-effective(e) hearing sequence are available judicial routes; possibility of relief is not an existing prior order.
6. **§1327(a) confirmation argument:** Debtor says confirmation and failure to object bound Citizens to unsecured treatment. Bank Reply2–6 says the argument was not raised below, outside the question presented, no cross-petition, and foreclosed by the unappealed later setoff order; alternatively it relies on§553 and the plan's silence about setoff. The preservation objection is itself contested advocacy; it is supported by the lower opinions' lack of an actual ruling on this ground, not an exhaustive audit of every filing. A merits decision on this alternative needs that procedural gate addressed.

Then-§362(h) requires an individual injured by a willful violation to recover actual damages including costs/fees and permits punitive damages in appropriate circumstances. Whether there is a violation precedes remedies; the circuit's statement of its willfulness test does not establish a Supreme Court rule. Bank also raises constitutional avoidance concerning destruction of setoff rights; debtor disputes any taking because judicial control preserves rather than extinguishes rights. Bank Br.2–3 and summary; debtorBr.6. Do not independently decide a takings claim without examining the full argument/preservation.

## Sufficiency and limits

These sources support a neutral presentation of the account hold and all three stay clauses, with the actual lower judgment and remedial distinction. Exact hold date and debt components must remain attributed unless independently resolved. The strongest precision limits are **temporary purpose, promptness of judicial application, amount held versus valid setoff, and ultimate court control**; this record does not support blanket authorization of indefinite freezes, postpetition setoff, coercive collection, or withholding sums beyond an available right.

No missing-record conclusion is made: IA has the primary record. If a proposed holding turns on an exact five-day delay, preservation of§1327, calculation of excess hold, or an independent constitutional claim, finish the petition, both complete merits briefs/reply and relevant record materials before adjudicating that premise. For the general question under the three stay clauses, those matters can be identified as limits rather than invented away. Relevant entering-law reading may additionally need **Barnhill v.Johnson** (deposit/check debtor-creditor analysis; state-law property/federal transfer distinction) and **Nobelman** (Chapter13 statutory reading) if the model actually uses them; neither settles the present stay question.
