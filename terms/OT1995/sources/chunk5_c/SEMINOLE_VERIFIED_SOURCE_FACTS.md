## Verified procedural facts

Seminole sued Florida and Governor Chiles, September19,1991, S.D.Fla. The court denied immunity dismissal June18,1992 (801F.Supp.655). Florida's collateral appeal was consolidated below with Poarch Band's Alabama appeals; only Seminole is this docket. January18,1994 appellate judgment reverses the Florida denial and remands for dismissal; affirms Alabama dismissals. Petition invokes §1254(1). Petition questions address Indian-Commerce abrogation and prospective officer relief under Young.

Separately, September22,1993 district summary judgment found for Florida on good-faith negotiations. Its separate appeal, Eleventh Circuit No.93-5256, was stayed pending immunity review. Petitioner merits footnote1 pp8–9 and petition p2 document this. Neither an immunity ruling nor claimed bad faith establishes the final merits of negotiations.

Petition describes Tribe request January1991, proposed compact March4,1991, State response May24 and later discussions. It sought a 60-day compact order and then mediator, plus declaration on scope of games to negotiate. Complaint allegations remain allegations.

Lower court: no express consent, no plan-of-convention tribal consent under pre-divergence Blatchford, no Parden conduct waiver merely from negotiations. Congressional intent to abrogate was sufficiently clear from §2710(d)(7)(A)(i), but Indian Commerce power was held insufficient. Union Gas was distinguished as interstate-commerce and private-market activity; the lower court did not itself overrule Supreme Court authority. It rejected Young on discretionary compact terms/decision whether to negotiate and real-party-State grounds. These are lower grounds, not entering Supreme Court holdings.

Footnote2 expressly declines the Tenth Amendment defense because raised first on appeal. A new anti-commandeering disposition requires independent lawful reach; New York does not remove that posture question. Lower PartV preserves IGRA remainder through §2721 and states that, after immunity dismissal, a Tribe may notify Secretary for procedures. Actual §2710(d)(7)(B)(vii) has prior judicial/mediator and nonconsent predicates. That mismatch must remain visible; no independent administrative bypass is established merely by immunity dismissal.

Material circuit conflict exists: Cheyenne River Sioux Tribe v. South Dakota,3F.3d273 (8th Cir.1993), holds contrary on IGRA abrogation. The runtime's no-conflict assertion is inaccurate.
