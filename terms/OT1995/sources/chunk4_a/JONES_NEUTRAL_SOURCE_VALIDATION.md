## Verified posture and a fuller neutral account

The petition bears an October 25, 1995 filing stamp. Its final certificate says respondents received the original on October 17 and the corrected forty-page petition on December 9, following the Deputy Clerk's direction. The four-page-ish cover list names many media organizations and personnel as well as public officials. These are allegations and asserted party designations, not proof that every named person was properly joined below.

The appended June 14, 1995 district memorandum, No.4:94CV1192 CDP, pp.1–8, supplies the actual underlying disposition. It dismisses with prejudice a §1985(3)/§1986 case against more than 300 media defendants. Its stated grounds are (1) the complaint alleges individual failures to publicize Jones's lawsuits without material facts showing agreement among defendants, (2) no constitutional or statutory right requires media defendants to publish a chosen story, and (3) §1986 is derivative of a §1985 violation and does not itself provide a procedure for adding parties without complying with ordinary rules. Personal-jurisdiction and service objections were expressly left undecided as unnecessary. Requests for default, service costs, sanctions and joinder were denied. These are the lower court's legal conclusions, not already affirmed Supreme Court propositions.

The same memorandum explains refusal to recuse: Jones had sued the district's judges, then sued an outside judge and the official who assigned that judge; the court invoked the rule of necessity and cited United States v. Will's related body of law through the authorities reproduced there. The packet must preserve the distinction between being named in a suit and an independently established disqualifying interest. No automatic recusal of this Court follows. The appended transfer order directs reassignment of six cases, including ABC-TV, to Judge Perry. That document is material to Jones's allegation that Perry simply took the case without assignment.

The appended Eighth Circuit order, No.95-2785, dated August 8, 1995, says the court reviewed the original district file, denied pending motions, and affirmed under Eighth Circuit Rule47A(a). Its September 19 order denied the listed clarification and stay motions. A district filing stamp on the first order is not its decision date. This review history does not establish the truth of Jones's allegation that he received no appellate review; it does identify his distinct objection to disposition without the requested briefing process.

The petition's strongest particular access and procedure allegations must be considered independently of its sweeping assertions of a twenty-year conspiracy. It challenges lower-court leave requirements for paid as well as unpaid filings; alleges that courthouse visits trigger marshal escort without explained cause; disputes the refusal to allow appellate briefing; challenges refusal to recuse; and argues that §1986 permits adding persons with knowledge and preventive power without a Rule15 motion. The attached lower orders support the existence of filing and party-addition controls, but not Jones's asserted conclusion that every federal and state court is closed to him. His claims about escorts and broad conspiracies remain unproved allegations. His financial and copying-cost objections are set out at printed pp.28,34–36 and38–39; the generalized conspiracy questions occupy the introductory questions and the corresponding twenty-nine reasons.

The competing source-grounded position relies on the actual present submission: the lack of facts of a media agreement, the asserted entitlement to compulsory publication, the attempt to use §1986 as a joinder device, and any failure to identify an arguable challenge to a dispositive ground. It cannot rest merely on the number of earlier filings or the district's adjectives. Conversely, ordinary failure to state a claim is not automatically legal or factual frivolousness; a supported access objection must be assessed on its own terms. Nothing in this supplement adjudicates that assessment.

## Financial showing and precise procedural text

At printed p.28 the petition says Jones began receiving $391 monthly SSI and $278 Veterans Affairs benefits in March1994. At pp.34–36 it alleges medical inability to work, payment of earlier litigation expenses from benefits, loans for copying, and approximately $10,000 copying/mailing expense for seven petitions. Its service list refers to a motion to pay $1,500 in fees at $100 monthly, based on total monthly income of $669, and says “Petitioner's affidavit herewith.” The financial affidavit and the motion itself are absent from the reproduced file. The petition's financial assertions and its reference to a missing affidavit do not establish the affidavit's contents, current assets, obligations or statutory eligibility. The listed $1,500 is not the Rule38 fee for this single case: Rule38(a) sets $300 for docketing a case.

Rule39.1 requires a motion and notarized affidavit or §1746 declaration in FRAP Form4, stating whether leave was sought and granted elsewhere. Its appointed-counsel exception requires qualifying statutory appointment and citation; none is established here. Rule39.2 requires those papers with the filing, original and ten copies unless an unrepresented confined inmate (original alone); incarceration is not inferred from the petition's account of past imprisonment. Rule39.3 permits Rule33.2 form with stated exceptions and requires legibility; due allowance for pro se presentation does not cure substantive or jurisdictional untimeliness. Rule39.4 provides docketing without fees upon presentation of the required documents and service proof. Rule39.5 permits a respondent to challenge the financial motion. Rule39.8: “If satisfied that a petition for a writ of certiorari, jurisdictional statement, or petition for an extraordinary writ is frivolous or malicious, the Court may deny leave to proceed in forma pauperis.” “May” remains discretionary.

Rule33.1 ordinarily requires booklet form, forty copies, and a thirty-page certiorari petition, with specified excluded pages and a good-cause excess-page application. Rule33.2 permits the 8½-by-11-inch format and a forty-page certiorari petition, subject to the same listed exclusions. The December correction to forty pages therefore does not itself demonstrate a rule violation. Rule48 makes the July26,1995 Rules effective October2,1995; after that date they govern except when application to a pending matter would be infeasible or unjust, in which event former procedure applies.

Then-effective §1915(a) permits commencement, prosecution, defense or appeal without prepayment by a person who makes an affidavit of inability to pay, describing the action and belief in entitlement to redress. A trial court's written certification that an appeal is not in good faith is distinct. Section1915(d) permits requested counsel and dismissal if poverty is untrue or the action frivolous or malicious. Sections1915(b),(c),(e) retain their separate record-expense, officer-process, remedies and costs functions. No later PLRA provision is used.

Their distinct current-paper authority is preserved: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined Martin's companion fee denial; Stevens dissented from that component, while joining the lead grant and prospective refusal. O'Connor authored Denton's controlling factual-screening rule joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; Stevens agreed with that factual standard while dissenting from the additional remand. Breyer does not participate (objective fact separately checked by control from 516U.S.364); no reason is supplied.

**Standards materially implicated:** “Factual frivolousness and review of fee-screening dismissals,” present operation Denton, May4,1992; “Private-conspiracy deprivation claims under §1985(3),” present operation Bray, January13,1993, only if assessing that ground's arguability; and “Objective appearance of judicial impartiality under §455(a),” present operation Liteky, March7,1994, only if assessing the recusal contention. The copied entries preserve the exact current rule and authority. No earlier historical Bray or Liteky rationale displaces their actual current formulations. In particular, the independent appearance standard controls, ordinary adverse rulings alone do not establish partiality, and a judicial-source exceptional-intensity requirement is not Court law. Neitzke's surviving distinction between legal frivolousness and ordinary pleading failure informs the fee inquiry; Denton's statutory appellate remedial rules are not imported wholesale into Rule39.

## Paths and remaining source limits

1. A current-paper Rule39.8 denial requires an articulated ground in the actual petition, with any permitted fee/form opportunity expressly specified. It does not itself deny certiorari. That path need not invent a financial finding favorable to Jones if its independent frivolousness prerequisite is supported.
2. A grant based on inability to prepay requires the missing financial motion and prescribed affidavit/declaration, or an independently established lawful exception. The present source packet does not verify that predicate. A limited request for or deferral pending those materials is distinct from a merits decision or a finding that he can pay.
3. Any future condition requires its own applicant-specific, category-specific supporting record and explanation why ordinary individual treatment is inadequate. Pre-divergence McDonald/Sindram and surviving Zatko authority permit properly supported controls; current Martin/Day/Sassower/Anderson/Attwood law supplies no categorical bar, warning prerequisite, number threshold or obligation to exhaust every imagined alternative. The historic Court filing totals, denials and warnings are not established current orders. The available lower orders cannot be relabeled Supreme Court warnings.
4. If an independently authorized certiorari event is considered, denial creates no merits holding or poll disclosure. This packet supplies the present petition and attached lower judgments; it does not supply the nineteen separately referenced appendix volumes. A petition-stage model must preserve its own channel, source limits and any required separate Stone certiorari commitment; a fee-only event must not silently decide it.

**Remaining blocker:** the financial motion and Form4 affidavit/declaration (including present financial particulars) are missing. The extensive separately referenced appendix volumes are also not in the retrieved case item; their absence limits any affirmative merits assessment that depends on their alleged proof. These gaps do not authorize inventing financial eligibility, adverse findings, or a new Supreme Court filing history.
