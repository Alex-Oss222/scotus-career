MEMORANDUM AND ORDER 


This matter is before the Court on numerous motions filed by 
Order Ehtered at .the on of the Court: various parties, including motions to dismiss filed by all 
\ichtal § defendants who have been properly served. 


Clerk, U.S. Court of = Eighth Circuit Plaintiff’s pro se amended complaint in this case names as 
defendants various television networks, television shows, 
newspapers, magazines, broadcast networks, entertainment 
production companies, and various individuals associated with 
those entities, all of whom are involved in local or national 
media in some form or another. There are over 300 named 
defendants. Additionally, plaintiff has filed a variety of 
motions for joinder and "notices" of additional defendants, 


although no additional defendants have actually been joined. 


Plaintiff’s amended complaint alleges that it is brought 
pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3) and § 1986. It alleges that 
these media defendants’ “inactions, indecisions, and omissions, 
ignored plaintiff’s request by the deliberate failure to respond, 
7 which conduct aided and abetted the cover up to conceal” various 
“crimes” committed by federal court officials against the 
piaintiff. The “crimes” that plaintiff alleges these defendants 


APPENDIX 


aided and abetted appear to be court rulings on various cases 
filed by the plaintiff in this and other federal courts. He 
essentially alleges that the media defendants should be held 
liable to him because, when presented with “evidence” that the 
federal courts were not handling plaintiff’s claims as he deemed 
appropriate, these media defendants failed to publicize 
plaintiff’s cases. He also includes general allegations that the 
media defendants have demonstrated racial bias in their handling 
of criminal or civil trials and investigations relating to 0.J. 
Simpson, Michael Jackson, Paula Jones, President Clinton and 
Michael Fay. 

Section 1985(3) prohibits conspiracies to deprive persons of 
constitutional rights based upon their race. In order to recover 


under this section from purely private actors, such as defendants 
here, 


a plaintiff must show, inter alia, (1) that “some 
racial, or perhaps otherwise class-based, invidiously 
discriminatory animus [lay] behind the conspirators’ 
actions,” Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88, 102, 91 
S. Ct. 1790, 1798, 29 L.Ed.2d 338 (1971), and (2) that 
the conspiracy “aimed at interfering with rights" that 
are “protected against private, as well as official, 
encroachment,” Carpenters v. Scott, 463 U.S. 825, 833, 
103 S. Ct. 3352, 3358, 77 L.Ed.2d 1049 (1983). 


Bray v. Alexandria Women’s Health Clinic, 113 S. Ct. 753 (1993). 


Thus, to state a claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3) a plaintiff must 
allege 


(1) A conspiracy; (2) for the purpose of depriving any 
person or class of persons of the equal protection of 
the laws or of the privileges and immunities secured by 
the law; (3) an act in furtherance of the conspiracy; 
and (4) a resulting injury to plaintiff’s person or 
property or a deprivation of a right or privilege 


secured for her as a United States citizen. 


Russell _y. City of Overland Police Department, 838 F. Supp. 1350, 


1353 (E.D. Mo. 1993). “A claim of conspiracy must be pleaded 
with particularity and specifically demonstrate with material 
facts that defendants reached an agreement to violate plaintiff’s 
rights." Id., 838 F. Supp. at 1353, citing City of Omaha 
Employees Betterment Association vy. City of Omaha, 883 F.2d 650, 
652 (8th Cir. 1989). Section 1985, however, confers no 
independent substantive rights and plaintiff therefore must plead 
and prove the violation of an independent constitutional right in 


order to recover under this claim. See United Brotherhood of 
Carpenters & Joiners vy. Scott, 463 U.S. 825, 832-34 (1983). For 


a person to recover on either a section 1981 or a section 1985(3) 


claim, purposeful discrimination must be established. McIntosh 
vy. Arkansas Republican Party-Frank White Election Committee, 766 
F.2d 337, 340 (8th Cir. 1985). 

Section 1986 provides a remedy against certain persons who 
have the power to prevent a violation of § 1985 and fail to do 
so. The section does not provide any independent cause of 
action; a violation of § 1985 must exist before any claim under § 
1986 may arise. See McIntosh, aupra, 766 F.2d at 340. 

Initially, the Court notes that plaintiff has brought 
numerous cases in this Court; at least two of the more recent of 
those cases directly challenge the court actions which he alleges 
the defendants in this case failed to publicize. See Jones Vv. 
Rehbnaguist, No. 4:94CV739 FIG, (E.D. Mo. Order of Dismissal dated 


January 23, 1995); Jones y. Gaitan, No. 4:95CV222 CDP (E.D. Mo. 
Order dated May 8, 1995). Thus, plaintiff’s direct challenges to 


the courts’ actions have been dismissed for failure to state a 
Claim. Additionally, because of plaintiff’s continued abuse of 
the judicial system, this Court has entered two en banc orders 
prohibiting this plaintiff from filing additional cases on either 
a fee paid or an jin forma pauperis basis, unless he has complied 
with certain requirements. See In re Sylvester Jones, Case No. 
4:95MC86 CDP (E.D. Mo. Order of Court En Banc dated April 10, 
1995). | 

Even liberally construing plaintiff’s complaint, he has 
failed to state a cause of action against any of the named media 
defendants in this case, under any theory. Plaintiff essentially 
argues that these defendants violated his rights by refusing to 
publicize his frivolous lawsuits. Plaintiff’s complaint that 
these defendants engaged in a racially-based conspiracy fails to 
allege any factual basis for the conclusory allegations. Indeed, 
although he uses the word “conspiracy”, his only factual 
allegation is that each of the individual media defendants, when 
allegedly approached and told of the Court’s mistreatment of 
plaintiff, failed to publicize the actions. Thus, if his 
complaint is read as a whole, he alleges that each defendant 
individually took actions with which he disagrees; he has failed 
to allege any facts showing that any of the media defendants 
conspired with one another to violate any of his rights. 

Moreover, under § 1985(3) plaintiff must allege that the 


-4- 


defendants engaged in a conspiracy to deprive him of equal 
protection of the laws or of the privileges and immunity secured 
by the laws. Merely alleging that media defendants conspired to 
engage in discriminatory news coverage is not sufficient to state 
a claim under § 1985(3), and plaintiff’s allegations do not even 
rise to that level. There is no constitutional or statutory 
right of action that would require a television progran, 
newspaper, or news magazine to publish articles or air stories 
which it chooses not to publish. Plaintiff’s mere 
dissatisfaction with the news coverage given his cases does not 
give rise to a cause of action under any theory he has alleged 
here.’ | 

Additionally, plaintiff’s various motions for sanctions and 
for leave to join defense counsel as defendants in this case will 
be denied. From a thorough review of the court file it is 
abundantly clear that none of the attorneys have done any more 
than what is required of them in their ethical responsibilities 
of representing their clients. 

Plaintiff has also attempted to join additional defendants 
by simply filing a “notice” pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1986 naming 
persons he contends are additional defendants. It appears that 
despite the fact that these proposed defendants have never been 
properly joined, some of them have been served with process or

has also moved for entry of default, for sanctions, and for costs

Neither Rule 4, Fed. R. Civ. P., nor 42 U.S.C. § 1986 
provides a means for a plaintiff to obtain service of process 


over persons who are not properly joined to the action. As 
plaintiff’s complaint fails to state a claim against any properly 


joined or proposed defendant, and as his various motions relating 
to proposed defendants seek relief to which he is not entitled, 
the case will be dismissed in its entirety, and all of 
plaintiff’s pending motions will be denied.

On April 10, 1995, this Court entered an en banc order 
enjoining plaintiff from filing any pro se action without first 
receiving leave of court to do so. In addition, the en banc order 
enjoins plaintiff from filing pleadings subsequent to any new 
action without first obtaining leave of court to do so. The Court 
entered these injunctions because of plaintiff’s pattern of abusive 
and frivolous lawsuits. See In re Sylvester Jones, Cause No. 
4:95MC8ACOP, Order of the Court En Banc (E.D. Mo. April 10, 1995). 

On April 10, 1995, plaintiff submitted a document titled 
“plaintiff’s notice to the court pursuant to 42 U.S.C. Section 1986 
28 U.S.C. Section 1331." A review of this document indicates that 
it is of the nature that this Court’s en banc order addresses and 
is clearly an attempt to circumvent Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a) by adding 
additional parties and claims without first receiving leave of 


court to do so. “Plaintiff’s notice to the court. .. " is an 


attempt to join as defendants, among other, all of the district 


judges of this district and add conspriacy claims that plaintiff 
has raised in other actions before this Court. This pleading also 
includes a challenge to this Court’s April 10, 1995, Order of the 
Court En Banc.' Because this pleading is obviously an attempt to 
amend the instant complaint and join parties without first 
receiving leave of court to do so, the Court will order that it not 
be filed. 

Plaintiff has submitted a "memorandum for clerk" in which 
he requests that this Court issue summons for a total of fourteen 
defendants he has attempted to join in the above-referenced 
pleading. Because plaintiff will be denied leave to file this 
pleading, the Court will direct the Clerk not to issue summons. 

Finally, because plaintiff has served summons in this or 
in other pending cases on persons who are not in fact defendants to 
this action, the Court will direct that the clerk’s office shall 
issue no further summons in this case except upon leave of Court. 

Therefore,
