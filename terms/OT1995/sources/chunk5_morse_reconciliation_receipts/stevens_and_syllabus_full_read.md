## Historical propositions verified

1. Stevens/Ginsburg reach both statutory questions and reverse/remand. They treat Virginia's special ballot-nomination scheme as a delegation over electoral choice, construe §§5/14 functionally with Allen, Sheffield and the White Primary Cases, treat the regulation's primary example as nonexclusive, and find this fee within the statute itself rather than relying exclusively on administrative deference. Their account reaches beyond a rule confined to conventions actually identical to primaries, while retaining delegated electoral authority and the voting nexus.
2. Their discussion of Williams identifies both state-law noninvolvement and the then-missing submission route; footnote 21 emphasizes the limited force of summary affirmance. This is a verified account of what Stevens wrote, **not independent reading of the full Williams opinion or permission to cure the neutral packet's source gap by treating the account as an undisputed Williams holding**.
3. The broad §2/§5 comparison and references to historical Holder are part of Stevens's explanation. Historical Holder is not imported as simulated law. The actual Holder holding and its precise §2 scope govern the reconciliation.
4. They reject the asserted administrative and association obstacles to this fee's preclearance. Notes 38–39 address overbreadth, prior restraint, and the asserted financing rationale. They do not hold every party rule covered or order all hypothetical convention regulations to survive constitutional review.
5. The private §10 action rests on the contemporary legal context, Allen's enforcement rationale, the Act's treatment of private voting enforcement, §12's references to rights secured by §10, and Congress's 1975 provisions. They reserve whether this particular fee is an unlawful poll tax/substitute and direct first-instance consideration below; no refund or amount is awarded.
6. Footnote 48 rejects mootness with the recurring fee practice, absence of disavowal, and unreturned $45, expressly invoking the capable-of-repetition-yet-evading-review doctrine. Other writers' silence does not establish a join in that footnote's rationale.
7. Breyer/O'Connor/Souter accept coverage of this primary-like convention fee while expressly withholding judgment on the outer state-action and First Amendment limits of party preclearance. Their final paragraph affirmatively recognizes the private §10 action and reserves its merits. A full O'Connor join cannot be reduced to §5 alone.
