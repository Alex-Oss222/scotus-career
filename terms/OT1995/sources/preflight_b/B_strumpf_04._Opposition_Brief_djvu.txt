STATUTORY PROVISIONS INVOLVED 
In addition to the five sections 


of the United States Bankruptcy Code 
(hereinafter "Bankruptcy Code"), 11 USC 
§101 et geg., stated by Citizens to be 
principally involved in this case; 11 USC 
§362(a) (3) (the text of which is set 
forth in Appendix E to the Petition for 
Writ of Certiorari) and 11 USC §362(f) 
(the text of which is set forth as 
Appendix A to this Opposition) are also 
significant to this case. 

The five sections cited by 
Citizens Bank are: §362(a) (7), 
§363(a), §506(a), §542(b) and §553. 

STATEMENT OF THE CASE 

On January 25, 1991, David Strumpf 
(hereinafter "Debtor") filed a voluntary 
bankruptcy petition seeking relief under 
Chapter 13 of the Bankruptcy Code. At 
the time the petition was filed, Debtor 


had an unsecured personal loan with 


2 


Citizens Bank and also maintained a 
checking account with the same bank. 
Prior to Debtor’s petition, Citizens had 
not accelerated the note nor setoff any 
amounts owed against Debtor’s checking 
account. 

On February 25, 1991, Citizens 
Bank and Debtor’s other creditors were 
notified of the bankruptcy filing by the 
United States Bankruptcy Court for the 
District of Maryland. The notice further 
advised Citizens Bank and Debtor’s other 
creditors of the bankruptcy automatic 
stay and that certain actions were stayed 
by the bankruptcy filing including "(3) 
any act to obtain possession of 
property... (7) the setoff of any debt 
owing to the Debtor that arose before 
the commencement of the case..." 11 USC 


§362(a). 


Debtor’s Chapter 13 Repayment 
Plan, 11 USC §1322, was confirmed, 11 USC 
§1325 by the United States Bankruptcy 
Court on March 20, 1991, without 
objection from Citizens Bank, 11 USC 
§1324, and on the recommendation of the 
U.S. Trustee, 11 USC §1302. The effect 
of confirmation is to: "bind the debtor 
and each creditor, whether or not the 
claim of such creditor is provided for by 
the plan, and whether or not such 
creditor has objected to, has accepted or 
has rejected the plan." 11 USC §1327(a). 

On October 2, 1991, more than six 
months after confirmation of Debtor’s 
Repayment Plan, and eight months after 
the bankruptcy petition was filed, 
counsel for Debtor received a letter from 
counsel for Citizens Bank acknowledging 
the bankruptcy, but stating that a hold 


was being placed on Debtor’s checking 


account while Citizens Bank sought relief 
from the bankruptcy stay. Citizens Bank 

in the Motion for Relief from Stay argued 
that they had a right to setoff based on 

Maryland common law. 

The "administrative" hold put on 
Debtor’s checking account denied Debtor 
access to his funds. Made it extremely 
difficult for Debtor to make his payments 
under his Chapter 13 Repayment Plan and 
had the effect of denying payment to all 
Debtor’s other creditors. 

Although Citizens Bank denied 
Debtor access to $3,500.00 of the funds 
in his checking account, Citizens Bank’s 
claim was for only $3,250.48 of which 
$317.40 was accumulated interest from the 
time Debtor filed his bankruptcy petition 
and $382.58 was for counsel fees for 
filing the Motion for Relief from 


Automatic Stay. 


- 
i * 


The United States Bankruptcy Court 
granted Citizens Bank’s Motion for Relief 
from Automatic Stay and for Setoff on 
December 2, 1991. However, before that 
ruling, on November 13, 1991, the United 
States Bankruptcy Court, on Debtor’s 
motion, held Citizens Bank in contempt 
for violations of the automatic stay, 11 
USC §362(a) when it placed a hold on 
Debtor’s checking account. The United 
States Bankruptcy Judge ordered Citizens 
Bank to pay nominal damages of $25.00, 
punitive damages of $375.00 and counsel 
fees of $500.00. The amount of Debtor's 
counsel fees were stipulated to by 
Citizens Bank. Testimony was presented 
by the Debtor that Citizens Bank in 
denying him his funds was a great 
hardship and made it difficult for him to 


meet his ongoing expenses. 


After the Court’s ruling holding 
Citizens Bank in contempt, Citizens Bank 
released their hold on Debtor’s funds and 
although Debtor was very pleased with the 
service he received from Citizens Bank, 
Debtor switched his funds to another 
bank. This made the granting of relief 
from the automatic stay meaningless to 
Citizens Bank. 

Citizens Bank appealed the 
Bankruptcy Court’s finding of contempt 
and assessment of nominal damages, 
punitive damages and attorney’s fees. 

The U.S. District Court reversed stating: 
"permitting the Bank to freeze the funds 
until such time as the Court rules on the 
Bank’s Motion for Relief from Automatic 
Stay and for Setoff is the only sensible 
procedural approach under these 


circumstances." In re Strumpf, 138 BR 
792 (D. Md. 1992) 


Debtor then appealed to the United 
States Court of Appeals for the Fourth 
Circuit, In re: Strumpf£, 37 F3d 155 (Ath 
Cir. 1994), which reversed the decision 
of the District Court and remanded with 
instructions to reinstate the Bankruptcy 
Court decision finding Citizens Bank 
violated the automatic stay (the finding 
of contempt was surplusage) and the 
awarding of nominal damages, punitive 
damages and attorney’s fees, 37 F3d at 
159. 

The Court of Appeals based its 
ruling on the clear, unambiguous language 


of the Bankruptcy Code. 37 F3d at 159. 


REASONS FOR DENYING 
PETITION FOR WRIT OF CERTIORARI 


I. There is no division among the 
“Circuit Court of Appeals that 


have considered the issue.  _ 
Citizens Bank cites In re 


Patterson, 967 F2d 505 (lith Cir., 1992) 


as supporting the position that an 
"administrative" hold on debtor’s funds 
does not violate the bankruptcy stay 
provision of 11 USC §362(a) and is, 
therefore, contrary to the Court of 
Appeals ruling in the instant case. This 


is not a proper interpretation. 


The Patterson court stated: 


"The Credit Union violated general 
provisions of the automatic stay 
when in froze the Patterson's 
accounts and reduced the amount of 
an outstanding loan by the amount 
in the account. Such conduct 
represents a unilateral 
determination by the Credit Union 
of its right to setoff, a 
determination more appropriately 
within the province of the 
bankruptcy court." Id at page 
514. 


Citizens Bank quotes at length 
(pages 14 and 15 of Petition) from 
Patterson, but excludes from the quote 


the Court’s statement "We disagree" In 


re Patterson 967 F2d at 511 (11th Cir. 


1992), thus changing the meaning of the 
quote. 

What the Patterson court suggested 
was an alternative to denying a debtor 
access to his funds is that the funds be 
placed into the registry of the 
bankruptcy court. However, in the 
instant case and the Patterson case it 
was not the fact situation before the 
Court that funds had been placed into the 
court registry. The ruling on the facts 
in Patterson are the same as the ruling 
below on this case. 

The other Court of Appeals case 
cited by Citizens Bank as contrary to the 
instant case is: In re Davis, 889 F2d 
658 (Sth Cir. 1989), cert. denied sub 
nom. Campbell _v. United States, 495 US 
933 (1990). 

The facts in Davis case involved 


the Internal Revenue Service having its 


10 


bankruptcy claim reduced for not turning 
over a tax refund as so ordered by the 
Bankruptcy Court. The tax refund was not 
paid pending an appeal by the IRS that 
was later withdrawn. The facts of the 
Davis case are, therefore, significantly 
different than the one in the instant 


case. 


II. The Courts of Appeals that 
have considered whether an 
"administrative" hold 
violates the Bankruptcy stay 
provision of 11 USC §362(a) 
have ruled similar to the 


4nstant case 


The Third Circuit Court of Appeals 
has held that banks cannot setoff debts 
against a corporate account as a matter 
of law during a bankruptcy reorganization 


without first obtaining relief from the 


automatic stay. In re Penn Central 


Transportation Co. 453 F2d 520 (3rd Circ. 
1972). 


11 


The Court of Appeals for the Third 
Circuit stated in United States v. 


Norton, 717 F2d 767 (3rd Cir. 1983): 


"If a bank could freeze the 
Debtor’s accounts upon the 

filing of a petition in 
Bankruptcy, the Debtor’s chances 
for successful rehabilitation 
would be substantially diminished. 
(at 773) 


The Fourth Circuit Court of 
Appeals quoted favorably from Norton 
in United States v. Reynolds, 764 F2d 
1004 (4th Cir. 1985). The Fourth Circuit 
again repeated this quote in the instant 
case in supporting its finding. 37 F3rd 
at 159 

III. The Apparent Split of 


Authority in United 
States Districts and the 


Bankruptcy Court. 
Several District Courts and 


Bankruptcy Courts that have ruled on this 


12 


issue have reached differing 
conclusions.' 

It is significant that many 
decisions that held it did not violate 
the bankruptcy automatic stay for banks 
to deny debtors their funds, including In 
re Edgins, 36 BR 480 (Bankr. 9th Cir. 
1984) were decided prior to the 1984 
Amendments to the Bankruptcy Code 
effective on July 10, 1984, in which 
Congress significantly strengthened 11 
USC §362(a) by adding to the acts that 
were stayed by the filing of a bankruptcy 
petition "... to exercise control over 
the property of the estate." 11 USC 


§362 (a) (3). 


‘An opinion which finds freezing a debtor's 
bank account is a violation of the bankruptcy 
automatic stay and gives a very good analysis of 
the Bankruptcy Code in In Re Patterson, 125 BR 40 
(N.D. Ala. 1990) affirmed 967 F2d 1992. A 
contrary view is In re Edgins, 36 BR 480 (Bankr. 
9th Cir. 1984) a 2 to 1 bankruptcy panel 
decision. 


Also as previously discussed, this 
issue has been settled in the Third, 
Fourth, Eighth and Eleventh Circuit 
Courts of Appeal and, therefore, contrary 
decisions in these Circuits would be 
without validity. 


IV. The Automatic Stay is the 
Cornerstone of the Bankruptcy 


Code . 
Upon the filing of a bankruptcy 


petition, one of the fundamental 
satustahes of the Bankruptcy Code goes 
into effect. That is the automatic stay 
provisions of 11 USC §362(a). The 
automatic stay gives order to the 
proceedings by preserving Debtors assets 
and assuring that all creditors are 
treated alike. 

Citizens Bank is arguing that they 
should be allowed to deny a debtor access 
to his checking account funds and put 


themselves ahead of all other of Debtor’s 


14 


creditors, including debts classified sas 
priority, secured and unsecured non- 
priority, 11 USC §507. Citizens Bank was 
only in a position to do this because 
Debtor was very pleased with the service 
he received at Citizens Bank and, 
therefore, chose to continue banking at 
Citizens Bank after his petition was 
filed. Debtor could have avoided 
Citizens Bank seizing his funds by merely 
withdrawing his money before his petition 
was filed. 

Citizens Bank had several 
alternatives to violating the bankruptcy 
automatic stay. Among these would have 
been for Citizens Bank to object to 
Debtor’s Repayment Plan and their 
treatment therein. Citizens Bank could 
also have waited until debtor was 
discharged and Debtor’s bankruptcy case 


was closed, the bankruptcy stay 


15 


terminated, and then taken steps at that 
time to enforce any lien they may have 
had. Liens are not discharged in 
bankruptcy, 11 USC §362(c). 

Citizens Bank could also have 
requested an ex parte motion. This is 
provided for in 11 USC §362(f) and was 
suggested as an alternative by the Fourth 
Circuit in the instant case, 37 F3d at 
156. 

V. The Clear Language of the 


Bankruptcy Code Does Not 
Support Citizens Bank’s 


Argument . 
Citizens Bank argues that Debtor 


violated 11 USC §363(c) (2) by using 
Citizen’s "cash collateral" (Petition 
page 28). This section of the code 
clearly applies to debtors engaged in 
business. In the instant case, Debtor 


wad retired and his whole source of 


16 


income was retirement benefits and social 
security. 

Citizens next argues that 11 USC 
§542(b) sanctions their actions, but 
again a reading of the code shows that 
this section is not an exception to the 
bankruptcy automatic stay provisions of 
11 USC §362. To quote §542(b) "Except as 
provided in subsection (c)..." and 
subsection (c) states "Except as provided 
in section §362(a)(7)." §542(b) goes on 
to state "that such debt may offset under 
§553 of this title." The language of 
§553 (another section relied on by 
Citizens Bank) states, "Except as 
otherwise provided in this section and in 
sections 362..." 

11 USC §506(a) also relied on by 
Citizens Bank also incorporate 11 USC 


§553. 


17 


Therefore, Citizens Bank’s 
argument that "This case presents the 
Court with an opportunity to return the 
law governing setoffs in bankruptcy cases 
to the carefully balanced statutory 
framework established by Congress in the 
Bankruptcy Code," is not supported by the 
clear language of the Bankruptcy Code. 

VI. Conclusions. 

Debtor prays that the Court deny 
the Petition for Writ of Certiorari 
because: 

1. There is no split of authority 
among the United States Courts of Appeals 
that have considered this issue, and 

2. There is not the apparent 
split of decisions in the United States 
District Courts and Bankruptcy Courts in 
light of the 1984 Amendments that 
strengthen the Bankruptcy Automatic Stay, 


11 USC §362(a). Also, this issue has 


18 


been settled in the Third, Fourth, Eighth 
and Eleventh Circuit Courts of Appeal, 
making opinions to the contrary invalid, 
and 

3. The literal language of the 
Statute does not support Citizens Bank’s 
argument that it does not violate the 
bankruptcy automatic stay provision of 
the Bankruptcy Code to seize and deny a 
debtor access to his checking account 
funds.
