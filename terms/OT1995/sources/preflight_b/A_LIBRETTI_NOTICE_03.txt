ARGUMENT 

Petitioner contends (Pet. 7) that this Court should grant 
review to resolve conflicts among the courts of appeals on two 
questions: first, whether Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(f) requires a 
district court to determine that a "factual basis" exists for a 
criminal forfeiture that is stipulated in a plea agreement; and 
second, whether a defendant has a constitutional right to a jury 
trial on a criminal forfeiture. 

1. a. The court of appeals correctly determined that Rule 
11(f£) does not require a district court to find a factual basis for 
a criminal forfeiture. Rule 11(f) provides that the court should 
not enter judgment on a guilty plea "without making such inquiry as 
shall satisfy it that there is a factual basis for the plea." By 
focusing on the factual basis for the plea itself, rather than on 
the consequences of the plea, Rule 11(f) is designed to ensure only 
"that the conduct which the defendant admits constitutes the 
offense charged," in order to "protect a defendant who is in the 
position of pleading voluntarily * * * without realizing that his 
conduct does not actually fall within the charge." Advisory 
Committee’s Notes to Fed. R. Crim. P. 11 (1966); see United States 
v. LeDonne, 21 F.3d 1418, 1424 (7th Cir.), cert. denied, 115 S. Ct. 
584 (1994); United States v. DeFusco, 949 F.2d 114, 120 (4th Cir. 
1991), cert. denied, 112 S. Ct. 1703 (1992). Thus, as the courts 
of appeals have recognized, the Rule does not require a factual 
basis for the defendant’s sentence, but only for his conviction. 
See United States v. Reyes, 13 F.3d 638, 640 (2d Cir. 1994) (no
