SUMMARY OF THE ARGUMENT 
Debtor filed a Chapter 13 Petition and Chapter 13 
Statement listing the unsecured claim of Petitioner Citi- 
zens. 
A Chapter 13 Plan was filed providing for distribu- 
tions to all unsecured creditors including Petitioner. All 
creditors were notified of the hearing on confirmation of 
that Plan and of their opportunity to object. When Citi- 
zens failed to object to confirmation and the Chapter 13 
Plan was confirmed, Citizens became bound thereby. 
Following that confirmation hearing, Citizens took no 
action to seek a modification of the confirmed Chapter 
13 Plan. Some eight (8) months following the filing of 
the Debtor’s voluntary petition under Chapter 13 of the 
Bankruptcy Code, and some six (6) months following 
confirmation of the Debtor’s Chapter 13 Plan, Citizens 
unilaterally and without prior notice froze the bank 
account of the Debtor maintained with Citizens without 
benefit of any application to or order of the bankruptcy 
court. Further, Citizens froze funds in the Debtor’s bank 
account in an amount some several hundred dollars in 
excess of that which Citizens claimed to be owed by the 
Debtor. By virtue of Citizens’ action in freezing the 
Debtor’s bank account, Citizens violated the provisions of 
11 U.S.C. § §362(a)(7), (a)(3) and (a)(6). 
Notwithstanding Citizens’ assertion that its actions in 
freezing the Debtor’s bank account are expressly counte- 
nanced by §542(b) of the Bankruptcy Code, careful 
analysis of that section reveals that the same is simply 
inapplicable without a prior request to and order from 


[PDF PAGE 8]
6 
the bankruptcy court modifying the automatic stays 
imposed by 11 U.S.C. §362 to permit such a freezing 
and setoff of the Debtor’s bank account. 
Further, the assertion by the Petitioner that the impo- 
sition of prior relief limitations on its setoff rights is an 
unconstitutional 
taking of its property without due 
process of law in violation of the Fifth Amendment to 
the United 
States Constitution 
is similarly without 
merit. Throughout the Bankruptcy Code, a careful bal- 
ance is maintained between the rights of debtors and 
creditors which sometimes results in the conditioning 
and/or delay of enforcement of creditors’ rights in favor 
of the Bankruptcy Code’s goal of an orderly restructur- 
ing of debtor/creditor relationships. 
As that goal is 
advanced by the limited restrictions placed upon credi- 
tors with rights of setoff without a deprivation of those 
creditors’ rights, there can be no serious argument that 
those restrictions are an unconstitutional taking of prop- 
erty without due process of law. 
Finally, the Respondent states that the amicus cunae 
brief of the United States advances a strained interpreta- 
tion of the Bankruptcy Code which would eviscerate the 
debtor protection provisions manifest in 11 U.S.C. § 362. 
Rather than adopt that view, Respondent urges that the 
statutory scheme can best be preserved and respected 
through the careful implementation of general or local 
rules of bankruptcy procedure similar to that adopted by 
the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of 
Maryland and providing an orderly mechanism within the 
Bankruptcy Code for the processing and setoff of debtors’ 
tax refunds and overpayments.

ARGUMENT 
THE CONFIRMATION OF THE DEBTOR’S PLAN FIXED 
THE RIGHTS OF CITIZENS AND THE TREATMENT TO 
WHICH ITS CLAIM WAS ENTITLED. 
A. Citizens’ Failure to Object to the Confirmation 
of Debtor’s Chapter 13 Plan Was a Waiver of 
Citizens’ 
Right to Object to Its Treatment 
Therein and Bound It to the Provisions Thereof. 
As noted in the Statement of the Case, supra, the 
Debtor’s Chapter 13 Plan was filed herein on January 25, 
1991, together with the Debtor’s Voluntary Petition 
under Chapter 13 of the Bankruptcy Code. The Debtor 
filed his Chapter 13 Statement on that date, which 
included the claim of Citizens in the amount of $2,500.00 
as an unsecured claim. On February 26, 1991, the bank- 
ruptcy court issued the First Meeting Notice and for- 
warded the same to all parties in interest, including Citi- 
zens, together with a copy of the Debtor’s Chapter i3 
Plan. Contrary to the assertion of the Petitioner 
in its 
brief, the Plan made full provision for the treatment of 
Citizens’ claim together with the claims of all other 
unsecured creditors in Class 3 thereof (J.A. 3a). The First 
Meeting Notice further noted that a hearing on confirma- 
tion of the Plan would be held on March 20, 1991, at 
2:00 p.m., and cautioned as follows: 
IF WRITTEN OBJECTIONS TO CONFIRMATION 
ARE FILED, A HEARING ON SUCH OBJECTIONS 
WILL BE HELD AT THE TIME OF THE CONFIR- 
MATION HEARING. 
(emphasis in original) (J.A. 7a). 
Notwithstanding the treatment of Citizens’ claim in 
the Chapter 13 Plan as unsecured and the bold warning 


[PDF PAGE 9]
to all creditors contained in the First Meeting Notice that 
objections to the Chapter 13 Plan would be considered 
at the confirmation hearing on March 20, 1991, Citizens 
took no action and interposed no objection to the treat- 
ment provided for it under the Chapter 13 Plan. Accord- 
ingly, the Chapter 13 Plan came on for confirmation at 
the scheduled hearing and was confirmed by order of the 
bankruptcy court entered on April 8, 1991 (J.A. 10a- 
lla). 
With the entry of the Order Confirming Plan, Citizens 
was bound to the treatment of its claim contained in the 
Chapter 13 Plan, pursuant to the provisions of 11 U.S.C. 
§ 1327(a). Even following the entry of that Order, Citi- 
zens took no action in the Chapter 13 case to assert any 
claim for different treatment than that proposed for it by 
the Debtor in his Chapter 13 Plan. While Citizens filed 
its Proof of Claim in the Chapter 13 proceeding on 
June 13, 1991, asserting a secured claim in the amount 
of $2,555.50, it took no action in the bankruptcy court 
to seek a revision of the Chapter 13 Plan to provide 
treatment thereunder as a secured creditor. Indeed, other 
than the filing of its Proof of Claim as aforesaid, 
Citizens 
took no action for the first eight (8) months of the 
Chapter 13 proceedings to assert its right to treatment 
under the Chapter 13 Plan other than as proposed 
by the 
Debtor. 
B. Citizens Was Not a Secured Creditor. 
Although Citizens asserts that it was a secured creditor 
in the Chapter 13 proceedings 
by virtue of its alleged set- 
off rights and the provisions of 11 U.S.C. §506(a) of the 
Bankruptcy Code, careful reading of that section makes 
clear that such secured status does not automatically arise 
upon the assertion of a right of setoff, but only upon a 
9 
determination by the bankruptcy court that a setoff will 
be permitted in a given case. 
An allowed claim of a creditor secured by a lien on 
property in which the estate has an interest, or that 
is subject to setoff under section 553 of this title, is 
a secured claim to the extent of the value of such 
creditor’s interest in the estate’s interest in such 
property, or to the extent of the amount subject to 
setoff, as the case may be, and is an unsecured claim 
to the extent that the value of such creditor’s inter- 
est or the amount so subject to setoff is less than the 
amount of such allowed claim. Such value shall be 
determined in light of the purpose of the valuation 
and of the proposed disposition or use of such 
property, and in conjunction with any hearing on 
such disposition or use or on a plan affecting such 
creditor’s interest. 
11 U.S.C. $506(a) (1988). 
Indeed, the above-quoted language makes clear the 
intention of the drafters of the Bankruptcy Code that 
any party desiring treatment as a secured creditor by 
virtue of an asserted (but disputed) right of setoff must 
participate in the hearing on confirmation of the Debtor’s 
Chapter 13 Plan or in some other hearing regarding set- 
off, or use of funds subject to setoff. The participation of 
such creditor is required, inter alia, because a creditor’s 
right of setoff is permissive in nature, rather than manda- 
tory, and the exercise of setoff is within the discretion of 
the bankruptcy court under general principles of equity. 
Cumberland Glass Mfg. Co. v. DeWitt, 237 U.S. 447, 
455 (1915); See also In re Blanton, 105 B.R. 321, 336-38 
(Bkrtcy.E.D.Va. 1989) (collecting cases reviewing allow- 
ance and disallowance of 
setoff). Only through 
a full air- 
ing of the creditor’s claimed right of setoff at the confir- 
mation hearing, or at some other hearing prior thereto, 


[PDF PAGE 10]
10 
can the bankruptcy court consider the equities of the 
asserted right of setoff, for the purpose of allowing or 
disallowing a requested setoff, and to determine any 
secured status pursuant to §506(a). As Citizens did not 
seck a determination of its alleged secured status at or 
prior to the confirmation hearing on the Debtor's Chap- 
ter 13 Plan, the confirmed Plan represents a judicial 
determination of Citizens’ mghts on proper notice to 
Citizens and to every other creditor. Citizens was bound 
to the treatment provided in the confirmed Plan, which 
treatment is as an unsecured creditor. 11 U.S.C. §1327 
(a) (1988). 
Il. 
CITIZENS’ ACTION IN UNILATERALLY FREEZING THE 
DEBTOR’S BANK ACCOUNT WAS UNLAWFUL AND 
VIOLATED 11 U.S.C. §362(a). 
A. Citizens Failed to Participate 
in the Chapter 13 
Proceeding; Became Bound by the Provisions of 
the Confirmed Chapter 13 Plan; and Then Ignored 
the Confirmation Order and the Bankruptcy 
Code. 
After Citizens’ inaction at and prior to confirmation of 
the Chapter 13 Plan resulted in a determination of its 
treatment under the Plan, Citizens took no appropriate 
action to cure what it came to believe was inadequate 
treatment. While Citizens could have filed an appropriate 
pleading seeking modification of the Debtor's Chapter 13 
Plan to provide treatment for Citizens as a creditor 
allegedly secured by setoff rights in the Debtor's bank 
account, or could have filed a Motion seeking relief from 
the various stays imposed by §362 to permit it to assert 
and exercise its rights of setoff against the Debtor’s bank 
account, Citizens elected neither of said options. Further, 
no other judicial remedy was pursued. Instead, Citizens 
1] 
chose to ignore both the court order confirming the Plan 
as well as the carefully orchestrated scheme of debt 
restructuring contemplated throughout the Bankruptcy 
Code, Citizens unilaterally, and without any recourse to 
the statutory scheme of the Bankruptcy Code or to judi- 
cial determination, setoff the Debtor’s bank account in 
the amount of $3,500.00 as a result of a pre-petition 
unsecured debt which was no more than $3,259.68. 
Indeed, the actions of Citizens to protect its narrow 
interests some eight (8) months into the subject bank- 
ruptcy case in absolute disregard of the rights of all other 
creditors illumines the wisdom of Congress in its inclu- 
sion in the Bankruptcy Code of the automatic stay pro- 
visions of 11 U.S.C. §362.> Commenting on the purpose 
of that bedrock provision, the House Committee on the 
Judiciary noted as follows: 
The automatic stay also provides creditor protec- 
tion. Without it, certain creditors would be able to 
pursue 
their own remedies against the debtor’s 
property. Those who acted first would obtain pay- 
ment of the[ir] claims in preference to and to the 
de ‘riment of other creditors. Bankruptcy is designed 
to provide an orderly liquidation procedure under 
which all creditors are treated equally. A race of 
diligence by creditors for the debtor’s assets pre- 
vents that. 
H.R. Rep. No. 95-595, 95th Cong., Ist Sess. 340 (1977), 
reprinted in, 1978 U.S.C.C.A.N. 5787. 
The purpose of the automatic stay, in essence, is to prevent 
the further desiccation of the debtor’s cash flow through the loss 
of bank accounts and accounts receivable thereby expediting the 
financial recovery of the debtor. Randy J. Shaw, Finally, An 
Answer to the Banker's Dilemma!, 68 Fla, B,J. 79 (1994).

All creditors, including Citizens, were bound by law to 
the treatment provided in the confirmed Plan. Citizens 
acted in derogation of that legal duty, in its own paro- 
chial interest, and to the complete disregard of all other 
parties in interest. 
B. Citizens Violated Three Sections of 11 U.S.C.

1. The Violation of §362(a)(7). 
The Fourth Circuit relied on opinions of numerous

Nothing in the Fourth Circuit’s opinion below suggests

mortages and deeds of trusts and other security interests 
in realty and personalty. In such circumstances, while 
the Bankruptcy Code conditions the rights of those credi-

application to and order from the bankruptcy court per-

enforcement of that security interest. To the contrary, 
the only limiting factor placed upon those secured credi- 
tors 
is the necessary intervention of the bankruptcy 
court and the delay incident thereto. This is not an 
impermissible or unwarranted burden on creditors. 
In imposing special conditions and limitations on the 
exercise of the rights of secured creditors as well as those 
seeking to exercise rights of setoff, the drafters of the 
Bankruptcy Code carefully balanced the competing inter- 
ests of the vested property rights of those creditors

§ 1304. Debtor engaged in business 
(a) A debtor that is self-employed and incurs trade 
credit in the production of income from such employ- 
ment is engaged in business. 
(b) Unless the court orders otherwise, a debtor engaged 
in business may operate the business of the debtor and, 
subject to any limitations on a trustee under sections 
363(c) and 364 of this title and to such limitations or 
conditions as the court prescribes, shall have, exclusive of 
the trustee, the rights and powers of the trustee under 
such sections. 
(c) A debtor engaged in business shall perform the dut- 
ies of the trustee specified in section 704(8) of this title.

LOCAL RULES OF THE 
UNITED STATES BANKRUPTCY COURT 
FOR THE DISTRICT OF MARYLAND 
Rule 25. Internal Revenue Service And Maryland 
Income Tax Refunds — Automatic Stay 
(a) Refunds. The Internal Revenue Service and the 
Maryland Comptroller of the Treasury, Income Tax 
Division, are authorized to make income tax refunds, 
in the ordinary course of business, directly to debtors 
in Chapter 7, 12, and 13 cases; provided, however, that 
in Chapter 7 cases no refund will be made to debtors 
within sixty (60) days after a petition is filed. 
There- 
after, unless otherwise directed by the trustee or the 
Court, a refund may be made directly to the debtor. 
(b) Offset; Assessment; Automatic Stay. 
(1) The Internal Revenue Service and the Maryland 
Comptroller of the Treasury, Income Tax Division, are 
authorized to offset against any refund or overpayment 
due a debtor any taxes due the United States or the 
State of Maryland, provided that a claim for such taxes 


[PDF PAGE 22]
8a 
has been filed and neither disputed nor disallowed in a 
federal or state court. 
(2) The Internal Revenue Service and the Maryland 
Comptroller of the Treasury, Income Tax Division, are 
authorized to assess any tax liability that may be satis- 
fied by offsetting an overpayment when such liability 
has not previously been assessed. Notice of such assess- 
ment and offset shall be given to the debtor prior to or 
substantially 
concurrently 
with such assessment 
and 
offset. 
(3) The Internal Revenue Service and the Maryland 
Comptroller of the Treasury, Income Tax Division, are 
authorized to assess, but take no other steps toward 
collection. of, tax liabilities shown on voluntarily filed 
returns and other agreed-to tax liabilities unless other- 
wise authorized by this Rule, or by order of court. The 
exception to automatic stay in 11 U.S.C. §362(b)(9) 
shall be deemed automatically modified in accordance 
with the provision of this Rule. 
(c) Notice to Trustee and Court. 
The debtor shall, 
within five (5) days of receipt of a tax refund or notice 
of tax assessment or deficiency, file with the Court and 
send to the trustee a copy of the refund check (in Chap- 
ter 7 cases) and transmittal letter, the tax assessment or 
deficiency notice, and other documents evidencing same.
