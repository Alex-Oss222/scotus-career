## What the existing source establishes, and its limit

The existing petition appendix preserves a complete, readable **two-sentence quotation** of the safe-berth provision, including its express exclusion for public channels, fairways, approaches, anchorages and other publicly maintained areas. It also preserves the finding that an April 4, 1988 amendment included single point mooring. Petitioners' merits p.10 n.11 quotes the same two sentences and cites findings 8, JA142–143. The damaged JA143 OCR therefore need not be reconstructed from inference.

The district court expressly introduces this quotation as the agreement **“in relevant part.”** These recovered passages do not establish that the quotation is the complete executed agreement, the complete original “Safe Berth Availability” paragraph, or every potentially applicable term. The bounded search did not locate a separately reproduced executed telex/exhibit containing the entire agreement. This is a limit of the recovered material checked, not a finding that additional terms do not exist in the full record. No safe-berth-agreement trial-exhibit number is identified in the quoted finding or merits footnote. The merits statement separately cites CR624 concerning the title-passage term; that citation does not itself supply the full agreement.

A separate instrument, **General Instructions, Discharging/Loading Orders and Indemnification**, is expressly identified as **Joint Trial Exhibit 92** in JA166 conclusion 21. Its quoted indemnification/pilotage text, the signing finding, and the relevant lower-court qualifications are reproduced below. The quoted safe-berth provision does not itself say “due diligence” or provide an express rule allocating all losses from the master's later navigation. The lower courts discuss due diligence, warranty scope, superseding causation and imputation as distinct legal issues. No unquoted contract term is supplied here.

## Lower-court qualifications and the due-diligence/strict-warranty distinction

The October 1992 order reserved which related corporations were bound, held that possible post-breakout breaches were not excused merely by a superseding pre-breakout force, and distinguished safe-berth breaches from other negligence for imputation. The subsequent district-court conclusions adopted due diligence and found no post-breakout breach under that standard. The court of appeals expressly left the due-diligence-versus-strict-warranty issue undecided and stated that Exxon did not contest compliance with due diligence. These are lower-court rulings and descriptions of party positions, not new factual findings or an adoption of those rules for this event.

**OCR caution:** JA116–117 renders Exxon's asserted lack-of-consideration position as “and, therefore, enforceable”; the internal context suggests a dropped negation, but this extract does not repair the source. The corporate names also have minor OCR errors. The previously identified incomplete footnote at JA112 is outside the block below and remains unreconstructed.
