#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Superseding cause under comparative maritime fault

* **Controlling Reasoning Below / Institutional Posture:** The district court found a new, independent, extraordinary navigational cause after the immediate mooring emergency had ended, and the Ninth Circuit sustained that finding.
* **Petitioner or Applicant’s Position:** Comparative fault should apportion responsibility to all negligent actors whose conduct contributed to the sequence; superseding cause improperly restores an all-or-nothing bar.
* **Respondent or Competing Position:** Fault allocation begins only after legal causation is established. The accepted findings make the captain’s later conduct an independent superseding cause.

**McDermott**

**Current rule:** The plaintiff’s claim against nonsettling maritime tortfeasors is reduced by the settling tortfeasor’s equitable proportion of responsibility, not by settlement dollars. A settler is protected from contribution when the nonsettler has a meaningful opportunity to litigate and obtain a reliable determination of that proportionate share. The nonsettler then pays its own adjudicated obligation and cannot recover the plaintiff’s voluntary settlement shortfall from the settler. A reliable proportionate credit is the premise for the contribution bar, not a fact established merely by identifying a settlement. Settlement risk belongs to the settling parties; the rule does not abolish surviving joint liability or transfer ordinary insolvency risk wholesale to the injured plaintiff.

**City of Milwaukee**

**Holding:** Maritime prejudgment interest ordinarily compensates loss of use of an otherwise compensable amount; departure requires a legally sufficient exceptional circumstance supported by the record. Mutual fault already reflected in principal damages and a genuine ordinary liability dispute, without more, do not justify denial; undue claimant-caused delay can qualify, but the Court adopts no exhaustive list, fixed liquidated-claim prerequisite, rate, period, compounding rule or sum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all eight participating Justices adopt the complete rule and affirm the calculation remand.
