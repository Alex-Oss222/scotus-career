1. Whether the Ninth Cir. and all California state courts 
which absolutely, completely, permanently, unlimitedly and in fact 
unconditionally prohibited Petitioner and all of petitioners’ past, 
current and future lawyers from filing any and all papers with the above 
courts is constitutional and is a violation of the provisions of the 
Treaty of Friendship, Commerce And Navigation between the Republic of 
China and the United States of American and thus is also 
unconstitutional, which has given rise to the disagreements among the 
lower courts, among other issues: (1) ist Cir., Sire v. Gabriel, 748 
F.2d 49 (lst Circuit 1984) (holding that "We stress that where access to 
the courts is concerned, an injunction should be drawn as narrowly as 
possible"); (2) 3rd Circuit: Castro v. The United States of America 775 
F.24 399, (2md Circuit 1985) (holding that "The court of appeals 
marrowly tailored the injunction to closely fit the specific vice 
encountered.") see also Brow v. Farrelly 994 F.2d. 1027, (3rd Circuit 
1993)), Brow v. Farrelly 994 F.2d. 1027, (3rd Circuit 1993) (holding 
that "The broad scope of the District court's power is limited by two 
fundamental tenets of our legal system -- the litigant's right co due 
process and access to the courts"), In re Oliver 682 F.2d 443, (icd 
Circuit 1982) (holding that imposing an injunction "is an extreme 
remedy, and should be used in exigent circumstances"), In re Oliver 682 
F.2d 443, (3rd Circuit 1982) (holding that "Litigiousness alone will not 


support an injunction restricting litigant's filing activities"); (3). 
2nd & 10th Circuits, Castro v. United States of America 775 F.2d 399, 


(2nd Circuit 1985) (holding that “If an injunction against future 
litigation issued under 28 U.S.C.A. 1651(a) were couched in overly broad 
terms, this impermissible infringes upon a litigator's right of access 
to the courts") see also Tripati v. Beaman 878 F.2d 351, (10th Circuit 
1989)); (4). 4th & llth Circuits, Robinson v. Marion 879 F.2d 863, (4th 
Circuit 1989) (holding that "A court imposing such an injunction must be 
careful not to order conditions that effectively deny access to the 
courts", see also Procup v.Strickland 792 F.2d 1069 (llth Circuit 


1986)); (5). D.C. Circuit, Urban v. United Nations 768 F.2d 1497, 1500 
(D.C. Circuit 1985) (holding that "A court must take great care not to 


unduly impair a litigant's constitutional right of access to the 
courts"); (6) Sth Cir., Harrelson v. the United States, 613 F.2d 114 
(Sth Circuit 1980) (holding that "Orders enjoining future lawsuits are 
generally unnecessary because the doctrine of res judicata or collateral 
estoppel usually suffice to protect defendants from repetitious 
lawsuits"); and (6) 2d Cir., N.Y. Chinese TV Programs v. U.E. 
Enterprises, 954 F.2d 847, 852 (holding that “the FCN treaty remains a
