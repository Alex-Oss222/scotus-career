## Record facts and their evidentiary status

**Contract and parties.** The actual agreement identifies Doctor's Associates, Inc. (DAI) and Paul S. Casarotto; its execution page bears DAI's authorized signature and Paul's signature. JA 64–65, 78. The lower opinions often refer collectively to Paul and Pamela. The petition, p. 5 n.4, asserts that only Paul signed and only Paul asserted claims against DAI and Lombardi; the complete amended complaint is not reproduced in this JA, so its characterization of the pleading should remain attributed. The district order itself addresses the “plaintiffs' claims” against DAI and its alleged agent Lombardi. JA 11. Claims against Daniel and Deb Hudson and D&D Subway were outside the stay and appeal at issue. JA 15; Pet. 5 n.4; Pet. App. 15a.

**Alleged misconduct is not a trial finding.** The December 1994 state opinion expressly presumes the complaint's allegations true for review of the stay. JA 13; Pet. App. 12a. The alleged bargain concerned an initially unavailable preferred Great Falls location: the Casarottos allegedly accepted a less desirable site in exchange for an exclusive opportunity to open at the preferred site when it became available; DAI and Lombardi allegedly awarded it to another franchisee, contributing to loss of the business and SBA-loan collateral. JA 14; Pet. App. 13a. Petition pp. 5–6 additionally describe interference with efforts to sell the store and list claims for tortious interference, breach of good faith, fraud, constructive fraud, negligent misrepresentation, deceit, and Montana Consumer Protection Act violations. These are litigant claims, not findings that the misconduct occurred.

**Actual district-court determinations.** On June 2, 1993, after considering briefs, counsel's argument, and submitted evidence, the district court determined that (1) the April 25, 1988 franchise agreement concerned interstate commerce, including a license to use the national, federally registered Subway mark; (2) the agreement contained the quoted arbitration clause; (3) claims against DAI and alleged agent Lombardi arose out of and related to the agreement and fell within the clause; and (4) DAI had demanded AAA arbitration. It stayed the claims against these two defendants until arbitration under the agreement, invoking FAA § 3. JA 10–11; Pet. App. 49a–50a. The state appellate disposition did not overturn the commerce, scope, or demand determinations; the August 1995 majority expressly assumed interstate commerce. JA 53–54; Pet. 6 n.5; Pet. App. 6a. This is not an unresolved interstate-commerce-record case.

**Affidavit supporting commerce.** Axelrod described recurring royalties and sales reports to Connecticut, national trademark and advertising benefits, Connecticut training, and continuing interstate contacts; he stated that DAI signed this agreement in Connecticut. JA 83–85, especially ¶¶ 7–20. These are affidavit statements supporting the district determination, not independent findings beyond it.

**Notice and assent evidence must be kept distinct.** The agreement lacked the specified first-page arbitration notice. JA 14, 49–50; Pet. App. 13a, 2a. But the contract contains a signed acknowledgment that each party read, understood, and consented to all its terms, JA 78 ¶ 13, and a recital that Paul received the offering prospectus at or before the first personal meeting and at least ten business days before signing, JA 66 recital I. The offering-circular excerpts warn readers to examine the contract carefully and, if possible, consult an adviser, JA 62; the circular expressly identifies binding AAA arbitration in Bridgeport, Connecticut, with equal costs, JA 63. Paul's affidavit says he read the agreement but received no explanation of arbitration or Connecticut law; it also asserts a nonnegotiable form and lack of actual understanding of surrendering a Montana judicial forum. JA 87, transcribed below. Neither lower majority made a freestanding finding that the arbitration clause was unconscionable, fraudulently induced, or actually unknown; the dispositive state ground was statutory notice noncompliance.

## Operative contract provisions

JA 75, ¶ 10(c), provides:

> Any controversy or claim arising out of or relating to this contract or the breach thereof shall be settled by Arbitration in accordance with the Commercial Arbitration Rules of the American Arbitration Association at a hearing to be held in Bridgeport, Connecticut and judgment upon an award rendered by the Arbitrator(s) may be entered in any court having jurisdiction thereof. The commencement of arbitration proceedings by an aggrieved party to settle disputes arising out of or relating to this contract is a condition precedent to the commencement of legal action by either party. The cost of such a proceeding will be borne equally by the parties.

The lower opinions identify this clause as paragraph 10(c) on page nine of the contract; the JA reproduction places it at printed JA 75. JA 14, 49. The contractual forum is Bridgeport, Connecticut, and costs are equal under the quoted clause. These terms are record facts. The majority's broader descriptions of likely arbitration costs and missing procedural safeguards are its stated reasons for public-policy treatment, not an adjudicated calculation of the Casarottos' actual arbitration expenses. JA 20–21; Pet. App. 19a–20a.

JA 77, ¶ 12, provides Connecticut governing law and states that the agreement contains the parties' entire understanding. It preserves representations in the agreement, offering circular, and franchisor advertising; the space for additional representations is blank in the transcription. The acknowledgment of reading and consent is ¶ 13, JA 78. Paragraph 10(f), JA 75, provides severability if a provision is construed or declared invalid. No disposition of a severability dispute appears in the lower decisions.

DAI's January 25, 1993 demand requested Bridgeport arbitration of the claims in Paul's Montana lawsuit; it announced an impending motion to dismiss or stay. JA 79–82. The demand is not an award, and these sources do not show an arbitration merits disposition.

## Preservation, lower grounds, and requested relief

- The January 29, 1993 motion sought dismissal or, alternatively, a stay pending arbitration. DAI advanced both Connecticut choice of law and FAA preemption; the Casarottos opposed on Montana choice-of-law and § 27-5-114(4) notice grounds. JA 15; Pet. App. 14a. The trial court chose a stay. The July 8, 1993 Rule 54(b) order certified the June 2 order for appeal. JA 16; Pet. App. 15a. These materials do not support describing the district disposition as a final judgment resolving the tort and contract damages claims.
- FAA preemption was expressly presented and decided. The state court also directed argument on *Volt* by its February 17, 1994 order. Pet. App. 47a–48a. No preservation gap appears for the federal notice-preemption question.
- In December 1994, Montana applied Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws §§ 187–188 and its *Emerson*, *Youngblood*, and *Trammel* decisions. It treated Montana as having a materially greater interest because negotiation, intended performance, subject matter, and the franchisees' residence were centered there, although Paul executed in New York and DAI had its Connecticut incorporation/home office. It treated the statutory notice rule as fundamental Montana public policy, displacing the contract's Connecticut choice. JA 16–21; Pet. App. 15a–20a.
- On preemption, the state majority used *Volt*'s purposes-and-objectives language and its recognition that parties need not arbitrate without agreement. It regarded front-page notice as ensuring knowing agreement, compatible with the FAA. It held the statute not preempted and, because this contract lacked the notice, reversed the stay and remanded for further proceedings. JA 24–28; Pet. App. 23a–27a. Its governing ground was the notice statute, not the author’s separate commentary about arbitration. The August 1995 majority expressly distinguished that commentary from its basis of decision. JA 54; Pet. App. 7a.
- The August 31, 1995 state opinion reaffirmed and reinstated the December 1994 opinion and remanded to the district court; it viewed *Allied-Bruce*'s commerce discussion as not altering its notice/consent analysis. JA 48–54; Pet. App. 1a–7a. The September 19, 1995 remittitur reproduced that reaffirmance/reinstatement. JA 60–61.
- The state dissenting analyses argued that the words “pursuant to this chapter” did not reach a contract choosing Connecticut law, and alternatively that a notice rule defeating an otherwise enforceable FAA agreement conflicted with *Southland*, *Perry*, and *Volt*. They distinguished a party-selected procedural stay that preserves eventual arbitration from invalidation under unchosen state law. JA 35–47; Pet. App. 34a–46a. These are lower dissenting arguments, not lower holdings.
- Petition question presented: whether FAA § 2 preempts a law making arbitration agreements, but not other agreements, unenforceable absent a particular first-page notice. Pet. i. Petition p. 17 suggested summary reversal or at least plenary review; p. 26 requested grant of the writ. Petition pp. 14–15 n.10 said a federal preemption decision made it unnecessary to decide whether the conflict-of-laws analysis was independently flawed.
- Exact merits relief, visually verified at petitioners' merits p. 29: “The judgment of the Supreme Court of Montana should be reversed. The case should be remanded with instructions to reinstate the Montana district court's order staying the plaintiffs' claims ‘until the arbitration has been had in accordance with the terms of the arbitration agreement.’ App. 11.” The request is restoration of the arbitration stay, not judgment for DAI on liability or damages.

## Montana statute, including all exceptions

The following is the whole text reproduced at Pet. App. 51a, checked against petitioners' merits Appendix 1a image. Line wrapping and capitalization in the heading are normalized. This is contemporaneous statutory text reproduced in the source, not an independent official-code verification.

> **27-5-114. Validity of arbitration agreement—exceptions.** (1) A written agreement to submit an existing controversy to arbitration is valid and enforceable except upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract.
>
> (2) A written agreement to submit to arbitration any controversy arising between the parties after the agreement is made is valid and enforceable except upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract. Except as permitted under subsection (3), this subsection does not apply to:
>
> (a) claims arising out of personal injury, whether based on contract or tort;
>
> (b) any contract by an individual for the acquisition of real or personal property, services, or money or credit where the total consideration to be paid or furnished by the individual is $5,000 or less;
>
> (c) any agreement concerning or relating to insurance policies or annuity contracts except for those contracts between insurance companies; or
>
> (d) claims for workers' compensation.
>
> (3) A written agreement between members of a trade or professional organization to submit to arbitration any controversies arising between members of the trade or professional organization after the agreement is made is valid and enforceable except upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract.
>
> (4) Notice that a contract is subject to arbitration pursuant to this chapter shall be typed in underlined capital letters on the first page of the contract; and unless such notice is displayed thereon, the contract may not be subject to arbitration.

The exclusions qualify subsection (2), with subsection (3)'s express qualification; they are not textually exceptions written into subsection (4). The lower judgment rests on subsection (4), not a holding that this franchise falls under one of subsection (2)'s listed categories. Avoid reducing subsection (4) to a general conspicuousness standard: it specifies typing, underlining, capitals, first-page placement, and the “pursuant to this chapter” language.

## Remaining limits

- The complete amended complaint, complete lower memoranda, full hearing transcript, complete offering circular, and complete AAA rules are not reproduced here. Their absence from these two source items is not proof that they do not exist elsewhere. The JA includes only selected record documents.
- The June 1993 stay and the state notice holdings do not resolve generally applicable unconscionability or fraud defenses. Any assertion that such a separate ground was preserved, waived, adjudicated, or independently sufficient needs the merits briefs and, if those do not supply it, the relevant lower filing. The affidavit alone does not answer that procedural question.
- The choice-of-law issue was decided below, but the petition's federal question and requested relief should not be enlarged into a separate challenge to every aspect of Montana conflicts law. The petition expressly says independent resolution of that issue is unnecessary if its FAA argument succeeds.
- Printed source citations control over OCR artifacts. For example, a JA procedural entry renders the December opinion date as “12/15/95,” but the opinion itself, JA 12, states December 15, 1994; the petition duplicates that date. Other damaged OCR, including the apparent negation in the lower dissent's *Threlkeld* quotation, should not be copied as an accurate quotation of that earlier case without consulting its own text.
