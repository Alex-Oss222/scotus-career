# Ornelas — objective record and source facts

Source-only extract. The authorized May28,1996 simulated merits stage and review-standard scope are supplied premises. Historical Supreme Court petition treatment is omitted.

1. A December11,1992 vehicle stop and search produced approximately two kilograms of cocaine. The officers used characteristics of the car and travel and two NADDIS entries to investigate. Their assumptions about names did not establish that petitioners were the database subjects.
2. The government conceded the defendants could challenge the search and that a reasonable person would not have felt free to leave the encounter. The initial consent did not authorize dismantling the interior; the government did not argue otherwise in the first appeal.
3. The original magistrate accepted reasonable suspicion but found no probable cause for the intrusive search. He recommended denial of suppression through inevitable discovery. The district judge adopted the stop/consent conclusions and upheld the search on probable cause. The district judge corrected the initial finding about an officer's gun: it was not visible.
4. The first appellate opinion applied clear-error review to reasonable suspicion, rejected inevitable discovery as waived, and remanded the separate loose-panel credibility question. It did not decide whether the totality including a credited loose panel ultimately sufficed for probable cause.
5. On remand, the parties stipulated to use of the existing record. The magistrate credited the loose-panel testimony but rejected probable cause. The district judge accepted that credibility finding and reached the opposite ultimate conclusion. The second appellate order affirmed for want of clear error. These are established lower-court actions, not this Court's answer.
6. The petitioners entered conditional guilty pleas reserving suppression and received prison terms of sixty and sixty-three months. The selected docket entries and judicial orders confirm the plea mechanism; complete written plea agreements are not reproduced in the JA.
7. Petitioners request reversal and appellate remand under independent ultimate review. The United States seeks vacatur and appellate remand under the same standard while retaining its position that suppression should be denied. The Isakoff and AELE/NDAA amici seek affirmance under clear-error review.
8. Neither the separate NADDIS discovery claim nor initial Supreme Court adjudication of the stop/search merits forms the authorized question. No finding of suppression, guilt, damages, officer dishonesty beyond the lower credibility finding, or database reliability is added by this extraction.
