## Supplemental qualifications for the neutral packet

1. **Culpability remains unresolved.** District opinion footnote 8 explicitly states that the parties did not raise, and the court did not decide, the appropriate standard of care. It mentions the circuit's police-chase conscience standard, notes possible relevance outside that setting and possible differences where a special relationship exists, and reasons only that favorable pleading inferences might permit intentional or conscience-shocking conduct. A merits path recognizing an affirmative-danger claim still needs a supported account of culpability; no intent, recklessness, deliberate indifference, or conscience finding was made. Mere negligence is distinguished in the district court's text.
2. **Undercharging causation was not established.** Footnote 13 expressly says, although defendants had not raised it, that plaintiff had not sufficiently shown how charging more serious offenses would have prevented release. The main text also treats undercharging alone as insufficient under DeShaney. The en banc narrative and dissent's statements linking misdemeanor charges to release are judicial characterizations, not findings that another charge necessarily would have detained Pittman overnight.
3. **The assurance theory requires its actual allegations.** The material causal allegation is that Johnson's assurance changed Pinder's course of conduct: she returned to work instead of remaining with or arranging protection for the children. Do not reduce this to mere official knowledge of danger or a general promise to help; conversely, do not convert reliance into state custody. The alternative warning theory in Russell's dissent rests on dispelling the danger allegedly created by the assurance.
4. **A knowing lie is not established.** The en banc dissent calls the commissioner representation false, while the majority recounts the statement without making a knowing-falsity finding. The district and panel opinions omit that statement. The acquaintance with Pittman is alleged, and influence on charging is suggested; neither motive is found.
5. **Custody and historical-account differences persist.** The district court recounts probation after an arson conviction; the appellate accounts describe release after an attempted-arson sentence. District footnote 9's suggestion of technical custody of the attacker is an analogy to habeas usage, not a finding that the mother or children were held in state custody. Preserve the packet's differences in the threat wording and procedural vehicle.

## Competing supported paths and remedies

- A notice-only affirmance can leave the constitutional question open: DeShaney was decided eighteen days before the incident, rejects a duty based merely on knowledge and an expressed intention to help, and the preconduct cases did not all apply the same conception of affirmative danger. Hamilton's and Motz's writings provide narrower versions of this reasoning.
- A constitutional affirmance can characterize the allegations as failure to protect against a private actor rather than affirmative state creation of danger. It must address the alleged assurance-induced withdrawal of private protection and cannot merely substitute the conclusion that no one was in custody for analysis of that separate theory.
- Reversal or vacatur for further proceedings can rest on a properly particularized affirmative-danger rule and pre-March 10, 1989 notice. The vacated panel and en banc dissent rely on DeShaney's no-creation/no-increased-vulnerability language, Bowers, Archie, Wells, Ketchum, Escamilla and Jensen, and Anderson's rejection of an exact-facts precedent requirement. This path must distinguish the special-relationship proposition that DeShaney rejected, identify culpability and causal limits, and avoid relying on later cases for notice.
- The two theories can be treated differently. No path establishes liability, damages, disputed motive, the permissible criminal charges, or the release consequences as facts. The authorized legal immunity appeal uses assumed facts; unresolved fact sufficiency cannot be adjudicated through that channel.
