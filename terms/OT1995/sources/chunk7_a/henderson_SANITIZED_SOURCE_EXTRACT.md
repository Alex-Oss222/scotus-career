### Henderson enacted text and transition source limits

- Official Pub. L. 97-462, 96 Stat. 2527–2530, recovered at `frcp1982_act.pdf/.txt`, was read in full. Congress itself enacted former Rule 4(j): dismissal without prejudice if service is not made within 120 days and the serving party cannot show good cause; notice before sua sponte dismissal; exception for foreign-country service under former subdivision (i). Section 4 made the amendments effective 45 days after January 12, 1983; §5 prevented the separately delayed 1982 proposals from taking effect. This was not solely a judicial rulemaking act.
- Existing official SAA sources are `../A_Henderson_SAA_41Stat525_1920.txt/.pdf` and `../A_Henderson_SAA_amend_74Stat912_1960.txt/.pdf`. The 1960 amendment changes only §742's first sentence; its §4 applies that amendment to proceedings brought after enactment. The valid-service sentence remains the 1920 text “United States **and** such corporation.” The 1960 first sentence ends “any corporation mentioned in section 1 of this Act.” The briefs' variant wording is not the exact enacted text.
- The 1920 §743 includes private-party principles/practice, costs and four-percent interest (or higher contractual rate), appeal/revision, election to proceed on in-rem principles without foreclosing proper in-personam relief, and government bond/stipulation exceptions. The private-party-practice sentence is not the whole section.
- `../A_Henderson_46USCApp745_LII.txt` reproduces the provision with amendment notes through 1950: two-year commencement period; exclusivity against the responsible federal agent/employee where an SAA remedy exists; a special one-year-after-December-13-1950 savings provision for specified wrong-defendant suits; and a pre-suit-interest prohibition after June 30, 1932 except express contractual interest. The antique savings clause is not a general equitable refiling extension for this action. This is an archived later codification with notes establishing no intervening amendment, not a newly recovered 1994 official volume.
- The Fifth Circuit separately quotes the 1993 Rule 4(m) text, including dismissal **or** a direction to serve within a specified time and a required extension on good cause. The 1993 Advisory Committee note for subdivision (m), extracted at `rule4_1993_notes.txt`, expressly states that relief is authorized even without good cause and identifies a refiling limitations bar as a possible justification. Only this dated note subsection was substantively read for this point; a current rule-page display is not used as 1993 operative rule text.
- The papers use former 4(j) and the government calls the renumbering irrelevant. They do not analyze the promulgation order's pending-case transition. The original 1993 transition order was not recovered in this intake. Which rule applies to 1993 conduct, the 1994 dismissal, or later proceedings must not be collapsed into an automatic answer based on service date. The source gap does not establish a remaining Rule 4 defect or compel a new good-cause inquiry.
