## Material chronology, preservation and remedies

1. The complaint was filed April 8, 1993; the Attorney General received it May 25, day 47; a postponement motion was filed August 30, day 144; the United States Attorney was served September 3, day 148. This was not completed dual service within the original 120 days. P pp.4–5 and App.2; O p.2 lines190–221; PB pp.4–6 lines617–721. PB p.40 line2089 says April 3 once, inconsistent with its own repeated April 8 and the complaint; do not substitute this isolated error for the supported filing date. P p.4 calls May 19 the service date, while PB pp.5–6 and JA distinguish May19 mailing from May25 receipt.
2. The actual order is a **fifteen-day** direction to serve, signed August31 and filed/entered September1, postponing the scheduling conference because defendant remained unserved more than four months after filing. It did not expressly find good cause, diligence, or clerk fault. JA p.15 lines760–787. PB's “presumably finding good cause” and “on or before September15” are characterizations; the order says “within fifteen days.” JA contains no sixty-day service extension. Sixty days appears in the administrative-disallowance account and in the government's answer-time rule, not as a service enlargement.
3. Counsel attributes delay to missing requested forms, a summons without the clerk's seal, additional forms needed by the constable, telephone/mail follow-up, and forms finally received August25 bearing a July22 date. PB pp.4–6 lines617–714; R p.11 lines754–773. Government conditionally accepts the declarations but disputes diligence before those errors and argues the forthwith failure was already complete. RB pp.30–32 lines1575–1670. Neither the alleged clerk fault nor the government's lack-of-diligence characterization is an express judicial factual finding in the extension order.
4. The district court initially denied dismissal without opinion; after *Holmberg* it dismissed without prejudice under Rule12(b)(1). P App.2,8–9; O pp.2–4 lines217–250,272–274. The Fifth Circuit affirmed because §742's requirement was jurisdictional, the Rule4 extension could not alter it, 148 days was not forthwith even assuming good cause/reasonableness, and both federal recipients had to be served forthwith. It declined to decide whether day47 service on the Attorney General independently was forthwith. P App.3–7; O p.4 lines276–296. Government's motion to dismiss the appeal as frivolous was denied: the court expressly said the appeal was not frivolous. P App.7.
5. The Fifth Circuit did not adjudicate the separate due-process claim: dismissal was without prejudice and Henderson had not argued another suit would be time-barred. That is not a finding that refiling was timely. O p.4 n.2 lines299–305 expressly says the due-process claim was not pursued in this Court. P pp.5,19 and PB pp.6–7,40–42 nevertheless expressly say limitations had expired; PB pp.9,35–36 uses due-process/equal-protection concerns as statutory-interpretation support. A standalone constitutional judgment should not be inferred from that advocacy.
6. The complaint invoked SAA/PVA. Government argues that the Clarification Act directs this Maritime Administration seaman claim exclusively into SAA, and that PVA in any event incorporates §742. RB pp.8–9 n.9 lines730–743,759–760; O p.2 lines190–204. Compliance with the administrative claim requirement is recited in RB p.8 lines705–711. This is not a separate merits dispute over injury liability.
7. Reply expressly objects that the government's Galveston-versus-Houston division criticism is new in this Court, and §742 names districts rather than divisions. R p.11 lines737–751. Government's claim that counsel filed by mail is expressly qualified as an inference from an unclear record, RB p.31 n., lines1650–1661; do not convert it into a recovered fact or standalone established venue defect.
8. P's two questions and PB/R's repeated questions are supersession and, alternatively, judicial extension of SAA service time in appropriate circumstances. PB p.43 lines2191–2203 and R p.12 lines791–796 seek reversal and remand for trial on merits. RB p.32 lines1667–1677 seeks affirmance. A decision rejecting statutory dismissal need not itself decide injury liability, service-related findings not made below, or an unpresented constitutional question. A narrower remand must specify what remains unresolved; the request for trial is the litigant's requested remedy, not an already compelled merits disposition.

## Supported competing arguments and paths

**Supersession by a procedural rule.** Petitioner argues service is procedural under *Hanna* and *Murphree*, the waiver appears in §742's opening sentence(s), §743 calls for private-party practice, and 1966 unified admiralty and civil procedure. Congress intended uniform service and expressly provided in §2072(b) that conflicting laws lose force. PB pp.17–30; R pp.9–10 lines637–725. R invokes *Murphree* specifically to distinguish rules implementing personal service from enlargement of subject-matter jurisdiction under Rule82. PB pp.19–20 cites the registered/certified-mail difference as evidence that service mechanics are procedural; reply says that issue is not itself in controversy and does not concede complete equivalence of methods, R p.8. This path requires an actual conflict and a lawful procedural displacement basis, not the mere observation that consequences differ.

**Congress's direct enactment as a distinct source.** PB pp.31–33 lines1714–1833 separately argues Congress rewrote Rule4(j), including its 120-day provision and good-cause exception, in Pub.L.97-462. The government concedes that provenance and calls §2072(b)'s “such rules” technically inapplicable to the congressionally enacted rule, while agreeing that provision supplies the best evidence of congressional intent. RB p.16 n., lines1017–1026. Do not describe former4(j) as solely a Court-promulgated rule. Whether the later statute actually superseded §742 remains contested; this concession does not concede repeal.

**Coexistence without resolving immunity.** Government argues Rule4(j) is an outer limit commanding dismissal absent good cause after120 days, not an affirmative right to wait120 days. Prompt service satisfies both laws; a particular earlier statutory service requirement survives a general rule under ordinary coexistence/specific-general principles. RB pp.16–22 lines1007–1272. *Hanna* involved affirmative permission for an alternative manner of service and thus a direct clash; the government invokes *Walker* on that distinction. It acknowledges Rule4(j) protects against dismissal within120 days for noncompliance with that rule itself, but not independent statutes (RB pp.20–21 n., lines1201–1204,1237–1238). This is an independently supported affirmance rationale that should not be collapsed into jurisdictional immunity.

**Immunity as an alternative ground.** Government separately argues “forthwith” is a condition of the waiver, located in §742 with the grant of jurisdiction, while §743 governs procedure after jurisdiction attaches. It invokes §2072(b), Rule82, *Sherwood*, *Mitchell*, *McMahon*, and analogies to administrative presentment; RB pp.26–29 lines1417–1569. Petitioner answers with the distinction between consent and service implementing consent, and analogous replacement of Tucker Act service provisions by Rule4. PB pp.21–30; R pp.9–10. A decision on this ground must resolve the status of this particular service direction; it should not automatically treat every instruction in any waiver statute identically.

**Statutory extension/reasonableness alternative.** Petitioner expressly preserves power to extend even if the statute survives, analogizing to *American Pipe* and *McCormick* equitable tolling of §745, PB pp.38–42 lines2010–2183; P pp.17–20. PB p.36 lines1930–1942 and R p.12 lines779–788 also propose reading Rule4 together with §742 when assessing forthwith service. Government says tolling another limitations provision does little to construe this requirement, O p.7 lines407–419, and argues even conditional acceptance of counsel's declarations shows no prompt initial action, RB pp.29–32. Do not treat the analogy as an already established entitlement to extension or a factual good-cause finding. Nor should “forthwith” be converted into an absolute24-hour deadline: RB quotes “usually” and argues24-hour service practicable on these facts (pp.29,31 lines1541–1547,1624–1629).

**Legislative-purpose positions remain advocacy.** Petitioner relies on equal procedural treatment of government and private shipowners and wards-of-admiralty principles; government replies that special SAA rules for seizure, limitations, juries and interest refute categorical uniformity, and §743 fills gaps instead of erasing specific commands. RB pp.23–25 lines1296–1410; PB pp.17–27; R pp.4–6. Government identifies witness dispersal and easy location of designated federal recipients as reasons for prompt service; no actual Henderson-specific prejudice finding or concession appears in the recovered government papers. Its quoted1910 drafting exchange supplies legislative-history support that petitioner disputes in R p.7; original official legislative material remains a separate verification task.

## Rule4 transition and statutory source limits

- The papers quote former Rule4(j), governing at filing/service:120 days, mandatory dismissal without prejudice absent good cause, and foreign-service exception. RB pp.7–8 lines669–702 and App.3a lines1807–1827. PB's quotation at p.4 omits the foreign-service exception, although its p.35 acknowledges it; neutral text should use the full rule when exact text matters.
- Both parties acknowledge renumbering to4(m). Government states the December1,1993 amendment was irrelevant (RB p.8 lines700–702; O p.3 n.1 lines253–269), but does not analyze the pending-case transition order or broader discretion to extend without good cause. The reproduced lower opinion quotes the new4(m) language separately from4(j), P App.4, including “dismiss ... or direct that service be effected within a specified time” and mandatory extension if good cause is shown. Rule4(m) transition therefore remains a legal/source inquiry; these briefs do not prove that old4(j) must govern every remand or that good cause remains essential under a later applicable rule.
- The sources support separate questions: what law applied to the1993 extension; what law applied when dismissal entered in1994; and what law governs further proceedings after review. Do not import an answer from the mere fact that the service occurred before December1,1993. If a remand requires an extension finding, current rule applicability, the existing order, and any preserved Rule4 objection need express treatment.
- §742 quotations differ between petitioner and respondent versions (“against the United States or against any corporation...” versus the operative first-sentence form; and “and”/“or” in the valid-service sentence). Official enacted/codified text, the1960 amendment, §743 in full, §745 in full, §2072, Rule82, Pub.L.97-462, and the1993 promulgation/transition order should control exact statutory/rule language. This receipt does not independently authenticate those instruments.
