## Court rule and its record-specific application

At 321 the Court states:

> A stay of execution pending disposition of a second or successive federal habeas petition should be granted only when there are “substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted.”

The quotation is from *Barefoot*, 463 U.S. at 895. The Court then identifies its reason for finding no such ground: the particular fourth petition clearly abused the writ. It cites then-operative Habeas Rule 9(b) and §2244(b), examines the asserted claim's prior availability, and concludes at 322 that the District Court abused its discretion. The opinion does not announce a rule that a numerical count of petitions alone proves abuse. It does not discuss the issuance or scope of a certificate of probable cause. It supplies no general prescribed duration for every execution stay.

The Court's recitation at 320–321 places the execution warrant, three earlier federal petitions, prior Supreme Court stay request, fourth petition, District Court stay, panel review, en banc review, and State application in their actual procedural sequence. The stay was entered by the District Court; the appellate court declined to vacate it. Those are this source's facts, not facts about any later application.

## Distinct noncontrolling explanations

Kennedy's §1651 discussion at 323 concerns failure by a court of appeals to act with sufficient promptness and cites *Maxwell v. Bishop*, 385 U.S. 650 (1967). Neither that discussion nor the cited case was silently incorporated into the per curiam opinion. This reading verifies Kennedy's actual words and joins; it does not independently read *Maxwell* or supply a complete standalone statutory source for §1651.

Brennan's “flagrantly abused its discretion” language at 324 is a quotation appearing in his dissent, followed by his argument for considerable lower-court discretion. It must not be substituted without qualification for the Court's own formulation. His quotation of *Barefoot*'s considerable-weight language at 325 is also part of his argument; *Barefoot*'s original procedural setting must be preserved when relying on that passage.

The dissent's starred note begins on 323 and continues on 324. It says the challenged claim was raised in an April 5 amendment before the current execution date was set; the earlier stay was dissolved April 24 and the Missouri Supreme Court set the May 11 execution date on April 27. That is a material dissenting account of timing, fully read and retained. The Court's account and Brennan's criticism must be attributed, not merged into a new finding.
