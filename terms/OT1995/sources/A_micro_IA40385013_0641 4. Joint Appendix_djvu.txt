Plaintiff LLOYD HENDERSON is a resident citizen of

Defendant UNITED STATES OF AMERICA is a sov- 
ereign which has consented to be sued herein under the 
Suits in Admiralty Act, 46 U.S.C. secs. 741-752, and the 
Public Vessels Act, 46 U.S.C. secs 781-790 and pursuant to 


Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(d)(5) by delivering a

At all times material hereto, Defendant UNITED

intercoastal, and foreign commerce. 


Il. 


Venue is proper in the Southern District of Texas, 
pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391(a), which states that the 
claim may be brought where Plaintiff resides. Plaintiff 
resides within the Southern District of Texas. 


Il. 


It has become necessary to bring this suit by reason 
of the serious and permanent injuries sustained by Plain- 
tiff, LLOYD HENDERSON, on or about August 27, 1991. 
On that occasion, Plaintiff was working in the course and 
scope of his employment for the Defendant, UNITED 
STATES OF AMERICA, more specifically, as a Merchant 
Marine for the UNITED STATES OF AMERICA and 
UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION 
and, by virtue of that employment, he was working on 


board a vessel owned and/or operated by Defendant, 
UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, which was on navigable 
waters. The Defendant was in the business of doing work 
for monetary profit with the use of vessels such as the 
one in which LLOYD HENDERSON was working at the 
time of his injuries. At the time of the injuries made the 
basis of this suit, Plaintiff, LLOYD HENDERSON was in 
the course and scope of his employment aboard the ves- 
sel in question. 


IV. 


By reason of the contractual relationship between 
Plaintiff LLOYD HENDERSON, as a member of the crew 
in navigable waters and the Defendant, UNITED STATES 
OF AMERICA, as employer of him, Plaintiff, LLOYD 
HENDERSON, is entitled to recover maintenance and 
cure for such time as he has been and will be either 
convalescing from his injuries under medical care or 
reaching his maximum improvement. This suit is there- 
fore also maintained for maintenance and cure for which 
this Defendant is obligated to Plaintiff, LLOYD HENDER- 
SON. 


Defendant, UNITED STATES OF AMERICA has been 
persistent in failing to furnish maintenance and cure to 
Plaintiff, LLOYD HENDERSON. Accordingly, Plaintiff 
herein sues for damages arising from Defendant's failure 
to pay maintenance and cure. 


Additionally, UNITED STATES OF AMERICA’s fail- 
ure to promptly pay maintenance and cure was willful 
and wanton and in callous and flagrant disregard of 
Plaintiff's rights. Such conduct on the part of Defendant 


UNITED STATES OF AMERICA was intentional, recal- 
citrant, arbitrary and capricious. Defendant UNITED 
STATES OF AMERICA has no reasonable excuse for its 
refusal to provide maintenance and cure and has exhib- 
ited laxness in their investigation of Plaintiff's claim. 
Moreover, Defendant UNITED STATES OF AMERICA has 
made intentional misrepresentations to Plaintiff regard- 
ing his entitlement to maintenance and cure benefits 
which actions constituted fraud, misrepresentation and 
conspiracy to commit same. Such fraud and misrepresen- 
tation on the part of Defendant UNITED STATES OF 
AMERICA in conjunction with the wrongful refusal to 
provide maintenance and cure benefits and their callous 
disregard for Plaintiff's rights, as set out above, was to 
such a degree that punitive damages should be assessed 
and awarded against Defendant, UNITED STATES OF 
AMERICA, for its intentional acts, as set out above, in 
failing to provide your Plaintiff with maintenance and 
cure. 


Additionally, because of Defendant UNITED STATES 
OF AMERICA’s wrongful refusal to promptly provide 
Plaintiff with maintenance and cure benefits, Plaintiff was 
forced to hire the undersigned counsel in order to recover 
the maintenance and cure benefits plainly owed Plaintiff 
and an award of attorney’s fees should be assessed 
against Defendant, UNITED STATES OF AMERICA for its 
arbitrary and capricious actions, as set out above, in 
failing to provide Plaintiff with maintenance and cure, 
which actions forced Plaintiff to hire the undersigned 
counsel. 


V. 


This case is instituted under and is controlled to an 
extent by the terms of what is commonly known as the 
“Jones Act.” Plaintiff, LLOYD HENDERSON, would 
show that nothing he did or failed to do on the occasion 
in question caused or in any way contributed to cause his 
injuries. To the contrary, the occurrence in which Plaintiff 
was injured and the injuries he sustained were prox- 
imately caused by the negligence, as that term is under- 
stood in law, of the Defendant, their agents, servants and 
employees, who were acting in the course and scope of 
their employment for the Defendant at all material to this 
action. Plaintiff, LLOYD HENDERSON, was employed by 
the Defendant, UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, and was 
injured in the course and scope of his employment while 
on board the vessel in question. The Defendant, UNITED 
STATES OF AMERICA, owed to Plaintiff a duty to furnish 
him a safe place to work and a seaworthy vessel, and as 
applied to Plaintiff, LLOYD HENDERSON, the Defendant 
failed in those respects in such unseaworthiness of the 
vessel in question caused and resulted in the injuries and 
damages sustained by Plaintiff. These conditions were 
brought about and caused by the named Defendant. 


Additionally, Plaintiff would show that the 
unseaworthy condition of the vessel in question was the 
result of the willful violation by Defendant, UNITED 
STATES OF AMERICA of its duties to furnish and main- 
tain a seaworthy vessel. Accordingly, punitive damages 
should be assessed against UNITED STATES OF AMER- 
ICA for its willful acts and conscious and purposeful 
disregard of its duty to provide a seaworthy vessel.

LLOYD HENDERSON, sustained bodily injuries. By rea-

VIL. 


The Defendant's conduct was in reckless disregard of

STATEMENT OF FACTS AND INTRODUCTION 


The plaintiff, Lloyd Henderson, claims he suffered 
injuries on August 29, 1991, while working as a seaman 
aboard the M/V DELMONTE, a vessel owned by the 
United States through the Maritime Administration 
(MarAd) and managed by its’ General Agent. On May 29, 
1992, the plaintiff filed an administrative claim, as 
required by the Clarification Act, 50 U.S.C. app. § 1291. 
Sixty days later, the claim was administratively disal- 
lowed by MarAd pursuant to regulations contained in 46 
C.F.R. Part 327.7. On April 8, 1993, this suit was filed in 
the Southern District of Texas against the United States. 
[See attached copy of first page of plaintiff's Complaint]. 
Time passed. On September 3, 1993, 150 days after filing 
the Complaint, the plaintiff's properly effected service 
upon the United States Attorney. [See attached copy of

Summons].' Subsequently, the case was transferred to 
this Court. 


This Court's jurisdiction over the plaintiff's claim 
derives from the Suits in Admiralty Act (SAA), 46 U.S.C. 
app. §§ 741-752. Pursuant to § 742 of the SAA, the plain- 
tiff’s claim against the United States should be dismissed 
pursuant to Rule 12(b) (1) for failure to affect forthwith 
service on the United States. 


ARGUMENT 


I. The Forthwith Service Provision Is A Jurisdictional 
Prerequisite. 


As an initial matter, the burden of establishing sub- 
ject matter jurisdiction lies with the plaintiff, McNutt v. 
General Motors Acceptance Corp., 298 U.S. 178, 189 (1936); 
government attorneys have “no power to waive condi- 
tions or limitations imposed by statute in respect of suits 
against the United States,” Munro v. United States, 303 
U.S. 36, 4. (1938) (citation omitted); and the immunity of 
the United States is not to be waived by a court's policy 
based determinations, however compelling. Library Of 
Congress v. Shaw, 478 U.S. 310, 321 (1985). It is equally 
well established that 


[t}he United States, as sovereign, is immune

IV. Forthwith Service Is A Condition Precedent To The 
Substantive Right To Sue The United States, And 
Cannot Be Superseded By Rule 4(j) Federal Rules Of 
Civil Procedure. 


The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure were enacted in 
1938 and enabled by the Rules Enabling Act of 1934 
(1988) which states, in pertinent part: “The Supreme 
Court shall have the power to prescribe general rules of 
practice and procedure. . . . Such rules shall not abridge, 
enlarge or modify any substantive right. All laws in con- 
flict with such rules shall be of no further force or 
effect...” 28 U.S.C. § 2072(a), (b) (1988). See Hanna v. 
Plumer, 380 U.S. 460, 470-71 (1965). Only if deemed “pro- 
cedural” can a law be superseded by rules promulgated 
pursuant to the Rules Enabling Act. See Sibbach v. Wilson, 
312 U.S. 1, 14 (1940) (defining procedure as “the judicial 
process for enforcing rights and duties recognized by 


29 


substantive law”); c.f. Amella, 732 F.2d at 713 (the assump- 
tion that a Federal Rule of Civil Procedure can amend an 
act of Congress is dubious).° 


The Supreme Court in Sherwood addressed the effect 


of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure upon the United 
States’ waiver of sovereign immunity: 


An authority conferred upon a court to make 
rules of procedure for the exercise of its jurisdic- 
tion is not an authority to enlarge that jurisdic- 
tion . 

... The matter is one not one of procedure but 
of jurisdiction whose limits are marked by the 
Government's consent to be sued. .. . The juris- 
diction [limited by conditions contained in the 
statute] is unaffected by the Rules of Civil Pro- 
cedure, which prescribe the methods by which 
the jurisdiction of the federal courts is to be 
exercised but do not enlarge the jurisdiction. 


Id. 312 U.S. at 589-90 (quoted in Libby, 840 F.2d at 820).

but is an integral element of the maritime waiver of 
sovereign immunity granted by the SAA, no procedural 
rule may supersede the forthwith service provision. Sib-

tion conferred by statute pursuant to the Rules Enabling 
Act); see Schlagenhauf v. Holder, 379 U.S. 104, 113 (1964) (a

ORDER OF DISMISSAL 
(Filed May 24, 1991) 


CAME ON TO BE HEARD this day the motion of the 
defendant, United States of America, to dismiss the plain- 
tiffs’ complaint and amended complaint in the above- 
captioned and numbered cause, and the Court, after con- 
sidering said motion, the response and the various argu- 
ments of counsel, is of the opinion that the motion is 
meritorious and should be granted. 


The facts of the matter are as follows. The plaintiff 
filed a complaint with the court naming the United States 
Department of Energy (DOE) as the defendant on Octo- 
ber 31, 1990. This complaint has not been served on DOE, 
the United States Attorney or the Attorney General of the 
United States. The statute of limitations ran on November 
3, 1990, pursuant to the two year limitations provision of 
the Suits in Admiralty Act (SAA). 46 U.S.C. §§ 741, 745. 
On November 26th, 1990, the DOE received a notice from 
the court indicating suit had been filed. DOE called the 
United States Attorney, who in turn contacted plaintiff's 
counsel and told plaintiff's counsel that the wrong party 
had been sued (DOE instead of the United States) and 
that service needed to be effected on the US. Attorney 


36 


General and the United States Attorney for the Eastern 
District of Texas. On December 5th, 1990, the plaintiff 
filed an amended complaint naming the correct parties 
which was served on the U.S. Attorney on the same day 
and on the Attorney General on December 10th, 1990. 


The plaintiff argues that under Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(c) 
the amended complaint should be allowed to relate back 
to the original complaint, which was filed prior to the 
expiration of the statute of limitations. The U.S. argues 
that because the incorrect party was sued in the original 
complaint and because the correct party was not served 
before the statute of limitations ran, this court does not 
have subject matter jurisdiction of the suit and there is 
nothing for the amended complaint to relate back to 
under the rule. 


The Fifth Circuit has held that statutes which 
embody the waiver of sovereign immunity are to be 
strictly construed. Carr v. Veterans Administration, 522 F.2d 
1355, 1357 (5th Cir. 1976). The SAA requires the United 
States of America be sued. 46 U.S.C. § 745; Williams v. 
U.S., 711 F.2d 893, 897-898 (9th Cir. 1983); Doyle v. 
Bethlehem Steel Corp., 504 F.2d 911, 912-913 (Sth Cir. 1974). 
The SAA further requires service on the Attorney General 
and the United States Attorney. 46 U.S.C. § 742. In this 
case, the United States was not served prior to the expira- 
tion of the statute of limitations on November 3rd, 1990 
(nor was the DOE, even though they were not a proper 
party). Thus, this court cannot allow the relation back of 
the amended complaint because there is no valid com- 
plaint filed within the statute of limitations to which it 
may properly relate back. The plaintiffs’ argument that 
the DOE had notice of the plaintiffs’ “claims” for over a 


37 


year fails to properly apply Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(c), which 
requires notice of the institution of the “action” against it. 
Notice of administrative claims is not equivalent to notice 
that a lawsuit has been filed. Craig v. U.S., 413 F.2d 854, 
858 (9th Cir.), cert. denied, 396 U.S. 987 (1969); Holden v. 
R.J. Reynolds Ind., 82 F.D.R.157, 161 (M.D. N.C. 1979). As 
DOE did not receive notice of the institution of this suit 
within the limitations period, this argument is unpersua- 
sive.! 


It is, therefore, ORDERED that the motion of the 
defendant, the United States of America, is GRANTED, 
and the complaint and amended complaint of the plain- 
tiffs are DISMISSED.

ll. The requirement of “forthwith” service has been 
superseded by Fed. R. Civ. P. 4. 


Defendant contends that Plaintiff’s service on the 
United States Attorney for the Southern District of Texas 
was not “forthwith” as provided by the Suits in Admi- 
ralty Act, 42 U.S.C. § 742. See Defendant's Brief, pp. 9-10. 
The question of whether “forthwith” service is a jurisdic- 
tional requirement is unsettled in the Fifth Circuit. 


The Second, Ninth, and Eleventh Circuits have held 
that “forthwith” service is jurisdictional. Batttaglia [sic] v. 
United States, 303 F.2d 683 (2nd Cir.), cert. dismissed, 371 
U.S. 907 (1962), Amella v. United States, 732 F.2d 711 (9th 
Cir. 1984), Libby v. United States, 840 F.2d 818 (11th Cir. 
1988). The Third Circuit, however, has held that the 
“forthwith” provision is merely procedural, and was

supplanted by Fed. R. Civ. P. 4. Jones & Laughlin Steel, Inc. 
v. Mon River Towing, Inc., 772 F.2d 62 (3rd Cir. 1985).° 


The Fifth Circuit has not addressed the issue. The 
case relied upon by Defendant, Kieu v. United States, 1993 
AMC 1789 (5th Cir. 1992) (not otherwise reported), dealt 
with an entirely different issue. In Kieu, the Plaintiff 
failed to name the United States as defendant in a Suits 
in Admiralty action until after the two year statute of 
limitations had expired. Id. The case was decided on the 
basis that the Plaintiff's amended pleadings could not 
“relate back” to the original statute of limitations date. A 
careful review of the district court's order of dismissal, 
attached to Defendant's Brief, confirms this. Kieu v. 
United States Department of Energy, No. 90-CV-861, slip. 
op. (E.D. Tex. May 24, 1991) (Schell, J.) (“this court cannot 
allow the relation back of the amended complaint because 
there is no valid complaint filed within the statute of 
limitations to which it may properly relate back”). 


The holding of Jones & Laughlin has found favor with 
district courts of the Fifth Circuit. See Estrain v. United 
States, No. 92-479, 1992 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 7946 (E.D. La. 
June 2, 1992) (decided after Kieu); Diversified Marine Int'l, 
Inc. v. United States, 774 F.Supp. 1005, 1008 (E.D. La. 1991) 
(“this Court agrees the Third Circuit has adopted the 
better position”); Farnsworth v. Sea-Land Service, Inc., No. 
87-5954, 1989 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 2270 (E.D. La. March 7, 
1989); Phillips v. United States Army Corps of Engineers, 629 
F.Supp. 967 (S.D. Miss. 1986). Contra Landry v. United

Ill. Plaintiff's service was “forthwith,” in that Plaintiff 
affected service with reasonable promptness, dili- 
gence and dispatch. 


Even if “forthwith” service is jurisdictional, and it is 
not, Defendant’s motion ignores that Plaintiff did affect 
service in a “forthwith” manner. As stated by the Fifth 
Circuit, “(t)}he word ‘forthwith’ is deliberately unde- 
fined, in our view, to allow courts to interpret it in the 
context of ‘reasonableness,’ on a case-by-case basis. We 
are convinced that ‘forthwith’ requires no more or less 
than reasonable promptness, diligence or dispatch.” 
United States v. Bradley, 428 F.2d 1013, 1015-16 (Sth Cir. 
1970) (emphasis supplied). 


In the instant case, summons were requested the 
same instant that suit was filed. See Affidavits of Dagnee 
McKinney, Melanie Seagler, and J. K. Ivey. The Summons 
was forwarded for service upon receipt of the same from 
the District Clerk for the Southern District of Texas. Id. 
The Constable returned the Summons once and lost it 
once. The matter was reviewed by Judge Gibson, who 


48 


granted Plaintiff a fifteen (15) day extension to complete 
service. 


Plaintiff attempted to perfect service in April, May, 
June, July, and“August, 1993. Unlike cases cited by Defen- 
dant where no efforts were made to obtain service, or an 
incorrect party was named as Defendant, Plaintiff has 
exercised reasonable promptness, diligence, and dispatch. 
Bradley, 428 F.2d at 1016. There is no evidence before the 
Court that Plaintiff has engaged in any delay of his own 
making. Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Dismiss 
should be, in all things, DENIED. 


IV. Judge Gibson has already impliedly found “due dili- 
gence” and “good cause” on Plaintiff’s part. 


As noted above, the law of the case is that “due 
diligence” has been exercised by Plaintiff. On August 31, 
1993, the Hon. Hugh Gibson, a judge of the Southern 
District of Texas, Galveston Division, considered Plain- 
tiff’s difficulties in obtaining service, and granted Plain- 
tiff a fifteen (15) day extension from the initial scheduling 
conference to obtain service on the United States Attor- 
ney’s office. It is inconsistent to hold that service was not 
“forthwith,” when another judge has found “good cause” 
to grant a fifteen (15) day extension to obtain service. 
Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Dismiss should be, in 
all things, DENIED. 


CONCLUSION 


“Equity abhors forfeiture,” is well established princi- 
ple of the law for good cause. Plaintiff has sustained

serious injuries that will go unredressed if Defendant's 
Motion is granted. 


Plaintiff contends that “forthwith” service is pro- 
cedural, not substantive, as numerous district courts of 
the Fifth Circuit have agreed. Even if “forthwith” service 
is jurisdictional, this court has latitude to decide on a 
“case-by-case” basis if “forthwith” service has been 
obtained. In this case, Plaintiff made persistent, repeated 
efforts to obtain service that were only delayed by the 
United States District Clerk’s and the Harris County Con- 
stable’s offices. This is not a matter to be decided by a 
form brief such as the one filed by Defendant,4 but by 
careful analysis of the facts. 


For the foregoing reasons, Defendant's Motion to 
Dismiss should be, in all things, DENIED.

STATEMENT OF FACTS AND INTRODUCTION 


The plaintiff, Lloyd Henderson, claims he suffered 
injuries while working as a seaman aboard a vessel 
owned by the United States. His claim is enforceable 
pursuant to the Suits in Admiralty Act (SAA), 46 U.S.C. 
app. §§ 741-752. On April 8, 1993, the plaintiff filed suit in 
the Southern District of Texas. On September 3, 1993, 150 
days after filing the complaint, the plaintiff properly 
effected service upon the United States. Subsequently, the 
case was transferred to this Court. 


On January 21, 1994, this Court denied the United 
States’ motion to dismiss for lack of subject matter juris- 
diction for failure to effect “forthwith” service as required 
by § 742 of the SAA. The trial date, however, was 
extended pending issuance of a controlling decision from 
the Fifth Circuit concerning forthwith service. United 
States v. Holmberg, Appeal No. 3468, (Fifth Cir. May 2, 


1994). According to the holding in that case, this action 
should be dismissed for lack of subject matter jurisdic- 
tion. 


ARGUMENT 


The Plaintiff's Action Should Be Dismissed Pursuant To 
Fifth Circuit Precedent In Holmberg. 


In Holmberg, the plaintiff failed to effect service of 
process until 106 days after filing his complaint. Like the 
instant case, the United States moved to dismiss pursuant 
to Rule 12(b)(1) for failure to serve forthwith. The district 
court in Holmberg denied the motion but certified the 
question of whether the forthwith service provision was 
superseded by Rule 4(J), Fed. R. Civ. P. Holmberg v. OMI 
Ship Management, Inc., No. 92-3749, WL 165774 (E.D. La. 
May 6, 1993). 


On appeal, the Fifth Circuit held that the forthwith 
service requirement of the SAA is a jurisdictional prereq- 
uisite to sue the United States which was not modified by 
Rule 4(J). While the Holmberg court did not define (in 
terms of days) what constituted forthwith service, the 
court concluded “that under any definition, service in 103 
or 106 days is not forthwith.” Holmberg at 3835. The court 
summarily rejected the plaintiff's argument that the hold- 
ing in Holmberg should not have retroactive effect, then 
reversed the district court’s decision and remanded with 
instructions to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdic- 
tion. Id. 


In the instant case, Henderson effected proper service 
upon the United States at 150 Days. The Holmberg court 
held that the forthwith service provision of the SAA is 


> 


90 


jurisdictional and service at 103 days is not forthwith. In 
fact, the court referred favorably to circuit court decisions 
holding that service after 60 days is not forthwith. Id. 
(citing Amella v. United States, 303 F.2d 711 (9th Cir. 1984); 
City of New York v. McAllister Bros, Inc., 278 F.2d 708, 710 
(2d cir. 1960)). According to Fifth Circuit precedent as 
established in Holmberg, the plaintiff's service of process 
at 150 days does not comply with the forthwith service 
requirement contained in § 742 of the SAA and, therefore, 
the complaint should be dismissed. 


CONCLUSION 


For the foregoing reasons, the plaintiff's complaint 
should be dismissed for lack of subject matter jurisdic- 
tion. Rule 12(b)(1), Fed. R. Civ. P.

ARGUMENT 


I. Plaintiff's service was “forthwith,” in that Plaintiff 
affected service with reasonable promptness, dili- 
gence and dispatch. 


Defendant’s Motion presents, for the third time, its 
time worn argument that “forthwith” service is jurisdic- 
tional. See Order denying Motion to Dismiss dated Janu- 
ary 21, 1994. Defendant’s motion ignores that Plaintiff did 
affect service in a “forthwith” manner. As stated by the 
Fifth Circuit, “(t)}he word ‘forthwith’ is deliberately 
undefined, in our view, to allow courts to interpret it in 
the context of ‘reasonableness,’ on a case-by-case basis. 
We are convinced that ‘forthwith’ requires no more or 
less than reasonable promptness, diligence or dispatch.” 
United States v. Bradley, 428 F.2d 1013, 1015-16 (5th Cir. 
1970) (emphasis supplied); See United States v. Holmberg, 
19 F3rd 1062 (5th Cir. 1994) (same). 


In the instant case, summons were requested the 
same instant that suit was filed. See Affidavits of Dagnee 
McKinney, Melanie Seagler, and J. K. Ivey. The Summons 
was forwarded for service upon receipt of the same from 
the District Clerk for the Southern District of Texas. Id. 
The Constable returned the Summons once and lost it 
once. The matter was reviewed by Judge Gibson, who 
granted Plaintiff a fifteen (15) day extension to complete 
service. 


Plaintiff attempted to perfect service in April, May, 
June, July, and August, 1993. Unlike cases cited by Defen- 
dant where no efforts were made to obtain service, or an 
incorrect party was named as Defendant, Plaintiff has 
exercised reasonable promptness, diligence, and dispatch. 


96 


Bradley, 428 F.2d at 1016. There is no evidence before the 
Court that Plaintiff has engaged in any delay of his own 
making. Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Dismiss 
should be, in all things, DENIED. 


Il. Judge Gibson and this Court have already found 
“due diligence” and “good cause” on Plaintiff’s part. 


As noted above, the law of the case is that “due 
diligence” has been exercised by Plaintiff. On August 31, 
1993, the Hon. Hugh Gibson, a judge of the Southern 
District of Texas, Galveston Division, considered Plain- 
tiff’s difficulties in obtaining service, and granted Plain- 
tiff a fifteen (15) day extension from the initial scheduling 
conference to obtain service on the United States Attor- 
ney’s office. It is inconsistent to hold that service was not 
“forthwith,” when another judge has found “good cause”

CONCLUSION 


“Equity abhors forfeiture,” is well established princi- 
ple of the law for good cause. Plaintiff has sustained 
serious injuries that will go unredressed if Defendant's 
Motion is granted. 


97 


Plaintiff contends that this Court has latitude to 
decide on a “case-by-case” basis if “forthwith” service 
has been obtained. In this case, Plaintiff made persistent, 
repeated efforts to obtain service that were only delayed 
by the United States District Clerk’s and the Harris 
County Constable’s offices. 


For the foregoing reasons, Defendant's Motion to 
Dismiss should be, in all things, DENIED.
