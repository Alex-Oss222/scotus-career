## Differences and limits in the reported accounts

The district-denial sentence reports three classifications: abusive, successive, or procedurally defaulted. The description of Hays's report adds a fourth alternative, meritless. Both formulations are retained. They do not identify which claim received which classification, whether any classification was an alternative ground, or how the district court treated any particular merits analysis.

The account of the summary order's lack of explanation and the separate account of the certificate of probable cause and argument setting must both be retained. The latter are concrete appellate actions relevant to any assessment of the record. They are not quoted reasons for the stay, a finding about any identified claim, or evidence of the certificate's precise scope. R1 reproduces no opposing allegation, evidentiary account, or claim-specific factual disagreement to extract. Disagreement about the implications of these procedural facts supplies no additional underlying fact.
