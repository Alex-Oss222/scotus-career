# Bowersox — additional reported facts predating the pending application

**Matter and factual cutoff:** A-828; facts predating consideration on April 9, 1996. This supplements, and does not alter, `REPORTED_RECORD_FACTS.md`.

**Source status:** R2 is the published report *Williams v. Delo*, 82 F.3d 781–785 (8th Cir. April 9, 1996), independently obtained through OpenJurist and the Caselaw Access Project (CAP). Its publication concerns a later procedural stage on that date. Only its retrospective descriptions of an already filed petition, proposed amendment, earlier evidence, and earlier district proceedings are extracted here. Its subsequent events, appellate findings, reasoning, disposition, and descriptions of the present Supreme Court's action are excluded. R2 is a reporting source for those earlier contents, not an entered event or governing appellate decision in this handoff. Statements whose timing cannot be separated from the later proceedings are identified below rather than backdated.

R3 is the pre-event published report *Williams v. Armontrout*, 912 F.2d 924–942 (8th Cir. August 15, 1990; reported rehearing material October 12, 1990), obtained from CAP and read completely. It directly corroborates parts of the earlier petition history and reproduces portions of the trial record. Printed pages and CAP paragraph IDs below allow exact checking without identifying the current Supreme Court's authors or alignment. No original petition, transcript, expert report, or Hays report is represented as recovered.

## Third-petition contents and prior district proceedings reported by R2

1. **Penalty-phase ineffective assistance claim.** R2 identifies a claim that trial counsel provided ineffective assistance during the penalty phase. It reports that Williams did not raise this claim in his second federal petition. This is a retrospective report of the claim's subject and prior presentation, not an independent finding about deficient performance, prejudice, or the legal effect of the omission. R2, pp. 783–784, paragraph beginning “Williams is not entitled,” CAP `b869-17`.

2. **Confinement account and permission to challenge the sentence.** In connection with the earlier omission, R2 reports Williams's account that conditions at the Missouri State Penitentiary were so bad that he preferred dying to remaining there. It reports that he presented evidence that he did not give counsel permission to challenge his death sentence until 1989, after transfer to a different prison. The evidentiary presentation is reported; the account's truth is disputed, and neither the underlying testimony nor permission document is reproduced. R2, pp. 783–784, `b869-17`.

3. **Contrary record account and district finding.** R2 reports that Williams filed grievances and civil-rights suits seeking improvements in his conditions, and that he represented to the district court that he had raised all legitimate challenges to his death sentence. It reports the district court's finding that prison conditions did not cause him to omit claims from the second petition. These are, respectively, reported conduct, a reported litigant representation, and a **reported district finding**. They are not independent conclusions by this extractor. R2 also refers to Williams's expert witnesses and the district court's treatment of their opinions, but supplies neither their names, their actual opinions, nor their evidentiary bases. The contrary confinement/permission evidence in item 2 must remain alongside this account. R2, p. 784, paragraph beginning “Williams's argument about his conditions,” CAP `b870-4`.

4. **Earlier opportunity reported.** R2 reports that the second petition came before the en banc court in 1990, after the transfer, and that Williams neither told that court that past confinement conditions had prevented presentation of the ineffective-assistance claim nor requested a remand to present it. This is a retrospective description of earlier submissions; those submissions themselves have not been recovered. No waiver or cause conclusion is supplied. R2, pp. 783–784, `b869-17`.

5. **First-degree felony-murder instruction claim.** R2 identifies a due-process claim concerning the trial court's failure to instruct on first-degree felony murder, and reports that Williams raised this claim in the second petition. R3 directly corroborates the earlier presentation and consideration; see below. R2, p. 784, paragraph beginning “Williams also cannot prevail,” CAP `b870-5`.

6. **Second-degree felony-murder instruction claim.** R2 separately identifies a claim that failure to instruct on second-degree felony murder violated due process and equal protection. It reports that the claim was not presented to the original appellate panel in the earlier petition's appeal. R2's discussion conditionally addresses whether it had been raised to the en banc court; it does not establish that contested presentation as an undisputed historical fact. R3's text does not supply the original panel briefs or resolve the complete presentation history. First-degree and second-degree instruction claims must not be conflated. R2, p. 784, paragraph beginning “Further, we cannot review,” CAP `b870-8`.

7. **Proposed amendment and its timing.** R2 reports that Williams moved to add a claim after the petition had been pending for four years, discovery was complete, a magistrate had prepared a report and recommendation, and the district court was ready to rule. These are relative procedural descriptions, not an exact filing date or a finding about counsel's motive. R2, pp. 784–785, paragraph beginning “Williams also contends,” CAP `b870-9`.

8. **Proposed proportionality-review claim.** The proposed amendment asserted that the Missouri Supreme Court violated due process because approximately two hundred Missouri capital-murder cases were absent from the files used in reviewing the proportionality of Williams's sentence. This is **Williams's allegation**, not proof that the cases were missing or material. No list of those cases or comparison of their facts is reproduced. R2, pp. 784–785, `b870-9`. The already reported denial of amendment remains supported by R1; no subsequent appellate disposition is imported.

9. **Earlier district certificate ruling.** R2 refers to the district court's refusal to grant Williams a certificate of probable cause. Only that earlier district act is extracted from the reference; its date and original text are not supplied. R2, p. 785, paragraph beginning “Having carefully examined,” CAP `b871-5`. R1 separately supports the appellate court's issuance of a certificate.

**Dating limit on additional argument descriptions:** R2 describes requests to revisit the first-degree issue under unspecified newer Missouri decisions and an account blaming earlier habeas counsel for the second-degree issue's preservation. Its text does not separately date those particular appellate formulations. They are therefore not certified here as submissions made before the pending application. Likewise, no assertion in a later motion or merits brief is treated as a newly recovered earlier filing. No substantive conclusion expressed by the later appellate court is supplied.

## Direct earlier corroboration and competing trial-record accounts in R3

- **Earlier proceeding and disposition:** R3 identifies appellate No. 88-1342. It reports that the first federal petition was dismissed for nonexhaustion; after state postconviction proceedings, Williams filed the second petition, denied in *Williams v. Armontrout*, 679 F. Supp. 916 (W.D. Mo. 1988). R3 affirmed that denial. The cited 1988 district opinion is a locator for the **second** petition, not the January 1996 order. R3, pp. 927–928, CAP `b1009-11`, `b1010-3`–`b1010-4`; p. 935, `b1017-6`.
- **Earlier first-degree issue:** R3 expressly records Williams's contention that failure to instruct on first-degree felony murder denied due process because the death occurred during kidnapping. It also records an equal-protection challenge to the treatment of the first-degree instruction in the earlier proceeding. R3, p. 928, `b1010-6`. This independently establishes the earlier first-degree claim; it does not establish that every earlier theory was repeated in the third petition.
- **Trial ruling as quoted:** R3 quotes the state trial judge describing kidnapping as “merely one link in the chain of events” planned in committing murder and concluding there was “no independent collateral felony” supporting felony murder. Those are the trial court's stated conclusions, not stipulated facts about intent. The quotation cites State Trial Transcript 613–614. R3, p. 929, `b1011-7`–`b1011-12`; the fuller quoted passage also appears at p. 936, `b1018-5`–`b1018-10`.
- **Requested instruction:** R3 reports that **both defense counsel and the prosecutor** requested the first-degree instruction. R3, p. 936, `b1018-5`. The court's refusal cannot be recast as failure by defense counsel to request that instruction.
- **Physical sequence in the trial accounts:** The report describes Brummett being forcibly removed from a vehicle, handcuffed, placed alive in its trunk, and transported; an initial river location was occupied by campers; further stops and travel preceded his attempted escape and drowning. The more detailed account places the initial seizure fifteen to twenty feet from a highway, describes a search of the trunk while Brummett was alive and conscious, and describes another five or six miles of travel before leaving the highway again. R3, pp. 927–928, `b1009-16`–`b1010-3`; pp. 938–939, `b1020-14`–`b1021-6` (reported transcript references 282–294, 304–313, 419). These details are reported evidence; their significance for an independent kidnapping was contested.
- **Morgan's testimony and credibility dispute:** R3 reports that accomplice John Morgan received immunity in exchange for testifying. The account describing a preplanned killing depends on his testimony concerning the events. The report also describes impeachment addressing his own interest in Brummett's death, incentive to secure immunity, chronic drug use, and inconsistent statements to police. Those reported impeachment matters are retained alongside the inculpatory account; neither account becomes an independent credibility finding here. R3, p. 928, `b1010-3`; pp. 939–940, `b1021-11`, `b1021-13` (reported transcript references 318–329, 344–352, 629–633). R3, p. 934, `b1016-5`, also reports the state postconviction court's determination that immunity and inconsistent statements were placed before the jury.
- **Other ineffective-assistance claims in the second proceeding:** R3 discusses pretrial investigation, witness selection and cross-examination, and requested guilt-phase instructions. Thus the reported absence of the **penalty-phase** claim must not be generalized into an assertion that no ineffective-assistance claim had been raised previously. R3, pp. 932–934, `b1014-8` through `b1016-12`.

## Remaining source limits after this recovery

The claim subjects, proposed amendment, opposing confinement accounts, earlier district cause finding, and portions of prior presentation history now have published support. The absence of the original documents does not erase that support. The initial R1-only extract's statements about what **that source** fails to identify remain accurate; they are no longer a description of the complete available source base.

The recovered reports still do not identify the specific penalty-phase attorney omission, omitted mitigation, or resulting prejudice asserted in the third petition; reproduce the confinement experts' actual opinions or underlying evidence; provide the full earlier pleadings needed to settle the disputed second-degree presentation history; identify the newer Missouri decisions invoked in the later account; or list the allegedly omitted proportionality-review cases and their asserted relevance. They do not reproduce the Hays report, January 11 district order, original third petition or amendment, March 8 stay order, certificate, or application papers. This is not a certification that the reported claim list reproduces every ground in the petition.

These limits concern source coverage, not conduct by a party or omissions from a court's file. No claim, concession, evidentiary finding, new response, simulated service, or deadline event is supplied to fill them. This addendum does not decide whether any remaining gap is material to an available ruling.
