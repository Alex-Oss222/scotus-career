### 2. Protected national-bank power and the actual scope revision

**Reconciled reusable principle:** In construing a federal grant of national-bank power, Congress ordinarily does not leave a State free to prevent or significantly interfere with the exercise of the power Congress conferred. The actual grant and its statutory setting must first establish what power is conferred and the scope of its protection. An express incorporation of state permission or restriction remains effective. State regulation that does not prevent or significantly interfere with the exercise of the protected federal power remains permissible on this ground. Neither national-bank status, regulatory overlap, a general preference for banking efficiency, nor federal silence alone establishes the required conflict.

Section 92 supplies the affirmative power here. It adds insurance-agency activity to existing national-bank powers, specifies the eligible association and agency activities, preserves state authorization of the insurer, and provides federal regulatory and substantive conditions. Its reference to state-authorized insurers does not give the State an additional veto over the bank's agency capacity because of its affiliation. The challenged Florida provision forbids the very activity Congress authorized for a qualifying association. That prohibition conflicts with §92 even though the bank could comply with both enactments by abandoning the federal option. No showing that this particular bank would fail without insurance income is necessary to establish that conflict.

Franklin supplies the banking-power analogy; de la Cuesta establishes that an affirmative federally protected option need not be a mandatory activity. McClellan preserves compatible state regulation. The branching cases demonstrate the force of an actual congressional incorporation of state restrictions. Respondents' competitive-equality account and the insurer-authorization condition are substantial contrary arguments, but neither inserts the state-bank-parity condition absent from §92 or converts the insurer's licensing condition into a veto over national-bank affiliation.

**What changes from the frozen model:** The model expressly committed each Associate to the particular §92 option against a categorical affiliation ban and left materially different requirements to their own terms and operation. Its qualifications did not affirmatively settle a reusable significant-interference principle. The reconciliation now does: a restriction need not amount to total prohibition to fall within the principle, if its actual operation significantly impairs the federally protected power. This is a real addition to the stated rule's reach. It does not adjudicate any unpresented licensing, education, experience, financial-responsibility, sales-practice or conduct rule, create a quantitative threshold, or adopt a new balancing or evidentiary framework. Ordinary indirect costs are not automatically significant interference; describing a rule as “licensing” does not automatically make it compatible either.

**Why this revision is supported:** The complete historical joins strongly support each Associate's acceptance of this identified-power principle, not merely the result. Its operative premises—an affirmative §92 grant, unchanged Franklin/de la Cuesta/McClellan authority, statutory conditions, and a categorical state veto—are already within the neutral alternatives. The principle is not supplied by a newly discovered record fact or a new route around McCarran. The simulated Gade and Freightliner decisions require attention to the actual source of conflict but do not displace the protected banking-power precedents. A categorical refusal to recognize significant impairment of an established power would require a concrete additional premise not established here. Conversely, this evidence does not justify a free-ranging rule that any state burden obstructs congressional preferences. The individual comparisons below explain why each Associate accepts the stated intermediate scope.

### 3. The insurance-specific federal exception

Section 92 specifically relates to the business of insurance within §1012(b). Its direct treatment of solicitation, policy sales, premium collection, agreed agency compensation, and insurance-specific guarantees reaches the insurer-policyholder transaction. Its banking purpose and location in a banking enactment do not negate that relationship. The containing enactment need not relate predominantly or exclusively to insurance; an incidental reference or mere application of a general federal law to an insurer is not enough on this reasoning.

No additional express-preemption words or post-1944 enactment date are required. Fabe's actual first-clause protection for policyholder performance and necessary administration remains intact; it does not substitute another condition for the enacted federal exception. John Hancock confirms the operative importance of insurance-specific federal provisions within a broader statutory scheme. National Bank of Oregon settles statutory survival, not either disputed geographic question. Congress's authority to confer powers on national banks supplies the answer to the pre-1944 premise; this case creates no new general Commerce Clause holding.

The McCarran exception permits ordinary preemption analysis; it does not itself establish conflict. The separately adopted protected-power analysis establishes conflict here. The antitrust proviso and §1013(b)'s Sherman Act boycott, coercion and intimidation qualification do no work in this case and remain distinct.

### 4. Reservations and statutory conditions

Assume without deciding that Florida's challenged law was enacted for the purpose of regulating the business of insurance. Neither the lower courts' findings nor the legal dispute about their implication is erased. The competition and protectionism arguments remain arguments, not new findings. No least-restrictive-means requirement, rule excluding regulation by prohibition, automatic inference from the statute's location, or new legislative-record burden is adopted.

All actual §92 conditions remain: the association must be located and doing business in a place whose population **does not exceed 5,000 under the last preceding decennial census**; the insurer must be authorized by the State where the bank is located; and agency activity remains subject to the Comptroller's rules. The permitted agency activity includes soliciting and selling policies, collecting premiums, and receiving agreed fees or commissions. The bank may neither assume nor guarantee premium payment nor guarantee the truth of the insured's application statements. No underwriting or guarantee of insurer obligations follows.

The federal population boundary differs from Florida's **less-than-5,000** independent-bank exclusion, which also excludes holding-company subsidiaries and affiliates from its benefit. Barnett's affiliation defeats that particular exclusion. Credit life and credit disability are separate exclusions from restricted agency activities. No other verified exclusion is found applicable. Provisions verified only in the historical proposed Florida bill are not converted into current law or grounds for relief. The complete effective remainder of §626.988 remains outside established coverage.

## Alternatives, changed premises, and limits of the comparison

**Affirm for lack of ordinary conflict:** The strongest alternative remains that §92 removes only a federal corporate-capacity disability, while insurance regulation and competitive equality preserve a nondiscriminatory state veto. The historical opinion considered that same construction, not a straw version requiring physical impossibility. Each reconciled Associate rejects it because this affirmative grant and its banking-law setting establish protection of exercise, and the insurer-authorization condition is narrower than respondents' veto. The simulated changes in Gade/Freightliner prevent treating mere overlap or silence as proof of a protected option, but they do not remove the actual §92 grant or Franklin. No vote departure is warranted on this record.

**Affirm under McCarran, or reverse on Florida's lack of insurance purpose:** These remain distinct alternatives. The state-purpose finding does not answer whether §92 specifically relates to insurance; the federal exception defeats reverse preemption even on the State's premise. Conversely, a ruling that Florida's law fails Fabe would require deciding the contested functional classification and responding to the accepted policyholder-protection evidence. Historical comparison supports the frozen exception route, not an extra alternative holding against Florida's purpose. No general insurer exemption or least-restrictive-means rule follows.

**Retain only a categorical-ban rule:** This is a lawful, narrower formulation; no necessity doctrine compels every possible generalization. But the complete historical evidence supports more than bare permission/prohibition matching: it accepts significant interference with an identified federal power and expressly preserves compatible regulation and incorporated state limits. The prior simulated decisions reinforce the need for those conditions rather than furnish a concrete reason to reject that principle for this case. The reconciliation therefore revises the frozen scope affirmatively; it does not claim the original model had already adopted it, or that history commands it. The revision uses the protected-power ground and precedents already framed neutrally. It adds no new neutral record premise or alternative legal route requiring restoration.

**Adopt every historical explanatory proposition:** Rejected. Historical catalogues of express, field, impossibility and obstacle preemption are not new holdings in each category. The Court need not rely on the 1916 letter, impose a general “relates to” formula, defer to the Comptroller, or decide all state insurance regulation. The actual in-world cases control their own present force. The current McCarran and statutory-power grounds remain sufficient without importing historical post-divergence law.

**Treat historical reversal as unconditional final relief:** Rejected. The historical opinion is silent on the two cross-appeals that the cleared lower opinion expressly preserves. Silence supplies no ruling against respondents on them. The express present remand is a procedural specification supported by the existing record, not a claim of a changed factual predicate or an invented historical directive. It does not revive an issue waived below, because these two issues were preserved and left undecided.

**Material historical departures:** No Associate changes the historical statutory result or rejects the bounded historical protected-power and insurance-specific grounds. The express remand and reservations make the adjudicated reach and surviving proceedings precise where the historical opinion was compressed. The scope change identified above is a change from the **independent model**, not a manufactured departure from history. No unsupported new ground is required to explain these commitments.
