## A. St. P. C. v. B. C., No. 94-7810

**Assigned event:** The neutral projection expressly establishes a merits assignment concerning the Louisiana judgment, State in the Interest of A.C., 643 So.2d 743 (La.1994), rehearing October 17, 1994, No.93-CA-1125. The packet does not obtain its Supreme Court posture from any historical Supreme Court action. The supplied event date is November 20, 1995.

**Direct verification:** The validator read the entire rehearing opinion, all footnotes, and Dennis's additional reasons in `C_ac_0743-01.txt`. That text supports the following limits:

- The issue decided below is procedural due process at the abuse-factfinding stage for the severe contact restriction in La.R.S.9:364(D). The lower decree affirms the trial court's declaration that §364(D) is unconstitutional. It does not announce a fresh abuse finding under clear and convincing evidence or itself prescribe a new contact schedule.
- The November 1992 CINC determination used a bare preponderance. The December disposition allowed weekly supervised visits up to one hour, subject to OCS shortening them in light of the child's reaction, with neutral-relative supervision contemplated when possible. That protective disposition is distinct from the mother's January 1993 request for §364(D)'s all-contact prohibition.
- Footnote 4 positively states that the father lacked notice at the CINC factfinding hearing that its result could expose him to §364(D)'s consequences. The lower court therefore rejected the mother's contention that his failure to appeal made that finding conclusive here. Do not silently transform the earlier finding into an adjudication under a higher proof standard or an unanswerable preclusion premise.
- The statutory trigger is a court finding of parental sexual abuse. Its consequence is mandatory prohibition of all visitation and contact, continuing until a contradictory hearing establishes both successful completion of the specified treatment and that supervised visitation serves the children's best interests. It does not promise unrestricted visitation after treatment. No proof burden is expressly written in the quoted subsection.
- Treatment under §9:362(6) means evaluation and psychotherapy designed for perpetrators of family violence by licensed mental-health professionals. Footnote 8 reports the actual OCS case-plan goal of an admission of abuse and an expert's assessment that continued denial prevented benefit from treatment then. This is not a statutory admission requirement or a finding that every available program requires an admission.
- The original opinion's state separation-of-powers ruling against mandatory greater weight for the child's therapist is expressly acknowledged in rehearing footnote 1. A federal proof-burden disposition does not silently reinstate that command. The court-access construction, fees issue, and Dennis's additional potential objections do not become additional adjudicated federal components merely because the sources mention them.

**Source sufficiency:** The lower opinion supplies the statute in terms, the proof standard actually used, the notice problem, the operative consequences, and the lower judgment. It is sufficient for the assigned proof-burden issue. Santosky and Mathews supply the relevant predivergence authorities; the actual Santosky majority and dissent must retain their respective grounds. Foucha and Heller in the entering slice do not themselves set the burden here. The prior unexplained DeBoer stay action supplies no substantive proof-burden rule.

**Exact conditional limits:** A direction to enter a new abuse finding, prescribe an exact present visitation arrangement, resolve a separate fee claim, or order enrollment in a particular treatment program requires the corresponding preserved claim and underlying order or program record. Those sources are not supplied. They do not block a disposition confined to the assigned legal proof standard and its lawful effect on the judgment below. No assertion is made that an unrecovered petition or appendix lacks additional material.

## Field v. Mans, No. 94-967

**Assigned event:** Merits review of the First Circuit affirmance, 36 F.3d1089 (1994), argued October 2, 1995; supplied decision date November 28, 1995. The underlying Chapter 11 filing was December 10, 1990. A later printed Code edition does not retroactively apply unrelated amendments.

**Statutory and record boundaries:** Section 523(a)(2)'s introductory limitation is a debt for money, property, services, or extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by the specified conduct. Subsection (A) concerns false pretenses, false representation, or actual fraud, excluding statements respecting financial condition. Subsection (B) has its own requirements of a materially false written financial-condition statement, reasonable creditor reliance, and intent to deceive. An interpretation of reliance under (A) does not delete the separate obtained-by requirement.

The bankruptcy court's oral findings at JA35–45 include actual subjective reliance, detriment, and an effective extension of credit through failure to accelerate, followed by its separate objective prudent-person reliance analysis. The source author's direct examination covered JA36–45. Do not describe the case as lacking an actual-reliance finding. Conversely, those findings do not authorize a new Supreme Court factual conclusion that every part of the debt satisfies the statutory causal nexus.

**Direct party-position check:** The validator read petitioners' merits argument, printed pp.10–23. Petitioners argue that Congress expressly required reasonable reliance in (B), not (A); their principal challenge is to the objective reasonable-investigation requirement, and their treatment of actual and common-law justifiable reliance preserves the latter's known-or-obvious-falsity limitation. Their actual prayer at p.23 is reversal and remand for entry of judgment for creditors. The supplied packet's remand-for-application possibility must not be substituted for that party request. The request does not establish entitlement to the broader remedy. Respondent separately contests whether the extension of credit was obtained by fraud, expressly at pp.32–33, and defends reasonable reliance or a functionally similar standard. The distinction between subjective reliance, justifiable reliance, and objective reasonable reliance must remain explicit.

**Source sufficiency and exact conditional limits:** The record and statutory text suffice for the supplied reliance-standard question. Existing findings must be evaluated under the selected legal rule, not overwritten. A final nondischargeability judgment on the entire amount, or an independent resolution of debt nexus or preservation, requires the relevant underlying findings and the parties' full treatment of that branch. Source availability is established in `C_records/field_*`; no absence assertion is inferred from selective reading. A disposition leaving those matters for proper lower-court application does not require resolving them here.

## NLRB v. Town & Country Electric, Inc., No.94-947

**Assigned event:** Merits review of 34 F.3d625 (8th Cir.1994), argued October 10, 1995; supplied decision date November 28, 1995. The Eighth Circuit denied enforcement of 309 N.L.R.B.1250 on its employee-definition ground. Town & Country and Ameristaff are distinct employer respondents; the separately filed union respondent's brief supports the Board. The Archive's generic respondent-brief label is not a statement of alignment.

**Direct record checks:** The validator read the Board brief's printed pp.2–11 and the employer brief's introductory statement and argument summary. The 11 discriminatees comprise two full-time union officials and nine other union members. Ameristaff hired and referred Malcolm Hansen; the other ten were not interviewed. Do not describe all eleven as full-time union employees or all eleven as workers who actually began employment. The union program provided wage or benefit support, including travel and health benefits, described in the briefs.

The validator also read JA36–37 and visually read the complete local salting resolutions at JA256–258 (PDF pages118–120). They authorize members to seek employment with nonsignatory contractors for organizing; require designated members to carry out organizing assignments and leave the employer or job immediately upon notification; maintain their out-of-work-list positions; and subject unauthorized nonsignatory employment to charges and discipline under the union constitution and bylaws. Local343 also provides the stated health-premium support. The transcript at JA36–37 identifies the two local resolutions and the authenticity stipulation. These terms are relevant to the employer's control and divided-service argument. They are not a finding that the particular workers refused their employers' work or actually committed misconduct.

**Live distinction:** Statutory employee eligibility, the common-law master-servant relationship, actual discrimination, qualifications, lawful work-time restrictions, and relief are separate. The Board relies on the statutory breadth and the possibility of simultaneous service consistent with the employer's work; the employer relies on union control of organizing and job tenure and asserted conflict of service. Neither a purpose-based generalization nor an assumption that the workers are already nonemployees may replace the statutory/common-law inquiry. Darden and Reid concern common-law construction; Lechmere concerns the distinct access rights of established nonemployees. The entering Health Care & Retirement and MCI authorities must be applied within their actual scope.

**Statute:** Section152(3)'s definition is not limited to an employee of a particular employer unless the subchapter explicitly provides otherwise; it also covers the stated labor-dispute or unfair-labor-practice cessation category subject to the substantially-equivalent-employment qualification. Preserve all seven exclusions: agricultural labor; domestic service in a home; employment by a parent or spouse; independent contractors; supervisors; employment by a Railway Labor Act employer; and employment by a person who is not an employer as defined. Union compensation is not an express additional exclusion. That textual observation does not settle common-law status.

**Conflict and relief corrections:** There is a circuit conflict; the supplied no-conflict sentence is not retained. Zachry and Elias Brothers take the exclusionary side; Willmar, Henlopen and Escada provide contrary date-eligible authority within their stated facts. The Board's brief p.8 n.4, citing petition appendix130a, explains that offers of employment and make-whole relief leave to compliance the determination of how many rejected applicants would actually have been hired. A status ruling supplies no automatic amount of backpay for all ten. The lower court did not reach other employer grounds, including the challenged inference of discriminatory motivation and work-time solicitation restrictions. These remain distinct from the threshold question.

**Source sufficiency and exact conditional limits:** The statutory-status issue is sufficiently supported. Full resolution of remaining unfair-labor-practice defenses, an employee-by-employee remedy, or an exact compliance award requires their underlying findings and preserved treatment. The standard/status question can be resolved without making those additional findings. No absence claim is made about the downloaded but incompletely read record.

## Thompson v. Keohane, No.94-6615

**Assigned event:** Review of the Ninth Circuit's §2254 treatment, 34 F.3d1073 (1994), argued October11,1995; supplied decision date November29,1995. The underlying Alaska decision is 768 P.2d127 (1989). The supplied question concerns characterization and review of Miranda custody, not an independent involuntariness claim.

**Direct procedure and relief checks:** The validator read respondent's introductory questions, statement and summary, including printed pp.6–12, and petitioner's printed pp.41–43 and complete statutory Appendix A. An initial dismissal for untimely objections was vacated to allow objections; the ultimate federal judgment was on the subsequent proceedings. Do not turn that abandoned default into the present ground. Respondent's second question and argument seek affirmance alternatively on lack of custody even under independent review. Petitioner p.43 requests reversal and remand for independent custody review or, if the Court itself decides custody, remand for the appropriate harmless-error analysis. Thus both parties address the alternative merits, although the supplied assignment can be resolved at the review-standard level.

**Statute in force:** Petitioner's AppendixA, pp.A-1–A-2, reproduces the complete pre-AEDPA §2254(d), directly read. The presumption concerns a factual determination after a hearing on the merits by a competent state court, in proceedings involving the applicant and State or state officer, with the required adequate written indicia. Preserve all eight exceptions: unresolved factual merits; inadequate factfinding procedure; inadequately developed material facts; lack of jurisdiction over subject matter or applicant; unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel to an indigent applicant; lack of a full, fair and adequate hearing; other due-process denial; and lack of fair support in the produced pertinent state record. Preserve the statute's conditions and allocation of the convincing-evidence burden at a federal evidentiary hearing. The attempted official §2254 download returned non-PDF content and is not evidence. AEDPA's later text does not apply to this event.

**Record limits:** The state suppression decision on written submissions described custody as close. Absence of a live evidentiary hearing does not by itself establish that every statutory requirement for a merits hearing failed. The officers' communicated accusations, freedom-to-leave statements, station setting, duration and the actual departure are identifiable circumstances. Private police intentions or postdeparture surveillance assertions cannot become objective custody factors without the necessary record basis and communication relevance. Actual historical facts, credibility determinations, and the ultimate legal significance of those circumstances must not be conflated.

**Entering law and conflict:** Stansbury supplies the objective arrest-equivalent custody standard; it does not itself decide Thompson's custody or review classification. Withrow permits the Miranda habeas claim notwithstanding a fair state opportunity, while preserving ordinary habeas and harmless-error requirements. Wright v. West did not establish general deference to a reasonable state legal decision or a composite Marks rule combining Teague and merits positions. Miller v. Fenton, Patton, Witt, Cuyler and Strickland must retain their distinct subject matter when used as classification analogies. A circuit division exists, including Feltrop and Jacobs; the supplied categorical no-conflict sentence is not retained.

**Source sufficiency and exact conditional limits:** The standard-of-review question has sufficient statutory and procedural support. A complete ruling on custody, harmlessness, admissibility, a §2254(d) exception, or entitlement to the writ requires the pertinent findings, preserved claim and underlying interview record. Those are conditional limits on expanded adjudication, not a general blocker to the supplied review-standard question. The failed official download is not a blocker because the complete date-eligible filed statutory appendix was read.
