#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Settled allocation and discrete defenses

**Holding and operative rule:** The decree's 75/25 natural-flow allocation cannot be replaced through these amendments by a mass beneficial-use ceiling. Reject the First Counterclaim and First Cross-Claim insofar as they seek that ceiling, while preserving discrete geographic, priority, unlawful-call and relevant waste defenses not dependent on it.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that retained jurisdiction does not give a State another opportunity to replace an adjudicated allocation. Nebraska's 1945 decision supplies the percentage allocation and its geography and priorities; the 1953 decree preserves those terms as modified. The April 20, 1993 decision distinguishes enforcement of an established right from modification requiring clear and convincing evidence of real and substantial injury. Wyoming's proposed mass beneficial-use ceiling substitutes a different measure for the settled percentage. A desire for quantified certainty does not itself establish the basis for that change. But rejecting the formula does not establish that Nebraska may make calls outside the decree, bypass priorities or waste water without legal consequence. Those discrete enforcement and defensive matters do not logically depend on the new ceiling and remain available in their proper scope. No alleged violation, waste quantity or final restriction is decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, 325 U.S. 589: preserves allocation, geography and priority terms.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, 345 U.S. 981: preserves the decree as modified in 1953.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: applies distinct enforcement and modification burdens; modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury. Its Inland Lakes acquiescence alternative is not generalized here.

##### Groundwater and equitable connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Specific upstream groundwater development and alleged depletion threatening the decree allocation may be pleaded. Nebraska's own pumping creates no categorical unclean-hands bar without relevant conduct and connection to the asserted harm; supported defenses and applicable eventual proof requirements remain.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that alleged groundwater effects may be examined without deciding that any well violates the decree. The April 20, 1993 decision supplies the distinction between existing-right enforcement and new allocation, including the latter's clear-and-convincing substantial-injury burden. Kansas v. Colorado supplies proof-sensitive treatment of pumping and concrete equitable defenses, but decides neither these States' hydrology nor a universal enforcement quantum. Nebraska identifies upstream development and alleged effects on protected natural flow. These allegations warrant inquiry; they do not invite general supervision of every well. Wyoming's strongest objection invokes Nebraska's own pumping. Similar activity alone does not establish legally relevant misconduct, injury to Wyoming or connection to this claim. Wyoming remains free to prove a properly connected defense. Leave therefore neither establishes interstate injury nor immunizes Nebraska's conduct. Actual injury, causation and any permissible restriction remain for evidence before the Master.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: applies distinct enforcement and modification burdens; modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury.
- Kansas v. Colorado, May 15, 1995: applies proof-sensitive pumping and defense analysis without importing Compact elements or factual conclusions.

##### Downstream and habitat evidence

**Holding and operative rule:** Downstream nonirrigation and habitat effects may be considered insofar as relevant to injury and lawful modification in admitted decree claims. Relevance creates no independent instream entitlement, habitat-flow quantity, free-standing environmental right or automatic priority; Count IV remains denied without prejudice.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidence of injury and entitlement to a new allocation are different questions. Nebraska's 1945 decision supplies the allocation's setting, and the April 20, 1993 decision supplies the proof required for enforcement or modification. Downstream conditions may demonstrate the consequences of threatened reduction of protected water. Excluding all nonirrigation consequences would remove potentially relevant evidence merely because the decree apportioned irrigation water. Nebraska's contrary risk is equally real: evidence relevant to injury cannot confer a new habitat right. The admitted claims and applicable modification burden supply the limit. Count IV's wider seasonal theory is not admitted, and no wildlife-specific quantity is adjudicated. The Master may examine relevant effects within the authorized controversy. Actual injury, changed conditions and entitlement remain for proof before a different allocation or operating obligation can issue; no independent public environmental right is established.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, 325 U.S. 589: supplies the allocation setting without independent habitat rights.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: applies distinct enforcement and modification burdens; modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury.

##### Horse Creek and supporting conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Horse Creek's location below Tri-State Dam does not alone bar a specific changed-condition allegation threatening the decree's balance. A material decree connection and eventual modification proof remain required; no tributary-wide apportionment or finding of depletion follows.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the pivotal reach does not exclude every supporting downstream condition from the retained proceeding. Nebraska's 1945 decision supplies the original allocation and its setting. The April 20, 1993 decision preserves warranted modification while demanding clear and convincing real and substantial injury. Nebraska alleges Horse Creek changes affect supply conditions and thereby threaten the existing balance. Entry below Tri-State Dam matters to that causal inquiry but does not alone resolve it. Wyoming's strongest objection is that an indirect relationship could become a device for a basin-wide allocation. The answer is a proved material connection and the governing modification burden, not automatic admission of every remote dispute. The bounded allegation may be tested; its geographic label neither proves nor defeats it. No entitlement to Horse Creek water, cause or magnitude of depletion, or new allocation among tributaries is decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, 325 U.S. 589: applies the allocation setting and preserves geographic limits.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: applies distinct enforcement and modification burdens; modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury.

##### Federal administration as decree predicate

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Cross-Claim may proceed only for alleged changed federal storage administration materially affecting the interstate decree's operation or supporting conditions. Both United States and Nebraska exceptions are overruled within that scope; independent contractual compliance remains outside the original proceeding and in its lawful separate channel.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, federal-administration portion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the object of this claim is the interstate decree, not enforcement of every storage contract. The original Nebraska decree and retained jurisdiction supply the allocation under examination. Texas v. New Mexico supplies meaningful enforcement of interstate obligations within their terms, while the April 20, 1993 Nebraska decision separates enforcement from modification. Wyoming alleges changed federal administration affecting conditions supporting the allocation. Storage water's exclusion from direct apportionment does not make those alleged effects irrelevant. The strongest objection points to Goshen litigation and original-jurisdiction limits. Those objections bar independent contractual compliance here but do not require leaving an alleged decree predicate unexamined. The Master must maintain that distinction. Admission establishes no federal breach, general immunity waiver, damages action or absent party's contract rights. It displaces no separate litigation and requires proof before decree relief is available.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, 325 U.S. 589, and 345 U.S. 981: preserve allocation and retained jurisdiction without allocating storage water.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554: applies interstate enforcement within operative scope, not general contract jurisdiction.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: applies distinct enforcement and modification burdens; modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury.

##### Exact leave and next stage

**Holding and operative rule:** Permit Nebraska Counts I-III, Wyoming Second-Fourth Counterclaims and Wyoming Second, Third and Fifth Cross-Claims unanimously within the preceding limits; permit the Fourth Cross-Claim within its decree-effects limit by eight votes, with Thomas opposed. Deny Nebraska Count IV without prejudice and reject only impermissible ceiling portions of First Counterclaim and First Cross-Claim. Return Glendo, Laramie and carriage-loss theories to the Master for applicable proof; leave establishes no injury, breach, water award, priority, damages or injunction.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the other leave dispositions and next-stage directions. Only Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join admission of the Fourth Cross-Claim; Thomas dissents from that admission.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an express pleading disposition defines the next stage without deciding its outcome. The April 20, 1993 decision supplies different demands for enforcing an established right and modifying an allocation. Its use of Rule 56 under Rule 17.2 is a guide to summary disposition, not a rule turning amendments into proof. This event grants bounded leave, not summary judgment on new allegations. The report's unexcepted Glendo, Laramie and carriage-loss recommendations receive an express procedural disposition alongside contested claims. Absence of an exception and the amendment label prove no substantive prerequisite. Nebraska Counts I-III and specified Wyoming pleadings may proceed under applicable conditions; Count IV's broader seasonal theory remains withheld without prejudice. No numerical Glendo release or revised carriage-loss formula is reconstructed. The Master must receive evidence within admitted scope, preserving factual, legal and remedial defenses; the original action remains open.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: applies distinct enforcement and modification burdens; modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury. Rule 56 remains a limited guide; leave is not proof.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Thomas concurs in all other components and dissents on the Fourth Cross-Claim. His objection concerns forum and scope, not an injury finding. Storage water was not apportioned, and he regards federal storage-contract administration as belonging to separate litigation. He would sustain both United States and Nebraska exceptions and exclude this pleading, preserving a later adequately distinct interstate modification claim. The majority's decree-effects limitation does not answer his alternative-forum objection. Federal party status and practical effects do not make independent administration appropriate for original adjudication. He finds no breach, immunity waiver or established contract relief and does not certify the separate forum's jurisdiction or complete remedy. His position is noncontrolling; the eight-Justice limited admission governs.
