### Celotex Corp. v. Edwards, No. 93-1504

Merits decision, April 19, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 6, 1994; decided April 19, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing its November 5, 1993 judgment, 6 F.3d 312, and December 22, 1993 rehearing disposition. The questions concern the operative bankruptcy injunction's reach, actual related-proceeding jurisdiction and interim adjudicative authority, and whether another court may authorize contrary enforcement of a surety's bond rather than require a challenge through the issuing proceeding and authorized review.

Bennie and Joann Edwards obtained a $281,025.80 personal-injury judgment in April 1989. Northbrook issued a $294,987.88 supersedeas bond, secured by insurance proceeds remaining payable under its coverage settlement with Celotex. The tort judgment was affirmed September 20, 1990. The mandate issued October 12, 1990, the same day Celotex filed its Chapter 11 petition in Florida; no time-of-day priority is established. The bankruptcy court entered its §105 restraint October 17, 1990. An intervening January 10, 1991 order permitted pending appeals to proceed while retaining bond protection.

The Edwardses moved under Rule 65.1 in the Northern District of Texas on May 3, 1991. The bankruptcy court's June 13, 1991 omnibus order, 128 B.R. 478, expressly continued the §105 stay and required creditors to seek relief there before proceeding against bonds, including where the appellate process had already concluded at the petition date. The Texas court authorized execution on May 27, 1992. The June command thus preceded the enforcement authorization even though it followed the enforcement motion.

The Fifth Circuit distinguished the surety's matured undertaking from estate property, rejected an automatic stay, and focused on the original October order's debtor-focused wording; it also addressed bankruptcy power. Judge Jones concurred on the automatic-stay and scope grounds without deciding broader power. The rehearing explanation likewise relied principally on scope. The Court considers the intervening June command, while leaving the automatic-stay and ultimate estate-property questions outside its necessary holding.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed, 7–2, and the case is remanded. The contrary execution authorization must be set aside while the operative §105 restraint remains in effect.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The operative June injunction reaches this proposed bond enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** The June 13, 1991 order expressly continued the §105 restraint and required relief from the issuing bankruptcy court before collection on bonds whose appeals had concluded when the bankruptcy petition was filed. Its coverage reaches the Edwards bond proceeding authorized in May 1992; this conclusion does not depend on an automatic §362 stay, estate ownership of the matured bond, or a finding of contempt.

**Authority:** This portion of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: seven direct rationale votes for this portion. Stone-Zsela's join does not extend to the separate actual-relatedness, affirmative interim-authority, or complete collateral-review portions. Justices Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from the judgment on the distinct §157 allocation objection.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the order operative when enforcement was authorized must be considered in full. The Fifth Circuit focused on the October 17 language concerning proceedings involving a debtor and distinguished Northbrook's independent undertaking. The June 13 order expressly addresses the omitted circumstance: a creditor whose appellate process had already concluded when the petition was filed must first seek relief from the continued §105 restraint before proceeding against the bond. The Edwardses filed their enforcement motion before June, but the Texas court authorized execution almost a year after that order. The independent nature of the surety's obligation therefore does not remove the proceeding from the later command's terms. Rule 65.1 permits enforcement of surety liability by motion; it does not itself dissolve an applicable injunction. The Court neither changes October's words retroactively nor decides a personal-notice or contempt question. Northbrook's undertaking and Celotex's tort debt remain distinct, and the validity of the restraint is a separate inquiry.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Rule 65.1 retains its motion procedure and surety-jurisdiction function; it does not itself answer the effect of another court's applicable injunction.
- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: preserves the distinction between the operative order and objections to its validity; no sanction or culpability finding is imported.

##### Immediate execution on this bond is related to the Chapter 11 case

**Controlling proposition:** Whether the Edwardses may immediately execute against Northbrook on this supersedeas bond is at least related to Celotex's Chapter 11 case under §1334(b). The bond, retained insurance-settlement collateral, and reorganization connection establish actual nonexclusive subject-matter relatedness; this holding does not establish exact depletion, final core authority, the outer limits of relatedness, or an independent jurisdictional grant under §105.

**Authority:** This portion of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six direct full-rationale votes, including the author. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this stronger resolution and concurs in the judgment on his separate colorable-jurisdiction ground. Stevens and Ginsburg's acceptance of district-court relatedness is not a join in this complete rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the immediate-execution proceeding is related to this bankruptcy. Section 1334(b) reaches related civil proceedings and expressly makes that jurisdiction nonexclusive; it is not limited to actions against the debtor or estate-owned property. Northbrook's independent bond obligation coexists with the retained insurance-settlement proceeds securing it and the administration and prospects of Celotex's reorganization. Those connections establish the statutory relation. The Fifth Circuit's concern about the unproduced particular contract and concrete depletion does not require treating that relation as merely possible. It limits assertions of automatic offset, quantified loss, successful avoidance, and the eventual justification for continued restraint. The Court makes none of those findings. Bancorp preserves the distinction between procedural power and unpresented underlying merits; it does not itself decide relatedness. Section 105 supplies no jurisdiction that §1334 lacks. Final core classification, consent, and the ultimate injunction merits remain distinct questions. The Court decides this proceeding's relation without adopting a comprehensive outer-boundary test for every economically consequential third-party suit.

**Precedent treatment:**

- U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, November 8, 1994: preserves the distinction between procedural power and unpresented underlying merits; its holding does not independently determine bankruptcy relatedness.

##### Section 157 permits this interim restraint without a final core determination

**Controlling proposition:** In this related proceeding, §157(c)(1)'s restriction on a bankruptcy judge's final noncore adjudication does not deprive that judge of authority to enter this interlocutory restraint preserving the matter for proper determination. That sufficient interim authority defeats the asserted collateral-displacement ground without deciding final core status, all-parties consent, every equitable predicate, or the ultimate propriety and duration of the §105 injunction.

**Authority:** This portion of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six direct full-rationale votes, including the author. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this stronger resolution and concurs in the judgment on his separate colorable-jurisdiction ground. Stevens and Ginsburg's acceptance of district-court relatedness is not a join in this complete rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the bankruptcy judge has sufficient interim adjudicative authority for the collateral posture presented. Section 157 separates referral, core determination, and noncore final-order procedure. For a noncore related matter, proposed findings ordinarily go to the district court for final judgment and review of timely, specific objections; all-parties consent provides a separate route. Neither final core status nor consent is established here. The dissent treats that final-order allocation as disabling this judge from restraining the Article III bond court. The Court rejects that objection to interim preservation of this related matter. The restraint does not conduct a new tort trial or finally award the bond. United Mine Workers distinguishes preservation while authority is contested from ultimate entitlement to relief; its analogy does not itself interpret §157. The statutory distinction between interim and final adjudication supplies the present conclusion. A core label or §105 alone cannot override statutory limits. The separate personal-injury trial and liquidation restrictions and the ultimate injunction merits remain intact.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: extends its interim-preservation reasoning to this substantial bankruptcy authority question without treating arguable authority as final legality.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: preserves distinct statutory functions and qualifying review routes; it does not independently validate the injunction or decide §157 classification.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, March 1, 1995: preserves attention to the actual authorized judicial act; its appellate holding is not a grant of bankruptcy injunction power.

##### This still-operative restraint must be challenged through orderly relief and review

**Controlling proposition:** The actual §1334(b) relation, sufficient interim §157 authority, substantial support for this applicable restraint, and concrete relief/review mechanism require a challenge through the issuing proceeding and authorized review rather than contrary execution elsewhere while the restraint remains effective. The holding preserves the prior authorities' jurisdictional and frustrated-review reservations and does not validate the injunction's ultimate merits or any contempt sanction.

**Authority:** This portion of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six direct full-rationale votes, including the author. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this stronger resolution and concurs in the judgment on his separate colorable-jurisdiction ground. Stevens and Ginsburg's acceptance of district-court relatedness is not a join in this complete rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Texas enforcement proceeding cannot substitute for proper challenge to this restraint. The established relatedness and sufficient interim authority support that conclusion together; a bare assertion of jurisdiction would not suffice. United Mine Workers requires obedience to unreversed orders issued with jurisdiction and permits interim preservation of substantial jurisdictional questions, excluding frivolous and insubstantial claims. Walker distinguishes transparent invalidity or a frivolous pretense and reserves timely challenge frustrated by delay. Neither supplies a universal exception or makes every substantial objection a license to disregard an order. The June motion route places the continuation burden on Celotex and permits statutory review; no frustrated timely challenge is established. Concurrent power over the bond does not confer appellate authority over the restraint. This is an application of orderly-review reasoning to a civil collection proceeding, not a contempt prosecution. Under United Mine Workers, civil remedial relief falls with an erroneously issued injunction; criminal punishment may stand differently. No such post-invalidation relief or contempt judgment is presented.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: applies orderly relief and review with substantial-jurisdiction and duration limits; preserves the different consequences for criminal punishment and civil remedies after invalidation.
- Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307: applies orderly-challenge reasoning while preserving its transparent-invalidity, frivolous-pretense, and frustrated-timely-review reservations; no announcement of knowing defiance, notice finding, or sanction is imported.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, March 1, 1995: preserves the requirement of an actual authorized review route; ordinary power over one proceeding does not confer appellate power over another court's order.

##### The execution authorization must yield while the creditors retain qualified relief and review routes

**Controlling proposition:** The contrary execution authorization must be set aside while the §105 restraint remains effective; the creditors may seek relief in the issuing bankruptcy proceeding, where Celotex bears the burden to justify continuation, and use an authorized review route. Section 158 preserves its finality and leave requirements, Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route, and the decision neither dissolves the injunction nor orders permanent restraint or payment of the bond.

**Authority:** This portion of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: seven direct rationale votes for this portion. Stone-Zsela's join does not extend to the separate actual-relatedness, affirmative interim-authority, or complete collateral-review portions. Justices Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from the judgment on the distinct §157 allocation objection.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that reversal preserves proper adjudication of the creditors' remaining objections and their independent bond entitlement. The June order directs successful judgment creditors to request relief and places the continuation burden on the debtor. Final §157 classification and procedure, supporting evidence, ultimate statutory authority, and duration remain open; the Court's actual-relatedness holding is not erased. Section 158(a) directs final-order appeals, and interlocutory appeals with leave, to the district court where the bankruptcy judge serves, here the Middle District of Florida. Qualifying final appellate decisions receive Eleventh Circuit review under §158(d). Germain preserves a separately available §1292 route without removing its conditions or making every interlocutory order immediately appealable. Withdrawal of reference under §157(d) has distinct predicates. The Court sets aside the contrary execution authorization without changing the tort judgment, awarding the bond, ordering recoupment, or deciding whether the injunction should ultimately survive. The issuing court need not await every possible appeal before modifying or dissolving its order.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: applies coexistence of §158(d) and an independently qualifying §1292 route, preserving each route's conditions.
- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: preserves the order's force only while operative and the availability of appropriate appellate or other relief.
- Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307: retains the significance of meaningful access to orderly relief; no unconditional immediate-appeal right is inferred.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

United Mine Workers supplies distinct interim-preservation and unreversed-order principles, limited by substantial jurisdiction and its civil/criminal remedial distinction. Walker supplies qualified orderly-review reasoning while reserving transparent invalidity, frivolous pretense, and frustrated timely challenge; its contempt facts are not imported. The Court applies those principles to this still-operative civil bankruptcy restraint without deciding its ultimate merits. Germain preserves overlapping statutory review at its actual scope; Swint and Bancorp retain their different procedural holdings and do not grant bankruptcy injunction power. No precedent is overruled and no §1481 legislative-history rule is adopted.

Effective April 19, 1995, immediate execution on this bond is actually related to Celotex's Chapter 11 case under §1334(b), and §157(c)(1)'s final-order limitation does not defeat the sufficient interim authority identified for this collateral posture. Six Justices directly join those holdings and the complete qualified collateral-review rationale. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh reversal vote on a narrower colorable-jurisdiction ground and joins the June-coverage and execution/review-remedy portions. Stevens and Ginsburg accept district-court relatedness while dissenting from the interim-authority and judgment conclusions; their assent does not add full-rationale joins. The Court does not equate relatedness with final core adjudication, make §105 a jurisdiction grant, treat a core label as dispositive, or accept management difficulty alone. Final classification, consent, ultimate statutory and equitable validity, evidence for continuation, and duration remain distinct. No Marks synthesis is needed.

##### Statutory distinctions and preserved routes

Section 1334(b) grants original but nonexclusive district-court jurisdiction over civil proceedings arising under title 11, arising in a title 11 case, or related to one, notwithstanding an Act of Congress conferring exclusive jurisdiction on a court other than the district courts. It differs from §1334(a)'s original and exclusive jurisdiction over cases under title 11 and the then-applicable §1334(d)'s exclusive jurisdiction, in the district where the case begins or is pending, over the debtor's property wherever located as of commencement and property of the estate. The 1994 amendments do not apply to this bankruptcy commenced in 1990; the later property designation (e), new jury provision, and changed appellate-panel/election provisions supply no rule here.

Section 157(a) permits a district court to refer title 11 cases and proceedings arising under title 11, arising in such a case, or related to one to its bankruptcy judges. Under §157(b)(1), referred core proceedings arising under title 11 or in a title 11 case may be heard and determined, with appropriate orders subject to §158 review. The bankruptcy court called its bond-property determination core under §157(b)(2)(A), (G), and (O); that is its classification, not this Court's final adoption. Those clauses concern estate administration; motions to terminate, annul, or modify the automatic stay; and other proceedings affecting liquidation of estate assets or adjustment of debtor-creditor or equity-security-holder relationships, except personal-injury tort and wrongful-death claims. Section 157(b)(3) places the classification question before the bankruptcy judge on the judge's own motion or timely party motion and forbids a noncore classification solely because state law may affect the outcome. It does not make the classification unreviewable.

For a noncore but related proceeding, §157(c)(1) permits hearing and proposed findings and conclusions submitted to the district court; the final order belongs to the district judge after consideration and fresh, independent review of the matters timely and specifically objected to. Fresh, independent review is not automatically required for every unobjected proposition. Section 157(c)(2) separately permits district-court referral for hearing, determination, and orders with all parties' consent, subject to §158 review. The record establishes neither consent nor an affirmative refusal; neither is presumed. Section 157(b)(5) requires the district court to order personal-injury tort and wrongful-death claims tried in the district where the bankruptcy is pending or where the claim arose, as the district court where the bankruptcy is pending determines. Section 157(b)(2)(B) includes allowance or disallowance of claims or estate exemptions and estimation for plan confirmation in its core category, but excludes liquidation or estimation of contingent or unliquidated personal-injury tort or wrongful-death claims for distribution. These distinct limits remain intact. No new tort trial or liquidation is authorized here.

Section 105(a) permits orders, process, or judgments necessary or appropriate to carry out title 11. A provision allowing a party in interest to raise an issue does not preclude the court from acting on its own when necessary or appropriate to enforce or implement orders or rules or prevent abuse of process. This is remedial authority, not an independent subject-matter jurisdiction grant or an override of §157. The June order distinguishes bonds during an ongoing appellate process, which that court regarded as estate property subject to §362, from bonds after a creditor succeeds through an authorized completed appeal and bonds whose appeals ended by the petition date. Its latter two categories require relief from the continued §105 restraint. The Court's coverage holding does not adopt every premise of the first category, decide §362's scope, or find that every completed bond remains estate property.

The issuing bankruptcy court may entertain a request to vacate or modify the restraint; its June order places the burden on Celotex to establish that continuation is warranted. The Court has settled the §1334(b) relation and sufficient interim authority for this collateral posture. Its reservation of direct challenge does not reopen those conclusions as wholly undecided. Remaining lawful coverage questions, final §157 allocation and procedure, ultimate statutory injunction power, particular evidence, and duration remain available through proper proceedings. The lower court's possible avoidance, collateral-contract, and reorganization inquiries are not completed adjudications of avoidance recovery or exact estate loss on this bond.

Under the applicable §158(a), final bankruptcy orders go to the district court for the district where the bankruptcy judge serves, and interlocutory review requires leave: here the Middle District of Florida. Section 158(d) permits Eleventh Circuit review of qualifying final appellate decisions, not immediate circuit review of every interlocutory action. No bankruptcy appellate panel with the necessary institutional and consent predicates is established. Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route from a district court's bankruptcy appellate order. Section 1292(b), when invoked, requires written district-court findings that a controlling question of law presents substantial ground for difference of opinion and that immediate appeal may materially advance termination, application within ten days, and circuit permission; the application itself does not stay proceedings. No finding that these predicates have already been met is made.

Section 157(d) withdrawal of reference is separate. The district court may withdraw for cause on its own motion or timely party motion; withdrawal on timely motion is required when resolution requires the specified consideration of both title 11 and other federal laws regulating organizations or activities affecting interstate commerce. No withdrawal motion or qualifying finding is established. Ordinary review and withdrawal are not interchangeable.

##### Prior-authority qualifications

United Mine Workers permits temporary preservation of a substantial jurisdictional question and expressly excludes a frivolous and insubstantial jurisdictional claim. Its distinct unreversed-order formulation concerns a court with jurisdiction over subject matter and person. Its orders remain operative until expiration or appropriate relief. After invalidation, remedial civil relief falls with an erroneously issued injunction, and still more clearly with an order beyond jurisdiction; criminal punishment may have a different survival rule, subject to independent procedural and remedial objections. Compensation, coercion, and punishment are not interchangeable. This case imposes none of them and finds neither contempt nor knowing personal violation.

Walker preserves its reservation for transparent invalidity or only a frivolous pretense to validity and its different constitutional posture for timely challenge met by delay or frustration. It does not define a universal exception or hold that any objection automatically licenses disregard. No such frustrated timely challenge is established here. Its treatment of Green does not create a general ban on jurisdictional objections. Its clear-notice, announced-defiance, two-day timing, and Alabama expedited-review facts are not imported. The present explicit motion, debtor-burden, and statutory review mechanisms supply the concrete basis for orderly challenge; no unconditional immediate appellate remedy is presumed.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins the June-coverage and execution/review-remedy portions. The injunction had at least a colorable basis in the relationship between the bond, surety arrangements, and reorganization, and was not transparently void. Parties subject to such an operative order must challenge it in the issuing proceeding and through authorized review; they may not obtain contrary execution from another court. He therefore reverses and would set aside the execution authorization while leaving proper relief available. He does not join the Court's affirmative actual-relatedness or sufficient interim §157 determinations, or their complete collateral-review rationale. His ground neither protects an injunction lacking even an arguable jurisdictional basis nor decides ultimate statutory power to restrain every nondebtor surety. It supplies no contempt finding, perpetual restraint, or automatic appeal; Celotex's continuation burden and the qualified review routes remain.

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Ginsburg, dissents. They accept that the June 13 order expressly reaches the bond proceeding and that immediate execution is actually related to the Chapter 11 case under §1334(b)'s nonexclusive district-court jurisdiction. Their objection concerns the allocation of adjudicative power to the bankruptcy judge under §157 and Article III. Northbrook's undertaking matured independently of Celotex's tort debt. A related controversy and the issuing judge's core label do not, in their view, empower that non-Article III judge to prevent the Article III bond court from deciding the undertaking when §157 assigns final determination to the district court. Calling the restraint interim does not answer that structural objection.

They accept that ordinary legal error does not generally authorize disobedience and do not reject orderly review of orders issued with the requisite authority. They regard that antecedent condition as unsatisfied here and would affirm enforcement of Northbrook's bond. They do not rely on the original October wording alone, deny all related jurisdiction, presume consent or a proved refusal, find exact collateral depletion, adopt a blanket ban on every nondebtor injunction, or assert the injunction's indefinite validity. Their ground concerns this statutory allocation and this restraint. It supplies no controlling rule and changes no amount of the undertaking.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded so that the Texas execution authorization is set aside while the §105 restraint remains effective. The Edwardses may seek relief in the issuing bankruptcy court and challenge the restraint through the authorized channels and conditions stated above; Celotex bears the continuation burden assigned by the June order. This Court neither dissolves the injunction nor directs its permanent continuation. The tort judgment and independent surety obligation are not extinguished. No payment, refund, avoidance recovery, contempt sanction, settlement, discharge, reorganization completion, or other later event is established.
