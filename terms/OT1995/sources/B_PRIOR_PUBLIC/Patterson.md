### Patterson v. Shumate, No. 91-913

Merits decision, June 15, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 20, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 943 F.2d 362. The question is whether an antialienation restriction enforceable under ERISA excludes this beneficial trust interest from the bankruptcy estate under 11 U.S.C. §541(c)(2). The restriction limits transfer of the interest. The Fourth Circuit reversed the district court's judgment including the interest in the estate.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas participated at argument and decision. Thomas was seated before argument.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Fourth Circuit judgment excluding Shumate's qualifying pension interest from his bankruptcy estate.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or nonreach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exclusion and requested turnover | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | The interest is excluded and is not subject to turnover as estate property. |
| Alternative §522(b)(2)(A) exemption | Not reached; all nine agree it is unnecessary | Blackmun, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas in the Court opinion; Stone separately | Exclusion makes an exemption determination unnecessary. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Holding 1: An enforceable federal pension restriction excludes this beneficial trust interest

**Controlling proposition:** Section 541(c)(2) preserves a restriction on transfer of a debtor's beneficial interest in a trust when that restriction is enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law, including federal law. This established ERISA-qualified trust contains an enforceable antialienation restriction, so Shumate's interest is excluded from the bankruptcy estate and cannot be turned over to its trustee as estate property.

**Authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; these eight Justices make the proposition controlling. Scalia also concurs separately without limiting his join. Stone's separate concurrence supports the same statutory exclusion subject to his stated qualifications, but is not a formal join in the Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that federal law can supply the enforceable transfer restriction that Congress preserved in §541(c)(2). Congress used express references to state law elsewhere in the Code but omitted that restriction here. The required pension clause restricts transfer of Shumate's beneficial trust interest, and ERISA authorizes enforcement of that clause. Guidry v. Sheet Metal Workers National Pension Fund enforces pension antialienation despite strong creditor equities; bankruptcy does not itself create an exception. Union Bank v. Wolas supplies the same-term application of enacted bankruptcy language despite an asserted policy limitation. General references to spendthrift trusts in legislative materials show that those trusts are included, not that federal restrictions are excluded. Section 522(d)(10)(E) still protects qualifying retirement interests that lack an enforceable restriction meeting §541(c)(2); overlap therefore creates no surplusage. The established trust and enforceability predicates exclude this interest without deciding an exemption. The Court leaves other plan qualifications, statutory exceptions, post-distribution treatment, and the exemption provision's coverage of presently payable benefits or undistributed corpus unresolved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Guidry v. Sheet Metal Workers National Pension Fund, 493 U.S. 365 (1990): applied for enforceability and the refusal to create an equitable exception; its statutory exceptions and reserved plan-fiduciary issue remain intact.
- Union Bank v. Wolas, December 11, 1991: applied for giving effect to an enacted bankruptcy provision despite a proposed policy limitation; its ordinary-course preference holding remains unchanged.
- Dewsnup v. Timm, January 15, 1992: distinguished as a context-specific construction preserving an allowed real-property lien, not a restriction of §541(c)(2) to state law; no holding is overruled.

#### Limits and Current-Law Effect

Section 541(c)(2)'s inclusion of federal law resolves a question left open by the prior framework and this Term's decisions. An established beneficial trust interest with this ERISA-enforceable restriction is excluded. The holding does not change exemption deadlines, ERISA employee classification, preference defenses, or Dewsnup's protection of an allowed real-property lien. Policy observations confirm the textual rule and do not create an independently sufficient alternative holding.

The Court does not determine other plans, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, distributed benefits, the alternative exemption under §522(b)(2)(A), or whether §522(d)(10)(E) covers presently payable benefits or undistributed corpus. Statutory avoidance remedies are neither adjudicated nor displaced.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Scalia joins the entire Court opinion and emphasizes consistent usage within the Bankruptcy Code. His criticism of the interpretive approach in Dewsnup, which preserved an allowed real-property lien, does not qualify the present holding or overrule that decision.

Stone concurs in the judgment on the enacted phrase's inclusion of federal law and the established trust and enforceability requirements. In his view, §522 independently protects interests not already excluded, and Guidry's enforcement of pension antialienation prevents equitable disapproval of the beneficiary from overriding the enacted protection. He reserves plans outside ERISA, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, and distributed benefits; his separate writing supplies no general pension immunity or constitutional pension entitlement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Court affirms the Fourth Circuit's judgment, 943 F.2d 362. Shumate's qualifying trust interest is excluded from the bankruptcy estate, so the trustee is not entitled to turnover as estate property. The alternative §522(b)(2)(A) exemption remains unreached. The Court fixes no payment amount, orders no new factual inquiry, and gives no special mandate timing.

Merits review in this Court is complete. Ordinary issuance of the mandate leaves the Fourth Circuit's reversal of the district court's inclusion judgment in effect. No continuing Supreme Court docket, special stay, retained jurisdiction, or new institutional practice is created.
