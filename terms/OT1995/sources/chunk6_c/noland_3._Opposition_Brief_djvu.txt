REASONS FOR DENYING WRIT 


This case does not present special and important reasons 
which warrant granting certiorari. There is no constitutional 
question, no conflict with this Court's decisions, no conflict with 
the decision of another United States court of appeals on the same 


matter, and no error in statutory interpretation or application. 


1. This case does not involve any issues of constitutional 
law. It involves only the narrow issue of whether under 11 U.S.C. 
§ 510(c)(1) the bankruptcy court properly exercised its discretion 
to equitably subordinate post-petition non-pecuniary loss tax 
penalty claims to the claims of general unsecured creditors who 
suffered actual pecuniary losses, when the assets of the debtor 
which the penalty was intended to punish or deter have been 
liquidated and the debtor has ceased operations. 


2. There is no conflict with this Court's decisions. The 
Bankruptcy Act of 1898, es amended, sontained no provision as 
to equitable subordination of claims analogous to 11 U.S.C. 
§ 510(c)(1), which became effective on October 1, 1979. The 
decision of the court of appeals which applied 11 U.S.C. 
§ 510(c)(1) to equitably subordinate the non-pecuniary loss tax 
penalty claims of the IRS against a liquidated corporate debtor to 
the actual pecuniary loss claims of general unsecured creditors 
therefore does not conflict with this Court’s decisions. 


3. There is no conflict with the decision of another United 
States court of appeals on the same matter. To the contrary, each 
court of appeals which has addressed the issue has concluded that 
equitable subordination under 11 U.S.C. § 510(c)(1) of non- 
pecuniary loss tax penalty claims against a liquidated corporate 
debtor does not require a finding of inequitable conduct. Matter 
of Virtual Network Services Corp., 902 F.2d 1246 (7th Cir. 1990);

matter of substantial recurring importance. Petitioner fails to

Services Corp., 902 F.2d 1246, 1250 (7th Cir. 1990); Schultz

Congress recognized that the development of the principles

Rec. H11,095 (Sept. 28, 1978) (statement of Rep. Edwards); 124 
Cong. Rec. $17,412 (Oct. 6, 1978) (statement of Sen. 
DeConcini). Over time, patterns may emerge which affect the 
administration of the bankruptcy laws or which give rise to 
questions of substantial recurring importance. Such is not the 
case here, however. The bankruptcy court correctly found that the 
unique facts of this case warranted the equitable subordination 
under 11 U.S.C. § 510(c)(1) of the post-petition pre-conversion 
non-pecuniary loss tax penalty claims of the IRS against the 
liquidated corporate debtor to the actual pecuniary loss claims of 
general unsecured creditors (Pet. 55a). The question which the 
courts below answered does not significantly affect the 
administration of the bankruptcy laws and is not of substantial 


recurring importance.

TANDEM WITH UNITED STATES V. REORGANIZED 
CF&I FABRICATORS OF UTAH, INC. 


The issues presented in United States v. Reorganized CF &1 
Fabricators of Utah, Inc., 53 F.3d 1155 (10th Cir. 1995), petition 
for cert. filed, 64 U.S.L.W. 3161 (U.S. Aug. 24, 1995) (No. 95- 
325), are not “closely analogous” to the issues in this case as 
stated in the Petition (Pet. (I)). In Reorganized CF &l, the primary 
issue is whether the pre-bankruptcy claim of the IRS under 26 
U.S.C. § 4971(a) for failure to make a pension funding payment 
is entitled to priority under 11 U.S.C. § 507(a)(7)(E). The 
secondary issue is whether that general unsecured claim should 
be subordinated to the claims of general unsecured creditors who 
suffered pecuniary losses, principally the Pension Benefit 
Guaranty Corporation as the largest creditor. The latter issue is 
only peripherally related to whether non-pecuniary loss tax 
penalty claims against a liquidated corporate debtor should be 
subordinated under 11 U.S.C. § 510(c)(1) to the pecuniary loss 
claims of general unsecured creditors.

1. The Debtor filed for Chapter 11 relief on April 10, 1986. 


2. On July 1, 1986, the Internal Revenue Service (“IRS”)
