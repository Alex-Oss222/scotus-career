## Material facts and actual agency reasoning

Independent contract growers furnish facilities and raise Holly Farms' chicks to about seven weeks/marketable size; Holly retains title. The contested crews catch, coop, load, and haul the birds to processing. The lower dissent identifies 118 catchers, 12 forklift operators, and 27 drivers; a typical crew has nine catchers, one forklift operator, a driver, and a supervisor. Distinct functions and locations permit distinct statutory treatment. Other agricultural hatchery/pullet activities do not confer a firm-wide exemption; feedmill/feed-haul/mechanic coverage is not this granted dispute.

The production manager oversees live haul, hatcheries, and feedmill up to processing. Catching schedules track processing shifts and seek a short catch-to-processing interval because bird weight changes. Drivers drop trailers at scales; live-haul staff handle holding/rotation before processing receivers take them. JA 28a–29a supplies actual integration evidence. Counsel's broader description of live haul as “processing” remains a legal characterization, not an independent finding that slaughter occurs at the farm.

The growing contract was marked and discussed in testimony but was not formally moved into evidence (Board brief 3–4 n.2). Do not call it an admitted exhibit. Described grower duties—husbandry, presence during removal, and preparing premises for crews—are relevant but do not dictate the crews' classification.

The Regional Director stated both that contracting out raising ended the employer's farmer status as to that activity and that live-haul employees working out of processing were functionally integrated with plant employees. JA 21a–23a. A reviewing court cannot replace this reasoning with newly invented agency findings; equally, it cannot pretend the integration ground was absent. The agency distinguished hatchery agricultural work. The legal issue is whether those actual grounds validly address each employee category under the actor/place and incident/conjunction requirements.

The election ended 95–95. The ALJ found an earlier authorization-card majority and serious unfair labor practices preventing a fair rerun; the Board sustained the resulting Gissel bargaining order. Classification affects the permissible unit and that order. A ruling excluding some live-haul workers requires appropriate reconsideration of those consequences; it does not automatically vacate every unrelated unfair-labor-practice finding involving covered employees. ## Current law and independent alternatives

**Bayside Enterprises, 429 U.S. 298 (1977)**, independently read in A's safe Cornell file and C's `Bayside.txt`, holds that the Board reasonably excluded from agricultural status feed-delivery drivers serving a processor's nonagricultural feedmill and independent growers. Stevens authored the Court opinion. The decision expressly says there was no claim those drivers worked “on a farm”; it adjudicated the “by a farmer” branch. Contract growers' farming is not simply attributed to the integrated company because it owns the birds, supplies inputs, and retains substantial control. The decision permits mixed operations and fact-specific agency classification. It strongly informs the employer-farmer argument but does not itself decide the statutory on-farm route for catchers.

**Town & Country**, November 28, 1995, Ginsburg for all nine, preserves broad employee coverage against a categorical union-pay exclusion while independently examining statutory/common-law boundaries before permitting a reasonable Board interpretation. Express exclusions remain effective. **Professional direction and genuine NLRA supervisory authority**, effective May 23, 1994 (Health Care), permits the Board's bounded distinction between skilled patient-care direction and genuine statutory supervisory authority; it does not authorize deletion of enumerated functions. **Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing**, effective June 17, 1994 (MCI), rejects agency replacement of a required statutory system through a power to modify it; it is not a universal rule against agency classifications. **NationsBank**, January 18, 1995, permits reasonable agency classification of annuities within the statutory boundaries. These authorities require independent textual bounds followed by the appropriate review of interpretation and findings, not agency victory or defeat from a label.

1. **Sustain a permissible, evidence-supported distinction between growing and processing-related removal.** The Board can preserve the on-farm alternative while finding these particular crews' work incident to a separately organized processing operation, after independent growers have completed raising. The actual schedules, supervision, and handling evidence support that reading. Bayside undermines attribution of grower husbandry to Holly merely through title. Strong contrary argument: preparing grown poultry for its first removal is ordinarily preparation for market, and economic coordination with a buyer/processor need not sever its relation to farm production; the “on a farm” branch expressly accommodates someone other than the farmer. This route must explain the relationship requirement and each category, not substitute the employer's overall business identity for the text.

2. **Find the exclusion controlling for supported on-farm preparation, with separate treatment of drivers.** Catching, cooping, and loading can plausibly complete the grower's farming operation and fall within expressly listed market preparation. A nonfarmer employer does not defeat that route. The strongest objection is that the listed activities remain conditional and that actual work organization can locate them in a distinct commercial undertaking after raising is complete. An exclusion ruling must address that evidence and the limiting word “such.” Off-farm hauling has a different actor/place problem and cannot receive the same answer solely because the truck begins at a farm. Rejection of a firm-wide rule need not decide all workers identically.

3. **Limited remand for an identified statutory or agency-rationale error.** If the actual decision treated lack of employer-farmer status as sufficient to foreclose on-farm work, or failed to apply the incident/conjunction requirement to a materially different category, a remand can require the proper inquiry and consequential unit/remedy reconsideration. But the record contains a stated integration rationale and concrete operating testimony. A remand cannot rest on an invented absence of findings or presumed missing filing; it requires explaining why the grounds actually supplied fail. It does not automatically require an evidentiary hearing or establish agricultural status.

The primary “harvesting” alternative first requires disposition of §160(e)'s documented preservation objection. If properly entertained, the specific statutory distinction between harvesting agricultural/horticultural commodities and “raising” poultry remains material. A model cannot bypass the preserved secondary issue by silently assuming preservation of the new theory.
