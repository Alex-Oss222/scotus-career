## Procedural and factual limits

American Life League and five individuals sued the Attorney General May26,1994, the day FACE was enacted. Intervenors included provider organizations, clinics, and physicians. District court dismissed the second amended complaint **with prejudice under Rule12(b)(6)** June16,1994: 855F.Supp.137, EDVa No94-700-A. Fourth Circuit affirmed February13,1995: **47F.3d642**, No94-1869. These are the challenged lower judgments, not current Supreme Court holdings. Appendix1a–4a,25a–37a. The May12,1995 petition presents congressional power, speech, and religious-liberty/RFRA questions; it expressly invokes the intervening Lopez decision.

The circuit describes plaintiffs as opposing abortion on theological, moral and other grounds; their allegations include prayer, demonstrations, sidewalk counseling, peaceful conduct, and **physical obstruction of access**. They allege continuing conduct will expose them to FACE enforcement. Appendix3a (visually verified). This is a pre-enforcement pleadings case, not a conviction for proven violence. Peaceful and nonobstructive are not synonymous.

The circuit assumed plaintiffs sufficiently pleaded a substantial religious burden because their religious opposition allegedly required peaceful physical obstruction. Appendix21a. Government opposition20–21 disputes necessity/religious character, relying on district opinion36a, and argues prayer can continue without obstruction. Thus no neutral digest should declare 'no substantial burden' an established fact, or invent testimonial findings resolving this discrepancy. The actual second amended complaint is the best source for precise pleaded facts if a disposition turns on that threshold; the appellate assumption suffices only for an expressly assumed-burden alternative analysis.

The lower court expressly excluded **the worship protections from the issues**, Appendix5a n.1, and **the reasonableness of the $5,000 amount** from its damages ruling,19a n.5. The latter considered the Claiborne-based categorical objection to statutory damages, not an as-applied fine or damages calculation. Preserve those boundaries absent a broader authorized question supported by record.

## Statutory commands and qualifications

FACE§3 enacts18U.S.C.§248. The following tracks the statute rather than an advocate's characterization. For exact words consult the official four-page PDF cited above.

- **§248(a)(1):** by force, threat of force, or physical obstruction, intentionally injuring/intimidating/interfering with, or attempting to injure/intimidate/interfere with, a person **because** that person is or has been obtaining/providing reproductive health services, or **in order to intimidate** that person/another/class from obtaining/providing such services. Both alternative motive branches matter. It is not a general ban on all activity causing discomfort or persuading a patient to change her mind.
- **(a)(2):** parallel force/threat/obstruction prohibition concerning a person lawfully exercising or seeking religious freedom at a place of worship. **(a)(3):** intentional damage/destruction, or attempt, to property of a facility because it provides reproductive health services, or property of a place of worship. The worship provisions are textually present although not challenged in this case below.
- **Exception following(a):** a parent or legal guardian of a minor is not subject to **any penalties under this section** for activities described in(a) **directed exclusively at that minor**. Do not omit the exception in any discussion of universality or claim that all identical harms receive identical treatment. Its existence alone does not decide its comparability to these plaintiffs' conduct.
- **Definitions(e):** 'interfere with' means restrict freedom of movement; 'intimidate' means place a person in reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to self or another; physical obstruction means impassable ingress/egress or passage rendered unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Reproductive health services are those provided in a hospital, clinic, physician's office or other facility, including medical, surgical, counseling or referral services relating to the human reproductive system, pregnancy or termination. 'Facility' includes its building/structure and grounds, or a building/structure where worship occurs. This definition reaches services concerning carrying pregnancy to term as well as abortion.
- **Criminal penalties(b):** ordinary first offense up to one year; after a prior conviction under the section, up to three years, with title18 fine or both. If exclusively nonviolent physical obstruction, first offense maximum six months/$10,000; subsequent maximum eighteen months/$25,000. Bodily injury permits up to ten years; death any term of years or life. The different penalty branches must not be collapsed.
- **Private civil action(c)(1):** a person aggrieved by(a) may seek statutory relief, but(a)(1) standing is limited to persons involved in providing/seeking to provide or obtaining/seeking to obtain facility services; worship(a)(2) claim to persons lawfully exercising/seeking religious freedom or the relevant religious entity. Relief **may** include injunction, compensatory and punitive damages, costs and reasonable attorney/expert fees. At any time before final judgment, plaintiff **may elect $5,000 per violation in lieu of actual compensatory damages**. It is an election, not a mandatory award in every case or necessarily a replacement for all other remedies.
- **Government civil enforcement(c)(2),(3):** U.S.Attorney General may sue on reasonable cause; state Attorney General may sue as parens patriae on reasonable cause for natural-person residents. Relief includes appropriate injunction/compensation and specified civil penalties. Nonviolent obstruction: $10,000 first/$15,000 subsequent; other violation: $15,000 first/$25,000 subsequent. These are different from criminal penalties and private elected damages.
- **Savings(d):** no prohibition of expressive conduct (including peaceful picketing/demonstration) protected by the First Amendment; no new remedies for interference with free-speech/free-exercise activities outside a facility, regardless of viewpoint, and no limit on existing remedies; no exclusive criminal penalties or preemption of state/local laws regulating those activities; no interference with state/local abortion or reproductive-health regulation. These clauses do not automatically answer whether particular obstructive expressive conduct is constitutionally protected.
- **Act§5:** severability covers provisions and applications. **§6:** applies to conduct occurring on or after May26,1994. A remedy aimed at a defective branch must consider severability rather than presume total invalidation.

**RFRA is an independent statutory route.** Section3(a) forbids substantial religious burdens even from generally applicable rules, except under(b): government must demonstrate application **to the person** furthers a compelling interest and uses the least restrictive means. 'Demonstrates' assigns both production and persuasion burdens. Section3(c) permits a claim/defense and appropriate relief, with ordinary ArticleIII standing. Section6 applies to federal and state law, implementation, pre- and post-enactment; later federal law is subject unless it explicitly excludes RFRA **by reference to RFRA**. FACE does not contain that express exclusion. RFRA's belief-saving clause and Establishment Clause neutrality remain. The official statute supplies the law; constitutional neutrality under Smith does not terminate the statutory inquiry.

## Live paths supported by the parties and lower judgment

**Commerce and alternative power:** Circuit applied pre-Lopez rational-basis language and relied on legislative evidence of interstate patients/staff, interstate medical/office supplies, and closures/lost services from blockades/violence. Appendix6a–8a, with7a visually checked. These are legislative findings as reported by the court, not individual findings that every plaintiff crossed state lines. The actual congressional reports have not been independently fully read here. The enacted Act has a purpose clause, not a printed recital of all those findings and not an individual interstate-commerce element.

Petition7–14 argues local noneconomic protest, no case-specific jurisdictional element, no sufficiently substantial commercial connection, impermissible inference chains and intrusion on state police power. Government opposition8–12 distinguishes Lopez: direct interference with a market for medical services; interstate patients, staff and supplies; aggregate closures and service losses. It advances **both** protection of persons/things in interstate commerce and substantially-affecting-commerce routes, invokes Robertson, and disputes that an individual nexus is always needed. These alternatives must be tested against **simulated** Lopez/Robertson/Harris from the entering-law slice, not the historical opinions quoted by advocates. Section5 of the Fourteenth Amendment is a separately identified possible basis which the circuit **did not reach** after sustaining commerce power. Petition invokes private-action limits/Bray; don't treat §5 validity as already decided below or assume an independent Tenth Amendment veto without its own legal analysis.

**Speech:** Petition argues the motive element privileges reproductive-service messages over equally obstructive labor/environmental protests and effectively targets one viewpoint; cites R.A.V., Boos, Ladue and expressive-conduct principles. Government and circuit argue the law regulates harmful conduct, protects services on both sides of the abortion question, and narrows coverage by motive as permitted by Mitchell. The circuit nevertheless subjected potentially expressive **peaceful obstruction** to intermediate scrutiny rather than stopping at the label 'conduct'. Appendix8a–17a; petition16–24; opposition13–18. The neutral questions therefore include content/viewpoint neutrality, applicable test, substantial interests, fit, and alternative ways to communicate. Madsen's injunction standard cannot be silently imported as a statute standard.

**Vagueness/overbreadth and threats:** Petition24–25 argues chilling, ambiguous threats/interference, and protected political speech. Circuit17a–18a and opposition18–19 rely on narrowing definitions, Cameron, substantial overbreadth requirements and ordinary understanding. Keep true threats, physical obstruction, and persuasive protected expression distinct; do not treat all politically phrased speech as either immune or criminal. Statutory(d)(1) matters but is not by itself a factual finding resolving every possible application.

**Damages:** Petition25–26 invokes Claiborne's personal-responsibility and causation limits, with a hypothetical mixing protected persuasion and obstruction. Circuit18a–19a answers that elected statutory damages attach to the violation/unprotected conduct and that Claiborne did not forbid liquidated damages exceeding actual loss. That answer does not authorize damages for group membership, another person's unlawful act, or protected persuasion alone; no particular award is under review. The amount's reasonableness was reserved below.

**Free exercise and RFRA:** Petition27–29 alleges targeting of religious practice and exemptions for analogous secular protest, invoking Lukumi separately from RFRA. Circuit19a–23a treats FACE as secularly neutral/general, then separately assumes a substantial religious burden and invokes public health/safety, reliable access, and the prohibition's confined scope to satisfy compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means review. Government opposition20–21 alternatively argues no burden and compelling/narrow justification. The government’s burden argument conflicts with the circuit's favorable pleading assumption; preserve that distinction. A finding of statutory least-restrictive means must explain why asserted accommodations fail **for these claimants and their conduct**, taking the parent/minor exception into account where relevant, instead of merely citing the speech intermediate-scrutiny result. Swanner/Clearwater in the simulated entering law may supply further governing limits.
