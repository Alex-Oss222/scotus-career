## Full-read qualification and neutral limits

The source-retrieval context read all of Daggs and Donnell, including their case statements, reported arguments where present, and complete opinions; it also visually checked Daggs page 555 and Donnell page 374. A separate bounded source-retrieval context read the full Marquette report, including all 31 numbered footnotes. These are source-collection reading attestations. They do not certify that a later model has read the opinions, and the present note is not a substitute for that model's required full reading. Distinguish syllabus, statement, and counsel argument from judicial reasoning.

- **Marquette:** The judgment permits the Nebraska national bank to charge Minnesota cardholders Nebraska-authorized interest rates. The opinion addresses the bank's location, interstate credit, §85's text and purpose, and competing state rate limits. Footnote 26 at 314 discusses the Comptroller's regulation incorporating most-favored-lender status. The case does not decide the classification of particular flat fees as “interest” or establish a general administrative-deference rule. Preserve its reservations concerning local participants themselves extending credit, 307–308 and n.24, and “organized or existing” in the statutory exception, n.19.
- **Daggs:** The notes bore ten-percent annual interest; territorial law permitted any written agreed rate. At 555, the Court construes “fixed by the laws” to mean “allowed by the laws,” not necessarily a rate numerically expressed in a statute. At 556, it identifies the contrary-seeming Johnson remarks as unnecessary to that decision and not expressing the Court's judgment. The separate counterclaim issue, 556–558, is also retained. This is an agreed-rate/territorial-law case; it contains no adjudication of flat late-payment charges.
- **Donnell:** The transactions included interest compounded more often than Missouri allowed and overdraft charges of roughly one percent per month. At 373–374, the bank's penalty and de-minimis descriptions of the overdraft charges are recorded and rejected; the extended reasoning concerns compounding and the incorporated state restriction. At 374, the Court says: “The suggestions as to the twelve per cent charge on overdrafts do not seem to us to need answer.” It also rejects curing statutory forfeiture by electing to remit excess interest after a usury defense is pleaded. Preserve both the compounding ruling and the express disposition of the overdraft arguments; the opinion gives no extended explanation of the latter and does not adjudicate a fixed-dollar late fee.

These sources supply their own historical holdings and limits. They do not resolve the current dispute or determine how any present Justice should vote.
