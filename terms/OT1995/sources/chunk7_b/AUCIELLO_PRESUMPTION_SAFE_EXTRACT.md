# Auciello — majority-presumption source passages

Bounded extracts from existing sources only. This handoff contains the Board's merits advocacy and separately identified lower-decision passages; it supplies no new adjudication or factual assumption.

## Source and attribution

Primary merits source: `terms/OT1995/sources/A_micro_IA40385013_0661 6. Respondents Brief_djvu.txt`, printed pp.17–19, original OCR lines 980–1111. The first footnote on printed p.18 (n.1), attached to the discussion of the contract-term presumption, supplies the concrete “unusual circumstances” examples. The complete three pages are reproduced below. Page 17 begins with a continuation from the preceding page. The main-text sentence and footnote 4 continuing onto p.20 are separately completed below.

The Board advocates a conclusive presumption normally for the certification year and during a collective agreement of up to three years, with the stated unusual-circumstances qualification. Its p.18 n.1 examples are **dissolution or defunct status of the certified union**, or **substantially all members and officers transferring affiliation to another local or international as the result of a schism**. The phrase “such as” makes the examples illustrative. The final sentence, “None of those circumstances is present,” is the Board's assertion in its merits brief, not independently identified here as a lower-court finding.

The distinct simultaneous-information/acceptance exception comes from the Board's supplemental decision, not from n.1's dissolution/schism examples. The actual lower-court treatment of that timing exception is supplied separately. The election-petition window is also separate: it does not itself authorize unilateral refusal to bargain whenever an employer doubts support during a contract.

## Board supplemental decision — separate simultaneous-event exception

Source: petition appendix printed p.54a, section L, with footnotes 76–77; footnote 77 continues on p.55a. The Board reserves case-by-case evaluation when the employer first receives signed anti-representation petitions virtually simultaneously with acceptance. This differs from evidence known earlier and assessed or asserted later. The same passage separately addresses both parties' knowledge of actual loss of majority status, and footnote 77 states that the present case does not allege actual loss. The quoted examples and reservations are the agency's expressed rule and case-description, not findings that those hypothetical events occurred here.

## First Circuit — actual treatment of the timing exception

Source: 60 F.3d 24, 27 n.2, recovered file `A_Auciello_lower_CA1_60F3d24.txt`, line 52. This is the lower court's conclusion about the timing exception, rather than the Board's litigation assertion about dissolution or schism:

> The Company seeks to bring this case within the exception set forth in the Board's opinion for cases in which a company’s good faith doubt arises simultaneously with the union's acceptance of the offer. See Auciello, 317 N.L.R.B. No. 60, at 11-12. We agree with the Board's implicit conclusion that this case does not fall within the exception, as the Company had all of the relevant information to support its claim prior to its acceptance of the offer.
