STATEMENT 


Petitioner was convicted by a general court-martial of 
premeditated murder, felony murder, attempted murder, 
and robbery (four specifications), in violation of Articles 
118, 80, and 122 of the Uniform Code of Military Justice 
(UCMJ), 10 U.S.C. 918, 880, and 922. The court sen- 
tenced petitioner to death, a dishonorable discharge, and 
forfeiture of all pay and allowances. The United States 
Army Court of Military Review affirmed. 34 M.J. 956 
(Pet. App. 229a-256a), reconsideration denied, 34 M.J. 
1065 (A.C.M.R. 1992) (Pet. App. 257a-265a). The

1. In United States v. Matthews, 16 M.J. 354 (1983), 


the United States Court of Military Appeals (now the 
United States Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces) 
analyzed the constitutionality of Article 118 of the UCMJ, 
10 U.S.C. 918, which authorizes the imposition of the 
death penalty on defendants who are found guilty of cer- 
tain murders. The court concluded that Furman v. Geor- 
gia, 408 U.S. 238 (1972) (per curiam), and its progeny, 
which assessed the constitutionality of state death penalty 
schemes, would apply without modification to the imposi- 
tion of the death penalty in the military, at least when the

After reviewing this Court’s Eighth Amendment cases, 
the Matthews court concluded that certain features ap- 
peared in a constitutionally valid death penalty procedure: 
a bifurcated sentencing proceeding; specific aggravating 
circumstances to be identified by the sentencing authority; 
selection of and findings on the particular aggravating 
circumstances used by the sentencer to impose the death 
penalty; unrestricted opportunity for the defendant to 
present mitigation and extenuation evidence; and manda- 
tory appellate review of the appropriateness of the sen- 
tence. 16 M.J. at 377. The court found all of those 
safeguards present in the military system except for a re- 
quirement that court members identify the specific aggra- 
vating factors upon which they relied in imposing the 
death penalty. /d. at 379. Because of the absence of that 
safeguard, the court reversed Matthews’ death sentence. 
Id, at 381-382. The court concluded, however, that either 
Congress or the President, in his capacity as Commander- 
in-Chief and/or pursuant to the powers that Congress 
delegated to him, could remedy the defect. Jd. at 380 
(citing Article 36 of the UCMJ, 10 U.S.C. 836). 

On January 24, 1984, “[b]y the authority vested in 
[him] as President by the Constitution of the United 
States and by Chapter 47 of Title 10 of the United States 
Code li.e., the UCMJ],” the President responded by pro- 
mulgating an amendment to the extant Manual for Courts- 
Martial (Manual) that provided procedures for adjudicat- 
ing cases in which the death penalty is authorized by the 
UCMSJ. See Exec. Order No. 12,460, 49 Fed. Reg. 3169 
(1984). When a new revision of the Manual was pub- 
lished later that year, the provisions of the Executive Or- 
der were included as Rule for Courts-Martial (RCM) 
1004. See Manual (1984 ed.).' 


' Prior to the decision in Matthews, the Department of Defense 
announced and solicited comments on the proposed changes to the

Briefly summarized, RCM 1004 requires that, before a 
death penalty may be imposed following a general court- 
martial conviction of an offense for which Congress has 
expressly authorized the death penalty, the accused must 
have been notified, prior to trial, of the aggravating factors 
the prosecution intends to prove; the members of the 
court-martial must unanimously find the existence of at 
least one of the aggravating factors specified in the Rule; 
the accused must be given broad latitude to present evi- 
dence in extenuation and mitigation; and the members 
must concur that such extenuating or mitigating circum- 
stances are “substantially outweighed” by any aggravating 
circumstances found to exist. See RCM 1004(b). In 
addition, the Rule prescribes a list of aggravating factors, 
some of which apply to all capital offenses, others of 
which apply exclusively to specifically enumerated crimes. 
See RCM 1004(c).

Article 118 of the Uniform Code of Military Justice

10 U.S.C. 918. Rule for Courts-Martial (RCM) 1004,

not violate the separation-of-powers doctrine or the Eighth 
Amendment.
