## Smiley: federal definition, interpretive delegation and preemption

| Source | Eligible Justice-specific evidence | Confined use |
|---|---|---|
| Adams Fruit, 494 U.S. 638 (1990), 649–650 | Unanimous opinion joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. | Delegation is a prerequisite to controlling agency construction; judicial private remedies differ from delegated administrative standards. The presence of some private enforcement does not alone resolve the distinct scope of OCC enforcement under §85. |
| Bowen, 488 U.S. 204 (1988), 208–213; 216–225 | Kennedy wrote for a unanimous Court including Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia; Scalia separately concurred on APA rulemaking and futurity. | No unauthorized retroactive rulemaking and no automatic deference to unsupported litigation positions. Any sharper view on the precise text, §43 procedure or retroactivity must be grounded in the packet and actual writings, not inferred from a generic attitude toward agencies.

## Loving: capital delegation, military function and remedy

| Source | Eligible Justice-specific evidence | Confined use |
|---|---|---|
| Mistretta, 488 U.S. 361 (1989), 371–379 and n.11 | Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy joined Blackmun's Court opinion. | Intelligible-principle and separation-of-powers analysis. Note 11 assumes, without deciding, capital-guideline authority based on congressional punishment authorization and substantial guidance; it does not resolve presidential murder aggravators. |
| Mistretta, 413–427 | Scalia dissented. | His objection to an institution exercising lawmaking apart from executive/judicial duties must be distinguished from discretion ancillary to a genuine executive function. The opinion expressly treats judicial policing of degrees of discretion as difficult. It is not a promise to invalidate every criminal delegation. |
| Touby, 500 U.S. 160 (1991), 164–168 | O'Connor authored the unanimous opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer were not yet participating Justices. | The Court left open a more demanding rule for criminal delegations because the actual statutory constraints sufficed even under that contention. It rejected the asserted impermissible concentration of scheduling and prosecutorial functions on that record. |
| Solorio, 483 U.S. 435 (1987) | O'Connor and Scalia joined Rehnquist's Court opinion; Stevens concurred only in judgment, 451–452. | Stevens would resolve the actual service connection without overruling the earlier rule. These writings illuminate differing scope methods but do not add a new jurisdiction question to this grant or decide military capital safeguards. |
| Actual Weiss, January 19, 1994 | Kennedy authored Part A, joined by all then-current Associates, including Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Their individual assessment must address the actual Articles 18/36/56/118 text, pre-1990 Article 36 reporting provision, pre-1991 RCM version, and Article II alternative rather than treat generalized military deference as a conclusion.

| Source | Eligible Justice-specific evidence | Confined use |
|---|---|---|
| Curtin Matheson, 494 U.S. 775 (1990), 786–796 | Stevens joined Marshall's Court opinion. | Rational Board policy need not presume striker replacements oppose the union; judicial review respects reasonable policy within the Act. It does not adjudicate when a post-acceptance doubt may be raised. |
| Curtin Matheson, 801–819 | Scalia dissented, joined by O'Connor and Kennedy. | Distinguishes Board policymaking from substantial-evidence review and demands consistency/explanation in evaluating evidence. This source is a real contrary route, not a general instruction to reject Board policy. |

| Actual MCI and Town & Country | MCI joins identified above; Ginsburg's Town & Country opinion was unanimous, including Breyer. | Textual limits coexist with agency choice within a genuine delegation. Town & Country settles no union-majority presumption or post-acceptance timing rule. |
