## Quackenbush, No. 95-244

**Source result.** The existing neutral accurately preserves the material record and competing routes. The actual Archive listing confirms petition, both merits briefs, reply and JA. The District Court order, lower opinion, rehearing waiver and targeted briefs support the following distinctions. No material factual replacement is required.

**Questions and channel.** The two presented questions concern appeal versus mandamus and whether abstention generally or Burford specifically is confined to equitable actions. The court below vacated the July 1, 1991 Burford remand and remanded to the District Court; it did not order arbitration. Treat ordinary §1291 finality and Cohen collateral-order finality as separate theories. Allstate alternatively seeks mandamus or remand for the Ninth Circuit to address that request if appeal is unavailable; a decision against appealability need not leave the alternative petition unanswered. Section 1292(b) remains a separate conditional channel, not a supplied certification.

**Threshold limits.** The District Court found diversity jurisdiction and rejected the asserted exclusive in rem jurisdiction of the liquidation court. It separately found no parallel Allstate state proceeding supporting Colorado River abstention; no challenge to that ruling is supplied. The Commissioner's attempt to make all remands unreviewable under §1447(d) was rejected below and is absent from the petition's questions; the reply reserves his disagreement but does not itself enlarge the grant. The actual Things Remembered rule covers timely removal defects and lack of subject-matter jurisdiction under §1447(c), including dual bankruptcy removal, and does not categorically decide review of this abstention remand.

**Law/equity and remedy fork.** An outright remand or dismissal surrendering federal adjudication must be distinguished from a retained-jurisdiction stay. A supported resolution may preserve authority to stay an action at law in appropriate circumstances while rejecting an outright Burford remand of damages claims. That distinction alone does not show that a stay is warranted here. The District Court expressly declined Allstate's proposed stay pending the state setoff decision. NOPSI's equity language, the historic Burford conditions, and the separate Thibodaux/McNary analogies must each be evaluated within their own procedural settings; neither merger of law and equity nor the existence of some law-action abstention automatically resolves this particular surrender of jurisdiction.

**Competing characterization and preservation.** The Commissioner argues that liquidation, marshaling, setoffs and requested declarations make this an equitable or quasi in rem matter. Allstate characterizes it as an ordinary contract action to enlarge the estate and says the declaration restates the damages claim. The Ninth Circuit refused to consider the declaratory-count argument first raised on rehearing. The neutral must retain that waiver and not silently convert the action into an uncontested declaration-only suit. Wilton therefore supplies no categorical answer: its declaration-only, adequate parallel state proceeding and retained jurisdiction qualifications remain.

**Independent grounds and reach.** Allstate argues no Burford disruption exists even if legal actions are eligible, and that the federal arbitration motion must at least be considered before abstention. The Ninth Circuit did not decide those independent grounds; the Commissioner argues the proper exercise of any discretion lies outside the question granted. State-law complexity and receivership context are supported contentions, not findings that every Burford prerequisite is met. Prudential resolved some setoff law; the later Imperial decision addressed particular treaties and a settlement release and does not establish that all remaining Allstate setoff issues disappeared. No order below adjudicated arbitration, and this source intake supplies no assent, enforceability, waiver, McCarran-Ferguson or FAA merits determination.

**Remedial guardrails.** Affirming the Ninth Circuit leaves its vacatur and District Court remand in place. Reversing its legal limitation or appellate-channel ruling requires a stated next step and attention to the preserved mandamus alternative. Neither outcome automatically compels arbitration, enters a money judgment, directs liquidation distribution, or holds that every removed abstention remand is appealable.

## Smiley, No. 95-860

**Material update.** Replace the neutral's statement that the United States/Comptroller amicus is unavailable. It is Archive item 0672, numbered **09**, now saved as `Smiley_09. Amicus Curiae Brief_djvu.txt`. Its actual text supports the below description. Also replace the stale March 18 law baseline: actual Barnett Bank was entered March 26, 1996 and the rule's formal effective date is April 1, 1996. Source admission and current projection are root responsibilities; neither a prepared packet nor this note supplies an ASR.

**Questions and common premise.** The petition asks federal statutory meaning, alleged delegation of the definition to South Dakota and constitutional delegation; the grant is not shown limited to one question. Both parties nonetheless agree the meaning of “interest” is federal. Citibank and the United States say this removes the first two questions; petitioner argues the exported rate's content still raises them. A federal definition plus home-state selection of allowed charges is not identical to a state defining the federal term. A statutory resolution can leave the constitutional question unnecessary, but the neutrality of that premise must be explained.

**Record and posture.** Review is of judgment on the pleadings in a putative class action under California Civil Code §1671. Smiley does not contest the ordinary periodic finance charge, Citibank's South Dakota location, or permission under South Dakota law. The allegation that the challenged contingent charges are penalties unrelated to loss remains an allegation; no cost, market, economic-efficiency or consumer-benefit finding is supplied. The Classic and Preferred agreements differ. In particular, the Preferred agreement combines $6 after the stated delinquency interval with an additional $15-or-0.65% charge at the later interval and succeeding qualifying months. Do not reduce the case to a stipulated identical monthly flat fee. The 1995 California legislation does not establish retrospective mootness or compliance of every challenged charge.

**Government position recovered.** The US/OCC brief pp.20–23 invokes §§93 and 1818 for administrative enforcement of §85 and §93a for rules carrying out the office's responsibilities. It distinguishes Adams Fruit because the present issue is §85's substantive term, enforceable administratively as well as judicially, rather than construction of §86's private penalty. It argues that the current interpretation is reasonable despite any prior inconsistency; construes the 1964 Saxon letter as addressing state periodic ceilings rather than defining §85; acknowledges the 1988 letter's state-definition approach and says the later federal-definition clarification agrees with petitioner on the governing source of meaning. Footnote 7 consequently regards the first two petition questions as not presented. Pages 23–26 argue that state restrictions obstruct §85 permission; their quotations from the post-divergence historical Barnett Bank brief citation do not supply simulated law. Actual Barnett's public holding must be substituted.

**Statutory routes.** A supported path is independent federal construction including default charges; another excludes these contingent sum-certain charges based on the combined phrase “interest at the rate,” historical usage and statutory structure; another finds unresolved statutory room and evaluates the agency's construction. Petitioner's concession that not every interest charge must be expressed as a percentage forecloses an oversimplified percentage-versus-flat rule. The ordinary lexicon, contemporaneous authorities, §85's purpose and §86's penalty structure point in disputed directions that must be addressed rather than mechanically scored. A conclusion on these late fees need not decide every fee listed in the regulation, every contract condition, or all DIDA institutions.

**Deference routes and exceptions.** A deference-based path must first identify actual interpretive authority, respect any unambiguous text, and assess this interpretation's permissible reach. Petitioner's private-remedy/Adams Fruit argument, §93a authority objection, §43 procedure objection, inconsistent letters, final-rule explanation, asserted litigation motivation and retroactivity objection remain distinct. The rule's formal April 1 effectiveness does not by itself answer interpretive weight for earlier conduct. Bowen's rule against unauthorized retroactive rulemaking and unsupported litigation positions must be distinguished from reliance on a formally promulgated interpretation of an existing statute. Good Samaritan preserves review of text, delegation and reasons despite a change of position; it does not grant automatic deference merely because an agency exists. No respondent answer to the April 15 reply is invented; later analytical answers are identified as analysis, not filings.

**Rule and state-law qualifications.** The exact official text and transition check are in the OCC note. Its nonexclusive definition retains ordinarily excluded appraisal, repayment-insurance, finder's, document and credit-report charges. Paragraph (b) retains material home-state class-of-loan conditions; paragraph (c) distinguishes federal exportability from a state's solely internal usury calculation. Section 85's most-favored-lender rule and home-state limit do not disappear. Section 86 forfeiture and twice-paid-interest remedies require their own statutory predicates and the latter's two-year period; Smiley does not obtain a §86 recovery merely by winning this California-law appeal.

**Preemption and relief.** Actual Barnett protects conferred banking powers against prevention or significant interference while retaining governing federal conditions and compatible state regulation; it neither decides §85's definition nor creates a new deference rule. Once the federal definition and authority are resolved, address the particular incompatible California restriction and any compatible laws within the actual scope. Affirmance preserves judgment on the pleadings. Reversal does not itself establish that the fees violate §1671, certify a class, award restitution, impose §86 penalties, or determine a dollar recovery.

## Auciello, No. 95-668

**Source result.** The existing neutral preserves the material forks. The Archive listing also contains numbered **4, Joint Appendix**, now recovered, with management testimony and resignation documents. Its presence removes any possible research-gap assumption. No new historical event is needed.

**Question and settled components.** The live issue is a post-acceptance good-faith doubt grounded in pre-acceptance events known to the employer, with the asserted reasonable assessment period. The prior 1992 enforcement proceeding sustained that the November 17 offer stayed open and that membership ratification was unnecessary; it remanded for explanation of the majority-status policy. Those predicates are not unresolved contract-formation questions simply because Auciello's November 28 response originally disputed them.

**Findings versus allegations.** No Board or appellate finding establishes either sufficient objectively grounded good-faith doubt or actual minority status at acceptance. Management's later assessment of employee sentiment is distinct from when it learned the underlying information. The Board found the information known while the offer was open; the employer argues the meaningful erosion occurred during the Thanksgiving-shortened interval. The JA and Board brief support the timing details, including twelve resignation forms dated by November 21 and the remaining form November 23; those dates do not by themselves establish an objectively sufficient majority-status doubt. Resigning membership, working during a strike and rejecting representation are not interchangeable.

**Presumptions and exceptions.** The certification-year conclusive presumption, absent unusual circumstances, and the limited contract-period conclusive presumption must be distinguished from the rebuttable presumption after expiration. The initial 1977 certification does not itself make majority status irrebuttable in 1988. The Board defends stability when a certified incumbent with an unchallenged presumption accepts an outstanding offer. Its supplemental decision reserves extraordinary cases in which doubt arises simultaneously with acceptance. The First Circuit found that exception inapplicable here because the information was known earlier. Do not turn the reservation into a categorical exception-free rule.

**Actual minority and preservation.** The Government invokes §10(e) because an actual-loss-of-majority claim was not urged to the Board; the statute's extraordinary-circumstances qualification remains, with no such showing supplied here. Board brief p.26 n.7 expressly takes no position on a post-acceptance actual-minority claim based on previously known events. The granted good-faith-doubt question does not settle that reserved category. Garment Workers' initial recognition of an actually minority union cannot be equated with the established incumbent and unproved minority status without reasoning.

**Alternative grounds.** The principal agency-policy route asks whether the rule is rational, reasoned and consistent with the Act; it is distinct from finding substantial evidence of an actual minority. A statutory employee-choice objection must confront the presumptions and timing, the employer's ability to withdraw its offer/assert doubt before acceptance, and the Board's stated exception. Alternatively, the Board argues Auciello actually had a reasonable assessment period even if one is legally required, pp.42–45. That path must address the employer's holiday-week timing argument and cannot assume sufficient objective doubt was conceded. A rejection of the categorical policy may call for agency consideration of the doubt or of a properly stated exception, rather than a judicial finding on evidence the Board never evaluated.

**Remedy and participation.** The First Circuit enforced the Board's order in full: upon the Union's request, write, sign and effectuate the accepted agreement retroactive to November 27, 1988, and make employees whole, together with the cease-and-desist directions. It declined a condition requiring a Board election; it did not eliminate every lawful future election route. Petitioner requests reversal and remand for the Board to decide its good-faith doubt. No automatic union decertification, finding of minority, judicially calculated make-whole amount, or fresh contract formation is supplied. The assigned neutral identifies all nine event-date Justices and no case-specific nonparticipation; this review uncovered no contrary participation source for any of the four assigned cases. Historical current-case Supreme Court opinion headers were not used to establish participation.
