# Loving — neutral source corrections and supported paths

Source preparation only. Cutoff: before June 3, 1996. No new vote, holding, historical Supreme Court disposition, or simulation event is supplied. Pinpoints refer to the primary documents in `SOURCE_MAP.md`.

## Corrections to the supplied neutral

The statement that Congress "delegated ... authority over ... sentencing factors" prejudges one of the presented issues. Replace it with: Congress enacted UCMJ provisions authorizing specified punishments and conferring rulemaking and punishment-limiting powers; whether those provisions authorize these capital aggravating factors, and whether that authority is constitutionally delegable and sufficiently bounded, is disputed.

The petition presents three questions: whether Congress may delegate its legislative responsibility to establish military capital aggravators consistently with Article I and the Eighth Amendment; whether it implicitly did so; and whether it supplied constitutionally adequate standards. Petitioner merits questions I–IV divide the second petition question into the asserted requirement of explicit delegation and the possibility of finding implicit delegation. The Government asks whether the President had authority to promulgate RCM 1004 where the UCMJ otherwise expressly authorizes death. The record supports statutory authority, required clarity, adequate guidance, constitutional nondelegability and Article II alternatives as distinct components; the present one-sentence question should not erase them. The merits sources identify an unqualified September 27, 1995 grant; no limitation to only the constitutional validity of an assumed delegation is supported.

CAAF's November 10, 1994 judgment, 41 M.J. 213, affirmed convictions and sentence; reconsideration was denied February 2, 1995. Its relevant discussion, Pet. App. 141a–142a, rejects the delegation challenge under United States v. Curtis, 32 M.J. 252 (1991), and Matthews, 16 M.J. 354 (1983). The Army Court of Military Review affirmed, 34 M.J. 956, with reconsideration denied, 34 M.J. 1065. Review rests on 28 U.S.C. §1259(1). Petition filed June 2, 1995. Petitioner seeks reversal of the CAAF judgment, merits p.50; United States seeks affirmance, p.47. Neither request by itself supplies an acquittal, release, new guilt trial, immediate execution or a resentencing protocol.

Loving, an Army private at Fort Hood, was convicted of premeditated murder of Bobby Sharbino and felony murder of Christopher Fay, attempted murder, and four robbery specifications. Fay was an active-duty soldier driving a taxi for extra money; his killing occurred on Fort Hood. Sharbino was a retired Army sergeant killed in Killeen. An eight-member general court-martial unanimously found the two retained murder offenses and imposed death, dishonorable discharge and forfeiture of all pay and allowances on April 3, 1989. Other overlapping murder/robbery specifications were dismissed as multiplicitous; do not count them as additional retained convictions. The panel unanimously found three aggravators: premeditated murder during robbery, RCM 1004(c)(7)(B); another Article 118 murder in the same case, (c)(7)(J); and actual perpetration of the killing in the felony murder, (c)(8). Government merits pp.5–7; petitioner pp.3–4; Pet. App. 8a–9a, 253a–254a. These source facts do not establish that every military murder, every off-base offense, or every civilian-victim offense has the same constitutional treatment.

## Applicable text and qualifications

**Article 118, 10 U.S.C. §918:** unlawful killing without justification or excuse is murder under four specified alternatives: premeditated design; intent to kill or inflict great bodily harm; inherently dangerous conduct evincing wanton disregard; or perpetration/attempted perpetration of burglary, sodomy, rape, robbery or aggravated arson. Only clauses (1) and (4) carry the stated death-or-life requirement, "as a court-martial may direct." Do not say every Article 118 murder is capital. The 1992 amendment changes a word in clause (3) for later offenses; it does not alter the charged (1)/(4) alternatives. JA 145 supplies the applicable text.

**Article 18, §818:** subject to Article 17, general courts-martial may try persons subject to the UCMJ for UCMJ offenses and, under presidential limitations, adjudge punishments not forbidden by the chapter, including death when specifically authorized by the chapter. A separate law-of-war jurisdiction sentence exists. A military-judge-only general court-martial under Article 16(1)(B) lacks jurisdiction over a death-eligible offense unless previously referred noncapital. This case concerns an eight-member panel, not that exception.

**Article 36, §836:** presidential regulations may prescribe pretrial, trial and post-trial procedures, including modes of proof; they shall, so far as the President considers practicable, apply principles of law and rules of evidence generally recognized in federal criminal trials, but may not conflict with the UCMJ. Subsection (b) requires uniformity insofar as practicable. The applicable version in JA 140 also requires reporting to Congress; the 1990 amendment removed that reporting clause. The practicability qualification does not eliminate the separate prohibition against inconsistency with the Code. Whether capital eligibility factors fall within "procedures" is contested, not stipulated.

**Article 56, §856:** a punishment directed by a court-martial may not exceed presidentially prescribed limits for that offense. The parties disagree whether a categorical limitation on the occasions for imposing death is such a limit; the statute should be quoted before resolving that issue.

**Article 106a:** Congress's 1985 espionage provision specifies aggravating factors and authorizes any other factor the President prescribes under Article 36. The Government treats this as confirming preexisting general authority; petitioner treats it as specific new authority and denies that later legislative views or silence validate the earlier murder regulation. It is not the offense of conviction. Do not substitute its express language for the distinct murder delegation question.

**RCM 1004 before 1991, JA 132–138:** death requires express authorization for the offense, unanimous conviction of that offense, at least one specified aggravator proved beyond reasonable doubt and found unanimously, and unanimous agreement that extenuation/mitigation is substantially outweighed by admissible aggravating circumstances. Accused receives broad latitude to offer extenuation/mitigation; members must consider all such evidence before adjudging death. Eligibility aggravators and the broader permissible sentencing aggravation are distinct. Required notice is before arraignment; late notice/proof is not barred unless the accused shows specific prejudice and that a continuance or recess is inadequate. The rule provides separate secret written ballots on aggravators and announcement of those found. The murder factors at issue are the versions reproduced above; later changes expanding felony-murder eligibility cannot be silently substituted. The rule also contains a distinct spying exception, Article 106, and offense-specific limits on other listed factors; none decides this murder case.

**Article 55** forbids flogging, branding/marking/tattooing, other cruel or unusual punishment, and irons except for safe custody. **Article 71(a)** requires presidential approval before the death part of a sentence can be executed. Its general authority to commute, remit or suspend is qualified: the death part may not be suspended. Section 71(c) separately requires finality of review in its stated circumstances. Affirmance of this delegation challenge would not be presidential approval or an execution order. No later approval, execution date, or collateral filing is posited.

## Adversarial routes supported by the briefing

1. **No statutory authorization / heightened clarity.** Petitioner distinguishes procedure and numerical punishment ceilings from the moral policy choice establishing death eligibility. Kent and Greene support narrow construction of ambiguous delegations touching protected liberty; lenity and constitutional avoidance reinforce his reading. The President has no independent peacetime authority to enact substantive criminal law, and later acquiescence cannot replace bicameralism/presentment. If this ground succeeds, the consequence concerns the death sentence resting on presidential aggravators; the validity of independent convictions and the permissible further military sentencing disposition require separately stated analysis.
2. **Authorized and sufficiently bounded limitation.** The Government reads Articles 18 and 56 to allow narrower capital availability within Article 118's already enacted maximum, with Article 36 supplying procedural authority and guidance. Congress need not foresee the precise post-Furman application. It points to military discipline, national uniformity, Article 55, civilian criminal-law principles, and Congress's own capital judgments; it distinguishes creating crimes/punishments from restricting their application. Petitioner answers that these sources supply no enacted selection standard, and that "so far as ... practicable" permits unguided choice.
3. **Nondelegability / impermissible concentration.** Petitioner separately argues that the death-eligibility judgment itself must remain legislative, and that assigning it to the commander who also supervises prosecution, tribunals and clemency impermissibly blends functions. The Government relies on Mistretta/Touby, historic military practice and institutional safeguards; it disputes a separate Eighth Amendment rule requiring every narrowing criterion to be enacted by a legislature. Mistretta n.11 assumed, without deciding, capital-guideline authority and required both original congressional authorization of the punishment and its substantial guidance. It is not a holding resolving this delegation.
4. **Article II alternative or a narrower contextual use.** The Government asserts independent Commander-in-Chief/Take Care authority within statutory bounds, or at minimum a less demanding delegation inquiry when executive authority overlaps. A Court could rely on statutory authorization while using the President's military role only in evaluating the amount of guidance required, leaving independent power undecided. A free-standing Article II disposition would require its own reasoned limits and answer to petitioner's Youngstown/Reid objection; it must not be smuggled into a statutory holding.
5. **Constitutional assumption and reach.** Government merits nn.5–7 records that the Supreme Court had left military application of Furman open, does not quarrel here with Matthews's limited additional-aggravator requirement, and questions whether Article 118 already narrows adequately. It states petitioner does not challenge whether these factors would sufficiently narrow if Congress enacted them. Thus a resolution can assume the relevant civilian narrowing requirement without declaring every civilian capital rule categorically applicable to all military offenses. Validity of individual unused aggravators, a nonservice-connected capital jurisdiction theory, and unrelated trial-bias/command-influence claims do not become independently granted questions merely because discussed as context.
