# Williams — trial instructions and narrow source limits

**Cutoff:** April 9, 1996. Source extraction only; no current Supreme Court disposition, vote, private position, or proposed disposition is supplied.

## Actual Williams instruction menu

*Williams v. Armontrout*, **679 F. Supp. 916, 923 (W.D. Mo. February 9, 1988)**, states:

> At trial, the jury was instructed on capital murder (the offense charged) and on the lesser included offenses of second degree murder and manslaughter. Respondent’s Exhibit A-3 at 93-95.

The same paragraph states that **both Williams's counsel and the State offered a first-degree murder instruction**, meaning killing while perpetrating or attempting kidnapping, and that the trial judge refused it as unsupported by the evidence. The source gives Exhibit A-2 at 610–613 and Exhibit A-3 at 112 for the offer, and Exhibit A-2 at 611–616 and 754–756 for the refusal. **Exact locator:** retained CAP `679fsupp916_CAP.html`, paragraph `b991-5`.

This is a direct reported description of **Williams's own trial**, not the Mondaine comparison in the later en banc opinion. The original Exhibit A-3 has not been recovered. The report establishes that noncapital second-degree murder and manslaughter options were actually supplied; it does not reproduce their full instruction texts or say that a **second-degree felony-murder theory** was supplied. Those are different propositions.

*State v. Williams*, **652 S.W.2d 102, 112 (Mo. 1983)**, Point X, CAP `b148-5`, independently confirms the refused first-degree kidnapping-murder instruction. Its complete judicial writings do not enumerate the other verdict options. Point IX's rejection of a demand to reduce the charge to manslaughter, pp. 111–112, is not a finding that no manslaughter instruction was given. The explicit 1988 account resolves that distinction.

## Earlier ruling and second-degree claim limits

The 1988 report quotes the trial judge describing kidnapping as a link in the planned murder and stating that there was no independent collateral felony; it also quotes the judge's explanation on the motion for acquittal or a new trial. **679 F. Supp. at 923**, CAP `b991-5`–`b991-13` and `ASl`; reported Exhibit A-2 at 613–614 and 754–755. These are the trial judge's explanations, not stipulated facts about intent. The opposing trial accounts already extracted from 912 F.2d 924 remain material source content.

The full 1983 and 1988 opinions do **not** specify the separate second-degree felony-murder theory later reported, its requested wording or precise predicate offense, or its presentation to the original appellate panel versus the en banc court. Do not turn the supported fact that an ordinary second-degree option was given into proof that this distinct claim was presented, preserved, granted, or resolved. The previously sanitized retrospective account remains the source for the later reported claim and its stated preservation limits.

## Proportionality review and the alleged omitted cases

The direct-appeal report expressly addresses Williams's contention that death was excessive and disproportionate. It compares *State v. Blair*, 638 S.W.2d 739, and states: “Considering the crime and the defendant, as required by § 565.014.3(3), we find no basis for holding that the death penalty was either excessive or disproportionate to the penalty imposed in similar cases.” **652 S.W.2d at 113–114**, Point XIV, CAP `b149-9`–`b149-14`. At **117**, CAP `b153-5`, it describes its death-sentence review as statutorily mandated.

These passages support the existence of the identified state-law review duty and the fact that review was conducted and stated. They do not reproduce the entire statute, prescribe a complete comparison-case universe, list the files actually examined, or establish the later allegation that approximately 200 relevant cases were missing. That allegation remains an allegation. Neither retrieved Williams opinion cites *Hicks v. Oklahoma* or establishes a particular Hicks theory asserted in the 1996 amendment. An analytical state-created-entitlement argument must be labeled as developed analysis; it cannot be attributed as Williams's actual preserved formulation on these sources alone.

## Relevant legal sources supplied separately

- **Schad:** [SCHAD_SOURCE_SCOPE.md](SCHAD_SOURCE_SCOPE.md) supplies the precise Court holding at **501 U.S. 645–648** and its limits. Part III rejects an entitlement under *Beck* to every supported lesser option where an evidence-supported noncapital second-degree option was supplied; it expressly does not establish that any unsupported nominal lesser option suffices. The fact that options were supplied in Williams is now established. Whether the necessary evidentiary and legal conditions are satisfied remains analysis, not an extracted fact. The broader Beck formulation in the 1988 district opinion is that earlier court's legal statement and must not replace Schad's later controlling qualification.
- **Foman:** [FOMAN_SOURCE_SCOPE.md](FOMAN_SOURCE_SCOPE.md) preserves **371 U.S. 182**: “absence of any apparent or declared reason,” including undue delay and the other listed grounds, and “without any justifying reason appearing for the denial.” The supported amendment timing and stage can therefore be considered when assessing an apparent justification, without inventing the district court's actual rationale. Unknown recovered reasons do not establish a reasons-writing violation; four years does not automatically establish undue delay or prejudice. This source work makes neither factual attribution nor adjudicative choice.
- **Habeas qualification:** The retained official 1994 §2254/Habeas Rules compilation at `terms/OT1994/sources/oneal/1994-28usc2254.txt`, **Rule 11**, states: “The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, to the extent that they are not inconsistent with these rules, may be applied, when appropriate, to petitions filed under these rules.” Its complete accompanying note preserves appropriateness and consistency and rejects use of civil rules to alter statutory exhaustion. Thus this supplement does not assume unqualified application of every civil rule to habeas. The full operative §2242 amendment paragraph was not recovered in this narrow follow-up; no purported quotation of it is supplied.

This supplement closes the instruction-menu factual gap. It leaves the distinct limits identified above explicit and does not require originals where the published record already supplies the proposition.
