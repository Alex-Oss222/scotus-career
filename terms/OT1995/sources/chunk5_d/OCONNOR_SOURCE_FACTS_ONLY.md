## What the lower documents say

The plaintiff James O'Connor was 56 at discharge; the identified replacement/retained manager Ted Finnell was **40**, turning 41 on August 27, 1990. “Over forty” is not the exact age in the district opinion. The district opinion and party briefs give August 9, 1990 as the termination date; the appellate narrative gives August 10. Preserve the date difference if material rather than silently selecting one.

Consolidated operated cafeteria and vending services as part of Canteen. O'Connor had managed 4Cs North, moved to troubled 4Cs South around the start of 1990, and had some territories reassigned to Mike Kiser in July. Canteen then consolidated North and South Carolina management into two larger districts. Finnell, age 40, and Kiser, age 35, headed the new districts. Allen Hunter, age 57, moved from management to sales; the appellate narrative describes demotion, while respondent disputes that characterization. O'Connor's employment ended.

The employer asserted slow responses to problem accounts and failure to deal promptly with food deliveries in unrefrigerated trucks, followed by concerns about handling the newly enlarged territory. O'Connor submitted evidence of a “commendable minus” 1989 review, a large bonus, comparative reviews/salary placement, improvement after taking over the troubled region, and a July memorandum describing redistribution as evening out volume. Whether these matters establish satisfactory current performance or undermine the employer's explanation is disputed in the papers; no new factfinding is recorded here.

The reported remarks by supervisor Williams include “young blood” two days before discharge in response to another employee approaching 50; “too damn old for this kind of work” in the preceding weeks, with O'Connor unable to recall the broader conversation; and a golf-related age comment. The appellate majority found no sufficient nexus to discharge. The postjudgment Philip Dennis affidavit reports Williams saying after the termination that they were all getting old, Jim was getting old, and others were approaching 50. It was offered after the summary-judgment record had closed. The district court found it insufficient to change the outcome and insufficient diligence in obtaining it; its reasons include counsel's knowledge of Dennis, decision not to depose, hypothetical concern about candor while employed and cost concerns.

The district court granted summary judgment July 27, 1993. It discussed the fourth element of a replacement prima-facie formulation, an alternative reduction-in-force/age-neutral-treatment formulation, and direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary proof. The appellate majority affirmed, reasoning that the consolidation was not a typical reduction-in-force case because O'Connor identified Finnell as his replacement. It applied the outside-protected-class requirement, then separately analyzed the alternative reduction-in-force performance/selection elements and ordinary discrimination proof. It also upheld the discovery ruling and denial of postjudgment relief. The opinion recognizes other circuits' rejection of the under-40 requirement; the runtime's “no material conflict” description does not accurately describe that source.

Judge Butzner concurred in following binding circuit precedent on the replacement's age while criticizing that rule's legal/policy basis. He disagreed with the treatment of the age remarks and Dennis affidavit and would have set aside summary judgment. This is a lower-court separate writing, not a position of any Supreme Court Justice.
