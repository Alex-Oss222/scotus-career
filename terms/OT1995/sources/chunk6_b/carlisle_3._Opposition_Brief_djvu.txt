Petitioner contends that the court of appeals erred in 
reversing the order of the district court granting a post-verdict 
judgment of acquittal. 

Petitioner was indicted by a grand jury sitting in the 
United States District Court for the Western District of 
Michigan. He was charged with conspiracy to possess marijuana 
with intent to distribute it, in violation of 21 U.S.C 846. 
Following a jury trial, petitioner was found guilty as charged. 

Petitioner filed a motion for judgment of acquittal on July 
23, 1993, one day beyond the seven-day period allowed for such 
motions under Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(c). The district court denied 
the motion on August 24, 1993. When petitioner appeared for 


sentencing on October 14, 1993, the court on its own motion 


2 
reversed its earlier decision and granted a judgment of 
acquittal. 

In a written opinion issued on October 28, 1993, the 
district court considered the motion for judgment of acquittal as 
if it were timely filed because Rule 29(c) permits an extension 
of the deadline for filing such motions. The court concluded 
that refusal to hear the motion would result in grave injustice, 
because the evidence was insufficient to establish that 
petitioner joined the conspiracy charged. Pet. App. Al1-AS. 

The court of appeals reversed. Pet. App. B1-B4. Relying on 
its decision in United States v. Davis, 992 F.2d 635 (6th Cir.), 
cert. denied, 114 S. Ct. 265 (1993), the court held that the 
district court lacked jurisdiction to grant an untimely motion 
for acquittal under Fed. R. Crim. P. 29. Pet. App. B3-B4. 

Petitioner contends (Pet. 3-6) that a district court has 
jurisdiction under Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(c) to grant an untimely 
motion for a judgment of acquittal. Whatever the merits of 
petitioner’s contention, it is not presently ripe for review by 
this Court. The court of appeals’ decision is not a final 
judgment against petitioner. After imposition of sentence by the 
district court, petitioner can again take an appeal to the court 
of appeals raising any sentencing issues that may be available to 
him and preserving the claim he has raised here. If the court of 
appeals affirms the final judgment against him, petitioner will 
then be able to present his current contention to this Court, 


together with any other claims he may have, in a petition for a 


3 
writ of certiorari. Accordingly, review by this Court of the 
court of appeals’ decision would be premature at this time.* 
It is therefore respectfully submitted that the petition for 


a writ of certiorari should be denied. 


DREW S. DAYS, III 
Solicitor General 


JUNE 1995
