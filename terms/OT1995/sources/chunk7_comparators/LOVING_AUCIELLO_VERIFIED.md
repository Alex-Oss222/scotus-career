## Loving v.United States, No.94-1966, 517U.S.748 (June3,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-748.{pdf,txt}, all1,474 lines pp.748–780, read contiguous1–500,501–1000,1001–1474. Syllabus; Kennedy Court opinion pp.751–774; Stevens concurrence pp.774–775; Scalia concurrence in part/judgment pp.775–777; Thomas concurrence in judgment pp.777–780 including sole substantive asterisk footnote; counsel asterisk note. No omitted writings/notes.

**Exact historical topology:** Judgment affirming CAAF unanimous9. Kennedy Court opinion fully joined by Rehnquist, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (six). O’Connor and Scalia additionally join PartsI,II,III,IV-B,IV-C (eight for those portions); do not join IV's introductory discussion or IV-A. Thomas joins no Court portion, judgment only. Stevens separate joined Souter/Ginsburg/Breyer. Scalia separate joined O’Connor. Thomas separate solo. Every Court portion has majority; no Marks plurality issue.

**Holding components:** (1) Assuming Furman/death-penalty jurisprudence applies to this peacetime domestic military murder, statute alone insufficiently narrows death eligibility because Article118(4) extends felony-murder death eligibility even to nonkiller lacking intent; additional aggravators specifying greater culpability required under Lowenfield/Zant/Enmund. Do not turn assumption into decided universal military Eighth Amendment rule. (2) ArticleI §8 cl14 does not make military punishment rulemaking exclusively nondelegable to President: English history and constitutional structure permit Congress to entrust measured implementation to Commander in Chief; no absolute rule barring delegated details of criminal punishment when Congress defines offense/regulatory violation and fixes statutory penalty (Grimaud,Touby). (3) Congress actually delegated through UCMJ Articles18,56, with36 providing additional indication: court-martial punishments remain within presidential limits and Code authorization; past practice and1985 Article106a recognition of Article36 regulatory aggravators confirm authority. Articles18,36,56 together clearly authorize RCM1004. Article36 standing alone expressly undecided. Enactment in1950 before Furman did not cancel valid delegation once narrowing changed from discretionary leniency to constitutional necessity. (4) No additional explicit aggravator-selection intelligible principle required in this delegation: fixed statutory boundaries plus President's independent Commander-in-Chief duties and continuing supervision of courts-martial suffice. Different newly created actor or nontraditional executive sphere might require greater guidance (Mistretta distinguished,Mazurie applied). Court does **not** decide inherent presidential authority to prescribe factors without delegation. RCM1004 within delegated authority; death sentence lawful against these challenges and judgment affirmed.

**Material details/limits:** RCM requires unanimous capital guilt, at least one aggravating factor, and finding admissible aggravation substantially outweighs mitigation/extenuation; broad mitigation evidence and consideration required. Historical tribunal found three factors: premeditated murder during robbery; defendant triggerman in felony murder; additional murder proved at same trial. These are record findings, not separately reviewed constitutional validity of every listed aggravator. Tribunal jurisdiction/service connection was not challenged. No independently sufficient majority alternative bypassing delegation; Article18/56 and additional36 combine as statutory authorization, not separate new constitutional holdings.

**Separate writings:** Stevens+Souter+Ginsburg+Breyer: victims' active-duty/retired-service circumstances establish adequate service connection here; Solorio noncapital holding does not resolve whether service connection constitutionally required for military death prosecutions; expressly open substantial question. Scalia+O’Connor: textual allocation and broad Commander-in-Chief role suffice; English governmental history irrelevant to allocation unlike historical inquiry into military jurisdiction; legislative power never lawfully delegated, but Congress may assign implementation responsibilities until excessive/unconstrained assignment amounts to unconstitutional delegation. Thomas: military Eighth Amendment aggravator necessity unestablished and doubtful, though assumes it to resolve case; if not required, added protection would defeat complaint. Approves scheme under unusually high military deference to Congress/President, takes no position on nonmilitary delegation/separation law, and rejects majority's speculative English-history inference. Do not treat Thomas's contingent observation as Court alternative holding.

## Auciello Iron Works,Inc.v.NLRB, No.95-668,517U.S.781 (June3,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-781.{pdf,txt}, all525 lines,pp.781–792; syllabus, complete Souter opinion, all six numbered footnotes including n2 continuation across784–785 and counsel note. Unanimous9; no separate writings. First Circuit affirmed, enforcing Board order requiring contract reduced to formal written instrument.

**Holding:** Board reasonably finds §§8(a)(1),(5) ULP when employer repudiates formed collective-bargaining contract on good-faith doubt of union majority based on information known before acceptance; contract-formation bright line protects stable bargaining and prevents employer keeping favorable deal while holding unilateral repudiation option. Court validates Board policy as rational/Act-consistent, not an inexorable statutory command independent of delegated policy choice. No independently sufficient alternative merits holding.

**Essential qualifications:** Contract formation accepted for decision under Board's criteria, which need not match general contract law. Actual lack of majority at formation is distinct from reasonable good-faith doubt, requires greater substantiation, and was not pleaded to Board, addressed by it, or within cert question; Court declines to decide invalid-ab-initio contention (n2). Certification presumption ordinarily one year; contract presumption term up to three years. “Conclusive” presumption may yield in unusual circumstances such as union dissolution, none present here (n3). Postexpiration presumption rebuttable by actual nonmajority or objective good-faith doubt under then-current Board rule. If evidence causing doubt arises **at same time as acceptance**, Board agreed case-by-case examination (n6); do not erase this qualification with blanket preexisting/instantaneous knowledge rule. Withdrawal before acceptance followed by election petition, refusal to bargain subject to ULP adjudication, or further good-faith bargaining during investigation was available; Court **assumes without deciding** offer withdrawal under these circumstances could not itself supply ULP/blocking-charge basis (n5). It does not immunize every withdrawal or authorize unilateral abandonment during contract term.

**Precedent treatment:** Fall River/Burns/Brooks support stability-presumptions and distrust of employer claiming to vindicate employee freedom; employees retain own decertification route subject to applicable Board regime. Curtin Matheson addresses striker-replacement inference, not timing, so its apparently similar chronology is no holding against bright line (n4). Garment Workers disallows mistaken-good-faith recognition of newly organized uncertified nonmajority union, distinct from established certified union with continuing presumption and opportunity for employee decertification. Both rules limit employer leverage; neither requires repudiation here.
