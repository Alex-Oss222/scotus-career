## Richards v.Jefferson County, No.95-386,517U.S.793 (June10,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-793.{pdf,txt}, all585 lines,pp.793–805; complete syllabus and Stevens Court opinion pp.794–805 including all six numbered footnotes/counsel note. Unanimous9, no separate writings. Reversed **and remanded** to Alabama SC for consistent proceedings (runtime's bare reversed is incomplete).

**Rule/application:** Due process forbids binding strangers to Bedingfield judgment where they had neither notice nor sufficient representation, thus cannot bar their federal constitutional challenge to direct levy of personal funds. Identical interests alone inadequate; earlier individuals neither sued as class representatives, asserted nonparty claims, nor obtained judgment purporting to bind nonparties. Birmingham finance director—even if assumed to represent city's taxpayers—did not represent these county taxpayers. Fiscal reliance/bond pledges cannot create preclusive rights over strangers; ordinary reliance on legal precedent remains different from binding them to prior judgment.

**Limits and reservations:** State procedural autonomy remains subject to constitutional extreme-application limit. Legitimate privity (guardian/trustee), adequate representative suits, control of prior litigation, and due-process-consistent special remedial bankruptcy/probate schemes can bind nonparties. Assumes without deciding some class representation could cure absence of notice; this informal action fails minimum safeguards in any event. Conversely notice alone may not suffice; strangers ordinarily have no duty to intervene(n5). No imposition of all federal Rule23 requirements on States. Whether officials always adequately represent subjects' personal rights left open(n6). Court assumes wide state latitude over taxpayer complaints about misuse of public funds/indirect public harms; distinguishes direct taking of taxpayer's money. Additional support: State did not confine this type of claim to public representative action, and personal chose in action itself is protected property(Logan). That observation reinforces same due-process result, not a separate merits tax holding.

**Scope/remedy:** Equal-protection tax-merits cert question was dismissed; only due-process preclusion question remained(n3). Rehearing application preserved this federal challenge. Federal employee class claims not before Court(n2). No determination tax substantively unconstitutional, money owed, or final entitlement to relief. Hansberry/Postal Telegraph/Wilks supply nonparty limits; Mullane/Shutts support notice and representation; Brinkerhoff-Faris supplies meaningful opportunity to challenge tax; no prior case overruled.

## Whren v.United States,No.95-5841,517U.S.806 (June10,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-806.{pdf,txt}, all620 lines,pp.806–819; syllabus, complete Scalia opinion pp.808–819, both numbered footnotes defining inventory/admin searches and counsel note. Unanimous9, no separate writings. Affirmed convictions/suppression denial.

**Holding/application:** Ordinary temporary traffic detention supported by probable cause to believe traffic violation occurred is reasonable despite subjective ulterior investigatory purpose; no further showing reasonable officer would actually make stop to enforce that violation. PC here accepted by petitioners and found below; Court did not independently resolve contested traffic facts. Officers approached stopped car, saw drugs in plain view; stop lawful and discovered evidence admissible on this challenge.

**All material grounds and limits:** Actual motive irrelevant to ordinary PC Fourth Amendment analysis(Robinson,Gustafson,Scott,Villamonte-Marquez). Proposed empirical normal-practice proxy rejected for same substantive reason, plus impracticability and unconstitutional variability by place/time/uniform/vehicle, not merely because subjective proof is hard. Noncompliance with department rule restricting plainclothes traffic enforcement does not supply constitutional suppression under that theory. Distinguishes inventory/admin searches premised on noninvestigatory purpose and exemption from PC: Wells/Bertine/Burger still require genuine qualifying purpose; does not erase purpose inquiries everywhere. Bannister's no-pretext footnote not contrary holding; Abel's regular-procedure recital not rule making deviation unconstitutional. Equal Protection Clause separately forbids intentionally discriminatory enforcement including race; no equal-protection claim's merits/remedy determined.

**Balancing component:** No new ad hoc interest balance for routine PC stop by plainclothes officers/unmarked vehicle; PC ordinarily determines reasonableness. Preserves extraordinary-manner exceptions unusually harmful to privacy/physical interests: deadly force(Garner), unannounced home entry(Wilson), warrantless home entry(Welsh), bodily penetration(Winston). Suspicionless traffic/random/checkpoint cases(Prouse,Martinez-Fuerte,Brignoni-Ponce) need separate balance because no PC. Traffic-code breadth supplies no manageable reason to nullify PC or choose which valid offenses merit enforcement. Does not declare all PC searches constitutional regardless warrant/manner limits, or all subjectively motivated seizures lawful independent of objective basis.

## Degen v.United States,No.95-173,517U.S.820 (June10,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-820.{pdf,txt}, all427 lines,pp.820–829; complete syllabus, Kennedy opinion pp.821–829, counsel asterisk note; no numbered substantive footnotes or separate writings. Unanimous9; reversed **and remanded** for consistent proceedings(runtime bare reversed incomplete).

**Holding:** Inherent power does not justify striking all claimant's filings/entering forfeiture judgment solely because claimant abroad avoids related criminal prosecution. Inherent power must respond reasonably to actual necessity and exercise restraint; secure in-rem jurisdiction means enforceable merits judgment remains possible despite absence. Criminal-appellate fugitive-disentitlement cases retained and distinguished; Ortega-Rodriguez connection/necessity reasoning applied, not expansion of blanket fugitive sanction.

**Material alternatives/limits:** Civil discovery asymmetry could prejudice prosecution, and indefinite civil stay while criminal prosecution cannot begin is not adequate solution. Court may instead issue Rule26(c) protective orders; manage proof or, in extreme case, permissible theories to prevent abuse of asymmetry; enforce legitimate pleading/discovery/evidence orders with ordinary sanctions including dismissal under Rules37/41(b) or inherent authority when appropriate. Particular measures left to district court, not mandated as fixed checklist or automatic sanction. Limitations/retroactivity defenses not decided; some could require little discovery. Government must meet forfeiture standards, claimant refute showing or lose on merits.

**Other asserted bases independently rejected:** Dignity and deterrence of flight are substantial interests but categorical disentitlement arbitrary/excessively blunt and can diminish respect for judgments. No holding that those interests always immaterial. Court expressly leaves open whether Degen counts as fugitive in every debated sense and whether disentitlement enacted under proper authority would violate due process. McVeigh/Hovey illustrate constitutional concern; actual holding rests on limited inherent authority, not definitive constitutional invalidation of every properly authorized rule.

## Exxon Co.,U.S.A.v.Sofec,Inc.,No.95-129,517U.S.830 (June10,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-830.{pdf,txt}, all579 lines,pp.830–842; complete syllabus and Thomas opinion pp.832–842, all three numbered footnotes/counsel note. Unanimous9, no separate writings; affirmed.

**Distinct holdings/components:** (1) Admiralty comparative fault under Reliable Transfer leaves proximate-cause/superseding-cause requirement intact. Liability apportioned only among proximate causes; later independent unforeseeable superseding cause can cut off negligent cause-in-fact's liability. Plaintiff whose extraordinary negligence is superseding and sole proximate cause cannot recover part of loss from earlier factual causes. Reliable Transfer displaced equal-divided damages, did not address/remove proximate cause. (2) Contract/warranty damages likewise require legal causation; sole-proximate-cause finding defeats recovery from respondents whose breaches only causes in fact. Italia Societa concerned workmanlike-service breach through defective equipment and did not decide causation damages limit; distinguish it. East River and contract foreseeability principles support contractual limit; no categorical holding contract foreseeability always stricter. (3) Concurrent lower findings left undisturbed absent obvious/exceptional error under Graver Tank; tension acknowledged but no sufficient showing here after lengthy trial. **Expressly does not necessarily ratify lower courts' particular application** of causation principles. (4) Rejects legal proposition that superseding cause can never be bifurcated because all parties' fault must always be compared; preserves causation-before-apportionment logic. Particular abuse-of-discretion/due-process bifurcation objection outside cert questions and **declined**, not affirmatively adjudicated as proper.

**Record/qualifications:** PhaseI limited postbreakout proximate cause, reserving causes of initial breakout; no finding respondents wholly blameless or initial defects unproved. Lower courts found safe position by1830 and extraordinary independent failure to plot fixes despite time/manpower and final unforeseeable shoreward turn. Footnote3 acknowledges tension with hose/crane collapse at1944 injuring crewman but relies on express independent-navigation findings; do not suppress that unresolved tension or transform navigational factual deference into broad substantive emergency rule. Footnote2 declines choice between comparing culpability alone and culpability plus causal contribution, assuming meaningful distinction exists. Independent legal components encompass tort and warranty; no merits fallback based merely on plaintiff's contributory negligence.
