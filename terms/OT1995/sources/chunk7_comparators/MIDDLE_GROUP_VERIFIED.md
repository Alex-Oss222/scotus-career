## Ornelas v. United States, No.95-5257, 517 U.S.690 (May28,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-690.{pdf,txt}; all 724 lines, pp.690–705: syllabus; Rehnquist Court opinion pp.691–700, all four numbered footnotes; Scalia dissent pp.700–705 in full.

**Lineup/remedy:** 8–1; Rehnquist joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; Scalia dissent alone. Vacated both Court of Appeals judgments (16F.3d714 and52F.3d328), remanded for that court's de novo review of district court's ultimate reasonable-suspicion/probable-cause determinations. Court did not itself decide either ultimate merits determination.

**Rules and qualifications:** Ultimate reasonable suspicion supporting investigatory stop and probable cause supporting warrantless search are mixed law/fact questions reviewed de novo. Historical facts reviewed only for clear error; due weight to resident judge/local officer inferences grounded in local context and expertise, including trial judge's credibility finding and reasonable inference. No categorical de novo review of all search decisions: deferential warrant-issuance review retained, reinforcing warrant preference. PC/RS are fluid commonsense context-dependent standards, not fine proof thresholds. Independent review promotes uniformity and appellate control of legal rules despite largely unique factual mosaics. Brinegar and Alabama v.White illustrate independent review; Miller v.Fenton explains law-development role; Gates warrant deference distinguished rather than overruled.

**No independent alternative holding:** Magistrate's inevitable-discovery rationale was not the Supreme Court basis: district judge did not reject it, but Government did not defend it on appeal and circuit deemed waived (n.2). Consent search of interior was factually established; Court assumes only for this decision the circuit rule requiring PC before dismantling panels (n.1); it does not adopt that consent limit. Government conceded an investigatory stop, and supported de novo review at SC; appointed amicus defended judgment (n.4). Footnote3 distinguishes term clear error for fact review from preferable abuse of discretion label for proposed deferential mixed-question review.

**Scalia:** Would affirm deferential review because factbound expertise outweighs small law-clarifying gains and duplicate review burdens resources; labeling a question mixed is not enough (Salve Regina, Cooter&Gell, Duberstein, Pierce). Exclusion issue concerns deterrence rather than innocence, so lesser appellate review does not reduce deterrence; Leon already incentivizes warrants. Criticizes due-weight qualification as incompatible with genuine de novo assessment. No other separate writing.

## Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., No.95-244, 517 U.S.706 (June3,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-706.{pdf,txt}; all1,300 lines, pp.706–734, read in contiguous bounded ranges1–650 and651–1300. Syllabus; O’Connor Court opinion pp.709–731; Scalia concurrence pp.731–732; Kennedy concurrence pp.733–734. No omitted numbered substantive footnotes (report includes counsel asterisk note).

**Lineup/remedy:** Unanimous O’Connor opinion; all eight other Justices join in full. Separate Scalia and Kennedy concurrences each solo. Ninth Circuit judgment affirmed on different grounds; district court's abstention remand impermissible in this damages posture. Report pp.706,710 describes Ninth Circuit as sending case to arbitration; runtime flags conflict with actual lower judgment47F.3d350,356 (vacate/remand for proceedings consistent). This receipt verifies report's words, does not turn that recitation into a new Supreme Court arbitration order or independently verify lower judgment.

**Separate holdings/components:** (1) §1447(d) does not block review because only remands on §1447(c)'s lack of subject-matter jurisdiction/removal-procedure grounds are shielded; Burford ground falls outside those. (2) §1291 permits appeal: Moses H.Cone's effectively-out-of-court/federal-jurisdiction-surrender analysis applies with even greater force to remand; also order conclusively resolves important federalism/comity issue separate from merits and cannot be reviewed through later federal final judgment, satisfying collateral-order doctrine. Preserve both routes discussed, while Court recognizes remand is not traditional Catlin finality. Thermtron's contrary no-appeal/mandamus-only statement disavowed to extent inconsistent with later Moses H.Cone, not its §1447 reading. (3) Abstention dismissal/remand available only when relief equitable or otherwise discretionary; damages action may be stayed where appropriate but not outright remanded/dismissed under those principles. Ninth Circuit's per se equity-only rule too rigid, since discretionary declaratory relief also qualifies and stays in damages actions are possible.

**Burford limits and reservations:** Extraordinary narrow exception, evaluating substantial federal interests against paramount state interests in difficult state-law policy issues transcending case or preventing disruption of coherent state policy of substantial concern. Presence of complex administration or potential conflict alone insufficient (NOPSI). FAA motion supplies substantial federal interest; setoff issue pending when district court acted has since been resolved by California SC. Commissioner had apparently conceded relief not equitable/discretionary despite general declaration originally requested. Court does not decide propriety of stay, whether exceptional Burford circumstances otherwise exist, or additional authority under Fair Assessment. Fair Assessment treated primarily as scope of §1983 cause of action; alternatively limited tax context where damages predicate requires declaration invalidating tax scheme. Forum non conveniens has distinct pedigree/factors and can allow damages dismissal to foreign alternative forum; not overruled by abstention restriction. Thibodaux stay distinguished from County of Allegheny dismissal.

**Scalia:** Full join, expressly rejects Kennedy's claimed reservation for damages dismissal to avert extraordinary federalism affront; binding nondiscretionary jurisdiction enacted by Congress cannot itself be that affront, and categorical rule made further exceptional-circumstances inquiry unnecessary. **Kennedy:** Full join, emphasizes independent federalism/comity foundations beyond equity and State's insurance-regulation interest (including McCarran-Ferguson); stay ordinarily preferred, but would leave open damages dismissal where otherwise unavoidable serious federalism affront. These reservations do not fracture unanimous Court opinion and are not controlling exceptions.

## Smiley v. Citibank(South Dakota),N.A., No.95-860, 517 U.S.735 (June3,1996)

**Full-read receipt:** USREPORTS-517-735.{pdf,txt}; all590 lines, pp.735–747; syllabus, Scalia Court opinion pp.737–747, all three numbered footnotes and counsel notes. No separate writings. Unanimous; California Supreme Court affirmed.

**Holding:** Comptroller reasonably interprets §85 National Bank Act interest to include late-payment fees, permitting national bank to charge home-state-permitted fees to out-of-state customers despite customer's state prohibition under Marquette. Chevron/NationsBank deference to reasonable authoritative construction of ambiguous banking statute controls; not declaration that inclusion is uniquely best reading.

**Every material supporting/alternative ground:** Regulation can merit deference despite issuance100+years after statute and impetus from litigation; this is formal Comptroller notice-and-comment regulation, unlike unsupported litigating position excluded by Bowen. Distinguishing charges compensating extension/availability/default from reimbursement specifically allocated to loan-processing/insurance/appraisal costs is rational. Contrary past interpretation is not automatically invalid: unexplained change or disregarded legitimate reliance may be arbitrary/capricious, but lawful change is possible. Independently, no official prior agency position changed here:1964 informal letter/1988 deputy-counsel case-specific opinion doubtful as binding policy and contradictory to each other. Definitions of interest/rate at enactment do not require variation by time or amount; flat fees can be interest. Penalty purpose does not categorically exclude interest: Meilink's narrower textual juxtaposition distinguished, Donnell §85 overdraft precedent supports inclusion.

**Reservations:** Court assumes without deciding that whether a statute preempts must always receive de novo judicial decision; here Marquette already establishes §85 preemption, and agency construes substantive term, not a preemption clause. Footnote3 rejects belated retroactivity objection in this record because no clear prior agency interpretation governed antecedent transactions; expressly acknowledges potential substance if regulation replaced prior interpretation. Does not resolve constitutional permissibility of leaving definition of interest to bank's home-state law: regulation supplies federal definition, so issue not implicated. Regulation's listed fee categories are quoted as its text, not separate litigation holdings about each fee.
