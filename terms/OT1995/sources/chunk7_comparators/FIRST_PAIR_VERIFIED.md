## Henderson v. United States, No. 95-232, 517 U.S. 654 (May 20, 1996)

**Full-read receipt:** official GovInfo United States Reports PDF `USREPORTS-517-654.pdf`, downloaded from https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-517/pdf/USREPORTS-517-654.pdf; complete `pdftotext -layout` transcription `USREPORTS-517-654.txt`, 1,250 lines, pp.654–680. Read syllabus pp.654–655; Ginsburg Court opinion pp.656–672 including all 25 footnotes; Scalia concurrence pp.672–673; Thomas dissent pp.673–680 including all four footnotes. Text retrieval initially truncated the middle; subsequent bounded reads covered the complete omitted middle and footnote transitions. No writing omitted.

**Historical lineup and remedy:** 6–3; Ginsburg for Court joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer. Scalia concurrence joined by Kennedy, both also joining Court in full. Thomas dissent joined by Rehnquist and O’Connor. Reversed Fifth Circuit dismissal; remanded for proceedings consistent with opinion. No merits personal-injury adjudication and no independently sufficient alternative disposition.

**Verified holdings and material qualifications:** Rule 4's extendable 120-day allowance irreconcilably conflicts with SAA §2/46 U.S.C. App. §742 service “forthwith”; it is an irreducible allowance, not an outer limit subject to shortening by §742. The forthwith direction is a nonjurisdictional procedural instruction, not a condition of this immunity waiver, and is displaced. Rule 4 governs summons/service in whole, not merely method apart from timing. Holding's general formulation expressly concerns actions arising under federal law commenced within applicable limitations; footnote 2 preserves state-law commencement requirements for state-created claims under Walker/Ragan, while distinguishing Hanna's method-of-service rule. No broad holding that Congress cannot condition immunity on procedure: Scalia/Kennedy expressly preserve that power and read this scheme as imposing no such condition.

**Critical statutory mechanics:** The operative 1993 filing was governed by former Rule 4(j), directly enacted by Congress in 1982, rather than a Court-promulgated rule under §2072. At pp.668–669 the Court expressly addresses that point: a congressionally enacted Rule supersedes conflicting law no less than one prescribed by the Court, and §2072(b) supplies evidence of legislative intent. Do not describe direct application of §2072(b) alone as the whole rationale. Former 4(j) required good cause for extension; then-current 4(m) allowed discretionary extension without good cause and required extension where good cause shown (n.5, n.10). Rule 4(i)(3)'s reasonable cure time where one required federal officer was timely served also supports integration of timing and method. No finding of service compliance needing new adjudication: Government did not contest Rule 4 compliance, notice or defense prejudice; 47 days to Attorney General, 148 days to U.S. Attorney under allowed extension. Timely filing, not service before limitations, rendered this federal claim timely (n.6). Public Vessels Act distinction immaterial because both claims proceed under SAA rules (n.1). Footnote 18 discusses lower-court relation-back remedy and refiling possibility as context, not an independent Supreme Court holding.

**Separate writings:** Scalia/Kennedy: Congress may make strict compliance with procedural provisions a jurisdictional condition of sovereign-immunity waiver, but this legislative scheme does not. Thomas/Rehnquist/O’Connor: §2's placement outside §3 general procedural assimilation makes service forthwith a waiver condition; at minimum statutory ambiguity must favor the sovereign. Rules cannot alter immunity/jurisdiction under §2072(b)/Rule82. Alternatively criticizes treating Congress-enacted 4(j) as an Enabling Act rule or finding implied repeal against the SAA's particular service requirement. 148 days plainly fails “forthwith” even without precise definition. Dissent preserves distinction between ordinary procedural rules in suits against government and Congress's power to attach specific procedural conditions; acknowledges its own reading would also make registered-mail method jurisdictional (n.4).

**Precedent / reservations:** West supplies filing commencement for federal rights; Walker/Ragan preserve state commencement limits; Hanna distinguished. Sherwood (eligible claimant/issues), Nordic Village (claims), Shaw (interest relief), Williams/Block (limitations) distinguished from auxiliary service processing. Mullane supplies adequate notice function. No blanket repudiation of sovereign immunity or statutory limitations. Historical comparator handoff should preserve full topology and congressional-rule qualification.

## Doctor’s Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto, No.95-559, 517 U.S.681 (May 20, 1996)

**Full-read receipt:** official GovInfo PDF `USREPORTS-517-681.pdf`, downloaded from https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-517/pdf/USREPORTS-517-681.pdf; complete `USREPORTS-517-681.txt`, 392 lines, pp.681–689. Read complete syllabus; Ginsburg Court opinion pp.682–689 and all three numbered footnotes (including n.3 continued on p.688); Thomas dissent p.689. No writing omitted.

**Historical lineup and remedy:** 8–1; Ginsburg for Court joined by Rehnquist, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer. Thomas alone dissents. Judgment reversed **and remanded** for further proceedings not inconsistent with opinion; runtime's bare “Reversed” is incomplete. No independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Verified rule and limits:** FAA §2 displaces Montana §27-5-114(4)'s requirement that notice of arbitration appear in underlined capital letters on first contract page, because that enforceability condition applies specifically and solely to arbitration clauses, rather than contracts generally. Displacement extends to arbitration agreements covered by FAA; trial court's unchallenged FAA coverage and clause coverage of all claims are posture, not newly decided holdings. Generally applicable fraud, duress, unconscionability remain available. Court may not use arbitration's uniqueness to disguise arbitration-specific unconscionability. Southland/Perry supply state-court applicability/equal footing; Allied-Bruce reiterates equal treatment; Scherk equal footing. Volt distinguished: parties there incorporated state rules coordinating order of proceedings; applying that rule enforced the agreed terms and did not invalidate the arbitration clause.

**Footnote3 scope reservation:** Respondents urged general informed consent/reasonable expectations for unexpected adhesion terms as an alternative basis. Court reviews only Montana's actual arbitration-specific statutory disposition: state court announced no such generally applicable rule. It neither decided that those general defenses defeated this clause nor barred genuine generally applicable defenses. The hypothetical general contract ground is not an independent alternative holding.

**Thomas dissent:** FAA §2 does not apply to state-court proceedings for reasons in his Allied-Bruce dissent; accordingly would dissent from reversal. No other separate writing or partial joins.
