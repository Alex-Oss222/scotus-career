## Wood v. Bartholomew — objective source differences and record

Sources: [Ninth Circuit, 34 F.3d 870](https://static.case.law/f3d/34/html/0870-01.html), local `A_Wood_lower.txt`; [Archive record item](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0583), local `A_micro_IA40385013_0583 2. Petition for Writ of Certiorari_djvu.txt` and `3. Opposition Brief_djvu.txt`, with matching PDFs. A second Archive copy is item `micro_IA40386013_0381`.

- The district court **denied** habeas on April 12, 1993 after an evidentiary hearing; the Ninth Circuit **reversed**, with instructions for a conditional writ requiring a trial on premeditation within a reasonable time or reduction to simple first-degree murder. The original packet's statement that the Ninth Circuit affirmed habeas relief does not match these documents. The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment is September 6, 1994, No. 93-35549; rehearing was denied November 16, 1994. Petition Appendix B contains the district opinion; Opposition Appendix A contains rehearing order.
- Opposition Appendix B reproduces a district judgment and alternative writ entered January 13, 1995 pursuant to the appellate mandate, requiring the State to retry premeditation within a reasonable period or reduce the degree of conviction. This is a lower-court act, not a new Supreme Court action. No subsequent compliance event is established in the materials reviewed here.
- The guilt-phase dispute concerned premeditation and the degree of murder, not identity as robber or shooter. Dwayne admitted the robbery and shots; he claimed accidental discharge and denied saying he would leave no witnesses. Aggravated first-degree murder exposed him to death or life without parole; simple first-degree felony murder allowed life with parole. After a penalty retrial he was serving life without parole. Source: Ninth Circuit pp. 871–73; Petition pp. 2–7.
- Rodney's polygraph questions concerned helping the robbery and being in the laundromat with Dwayne. Negative answers produced an examiner finding of deception. Tracy's questions concerned helping the robbery and holding the gun. **The sources differ in formulation:** Petition p. 4 says her answers were found truthful; the Ninth Circuit p. 872 and Opposition p. 8 n.5 say the test was inconclusive, while the examiner's personal impression was that she was truthful. Neither of Tracy's questions addressed Rodney's help. Treat technical result and personal impression separately.
- Rodney and Tracy testified that Dwayne intended to leave no witnesses. The Ninth Circuit called theirs the only direct premeditation testimony at the guilt phase. Stanley Bell's testimony was at penalty only. The gun's single-action mechanism was circumstantial evidence, disputed by the accidental-discharge defense. Source: Ninth Circuit pp. 871–75. Petition pp. 4–6 argues the mechanism required separate intentional cocking and firing and therefore supplied evidence beyond the two witnesses. Opposition p. 6 n.4 reports that the gun was disassembled when seized and reassembled before laboratory testing, citing H.Tr. 39–40 and trial transcript 472; that asserted record fact is briefing-supported here, not independently verified from the full underlying hearing transcript.
- The Ninth Circuit did not determine final admissibility of the tests. It described Washington's general rule excluding them even for impeachment absent stipulation, citing State v. Ellison, 36 Wash. App. 564, 676 P.2d 531, 535 (1984), and rested its rule on possible admissible fruits. It identified depositions and prior inconsistent statements, including different later accounts of whether Rodney returned for another examination. Source: pp. 875–76. Tests were admitted at the later capital penalty retrial under a separate Washington sentencing rule; this does not establish their guilt-phase admissibility.
- The State's Petition pp. 7, 18–21 reports that trial counsel was already aware Rodney might lie and testified that disclosure would not have changed his investigation, strategy, or cross-examination; it argues the later five-page examination elicited no helpful new fact. The full five-page Rodney hearing transcript appears in Petition Appendix C (H.Tr. 84–88). Rodney said there were several tests, he returned after an initial examination while affected by drugs, he did not remember being told results, never spoke to trial counsel before trial, and maintained his trial testimony was true.
- Opposition pp. 9–10 reports prosecutor Johnson deliberately withheld the result, relying on inadmissibility; counsel Anderson's already-existing attack on Rodney as a participant; expert Murdach's opinions about polygraph reliability and charging/plea use; and conflicts in recollection about when defense counsel learned of tests. These are the opposition's descriptions of hearing testimony. In particular, an expert witness's asserted reliability percentage is not a judicially established scientific fact.
- The Ninth Circuit did not reach ineffective assistance or the separate presentation-of-allegedly-false-testimony claim. Opposition pp. 16–20 asks to sustain the judgment on the latter alternative under Napue/Giglio if review is granted; it also advocates constitutionally required admission. Failed polygraph results are the premise of that argument, not an established finding of perjury. Petition p. 16 n.3 raises Teague; Petition p. 19 n.5 relies on historical Brecht, which cannot replace the actual simulated rule.

Record limitation: The complete December 22, 1992 hearing transcript and original guilt trial are not reproduced as complete standalone items in the downloaded Wood index. Appendix C is only Rodney's five-page testimony. Full-item absence has not been established by complete review of both duplicate scans; no assertion is made that other quoted testimony cannot be obtained. The exact counsel testimony should be verified in that transcript before resting a disposition on a stronger concession than the brief reports. No post-mandate state compliance fact is supplied.

## Doe v. Taylor Independent School District — source record

Sources: [Fifth Circuit en banc, 15 F.3d 443](https://static.case.law/f3d/15/html/0443-01.html), local `A_Doe_lower.txt` (full reporter opinion including separate writings); cross-check [OpenJurist text](https://openjurist.org/15/f3d/443). The public opinion's main writing and factual account were read; the lengthy separate writings are preserved but not fully reviewed in this preflight source pass. No Supreme Court disposition is contained in this digest.

- District court denied qualified immunity to principal Eddy Lankford and superintendent Mike Caplinger; the en banc court affirmed as to Lankford and reversed as to Caplinger. The appeal was interlocutory. Its reported facts resolve disputes for Doe at summary judgment, not after a liability trial. Source: pp. 445–50, 456–59 and nn.1,11,13.
- Lankford had warnings before Caplinger arrived: fall 1985 reports of favoritism, suggestive teaching, hugging/grabbing students and excessive familiarity; spring 1986 counselor and parent reports; May 1986 librarian report of conduct she described as molestation in a darkened copy room. Lankford downplayed it and documented no complaints. Caplinger began July 1986 and was not informed of this history. Source: pp. 446–47.
- Doe entered as a freshman August 1986. Stroud wrote suggestive notes, excused work while awarding grades, and used school relationships and premises. Kissing/petting began late 1986; intimate touching continued in January 1987; first intercourse was late March or early April 1987. Source: pp. 447–48; footnote 4 describes the asserted nexus between official teaching/coaching authority and abuse.
- In January 1987 Lankford heard of a concert outing and classroom favoritism; main factual narrative says he notified Caplinger of possible problems. In early February Caplinger learned of inappropriate horseplay at a basketball game and directed Lankford to speak to Stroud. The later application discussion p. 457 calls the basketball report Caplinger's first notice. Preserve this internal chronology tension; do not silently state he had no January notice.
- In February a student brought Lankford an unsigned Valentine in handwriting he acknowledged looked like Stroud's, coupled with suspicions of a sexual relationship. He did not retain a copy, investigate, or inform Caplinger/parents; he transferred the reporting student, not Doe, out of Stroud's class. Source: pp. 448, 457.
- In June Caplinger received reports about the Corn Festival and notes; he contacted another girl's parent, who said that girl had not attended. In July Doe's parents brought photographs with messages. Caplinger questioned Doe, who denied a sexual relationship; he and Lankford warned Stroud to keep away and warned of firing. A promised broader meeting did not occur. Abuse resumed when school resumed, ending after disclosure in October; Caplinger immediately suspended Stroud when the attorney reported abuse and another parent reported a separate touching. Source: pp. 448–49, 457–58.
- Main lower writing's test: learned facts/pattern pointing plainly to sexual abuse; deliberately indifferent failure to take obviously necessary action; causal constitutional injury. It distinguishes intentional personal responsibility from vicarious liability. Its notice discussion relies on Shillingford (1981), Wanger (1980), Bowen (1982), Languirand (1983), Hinshaw (1986), Jefferson and Lopez (both 1987). Exact pre-May conduct must not receive notice retroactively from Jefferson/Lopez. Main opinion also relies on City of Canton (1989) in formulating current fault/causation, which postdates these defendants' conduct.
- The equal-protection theories included sexual abuse as harassment, classroom favoritism as harassment, and gender discrimination. The lower court declined to decide them because Doe did not identify additional damages or a different basis for Caplinger's liability. Source: Part IV, p. 458. No Title IX question is presented by the assigned scope.
- Separate lower writings are in the full text: Higginbotham concurrence begins at character 62696; Garwood dissent at 90948; Jones dissent at 141304. These should be read before the fresh validator certifies the strongest counterarguments. They are lower-court material, not a Supreme Court comparator.

Record limits: no assigned Supreme Court docket or argument date is supplied; none is inferred. No missing lower trial findings are invented. Internet Archive collection search for Doe + Taylor returned no matching case, but a caption-variant search for individual supervisors was not completed; the search is not a declaration that the collection lacks an item.

## Hodge v. Jones — objective corrections and alternatives in the lower writing

Sources: [Fourth Circuit, 31 F.3d 157](https://static.case.law/f3d/31/html/0157-01.html), local `A_Hodge_lower.txt`, read in full including Powell concurrence and every footnote; cross-check [OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/31/f3d/157); [Archive petition and appendix](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386012_0613), downloaded as matching PDF and OCR (appendix not fully read in this pass).

- January 20, 1989 suspected fracture led to mandatory report; investigation started next day and found no abuse. A later specialist diagnosed osteomyelitis. The worker filed the report January 27, 1989; the mother later supplied the diagnosis; father wrote February 16; Katz replied in March with a redacted report stating ruled out/unsubstantiated. The communications requesting full disclosure and expunction ran February 1989–May 1990. Source: pp. 160–62.
- Classification transition: MDHR adopted new categories January 27 but they took effect February 6, 1989, changing four categories to two. The worker apparently used both old ruled-out and new unsubstantiated terminology. The later 1993 statutory definitions quoted in footnote 1 are distinct: ruled out means no abuse occurred; unsubstantiated means insufficient evidence for indicated or ruled-out. Do not silently equate these terms for every period.
- Footnote 3 reports the then-applicable five-year expunction command for unsubstantiated reports **if no further incidents involving the same alleged perpetrator were reported**. It cites Md. Fam. Law §5-707(b), 1984 replacement/1987 supplement, and 1987 Md. Laws ch.635 §2. It describes the 1991 change requiring ruled-out reports to be expunged within 120 days and expressly notes no applicable retroactivity provision. January 1994 was the five-year date here. The lower court says plaintiffs did not contend required expunction then failed; it treated prospective relief as moot while damages and fees remained live. This is not an independently retrieved destruction certificate.
- The CPS report was accurately cleared, but **the AMF computer coding was erroneous for a period**: footnote 9 states that codes described indicated sexual abuse from January 23, 1989 until January 21, 1992. The court found no amended complaint adding that inaccuracy as a basis for recovery. The original packet's accurate-clearance phrase must not erase this fact or preservation issue.
- The AMF is a coded record of all Maryland citizens receiving social services, not solely an abuse list. General public access was barred. Statutorily limited recipients included officials performing specified functions, medical caregivers, and parents/custodians or alleged abusers under stated conditions. The opinion did not find public publication, child removal, interference with decisions, loss of a benefit or present loss of employment. It mentions feared effects on the parents' security clearances, not an established revocation. Source: pp. 161, 165–66.
- District court granted interlocutory summary judgment for plaintiffs on liability and rejected immunity; its AMF-as-central-registry construction under §5-715 is a **district determination**, not an uncontested command established in this preflight. Fourth Circuit footnote 4 expressly declined to review that classification. The appellate majority rejected the asserted federal privacy/procedure deprivation and alternatively found no clearly established right. It ordered judgment for defendants. Powell concurred in the judgment **only on immunity**, assuming possible rights and declining to decide the constitutional merits; he agreed prospective relief was moot. Source: pp. 162–69 and n.4.
- Footnote 5 raises Joseph's standing: allegations focused on injury to the parents as alleged abusers; no specific child injury was alleged, and the cited procedural safeguards concerned persons suspected of abuse. It did not decide a separate child claim on invented facts.
- State-created rights: Powell distinguished statutory substantive limits on discretion from mere procedural guarantees, citing Hewitt/Olim. Majority footnote 11 distinguished Valmonte's public/employment barrier and noted that its 1994 date cannot establish earlier notice. The majority's broad references to later cases are lower-court arguments; actual simulated Albright/Elder/Johnson control only their own propositions.

Record limits: exact full §5-715 and historical regulatory text are not authenticated here outside quotations in the reported opinion; Appendix may supply them, but was not fully read. A merits holding construing those provisions beyond the reported limited propositions needs the actual text. The full-read lower opinion supports an immunity-only path without claiming the source text settles substantive entitlement.

## Tuggle v. Netherland — actual briefing and lower judgment

Sources: [Fourth Circuit, 57 F.3d 1356](https://static.case.law/f3d/57/html/1356-01.html), local `A_Tuggle_lower.txt`; bounded `A_Tuggle_lower_Ake_Zant_excerpt.txt` contains Part II.A. Source [Archive 95-6016](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0784): petition, opposition with respondent appendix, and item labeled Reply Brief, matching PDFs/OCR. **The labeled Reply Brief is the Warden's Reply, not petitioner's reply.** It references a petitioner reply that was not separately listed in the downloaded item metadata. Petition and Warden reply were read completely; opposition body reviewed with small OCR gaps and entire appendix not yet read. Whole lower opinion outside the Ake/Zant and related sentencing portions not fully read in this pass.

- Conviction and sentencing occurred in 1984. Jury found both future dangerousness and vileness. The 1985 Virginia remand opinion concluded the independent-psychiatrist denial violated Ake but sustained the death sentence on vileness. In that 1985 proceeding, the Commonwealth had conceded error and advocated resentencing. Source: Petition pp. 9–10; Opposition p. 3 n.1; Fourth Circuit pp. 1362–64.
- **The 1995 opposition does not maintain that concession.** Footnote 1 says earlier error position was mistaken. Pages 6–11 contend Tuggle requested only competency/sanity reevaluation, not sentencing assistance; was already evaluated by a psychiatrist and psychologist; did not make the threshold showing or request a proper expert; requested a doctor of his own choosing; and failed to demonstrate prejudice. It invokes state findings and preservation. These are respondent contentions, not fresh findings by this digest.
- Petitioner disputes that characterization: Petition pp. 3–4 says after learning of Centor's expanded future-danger evaluation, counsel sought independent help and offered personal payment, which was denied. Petitioner describes the examination as outside the referral and without counsel's notice. Opposition pp. 7–9 contests any sentencing purpose and quotes the motion, hearing and state findings. The relevant actual motion/order/hearing excerpts are in respondent Appendix pp. 13–34; a final decision resolving this factual/scope dispute should read those scans, not treat either advocate's paraphrase as settled.
- District court granted relief June 8, 1994; Fourth Circuit June 29, 1995 reversed and directed dismissal. Fourth Circuit Part II.A relies on nonweighing status and a surviving vileness finding, citing Zant/Stringer and distinguishing Johnson v. Mississippi because of weighing. Part II.E treats that conclusion as disposing of the denied-expert and Estelle claims. It does **not** conduct an independent whole-record harmlessness test. Its statement that future dangerousness is no longer part of the case concerns its appellate legal reasoning, not historical absence of that evidence at trial.
- Petition Question 1 expressly includes both Ake and Estelle v. Smith and challenges use of Zant to uphold the sentence. Other questions challenge vileness instructions, voir dire, and rape-evidence sufficiency. Packet's supplied review scope is narrower. Fresh control/neutral validation must identify which question/components are submitted for this event and preserve outside-scope claims without deciding them by implication.
- Petitioner pp. 14–20 distinguishes valid death eligibility from the whole body of evidence informing sentencing choice: psychiatric prosecution evidence entered and defense expert rebuttal/mitigation did not. It invokes Zant pp. 886–87 and n.24, Johnson v. Mississippi, Ake, Estelle, Clemons and Buttrum. Opposition pp. 11–16 invokes Teague, then-current Stringer and Romano, eligibility narrowing, independent vileness and record-based lack of prejudice. Historical post-divergence Supreme Court rules cited in briefs must be replaced by the actual simulated rules, not adopted from the briefs.
- Opposition pp. 16–17 separately claims Fifth Amendment procedural default; disputes the Sixth Amendment claim because counsel requested examination, warnings were given, and a written waiver signed; relies on Buchanan. Petitioner contends counsel lacked notice of the expanded purpose and the district court found ineffective waiver. No merits finding on these disputed claims is supplied here.
- Trial instruction reproduced at 57 F.3d 1371–72: either future dangerousness or vileness had to be proven beyond reasonable doubt; even with one proven, the jury could choose life if death unjustified by all evidence. No proven aggravator required life. Vileness instruction included torture, depravity of mind, or aggravated battery beyond the minimum necessary to accomplish murder. Requested additional Smith definitions were refused. Keep these alternatives distinct.
- Warden's Reply (September 20, 1995 service certificate; apparent later filing stamp) attaches §19.2-169.7 and portions of §19.2-264.3:1(G). It argues the former covers statements concerning the alleged offense and guilt-stage use, with insanity-issue exception; the latter was enacted after the trial. The printed statutory source must be visually verified before quoting OCR. This reply is not proof of the claimant's admission of its construction.

No earlier post-divergence Supreme Court stay or certiorari disposition in the raw briefs is admitted as an in-world action. Raw Tuggle materials contain such historical procedural paragraphs and should not be indiscriminately supplied to a blind modeling context. The sanitized facts above and the bounded lower excerpt carry no such result.
