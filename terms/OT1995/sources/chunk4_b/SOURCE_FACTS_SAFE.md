## Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Hiles — verified record facts

The source petition states the question whether an employee injured while straightening a misaligned drawbar is entitled to judgment as a matter of law under the SAA even without evidence the drawbar was defective. The record is from Illinois, with the appellate judgment reported at 268 Ill. App. 3d 561, 644 N.E.2d 508. The petition invokes 28 U.S.C. §1257(a). It reproduces the December 29, 1994 appellate opinion, April 5, 1995 state leave denial and May 24, 1993 trial judgment. These are lower-court facts, not imported Supreme Court actions.

Read in full: `SAFE_hiles_02._Petition_for_Writ_of_Certiorari.txt`; `SAFE_hiles_JA_lower_court_record.txt` (printed JA 1–25, the full substantive appendix); `SAFE_hiles_07._Petitioners_Brief.txt`; `SAFE_hiles_08._Respondents_Brief.txt`; `SAFE_hiles_09._Reply_Brief.txt`. The original petition's middle portion was separately read to cure display truncation. The safe petitioner's merits copy omits its same-matter Supreme Court jurisdiction metadata paragraph; no argument is omitted by that removal.

Source observations for the validator:

- JA 4–5 reproduces the one-count SAA complaint. No separate negligence claim is pleaded. The railroad's amended answer asserts ordinary misalignment rather than defective equipment; JA 8–9 also contains its other pleaded defenses. This is not an adjudication of those defenses.
- JA 10–11 and 21 contain foreman Miller's affidavit and offer of proof: an inspection less than two hours after the injury found no mechanical defect and compliance with the AAR rules. The trial court excluded the proffer as irrelevant under its statutory theory. Do not recast an excluded proffer as a jury finding.
- JA 13–18 reproduces Fauver's and Hiles's testimony. They were preparing the drawbar for coupling; the other car was four or five car lengths away. Fauver said the drawbar did not move easily. JA 24 says the drawbar was already slued when Fauver uncoupled the car. Counsel additionally offered to prove a curve or slope. Respondent's merits brief at 2 n.2 and 23 n.10 disputes the petitioner's assertion that this uncoupling “must have” occurred on a curve. That asserted specific cause is not an agreed fact.
- JA 22–25 records the legal directed-liability ruling and jury instruction. The jury assessed $492,500 damages; the petition appendix reproduces that judgment. The appellate opinion sustains liability from failure to couple and injury during manual realignment, regardless of mechanical defect.
- Petitioner's merits brief at 19–20 and 24–25 argues for opportunity to present its explanation and asserts absence of actual attempted impact. Reply at 8 expressly says the employee need not prove a mechanical defect as part of a prima facie case, while maintaining the railroad may show ordinary nondefective misalignment as a defense. Reply conclusion requests reversal and remand. These qualifications must accompany any account of the railroad's position.
- Respondent's merits brief distinguishes unopened knuckles, which can be opened from outside, from drawbar alignment, which requires entry between cars. It invokes the statutory performance duty and Affolder, Carter, O'Donnell, Wagner and Parker. At 27 n.13 it agrees an actual prior failed impact should not determine liability; a futile coupling attempt need not precede realignment. It challenges the inference of actual curvature and the legal sufficiency of postinjury inspection evidence.

The injury occurred in 1990. The petition prints then-applicable 45 U.S.C. §2 and the 1994 recodification, 49 U.S.C. §20302(a)(1)(A), and explains the asserted nonsubstantive recodification. Official 1994 Code text was independently retrieved in `hiles_20302_1994.htm`. The safe record preserves the distinction between statutory compliance and ordinary negligence; no fresh construction is supplied here.
