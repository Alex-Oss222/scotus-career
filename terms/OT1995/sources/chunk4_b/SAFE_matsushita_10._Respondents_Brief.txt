QUESTION PRESENTED 

Jurisdiction of claims under the Exchange Act is vested 
exclusively in the district courts. 15 U.S.C. §78aa. SEC rule 
14d-10, the so-called "all-holders, best-price rule” , promulgated under 
Sections 14(d)(7)f and 14(e) of the Act, requires shareholder equality 
“during [a] tender offer." In this class action proceeding, the Ninth 
Circuit held, inter alia, that a tax-driven special stock arrangement 
between Matsushita and Lew Wasserman, MCA’s chairman, violated 
the rule, and that a jury could so find with respect to a $21,000,000 
bonus paid to Sidney Sheinberg, MCA’s chief operating officer. 


This federal class action proceeding has been vigorously 
litigated. By contrast, petitioners’ counsel represented to the district 
court that a pending Delaware class action raising only state law 
claims was "not being prosecuted in any way” after the state court 
had rejected a settlement as “illusionary", characterized the state law 
claims as “at best, extremely weak,” and had asserted that the federal 
claims alone had value. No state class had been certified, and no 
litigation activity occurred for eighteen months in Delaware. After 
respondents’ merits brief had been filed in the Ninth Circuit, 
petitioners announced another Delaware settlement. In it, the federal 
claims of non-opt-outs purportedly were released, although Delaware 
law states that it is ultimately for the federal courts to determine the 
effect of the release on Exchange Act claims. The Delaware court 
approved the settlement without making any inquiry into the 
representatives’ class status or the adequacy of their representation. 
The court found the class benefits to be "meager", and stated that 
"suspicions abound” of collusion. 


Did the court below err in refusing to give preclusive effect 
to a Delaware settlement and release to the extent that the federal 
claims were factually and legally unrelated to any state law claims?
