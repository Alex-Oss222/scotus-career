## The motion and memorandum

The United States (Solicitor General Drew S. Days, III) and Massachusetts (Attorney General Scott Harshbarger) jointly move for entry of a supplemental decree in the form attached. The memorandum recounts the 1975 decision and decree (420 U.S. 515; 423 U.S. 1), the severed Rhode Island/New York and Massachusetts proceedings, the 1981 Massachusetts supplemental decree (452 U.S. 429), and the February 25, 1986 decision overruling Massachusetts's exception on Nantucket Sound (475 U.S. 89), which directed the parties "to prepare and submit a decree conforming to the recommendations of the Special Master" (475 U.S. at 105). It states that work on the decree stalled because of personnel changes and other government business, that the parties have resumed and completed it, and that the proposed decree describes the coastline near Vineyard and Nantucket Sounds "in accordance with this Court's February 25, 1986, decision, the Special Master's report, and agreements reached between the parties."

## Proposed supplemental decree (verified transcription)

The recital states that by its February 25, 1986 decision the Court adopted the Special Master's recommendation that Vineyard Sound constitutes historic inland waters and overruled Massachusetts's exception insofar as it challenged the Master's determination that the whole of Nantucket Sound does not constitute historic or ancient inland waters.

1. For the purposes of the Court's Decree of October 6, 1975, 423 U.S. 1, the coastline of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts shall be determined on the basis that the whole of Vineyard Sound constitutes state inland waters and Nantucket Sound (with the exception of interior indentations described in paragraphs 2(c), (d) and (e)) is made up of territorial seas and high seas.

2. For purposes of the Decree of October 6, 1975, the coastline of Massachusetts includes the following straight lines:

   (a) A line from a point on Gay Head on Martha's Vineyard (approximately 41°21'10"N, 70°50'07"W) to the southwestern point of Cuttyhunk Island (approximately 41°24'39"N, 70°56'34"W);

   (b) A line from a point on East Chop (approximately 41°28'15"N, 70°34'05"W) to a point on Cape Cod (approximately 41°33'10"N, 70°29'30"W);

   (c) A line from a point southeast of East Chop (approximately 41°27'30"N, 70°33'18"W) to a point west of Cape Pogue (approximately 41°25'06"N, 70°27'56"W) on the island of Martha's Vineyard;

   (d) A line from a point on Point Gammon on Cape Cod (approximately 41°36'36"N, 70°15'40"W) to the southwestern-most point of Monomoy Island (approximately 41°33'02"N, 70°00'59"W); and

   (e) A line from a point on the west coast of Great Island (approximately 41°37'08"N, 70°16'15"W) to a point on Hyannis Point on Cape Cod (approximately 41°37'27"N, 70°17'34"W).

3. The Court retains jurisdiction to entertain such further proceedings, enter such orders, and issue such writs as from time to time may be deemed necessary or advisable to effectuate and supplement the decree and the rights of the respective parties.
