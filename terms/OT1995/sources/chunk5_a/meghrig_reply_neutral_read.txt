I. INTRODUCTION/SUMMARY                          OF ARGUMENT
      It is difficult to know where to begin to respond to the over 150
pages of argument and authorities submitted by Respondent and five
different amici curiae in opposition to Petitioners’ Brief on the Merits.'
Respondent and its supporting amici barely discuss the extensive
Statutory analysis set forth in Petitioners’ Brief on the Merits (which
analysis also formed the backbone of the Petition for Writ of Certiorari
                     ) and also spend little time addressing the
conflicting decisions rendered by the Ninth Circuit and the Eighth
Circuit courts of appeal. Instead, Respondent and its supporting amici
attempt an end run by fashioning an entirely new analytical focus: they
contend that federal district courts have broad, inherent authority to
render equitable relief and that Respondent here seeks equitable restitu-
tion, not damages.
      That analysis is seriously flawed because it proceeds from a series
of false assumptions, the most notable of which include:
       1. A misapprehension of the limited jurisdiction of federal
courts to render equitable relief in the face of a statute such as the
RCRA citizen suit provision here at issue;
      2. A mischaracterization    of this action as an equitable restitution
case, and
      3. A pervasive, but absolutely erroneous, inference that the
citizen suit provision of RCRA is the only remedy available where
solid or hazardous waste presents an imminent and substantial endan-
germent to pullic health or the environment.
      In attempting to carry forward this set of false assumptions,
Respondent and its supporting amici fail to meaningfully address
several critical features of the RCRA citizen suit provision, as well as
established authority of this Court and others:
      1. Federal tourts are courts of limited jurisdiction and do not
have unbridled authority to grant equitable relief;
      2. The full text of the jurisdictional section of the RCRA citizen
suit provision, not the excerpted portion repeatedly quoted by
