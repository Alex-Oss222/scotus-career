## Plan instruments and allocation

J.A.8–26 reproduce excerpts from the 1978 Massey-Ferguson Inc. Employee Benefits Plan (Trial Exhibit156). Company means Massey-Ferguson Inc.; Board means the Board or executive committee acting for it; Committee is the appointed administrative committee. The instrument separately allocates functions. Committee responsibilities include general administration and interpretation (6.1), claim and eligibility interpretation (6.9,6.13), and determinations of employee rights (8.1.2). Its chair is Plan Administrator; 8.1.3 allocates statutory filing, disclosures and claims duties. Trustee investment/distribution duties are separately stated. Board amendment, appointment/removal, contribution and termination powers appear in8.1.1.

Section7.4 reserves amendment/termination by Board action, but forbids diverting funds outside exclusive employee benefit except7.1 and protects claims to which employees had become entitled before the exercise. Section7.1 states loyalty/prudence/plan-compliance terms, plus contribution-return provisions for initial nonqualification, disallowed deductions, loss of qualification, posttermination surplus and mistake of fact. They do not establish any specific disputed transaction was valid. Section8.2 limits each named fiduciary's responsibility to allocated duties except as ERISA otherwise provides. Section8.3 provides indemnification except proved fraud, misfeasance or willful neglect, with possible trust reimbursement as an expense. Section10 requires communication of principal plan terms and inspection access. Section15 permits participation by subsidiary/affiliate resolution and states transfers between participating employers do not interrupt employment or adversely affect eligibility/benefits; it does not establish MCC participation.

Section11 contains claims/review machinery; section12 denies a continuing-employment contract. Section16 includes severability, Iowa governing law, counsel-reliance and a qualified no-recourse provision; existence of these clauses is not their enforceability under ERISA. The 1983 medical-plan summary J.A.39 expressly makes the plan document prevail over discrepancies. J.A.43 separately reserves to Plan Administrator termination, suspension, withdrawal, amendment or modification of any class's coverage. J.A.59 generally terminates coverage when active work ceases, with sickness/injury continuation and leave-of-absence continuation upon required group payment; a distinct one-year disability expense extension appears there. These are documentary terms, not a fresh vesting determination.

## Communications and chronology

J.A.28–30 December1,1983 Ruth memo announces elimination of specified employee contributions effective January1,1984 and new major medical arrangements; J.A.32–34 Quandt pensioner memo explains common active/retiree coverage against financial difficulties. J.A.36–62 supply the December12 memorandum/major-medical summary and substantive benefits conditions. Detailed covered-service limitations are reproduced, but no present payment claim is calculated by this note.

J.A.64–66 April24,1986 internal communications distinguish the employer responsible for termination costs if employees refuse MCC employment, employee questions about severance/retirement/payroll, and perceived conflicts if MCC personnel answer MF questions. J.A.68–73 put MF and MCC benefit summaries side-by-side, including health benefits, pensions and other programs. This is not proof that assets, legal obligor, solvency or future protection were identical.

J.A.75–77 MCC questions/answers say signed acceptance confirms employment without disruption of salary or benefits; benefit programs remain unchanged and there is no seniority/pensionable-service loss. They also state future employment conditions depend on company success and may change, describe MCC as operationally/staffing separate, and state transfer-back uncertainty. Pension provisions discuss assumption of earned pension benefits and transfer/protection of associated pension assets; do not silently apply pension-specific statements to unfunded welfare coverage.

J.A.79–80 Porter video-message transcript describes a new stand-alone corporation, programs/five-year business plan, lender and government support and financial restructuring providing funds necessary for future viability. It couples assurances of unchanged pay/benefits/no seniority loss with statements that future employment conditions depend on success and changes may be made. J.A.82–83 April30,1986 signed-offer letter similarly describes stand-alone status, unchanged programs and bright future while reserving necessary/appropriate future changes; the return deadline is May19 with mailed offers postmarked May16.

J.A.85–87 August1987 Porter-to-Rice note describes his secondment/retention arrangements and anticipated return to Varity, a possible MCC restructure/wind-up/sale, minimizing Varity exposure, and uncertainty from MCC's fragile condition. It is a later internal document, not by itself proof of what any employee heard before transferring. J.A.89–90 November2,1987 Varity letter promises to indemnify Porter if MCC cannot, subject to the quoted lawful indemnity scope; J.A.92 separately requires confidentiality. Neither letter itself establishes a private assurance to transferring employees.
