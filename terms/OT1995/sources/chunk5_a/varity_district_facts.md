# Varity district opinions: source facts and reading receipt

The complete March26,1993 district posttrial order and findings/conclusions, petition appendix24a–115a, have been read in full from the separately extracted archive OCR `varity_lower_district_opinions.txt` (4740lines). The corresponding separate IA petition PDF was downloaded as `varity_ia_02.pdf` (36,630,953bytes), converted to `varity_ia_02_pdf.txt`; dispositive pages114a–115a were cross-checked in that PDF extraction. This source receipt states what the lower court did and found, without endorsing its legal rules or suggesting a Supreme Court disposition. All propositions remain subject to later appellate treatment and the submitted issues.

## Lower procedural record

Posttrial order24a–47a distinguishes advisory equitable jury findings from legal claims. It set aside contract and fraudulent-misrepresentation verdicts, all MCC-Terminated subclass verdicts, and punitive damages. It preserved the §510 protected-rights verdict for MCC-Retired and ten individual plaintiffs, and let those plaintiffs elect the surviving jury monetary remedy or the separate equitable order. Disability claims had been settled before trial (26a n3). Plaintiffs' eventual election is a later procedural fact to obtain from appellate opinion/briefs; this district order itself directs election within20days.

Findings48a–115a identify 83 MCC-Retired class members (transferred from M-F after May9,1986 and retired before March4,1988 receivership),140 MCC-Terminated class members (still working on receivership), and ten individual retirees. Ten-individual status is separate from the83-member class; the district found none authorized benefit-obligation transfer and none worked for MCC except Anita Crowe's one-time postretirement project. There are no pension-benefit claims (51a¶2). Plaintiffs were salaried management/nonunion at-will employees without collective-bargaining/employment contracts (51a¶4).

## Selected findings and their status

The court made findings under Rule52, not merely accepted pleading allegations. ¶¶15–30 (53a–57a) find ProjectSunshine financial restructuring, purpose to reduce corporate debt/benefit costs, MCC initial negative worth and prospects, executive/employee arrangements, and transfer of benefit obligations covering a wider universe of roughly4,000 retirees and1,500 active employees. Those totals do not expand the plaintiffs/classes here. ¶¶37–57 (59a–62a) contain control/shared-services/corporate-separateness findings; the district's alter-ego conclusion must not be converted into a new statutory violation. ¶¶58–70 (62a–65a) find the Porter/McAdams letter packets and meeting arrangements, communications about prospects and benefits, deliberate incomplete/deceptive answers, rejected fuller disclosures, reliance, and unconsented transfers of individual-retiree obligations. ¶68 finds material misleading representations known when made and detrimental reliance; this is an actual trial finding. The higher court/briefs determine what is preserved or challenged.

¶¶71–101 (66a–76a) separately examine YouandM-F SPD, amendment reservations, communications and expectations. The court found plan summaries failed to disclose the claimed amendment/termination right effectively, although1983 communications contained reservation language; ¶98 records MCC adoption of M-F planMay1,1987 after using it earlier. Do not conflate a district conclusion that disclosure was inadequate with a Supreme Court rule vesting benefits. ¶¶102–110 address termination allowance, whose subclass claims the court ultimately rejected. ¶¶111–114 (78a–79a) and fiduciary analysis95a–96a discuss M-F/Varity control and administration and more than mere corporate identity.

## Legal grounds below and limits of the receipt

District fiduciary discussion89a–100a acknowledges dual corporate/fiduciary roles, quotes §3(21)(A)'s function-specific trigger, identifies alleged communication/administration and disclosure breaches, relies on §404 and §502(a)(3), and contrasts individual relief with §409/502(a)(2) relief to a plan. Its own broader compensatory-damages reasoning predates Mertens; it is a historical lower-court ground, not current governing law. The district lists multiple breach particulars96a: deficient SPD, material transfer misrepresentations/omissions, favoring other groups, nonpayment under plan, and invalid unilateral assignment of individual-retiree rights. Which grounds survive or are contested is a question for appellate/merits sources.

The estoppel discussion100a–112a is a distinct alternative below, with asserted reliance/disclosure rules. It declined to reach promissory estoppel after recognizing equitable estoppel. Its statement denying any amendment/termination right must be read with appellate clarification, not used alone as the reviewed remedy. Contract lifetime-vesting claims had been dismissed32a–36a. Fraud count was rejected as preempted44a–45a. Punitive awards were set aside113a, not surviving relief.

## Exact district remedy and monetary discrepancy check

District114a–115a offered $696,195 for MCC-Retired past losses, itemized past amounts for the ten individuals, reinstatement in the plan as of retirement, retained jurisdiction for terms and intervening unpaid benefits, and denied all MCC-Terminated claims. The ten itemized figures at79a–80a and115a are $5,000,3,160,7,661,13,106,11,877,1,700,8,219,3,150,18,284,10,655. Their arithmetic sum is **$82,812**. This independently confirms the $82,812 figure despite a later appellate passage printed $81,812. The actual appellate clarification controls reinstatement scope; the district's original date-of-retirement language is not the final appellate remedy.
