The order cites 28 U.S.C. §2072 as its rulemaking authority. Paragraph 2 provides that the amendments “shall take effect on December 1, 1995, and shall govern all proceedings in criminal cases thereafter commenced and, insofar as just and practicable, all proceedings in criminal cases then pending.” That is a qualified pending-case clause, not universal retroactivity to completed 1993 conduct.

## Exact pertinent text effective December 1, 1995

Rule 57(a)(2):

> A local rule imposing a requirement of form shall not be enforced in a manner that causes a party to lose rights because of a nonwillful failure to comply with the requirement.

Rule 57(b), “Procedure When There Is No Controlling Law”:

> A judge may regulate practice in any manner consistent with federal law, these rules, and local rules of the district. No sanction or other disadvantage may be imposed for noncompliance with any requirement not in federal law, federal rules, or the local district rules unless the alleged violator has been furnished in the particular case with actual notice of the requirement.

Section (a)(1) requires a local rule to be consistent with, but not duplicative of, Acts of Congress and rules adopted under §2072. Section (c) governs the effective date, publication, and notification of local rules.

The 1995 advisory note confines (a)(2) to nonwillful violations of **local** form requirements. The note describes (b) as flexibility “when there is no controlling law,” with consistency required, and explains the actual-notice protection for internal directives not found in federal or local published rules. These notes illuminate the amendment; they do not themselves enlarge its text.

## Carlisle temporal and dependency limits

Carlisle’s missed Rule 29 deadline and district rulings occurred in 1993. The trial-era Rule 57 excerpt in the clean lower-record extraction must be identified as that version. The amended Rule’s effective date precedes the assigned April 29, 1996 event, but its qualified pending-case transition must not silently become authority to excuse earlier conduct. Neither (a)(2)’s local-form protection nor (b)’s uncodified-directive notice protection automatically covers a deadline expressly stated in national Rules 29 and 45. Those propositions leave the disputed construction and independent constitutional arguments to merits analysis.
