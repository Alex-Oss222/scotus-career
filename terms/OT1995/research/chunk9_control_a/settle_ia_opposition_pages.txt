SUMMARY OF THE ARGUMENT 
I. 
This case presents no issue 
appropriate for the Court’s exercise of 
its discretionary jurisdiction. 
The 
actions of the respondent teacher were 
"reasonably related to legitimate 
pedagogical concerns," and therefore 
there is no first amendment isuue 
presented in this case. 
II. 
The viewpoint discrimination 
argument advanced by Petitioner is 
inapposite in this case because this case 
involved a research and writing 
assignment within a secondary school 
curriculum, 
supervised by faculty, 
and 
designed to impart particular research 
and writing skills, which is a scenario 
properly viewed under this Court’s 
decision in 
istri 
, 484 U.S. 
260, 
108 S. Ct. 
562, 
98 L. Ed. 2d 592 (1988).

REASONS FOR DENYING THE WRIT 
The Petitioner fails to satisfy 
criteria traditionally applied by the 
Court in determining whether to grant a 
Writ of Certiorari. 
The court below 
properly held that this Court’s decisio 
in 
h 
i=, 
484 U.S. 
260, 
108 S. Ct. 562, 98 L. Rd.

and that there were no disputed 
issues of material fact as to whether the 
actions of the respondent teacher were 
“reasonably related to a legitimate 
pedagogical concern." 
I. 
THE SIXTH CIRCUIT PROPERLY 
VIEWED THIS CASE UNDER THIS COURT’S 
HOLDING IN HAZELWOOD. 
This case is about the right of a 
teacher to control her classroom, 
and 
whether the federal courts should be 
called upon each time a student does not 
get his or her way. 
Looking properly at 
the issue in this case, 
it is clear that 
this Court’s holding in Hazelwood School 
District 
v. Kuhlmeir, 
484 U.S. 260, 108 
S. Ct. 562, 98 L. Ed. 2d 592 (1988) 
supplies the rule of decision, 
and that 
there is no conflict among the circuits 
or among this Court’s decisions, 
as 
suggested by Petitioner. 
In Hazelwood this Court made it 
abundantly clear that educators have far 
greater control over student expression 
when it occurs within activities which 
11 


=== PDF PAGE 21 ===
"may fairly be characterized as 
part of the school curriculum, whether or 
not they occur in a traditional classroom 
setting, 
so long as they are supervised 
by faculty members and designed to impart 
particular knowledge or skills to student 
participants and audiences." Hazelwood 
484 U.S. at 271, 
108 S. Ct. at 570, 98 L. 
Ed. 2d at 605. 
Without question, 
the research paper 
assignment at issue in this case was 
within the school curriculum, 
was in the 
classroom setting, was supervised by 
faculty and was designed to teach 
particular research and writing skills. 
Likewise, 
there are no disputed material 
fact issues as to whether the respondent 
teacher’s actions were "reasonably 
related to legitimate pedagogical 
concerns," a requirement also articulated 
by this Court in Hazelwood. 
As found by the District Court, 
and 
as affirmed by the Sixth Circuit, 
the 
respondent teacher’s refusal to allow the

blatant refusal by the Petitioner to 
conform to the procedural deadlines was 
"reasonably related to a legitimate 
pedagogical concern." 
Furthermore, 
the 
lack of resources 
in the Dickson County 
Junior High School library, 
the student’s 
familiarity with the subject and the ease 
by which it could be written, 
as well as 
avoiding potential misunderstandings that 
may arise from classroom criticisms of 
grammar, 
organization, 
style or content 
of the paper were all "legitimate 
pedagogical concerns" which supported the 
teacher’s actions. 
Simply put, 
there is
