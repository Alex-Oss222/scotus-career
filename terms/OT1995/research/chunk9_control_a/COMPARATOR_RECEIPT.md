## Material reconciliation distinctions

Lane: The Court required an unequivocal textual waiver extending specifically to monetary relief. It distinguished ?504?s executive-program coverage from ?505(a)(2)?s recipient/federal-provider remedial language; compared the express federal-employment provisions and the 1991 Act; rejected both the general-remedies inference and reliance on ?1003?s state-remedy comparison. Its ?1003 alternatives need not themselves be wholly satisfactory to defeat an unequivocal waiver. Stevens and Breyer treated the addition of federal programs to the existing remedial structure as carrying the established remedy; ?505 codified rather than curtailed that structure, and the history and equalization provision supported damages. The dispute is remedy and waiver, not an affirmance of the underlying discriminatory exclusion. All fifteen dissent footnotes were read.

CF&I: The eight-Justice tax conclusion uses the exaction?s operation, not the Internal Revenue Code?s label, to distinguish revenue from punishment; ?4971 punishes failure to make separately required pension contributions. Part II-D?s legislative-history rationale lacks Scalia?s join. Thomas accepts congressional tax characterization for a federal exaction and distinguishes precedent concerning state-law labels. Part III rejects categorical judicial reordering under ?510(c), applying Noland to both between-class and within-class reorderings; it does not decide the proposed ?1122(a) or ??1129(a)(7)/726(a)(4) alternative defenses. Those alternatives and the Class 12 fallback must remain available on remand. Only the allowed 1989 ?4971(a) claim is at issue.

Brown: The Court applies the exemption to joint implementation of the last best good-faith wage offer after impasse, on the lawful-bargaining assumptions of this case. Its expressed limits are conduct during and immediately after negotiations, directly related to their lawful operation, addressing a mandatory bargaining subject, and concerning only bargaining parties. It leaves open sufficiently remote conduct after bargaining has ended. The Court rejects consent-only, automatic-impasse-cutoff, and tactics-versus-terms rules. Stevens objects to protecting an employer-created wage restraint that newly displaces individual salary negotiation; he distinguishes union-driven agreements and emphasizes Pennington?s narrower rationale over the Goldberg separate writing in Jewel Tea. He does not make all league restraints automatically unlawful: antitrust justification remains a distinct inquiry. His six footnotes, including the actual prior-contract and bargaining-record qualifications, were read.
