# Additional operative statutory reading — Medtronic preparation

Preparation only; no Justice commitment, new Court law or frozen June 26 model. The date-specific baseline remains required.

The authorized official 1994 U.S. Code extracts from the complete govinfo Title 21 text have now supplied bounded complete reads of:

- **21 U.S.C. §360(k):** a registrant proposing interstate commercial introduction must report at least ninety days beforehand, in the prescribed form, the device's classification or the determination it is unclassified and the basis, and action taken to comply with applicable §§360d/360e requirements. This is a reporting requirement, not an enacted declaration of substantive safety approval.
- **§360c(a):** all three classes and the complete safety/effectiveness framework, including the difference between general and special controls and class III premarket approval; intended users, labeled conditions and probable benefit/risk; well-controlled investigations and the specified valid-scientific-evidence alternative.
- **§360c(f)–(i):** initial class III classification and its stated equivalent-device/reclassification alternatives, complete petition/reclassification provisions, the particular adverse-data certification, requested classification information, definitions, and the full substantial-equivalence provision. Section (i) distinguishes same intended use plus same technological characteristics from the alternative showing for different technological characteristics, retains the no-different-safety/effectiveness-questions condition, and bars reliance on a predicate removed at the Secretary's initiative or judicially found adulterated/misbranded. Its disclosure provisions remain distinct. The 1990 codification is not silently made the verbatim law of the 1982 clearance; the approved lower-court account expressly treats that historical practice premise as an assumption.
- **§360e(a)–(d):** the exact class III approval trigger, including its referenced investigational exemption; the rulemaking mechanism for pre-1976 and substantially equivalent devices; the complete application requirements, panel referral provisions and qualifications; and the approval/denial rules and their enumerated safety, effectiveness, manufacturing, labeling and performance-standard grounds. The time-extension, conditioned-sale and review provisions read with these subsections are not collapsed into an unconditional 180-day guarantee. The sample-submission exception and discretionary/mandatory panel-referral distinction remain intact.

These complete operative subsections support comparison between clearance, generally applicable controls and substantive approval without treating all class III devices as having undergone PMA. No device-specific performance standard, PMA order, failure to comply, or causal breach is inferred. The entire larger sections and every historical amendment note have not been read and are not represented as fully reviewed.

The same official full-title URL was also retrieved directly to prepare `USCODE1994_21_360j_MODEL_PREP.txt` and `USCODE1994_21_371_MODEL_PREP.txt`. These are statutory text only. The complete **§360j(f)** and **§371(a)** have now been read. Section 360j(f) authorizes prescribed GMP regulations, including pre-production design validation assessing performance but excluding evaluation of the device's safety or effectiveness. It retains advisory-committee and oral-hearing conditions, the separate exemption/variance petition showings, decision periods, conditions of a granted variance and postorder informal hearing. These are not §360k(b)'s state-requirement exemption. Section 371(a) vests efficient-enforcement rulemaking authority in the Secretary except as otherwise provided in that section; it is not a textual delegation of unlimited free-standing preemption power. No particular granted exemption or variance is established here. The full statutory files are preserved, but only these specified operative portions are represented as read.

For the distinction between a state parallel duty and a private action directly enforcing the FDCA, the same official source also supplied the complete **§337**, preserved in `USCODE1994_21_337_MODEL_PREP.txt` and read with its effective-date notes. Subsection (a) reserves proceedings to enforce or restrain violations of the chapter to the United States except as (b) provides. Subsection (b)'s State civil-enforcement exception concerns enumerated food provisions, location, advance notice, ongoing federal action and settlement; it is not a medical-device exception or a private-plaintiff cause of action. This text identifies an enforcement boundary. It does not by itself decide whether a genuinely independent state tort duty is expressly preempted, prove Florida recognizes the alleged duty, or import a later Supreme Court implied-preemption holding. No new §337 claim or defense is presumed presented.
