## Competing legal paths

**A. Bounded vacatur or reversal of summary judgment.** Apply substantial deference to curricular choices but require an actual legitimate pedagogical relation. The reasons and comparisons, taken favorably to the student, can support an inference that the asserted research restrictions concealed rejection of her religious perspective. Hazelwood does not convert a possible educational justification into conclusive actual causation. The lower court's approval of every stated reason and categorical treatment of religious-content concerns could therefore be legal error; material factual issues would remain for ordinary proceedings.

The strongest objection is that this imports a private-forum rule into a graded assignment. The answer must be narrow: a teacher retains authority over topic, genre, method, sources and academic quality, and a student has no unilateral right to select any topic. The asserted defect is exclusion of otherwise qualifying work because of its religious perspective, or reliance on a supposed legal ban on academic religious content, rather than a genuine curricular choice. The rule must not forbid all viewpoint choices inherent in teaching a defined curriculum or demand strict scrutiny for every grading judgment.

**B. Affirm under curricular topic control.** Hazelwood recognizes instruction's independent educational purposes; the teacher may require approval, new research, appropriate source methods and an academic rather than testimonial product. Tinker does not create an unrestricted right to choose the subject of required coursework, and the opened-access holdings do not make each graded assignment a forum. The narrower lower concurrence offers this route without endorsing every explanation.

Its strongest objection is that topic control itself can be used to exclude religion and that the recorded reasons create a motive dispute. Affirmance requires an adequate account of why the actual restriction remains legitimately curricular on the favorable record, or an actually established independently sufficient ground. Merely asserting that one lawful reason could have existed, accepting contested explanations as found facts, or declaring all religious topics unprotected would not answer that objection.

**C. Further legal clarification with ordinary remand.** The Court could reject the lower court's excessive deference without finding the record necessarily sufficient for trial on every element. Such a disposition must identify what legal ruling is erroneous and which remaining matters the lower courts may assess. It cannot guarantee a grade, damages, trial, new evidence or immunity denial. Personal immunity, municipal attribution, causation, available remedies and any otherwise valid remaining defenses retain their separate requirements if actually presented. No finding establishes a teacher's personal religious animus; the prohibited basis can be the decision's religious-viewpoint ground without personal hostility.
