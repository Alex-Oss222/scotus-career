# Lewis v. Casey: bounded submissions and procedural source additions

No. 94-1511; source cutoff June 24, 1996. These are positions in historical submissions and an appellate procedural recital, not findings of injury, waiver, default, or conclusions about the Court's jurisdiction. They supplement the existing neutral distinction among standing, merits proof and remedial scope. No judicial outcome or reasoning from the same-case Supreme Court decision is included.

## Petitioners' expressly stated standing position

Petitioners' merits brief, printed p.33 n.23, states:

> Although the lack of a showing of injury means that Respondents are not entitled to any relief, the State does not contend that the Respondents lacked standing to raise these claims in the first instance. Respondents clearly met the threshold of an actual case or controversy pursuant to Article III of the United States Constitution. They simply failed to prove the existence of a constitutional violation, including causation of injury, that would entitle them to relief.

The quotation is authenticated by the official published judicial quotation of the brief and corroborated by Respondents' Brief p.29, which cites p.33 n.23 and describes the State as conceding initial standing. The recovered petitioners' brief OCR becomes badly degraded in printed pp.30-35; the absence of a searchable word in that OCR is not source absence. The original brief is preserved in the Internet Archive docket item `micro_IA40385013_0589`; the local text is `Casey_IA_07. Petitioners Brief.txt`.

This is a litigant's distinction between initial standing and proof entitling it to relief. It is not a concession of constitutional liability, classwide injury or a proper injunction; nor does a party's position resolve nonwaivable jurisdiction.

## Respondents' preservation position and a supported alternative

Respondents' merits brief, pp.25-30, argues that a general actual-injury issue was not properly raised below, distinguishes core access challenges from photocopying, telephone and typewriter claims, and separately argues that threatened as well as completed injury can support prospective relief. The brief invokes the lower court's identified dismissal/inability-to-file findings as an additional reason not to treat this record as a clean absence-of-injury case. These remain advocacy, not independently established preservation rulings.

At printed p.25, the brief states:

> It is possible that an efficient paging system, with provision to prisoners of an inventory of the law library's contents and prompt delivery of requested materials, would pass constitutional muster for those prisoners who are able to use a law library on their own.

The brief immediately maintains that the actual restrictive ADOC paging system was inadequate. Thus the alternative is conditional on inventory, prompt delivery and prisoners capable of using a library independently; it is not agreement that the actual system sufficed for every inmate or that physical library entry is always required. Source: `Casey_IA_08. Respondents Brief.txt`, pp.25-30, recovered directly from the same Internet Archive item.

## Appellate mediation recital

The Ninth Circuit's reported opinion, 43 F.3d 1261, footnote 1, recites that submission was deferred for thirty days after oral argument to permit settlement, but defendants declined mediation before that process began, so the court returned the appeal to active status. This is a specific appellate procedural event, not a general finding of bad faith, waiver or forfeiture of the State's preserved objections. Source: `Casey_lower_43_1261.txt`.

The merits citation is 518 U.S. 343. Cornell's older banner uses 516 U.S. 804; the official bound report confirms the corrected merits citation. The source correction supplies no merits proposition.
