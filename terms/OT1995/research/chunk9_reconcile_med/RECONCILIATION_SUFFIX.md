### Stevens — independent support and historical test

His actual Cipollone analysis identifies the liability duty and statutory boundary claim by claim. His Sweet Home and full Smiley joins support reasonable authorized interpretation after the Court determines the statutory boundary. Those premises support the frozen design, parallel and generic-regulation application commitments. Historical Parts III and V confirm them; Parts IV and VI show that rejection of wholesale displacement coexists with reservation of the categorical issue.

The strongest contrary argument is that an enforceable GMP or warning instruction already supplies the federal requirement, so a jury's additional conduct rule is preempted. Stevens agrees these are real federal obligations but concludes that enforceability and shared subject matter do not establish the requisite specific federal counterpart here. The alternative that every tort duty is exempt goes farther than his necessary conclusion; it remains reserved. He will join a bounded opinion retaining the actual comparison and regulatory support; he will not join automatic preemption from manufacturing/labeling overlap or findings of Florida entitlement. No changed historical premise warrants a different judgment.

### O'Connor — independent support and historical test

Her actual Cipollone join establishes the regulatory force of common-law warning duties. Her Easterwood and Freightliner positions require an operative federal predicate; this record supplies enforceable manufacturing and warning commands, unlike the absence of a substantive design requirement. Her Sweet Home and Smiley positions allow deference inside statutory boundaries, not against a clear enacted command. Her historical separate opinion at pp. 509–514 makes the last distinction decisive and confirms the component dispositions.

The strongest contrary reading relies on §808.1(d)'s specificity and general-applicability terms. Her answer is first statutory: Congress said applicable requirements, without an additional specificity limitation. Even a compatible reading of the regulation must account for court decisions and divergent substantive applications; it cannot erase a real different/additional duty simply because the generally framed federal instruction governs many devices. Conversely, preempting equal duties merely because damages add an incentive fails the statutory distinction. She withholds assent from both sweeping nonpreemption and total count dismissal where a genuinely parallel theory remains. Applicable exceptions, the actual conduct duty and Florida predicates remain to be determined without an invented compliance finding.
