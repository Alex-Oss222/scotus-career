## Competing paths and their strongest objections

**A — Vacate the challenged appellate dismissal for a claim-specific inquiry under the old meaningful-opportunity rule.** The actual complaint and supported lower findings concern alleged impairment of the defense's ability to meet materially different evidence at capital sentencing. Gardner's core cannot be satisfied solely because the information eventually appeared in open court and counsel could ask questions. The operative rule is no fixed disclosure interval: a defendant must have a meaningful opportunity to deny or explain material capital sentencing information, assessed against the supported preparation circumstances and any misleading assurance. Actual Graham requires testing that existing function rather than treating every application involving delay as new. The strongest objection is that Gardner concerned wholly secret information and Weatherford denies a discovery entitlement. A answers by requiring demonstrated impairment beyond surprise; it does not create general pretrial disclosure, guarantee an exclusion remedy, infer a continuance request, or establish deception as fact. The present appellate reasoning does not properly perform that narrower inquiry.

**B — Preserve the new-rule bar to the ordinary advance-notice theory, but separately remand the materially different deception/preservation issues.** Gardner does not dictate a right to earlier notice of evidence disclosed before the hearing; extending its secret-information rationale to preparation time was at least reasonably open in1987. Neither substantive nor watershed exception permits that newly formulated discovery rule. The strongest objection is that the complaint and lower findings cannot fairly be reduced to a request for more discovery. B addresses that objection by withholding any global dismissal: alleged misleading assurances, their presentation, their actual effect and any distinct Brady theory receive their own analysis. That remand does not concede the old-rule status or merits of a new substantive theory, and does not revive a finally disposed claim without a lawful basis.

**C — Affirm all habeas dismissal on an independent bar, or reinstate the entire writ now.** Either terminal alternative needs more than the supplied support. The first requires a claim-specific preserved procedural ground or constitutional rejection, not Hall's displaced hearing rule or an assumed appellate omission. The second requires proper availability, review of actual findings, the precise violation and applicable error-effect/materiality analysis. The uncertain original amendment/footnote scope and the actual competing legal grounds preclude treating either terminal result as presently established. A bounded appellate remand can resolve those legal tasks on the litigation record without an operator-driven production order or fabricated response.
