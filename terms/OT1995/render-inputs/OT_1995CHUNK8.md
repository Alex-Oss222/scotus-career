## Event

**Lockheed Corp. v. Spink, 517 U.S. 882, No. 95-809 — June 10, 1996.** Argued April 22, 1996, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 60 F.3d 616. The questions concern fiduciary status and prohibited transactions under an amended pension plan offering enhanced retirement benefits for employment-related releases, and OBRA's application to earlier uncredited service.

Spink returned to Lockheed in 1979 at age 61 and was then lawfully excluded from its plan. He entered on December 25, 1988, the first day of the relevant plan year. He declined a 1990 enhanced-retirement offer because he would not release his claims. The complaint distinguishes sponsor adoption from Retirement Committee implementation; its allegations are accepted at the dismissal stage, not found after trial. The Ninth Circuit reinstated Counts I–III while leaving independent loyalty and anti-inurement grounds unaddressed and awarding appellate fees.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The reviewed adverse portions of the judgment are reversed and the case is remanded. The appellate fee award is vacated for reconsideration.

## Holdings

### I. Plan design and plan administration are different functions

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** An employer acts as a plan sponsor, rather than an ERISA fiduciary, when designing and adopting the pension-plan amendments at issue. Subject to section 408's statutory exceptions, section 406(a)(1) requires a fiduciary to cause a transaction that the fiduciary knows or should know is of a prohibited kind; employer party-in-interest status alone does not satisfy that requirement, and actual implementation must be examined as a separate function.

**Controlling explanation:** Curtiss-Wright recognizes the sponsor's plan-amendment role. Section 3(21) attaches fiduciary status to the functions performed, not to every employer act affecting future expenditures. Pension funding and vesting requirements do not transform design itself into fiduciary administration. Varity requires a separate inquiry when the employer actually performs an administrative function; John Hancock likewise separates assets, control and fiduciary duties. Thus sponsor status cannot dispose of the distinct allegation that the Committee controlled plan distributions. The asset-disposition branch does not acquire an extra discretion requirement. The holding leaves amendment procedures and ERISA's other substantive commands in force, and does not establish authorized adoption or lawful implementation merely from amendment power.

### II. Employer benefit from the release does not alone prohibit the payment

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the seven identified in Opinion Topology.

**Operative rule:** Payment of the described genuine enhanced retirement benefits under an otherwise lawful plan is not the transaction prohibited by section 406(a)(1)(D) merely because the required employment-related releases benefit the employer. This rule assumes fiduciary implementation for the legal inquiry and does not immunize a sham asset transfer, kickback, independently unlawful plan or unenforceable release.

**Controlling explanation:** The prohibited-transaction provision addresses dealings with parties in interest of a different character from these plan benefits. Employers obtain recruitment, retention and retirement advantages from ordinary benefit programs; that advantage alone cannot turn payment to participants into prohibited diversion. The release has value, but Keystone's exchange of property to discharge an enforceable funding obligation is a distinct transaction. Surplus does not become employer property, and the Court creates no section 408 exemption or general net-benefit defense.

The rule rejects the asserted per se section 406 theory on the accepted allegations. Independent participation, funding, vesting, loyalty and anti-inurement requirements remain. Section 406(b)'s different prohibitions are not merged into the presented question. Whether a particular waiver is enforceable under the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act or other law is undecided. No sham or kickback is alleged in the issue decided; no finding of loss, deception or hidden diversion is made.

### III. OBRA does not require the claimed credit for earlier lawful exclusion

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the eight Associate Justices. The Chief Justice agrees with the disposition only.

**Operative rule:** OBRA sections 9201 and 9202 do not require pension credit for Spink's service during his earlier lawful exclusion from participation. Section 9204(a)(1)'s express direction applies their new accrual protections to plan years beginning on or after January 1, 1988 and employees with an hour of service in a covered year; a smaller total benefit from earlier uncredited service is not itself a reduced accrual rate during a covered year.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf requires the Court first to apply Congress's enacted temporal direction. That direction resolves this claim. Applying the new protection to a participant in a covered plan year does not convert all prior lawful nonparticipation into credited service. The later date of benefit calculation and the statute's general antidiscrimination purpose supply no contrary command.

Section 9204(b) separately applies section 9203's participation amendments to covered plan years and service performed on or after January 1, 1988. Its additional service-date language is not inserted into subsection (a). The provisions perform distinct work. The plan year began on December 25, 1988; this ruling establishes no independent violation during the preceding part of that calendar year.

For a plan maintained under one or more collective-bargaining agreements between employee representatives and employers ratified before March 1, 1986, section 9204(a)(2) applies subsection (a)(1) to benefits pursuant to, and individuals covered by, those agreements using a substituted date: the commencement of the first plan year beginning on or after the earlier of January 1, 1990 or the later of January 1, 1988 and termination of the last qualifying agreement, disregarding extensions after February 28, 1986. No allegation invokes that transition. Section 9204(c) permits delayed formal amendment until the first plan year beginning on or after January 1, 1989 only if interim operation complies and the amendment covers that interval retroactively. A pension plan is not treated as failing to provide definitely determinable benefits or contributions, or to operate according to its provisions, merely because it operates under that subsection. This does not postpone substantive compliance. No general rule forbidding every use of a past fact, or new default presumption of temporal reach, is adopted.

### IV. Independent claims and fees follow their own grounds

**Authority:** Part IV-A has nine joins; Part IV-B has eight Associate joins and the Chief Justice's agreement in the disposition.

**Operative directions and explanation:** The sponsor-capacity holding forecloses section 404 liability resting solely on design or adoption. Actually preserved independent fiduciary implementation and section 403(c)(1) anti-inurement grounds remain for ordinary lower-court consideration; no new allegation or claim is supplied. Varity, Mertens and Peacock preserve the distinct statutory predicate and remedy requirements rather than guaranteeing relief. The appellate fee award is vacated because its stated grounds included the substantive conclusions rejected here; reconsideration must rest on the remaining litigation and applicable fee law.

## Event

**United States v. International Business Machines Corp., 517 U.S. 843, No. 95-591 — June 10, 1996.** Argued March 18, 1996, on certiorari to the Federal Circuit. The question is whether the Export Clause permits application of the general foreign-insurance premium tax in 26 U.S.C. §4371 to insurance of goods in export transit.

For 1975–1984, IBM paid assessments concerning foreign policies covering its exported products and sought refunds. The Federal Circuit, 59 F.3d 1234, affirmed the Court of Federal Claims' ruling that this application was unconstitutional. The policies attach to identified shipments; their premiums reflect both cargo value and shipment-specific risks. Section 4371 is not confined to exports.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. Justice Stevens takes no part in consideration or decision.

## Public Action

The Federal Circuit's judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Kennedy, Ginsburg.

The judgment requiring refund of the challenged unconstitutional assessments stands within the claim and amount properly preserved. The Court neither determines a new refund amount nor invalidates section 4371 in all its applications.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer. Justice Kennedy dissents, joined by Justice Ginsburg.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, with the six direct joins identified above, controls the rule, application and limits below.

### The general tax cannot reach this export-transit insurance

**Operative rule:** A federal tax on goods in export transit does not escape the Export Clause merely because it applies without discrimination to other transactions. Thames & Mersey remains controlling for premiums on insurance of goods during that transit, so section 4371 cannot be applied to the insurance at issue here; the rule does not exempt every activity or expense that indirectly affects exporting.

**Controlling explanation:** The Constitution expressly forbids federal taxes or duties on articles exported from a State. Fairbank protects export bills of lading, and Hvoslef protects charter arrangements for export carriage; those cases prevent taxation of the protected export transaction through its integral documents. Thames & Mersey directly applies that protection to insurance of goods during the export voyage.

The Government's contrary argument rests on nondiscrimination and changes in other tax doctrines. Those doctrines answer different constitutional questions. Michelin concerns the States' Import-Export restriction and goods no longer in transit. Washington Stevedoring concerns a service tax and reserves direct taxation of goods in transit. Neither converts the federal Export Clause into a prohibition only of discriminatory taxes. Itel preserves the direct-export question while sustaining a distinct local container transaction; Jefferson Lines concerns a local retail sale under the Commerce Clause. Fulton requires comparison of actual taxable events within that Clause, rather than transferring its standard to different constitutional text.

The insurance here covers the identified outward shipments. Risk as well as cargo value affects the premium, but that does not distinguish the export-insurance category already decided in Thames & Mersey. The Government's asserted interclause developments do not justify overruling that directly applicable precedent. Its decision to press nondiscrimination, while declining to advance a separate goods-versus-services challenge, also counsels against undertaking a comprehensive reconsideration of that boundary here. An included legal argument may be considered; the Court is not required to decide it.

The Court retains the distinction between taxing exports and imposing ordinary burdens before exportation or on genuinely distinct or tangential activities. It leaves fuller classification of other services open. Applying the existing insurance rule is sufficient to preserve this refund judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Thames & Mersey Marine Insurance Co. v. United States, 237 U.S. 19:** Retained and applied to export-transit insurance; its directly governing rule is not displaced by nondiscrimination alone.
- **Fairbank v. United States, 181 U.S. 283:** Applied as the export-document precedent supporting the Clause's protection of the export transaction.
- **United States v. Hvoslef, 237 U.S. 1:** Applied as the export-carriage precedent supporting the Clause's protection of the export transaction.
- **Michelin Tire Corp. v. Wages, 423 U.S. 276:** Distinguished by its state-tax setting and goods outside export transit; it does not replace the federal Clause's rule.
- **Department of Revenue v. Association of Washington Stevedoring Cos., 435 U.S. 734:** Distinguished as a service-tax decision reserving direct goods-in-transit taxation.
- **Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston:** Distinguished by the separate local container transaction and its reserved direct-export question.
- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc.:** Its transaction-specific Commerce Clause rule remains effective in its own setting and supplies no federal Export Clause transformation.
- **Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner:** Its transaction-specific Commerce Clause rule remains effective in its own setting and supplies no federal Export Clause transformation.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Its permission to consider an included argument remains discretionary. The Court announces no new review-scope restriction.

## Law After Decision

The Export Clause continues to protect the export-insurance application governed by Thames & Mersey even under a generally applicable, nondiscriminatory federal tax. No comprehensive immunity for export-related services or incidental exporter costs is adopted. No earlier decision is overruled.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would reach the narrower argument that the tax falls on a purchased indemnity service rather than the exported articles. Itel and Jefferson Lines illustrate the need to identify the actual taxable transaction. The stipulated premium reflects shipment risks as well as cargo value; that relationship does not make the service identical to the goods. The dissent would expressly overrule Thames & Mersey to the extent its commercial-integration rationale treats this genuine insurance-premium tax as a tax on exports.

The dissent regards the narrower issue as fairly included in the question presented and sufficiently supported by the stipulated record despite the Government's emphasis on nondiscrimination. It would reverse and remand without validating all export-associated taxes, creating a nondiscrimination safe harbor for an actual goods tax, or determining a new assessment amount. Its service classification does not command a majority.

The refund judgment remains in force subject to ordinary mandate procedures. No new trial, tax computation or further Supreme Court proceeding is ordered.

Reported argument statements establish the described presentation limits without resolving any unpresented exemption or computation question.

## Event

**United States v. Myers — June 11, 1996.** On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Seventh Circuit's judgment, 46 F.3d 668, in No. 94-1912. The questions are whether the targeted thermal examination of Myers's home was a Fourth Amendment search and what follows for his preserved suppression claim.

Police used an Agema 210 on March 12, 1992, to record comparative heat from the residence. The passive instrument emitted no rays and did not penetrate the viewed object; the reported pattern indicated concealed high-intensity cultivation lights. Police used the scan and other evidence to obtain the April 22 entry warrant. After suppression was denied, Myers entered a conditional guilty plea under §841(a)(1), preserving review of that denial, including his contention that the later warrant rested on the unlawful scan. The Seventh Circuit affirmed on the ground that the scan was not a search.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The affected judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated in the stated part and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Kennedy, Breyer.

The Court vacates the Seventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains denial of Myers's preserved suppression claim and the dependent conviction affirmance on the no-search premise. The lower courts must dispose of that claim under Pinson and the preservation and remedy limits below. The Court orders neither final suppression nor final admission of the evidence, and does not order acquittal, release, retrial, a replacement sentence or immediate withdrawal of the conditional plea.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Parts I and III of Scalia's opinion control with seven direct joins: the six-Justice whole-opinion coalition identified above and Stone-Zsela. Part II controls with the six whole-opinion joins. No dissenting or judgment-only position is needed to establish any holding.

### I. This targeted acquisition of concealed domestic information is a search

**Operative rule:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home's heat patterns is a search when it reveals otherwise unobservable information about concealed domestic operation. External reception, passive equipment and the absence of a recognizable interior image do not defeat that protection; equipment use or investigative purpose alone does not establish a search.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinson supplies the precise rule for this acquisition. Its application of Katz protects concealed domestic information, and Karo establishes that technology can obtain protected information about a home without a physical entry. The Agema 210's heat pattern disclosed the operation of indoor cultivation lights that ordinary outside observation did not reveal. The Court need not find that the instrument identified a person, produced an intimate silhouette or penetrated a wall.

The contrary argument confuses the physical location of heat with the information acquired. Roof vents and failure to conceal emissions do not establish that Myers exposed the operation of his home to this examination. Greenwood concerns garbage delivered for collection; the underlying domestic information here was not similarly discarded. Place concerns detection confined to contraband; thermal patterns can reveal lawful domestic activity as well. The absence of curbside garbage supplied an investigative inference, not exposure of the scanned activity. No different record involving unaided observation of visible smoke is before the Court.

Pinson therefore forecloses the lower court's no-search ground. This application adopts no general rule for all instruments, emissions or uses of technology, and no new test based on market availability or predicted future capabilities.

### II. The later entry warrant did not authorize the earlier scan

**Operative rule:** This search required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied. No such exception is established on this record, and the April 22 entry warrant could not retroactively authorize the March 12 scan.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinson's warrant holding applies to the same protected acquisition. Investigation supported by other suspicious circumstances does not itself establish an exception to the warrant requirement. Whren's requirement of authority for the particular intrusion reinforces that distinction; its rule for an objectively justified ordinary traffic stop supplies no authorization to examine a home thermally. The Court finds no established exception here, while reserving the distinct admissibility of evidence obtained under the later warrant.

### III. The preserved suppression claim returns for a separate remedy determination

**Operative rule:** When rejection of a preserved suppression claim rests on this erroneous no-search ground, the affected judgment must be vacated and the claim decided under the correct search rule. Remand permits consideration of the actual preserved derivative-warrant contention and independently supported, procedurally available alternatives; it neither presumes exclusion nor establishes a new defense or revives a waived claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinson separates a search violation from the remedy. Karo permits a distinct inquiry into whether untainted information supports a later warrant. Unlike Pinson's litigant, Myers expressly challenged the warrant's dependence on the scan and preserved suppression review. That reservation does not preserve every newly formulated objection.

The published record does not establish the full affidavit's independent sufficiency, objective reliance, independent source or inevitable discovery. Other investigative information and the fact that a warrant issued cannot substitute for those determinations. Leon is available only if independently supported and procedurally open. It does not protect knowing or reckless deception of the magistrate, abandonment of judicial neutrality, reliance on an affidavit so lacking probable-cause indicia that reliance is wholly unreasonable, or reliance on a facially deficient warrant precluding reasonable reliance. Its inquiry includes officers obtaining or supplying information and assumes execution within the warrant's scope. Evans concerns qualifying non-law-enforcement court-employee clerical error and objectively reasonable reliance; it creates no exception for this deliberate police scan.

The mandate requires no new hearing, discovery or automatic affidavit-excision proceeding. It decides no absent factual predicate. A genuinely forfeited additional issue would remain subject to Olano's requirements of error, plainness and an effect on substantial rights, ordinarily outcome prejudice, followed by the separate discretionary requirement of serious effect on fairness, integrity or public reputation. Intentional waiver extinguishes the relinquished error. Those requirements are not imposed on the claim Myers preserved. Rule 11(a)(2) permits withdrawal of a conditional plea if the defendant prevails on appeal; this limited correction, leaving the suppression outcome unresolved, does not itself order withdrawal. Ordinary proceedings must resolve the available grounds before any final admission or exclusion determination.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Pinson, May 8, 1995:** Applied to the demonstrated thermal acquisition, its warrant requirement and its separate remedy inquiry. Its individual affidavit nonchallenge is distinguished from Myers's preserved derivative contention; its rules remain intact.
- **Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347:** Applied through Pinson to protection of concealed domestic information; physical penetration is not indispensable.
- **United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705:** Applied to technological acquisition of protected home information and retained for the distinct possibility that untainted information sustains a later warrant.
- **California v. Greenwood, 486 U.S. 35:** Distinguished because deliberate disposal of garbage does not establish exposure of concealed domestic operation through heat emissions.
- **United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696:** Distinguished because this examination can reveal lawful domestic activity and is not confined to contraband.
- **Whren v. United States, June 10, 1996:** Its particular-intrusion requirement is retained; objectively justified traffic enforcement supplies no home-scan exception.
- **United States v. Leon, 468 U.S. 897:** Retained with its factual, procedural and reliance limits. No qualifying reliance finding or new police-technology exception is made.
- **Arizona v. Evans, March 1, 1995:** Confined to its qualifying court-clerical-error setting; it does not excuse the deliberate thermal examination.
- **United States v. Olano:** Retained for genuinely forfeited additional issues and distinguished from the suppression claim actually preserved here; intentional waiver remains distinct from forfeiture.
- **Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996:** Its legal and factual review allocation remains intact. It neither shields a categorical legal error nor orders a new hearing or displaces the distinct review of an issuing magistrate's warrant determination.

## Law After Decision

Pinson continues to govern targeted thermal examinations that reveal otherwise unobservable concealed domestic operation. Neither passive external reception nor the lack of a recognizable interior image removes this examination from the Fourth Amendment. Its warrant and exclusionary inquiries remain distinct. Myers's actual reservation preserves the derivative-warrant contention without enlarging it to every possible objection. No earlier decision is overruled, and no final rule admitting or excluding the later seized evidence is adopted.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent maintains the content-specific position it took in Pinson: a comparative measurement of exterior warmth, followed by an inference about its cause, does not itself acquire the concealed interior fact protected in Karo. Katz requires attention to the information actually obtained; neither concern about more revealing future equipment nor the fact that police used a device resolves that question. The dissent would reject Pinson's application to this kind of exterior measurement and affirm. It does not claim that a lower court may disregard existing precedent or that all externally received information is unprotected.

The dissent does not adopt a general rule that heat is abandoned property, treat the scanner as confined to contraband, or sustain admission on an unproved reliance or warrant-sufficiency ground. If the Court retains Pinson, the distinction between violation and remedy and the actual preservation limits still apply. That conditional agreement supplies no vote for the Court's vacatur or for its search holding.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts for lawful disposition of the preserved suppression claim and the judgment dependent on it, subject to ordinary mandate procedures. The search determination binds those proceedings. Available remedial grounds remain for decision within their actual factual and procedural predicates; the Court schedules no further event.

## Event

**Bush v. Vera; Lawson v. Vera; United States v. Vera, Nos. 94-805, 94-806 and 94-988 — June 13, 1996.** These direct appeals under 28 U.S.C. §1253 arise from the three-judge Southern District of Texas court's declaration and injunction concerning congressional Districts 18, 29 and 30. The question decided is whether the plaintiffs have Article III standing based on their alleged personal race-based assignment, without another asserted electoral injury. The constitutional merits are outside this decision.

Blum and Powers reside in District 18, Thomas and Vera in District 29, Orcutt in District 30, and Chen in District 25. The five residents challenge their own assignments; Chen alleges no personal connection to the challenged assignments in the other three districts. The lower court invalidated those districts and rejected challenges to 21 others. Its August 17, 1994 opinion was followed by a September 2 relief order and September 20 amended injunction. ## Holdings

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion controls all propositions below with the eight direct joins identified above. Stone-Zsela's concurrence adds the ninth judgment vote but no join to the Court's reasoning.

### The five residents' personal-assignment claims satisfy standing

**Operative rule:** Under Miller, voters personally subjected to the challenged electoral assignment may litigate their asserted unequal treatment when their own exposure, causation and likely redress are established, even if the substantive theory fails. Residence alone or disagreement with racial districting is insufficient, but these five plaintiffs' own assignments satisfy that personal relationship without an additional financial loss, electoral defeat or denial of service.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller directly separates personal-assignment standing from the merits of a design-only equality claim. Blum and Powers challenge the State's assignment of them to District 18, Thomas and Vera their assignments to District 29, and Orcutt his assignment to District 30. The plan causes the complained-of treatment, and relief against its use would address that treatment if the legal theory succeeded. No speculative chain of independent third-party decisions intervenes. Northeastern Florida confirms that exposure to challenged unequal treatment need not be accompanied by proof of obtaining the ultimate benefit; Warth preserves the distinction between standing and substantive success.

The objection that exposure does not always establish injury remains sound as a general caution. Here, however, Miller specifically recognizes the personal-assignment relationship alleged, without an additional downstream loss. The Court retains that threshold holding; the absence of a further injury does not distinguish it. Shaw and Miller's separate substantive boundary cannot itself decide jurisdiction or supply a merits judgment on Texas's plan.

Lujan's stage-appropriate factual-support requirement remains. The lower adjudication supplies the identified residents' relationship to their assignments; this decision does not hold that unsupported allegations invariably suffice after trial. No genuine dispute requiring new residence or exposure findings is supplied. Nor does the Court require an expert report, alternative map or proof of a changed election winner. General testimony about precinct confusion and misdirected ballots, and Blum's candidacy, is not recast as another personal injury.

### Chen supplies no independent standing for these districts

**Operative rule:** A plaintiff who shows no personal connection to the challenged assignments cannot obtain standing for that relief merely from other plaintiffs' exposure or a generalized concern about the plan. Chen's residence in District 25 supplies no independent basis for relief concerning Districts 18, 29 and 30, but his deficiency does not defeat the claims of their personally affected residents.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a personal stake rather than the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful government. Chen alleges neither assignment to the three districts nor another individual connection to their challenged classifications. Each district nevertheless has at least one personally affected claimant. The Court therefore need not decide every possible nonresident or cross-district theory. These defendants' appeals do not reopen Chen's unsuccessful District 25 merits claim or the rejected challenges to other districts, and the previously dismissed statutory and constitutional claims are not revived.

### Direct jurisdiction permits the limited decision while the appeals remain pending

**Operative rule:** The required three-judge court's merits grant of an injunction in this substantial constitutional apportionment controversy supports direct review under §§2284 and 1253. The Court resolves the standing question now and retains all three appeals without affirming, reversing or vacating the unreviewed constitutional merits judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller distinguishes a merits grant or denial of injunctive relief from a nonmerits dismissal requiring its own review-channel analysis. The present declaration and injunction fall within that direct-review channel. Standing does not confer entitlement to an injunction, and the availability of review does not make every possible issue part of this decision.

Retention preserves the actual limited question and the outstanding appeals. A terminal merits affirmance would decide more than the threshold result establishes; a merits vacatur under Shaw or Miller would likewise decide an excluded issue. No unresolved residence fact calls for a lower-court evidence remand. The Court therefore leaves the current orders unchanged while the appeals await their remaining lawful disposition. This treatment is the disposition of these appeals at this stage, not a rule requiring retention after every limited appellate decision.

## Separate Writings

Exposure identifies the person affected, but does not necessarily establish concrete unequal treatment. A common constituency does not, merely through its design, assign different political identities or make its representative responsible to only one race; predicted neglect or inferior status needs a supporting governmental operation.

## Event

**Jaffee v. Redmond, 518 U.S. 1, No. 95-266 — June 13, 1996.** Argued February 26, 1996, on certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 51 F.3d 1346. The questions concern a federal privilege for confidential psychotherapy, its application to a licensed clinical social worker, and case-specific balancing of confidentiality against evidentiary need.

After the shooting, Redmond received psychotherapy from licensed clinical social worker Karen Beyer. In the ensuing federal excessive-force and Illinois wrongful-death action, the jury was permitted to infer that withheld counseling notes were unfavorable and was told that withholding lacked legal justification. The earlier exclusion of Redmond's shooting testimony had been withdrawn. The Seventh Circuit reversed the civil judgment for a new trial, recognizing a privilege but allowing case-specific balancing.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed. The new-trial disposition stands; the general balancing qualification does not.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia.

The Seventh Circuit's reversal and new-trial remand remain in force. Further proceedings must protect privileged treatment communications and may not impose an adverse inference from their valid withholding. The Court does not reinstate the $45,000 federal and $500,000 state verdicts, decide that the shooting was lawful, or reopen the separately rejected deadly-force instruction challenge.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion controls on each rule below, joined by all eight judgment supporters. The future-contours illustration is not necessary to any holding.

### I. Confidential psychotherapy communications are privileged

**Operative rule:** Federal common law protects confidential communications between a patient and a licensed psychotherapist in the course of diagnosis or treatment, including confidential psychotherapy provided by a licensed clinical social worker. The protection covers the treatment conversations and counseling notes at issue; a professional title alone does not protect administrative conversations, ordinary workplace counseling or communications outside a confidential treatment relationship.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule501 directs courts to interpret common-law privilege through reason and experience, subject to the Constitution, Acts of Congress and rules prescribed under statutory authority. Trammel supplies the responsibility to examine a privilege's present justification; University of Pennsylvania requires a defensible benefit and boundary rather than an assumption that secrecy is always useful. Effective psychotherapy depends on frank disclosure, which predictable confidentiality makes possible. Its benefit extends beyond the patient to the treatment of mental illness.

Widespread state recognition corroborates that judgment, without proving that every State uses identical definitions or exceptions. Proposed Rule504 reflects considered professional judgment but was not enacted and does not govern. The loss of relevant evidence is serious, especially in a dispute about a killing. Rule501 nevertheless permits a defined relationship privilege where its public function justifies that cost.

Beyer's license, training and actual psychotherapy bring this treatment within the rule. Clinical social workers perform the same protected function and provide access for patients unable to afford other providers; the record supplies no confidentiality distinction warranting exclusion of this relationship. The holding creates no general physician-patient privilege or privilege for every conversation with a social worker.

Rule501's separate state-law sentence remains operative for an element of a civil claim or defense governed by state law. Recognition here makes it unnecessary to decide which privilege law would govern evidence relevant to both federal and state claims if the two laws conflict. The Court adopts no general federal-preemption rule for that situation.

### II. Protection does not depend on routine balancing in each lawsuit

**Operative rule:** A communication within this privilege does not lose protection merely because a judge finds the opposing party's evidentiary need more important in the particular lawsuit. A valid assertion of the privilege cannot support the adverse inference and lack-of-legal-justification instruction given here.

**Controlling explanation:** A patient must know the protection's character when speaking. If disclosure turns on an unpredictable later assessment of usefulness, the uncertainty can deter the very communication that makes treatment effective. The Seventh Circuit's reliance on the availability of other eyewitnesses cannot establish a general balancing test within the privilege.

United States v. Nixon concerns a presumptive executive privilege and demonstrated criminal-trial need; it does not require routine balancing inside every recognized confidential relationship. Daubert and Williamson concern admissibility and reliability under different evidence rules, not this relationship's protection under Rule501.

Rejecting general balancing does not make the privilege exceptionless. A patient may waive it. The account of partial responses and three redacted pages supplies no adjudicated waiver of all treatment communications; whether any waiver occurred remains undecided. The Court decides no waiver rule, court-ordered-examination rule, litigation-use exception, criminal constitutional override or serious-threat dispute. No adverse inference may rest on a valid assertion of the protected core. Because the actual instruction penalized withholding protected treatment material, the appellate new-trial judgment stands without its balancing rationale.

**Reserved contours in the Court's opinion.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer observe that the privilege must yield in a hypothetical situation where a serious threat to the patient or others can be averted only by therapist disclosure. No such threat is found here; the illustration decides no operative exception, procedure or ordinary evidentiary-need claim. Stone-Zsela does not join that affirmative illustration. All eight leave the actual boundaries for a case presenting them.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Trammel v. United States, 445 U.S. 40:** Applied for reason-and-experience development of privilege with attention to present function and evidentiary cost; its distinct marital rule is unchanged.
- **University of Pennsylvania v. EEOC, 493 U.S. 182:** Distinguished on the proposed relationship and demonstrated justification. Its rejection of an unsupported peer-review privilege does not foreclose this defined treatment privilege.
- **United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683:** Distinguished as executive privilege in criminal process; it supplies no universal case-by-case balancing requirement for confidential treatment.
- **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals:** Its admissibility and reliability rules remain intact and do not decide Rule501 privilege.
- **Williamson v. United States:** Its admissibility and reliability rules remain intact and do not decide Rule501 privilege.

## Law After Decision

Federal common law now protects the defined confidential psychotherapy relationship, including licensed clinical social-work psychotherapy. Evidentiary usefulness alone does not defeat the privilege, and valid withholding cannot be penalized by the instruction used here. State-law privilege obligations under Rule501 remain distinct. Waiver and other particular limits require their own supported questions; this decision supplies no comprehensive code of exceptions and overrules no earlier decision.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting.** Rule501 authorizes common-law development, but the dissent finds the asserted benefit insufficient to justify withholding reliable evidence about disputed conduct. The possibility that confidentiality assists treatment does not by itself establish how much this evidentiary privilege adds to ordinary professional secrecy, while lost evidence directly impairs the adjudicative task. University of Pennsylvania supplies the need for a supported justification, not a categorical prohibition on creating privileges.

The dissent also disputes the professional boundary. Proposed Rule504 did not include social workers and was never enacted; differences in training, functions and state exceptions caution against treating all such treatment as one established category. Scalia would reverse the federal-privilege basis for the new-trial judgment, leaving unresolved matters in their proper channels. He does not find wholesale waiver or hold that every note must be disclosed, and his objections create no controlling balancing alternative.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the new trial ordered by the Seventh Circuit, subject to ordinary mandate procedures and the privilege announced here. Independent, properly presented issues retain their own requirements. No new disclosure, damages amount or finding about the shooting is ordered.

State experience establishes widespread recognition, not uniform scope or exceptions; the Court leaves whether any waiver occurred undecided.

## Event

**Koon v. United States; Powell v. United States, 518 U.S. 81, Nos. 94-1664 and 94-8842 — June 13, 1996.** Argued February 20, 1996, on consolidated certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 34 F.3d 1416. ## Holdings

### I. Review respects sentencing judgment while correcting legal error

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part I in full. Stone-Zsela joins its abuse-of-discretion rule and permission to correct legal error and unsupported grounds; those propositions have nine votes. The additional factor-classification, degree, unmentioned-factor and commentary methodology has eight direct joins.

**Operative rule:** A departure decision receives review for abuse of discretion: legal interpretation and permission are independently reviewed, historical findings are protected unless clearly erroneous, and supported judgments about case-specific unusualness and degree receive substantial deference. Section 3553(b) requires the applicable range unless a circumstance of a kind or degree inadequately considered by the Commission should result in a different sentence; adequate consideration is assessed from the Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3742(e) expressly requires due regard for credibility, protection of factual findings and due deference in applying the Guidelines. Cooter & Gell shows that deferential review still corrects erroneous law; Pierce explains why an experienced trial judge may better assess a concrete, poorly generalizable judgment. Uniformity does not require appellate substitution on every such assessment. Ornelas supplies an issue-specific allocation, not a universal rule for mixed questions.

The Commission's categories matter. A forbidden factor cannot support departure. An encouraged factor can support it when the applicable guideline has not already taken that factor into account. A discouraged factor, or an encouraged factor already accounted for, requires exceptional degree or another supported feature distinguishing the case from its ordinary form. Where the applicable guideline and adjustments account for the factor, §5K2.0 requires a degree substantially exceeding that ordinarily involved. An unmentioned factor requires examination of the relevant guideline's structure and theory and the Guidelines as a whole; unguided departures should be highly infrequent. Rarity alone does not establish that a different sentence is warranted.

Stinson makes interpretive commentary authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to statute, inconsistent with the guideline or plainly erroneous; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Rivera illustrates the useful categories but imposes no compulsory sequence. Sentencing purposes—just punishment, deterrence, protection of the public and needed training, medical care or correctional treatment—remain relevant to the particular sentence, without authorizing additional categorical bans beyond the Commission's scheme. Errors of law or calculation receive no deference.

### II. The separate five-level victim-misconduct departure stands

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this component's judgment and joins no reasoning about application or extent.

**Operative rule:** Under §5K2.10, wrongful victim conduct that significantly contributes to provoking the offense behavior may support a reduced sentence, with the extent governed by the provision's considerations and qualifications. The sentencing court's complete findings support both use of that ground and the five-level reduction here, despite the end of resistance and danger before the force became criminal.

**Controlling explanation:** The relevant guideline covers aggravated assaults under color of law, including deliberate unprovoked assaults; it is not confined to arrests in which initially lawful force becomes excessive. Its six-level official-conduct addition applies to provoked and unprovoked conduct alike. The judge found significant earlier provocation and a transition from lawful to unlawful force within seconds. That supported comparison with the guideline's typical cases is more than a statement that the arrest happened first.

Section 5K2.10 directs attention to comparative size, strength and other relevant physical characteristics; persistence of the victim's conduct and efforts to avoid confrontation; reasonably perceived danger, including reputation for violence; actual danger; and other victim conduct substantially contributing to danger. The end of resistance and danger remains material under those considerations, but the provision does not require provocation to cease having relevance at the precise instant force becomes criminal. The judge's complete account supports the five-level extent within the governing discretion. The holding excuses no part of the criminal assault and supplies no finding that danger continued.

The policy's limits remain: victim misconduct ordinarily does not justify its application to criminal sexual-abuse offenses and usually does not make it relevant to nonviolent offenses. Unusual substantial misconduct may nevertheless justify mitigation in a nonviolent case, as with extended provocation and harassment leading to property retaliation. No later-added factor is applied.

### III-A. These employment consequences do not justify departure

**Authority:** Part III-A, joined by eight; Stevens does not join.

**Operative rule:** Loss of public employment and future law-enforcement prospects following these official civil-rights offenses does not justify departure because the applicable guideline adequately contemplates those consequences. The Court does not categorically exclude every unusual employment consequence or equate job loss with socioeconomic status.

**Controlling explanation:** Loss of the position whose authority the offender willfully abused is a foreseeable consequence of this class of conviction. The unusual-circumstances requirement does not turn that ordinary consequence into additional mitigation here. Section 5H1.5 makes employment record not ordinarily relevant to a sentence outside the range; its separate permission to consider employment in setting probation or supervised-release conditions does not authorize a prison-range reduction. Section 5H1.11 similarly treats military, civic, charitable or public service, employment-related contributions and prior good works as not ordinarily relevant. Those are discouraged considerations, distinct from §5H1.10's exclusion of race, sex, national origin, creed, religion and socioeconomic status. This application preserves those distinctions while rejecting the ground offered in these cases.

### III-B. Category I already includes the lowest recidivism risk

**Authority:** Part III-B, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** A first offender already in criminal-history category I cannot receive a departure below that category's lower range on the ground that his risk of recidivism is exceptionally low. Section 4A1.3 expressly sets that lower bound for the first offender with the lowest risk.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an actual Commission limit, not a merely discouraged factor open to another unusual-degree assessment. Individualized predictions of particularly good future conduct cannot evade that express treatment. The rule concerns this criminal-history ground and does not foreclose independently valid circumstances belonging to another departure inquiry.

### III-C. The supported extraordinary vulnerability may be considered

**Authority:** Part III-C, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Operative rule:** The sentencing court may consider the extraordinary prison vulnerability it found from the combination of these defendants' police status and exceptional public notoriety. Police status alone creates no departure entitlement, and this ground does not independently establish three levels or any particular sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Commission has not made every circumstance of confinement categorically irrelevant. The inquiry is whether the supported burden differs sufficiently from the ordinary case to warrant consideration under §3553(b) and §5K2.0. The judge did not rely on occupational prestige alone; the extraordinary publicity materially distinguished these defendants' position in prison. The Court respects that case-specific assessment without finding that continuous solitary confinement is inevitable.

The objection that the crime's notoriety contributed to the risk does not establish a categorical prohibition absent from the Commission's scheme. The majority permits the actual vulnerability to be considered; it does not announce mitigation for every notorious crime, presume greater desert based on professional standing, or decide the amount attributable to this factor. The ultimate reduction remains subject to the separate valid-ground and reasonable-extent inquiry.

### III-D. The significant burden of these successive proceedings may be considered

**Authority:** Part III-D, joined by the same five Justices as Part III-C.

**Operative rule:** The District Court may consider its finding of a significant burden from these lengthy state proceedings followed by federal prosecution for the same underlying conduct. Lawful succession alone creates neither an entitlement to departure nor a categorical bar to considering a genuinely unusual, inadequately considered burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Separate sovereign authority explains why the federal prosecution was lawful; it does not erase the actual burden the sentencing judge assessed. The Commission's treatment of unmentioned circumstances allows examination of that burden under the relevant guideline and the Guidelines as a whole. The Court sustains this particular ground without finding a double-jeopardy violation, prosecutorial misconduct or an automatic entitlement for defendants tried by two sovereigns. Nor does the holding establish that the ground warrants all three combined levels. Its permissible extent remains to be determined on valid reasons under Williams.

### IV. Invalid factors require further sentencing under Williams

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** Once reliance on an invalid departure factor is established, the sentence defender must show from the whole record that the sentencing judge would have imposed the same sentence without that factor, and any departure extent must independently be reasonable on valid reasons the judge actually identified. That showing is absent here; both cases must return to the District Court for further sentencing while retaining the separately sustained five-level departure.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams requires actual harmlessness of the invalid reliance, not merely the possibility that a lawful sentence of the same length could be imposed. All nine reject low recidivism as used here, and the Court also rejects the employment ground. The judge combined four considerations, stated that none alone justified three levels, and supplied no determination that the surviving valid combination would produce the same sentences. Petitioners, who defend those sentences, have not carried the required burden. The challenger need not prove that the invalid factor changed the result.

The appellate court may not invent replacement reasons. Even where valid circumstances can collectively justify departure although individually insufficient, their combination cannot preserve invalid considerations or substitute for a reasoned and independently reasonable extent. Upholding the separate five levels therefore does not restore either 30-month sentence. The District Court must sentence again within these holdings; the Court selects no final number of months and reopens neither guilt nor the bodily-injury adjustment.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Williams v. United States, March 9, 1992:** Applied without alteration. The sentence defender bears the whole-record same-sentence burden after invalid reliance; reasonable extent separately requires actual valid sentencing reasons.
- **Stinson v. United States, May 3, 1993:** Applied to the Commission's commentary with its constitutional, statutory and textual-consistency limits; commentary does not amend a contrary guideline.
- **Cooter & Gell v. Hartmarx Corp., 496 U.S. 384:** Applied for deferential review that still corrects erroneous legal premises and clearly erroneous factual assessments.
- **Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552:** Applied for the relevance of institutional competence and limited generalizability to the particular review allocation.
- **Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996:** Distinguished by its particular Fourth Amendment task; it does not compel independent review of every sentencing application.
- **United States v. Rivera, 994 F.2d 942:** Its distinction among forbidden, discouraged, encouraged and unmentioned considerations informs the framework. Its suggested sequence is not mandatory, and its discussion of Williams cannot displace this Court's controlling rule.

## Law After Decision

Departure review protects supported sentencing judgments while independently correcting legal error. The Commission's actual treatment of a factor controls whether and on what terms it may be used. The five-level victim-misconduct departure stands; employment consequences and lowest category-I recidivism cannot support these sentences, while the particular vulnerability and successive-prosecution findings may be considered. Their permissible use does not validate the combined three-level amount. Williams requires new sentencing because the invalid reliance has not been shown harmless and departure extent needs an independent valid-ground explanation.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stevens would also permit the exceptionally severe combination of employment and professional consequences described by the sentencing judge. Sections 5H1.5 and 5H1.11 discourage ordinary consequences but do not forbid unusually substantial ones. His position rests on the judge's case-specific assessment, not preference for police officers or a right to mitigation for ordinary job loss. Valid circumstances that do not separately justify departure may sometimes do so in combination. That principle cannot rescue prohibited considerations, and the invalid recidivism ground still requires the Williams remand. He joins the Court's remaining holdings.

**Souter, joined by Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** A departure requires an unusual difference that rationally warrants mitigation within the sentencing scheme, not unusualness alone. On this record, notoriety generated by the brutality cannot make more conspicuous misconduct a reason for less punishment. Nor does the relationship between the state and federal outcomes warrant an additional benefit because the state system apparently failed to vindicate the same underlying wrong. This is an assessment of the claimed mitigation, not a finding of misconduct by a state official. Souter would reject both grounds here while leaving genuinely different extraordinary physical vulnerability or a materially different comparison of verdicts open. He accepts the review framework, all five victim-misconduct levels, rejection of employment and recidivism, and resentencing.

**Breyer, joined by Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The relevant civil-rights guideline contemplates a federal corrective proceeding when state enforcement has been inadequate; ordinary succession does not put these offenses outside that scheme. Rivera's distinction between legal permission, Commission consideration and entrusted application preserves that limit. Prison treatment also varies among convicted officers. Even with due deference, this record does not establish vulnerability sufficiently unusual relative to that relevant class. Breyer agrees with Souter's conclusions and pertinent considerations but does not join his separate writing in full. He would reject both factors on this record, while joining the other Court holdings and the Williams remand.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Being a convicted officer does not itself distinguish these defendants from other officers entering prison, and publicity is not a substitute for evidence of exceptional risk. Genuinely extraordinary vulnerability may matter, but the offered justification is legally insufficient here. ## Event

**Rise v. Oregon — June 14, 1996.** On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Ninth Circuit's judgment, 59 F.3d 1556, in No. 93-35521. The §1983 action concerns Oregon's compulsory convicted-offender blood collection and identification program, the general demand for a precollection hearing, retrospective punishment, and Milligan's separate claim against Knox and Pearce.

Rise, Durham and Rhodes had qualifying convictions before enactment. Milligan's attempted-murder and unlawful-weapon-use convictions were outside the statute. The Ninth Circuit affirmed summary judgment, sustaining collection even on the assumption that ordinary law enforcement was its only purpose. Its Milligan ruling rested on the absence of evidence connecting the remaining defendants' own acts to the mistaken demands and discipline.

**The statutory scheme.** ORS 137.076 covers the listed rape, sodomy, unlawful sexual penetration, sexual-abuse, public-indecency, incest and child-display offenses; burglary intended to commit a listed sexual offense; promoting or compelling prostitution; attempt or conspiracy to the felonies in those groups; and murder or aggravated murder. The attempt provision does not extend to murder. It does not cover every prior offender or only violent sex crimes.

An appropriate-agency request creates the sample duty even if the judgment omits a separate order. The court must enter the order, require reimbursement unless the person lacks ability to pay, and impose the sample condition when probation applies. DOC handles persons in its legal and physical custody; otherwise the law-enforcement agency attending the court acts. Collection is as soon as practicable and, for incarceration, before release. State Police notice of inadequacy requires another sample; notice of an adequate previously received sample under §137.076, §161.325 or §419C.473 excuses another draw. A court finding of substantial and unreasonable health risk also excuses it. The same conditions apply to the designated persons incarcerated as a sentence or probation condition on September 29, 1991. There is no absolute one-draw limit or permission for arbitrary repeats.

Collection must be medically acceptable and performed by a licensed professional or practical nurse, qualified medical technician, licensed physician, or someone under physician direction or control, with prescribed procedures. The civil-liability protection concerns authorized medically acceptable collection. A procedural deviation not affecting reliability does not alone exclude a test result or opinion.

ORS 181.085 permits retention of samples and derived physical material, genetic-profile or identity analysis by the State or qualified contracted laboratories, identification databases, and population-frequency databases not identifying particular persons. Accurate and reliable identification techniques may be used. Transfer or disclosure is limited to specified law-enforcement agencies, district attorneys or the Justice Department's Criminal Justice Division for identification in criminal investigations or proceedings; parties receiving independently required criminal or juvenile discovery; and lawful court or grand-jury process for otherwise unprivileged evidence. Receiving public agencies remain subject to those limits.

A person may inspect the database information on request, unless a reasonable likelihood of prejudice to a pending investigation justifies denial. The Department need not permit testing of physical material. Procedures must address inspection and accuracy challenges while preserving material from contamination and destruction. A written destruction request and certified reversal of the predicate conviction, judgment or order generally require destruction of the person's sample, derived evidence and identification records. Exceptions preserve a separate qualifying obligation arising from another conviction, juvenile adjudication or guilty-except-for-insanity finding, and physical evidence whose destruction would also destroy evidence relating to another covered person. A §137.225 set-aside does not itself affect the material; release alone creates no destruction right. These terms establish neither unrestricted use nor universally permanent retention, and no actual unauthorized genetic analysis is found.

## Holdings

### I. The offered identification rationale does not justify this suspicionless bodily search

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Operative rule:** A qualifying conviction and an ordinary future-crime identification objective do not, on this record, justify compulsory suspicionless blood extraction and protected examination of the resulting material under this program. The State's reduced-privacy, identification and medical-safeguard arguments do not establish an exception authorizing this bodily acquisition; the Fourth Amendment summary judgment must be reversed.

**Controlling explanation:** Schmerber distinguishes lawful grounds for entering the body from medically acceptable means of doing so; Winston confirms that safe means alone do not resolve bodily privacy. Skinner and Von Raab address their particular safety or supervisory needs. Vernonia sustains a documented athletic-safety program with confidential, nonpolice use and limited consequences. Bell's institutional-security rationale cannot simply be transferred to an ordinary investigatory database. Sitz permits a defined brief highway intervention, but does not authorize extracting and retaining internal biological material.

The contrary identification argument is substantial. Conviction reduces privacy, the State legitimately identifies offenders, selection is objective, the draw is medically modest, and the statutory sample, health, use and retention protections constrain the program. Evidence concerning certain offenders' recidivism and useful biological evidence supports its practical value. Those facts distinguish this scheme from arbitrary testing of the public, but do not eliminate the body's separate protection or justify this extension for unidentified future crimes. Fingerprinting remains a useful analogy, not an equivalence that erases compulsory extraction and retention.

The holding assumes neither misuse nor unavoidable medical harm. Whren's ordinary traffic-probable-cause rule supplies no missing bodily-search authority; Myers's home-information holding decides no offender exception. A different security or health program, consent, a supported warrant or exception, materially different noninvasive identification, and hypothetical secondary misuse remain undecided. Collection, analysis, disclosure and retention remain distinct when relief is framed; this ruling itself orders no destruction or damages.

### II. The general hearing claim requires the correct legal premise, if still live

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Operative rule:** The preserved general-process claim cannot be resolved on the categorical premises that medically safe extraction presents no procedural liberty concern and an adjudicated conviction exhausts every material collection condition. Its summary judgment is vacated for consideration under ordinary due-process principles only if an independent issue within that existing claim remains live after the Fourth Amendment relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Bodily integrity is a direct liberty interest. The statute also makes correct coverage, adequate-prior-sample notice and a court-determined substantial and unreasonable health risk material. Sandin preserves inquiry into meaningful substantive constraints and significant deprivation without treating every statutory command as a constitutional entitlement to procedure. Mathews requires examination of the private interest, risk of error and probable value of added safeguards, and governmental burdens. A final conviction is not retried.

The strongest contrary argument is that no covered plaintiff establishes an individual sample or health exception dispute and therefore no universal additional hearing is owed. The Court agrees that there is no automatic formal-hearing entitlement. It nevertheless declines to sustain the existing judgment on the false conviction-only premise or an assumed account of available procedures. The statute's conditions bear on the general claim actually decided; considering them creates no new individual exception claim and finds no exception denied.

The lower court must consider actual lawful opportunities to invoke material conditions. Ordinary record verification and existing agency or court access may suffice. The mandate requires no new evidentiary hearing, discovery, prescribed notice period, added proof burden or finding of deficient procedure. Existing preservation and remedial rules govern; no new claim or amendment is authorized. Missing detail supplies no adverse inference. If search relief leaves no live independent process issue, no further process determination is needed. Procedure cannot cure an unconstitutional search.

### III. The presented obligation is not additional punishment for earlier crimes

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** Applying this regulated identification obligation to persons whose qualifying convictions predated enactment does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause on the presented record. Its design and demonstrated operation do not establish additional punishment, independently of the Fourth Amendment defect.

**Controlling explanation:** Collins and Morales preserve the distinction between added punishment and every later burden associated with a conviction. Compelled blood collection, retained material, probation conditions and possible deterrent effects require examination; a civil label does not settle the question. Here the controlled identification purpose, limited medical procedure and reimbursement exception for inability to pay do not establish added penal severity. No enlarged sentence, lost release opportunity, newly criminalized conduct or eliminated defense is shown.

Morales's protection of a meaningful opportunity for earlier release does not make every new regulation punishment or transfer its particular substantiation rule to this scheme. The Court approves no unspecified sanction, added imprisonment or extension beyond a lawful release date. Milligan's unauthorized demands and discipline are separate, rather than lawful statutory applications proving general punitive operation. An unreasonable search need not be punishment, and a nonpunitive purpose does not make the search reasonable.

### IV. Milligan has not connected Knox or Pearce personally to the mistake

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by all nine.

**Operative rule:** Individual-capacity liability under §1983 requires evidence of the defendant's own causal involvement in the federal violation; supervisory title or general implementation responsibility does not suffice. Summary judgment for Knox and Pearce stands because the record does not connect their own acts to Milligan's mistaken collection demands or ensuing discipline.

**Controlling explanation:** Hafer requires each officer's own violation. Milligan's attempted-murder and weapon convictions were not statutory predicates. Tuttle's uncontroverted affidavit identifies his erroneous extension of the attempt provision to murder, but neither Tuttle nor the disciplinarians is a defendant. No blood draw from Milligan is established.

Repeated unauthorized demands, a fine and segregation may present serious liberty questions. Sandin preserves those questions in a properly supported claim; it supplies no missing causal act by these two defendants. The Court does not adopt the District Court's negligence characterization as a universal answer under Daniels, adjudicate unsued actors, or infer waiver from Milligan's failure to raise coverage at his two hearings. Segregation duration and conditions, responsible-actor liability, damages and defenses remain undecided. The unappealed dismissals of DOC, State Police, Madsen and the John Doe defendants remain undisturbed.

## Holdings

### Effective relief remains because the retrial obligation is unperformed

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Operative rule:** A State's steps toward a new trial in response to an unstayed conditional habeas order do not moot its maintained appeal when reversal could still remove the obligation to conduct the trial. Because retrial has not begun and that effective relief remains possible, the appellate mootness dismissal cannot stand.

**Controlling explanation:** Mills requires dismissal when a court can no longer grant effectual relief. Church of Scientology preserves appellate review after compliance when meaningful partial relief remains; complete restoration of the earlier position is unnecessary. Here a favorable appellate decision could remove a concrete future obligation. Responding to the conditional order while maintaining an appeal does not itself concede the habeas claim or abandon review.

Moore argues that granting a new trial spent the conditional order and also invokes prudential restraint. The supported record establishes neither a completed retrial nor legal irrevocability that eliminates relief. Whether the State complied sufficiently with the sixty-day order is a distinct disputed issue, not decided by the existence of review. W.T. Grant's discretion over equitable relief does not make an ongoing obligation constitutionally moot. The two-line dismissal identifies no independently adjudicated prudential ground, and the Court will not invent one to sustain it. Lonchar preserves source-specific habeas and stay discretion, not an unrestricted substitute for the effectual-relief inquiry.

The State's ability to seek relief establishes no right to prevail on Faretta or the underlying judgment. Properly available independent questions may be considered below. The Court announces no rule for a completed retrial, a superseding judgment or an appeal offering no legally effective relief, and relies on no exception for the importance of capital litigation. No AEDPA temporal-merits question is necessary to this decision.

### Moore's statutory appointment supplies the financial-leave exception

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion and independent filing order.

**Operative rule:** Under the governing Rule 39.1, appointment of counsel by a federal district court or court of appeals under the Criminal Justice Act, 18 U.S.C. §3006A, or another applicable federal statute excuses the ordinary affidavit or declaration, but the motion must cite the appointment statute and state any earlier leave requests and outcomes. Moore's unchallenged motion identifies the District Court's CJA appointment and absence of earlier requests, so leave is granted without requiring that affidavit.

**Controlling explanation:** The signed motion invokes the rule's actual appointment route. Financial leave rests on that route, not on success or failure of the habeas appeal. The Court accepts the unchallenged representation of statutory appointment and applies the rule's affidavit exception.

Other filing rules remain. The motion must accompany the document and comply with Rule 21, with a copy preceding and attached to each document copy. A represented party ordinarily files an original and ten copies; only a confined unrepresented inmate may submit the original alone. Rule 33.2 preparation applies unless impossible, except where the Rules expressly require Rule 33.1. Legibility, substantive compliance, jurisdictional timeliness and Rule 29 service remain necessary. No defect or challenge is established here. Rule 39.8's screening of certiorari petitions, jurisdictional statements and extraordinary-writ petitions supplies no finding that this represented respondent's opposition is frivolous or malicious. The grant neither dispenses with every filing requirement nor declares the habeas merits resolved.

## Holdings

### I. Statutory-minimum relief requires Government authorization of that relief

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all participating Justices except Stevens: eight direct joins.

**Operative rule:** Section 3553(e) requires the Government to request or consent to a sentence below the statutory minimum to reflect substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another person who committed an offense; useful assistance alone and Commission policy cannot supply that authorization. The request may express the necessary authorization in substance without a separate document, prescribed formula or citation to §3553(e).

**Controlling explanation:** The motion condition attaches to the statutory relief Congress makes available. Reading any acknowledgment of assistance as authorization to disregard the statutory floor would detach the Government's request from that relief. The court decides the appropriate reduction only after the relevant authority exists; this does not give the prosecutor power to dictate the precise extent of an authorized statutory departure.

Section 3553(e) also requires the authorized sentence to accord with Commission guidelines and policy statements under §994. Section 994(n) directs the Commission to reflect the general appropriateness of lower sentences for substantial assistance, including sentences below statutory minima. Neither provision empowers the Commission to replace Congress's motion condition. Wade preserves the motion prerequisite, while reserving the independent operation now at issue. Stinson makes explanatory commentary authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to a federal statute, inconsistent with the Guideline or a plainly erroneous reading of it; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Neal likewise preserves distinct statutory and Guidelines measures within their respective fields.

The requirement concerns the substance of authorization, not the number of papers. Nor is it declared an Article III jurisdictional limit. Wade's existing review for unconstitutional purpose, including race or religion, and refusal unrelated to any legitimate governmental end remains; useful assistance alone establishes neither. Discovery or a hearing still requires a substantial threshold showing of improper motive; useful assistance, with or without generalized accusations, is insufficient. Its particular corrective remedies remain unresolved. No such refusal claim is decided here.

### II. The present Guidelines do not convert this limited request into statutory permission

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six direct joins.

**Operative rule:** The existing §5K1.1 provisions permit a Guidelines assistance departure without making every such departure conditional on authorization to sentence below an applicable statutory minimum. The motion actually made here requested Guidelines relief alone, so it did not authorize a sentence below 120 months and the sentence must be affirmed.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5K1.1 permits departure from the Guidelines on the Government's appropriate assistance request. Application note 1 addresses below-statute sentencing under the circumstances of §§3553(e) and 994(n); it preserves the statutory condition rather than treating a limited request as authorization for different relief. The Government's letter requested reduction from the Guidelines minimum, and the plea agreement promised no particular sentence. Neither assistance's value nor the letter's mere existence supplies an unexpressed below-floor request.

The contrary reading combines two different proposals. A Guidelines-only request cannot be converted into statutory authority Congress conditions on a request for that relief. A system conditioning Guidelines departures on the kind of motion required for statutory relief presents a different question. The Court concludes that the existing provisions do not impose that condition; whether the Commission could lawfully adopt such a system remains open. Text and structure resolve this case, so Granderson's residual lenity does not change the result merely because interpretations differ.

Once proper authority exists, the court determines the appropriate reduction for stated reasons. The nonexclusive assistance considerations include significance and usefulness with attention to the Government's evaluation; truthfulness, completeness and reliability; nature and extent; resulting injury, danger or risk to the defendant or family; and timeliness. Not all five must be proved. Assistance remains distinct from acceptance of responsibility, and the Government's assessment receives substantial weight, especially when value is difficult to assess. Those standards inform lawful reductions without requiring the Court to assign different direct referents to §5K1.1's two sentences. They do not independently authorize crossing the statutory floor.

The Court does not decide a particular reduction, safety-valve entitlement, drug-quantity issue or plea withdrawal. It also declines the distinct plea-interpretation theory: the statutory question is resolvable without new contract findings, and no basis for exceptional reach is established. This is nonreach, not a merits rejection or finding of intentional waiver.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring.** Souter joins the Court's opinion because it does not make a sentence-by-sentence division of §5K1.1 necessary to the result. ## Holdings

### I. The independent Brady dismissal remains separate

**Operative rule:** Reopening the identified due-process challenge to the Sorrell evidence did not reopen the independently dismissed Brady ground when the amendment expressly preserved the remaining dismissals. The independent dismissal remains undisturbed in this review and is excluded from a general availability or merits remand; evidence relevant to the properly available due-process injury may still be considered for that distinct purpose.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on this component. The eight direct joins supply the controlling explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The amendment identified the claim it reopened and preserved the other dismissals. Federal Ground A's suppression allegations and Gray's continued-withholding argument do not undo that procedural distinction. Kyles describes the collective lawful uses of favorable evidence on an available Brady claim; it does not itself reopen a dismissal. The Court therefore grants no independent suppression relief and makes no new suppression or materiality finding.

The opposite conclusion, that every use of related evidence is barred, would also disregard the amendment. Picard and Anderson require the substance of the constitutional claim, while Vasquez distinguishes supplementation from fundamental alteration. Evidence bearing on the inability to investigate and answer the penalty case may establish the injury or effect of the live due-process claim without reviving the independent suppression ground. Shared evidence alone neither merges the claims nor establishes their separate procedural predicates.

The initial order's nonpresentation and state-habeas factual-availability determinations were reported as uncontested in this Court. That description does not resolve the parties' dispute over counsel's knowledge at the 1985 trial. The Court does not adopt an additional appellate-abandonment finding or decide every asserted predicate of a Virginia adequate-and-independent-ground defense. Those additional propositions are unnecessary to the limited disposition of this independent ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Kyles retains its available-claim materiality rule without becoming a reopening rule. Picard, Anderson, and Vasquez supply claim-specific comparison; Coleman retains its distinct rule for a demonstrated independent and adequate state claim default.

### II. A meaningful opportunity requires more than eventual disclosure

**Operative rule:** The established right to deny or explain material information used to select a capital sentence is denied when the State's supported assurances and materially different last-minute presentation deprive the defendant of a real opportunity to investigate and answer that case. Applied to this supported loss of the protected function, rather than to a fixed advance-disclosure interval, the claim invokes law dictated before Gray's October 5, 1987 finality and is not barred as a new rule under Teague.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer: six direct joins. O'Connor's join does not extend to the final merits application or remedy in Parts III-IV.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner protects an opportunity to meet the information used to impose death. Its protection would be empty if disclosure immediately before use always sufficed, regardless of an established inability to investigate, prepare objections, or answer the proof. Weatherford denies a general constitutional discovery entitlement; it does not establish that State-created impairment of this protected opportunity is permissible whenever the evidence eventually appears in open court.

The Commonwealth emphasizes that Gardner involved undisclosed information and that Gray could cross-examine and testify. Those facts matter, but the issue is whether those formal opportunities could perform their adversarial function after the supported assurance and last-minute change. Graham requires that functional comparison without dispensing with Teague's dictated-at-finality requirement. Caspari likewise demands the actual rule and finality date, not a general appeal to fairness. Gardner supplies the pre-1987 protection; the later cases do not insert new law into that date.

The Court adopts no universal notice period, automatic exclusion remedy, or right to more preparation merely because counsel encounters surprise. It requires a supported deprivation of the existing opportunity. No Teague exception is invoked, and neither contemporary exception is abolished. A separately reformulated affirmative-deception claim still requires its own proper presentation and legal predicates; this old-rule holding does not automatically decide every such claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Gardner is applied to the effective opportunity it protects; Weatherford retains its no-general-discovery rule. Teague and Caspari retain finality-specific limits. Graham supplies the functional method of identifying a dictated application, not a replacement for prefinality authority.

### III. This presented injury and its effect are established

**Operative rule:** Gray's reopened due-process claim fairly encompasses the preparation injury adjudicated here, and the accepted existing findings establish denial of the protected opportunity and a prejudicial effect on selection of death. Neither the lack of a formal continuance motion nor the surviving vileness finding defeats relief on this record; the constitutional entitlement to sentencing relief does not depend on prevailing on a newly recast deception claim or the dismissed Brady ground.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer: five direct joins. The holding determines this claim's availability, violation, and effect without declaring all related theories exhausted or all due-process errors structural.

**Controlling explanation:** Claim IX and the state appeal presented delayed disclosure, impaired preparation, and fundamental unfairness; federal Ground A carried that complaint forward. The amendment expressly reopened that consistently presented due-process claim. Picard and Anderson require its substance, not identical wording; under Vasquez, the evidence accepted here develops the same preparation injury rather than fundamentally altering it. Claim IX's previously-adjudicated treatment is not converted into another claim's omission-based default. Keeney retains Townsend's hearing requirement when a properly presented claim turns on material facts inadequately developed in state proceedings, unless the prisoner deliberately bypassed the available state process. Through Fay, that qualification concerns the prisoner's knowing, considered choice to forgo the state opportunity; ordinary counsel negligence, or counsel's choice without the prisoner's participation, does not automatically establish bypass. Deliberate bypass permits refusal of the otherwise required hearing rather than compelling denial, and does not replace Coleman's distinct claim-default rule. Hall's negligence-based substitution therefore cannot sustain dismissal on this existing hearing record.

The District Court found an unequivocal statements-only assurance, reliance, and concrete impairment of investigation, impeachment, objections, and response when photographs and police and medical-examiner evidence were introduced. The preparation objection and renewed exclusion request were real, although no formal continuance motion was made. That omission does not establish a feasible cure or an adequate procedural bar here. The Court accepts the findings of the protected injury and a substantial probability of a different sentence.

Other violent-crime evidence and lawful eligibility do not resolve the effect of an effectively unanswered graphic presentation on the choice of death. Tuggle distinguishes eligibility from that choice. The existing findings and whole penalty record establish substantial injurious influence and also preclude a conclusion beyond reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. Brecht and O'Neal retain their category-specific holdings; the Court need not choose a general standard for this different category because relief follows under either competing approach. This conclusion neither identifies another perpetrator nor prohibits all uncharged-crime evidence.

**Precedent treatment:** Picard, Anderson, and Vasquez govern the actual presented claim; Keeney retains Townsend's hearing rule and Fay's deliberate-bypass qualification for factual development without reviving Fay's displaced claim-default rule or displacing Coleman. Tuggle's eligibility/selection distinction is applied. Brecht and O'Neal are preserved at their stated scope; no general Chapman extension or structural-error rule is adopted.

### IV. Conditional sentencing relief requires lawful prospective implementation

**Operative rule:** The reopened claim's established constitutional violation and prejudicial effect require reversal of the adverse appellate disposition and restoration and implementation of conditional relief from the death sentence. Before an operative writ is entered, any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory requirement material to that relief must be resolved; once met or held inapplicable, the Commonwealth must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable prospective District Court period or replace death with a lawful noncapital disposition.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer: five direct joins to this mandate and all its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The defect concerns the penalty proceeding. A new constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding, or lawful noncapital disposition, corrects that defect without retrying guilt or disturbing independent custody. Graham and Simmons preserve the distinction between sentencing relief and the unaffected conviction. The existing constitutional violation and effect are decided, rather than returned for unrestricted reconsideration.

The 1996 habeas amendments nevertheless cannot be bypassed by a constitutional conclusion if a statutory restriction actually governs. An applicable amended section 2254(d) restriction on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court requires its own temporal and predicate analysis. An applicable state-fact presumption or chapter 154 requirement likewise retains its force. Section 107(c)'s pending-case clause neither establishes Virginia's qualifying counsel system nor settles chapter 153's transition. No express exhaustion waiver, statutory exception, or satisfaction of an unadjudicated condition is presumed. Landgraf requires attention to the provision's enacted direction and actual operation, rather than resolution by the bare fact that litigation is pending.

This is a reservation for an actual applicable legal requirement, not a direction to reopen settled issues or search for hypothetical impediments. The federal evidentiary hearing occurred in 1994; no new hearing is authorized, and no exemption from section 2254(e)(2) is assumed. The receiving court must settle prospective implementation terms without treating the old 180-day calendar as presently running, automatically restored, or expired. The mandate fixes no immediate hearing date and orders no acquittal, guilt retrial, particular noncapital sentence, or release from independent lawful custody.

**Precedent treatment:** Graham and Simmons supply the bounded sentencing-remedy distinction. Landgraf's provision-specific temporal inquiry remains operative; Keeney does not erase a genuinely applicable later statutory restriction. No general AEDPA temporal holding is made.
