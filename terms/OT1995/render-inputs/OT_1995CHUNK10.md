## Event

Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission, No. 95-489. Argued April 15, 1996; decided June 26, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 59 F.3d 1015, under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1).

The question is whether the First Amendment permits applying the federal party-expenditure limit to an expenditure made independently of a candidate. The Party also seeks broader declaratory relief concerning the Senate-election spending ceiling. That counterclaim raises a separate question about the decision's permissible reach.

In April 1986, before either party nominated its Senate candidate, the state committee paid $15,000 for a radio advertisement criticizing Democratic candidate Timothy Wirth's record. The committee had assigned its entire $103,248 adjusted allowance for the Senate contest to the National Republican Senatorial Committee. The District Court entered summary judgment for the Party and dismissed its constitutional counterclaim as moot. The Tenth Circuit reversed and directed judgment for the FEC and determination of an appropriate civil penalty. No penalty amount had been fixed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is vacated, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's protection of the independent advertisement.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg.

The direction to enter judgment for the FEC and assess a penalty for this independent advertisement cannot stand. No damages, refund, fixed penalty or new factual trial is ordered. The six supporting Justices agree on that relief but divide over the broader constitutional counterclaim. The Court grants no facial declaration concerning coordinated party expression and does not affirm the counterclaim's mootness dismissal as a substantive rejection of that claim. Its further treatment remains for the proceedings below, subject to the controlling protection of this advertisement. The dissent would affirm enforcement and leave the amount of a penalty to the lower court.

## Holdings

### Independent party expression

**Controlling proposition:** A political party's own campaign advertisement that is genuinely independent of a candidate may not be subjected to the Senate-election expenditure ceiling in 2 U.S.C. §441a(d)(3) merely because its speaker is an official political party. Party identity and a continuing electoral relationship do not, without more, justify applying that ceiling to this independent expression; the adverse enforcement judgment must therefore be vacated.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. This is their expressly shared sufficient ground for protecting the independent advertisement, not a six-Justice adoption of every further explanation or reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Paying for its own political message directly exercises the Party's speech and its members' association. Buckley distinguishes independent expression from resources furnished to a candidate; NCPAC protects genuinely independent advocacy against expenditure limits. Those principles do not disappear when the independent speaker is a political party. The committee's general purpose of electing candidates does not establish that a candidate directed, requested or helped arrange this advertisement.

The government relies on the continuing party–candidate relationship and the risk that donors will use parties to evade candidate limits. Corruption and realistic circumvention are legitimate concerns. But those concerns do not justify treating this independently arranged message as a candidate contribution solely from the speaker's identity. Day likewise preserves both meaningful collective advocacy and regulation of real contribution conduits; its intermediary holding did not decide the separately excluded party category, so the present conclusion rests on this transaction and the independent-expression authorities.

The advertisement preceded nomination, and the record supplies no actual candidate connection overcoming its independence. The fact that the adjusted party allowance had already been assigned explains why the FEC sought enforcement; it does not cure the constitutional defect. The Court requires neither proof of a completed bribe in every financing case nor immunity for every party payment. Whether coordinated party-authored expression receives the additional protection urged by three Justices is not resolved by a majority.

### The alternative contribution theory

**Controlling proposition:** For the government's theory that this advertisement is a contribution under §441a(a)(7)(B)(i), party status alone cannot substitute for expenditure made in cooperation, consultation or concert with, or at the request or suggestion of, a candidate, the candidate's authorized political committees or their agents. On the accepted independence premise, that theory cannot preserve the adverse enforcement judgment under §441a(a)(2).

**Authority:** Part I's same six-Justice coalition. The proposition concerns the required connection and this alternative theory; it does not decide the constitutionality of every transaction in which the connection is present.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress stated a candidate-connection predicate. The agency's categorical description of party activity does not make that predicate a factual finding about this advertisement. Nor does the government's assertion that its theory was uncontested establish an actual concession. The Party's claim that the theory was newly raised supplies no independent forfeiture holding.

The separate contribution provisions therefore do not automatically disappear when the expenditure ceiling cannot be applied. They remain subject to their own definitions and qualifications, including the exemption from §441a(a)(1) and (2) for transfers among national, State, district, local and subordinate committees of the same political party. Financing the dissemination, distribution or republication of candidate-prepared materials under §441a(a)(7)(B)(ii) and attributing earmarked or intermediary-directed contributions to their original source under §441a(a)(8) present different predicates. None is established for this advertisement. The decision does not settle every interaction between those provisions and §441a(d)(1)'s notwithstanding clause.

### Limits and questions not reached

The Court establishes no general exemption for coordinated party spending, direct candidate transfers, candidate cash, earmarked donations, republication, sham independence or demonstrated donor conduits. It also sustains none of those applications by implication. The broader Senate-election counterclaim remains without a majority constitutional resolution; only three Justices would invalidate the ceiling for party-authored expression even when coordinated, and only three join the complete prudential rationale for reserving that issue.

The presidential allowance in §441a(d)(2), other House-election limits, a permanent constitutional dollar floor, a universal vagueness ruling and a general administrative-deference rule are not decided. The Court does not hold that the absence of a particular verbal formula always excludes election-related speech from the statute. No Justice adopts a general rejection of the contribution/expenditure distinction.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1:** Its distinction between independent expenditures and candidate contributions supplies the present independent-advertisement rule. Its treatment of the party provision's Fifth Amendment challenge did not decide this First Amendment question. The general distinction remains in force.
- **FEC v. National Conservative Political Action Committee, 470 U.S. 480:** Its protection of genuinely independent political advocacy supports the result; it supplies no categorical immunity for actual contribution channels.
- **Day v. Holahan, May 15, 1995:** Its controlling protection of meaningful intermediary association and preservation of realistic conduit regulation inform the required distinction. Its recipient category excluded political-party units, and its responsive-financing judgment had no unified controlling rationale. Neither is converted into an earlier decision of this party-spending question.
- **FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life, Inc., 479 U.S. 238:** Its protection of political expression and its nonprofit setting remain distinct; the Court does not rest today's judgment on a universal express-advocacy formula or extend the nonprofit exception to every entity.

## Law After Decision

Section 441a(d)(3) cannot support enforcement against genuinely independent party campaign expression at the scope decided here. A party-status presumption also cannot alone supply the actual candidate connection required by the government's alternative contribution theory. The holding adds this protection to the existing contribution/expenditure distinction without repealing the separate contribution channels.

There is no controlling rule today either invalidating all coordinated party-expression limits or approving their constitutionality. Neither plurality treatment becomes law through the six votes for protecting this independent advertisement. Ordinary contribution doctrine, the distinct rules for candidate-prepared material and earmarked contributions, and their operative qualifications remain available within their own scopes.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Souter and Breyer.** These Justices apply the independent-expenditure framework while preserving a separate inquiry into actual candidate resources and preventive contribution controls. Buckley supplies the functional distinction; MCFL and NCPAC support protection of independent expression; Day preserves both collective association and realistic conduit regulation. They give legitimate predictions about corruption their due but reject replacing the advertisement's actual circumstances with a party label. They do not demand a proved bribe for every preventive rule.

They would address the remaining counterclaim separately because party-authored expression, direct payment of a candidate's bills and undifferentiated transfers may involve different constitutional and statutory questions. The relation between the party allowance and ordinary contribution limits, and any necessary severability issue, should receive proper treatment before a broader declaration. The counterclaim is not jurisdictionally barred, and the advertisement need not be retried merely to recreate a fact of coordination. Their proposed restraint is supported by these three Justices; it is not the full rationale of the six-Justice holding.

**Kennedy's concurrence, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices accept Part I's protection of the independent advertisement but find that relief insufficient. A party pursues its own political program through the candidates it supports. Its own campaign message does not become another person's speech merely because the party and candidate cooperate. Buckley's valid treatment of ordinary contributions therefore does not decide the distinct restriction on that expression. Kennedy's Austin position and Day's protection of political association support the distinction; Day's preserved contribution rule continues to govern genuine contribution channels.

The broad Senate-election counterclaim and allegation that the ceiling chilled additional spending warrant resolving that party-expression claim now, in their view. They would invalidate §441a(d)(3)'s ceiling on the Party's own Senate-campaign expression, including expression produced in cooperation with its candidate, and remand for residual statutory and remedial consequences. They do not invalidate every §441a(a)(2) recipient limit, decide presidential financing, immunize undifferentiated party transfers or candidate cash, or protect donors using earmarked or sham conduits. Thomas joins this party-specific ground within Buckley's framework; he proposes no general merger of contributions and expenditures.

**Stevens's dissent, joined by Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg.** These Justices accept that the advertisement was independently arranged. They nevertheless regard the official party's continuing selection, support and financing of its candidates as a distinctive contribution channel. Donors who may give more through party channels than directly to a candidate could evade lawful candidate limits if all party election spending were uncapped. The official nominating institution's relationship survives the fact that this particular advertisement preceded nomination. Congress may address that institutional risk without proving an actual bargain or consultation for each advertisement.

They would uphold this election-related, inflation-adjusted allowance because it leaves meaningful collective advocacy while constraining the contribution channel. Ordinary issue discussion outside the statute's relevant candidate-election coverage is not automatically included. Buckley supplies legitimate contribution and anti-circumvention interests, and Day preserves realistic conduit regulation while requiring fit: Day's poorly fitted $100 intermediary ceiling does not itself decide this different party allowance. They do not rely on equalizing influence, reducing campaign costs or disliking expensive speech, and they do not extend their conclusion to every PAC, individual, press organization or nonprofit. The dissent would affirm enforcement under §441a(d), leave the penalty amount for the lower court and reject the requested facial invalidation without adjudicating every hypothetical coordinated transaction or finding an independent §441a(a)(2) violation.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Tenth Circuit. Its prior instruction to enter judgment for the FEC and determine a penalty for the independent advertisement is displaced. Further proceedings must respect the six-Justice protection of that advertisement and address the remaining counterclaim and any genuinely remaining statutory or remedial questions without treating either three-Justice constitutional approach as the Court's holding. No particular subsequent filing, evidentiary hearing, payment or disposition of the broader claim is ordered or presumed.

## Event

Board of County Commissioners, Wabaunsee County v. Umbehr, No. 94-1654. Argued November 28, 1995; decided June 28, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 44 F.3d 876, under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1).

The question is whether an existing independent contractor is categorically excluded from First Amendment protection against termination or nonrenewal in retaliation for public criticism, and what balance, causation rules and defenses govern the reviewed official-capacity claims.

The County did not itself buy Umbehr's hauling services. Its renewable agreement allowed participating cities to use his service and protected an exclusive arrangement while it operated. Cities could decline participation or withdraw on ninety days' notice. The contract renewed yearly unless a party gave sixty days' termination notice or ninety days' notice to renegotiate. Umbehr served six of seven cities, criticized county affairs at meetings and in newspapers, and lost the agreement after the January 1991 termination vote. He later obtained direct contracts with five cities.

The District Court assumed protected speech, motivating retaliation and injury solely for summary judgment, then relied on categorical contractor exclusion and personal immunity. The Court of Appeals rejected the categorical exclusion and remanded the surviving official-capacity claims while affirming the individual-immunity judgment and McClure's separate causation disposition. Those latter judgments are not before the Court for reopening.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals on the reviewed official-capacity claims is affirmed, and the case is remanded for application of the governing speech, contracting-interest and causation standards.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The categorical contractor-status ground does not defeat the surviving official-capacity claims. The Court determines neither actual retaliation nor ultimate injury, the balance, municipal responsibility or relief. The individual-capacity immunity judgment and McClure's separate judgment remain undisturbed. No automatic renewal, reinstatement, damages award or trial is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. The seven-Justice opinion supplies both holdings below and their stated limitations.

## Holdings

### Protected expression in an existing contractual relationship

**Controlling proposition:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing independent-contractor relationship because the contractor spoke as a private citizen on a matter of public concern when the speech and public interests outweigh the government's legitimate contracting and operational interests. Courts apply Pickering's balance to the actual contractual relationship, with appropriate regard for genuine service needs; contractor status, retained termination power and absence of a property entitlement do not by themselves defeat the claim.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion and the seven-Justice coalition identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Perry establishes that lack of entitlement to a government benefit does not authorize withholding it for an unconstitutional reason. An existing contractor can suffer economic retaliation for citizen criticism just as an employee can. The government cannot answer that claim simply by pointing to the contractual form, but neither does a contractor receive immunity from legitimate service management.

Connick requires attention to the content, form and context of the expression in determining public concern. Pickering then balances the speaker's and public's interests against genuine needs concerning performance, efficiency, cost, trust and disruption. The contractor's independence, other clients, actual contacts and responsibilities may change that assessment in either direction. Mere offense at criticism is not a service justification, and contractors are not invariably more or less protected than employees. Givhan also prevents treating private rather than public delivery as automatically unprotected.

Here the County arranged and supported a valuable service relationship even though it did not itself purchase the hauling. Loss of exclusivity, coordinated negotiation and notice protections can matter. The later city contracts do not conclusively erase injury. But the summary-judgment assumptions do not establish protected speech, improper motive, harm or the ultimate balance. Those matters remain for proper application below. The Court rejects both categorical exclusion and a universal strict-scrutiny rule. It decides no claim by an unsuccessful bidder lacking a prior relationship and creates no entitlement to every future award.

### Causation, the same-decision defense and the limited remand

**Controlling proposition:** The contractor must establish that protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor in the challenged action. The government may defeat the retaliation claim by proving by a preponderance of the evidence that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech; identifying a merely possible lawful reason does not establish that defense. Official-capacity liability and relief also require the ordinary entity-attribution and remedial predicates.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion; Mt. Healthy supplies the governing causation sequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Protection against retaliation does not guarantee continued business. Deficient performance and genuine operational concerns remain available, but the legal possibility of terminating for such a reason is different from proof that the same decision would actually have occurred without protected speech. Mt. Healthy keeps the claimant's motivating-factor burden and the government's counterfactual proof separate. Waters and Settle preserve the need for a genuine ground and actual causal analysis at their established scopes; neither supplies a universal investigation requirement or answers a factual-mistake issue not presented here.

The District Court's assumptions allowed a legal contractor-status ruling; they were not findings binding the merits. The remand therefore permits the relevant protected-speech, service-interest, causation, same-decision, injury and remedy inquiries rather than limiting the case to a presumed constitutional violation. Kentucky v. Graham distinguishes the official-capacity claim from personal immunity, while Monell retains independent requirements for attributing liability to the governmental entity. The affirmed personal-immunity and McClure dispositions remain intact. Neither liability nor a particular remedy follows simply from rejection of the categorical bar.

### Limits and questions not reached

The holding concerns termination or nonrenewal of an existing relationship. It does not decide initial competitive bidders, every future contract award, pure political-affiliation patronage, a comprehensive rule for mistaken accounts of speech, or employee status for unrelated statutory and constitutional purposes. Genuine performance and operational grounds remain available within the balance and causation rules. Possible later-acquired evidence and mitigation are not facts found here and receive no independent remedial holding.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Pickering and Connick:** Extended to existing-contractor retaliation through a balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, preserving private-citizen/public-concern and genuine operational-interest inquiries.
- **Perry v. Sindermann:** Applied to distinguish absence of entitlement to continued benefits from an unconstitutional retaliatory condition.
- **Givhan:** Preserves protection for qualifying citizen expression communicated privately; delivery method alone does not resolve the claim.
- **Mt. Healthy:** Applied without changing the motivating-factor burden or the government's preponderance same-decision defense.
- **Waters and Settle:** Their actual genuine-ground and causation distinctions remain operative; no full investigation framework or unrelated curricular rule is adopted here.
- **National Treasury Employees Union:** Its broad prospective-restraint rule remains distinct from this individual termination; its full justification allocation is not automatically imported.
- **Kentucky v. Graham and Monell:** Preserve the distinction between personal immunity and entity claims and the independent requirements for governmental attribution and relief.

## Law After Decision

Existing independent contractors may pursue First Amendment claims against retaliatory termination or nonrenewal under the adjusted public-concern balance and ordinary causation sequence. Contract form alone supplies no constitutional exclusion, and the rule reaches the valuable relationship at issue even though the County was not the direct purchaser of hauling services.

Governments retain genuine service-based decisionmaking and the proved same-decision defense. The decision supplies neither guaranteed renewal nor established liability, and it leaves initial bidders, pure affiliation and unrelated employee-status questions unresolved.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia's dissent, joined by Thomas.** They would not extend public-employee retaliation doctrine to this commercial relationship. The traditional distinction between government employment and contracting, legislative responsibility for procurement and the contractor's separate business position counsel against judicial creation of this cause of action. Courts would otherwise have to evaluate political criticism, service judgments and lost commercial opportunities throughout government contracting, with litigation itself burdening administration. Their Rutan and National Treasury Employees Union positions support preserving that boundary rather than treating every economic government relationship as employment.

They acknowledge the existing employee-speech and unconstitutional-conditions precedents but reject the majority's proposed extension; a conditional preference for Pickering over universal strict scrutiny is not agreement that either protection must apply here. Direct sovereign regulation and government programs expressly supporting private expression present different questions. They would reverse the reviewed appellate ruling and leave official-capacity summary judgment in place on nonextension grounds. They do not find the commissioners' motives lawful, the criticism false or injury absent, and they do not reopen the individual-immunity or McClure judgments.

## Procedure After Action

The surviving official-capacity claims return for proceedings consistent with the adjusted balance, protected-speech and causation requirements and ordinary liability and remedy rules. The courts below may resolve genuine factual issues as required; this mandate orders no particular hearing, trial, monetary amount or continuing contract. The unreviewed personal-immunity judgment and McClure's separately affirmed disposition remain in force.

## Source Notes

The [Tenth Circuit opinion, 44 F.3d 876](https://static.case.law/f3d/44/html/0876-01.html), supplies the actual contractual terms, purchaser distinction, summary-judgment assumptions and limits of the appellate disposition. The [No.94-1654 record](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0595) supplies the petition, briefs and Appendix. Allegations about county conduct and lawful termination grounds remain allegations; the summary-judgment assumptions are not findings of retaliation or injury. The County's arrangement and the later direct city contracts do not establish an amount of damages or an entitlement to renewal.

## Event

Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC; Alliance for Community Media v. FCC, Nos. 95-124 and 95-227. Argued February 21, 1996; decided June 28, 1996. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the D.C. Circuit's June 6, 1995 en banc judgment, 56 F.3d 105. The earlier panel decision was vacated.

Programmers and viewers challenge three distinct provisions of §10 of the Cable Television Consumer Protection and Competition Act of 1992 and their FCC implementing rules. Section 10(a) permits specified operator restrictions on leased-access programming; (b) requires identification, segregation and default blocking of covered leased-access programming not voluntarily prohibited; and (c) enables operator restrictions on public, educational and governmental access capacity. The questions concern protected adult expression, child protection, private editorial interests, Congress's own content selection, the compulsory system's fit and the appropriate relief. This is a facial statutory and regulatory challenge, not adjudication of the removal of a named program.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated. All nine heard argument and took part in the decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms in part, reverses in part and remands. It upholds §10(a), invalidates §10(b)'s compulsory combination and invalidates §10(c)'s authorization to suppress protected nonobscene indecency. The independent 1996 enactments are not adjudicated.

## Holdings

### 1. Section 10(a): particular permission upheld; divided sufficient grounds

**Operative rule and authority:** By six votes, the Court rejects the facial challenge to §10(a) and retains its permission after (b)'s removal. It permits an operator to enforce prospectively a written and published policy prohibiting programming the operator reasonably believes describes or depicts sexual or excretory activities or organs in a patently offensive manner measured by contemporary community standards; the FCC specifies standards for the cable medium. It permits, rather than requires, prohibition. The judgment has the six supporters identified above, but Part II's sufficient rationale has only Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; Scalia and Thomas supply the distinct ground stated below.

**Plurality explanation:** The four regard this confined permission as a permissible accommodation of child protection and genuine private editorial choice. ACT requires review of necessity and means despite the importance of protecting children; Turner and Hurley require taking actual editorial interests seriously. Unlike a command barring a willing operator's transmission, (a) leaves that operator free to carry protected programming. Prospectivity, publication, a written policy and reasonable belief constrain the permission. Removing (b) removes the compulsory alternative challenged as pressure to ban, without deciding that every cost incentive disappears. Congress need not freeze every prior statutory access allowance, but cannot avoid First Amendment review merely by calling a content-selected change private choice. The operative judgment does not create general immunity for a particular policy or an actual governmental facility.

Scalia and Thomas instead find no freestanding constitutional entitlement to compel an unwilling private editor to carry another's expression. They reject contextual balancing as the sufficient ground; their opinion is summarized below. The (a) dissenters find the statutory content-defined veto insufficiently tailored even as a permission.

The definition covers protected nonobscene indecency; it is not Miller's complete obscenity test and contains no categorical exemption for serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value. Context remains relevant. The judgment grants no separate relief on the definition-based theories, but supplies no additional majority rule universally validating indecency definitions, certification requirements or private policies.

### 2. Section 10(b): the integral compulsory design violates the First Amendment

**Holding and authority:** When covered leased-access programming is not voluntarily prohibited under (a), government may not impose this combined identification/notification, channel-segregation, default-blocking and advance written-election system. Its cumulative burdens on willing speakers and adult recipients are not adequately justified by the child-protection and parental-assistance interests offered for this design. Souter's Part III has seven joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The statute directs FCC rules within 120 days of enactment and requires operators to comply. Programmers identify and notify operators of covered indecency; the operator concentrates it on a designated blocked channel. Access requires a subscriber's written request, with the FCC's statement that the subscriber is eighteen or older. The operator must fulfill the request within thirty days; the rule does not require a thirty-day wait. An additional blocked channel may be used if the first lacks capacity. The government's description of the FCC accommodation for systems without premium or pay-per-view channels permits an operator-installed lockbox, with the operator retaining its key or code until the subscriber requests unblocking. That mechanism is distinct from a parent-controlled household device and supplies no numerical eligibility rule here.

**Controlling explanation:** Protecting children and assisting parents are compelling interests. ACT nevertheless requires independent examination of necessity and means; Sable preserves adults' access to protected expression, and Turner prevents automatic transfer of broadcast scarcity rules to cable. Here willing speakers and willing operators must identify and regroup covered programming, while every adult begins without access and must make a written election for the segregated channel. This combination creates substantial delay, deterrence and channel-wide burdens even though actual fulfillment may occur before thirty days. It is not merely an age check or an ordinary subscription request. Mixed, unpredictable programming makes parental supervision difficult, but that difficulty does not justify this compulsory system's full burden. Existing requested household controls provide a materially different way to assist parents; their relevance does not require a finding of perfect protection, universal affordability or actual uptake. The operator-retained lockbox accommodation preserves default denial and written opt-in and thus does not cure the constitutional defect. Parents can still employ household controls after requesting access; the holding does not assume otherwise. The enacted 1996 provisions provide separate legal comparisons, not proof that particular equipment works everywhere. The required fit is lacking even accepting the practical concerns advanced for the law; no absence of all supporting evidence, actual public registry or universal technical alternative is found.

The integral arrangement and its corresponding rules must be removed. The Court does not rewrite it into a different channel system or separately hold every notification, age screen, voluntary request or blocking device unconstitutional. Its sufficient ground makes a further general ruling on vagueness, prior restraint or certification unnecessary.

### 3. Section 10(c): protected-indecency authority invalidated; reasons remain divided

**Operative rule and authority:** Six Justices reverse approval of §10(c) and its implementing rule insofar as they authorize suppression of protected nonobscene indecency on PEG access capacity. The statute directed the FCC within 180 days to enable operators to prohibit obscenity, sexually explicit conduct and material soliciting or promoting unlawful conduct. The FCC reads sexually explicit conduct through its indecency definition and the unlawful-conduct category as otherwise proscribed material; its rules permit an operator to obtain certification that programming lacks prohibited content. The provision directs enabling regulations, not a command that operators censor. It contains neither (a)'s written/published-policy and reasonable-belief limits nor (b)'s compulsory subscriber mechanism.

Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens and Breyer, and Kennedy's separate opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg, each support final protected-indecency relief. No majority adopts either sufficient framework. The decision does not independently adjudicate the unlawful-solicitation category, invalidate lawful obscenity controls or decide a freestanding certification claim.

**Plurality explanation:** Stevens, Souter and Breyer find the additional federal veto insufficiently fitted to its asserted purposes in these access arrangements. Locally arranged access expression is not established as the operator's own presentation merely because the operator owns the facilities. Hurley protects an actual private expressive presentation, and the contextual attribution authorities do not supply one by title alone. The federal permission lacks (a)'s confined policy architecture and can exclude protected speech from adults altogether. Child protection remains compelling, but independent means review and the distinct local/parental arrangements do not support this additional unqualified protected-indecency authority. The statutory defect can be decided without classifying every local institution as a public forum, finding all operators governmental or ordering a further investigation of every franchise. A concrete private-editor claim remains possible on its own facts.

**Separate sufficient ground:** Stone-Zsela, Kennedy and Ginsburg apply strict scrutiny to Congress's content-defined withdrawal of access protection. The challenged governmental act is the enactment itself, which permits exclusion of protected access expression; the claim does not require attributing every later operator choice to the State. Turner supplies the content-selection inquiry and ACT requires justification of necessity and means. Child protection is compelling, but total discretionary exclusion of adults' protected expression is not narrowly tailored merely because the operator could choose to carry it. Household choice and narrower safeguards matter; an assumed compelled-message injury does not establish that all independent access programming is the operator's own speech. Actual government dedication may support a forum rationale in a concrete case, but no universal PEG classification is necessary. This is the same sufficient ground on which these three would also invalidate (a), with its own terms considered.

### 4. Severability and the scope of relief

**Holding and authority:** Section 10(a) remains independently operative after (b)'s removal. Souter's V-A has six joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. The seven invalidating (b) remove its integral compulsory machinery; the six invalidating protected-indecency authority under (c) require corresponding implementing relief. All nine preserve the decision's boundaries as to other enactments and unpresented claims.

**Controlling explanation:** Alaska Airlines requires attention to independent operation and congressional intent. Section 10(a)'s prospective permission can operate without compulsory segregation and default blocking; retaining that choice still serves Congress's identified accommodation and child-protection purposes. The defect in (b) therefore does not itself require destruction of (a). The seven rejecting (b), however, do not choose a new combination of its interdependent mandatory steps for Congress. The limited (c) disposition reaches the protected-indecency authorization, leaving lawful obscenity and otherwise-unprotected or unlawful-material controls outside that invalidation. Independent 1996 permissions are different enactments and are not decided through review of the 1992 rules. Setting aside an invalid 1992 authority alone therefore neither establishes an unconditional carriage right nor decides a future claim involving a genuinely private presentation, governmental facility or enforceable local obligation.

## Precedent Treatment

- **ACT/Pacifica (March 4, 1996):** The Court applies its protection of nonobscene indecency and independent necessity/means review to the compulsory (b) arrangement. Its broadcast timetable disposition is not a cable rule. The divided permission opinions preserve the governing protection while disagreeing on application.
- **FCC v. Pacifica Foundation:** Its narrow, contextual broadcast holding remains; it does not automatically sustain every cable indecency restriction or establish that the present protected speech is of lower constitutional value.
- **Sable Communications v. FCC:** The (b) majority applies the distinction between compelling protection of children and adequately justified restrictions on adults. Scalia's separate willing-outlet analysis concerns the permissions and is not the Court's sufficient (b) ground.
- **Turner Broadcasting:** Cable editorial interests retain ordinary speech protection. The prior content-neutral full-power must-carry classification and intermediate standard do not govern an expressly content-selected indecency scheme. Neither the earlier limited shared predictive-review principle nor its fractured record-insufficiency discussion requires remand in every cable case.
- **Hurley:** The protection of an actual private expressive presentation remains intact. The component opinions disagree about the legal consequences of access duties; none establishes that ownership alone makes every independent access program the operator's message.
- **Perry and Cornelius:** Their attention to the actual forum and government's arrangement remains. The Court creates no universal cable-access forum; Kennedy's conditional forum ground does not become such a holding.
- **Rosenberger, Pinette and Chabad:** Their program-specific viewpoint, attribution and contextual rules are not converted into a presumption that every regulated channel is a public forum or governmental speaker. The divided opinions' use of those limits creates no new general forum or attribution test.
- **Blum, Jackson and Lugar; Reitman and Crawford:** The distinction between a private act and governmental alteration or encouragement remains relevant. No categorical operator immunity or rule making every private refusal government action is adopted.
- **Alaska Airlines:** The Court applies independent operation and congressional intent in retaining (a) after invalidating (b).

No earlier decision is overruled. The separate opinions' broader grounds do not independently alter controlling law.

## Law After Decision

The challenged 1992 §10(a) permission remains available with its own prospective, written/published-policy, reasonable-belief and content terms. Its retention does not establish a majority complete theory governing all statutory carriage permissions. Section 10(b)'s integral mandatory arrangement cannot stand. Section 10(c)'s authority to suppress protected nonobscene indecency and the corresponding implementing rule cannot stand, although no unified sufficient constitutional rationale commands five votes. Lower courts must implement these provision-specific dispositions without treating either plurality or a separate opinion as a complete majority framework for other disputes.

Independent 1996 law remains distinct:

- **Section 504, 47 U.S.C. §560:** On a cable-service subscriber's request, the operator must, without charge, fully scramble or otherwise fully block the audio and video programming of each covered channel so a nonsubscriber does not receive it. The provision has no independent sexual-content or indecency trigger. Scrambling rearranges the signal so the programming cannot be viewed or heard understandably.
- **Section 505, 47 U.S.C. §561:** For channels primarily dedicated to sexually oriented programming, a multichannel video programming distributor must fully scramble or block sexually explicit adult programming or other indecent programming, in audio and video, to prevent nonsubscriber receipt. Until compliance, it must withhold that programming during hours the FCC determines a significant number of children are likely to view it. The same understandable-signal definition applies. Its amendment took effect March 9, 1996; no actual hours, compliance or technical capability is established by this decision.
- **Section 506(a):** Independently permits refusal of public-access programs or portions containing obscenity, indecency or nudity. Its public-access language does not automatically encompass all educational and governmental capacity.
- **Section 506(b):** Supplies the separate leased-access permission for those categories. Neither subsection commands refusal or imports the 1992 written-policy, reasonable-belief or subscriber-request conditions. Both operate from February 8, 1996. Their constitutionality and a particular exercise are not decided or approved here; their independent authority is not automatically eliminated.
- **Act §561, a different provision from 47 U.S.C. §561:** A civil facial challenge to Title V, its amendments or provisions requires a three-judge district court under 28 U.S.C. §2284. An interlocutory or final judgment, decree or order holding a covered provision unconstitutional is appealable as of right directly to this Court within twenty days of entry. That route is not a general rule for all communications litigation and does not enlarge this review of the 1992 provisions.

Congress may enact constitutionally tailored child protections, including parental-control and subscriber-choice measures. Operators retain genuine editorial rights; lawful obscenity prohibitions remain. No unconditional right to carriage, automatic repeal, mootness ruling, universal public forum or general permission to suppress protected advocacy follows.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg, concurring in the judgment on (c) and dissenting on (a):** Congress itself selects protected content for loss of access protection. Legislative action is reviewable even when every resulting private refusal is not governmental. Turner supplies ordinary cable protection and the content inquiry; ACT and Sable require adequate means as well as a compelling purpose. A permission can deny willing adults access altogether and does not become tailored merely because a private operator chooses whether to use it. The policy safeguards in (a) do not cure that underlying selective veto; (c) does not even contain those same limits. Neither popular offensiveness nor moral disapproval alone supplies a sufficient governmental justification, and the protection extends to unpopular expression without ideological preference.

These Justices recognize Hurley's genuine private-presentation right, but reject assuming that every independently programmed access channel carries the operator's own message. A locally dedicated channel open to private speakers may support a forum analysis under Perry and Cornelius; their opinion does not find that dedication in every franchise, declare all PEG governmental or authorize local managers to censor protected expression at will. They join the Court's (b) explanation because the combined compulsory burdens fail the required means review. They reserve §506(a) and (b), lawful obscenity prohibitions, distinct unlawful-conduct questions and concrete editorial claims. They seek no unconditional carriage order. Their strict sufficient framework for (a)/(c) has three votes.

**O'Connor, dissenting on (c):** She accepts constitutional scrutiny of Congress's permissive law, but finds its accommodation of genuine independent editorial choice and protection of children sufficient against this facial challenge. Turner and Hurley give that editorial interest substantial weight. The permission does not itself order an unwilling operator to exclude speech, and the different origins of leased and locally arranged access do not, for her, justify the different constitutional result. She recognizes that (c) lacks (a)'s written/published-policy and reasonable-belief conditions; she does not silently supply them. Their absence does not persuade her that all applications of this uncoerced permission must fail. Actual governmental institutions, an established local obligation or coercive administration may present different claims.

Her position differs from Scalia and Thomas's independent private-editor ground: private choice does not end review of Congress's content selection. It also differs from her vote on (b), where willing communication encounters the government's integral compulsory structure. An ordinary age screen or subscription request alone is not condemned, but the operator-retained lockbox accommodation leaves that structure intact. She joins the Court's (b) invalidity and the four-Justice contextual (a) rationale, without joining a universal cable forum or a categorical absence of governmental action.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in the judgment on (a) and dissenting on (b)/(c):** Their permission analysis starts with the difference between a protected speaker's ability to seek a willing outlet and a constitutional right to force an unwilling private editor to carry the message. Scalia's Sable concurrence, their Turner positions and Hurley support that distinction. Congress's partial relaxation of imposed access duties is not itself a direct governmental ban on the programmer's speech. Statutory designation alone does not constitutionalize a forum or every former access entitlement. They reserve an actually governmental institution and independently established obligations without finding that any particular franchise lacks them; they decide neither the entire access regime's constitutionality nor a taking. This is a merits ground, not a denial of Article III standing. They do not join the plurality's contextual balancing as the sufficient reason to sustain (a) or (c).

They separately apply strict scrutiny to (b), which burdens transmission by a willing operator. Mixed and unpredictable schedules make ordinary subscriber-initiated blocking a less effective default protection; segregation leaves the other basic channels available while preserving a real adult election. A written request can reduce requests made by children without authorization, and thirty days is a compliance maximum, not a compulsory waiting period. Household controls remain usable after opt-in. They find that design sufficiently fitted despite its adult-access burdens, rather than requiring government to accept a materially weaker parental-assistance mechanism. They neither require a flawless alternative nor presume an official list, universal affordability, actual disclosure or implementation of the 1996 blocking laws. Thomas distinguishes ACT's mandatory broadcast-hours exclusion from the present adult election; Scalia's practical ACT concerns do not lower the governing standard. Their strict-(b) analysis has two votes.

## Holdings

### The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Authority:** Part I-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The review bar expressly addresses the circuit's authorization determination. Section 2241 separately names this Court and its Justices as habeas decisionmakers, and AEDPA leaves that grant intact. Section 2244(b)(3)(A)'s advance-authorization mechanism expressly precedes filing in a district court; it does not direct applicants to obtain circuit permission before filing an original petition here. The amended circuit-transfer rule likewise does not name this Court.

McCardle requires respect for withdrawal of a specified appellate route. Yerger preserves a separate habeas source against an unwarranted implied repeal, and Bollman explains its statutory source and constitutional appellate character. An application filed directly here is called original as a matter of procedure; it is not an expansion of Article III's enumerated original jurisdiction. Section 1651 authorizes writs in aid of jurisdiction independently possessed, not a new freestanding source. The Court does not decide every possible certified-question, interlocutory or other supervisory route not presented here.

### Temporal operation of the channel rule

**Controlling proposition:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Authority:** All nine adopt this channel result. Part I-B's explanation is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy concur through the distinct relevant-activity method.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf requires attention first to Congress's temporal direction and otherwise to whether applying the particular provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events. A procedural or jurisdictional label alone supplies no answer. This provision governs the new May authorization determination and the ensuing request for its review. Its application to that post-enactment proceeding does not depend on retroactively changing the lawfulness of trial conduct. The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 does not automatically govern every different amendment; Georgia's qualification under that chapter is not established or assumed here.

### Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion

**Controlling proposition:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative distinctions:** For the second or successive §2254 applications described by the statute, paragraph (1) requires dismissal of a claim already presented in a prior application. Paragraph (2) requires dismissal of a new claim unless either it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral cases by the Supreme Court, or both of these conditions are met: the factual predicate could not previously have been discovered through due diligence; and the facts, if proved and viewed with the evidence as a whole, establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense.

Paragraph (3) requires permission from the appropriate court of appeals before district-court filing; a three-judge panel decides whether there is a prima facie showing and must act within thirty days. Its grant or denial is not appealable and is not subject to a rehearing or certiorari petition. Under paragraph (4), authorization does not finally establish entitlement: the district court must dismiss an authorized claim unless the applicant establishes the statutory requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** McCleskey recognizes lawful restrictions on abusive successive requests; it does not collapse these distinct statutory conditions. A request that this Court announce a new rule is not reliance on an already announced, previously unavailable rule made retroactive by this Court. New medical disagreement, an assertion of innocence or satisfaction of some other gateway does not automatically establish paragraph (2)(B)'s cumulative diligence, constitutional-error and clear-and-convincing conditions. The present submission does not establish those conditions; the Court creates no new default, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure finding to reach that assessment.

Rule 20 separately requires the petition to explain why relief was not sought in district court, state how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, demonstrate exceptional circumstances warranting this Court's discretionary exercise, and show that adequate relief cannot be obtained in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1)'s alternatives are exhaustion of available state-court remedies, absence of available state corrective process, or circumstances rendering that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Paragraph (2) separately permits, but does not require, merits denial despite nonexhaustion. Under paragraph (3), a State is not deemed to waive exhaustion or estopped from invoking it unless the State through counsel expressly waives it. No exhaustion, process-availability, effectiveness or waiver question is decided here. The denial below explains the direct request, but does not prove its substantive sufficiency. The Court denies this petition for want of the exceptional original showing; it does not find an otherwise disputed state remedy conclusively available or adequate. Section 2241 permits a lawful transfer, but none is ordered.

### The constitutional objection in this case

**Controlling proposition:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Authority:** Part II's nine-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Suspension Clause protects the writ's constitutional function; it does not exempt every repeated application from source-bound procedural and substantive regulation. The retained §2241 route is used here, not merely named as an inaccessible alternative. The Court independently considers the actual claims and finds no entitlement under the traditional extraordinary standard. Thus no otherwise sufficient claim is denied solely because all meaningful review has been eliminated.

Yerger supports preserving the separate route, while Klein's distinction between changing governing law and prescribing an outcome prevents treating every jurisdictional amendment as an unlawful command. The provisions applied here establish channels and successive-claim standards rather than direct the Court to falsify a fact or decide under an unchanged rule in a prescribed way. The Court does not approve elimination of every meaningful forum, resolve a case of established innocence excluded from all relief, or decide the constitutionality and temporal operation of §§2254(d)–(e), all of Title I, or every possible supervisory-review restriction.

### The reasonable-doubt claim

**Controlling proposition:** The challenged voir-dire statements and complete final charge do not create a reasonable likelihood that the jury convicted without proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every essential element. The presented constitutional instruction ground therefore supplies no habeas relief.

**Authority:** Part III-A, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone concurs in denial on successive-application and extraordinary-writ grounds without deciding this constitutional question.

**Controlling explanation:** Victor and Sandoval require examination of the complete instruction, not automatic approval or invalidation of the words moral certainty. This charge kept the burden on the State from beginning to end, distinguished criminal proof from civil preponderance, and defined doubt through evidence, lack of evidence or conflicting evidence. It required acquittal when jurors remained wavering, unsettled and unsatisfied. When the proof was entirely circumstantial, it required exclusion of every other reasonable theory; it forbade conviction on suspicion, conjecture or possibility and again directed acquittal for reasonable doubt.

That connected explanation, together with the court's direction concerning counsel's statements, defeats the proposed understanding that a lesser moral impression could replace evidentiary proof. Correct boilerplate alone would not cure a contradictory charge; the instructions' actual operation is what matters here. Sullivan would require reversal without harmless-error reweighing if the proof standard were constitutionally vitiated, but that predicate is absent. The Court does not use the strength of the prosecution evidence to excuse a defective instruction or decide a new instruction claim, waiver or retroactivity issue.

### The medical-testimony trial-process claim

**Controlling proposition:** On this record, admission of Tillman's testimony and the submitted forensic criticisms do not establish constitutionally fundamental unfairness or the requested constitutional rule that only a physician may provide such testimony. This is a determination of the presented trial-process claim, not a finding that each challenged medical conclusion was correct.

**Authority:** Part III-B, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone rests on the separate extraordinary-review ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lisenba asks whether the evidentiary treatment denied fundamental fairness. Ake, Ford and Eddings do not establish the proposed automatic physician-only rule. Tillman's credentials were exposed through examination, contrary medical testimony was offered, Whitaker supplied independent qualified testimony, and jurors were instructed to determine expert weight and reject an opinion whose factual assumptions they did not find true.

Spitz and Arden do more than criticize credentials: they attack the forensic basis of the earlier-death estimate, observations, samples and other interpretations. The Court considers that substance. But expert disagreement and criticism of the work do not on this showing prove knowing use of false evidence, concealed qualifications, deprivation of a constitutionally required expert or another established trial-process violation. Neither an altered estimate nor the experts' asserted certainty proves perjury. The ruling neither certifies every forensic statement nor invents additional claims from supporting observations concerning injury or binding.

### Innocence as a gateway

**Controlling proposition:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Authority:** Part IV-A, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept insufficiency under the governing standard while retaining their stricter view; Stone does not join this additional application.

**Controlling explanation:** The experts' three-to-five-day estimate would support the alibi and is supported by detailed criticism of the forensic chronology. Their review included Whitaker's testimony; the Court does not disregard their opinions merely because they leave his professional qualifications unchallenged. Whitaker nevertheless independently favored death near disappearance while acknowledging the later interval as possible. The disappearance, abandoned car, call from Felker's home and unchanged clothing remain additional evidence bearing on chronology.

Considering those grounds together, the new opinions do not remove reasonable adverse assessment to the probability required by Schlup. The Court does not find the affidavits false or use other-offense evidence beyond its stated motive, intent and state-of-mind purpose. The legal conclusion is about this gateway showing. Even a successful gateway would permit examination of an underlying constitutional claim; it would not itself prove that claim or establish paragraph (2)(B)'s distinct requirements.

### The separate constitutional innocence claim and original relief

**Controlling proposition:** Even assuming a possible constitutional claim based on actual innocence, this considered submission does not establish exceptional circumstances warranting the Court's original habeas intervention. Original relief is denied without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence burden, rejecting every such constitutional claim, or requiring an additional return merely because new expert opinions are offered.

**Authority:** Part IV-B, joined by all nine Justices; this is an independently sufficient original-writ ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Herrera resolved the insufficiency of its particular submission while leaving the Justices divided on a general innocence right and its threshold. It supplies no single general burden to import here. Rule 20 requires an exceptional original showing even when jurisdiction exists and a constitutional theory is assumed.

The later opinions and their detailed physiological criticism receive consideration alongside Whitaker's estimate, his acknowledgment of the later interval and the circumstantial chronology. The criticism raises a substantive medical dispute. It does not, on the submission as a whole, demonstrate the extraordinary basis for this Court to intervene directly. The Court does not substitute the mere existence of a conviction or the circuit's denial for that examination, declare the experts dishonest, or assume that an unexamined Georgia remedy adequately resolves the claim. No new general innocence standard, factual hearing, transfer or further return is necessary to decide this original request. This ground remains distinct from both the trial-process holdings and Schlup's procedural gateway.

### Pending application A-890

**Controlling proposition:** Execution's irreversibility does not alone establish entitlement to a stay. Because the independently considered claims show no sufficiently substantial basis for relief through the available proceedings and no identified unfinished legally required process warrants protection, application A-890 is denied.

## Event

O'Hare Truck Service, Inc. v. City of Northlake, No. 95-191. Argued March 20, 1996; decided June 28, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 47 F.3d 883, under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1).

The First Amendment question concerns removal from an existing recurring towing-referral relationship for refusing requested political support and supporting the incumbent mayor's opponent. The separate procedural-due-process judgment rejecting a property entitlement is not before the Court for reopening.

The case was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6). O'Hare alleges longstanding participation in a police rotation list, assurances concerning satisfactory service, refusal of a requested contribution, support and posters for an opposing candidate, and removal at the reelected mayor's direction. Those allegations are accepted for the motion, not established as true after factfinding. Northlake neither paid O'Hare nor entered a direct towing-services contract; vehicle owners paid. The governmental benefit was recurring access to controlled referrals.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment dismissing the First Amendment claim is reversed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the qualified affiliation, speech and causation rules. The separate due-process disposition remains undisturbed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: First Amendment dismissal reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The claim may proceed beyond categorical contractor exclusion. The Court establishes neither actual retaliation nor municipal attribution, personal liability, a property right or a damages amount. It orders no restoration to the list, permanent referrals, evidentiary hearing or trial and decides no new immunity question. Summary judgment remains available under ordinary standards on an appropriately developed record.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. The seven-Justice opinion directly supports the complete qualified framework below; no combination of competing rationales is necessary.

## Holdings

### Political affiliation in an existing recurring service relationship

**Controlling proposition:** Government may not withdraw an existing independent-contractor or regular service-provider relationship because the provider refuses partisan support or supports an opponent, unless the government demonstrates that political affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance of the actual work. That protection reaches the pleaded recurring towing referrals even without a direct municipal purchase, employment relationship or property entitlement.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion and the seven-Justice coalition identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Elrod and Branti protect against withdrawal of governmental work for an impermissible partisan condition. Rutan confirms in the employment setting that the constitutional question is not confined to formal tenure or a narrow category of benefits. Perry separates an entitlement to receive a benefit from the government's authority to withhold it for a forbidden reason. The recurring relationship alleged here supplies the concrete benefit and retaliation risk needed for the extension.

Branti's exception depends on function, not a label. A genuinely policymaking or confidential role can justify a political requirement when the government shows why allegiance is appropriate to effective performance. Calling someone a contractor, policymaker or confidential worker does not itself make that showing. General patronage benefits, rewarding allies and dislike of opposing support do not alone satisfy it.

The pleaded towing work formulates no policy, occupies no confidential political role and does not speak for the administration; no appropriate partisan qualification is shown at this stage. The City's control over referrals can impose an economic penalty even though customers pay the bills and the provider has other business. Legitimate safety, responsiveness, reliability, economy and integrity requirements remain available. But a generalized service concern cannot contradict the accepted allegation that political retaliation caused removal. The Court decides neither the truth of that allegation nor every initial bid, later allocation or commercial dispute.

### Distinguishing affiliation demands from speech disputes

**Controlling proposition:** The functional affiliation rule does not replace Pickering's analysis of public-concern speech or materially mixed speech-and-affiliation claims. Courts must identify the developed claim and apply the appropriate inquiry: whether political allegiance is genuinely required for the work, or whether protected citizen speech and the public's interests outweigh the government's legitimate service interests in the actual relationship.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** An asserted requirement of partisan allegiance poses a different question from the effect of particular expression on performance. Branti's specific appropriate-affiliation condition cannot be replaced by an undifferentiated claim of managerial discretion. Conversely, the presence of political association does not predetermine every dispute involving criticism, statements or their effects on services.

Pickering supplies the service-adjusted balance for the speech inquiry, with Connick's attention to content, form and context in identifying public concern. The courts below may determine in appropriate further proceedings how the refusal of support and the affirmative electoral expression relate in the developed claim. That reservation does not reinstate categorical contractor exclusion or establish a special constitutional investigation code. NTEU's broad prospective restriction and Waters's limited genuine-ground propositions retain their actual scopes; neither mechanically decides this pleading case. The controlling affiliation extension is complete with its qualifications even though particular mixed applications remain for development.

### Causation, defenses and relief

**Controlling proposition:** The claimants must establish the relevant protected conduct and show that it was a substantial or motivating factor in the removal. Under Mt. Healthy, the City may prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the same decision would have occurred absent that conduct; a hypothetical permissible reason is insufficient. Governmental attribution, individual defenses and remedies retain their ordinary independent requirements.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion applies Mt. Healthy's substantial-or-motivating-factor and same-decision sequence.

**Controlling explanation:** The First Amendment does not guarantee permanent referrals or insulate deficient performance. It forbids a causally effective unconstitutional condition, subject to proof that a lawful decision would have occurred anyway. Neutral service, safety, cost, responsiveness, reliability and integrity reasons remain available when supported by the actual record. Their possible existence cannot defeat the pleaded retaliatory reason by assumption.

Waters and Settle preserve the distinction between a genuine reason and an imagined sufficient one without establishing a new comprehensive mistake-of-fact rule here. Monell preserves separate municipal-attribution requirements; the constitutional extension does not establish the mayor's actual direction as a fact or determine personal liability. The remand leaves immunity and damages questions for lawful resolution and does not reopen the separate property/due-process holding. If the developed record presents no genuine material dispute and otherwise warrants judgment, summary judgment remains available; a trial is not the inevitable consequence of reversal.

### Limits and questions not reached

This decision concerns an existing recurring relationship, not an initial unsuccessful bidder or every governmental allocation. It creates no property entitlement, general employee status for unrelated purposes, criminal bribery ruling or right to deficient performance. It does not decide what every hypothetical reduction in referrals would require, or establish liability, immunity, attribution or relief from allegations alone. The qualified affiliation rule and the mixed-speech inquiry remain distinct.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Elrod and Branti:** Their protection against impermissible affiliation conditions is extended to the existing contractor or regular-provider relationship, retaining the government's functional appropriate-affiliation exception.
- **Rutan:** Its employee-benefit extension supports examining the reason for government action rather than confining protection to tenure; it did not itself previously resolve this recurring-provider setting.
- **Perry v. Sindermann:** Applied to distinguish lack of a property entitlement from freedom to impose an unconstitutional condition.
- **Pickering and Connick:** Supply the distinct speech/public-concern inquiry for an appropriately developed speech or mixed claim, adjusted to actual service interests rather than displaced by a universal affiliation test.
- **Mt. Healthy:** Applied without changing the claimant's motivating-factor burden or the City's preponderance same-decision defense.
- **Waters and Settle:** Their genuine-ground and actual-cause distinctions remain in force; no new complete factual-error or investigation framework is adopted.
- **National Treasury Employees Union:** Its advance-restraint rule remains distinct from this individual referral removal.
- **Monell:** Its independent governmental-attribution requirements remain necessary; reversal does not establish municipal liability.

## Law After Decision

An existing contractor or recurring service provider may challenge withdrawal of the relationship for refusing partisan support or adopting an opposing allegiance. Government must justify any asserted political qualification by the actual work, while retaining neutral service criteria and a proved same-decision defense.

The absence of direct municipal payment or a property entitlement is no categorical defense to the First Amendment claim. Speech or materially mixed disputes still require the appropriate service-adjusted analysis. The decision establishes the qualified rule and permits further proceedings, without adjudicating the pleaded motive or conferring permanent work.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia's dissent, joined by Thomas.** They would affirm because the Court should not extend public-employment patronage restrictions to this nonemployment allocation of commercial referrals. Their Rutan and National Treasury Employees Union positions favor preserving the traditional political discretion surrounding governmental work, recognizing contractors' separate business opportunities and leaving procurement rules principally to political institutions. They question the claim that loss of a commercial benefit necessarily coerces belief in the same way as employee patronage and warn that constitutional litigation would burden routine allocation and service judgments.

They also object that distinctions between affiliation and speech become difficult to administer when refusing support, displaying campaign material and criticizing officials overlap. A conditional insistence on actual causal proof and legitimate service decisions does not amount to a join in the majority's extension. Their position leaves existing employee doctrine and separate restrictions on sovereign suppression of advocacy intact. They accept the allegations for the motion and do not find that O'Hare provided deficient service, that the City had a lawful actual motive or that any criminal offense occurred. Lack of a property entitlement is not their sole ground, and the separate due-process disposition remains outside review.

The separate procedural-due-process disposition is undisturbed.

## Event

Pennsylvania v. Labron, No. 95-1691; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, No. 95-1738. Certiorari granted and summary merits decisions issued July 1, 1996, without plenary argument. On petitions under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) from the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania.

Labron, 543 Pa. 86, 669 A.2d 917, concerns a pretrial suppression judgment: its opinion was dated December 29, 1995, and issued with entry of judgment January 12, 1996. Kilgore, 544 Pa. 439, 677 A.2d 311, concerns a suppression ruling overturning the adverse ruling after conviction and requiring further proceedings: its opinion was dated December 26, 1995, and issued with entry of judgment January 24, 1996.

The question is whether the Fourth Amendment automobile exception requires an additional case-specific exigency when police have probable cause to search an automobile, and how that rule applies to these vehicle searches. Finality, independent state grounds and the limits of federal review are considered separately for each petition.

In Labron, officers observed drug transactions on a Philadelphia street, with drugs retrieved from the trunk of a parked 1977 Lincoln. The trial court suppressed; the Superior Court reversed; the Pennsylvania Supreme Court restored suppression because the Commonwealth had not shown the additional exigency that court required. The parties disputed the significance of prior investigation and time to seek a warrant. When officers attempted to impound the Lincoln, it would not start. Testimony described four tires, glass, a tag and an apparently highway-legal exterior; the trial judge discussed its apparent potential mobility. The timing of the failed start relative to the trunk search and the officers' prior knowledge of its condition are not established.

In Kilgore, a controlled purchase led officers to a pickup at a private farm. Observation of the approach to the truck was interrupted; the former wife then delivered cocaine, but the additional cocaine and purchase money were not found on her. An officer approached the farmhouse's back door, saw a person run from the kitchen, announced police presence, entered and detained Kilgore in the living room. The homeowner consented to a premises search; direct testimony specifically describes house consent. Police searched Kilgore's truck behind the residence without a warrant or his consent and found cocaine in a cup beneath other contents on the passenger floor. Money was later recovered under a carpet after its location was disclosed. The state court accepted probable cause but found no additional exigency because officers could guard the truck while obtaining a warrant.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated. There was no plenary argument.

## Public Action

The petitions for certiorari are granted. The Court summarily reverses both judgments in a reasoned opinion and remands for proceedings consistent with its federal decision. The petition-stage tally is not disclosed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reverse and remand in Labron, 7–2; reverse and remand in Kilgore, 7–2.

**Supporting both judgments:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing both judgments:** Stevens and Ginsburg. They would decline review of Labron because they read its judgment as resting on an adequate independent state ground, and would deny discretionary review of Kilgore despite its express federal conclusion. Their dissent does not affirmatively adopt a federal rule requiring an additional automobile exigency.

The Court applies the automobile exception to the reviewed searches and rejects the separate federal urgency requirement used to suppress the evidence. The remand does not leave that application wholly undecided. It permits lawful consideration of genuinely remaining matters, including any properly available independent state constitutional ground and search-scope issue, without deciding that an additional claim is preserved, waived or meritorious. It orders no automatic evidence admission, conviction, new trial, new hearing or particular state-law disposition.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor writes the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven Justices join its finality and independent-state-ground analysis, reasoned summary treatment, automobile-exception rule and application, and limited remand.

Ginsburg dissents, joined in full by Stevens. Their shared writing opposes federal review for different reasons in the two cases. Neither adds a conditional merits opinion endorsing an additional federal exigency requirement. There are no other separate writings.

## Holdings

The following holdings and explanations are those of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: seven direct joins throughout. The dissenters' review objections are not additional merits votes.

### 1. These suppression judgments satisfy the applicable finality requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1257 permits review under Cox's third practical-finality category where the highest state court has finally resolved the federal issue, further criminal proceedings remain, and later effective review for the State will be unavailable whether it wins or loses. These binding suppression determinations satisfy that route; the Court does not create a general exception for every interlocutory suppression order.

**Controlling explanation:** Cox Broadcasting distinguishes completion of all proceedings from final resolution of a federal issue in circumstances where later review cannot effectively occur. Labron's case remains at the pretrial suppression stage; Kilgore's suppression ruling follows conviction and requires further proceedings. In each, Pennsylvania's highest court has finally imposed the disputed federal condition. A later conviction would remove the Commonwealth's complaint about suppression, while an acquittal could not be appealed. The federal question therefore cannot simply await an ordinary final prosecution judgment. This is Cox's third category, not its different fourth category invoked merely because an evidentiary issue is important. The petitions' finality labels and the presence of a remand decide nothing by themselves. Finality also does not answer whether an adequate independent state ground defeats jurisdiction or whether discretionary review is warranted.

### 2. Federal review is available under Long in each case

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily to rest on federal law or to be interwoven with it and an adequate independent state ground is not clear, Long permits review absent a plain statement of an independently sufficient state basis. Labron's federal/state automobile analysis is interwoven without that clear independent basis; Kilgore expressly concludes that the search violated the Fourth Amendment. Both federal premises are reviewable, without forbidding Pennsylvania from applying a properly available independent state rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Long, retained in Arizona v. Evans, requires attention to the judgment's actual sources rather than a count of state citations. Labron invokes both constitutions and speaks of the Commonwealth's jurisprudence, but derives its exigency analysis through Carroll, Chambers and state automobile decisions treating federal search principles. Its reasoning does not clearly identify an additional state constitutional condition sufficient regardless of federal law. Kilgore repeatedly uses Fourth Amendment terms and expressly rests suppression on a Fourth Amendment violation; its federal ground is clearer still. The dissent's account of Pennsylvania's capacity and history of giving greater protection does not establish that these judgments already rest on an independent basis that forecloses correction. Sochor concerned an express sufficient state preservation ground; Middlebrooks involved actual independent state constitutional reasoning confirmed by state clarification. Neither makes every reference to state law independent. The Court corrects the federal rule and leaves the State free to use an actually independent, adequate ground through its proper procedures. It predicts no such result and resolves no unpresented preservation question.

### 3. Probable cause and ready mobility do not require a fresh emergency

**Holding and operative rule:** Police may search a readily mobile automobile without a warrant when probable cause supports belief that it contains evidence or contraband; the Fourth Amendment requires no additional showing of unforeseen circumstances, imminent departure or insufficient time to obtain a warrant. Police custody, the ability to guard the vehicle or an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant does not itself impose that extra condition. The object sought limits the search to places and containers capable of containing it.

**Controlling explanation:** Carroll and Chambers recognize automobile authority resting on mobility rather than a warrant-impracticability inquiry renewed for every search. Carney adds the reduced privacy associated with vehicles' regulated, public character. Johns confirms that police custody does not by itself extinguish authority to search on probable cause; its separate protection against unreasonable delay remains. The Commonwealth need not prove that officers lacked every earlier opportunity to seek a warrant or that guarded vehicles were about to escape. Those demands would replace the exception's established predicates with an additional urgency test. Probable cause remains indispensable, and Ross ties the permissible search to its object and the places where that object may be found. This vehicle rule supplies no independent authority to enter a home or protected curtilage, treat an immobile structure as an automobile, dispense with probable cause or exceed object-based scope. Carney's reservation concerning different residential uses and the independent requirements for access to protected premises remain intact.

### 4. The automobile rule applies to the reviewed searches

**Holding and operative rule:** The accepted probable cause and adjudicated automobile circumstances bring these searches within the exception for purposes of the federal additional-exigency ground on which the judgments rest. The Court reverses that ground in each case and remands; its application is neither an unconditional approval of every entry and search act nor an instruction merely to reconsider whether the rejected urgency requirement should remain.

**Controlling explanation:** In Labron, observed drug transactions and retrievals from the Lincoln's trunk supplied the accepted probable cause. The car was an ordinary street-parked automobile with the described apparently highway-legal exterior; the police's attempted impoundment revealed a failed start. That evidence does not establish when the failure was learned or a finding that the car had ceased to be an automobile within the exception. The Court applies the vehicle rule to the adjudicated setting without finding that the engine worked or treating appearance as a universal answer to known mechanical incapacity. The dispute over advance information does not justify the additional warrant-opportunity requirement. In Kilgore, the controlled-purchase circumstances and connection to the truck supplied probable cause accepted below despite the interrupted observation. Private parking, custody and available guards do not themselves supply the extra federal urgency condition. Lack of vehicle consent or visible contraband does not defeat an independently applicable automobile exception. This conclusion does not establish lawful crossing of a protected boundary, classify the farm location as outside curtilage, or turn house consent into truck consent. Those are distinct inquiries, not facts supplied by the automobile rule. The decision applies the exception to the reviewed searches while leaving genuinely different, properly available issues to lawful state procedure; it does not prescribe a new mechanical-condition or property-access hearing.

### 5. Reversal permits ordinary state proceedings within the federal holding

**Holding and operative rule:** Each judgment is reversed, and each case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's automobile and jurisdictional holdings. Pennsylvania may apply an actually adequate and independent state ground if properly available, and may address genuinely remaining scope or other distinct issues under its procedures; this mandate establishes neither their availability nor their merits and directs no automatic admission or prosecution outcome.

**Controlling explanation:** Correcting the federal error does not make the federal Constitution the ceiling of state protection. Long and Evans preserve genuine state independence without assuming it in the judgment under review. The remand also respects the distinction between a vehicle search's authority and a separate question about access, consent, execution or scope. Whren decided the authority for its ordinary initial stop, not every ensuing search; Wilson addressed dwelling-entry reasonableness, not an automatic justification for Kilgore's entry. Ornelas separates independent legal review from historical factfinding and supplies no missing finding here. Evans required further application because its own error-attribution predicate was unresolved; it does not compel a rule-only vacatur in every search case with an unanswered question. The present opinion applies the automobile exception to the reviewed extra-exigency issue, then leaves only legitimately remaining matters to the state courts. It does not restore a conviction, compel admission, reopen a forfeited claim or promise a particular independent state result.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn:** The Court applies the third practical-finality category to these binding suppression determinations and the State's inability to obtain later effective review. No general interlocutory-review rule is added.
- **Michigan v. Long:** Its federal/interwoven-ground inquiry is applied separately to Labron and Kilgore. A State's power to afford greater protection remains, but possible state authority is not proof of an actual independent judgment.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Its Long holding remains controlling. Its separate exclusionary-rule exception and unresolved clerical-error attribution do not authorize these searches or require the same factual remand in a different case.
- **Sochor and Middlebrooks:** Their actually sufficient independent preservation and state constitutional grounds remain respected; the Court distinguishes them from the grounds under review here.
- **Carroll v. United States and Chambers v. Maroney:** The established automobile exception is applied without a new case-specific warrant-impracticability requirement. Police control does not by itself require a different federal rule.
- **California v. Carney:** Mobility and reduced automobile privacy support the rule; its actual setting and reservation concerning differently situated residential uses remain. It creates no general authority to enter protected property.
- **United States v. Johns:** Custody alone does not eliminate vehicle-search authority. The Court does not remove Johns's separate protection against unreasonable delay or decide a delay claim here.
- **United States v. Ross:** Probable cause's object determines the permitted places and containers to search; it is not a license for a search beyond that object.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Independent review of ultimate constitutional probability questions, clear-error protection for historical facts and due weight to grounded inferences remain. The case supplies no new fact, waiver or probable-cause presumption.
- **Whren v. United States:** Objective authority for the initial stop remains separate from search, manner, scope and duration. Its unanimity does not supply an additional vote or factual premise here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas and Pinson:** The distinct protections governing dwelling entry and the particular technological examination of domestic information remain. Neither is a general automobile rule or establishes Kilgore's property boundary or lawful access.

No prior decision is overruled. The Court applies the inherited automobile exception to reject the added federal condition; it does not adopt a new general mechanical-defect, curtilage or premises-consent rule.

## Law After Decision

Ready mobility and probable cause support an automobile search without a separate showing that obtaining a warrant was impracticable. Custody, available guards and an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant do not alone create such a condition. The object sought continues to limit scope. The rule does not dispense with lawful authority to enter protected premises or convert an immobile structure into an automobile.

These two judgments cannot stand on the additional federal exigency requirement they applied. The Court's case-specific application does not establish actual operation of the Lincoln's engine, a universal rule resolving known mechanical incapacity, truck-owner consent, the outdoor extent of house consent or a curtilage finding. It also does not resurrect the Commonwealth's separate Labron privacy-interest objection as an established alternative basis; that objection's treatment below is not displaced by this holding.

Federal review remains bounded by §1257 finality and genuinely adequate independent state grounds. Cox's third category and Long/Evans govern for their stated reasons, not simply because a suppression order is important or cites federal law. Properly available independent state protection remains possible. No independent state outcome, evidentiary admission, conviction or further hearing is predetermined.

## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, dissenting from review and summary reversal:** Labron's opinion, read in the setting of Pennsylvania's decisions and Article I, §8, rests on an adequate independent state rule in their view. Its reliance on the Commonwealth's jurisprudence and White is substantive, not an incidental state citation accompanying an exclusively federal judgment. Federal search cases may describe the constitutional floor while the State imposes its own greater protection. They regard the state discussion, including the distinctions in White between automobile exigency and its separate Belton issue, and the state opinions' treatment of Pennsylvania protection as evidence that the judgment would remain independent of the federal answer. A disagreement within the state court over the wisdom or foundation of that rule does not itself make the ground federal.

Long's presumption begins with a judgment that fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven; these Justices would not extend it to a judgment they read as primarily state law merely because it lacks a particular verbal formula. Their actual Evans jurisdictional position reinforces the need for an affirmative basis for federal intervention. Cox finality cannot cure an adequate independent state ground. They therefore would deny Labron's petition and would not reach its federal automobile merits.

Kilgore is different: its express Fourth Amendment conclusion makes the federal ground much clearer. They nevertheless would deny discretionary review. The surrounding state jurisprudence makes renewed suppression on an independent Pennsylvania basis the expected result in their judgment, so a federal correction and remand would consume both courts' resources without a useful change in outcome. That is their reason for restraint, not an entered future state decision or a jurisdictional finding that Kilgore already rests exclusively on state law. Stevens's institutional caution in Carney supports examining whether a vehicle actually warrants intervention, rather than treating every asserted automobile error as requiring immediate review.

They do not turn the Lincoln's failed start into a new mechanical-incapacity holding or the farm's private location into an adjudicated curtilage claim. Nor do they adopt an extra federal exigency requirement as an affirmative merits position. Johns's distinction between custody and continuing vehicle-search authority remains consistent with their review objections. Their dissent concerns the authority and utility of deciding these cases, not a separately stated federal rule sustaining every suppression rationale below.

## Procedure After Action

Both petitions are granted and the federal merits are resolved by summary reversal. Both cases return to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court for proceedings consistent with the Court's decision. The federal added-exigency ground has been rejected and the automobile exception applied at the stated scope; it is not left open for reconsideration under the same additional requirement.

Any genuinely remaining issue must be addressed through proper state procedures and within the mandate. The Court does not require a new hearing, create preservation for a previously unavailable claim, decide a distinct access theory's merits, prescribe a state constitutional result or direct automatic admission or conviction.

## Event

United States v. Winstar Corp., No. 95-865. Argued April 24, 1996; decided July 1, 1996. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Federal Circuit, 64 F.3d 1531, which on August 30, 1995 affirmed partial summary judgments on contractual liability after certified interlocutory review under §1292. Damages remain undetermined.

The consolidated disputes concern the Glendale–Broward transaction in 1981, Winstar/United–Windom in 1984 and Statesman in 1988. The institutions assumed failing institutions' liabilities in transactions containing particular accounting and capital arrangements. The questions are whether the integrated writings establish actual authorized promises, whether later withdrawal of the promised treatment breaches them, and whether unmistakability, reserved sovereign powers, sovereign acts or impossibility defeat contractual liability.

FIRREA imposed new capital requirements and limited supervisory goodwill. OTS separately treated capital credits as qualifying supervisory goodwill through 12 C.F.R. §567.1(w); that exact treatment was not itself statutory wording referring to capital credits. Congress's authority to regulate, the institutions' duty to obey valid law and the Government's financial responsibility under its promises are distinct questions. The case does not adjudicate FIRREA's validity or a damages amount.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Federal Circuit's contractual-liability judgment is affirmed. The cases return for determination of contract-specific causation, compensable losses, damages and genuinely remaining issues. Congress and the regulators remain free to enforce valid law.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 8–1.** The same vote applies to Glendale's undertaking, Winstar's undertaking, Statesman's goodwill undertaking and Statesman's separate capital credit.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Ginsburg, who would vacate the present liability affirmance and remand for separate construction of the undertakings under unmistakability, without directing judgment for the Government or a trial.

The affirmed liability is partial contractual liability, not a fixed monetary award. Causal loss, recoverable categories, mitigation, applicable periods and amounts remain for ordinary proceedings. No goodwill balance or capital credit equals damages automatically; no finding establishes that the regulatory change caused an institution's failure. No injunction, invalidation of FIRREA, specific performance against valid law, solvency guarantee or takings judgment is entered. The unadjudicated takings claims and Winstar's implied-in-fact alternative are not silently decided.

## Holdings

### 1. Actual contracts and actual authority are required; these writings establish the identified promises

**Holding and authority:** The Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. §1491(a)(1), provides contract jurisdiction but does not create a substantive promise. A claimant must establish an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking, not an obligation imposed only for fairness; all nine join that requirement. Eight join the holding that these integrated transaction writings establish express promises of the particular accounting and capital treatment, with the financial risk of its withdrawal, subject to their actual qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Hercules requires identification of the duty actually undertaken and preserves genuine authorized warranties despite uncertain eventual damages. Romein prevents converting regulation alone into an immutable contract; Cisneros requires enforcing the bargain with its real limits. These institutions rely on more than a generally available accounting rule. The assistance agreements preserve contemporaneous resolutions and letters that identify the treatment offered in exchange for acquiring liabilities. The transaction-specific forward terms give content to that exchange. Winstar's en banc judgment rests on an express agreement, not solely on the trial court's implied-in-fact theory. Statesman's express capital credit is a separate promise from its goodwill allowance. The Government's present-permission construction must account for those incorporated benefits and their particular durations, rather than treating approval as the entire bargain. The Court finds the specified undertaking, not insurance against every future regulation. General assistance terms, changing-regulation language and lawful-action clauses remain relevant; the precise benefit is read with them, not erased by them or enlarged into a perpetual exemption. The separate doctrinal opinions differ over unmistakability's threshold operation, but all eight find the actual financial undertaking sufficiently established in these writings.

### Transaction-specific operative terms

The operative terms are distinct:

- **Glendale:** The November 19, 1981 Supervisory Action Agreement preserves contemporaneous Board/FSLIC resolutions and letters rather than superseding them. Resolution 81-710 requires a satisfactory independent accountant's opinion describing goodwill, justifying purchase accounting and substantiating amounts and amortization, with compliance with Memorandum R-31b. The approved accountant treatment allocates $18 million over twelve years and $716.666 million over forty years, straight line. The lower judgment found the accounting condition satisfied; the Court does not turn that finding into a concession or treat every dollar as forty-year goodwill.
- **Winstar:** The integrated agreement, Resolution 84-363 and July 13, 1984 letter permit straight-line amortization of purchase-accounting intangibles over a period not exceeding thirty-five years for regulatory reporting. The maximum is permission, not a required period or a promised damages term. Those accounting forbearances are not acceptable in financial statements required to follow GAAP. Ongoing reserve/net-worth records and regulatory reporting remain required. The Net Worth Maintenance obligation concerns the required capital level, while the agreement's accounting terms define what counts toward it; a change in level is not identical to withdrawal of a promised component.
- **Statesman goodwill:** The March 11, 1988 agreement and Resolution 88-169 permit straight-line amortization not exceeding twenty-five years, subject to a satisfactory accountant's analysis within ninety days describing intangibles and substantiating amounts and methods. The lower judgment found the condition satisfied. Approximately $25.8 million of acquisition goodwill is distinct from the capital credit.
- **Statesman capital credit:** The express $26 million credit consists of $21 million of the FSLIC contribution and $5 million debenture principal. It is credited to the regulatory capital account and constitutes regulatory capital under the identified Insurance Regulation §561.13 definition. Its amount and scope do not borrow the goodwill undertaking's twenty-five-year maximum.

### 2. Accounting priorities, reservations and duration qualify the promises

**Holding and authority:** Eight join the construction that the incorporated specific benefits retain contractual significance alongside ordinary regulatory evolution and the assistance agreements' general terms. That construction does not delete a limitation, make a maximum period mandatory or establish that a termination event occurred. The following qualifications govern the liability holding and any later relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government correctly insists that the entire writing be read. A priority clause among agency instruments cannot prevent Congress from legislating; a general reference to amended regulations also cannot be read in isolation from the particular preserved consideration. The specific accounting undertaking explains what was purchased, while compliance and lawful-action provisions preserve obedience to valid law. Ordinary assistance periods do not automatically reduce every separately specified forward accounting benefit to that shorter period. Conversely, the long accounting period does not eliminate a proved termination or dictate the duration of compensable loss. The Court affirms the promise and breach at the scope established by the integrated documents. It does not invoke a general surviving-indemnity clause, infer that a qualifying sale or insurance event occurred, or guarantee payment through every stated maximum. A contractual good-faith-contest proviso is preserved as written but supplies no finding that a contest was made or that public law permits noncompliance. Those distinctions answer both an unlimited regulatory-guarantee construction and an automatic cancellation of the particular negotiated financial undertaking.

### Operative accounting, compliance and termination qualifications

**Accounting hierarchy and reserved powers.** Winstar §10 begins with GAAP for going-concern savings-and-loan computations except where the agreement, applicable regulations or contemporaneous Board approval requires otherwise. Ambiguity is resolved consistently with the regulations and approval; when those conflict, the Board's transaction resolution or action controls. The section also includes subsequent Board/FASB clarification, interpretation and amendment, with Board requirements controlling conflicts with FASB. Both priority and change language remain operative.

The July 13 letter reserves statutory powers except enforcement of its enumerated waived requirements and their authorizing statutory provisions insofar as waived. The referenced benefits concern direct net-worth credit for the FSLIC cash contribution; the amortization just described; loan-discount recognition by a level-yield method assuming contractual payments for fifteen years and repayment of the balance at that point, or another level-yield method described in R memoranda or other accounting documents acceptable under GAAP; and dividend limits. The dividend waiver permits sixty, seventy and sixty percent of net income for fiscal years beginning July 1, 1985, 1986 and 1987 respectively, while forbidding dividends that reduce net worth below the level §563.13 requires for institutions insured at least twenty years. These ancillary terms identify the reservation's scope and add no separate damages claim. Powers outside those waivers remain reserved; the exception is neither an unconditional cancellation right nor surrender of Congress's authority.

Statesman §17 similarly preserves agreement/approval exceptions to GAAP, resolves ambiguity by the regulations and transaction approval, and gives that approval priority over conflicting regulations. Its changing-accounting language refers to Board changes, amendments, clarifications and interpretations, including the proposal at 52 Fed. Reg. 39112, its staff, FASB and the stated AICPA-successor reference; Board interpretation controls a conflict with FASB or AICPA. Real-estate valuation separately follows the proposed regulations at 52 Fed. Reg. 39070, those regulations as finally adopted by the Board, or any successor regulations then in effect. It is not simply Winstar's clause under another name.

**Compliance, duration and termination.** Glendale's assistance agreement ordinarily terminates after ten years, when FSLIC payment obligations cease. Appointment of a receiver or other official to liquidate Glendale, or termination of Glendale's or its successor's FSLIC-insured status, terminates the agreement and all FSLIC obligations at that event. Contemporaneous resolutions and letters remain preserved; modifications require written agreement by the parties or successors. The agreement requires no unlawful action or inaction. The particular forward accounting benefit is construed with, not equated to, the ordinary assistance/payment term.

Winstar §13(a) ordinarily ends the agreement after two years except as otherwise specifically provided, or at a different date agreed in writing. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the resulting association, or cessation of its insured-account status, separately terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations at that event. Section 15 requires no unlawful action or inaction. Section 13(b) concerns limited continuing indemnity for the liabilities/claims and related or prosecution litigation expenses it identifies, conditioned on litigation at termination and continued observance of the agreement's conditions, covenants, cooperation and best-efforts duties, with recoveries payable over under its referenced limits. It is not a general continuation of every contractual claim; this judgment does not decide an interaction with a receiver event or find a qualifying indemnity claim.

Statesman §20 requires the acquiring association and its subsidiaries, affiliates and service corporations materially to comply with applicable public laws, regulations, orders and restrictions governing property and business, but does not itself require compliance while applicability is contested in good faith. The proviso establishes neither an actual contest nor an exemption from valid public law. Section 19 ordinarily supplies a five-year term or a different written agreed date, with its specific-exception language and separate §16 sale provision. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the acquiring association or cessation of its insured-account status terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations. Its lawful-action term remains. FIRREA's enactment within the five-year period defeats only an assertion that ordinary expiration had already eliminated every undertaking; it decides neither other triggers nor recoverable duration.

Statesman §19(b) directs distribution/payment of the Special Reserve Account's net credit or debit at termination under the agreement. Section 19(c)'s identified liability/claim and related litigation indemnities require litigation at termination and continuing covenants, cooperation, reporting and best efforts; recoveries must be paid over under the applicable limitations, and continuation remains subject to the liquidation/insurance terminating proviso. Section 19(d) requires action on reports, applications, requests and other submissions actually received by termination that require FSLIC approval, consent or action, with cooperation and final-accounting treatment. None is a general survival clause or a finding of an eligible claim or pending submission in this case.

**Statesman's separate sale conditions.** During seven years, a defined Sale by the acquiring association of any of its own assets, or of capital stock of a subsidiary or affiliate formed to conduct its operations or hold its assets, requires prior written approval and prompt payment of an agreed percentage of net proceeds, capped at fifty percent. In calculating net proceeds, an appropriate return of the acquirer's investment is allocated in proportion to the assets sold relative to total assets. Sale aggregates sales consummated or agreed during ninety days with an aggregate sales price of $20 million. An unapproved defined Sale during five years terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations immediately after consummation notwithstanding §19; approval lies in FSLIC's sole discretion. The exclusion concerns transfers to an acquirer affiliate of stock or assets and liabilities of Federated Financial, Combined Mortgage Corporation, Homestead Mortgage Corporation or Perpetual Financial Corporation, not every affiliate transaction. Seven-year approval/proceeds duties and the five-year termination consequence remain distinct. No such Sale, approval, excluded transfer or termination is found.

### 3. Actual authority supports these financial undertakings without disabling legislation

**Holding and authority:** Eight hold that the transaction-effective contracting and assistance powers, exercised through the actual resolutions and approvals, authorized the identified financial undertakings. No separate delegation to prevent Congress from changing the law is required for this ordinary monetary responsibility, because the contracts do not confer that veto. General agency power alone would not cure an undertaking outside actual authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Hercules and Sutton require actual authority for express and implied-in-fact promises alike. Former §1725(c)(3) authorized FSLIC to contract, and the transaction-effective §1729(f) powers authorized the assistance arrangements with their statutory conditions. The authority supporting Glendale included assistance to prevent default or restore normal operation and assistance facilitating merger or assumption of a defaulting institution's liabilities. Contributions and guarantees were limited to the amount found reasonably necessary to save liquidation costs unless continued operation was found essential to adequate community savings or home-financing services. The actual transaction resolutions supplied the required determinations and approvals; no failure of such a condition is established. Winstar and Statesman likewise rest on their effective assistance powers and approvals, not merely a later general description of agency mission. The Government's argument that an agency cannot surrender Congress's regulatory power addresses a different undertaking. Congress remains free to change capital law, and the institutions remain subject to it. Lynch preserves the Government's contractual obligations; the authority and retained-powers cases do not erase an authorized financial promise because the promisor is sovereign. No hypothetical appropriation or delegation defect supplies a defense, and the Court finds no authority for a regulatory injunction or a promise inferred solely from assistance policy.

### 4. Withdrawal breaches the allocated undertaking; assumed risk defeats discharge

**Holding and authority:** Eight hold that withdrawal of the promised treatment breached these actual financial undertakings. Even assuming the relevant sovereign action is public and general, the sovereign-acts defense and ordinary impossibility do not discharge the change-of-law risk the contracts allocated to the Government. This sufficient holding does not depend on whether every monetary claim escapes unmistakability or whether the challenged measure fails public/general classification.

**Controlling explanation:** Horowitz prevents ordinary contracting liability from disabling public and general sovereign action; it does not place the Government in a better contractual position than a private promisor on a risk that promisor assumed. Ordinary impossibility therefore still requires examination of the bargain's risk allocation. Here the specified regulatory treatment was the subject of the exchange, and its withdrawal is the risk attached to the particular promises. Maintaining legislative power does not make that financial undertaking disappear. Bowen's retained power to amend its statutory arrangement does not prove that every negotiated allocation of monetary consequences is void. The institutions must obey the new law; liability does not compel prohibited reporting or specific performance. FIRREA's supervisory-goodwill limits and OTS's separate capital-credit treatment defeat the promised treatment, but their validity is not the issue. The Government cannot use the occurrence of the allocated risk alone to excuse the contractual consequence. Nor does every later regulation breach a contract: the result depends on the particular duty and qualifications established here. Actual loss, its connection to that breach and the amount recoverable remain different questions.

### 5. Liability affirmance leaves causation and damages for ordinary adjudication

**Holding and authority:** Eight affirm partial liability and remand for contract-specific causation, compensable loss, mitigation, applicable contractual periods and damages. They do not treat nominal goodwill or capital-credit figures as awards, promise a particular recovery or direct a hearing or trial regardless of what remains disputed.

**Controlling explanation:** A breach of an authorized promise is different from proof of every consequence alleged to follow. Hercules preserves genuine contract remedies while requiring the actual undertaking; it supplies no automatic causal chain or fixed measure here. Each institution must establish the loss recoverable under its own bargain and ordinary contract principles, with applicable defenses and qualifications. The distinct goodwill periods, capital-credit terms, regulatory obligations and any properly proved termination matter to that inquiry; one component cannot borrow another's amount or duration. Nothing in the liability judgment finds that the regulatory change caused insolvency or guarantees that the entire remaining goodwill balance would have been usable. Mitigation and other genuinely remaining issues belong below. The en banc express-contract judgment makes it unnecessary to decide Winstar's implied-in-fact alternative independently, and unadjudicated takings claims receive no merits disposition. Congress's authority and the validity of FIRREA remain undisturbed.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Hercules:** Its actual-authorized-duty requirement controls. The Court distinguishes the absent reimbursement undertaking there from the specific integrated promises here, while preserving its limits on fairness-based liability and automatic damages.
- **Sutton and Merrill:** Actual governmental authority remains necessary; neither the Tucker Act nor equitable appeal supplies missing assent or authority. No particular approval defect is found here.
- **Romein:** Regulation governing a relationship is not itself an immutable contractual obligation. These holdings rest on the additional transaction writings and incorporated promises.
- **Cisneros:** The obligation is enforced with its real qualifications, not enlarged into an unconditional payment or regulatory guarantee.
- **United States v. Texas:** An existing obligation is distinct from a mechanism governing its enforcement. It supplies no damages amount or formula in this case.
- **Lynch and Perry:** Genuine government contractual obligations remain legally significant despite sovereign status. This decision preserves legislation and applies the financial undertakings actually made; it does not adopt a general power to enjoin legislative change.
- **Horowitz:** Public/general sovereign treatment does not itself answer ordinary assumed-risk impossibility. The eight-Justice holding rests on allocated risk, not the additional three-Justice substantial-effect theory.
- **Bowen:** Its retained-power and promise-construction principles remain. The Court does not convert the statutory arrangement there into a rule cancelling every express financial-risk undertaking here.
- **Merrion, United States Trust and Home Telephone:** Retained governmental powers and the particular commitments at issue must be distinguished. No majority adopts a universal rule making the sovereign-construction doctrine irrelevant to all money claims.
- **OPM v. Richmond:** Authority and appropriations limits are not erased by monetary relief; no unsupported defect or general equitable right to payment is found.

No earlier decision is overruled. The additional unmistakability and substantial-effect explanations have only the joins stated in the topology; they do not independently alter controlling doctrine.

## Law After Decision

An actual authorized promise of identified treatment can allocate the financial risk that valid later law prevents performance. These incorporated agreements did so, with their contract-specific conditions, and the Government is liable for the breach. Even assuming public/general sovereign action, ordinary impossibility does not excuse that allocated risk. Congress retains its legislative authority, and the institutions must comply with valid law.

The decision establishes neither a promise from regulation alone nor compensation whenever regulation changes. Calling a claim damages does not prove formation, authority, assumed risk or a recovery amount. The four-Justice limited-inapplicability account of unmistakability is not a complete Court framework; neither is the three-Justice account applying the canon and finding it met. The Chief Justice joins the narrower common financial-risk holding. The objective substantial-contractual-effect alternative has three votes, not five, and is not controlling.

The actual contract terms govern future proceedings: distinct sums and maximum amortization periods, accounting priorities and changing rules, compliance qualifications, statutory authority, assistance duration, sale restrictions and applicable termination or surviving-claim conditions. No trigger event is presumed. Liability does not establish causation of institutional failure, a goodwill-balance award, a guaranteed recovery period or a takings entitlement. The ordinary damages process remains.

## Separate Writings

**Souter's Part V, joined by Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer:** The unmistakability doctrine protects against an effective surrender of sovereign authority, but these particular financial undertakings leave Congress free to regulate. Lynch's recognition of government obligations and the distinctions in Bowen and Merrion require identifying what the promise and remedy would actually do. A demand for money does not automatically avoid the doctrine: repayment that effectively supplies a tax or comparable sovereign exemption can implicate it. Here, however, the authorized transaction allocated financial risk rather than immunizing institutions from regulation. The Government cannot obtain the acquired liabilities' assumption on the particular promised terms and then erase the ordinary contractual consequence merely by invoking its power to change law. These four distinguish the protected exercise of sovereign power from the limited obligation actually undertaken; they do not declare all damages suits outside unmistakability. Stone does not join this complete threshold account, and Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas instead apply the doctrine.

**Souter's Part VI, joined by Stevens and Breyer:** Independently of assumed risk, these three would reject the sovereign-acts protection where release from the Government's own contractual obligations is a substantial effect of the challenged measure rather than an incidental consequence of public regulation. The inquiry is objective. They do not require subjective bad faith, a predominant contractual-avoidance motive or predominant contractual effect. Inclusion in a broad financial reform statute does not itself make direct release from the promised accounting/capital performance incidental. The particular disallowance of that treatment has the required substantial effect for them. They add no quantitative industry-impact finding, condemn no legislative motive and do not classify every FIRREA provision as non-general. O'Connor withholds this ground; the other affirmers rely on the independent assumed-risk conclusion. The additional theory has three votes.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring:** Unmistakability applies and is satisfied. Sovereign contracting displaces the ordinary presumption that a promisor undertakes not to disable performance by its own acts; courts should not lightly infer a surrender or financial undertaking against legislative change. But the unmistakable subject of these bargains was the identified future accounting and capital treatment. The Government need not utter a second promise that it will keep the first before that actual promise has effect. Incorporation, priority, duration, reservation and compliance terms must all be read, not replaced by a general rescue-policy assumption. They find the particular risk assignment clear even under the sovereign-contract canon, while recognizing immediate regulatory permission could form a different bargain in another case.

Actual statutory assistance authority supports the financial commitments. No agency veto over Congress is needed, and no implied-in-law indemnity or universal governmental insurance is recognized. Clear assumption of this risk defeats the asserted sovereign-acts and impossibility discharge. They join the Court's contract, authority, common discharge and remedy parts, not the claim that unmistakability is inapplicable or the separate objective substantial-effect route. Damages still require proof under the individual contracts.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring:** Sovereignty determines whether Congress may change the law; the established contract determines who bears the financial consequences of withdrawing the promised treatment. These express authorized undertakings supply the relevant risk allocation. The doctrines protecting legislative authority from implied surrender cannot erase that actual monetary responsibility, but regulation alone would supply no contract. He joins the Court's common parts and its damages remand without choosing a complete threshold rule between the other affirmers' unmistakability accounts. He does not join the substantial-effect alternative or find bad faith. The result neither requires compensation for every legal change nor surrenders sovereign power or authorizes specific performance contrary to a valid statute. Causation, the losses covered and their amounts remain contract-specific.

**Ginsburg, dissenting from present liability affirmance:** The existence of written assistance arrangements and valuable regulatory permissions does not itself resolve the alleged promise to bear future legislative risk. Unmistakability is a construction rule that must inform identification of the undertaking before a court declares that risk was assigned. Immediate permission to use favorable accounting can be valuable consideration even if no longer-term protection against legislative change was promised. Describing the bargain as risk shifting and the remedy as damages does not answer that prior question.

She requires separate attention to Glendale's incorporation and assistance term, Winstar's reporting permission, regulatory-change and reservation language, and Statesman's distinct goodwill and express capital credit with their own conditions. She does not deny the actual $26 million credit wording or hold that no contract or authorized warranty can exist. Actual Hercules requires the real duty; it does not supply the further continuing promise merely because damages would be lawful if such a promise were proved. General agency authority and partial liability findings reached under an insufficient construction criterion do not settle that dispute.

She would vacate the present interlocutory affirmance and remand for bounded reconsideration under unmistakability for each writing set and each Statesman benefit. She would not order ultimate judgment for the Government, declare a specific delegation absent, adopt a broad sovereign-acts discharge, require a new trial or decide damages and takings. Her disagreement is with present affirmance of the asserted continuing risk undertaking, not a categorical denial of government contract liability or a refusal to recognize express terms.

## Procedure After Action

The Federal Circuit's partial contractual-liability judgment stands. The cases return for ordinary contract-specific causation, damages and genuinely remaining proceedings consistent with the established promises and qualifications. No amount, hearing date, trial requirement or final recovery period is prescribed.

The institutions remain subject to valid regulatory law. The mandate does not compel regulatory forbearance, invalidate FIRREA, award compensation on an unadjudicated takings claim or independently decide Winstar's implied-in-fact alternative.
