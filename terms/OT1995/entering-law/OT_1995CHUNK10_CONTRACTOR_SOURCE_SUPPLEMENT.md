## Umbehr, No.94-1654

### Jurisdiction, judgment and actual contract

The petition invokes 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) after a §1983 action within 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1343 and an appeal under §1291. The Tenth Circuit judgment was entered January 4, 1995, with rehearing denied February 10. Petition pp.i–ii and 1–2 identifies the petitioners as Heiser and Spencer in their official capacities as county commissioners. The pending merits review does not reopen the individual-capacity immunity judgment or McClure's separate causation judgment.

The full appellate opinion, 44 F.3d 876, establishes these particulars:

- The operative 1985 contract, succeeding a 1981 arrangement, allowed Umbehr to haul residential refuse for cities that endorsed and ratified it. The county did not itself purchase trash-hauling services under that contract. Cities were not obliged to participate and could opt out on ninety days' notice. The agreement automatically renewed for one-year terms unless a party gave sixty days' notice of termination or ninety days' notice of an intention to renegotiate. The county and participating cities agreed not to contract with another residential-refuse provider while the contract operated. These are distinct notice provisions, not immediate termination at whim.
- Umbehr served six of seven cities from 1985 to 1991. He criticized landfill rates, charges for public records, possible Open Meetings Act violations, and alleged road-and-bridge mismanagement in commission meetings and newspapers. Those criticisms are described as allegations and speech; the courts did not adjudicate their truth in resolving this motion.
- The attempted 1990 termination was ineffective, and the contract renewed. The valid January 1991 termination followed another two-to-one vote. Heiser and Spencer supported termination; McClure opposed the earlier action and was no longer in office at the effective termination. Umbehr later made direct contracts with five of the six cities; the county made no replacement hauling contract. The successor arrangements do not by themselves establish zero injury.

The district court **assumed solely for summary judgment** that the speech would have been protected had Umbehr been an employee, that the comments motivated the termination votes, and that he suffered injury. It did not find those propositions after trial. It granted judgment on a categorical contractor-status ground and, alternatively, individual qualified immunity. It also found insufficient evidence of McClure's causation. The Tenth Circuit rejected the categorical constitutional bar, applied Pickering to contractor retaliation, affirmed individual immunity and McClure's disposition, and remanded the surviving official-capacity claims. The appellate court separately held standing supported by alleged monetary harm and loss of valuable contract benefits, including exclusivity, centralized negotiation and notice protection; a property entitlement is not a prerequisite to the speech claim. See 44 F.3d 877–879, 883–884 and nn.1–3, 6.

The lower court did not find damages, impose liability, or direct a particular award or reinstatement. Its opinion notes that damages may be small and difficult to prove. Personal legislative immunity and official-capacity liability are distinct: the appellate opinion invokes Kentucky v. Graham's entity-based treatment of the latter and does not allow individual immunities to become entity immunities by changing the caption.

### Actual competing submissions and record limits

Petition pp.i–ii asks whether Pickering protection extends to terminable government contracts, how the test should be adapted, and whether government affirmative defenses survive. Petitioners' merits brief pp.12–20 distinguishes retrospective criticism from patronage affiliation and broad prospective speech bans. It emphasizes the government's retained responsibility for services, the contractor's day-to-day independence, and termination as an important means of control. It invokes Mt. Healthy to oppose an automatic tenure rule and argues that the employee/contractor distinction can matter to managerial needs. Its assertion that protection makes services ungovernable is an argument, not a record finding.

Respondent's merits brief pp.13–19 argues that an independent contractor remains a citizen protected against economic retaliation, seeks demanding review of viewpoint-based retaliation, and argues that ordinary workplace disruption interests may be absent outside an employment setting. He asks for affirmance and a remand limited to motive and legitimate independent reasons, contending the speech is protected under any applicable standard. That is a litigant's requested limitation on remand, not a finding already supplied by the lower judgment. The difference between his stricter proposal and the appellate Pickering rule is material.

The commissioners' petition also describes disputes over landfill charges and related state litigation. Its description of arrearages or misconduct investigations is advocacy and does not establish an actual reason for the challenged votes. Motive, public-concern characterization, operational effects, same-decision proof and injury must retain their separate source status. Possession of a possible lawful ground is not proof that the decision would have occurred without protected speech.

### Conflict and available legal issues for fresh validation

The brief's “no material conflict” statement is inaccurate. The Tenth Circuit expressly acknowledged conflict with the Seventh Circuit's LaFalce/Triad/Downtown Auto Parks approach and decisions from other circuits. It rejected generalized assumptions that contractors always depend less on government than employees and considered Rutan's extension to lesser employment benefits material. Its analysis also distinguishes public-issue retaliation from political affiliation. This conflict and the scope of an existing renewing relationship, rather than initial competitive bids, must be preserved. A narrow remand retaining ordinary speech, causation and government-interest defenses is a supported litigation possibility; so are the parties' opposing threshold and stricter-protection positions. No result is assigned here.

## O'Hare Truck Service, No.95-191

### Jurisdiction, pleadings and separate claims

Review lies under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Seventh Circuit's February 10, 1995 judgment, 47 F.3d 883, rehearing denied March 3. The petition's question is the First Amendment consequence of terminating business because the contractor supported the incumbent's opponent. The lower court also rejected procedural due process; that separate holding is not enlarged into the submitted First Amendment question.

The case was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6). The following facts are **well-pleaded allegations accepted for that motion**, not established findings:

- The police maintained a rotation list of towing businesses, calling each in turn and advancing to the next if one could not respond. The system existed since at least 1965; the petition alleges O'Hare's participation over that period.
- Northlake did not pay O'Hare or make a direct towing-services contract; vehicle owners paid the charges. This is recurring access to government-controlled referrals, not an assumed procurement contract paid from municipal funds.
- Successive mayors allegedly assured the business that satisfactory service would preserve its place. Before the 1993 election, the mayor's committee requested a contribution. Gratzianna refused and supported the opposing candidate; the petition also identifies opponent posters in O'Hare's business. After the incumbent won, the police chief allegedly removed O'Hare at the mayor's direction.
- The complaint alleged no statute, ordinance or written list rule creating entitlement. The lower court rejected implied-contract/informal-rule entitlement under Illinois law. That property question is distinct from the First Amendment theory; lack of a property right alone is not the same proposition as a lawful retaliatory reason.

The full appellate opinion, including notes, is 47 F.3d 883–886. The district court dismissal was 843 F. Supp. 1231. The appellate court followed its own contractor precedents, expressly declined to extend Elrod/Branti/Rutan, and affirmed both claims. Its First Amendment rationale rested on perceived lower dependence, procurement litigation burdens and the view that any extension should come from the Supreme Court. It acknowledged contrary Fifth and Tenth Circuit approaches. There is therefore a concrete conflict, contrary to the neutral brief's generic “no material conflict” line.

### Strongest recorded positions and possible scope

The petition pp.3–6 and 12–18 frames the injury as economic retaliation for refusing support and choosing an opponent. It argues that the ordinary unconstitutional-conditions and patronage cases cannot turn on formal employment status and distinguishes Umbehr's public-criticism theory from political association. The City merits brief pp.5–11 invokes the traditional role of patronage, contractor independence and other clients, the risk of turning every unsuccessful public bidder into a constitutional plaintiff, and the government's ability to organize services efficiently. It cites Scalia's Rutan dissent and LaFalce, Sweeney, Horn, Triad and Downtown Auto Parks. Its claims of lesser economic burden or neutral service authority are arguments; the Rule 12(b)(6) posture cannot convert them into contrary facts defeating the accepted retaliation allegation.

The existing referral relationship, refusal to contribute, affirmative opposing support, and possible distinction between affiliation demands and speech-performance disputes must remain separate. Elrod and Branti contain a functional exception where political affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance of the position; no source here establishes that partisan affiliation is an appropriate qualification for this towing referral. A remand on the First Amendment claim would not itself establish motive, municipal liability, personal immunity, a damages amount or a property entitlement, and would not automatically reopen the separate due-process judgment. Neutral qualifications, service defects and a properly proved same-decision defense remain different from a categorical right to demand partisan support.

## Date-eligible authority map common to the two cases

These are authority inputs, not a commitment table:

- **Pickering, 391 U.S. 563**, supplies public-concern speech protection balanced against the government's service interests in the employment relation. **Connick, 461 U.S. 138**, assesses content, form and context and permits a genuine operational-interest showing; personal offense at criticism is not itself the whole inquiry. **Givhan, 439 U.S. 410**, preserves relevant private speech protection. **Perry v. Sindermann, 408 U.S. 593**, separates absence of tenure or entitlement from unconstitutional reasons for withholding a benefit.
- **Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274**, separates the claimant's showing that protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor from the government's preponderance showing that the same decision would have occurred absent it. Protection creates no automatic continued-contract right.
- **Elrod, 427 U.S. 347**, and **Branti, 445 U.S. 507**, concern political affiliation dismissals; Branti's functional exception asks whether affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance. **Rutan, 497 U.S. 62**, applies patronage protection to promotion, transfer, recall and hiring; its employee setting and the competing dissent remain relevant to extension. A broad proposition about contractors is not already settled by those cases. Full writing/joinder verification of a proposed Rutan-specific Justice row belongs to the fresh modeling source check; this supplement does not certify unexamined historical joins.
- **Waters (simulated May 31, 1994)**: the speech-law slice supplies O'Connor's objective-reasonableness plurality, Souter and Ginsburg's joins, Scalia's broader genuine-ground concurrence joined by Kennedy and Thomas, and Stevens's actual-speech dissent. The explicit common safe harbor and unreasonable-false-account limit control only at their stated level. No comprehensive five-Justice investigation framework exists. These prior positions may inform the scope of any contractor analogy but do not decide whether such an analogy applies.
- **National Treasury Employees Union (simulated February 22, 1995)**: Stevens's prospective-burden holding has Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer among its joins, together with Stone. It demands justification commensurate with a broad prospective restriction's actual reach, but does not automatically substitute that context for individualized contract termination. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas cannot be assigned the majority's complete rationale without consulting the supplied earlier public positions.
- **Kentucky v. Graham, 473 U.S. 159**, and **Monell, 436 U.S. 658**, distinguish entity/official-capacity claims from individual immunities and retain independent municipal-liability requirements. Neither is a finding that every element of recovery has been proved here.
