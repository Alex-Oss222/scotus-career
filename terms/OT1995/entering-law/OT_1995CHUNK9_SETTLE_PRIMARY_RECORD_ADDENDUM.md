## What is and is not recovered about the outlines

The first proposed outline is titled **“The Life of Jesus Christ”** (the district memorandum also uses “Life of Jesus Christ”). The Sixth Circuit says a second outline, tendered April 3, was titled **“A Scientific and Historical Approach to Jesus Christ.”** Petition App. 2–3, 20; PDF pp. 42–43, 60. Neither complete outline, its headings, source list, or substantive paragraphs appears in the recovered filings or appendix. Thus the original limitation on outline contents remains after examining the original cert-stage documents. It is no longer merely a limitation of the published appellate opinion, but it still does not establish that the underlying exhibits are irretrievable.

Separately, the petition says Settle's father proposed a compromise about the **“psychological and scientific aspects of Jesus Christ,”** which Ramsey and Evans rejected as **“a play on words.”** Petition p. 5, citing J.A. 83 (PDF p. 15). That is counsel's account of a proposed compromise and cited testimony, not the text or exact title of the April 3 outline. Do not merge the two descriptions into a fabricated document. The appellate majority's reference to papers about the transfiguration is its explanatory illustration, not a reproduced finding that Settle's outline contained that subject. Petition App. 8 (PDF p. 48).

The petition further states that Settle eventually tendered her completed paper on “The Life of Jesus Christ” and received zero without Ramsey reading it. Petition p. 6, citing J.A. 164–179, 186, 220–231a (PDF p. 16). This is the petition's record account; the actual finished paper is not reproduced. The recovered district decision states that the zero followed noncompliance, without supplying the paper's contents. App. 22 (PDF p. 62).

## Permission, timing and the competing record accounts

1. **Initial procedure:** March 15–22 was the sign-up/change period. After removal of the sheet on March 22, the Sixth Circuit says permission was required to change topics. The district memorandum says students could still change after notifying Ramsey; the opposition more specifically says notification, specific approval, and an explanation were required. Outlines were due March 26. The district memorandum and opposition say no changes after that date except modification of issues within a topic. Petition App. 2, 20; opposition pp. 3–4, citing J.A. 37–38, 42 and the assignment pages (PDF pp. 12–13). The initial listing of Drama does not establish a separately documented express approval of Drama.

2. **The first tender:** The petition acknowledges that Settle decided over the weekend to change from Drama and did not discuss it with Ramsey until handing in the March 26 outline. Petition pp. 3–4 (PDF pp. 13–14). The district and appellate accounts similarly state that she lacked prior approval. That procedural circumstance is supported across the recovered presentations; it does not itself resolve why a later permission request was rejected or whether the same result would have followed a timely original choice.

3. **An exception/opportunity after the first rejection:** The district memorandum expressly reports that Ramsey offered another chance to submit a different topic **the following day without penalty**, because Settle might not have understood the procedure or genuinely wanted Drama. It also reports willingness to accept religious aspects if the paper did not deal solely with Christianity or Christ's life. App. 21 (PDF p. 61). The opposition repeats the next-day opportunity, citing J.A. 60, and describes permissible religious aspects as not dealing solely with Settle's personal religious redeemer, citing J.A. 69. It says that offer was rejected on counsel's advice. Opposition p. 5 (PDF p. 14). The advice/rejection assertion is respondents' record presentation, not an independently tried finding.

4. **Ramsey's reported mixed explanation:** The district memorandum quotes her deposition, pp. 27–28, in response to whether rejection was for the missed approval deadline or the choice of Christ's life: **“It was both.”** She says proper submission was a primary concern, that the classes knew the procedure, and that she did not make exceptions; she adds that the topic dealt too closely with Settle's private religious beliefs and was inappropriate for her age. App. 26 n.3 (PDF p. 66). This is reproduced testimony. It must be read alongside the documented next-day opportunity, not converted into an adjudicated finding that no exception was available.

5. **The original-choice hypothetical:** The petition reproduces a school-board exchange asking whether Ramsey would have accepted this subject if it had been the original choice. Her quoted answer is: **“No, I wouldn't. Just as I wouldn't have accepted one on Buddha or Muhammed.”** She distinguishes papers on religious issues from what she regarded as a religious paper. Petition p. 5 n.3, citing the board transcript at J.A. 231–231a and additional deposition testimony at J.A. 67 (PDF p. 15). This is attributed transcript evidence, not a judicial resolution of discrimination, sincerity, or causation.

6. **Competing implications:** Petitioner argues that willingness to waive the deadline for another topic, together with rejection of this topic even as an original choice, makes deadline and other explanations pretextual or insufficient to establish the same decision absent the religious reason. Petition pp. 14 n.9, 25–28 and reply pp. 4–6. Respondents argue that the procedural failure, research objectives, familiarity, resources and difficulty of critique adequately justified the restriction and left no material motive dispute. Opposition pp. 7–12, 14. These are competing party arguments. Neither the district court's statement that the change *could* have been rejected on timing alone nor the appellate majority's no-genuine-dispute conclusion is a trial finding that the same decision *would* have occurred without a disputed reason.

7. **Dates not silently repaired:** The district memorandum and opposition both recount a March 28 contact with Evans and then a March 27 meeting. App. 21–22; opposition pp. 5–6. The district court also says it is unclear whether a later-described meeting with Caudill was the same meeting. App. 22. The recovered set does not resolve this chronology. No exact separate permission-request date before the March 26 submission is established; the petition expressly says no earlier discussion. The district memorandum dates board review to April 1, while the appellate opinion dates the second outline to April 3; these are separate reported events, not a basis to invent an adjusted date.

These are the Sixth Circuit's enumeration of reasons Ramsey gave at the board hearing and in depositions. They are **reported explanations**, not six independent adjudicated facts proving each explanation true or legally sufficient. The district memorandum supplies additional testimony as identified above.

The appellate majority treated all six as within teacher discretion, even potentially mistaken reasons, and rejected a genuine motive dispute. The concurrence disagreed with aspects of the factual account, found some explanations unconvincing, and rejected a general exclusion of religious topics while affirming on its different topic-control analysis. App. 7–16. These lower-court conclusions remain the rulings/reasoning under review; this addendum does not adopt them.

## Research sources and comparator circumstances

**Source requirements.** Ramsey's testimony as reproduced by the appellate opinion says she required **two books and two encyclopedias or resources**, allowing magazine articles or pamphlets as substitutes. App. 5, J.A. 38 (PDF p. 45). The petition similarly identifies four secondary sources, pp. 3, 27 n.14. The opposition discusses the small school library as a pedagogical concern, pp. 3, 12, 14. The reply answers with Ramsey's quoted testimony that students were **not restricted** to school-library materials but had to be willing to produce outside materials if she wanted to see them. Reply p. 5 n.3, J.A. 38 (PDF p. 9, visually verified). Thus neither a primary-source-only rule nor a school-library-only rule is supported. The reply's conclusion that the resource explanation was pretextual is advocacy, not a finding.

**Other assignments.** The petition reports the following accepted subjects and cites the underlying student-material ranges: reincarnation, including Hinduism/Buddhism (J.A. 42, 120–135); spiritualism, ghosts and encounters with deceased persons (J.A. 136–147); witchcraft, black magic and the occult (J.A. 148–157). It also identifies the Pony Express and a jazz singer from the 1920s/1930s (J.A. 44–47). Petition p. 3 (PDF p. 13). These are counsel's descriptions with record references, not newly recovered complete student papers.

**The district court's more specific comparator account.** App. 21 n.1 (PDF p. 61, visually verified) says Ramsey accepted outlines concerning reincarnation, spiritualism and the occult that discussed historical religious figures and doctrinal teachings. It describes the religious material as background rather than the papers' focus. Its examples: the reincarnation paper discussed Buddhism and Hinduism; “Spiritualism” noted religious incorporation of such beliefs; “Magic Throughout History” contained headings **“Jews believe in Baal,” “Demons Are Evil Spirits,” and “Gods Are Good Spirits.”** This is the district opinion's description and characterization, not direct inspection of those student exhibits or an independent factual resolution by this source-recovery task.

**Limits of comparability.** The petition says Ramsey did not regard these other papers as religious, citing J.A. 62–67, 75–82, 88–99 (p. 5 n.3), and says no other proposed topic was rejected (p. 11). The latter remains petitioner's assertion. The complete recovered filings still supply no individual comparator approval dates, deadline-change histories, complete outlines, complete source lists, or student-belief circumstances. Accordingly they support more detail about comparator *content*, but not a finding that all procedural or personal circumstances were identical. The concurrence's conclusion that the other topics could require objective research and were permissibly distinguished remains judicial reasoning under review, App. 16.

## Other potentially material record qualifications

The district appendix now reproduces all three relevant paragraphs of the school board's **Religion In The Curriculum** policy. It supports objective, respectful teaching **about**, not **of**, religion; permits religious literature, music, drama and arts when intrinsic to the learning experience and presented objectively; limits thematic emphasis to a balanced, comprehensive treatment that neither promotes tenets nor demeans beliefs; and says student-initiated belief/nonbelief in responses to questions or assignments shall be accommodated, including compositions, art, music, speech and debate. App. 27–28 (PDF pp. 67–68). Ramsey distinguished a composition containing personal experience/observation from an objective research paper. The district court accepted that construction and found no policy violation. That interpretation is the lower ruling, not a newly proved fact or a replacement for the policy's qualifications. The petition quotes only the accommodation paragraph at p. 6 n.4; use the complete appendix passage for the operative policy context.

The district court did not reach qualified immunity after finding no constitutional violation. App. 29 (PDF p. 69). The petition lists Ramsey in her official capacity (preliminary p. ii) while the opposition says the complaint included an individual-capacity claim, that immunity was asserted, and that respondents **presume** the individual claim abandoned. Opposition p. ii n.1 (PDF p. 4). A respondent's presumption is not an established abandonment. Similarly, petition p. 2 n.2 says Settle had graduated, sought damages, and continued seeking removal of zero from her academic record. The district memorandum identifies declaratory/injunctive relief but does not reproduce the complaint. Do not infer conceded mootness, waived damages, or adjudicated immunity from these different descriptions.

The district memorandum and order are the actual summary-judgment documents, not evidence of a merits trial. The reproduced notice of appeal identifies the district order as entered August 17, 1993 and the notice as filed September 2, 1993. App. 30–32. The appellate judgment is dated May 8, 1995, with its mandate notation May 30. App. 17–18. No later disposition is supplied here.

## Scope of the addition

The material additions to the earlier published baseline are the expressly described no-penalty next-day alternative, the quoted original-choice and mixed-reason answers, the outside-library qualification, the district comparator descriptions, and the complete quoted policy context. Full outline contents and the comparator timing histories remain unestablished in this recovered set. All motive, same-decision, legal-sufficiency and remedy implications remain for the authorized neutral modeling and later stages, using their proper entering-law snapshot. No independent adjudication is made by this source recovery.
