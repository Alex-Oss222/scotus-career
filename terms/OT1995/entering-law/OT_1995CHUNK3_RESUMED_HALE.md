## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. As of this date the Court has not adopted a general rule governing remote historical movement in every statute.

**Authority by component:**

- Architecture; connection before magnitude; recognition of necessary inclusion; no-insertion rule: [United States v. Lopez](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Lopez_merits_1995-04-26.md#public-projection), April 26, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.
- Economic vehicle-taking class and enacted vehicle nexus: [United States v. Harris](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md#public-projection), April 27, 1995; O’Connor’s eight-Justice commerce rationale. Stone concurs in the judgment on the narrower direct-instrumentality/vehicle-specific nexus ground.

**Present operation effective:** Harris, April 27, 1995, for the latest application; the controlling architecture dates to Lopez, April 26, 1995.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. The Court has not yet adopted a general material-interstate-externality doctrine. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Material development:**

- April 26, 1995 — Lopez: ADDED / CHANGED. Added the controlling decision sequence of regulated class → interstate mechanism → magnitude/aggregation → fit/boundary. Changed the prior substantial-effects law only to the extent necessary to reject aggregation of generalized downstream consequences as a substitute for the missing Commerce connection. Preserved Wickard and Perez within their genuine economic-class settings. Reserved fuller Necessary-and-Proper inclusion questions.
- April 27, 1995 — Harris: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that Lopez does not disable direct regulation of an economic vehicle-taking class connected to an interstate motor-vehicle regime when the statute separately requires a particular-vehicle interstate nexus. Stone’s narrower concurrence emphasizes direct protection of the transportation instrumentality. No general remote-movement rule is added.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Lopez](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-lopez); [United States v. Harris](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-harris).

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

### Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

**Governing question:** What must be proved when a conventional-looking rifle falls within the whole-weapon machinegun definition.

**Current rule:** For §5861(d) possession of an unregistered rifle prosecuted as a whole-weapon machinegun under §5845(b), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics making it a machinegun: it shoots, is designed to shoot, or can readily be restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. Knowledge that the weapon is unregistered or that possession is illegal is not required. Ordinary possession of a rifle is not enough to presume knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge of the regulated weapon characteristics: [Staples v. United States — Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Staples_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Present operation effective:** Staples v. United States, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** The holding does not decide every separately defined machinegun part or combination, every other regulated weapon, or the sufficiency of proof in every conversion setting.

**Holdings navigation:** [Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#knowledge-of-the-characteristics-making-this-rifle-a-machinegun).

## Standards and Tests selection: Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions

### Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions

**Governing question:** Whether production of the declarant or proof of unavailability is a prerequisite to admission under these accepted hearsay exceptions.

**Current rule:** When statements satisfy the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration or medical-treatment exception, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The exception's actual foundations remain necessary; naming an exception cannot establish a disputed foundation or validate every police interview. The Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that proposed limitation cannot supply an alternative ground for admission. The received exception-sensitive confrontation framework remains in force.

**Authority by component:**

- Exception-specific absence of a production or unavailability gate: [White v. Illinois — No universal production or unavailability prerequisite](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/White_v_Illinois_merits_1992-01-15.md), January 15, 1992; Part I of Stone's opinion joined by all nine Justices.
- Rejection of a formal-accusation-only boundary: [White — The government's categorical definition of witness is rejected](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/White_v_Illinois_merits_1992-01-15.md), January 15, 1992; Part II joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter with Stone. Scalia and Thomas do not join that component.

**Present operation effective:** *White v. Illinois*, January 15, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Former-testimony unavailability analysis, constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions, and safeguards for altered presentation of trial witnesses remain distinct. No later testimonial or primary-purpose test is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** [White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992) — Confrontation and hearsay](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#white-v-illinois-502-us-346-1992).

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection — Commerce power and armed vehicle taking

Authority: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`.

### Commerce power and armed vehicle taking

#### [United States v. Harris](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Criminal Procedure — Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment (Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content; Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking; Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution).

##### The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection

**Holding:** Congress may regulate the armed forcible acquisition of the covered vehicle proved here as an economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme, while original §2119's enacted requirement that the particular vehicle have been transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce must be proved. Neither remote movement alone nor generalized crime, insurance, or productivity costs independently supplies this holding's constitutional ground. The decision creates no rival commerce test and no automatic instrumentality rule beyond these facts and the enacted nexus.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's commerce portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower ground: he would sustain the application because the offense directly attacks possession and control of a covered motor vehicle functioning as an interstate transportation instrumentality or thing in commerce, and because the statute requires proof of the particular vehicle's interstate connection. He does not rely on remote historical movement alone.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: applies its controlling connection-before-magnitude architecture for the first time, in a direct vehicle/instrumentality and economic vehicle-taking setting; the economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme supplies the connection, and Lopez's inadequate stand-alone possession/downstream-effects chain is distinguished; no missing element is supplied.
- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111 (1942): applies aggregation within a regulated economic class rather than demanding a substantial effect from each individual taking.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146 (1971): applies Congress's authority over the economic class despite an individual instance's local character.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: preserves its distinction between actual commercial operation and remote object history; prior manufacture alone is not the constitutional ground here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and each defendant's forty-one-month Count One term plus consecutive sixty-month Count Two term are affirmed on all three presented challenges. No merger, vacatur, concurrent conversion, sentence increase, resentencing, new trial or exploratory remand is ordered.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection — Commerce power and school-zone firearm possession

Authority: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`.

### Commerce power and school-zone firearm possession

#### [United States v. Lopez](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Lopez_merits_1995-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection — Firearm-characteristic knowledge

### Firearm-characteristic knowledge

#### [Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States, 511 U.S. 368](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Beecham_and_Jones_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-16.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-445; Jones included under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### The law of the convicting jurisdiction governs the conviction exclusion

**Holding:** Section 921(a)(20)'s direction to use the law of the jurisdiction where the proceedings occurred governs the related exclusion for expungement, set-aside, pardon, or restoration of civil rights, so state restoration alone does not remove a federal conviction as a §922(g) predicate. The enacted exclusion remains subject to its qualification that a conviction is not excluded if the pardon, expungement, or restoration of civil rights expressly provides that the person may not ship, transport, possess, or receive firearms; the Court decides which sovereign's law governs, not whether a particular federal restorative mechanism exists or has been satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous; all nine Justices join the complete rule and both affirmances.

**Material application:** State restoration alone does not remove either federal predicate. Jones's two restored West Virginia convictions remain excluded; the decision does not revive them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court creates no federal restoration process and does not decide a particular federal pardon, the availability or requirements of §925(c) relief, the civil-rights bundle, automatic versus affirmative restoration, or whether federal habeas qualifies as a set-aside. It enters no guilt finding or independent ruling on the linked false-statement count's elements, materiality or intent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Fourth Circuit judgments are affirmed, 9–0 each. Jones's indictment-reinstatement and remand disposition remains operative; Beecham receives no new sentencing or restoration order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): applied to reserve lenity for unresolved penal ambiguity after interpretation; competing proposed readings alone do not trigger it.
- United States v. Granderson, decided March 22, 1994: its contextual interpretive approach and residual role for lenity remain intact; its distinct sentencing ambiguity does not establish ambiguity here.
- Jarecki v. G. D. Searle & Co., 367 U.S. 303 (1961): its neighboring-terms principle supports, without mechanically compelling, the shared sovereign reference.
- Dickerson v. New Banner Institute, Inc., 460 U.S. 103 (1983): the intervening statutory choice-of-law text governs; its former federal-law definition is not used to bypass the enacted provision.

The contrary separate-clause reasoning of Geyler and Edwards is rejected on sovereign selection. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### [Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Staples_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1441.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun

**Holding:** To convict a defendant under 26 U.S.C. §5861(d) for possessing an unregistered machinegun in the circumstances presented here, the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics bringing the rifle within the complete-weapon machinegun definition—a weapon that shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger; circumstantial evidence may establish that knowledge. It need not prove knowledge of nonregistration, the registration requirement, or illegality, and the omitted-element instruction requires reversal and remand without a finding of evidentiary insufficiency or an order of acquittal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246 (1952): applies the presumption of criminal fault despite statutory silence.
- United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422 (1978): applies contextual examination before dispensing with fault.
- United States v. Freed, 401 U.S. 601 (1971): distinguishes knowledge of the object's regulated characteristics from knowledge of nonregistration; its registration holding remains intact.
- United States v. Balint, 258 U.S. 250 (1922): preserves the bounded public-welfare category without extending its narcotics construction automatically to this rifle.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its express-willfulness and knowledge-of-illegality rule; no universal legal-knowledge requirement follows.
- United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., June 8, 1992: leaves its making rule and limited parts-package disposition unchanged; neither decides this possession element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate frame/receiver, conversion-part, conversion-parts and one-person-controlled assembly-parts branches of §5845(b) remain undecided. Section 5845(a)'s antique exclusion and its separate exclusion for Secretary-designated collector's items unlikely to be used as weapons remain; the collector exclusion does not encompass machineguns or destructive devices and depends on manufacture date, value, design and other characteristics. No exclusion is established for this rifle. No Second Amendment, facial validity, ultimate knowledge or error-review result is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit is reversed and remanded, 7–2, for the required factual-knowledge instruction. No acquittal, mandatory retrial or new sentence is ordered; properly available sufficiency and error-review questions retain their own requirements.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection — Confrontation and hearsay

### Confrontation and hearsay

#### White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6113.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions require prior production or proof of unavailability

**Holding:** Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; nine Justices endorse this rule.

**Material application:** The limited grant accepts the state-law exception foundations for the child's statements as its premise; the Court makes no finding that disputed foundations are satisfied. Their spontaneous and treatment settings supply evidentiary value that later trial testimony cannot recreate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, harmless-error holding, or ruling on state-law admissibility outside the granted premise is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed on the limited Confrontation Clause claim; no new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 — extended from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.
- *Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56 — preserved for its received confrontation framework; its former-testimony unavailability analysis is not universalized.
- *Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805 — preserved for constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions; no unsupported judicial assurance substitutes for its requirements.
- *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 — firmly rooted exceptions retain their established reliability significance.
- *Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836 — distinguished as trial-witness presentation cases; their safeguards remain intact.

##### Whether confrontation is categorically limited to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes

**Holding:** The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

### White v. Illinois, No. 90-6113

Merits decision, January 15, 1992

*White v. Illinois*, 502 U.S. 346 (1992), No. 90-6113. Argued November 5, 1991; decided January 15, 1992. The Court granted review of the judgment of the Illinois Appellate Court, Fourth District, 198 Ill. App. 3d 641, 555 N.E.2d 1241, after the Illinois Supreme Court denied review. Review is limited to whether the Confrontation Clause requires production or an unavailability showing before admission of statements accepted as spontaneous declarations and statements for medical treatment. Petitioner seeks reversal of his convictions on that ground.

All nine Justices participated at argument and at decision.

The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed 9-0 on the limited Confrontation Clause claim. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support affirmance.

Stone delivers the opinion of the Court. White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter join in full. Scalia and Thomas join Part I, the exception and unavailability analysis, but not Part II, rejecting the government's proposed limitation of confrontation to formal accusations. Thomas concurs in part and in the judgment, joined by Scalia.

Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

Part I of Stone's opinion controls this rule. White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas join it; all nine Justices endorse the rule.

The Court holds that absence alone does not impose a production requirement on these accepted exceptions. Inadi explains why trial testimony cannot necessarily replace a statement whose evidentiary value derives from the circumstances in which it was made. A spontaneous response and a statement made for treatment carry features that later testimony cannot recreate. Roberts does not establish a universal preliminary demand for unavailability; its former-testimony setting is different. Under the received reliability framework, the firmly rooted exceptions at issue supply the requisite assurance when their foundations are satisfied. Wright preserves the separate inquiry applicable outside such exceptions, and an exception label cannot settle disputed facts. Here state-law membership in the exceptions is the premise of the limited review. Coy and Craig concern safeguards when a trial witness is presented through an altered procedure; they do not require the extra evidentiary gateway sought here. No question of harmless error arises on the claim resolved.

The Court extends *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387, from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.

*Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56, remains part of the governing confrontation framework; its unavailability analysis for former testimony does not apply universally.

*Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805, preserves constitutional reliability requirements outside firmly rooted exceptions; unsupported judicial assurances cannot substitute for those requirements.

*Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171, preserves the established reliability significance of firmly rooted exceptions.

*Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836, concern the presentation of trial witnesses and are distinguished on that basis; their safeguards remain intact.

The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

Part II of Stone's opinion controls this rejection of the proposed categorical narrowing. White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter join it, forming a seven-Justice majority with Stone. Scalia and Thomas do not join this part.

The Court declines to remove this case from confrontation law by defining its protection to cover only formal accusations. The decisions governing hearsay and confrontation address more than affidavits or their formal equivalents. Mattox recognizes the constitutional problem created by absent testimony while permitting an established exception, and the later reliability cases continue to examine the substance and setting of out-of-court statements. Inadi itself supplies an exception-specific answer rather than declaring that informal statements can never implicate confrontation. The government's proposed categorical boundary would discard that framework without a need to do so here. The accepted spontaneous and treatment exceptions resolve the actual production question. This conclusion does not establish that every hearsay statement violates the Constitution, nor does it decide the constitutional treatment of a formal investigative accusation on a disputed record. Those questions require their own factual premises and candid consideration of the governing precedents.

*Mattox v. United States*, 156 U.S. 237, remains evidence of confrontation's substantive protection and its coexistence with established exceptions.

*California v. Green*, 399 U.S. 149, and *Dutton v. Evans*, 400 U.S. 74, remain in force; their treatment of out-of-court statements is not displaced by a definition limited to formal affidavits.

*Inadi* and *Roberts* apply as confrontation decisions sensitive to the particular exception, without replacement by a new categorical testimonial rule.

The two accepted firmly rooted exceptions require no separate production or unavailability showing. Roberts, Inadi and Wright remain the governing framework, with the foundations for each exception preserved. The Court adopts no new categorical testimonial test, blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, or harmless-error holding.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, agrees with the exception-based judgment and its analysis but declines to reject the government's proposed definition of a witness. He would leave open reconsideration of whether confrontation particularly addresses formalized testimonial materials; that suggested reconstruction is not adopted law.

The affirmance ends Supreme Court review of the granted claim. No new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention. State-law admissibility outside the granted premise and any other claims receive no new adjudication.
