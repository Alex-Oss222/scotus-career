## United States v. Ursery and United States v. $405,089.23

Sources: objective opening narrative of [518 U.S. 267](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-345.ZO.html), identifying the lower judgments at 59 F.3d 568 and 33 F.3d 1210 (and denial of rehearing 56 F.3d 41). This extraction does not adopt the source's ensuing Supreme Court analysis. The lower courts' characterization of the governing precedents is their reasoning, not entering law.

Ursery's house was the subject of a civil action under 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(7), alleging use to facilitate unlawful processing and distribution of a controlled substance. He paid $13,250 to settle that claim in full. Shortly before settlement was consummated he was indicted under §841(a)(1); he later received a jury conviction and 63 months' imprisonment. The Sixth Circuit reversed the criminal conviction on double-jeopardy grounds, treating the completed civil forfeiture as prior punishment. The reviewed judgment is the conviction's reversal, not a direct appeal from the settlement.

Arlt and Wren's case has the opposite order. The Government filed the civil complaint before their criminal trial, alleging property involved in money laundering under 18 U.S.C. §981(a)(1)(A) and drug proceeds or facilitating property under 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(6). The parties deferred civil litigation during the criminal prosecution. Following convictions under §§846, 371 and 1956, the District Court granted summary judgment in the forfeiture action more than a year later. The Ninth Circuit reversed that forfeiture judgment on double-jeopardy grounds. The claims against various property cannot all silently be characterized as proven proceeds; the complaint invoked overlapping statutory routes. No new finding as to a particular item's provenance or value is supplied.

These distinct judgment objects and sequences must remain separate. Determining a sanction's constitutional character does not alone decide retained Grady/Blockburger identity questions if a lawful path makes those questions material. Neither this extraction nor the packet establishes an unlitigated innocent-owner ruling or an Eighth Amendment excessiveness determination.

## Lewis v. United States

Sources: [65 F.3d 252](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/65/252/529239/) as identified and summarized in the approved neutral packet; objective narrative in [518 U.S. 322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-6465.ZO.html). Control retrieved the latter complete text and reviewed the pertinent narrative; the browser is unavailable, so it does not claim a fresh full browser reading of the former.

The two §1701 charges each authorized six months' imprisonment. The magistrate granted the Government's bench-trial request after promising not to sentence Lewis to more than six months under any circumstances. Both convictions and concurrent three-year probation sentences were already entered; the case is not an interlocutory jury demand. The jury request was preserved. The Second Circuit treated each offense's statutory classification as decisive and discussed the cap only as an unnecessary alternative question. The statement's legal effectiveness is disputed law; the extraction does not establish an external statute removing sentencing authority. The cap should neither be omitted nor transformed into a legislatively reduced maximum.

## Gasperini v. Center for Humanities

Source: objective opening narrative in [518 U.S. 415](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-719.ZO.html), reporting 66 F.3d 427. Gasperini invoked diversity jurisdiction for state-law claims including contract, conversion and negligence. The Center conceded liability for lost photographic transparencies. The jury awarded $450,000, calculated as $1,500 for each of 300 slides. The Center's Rule 59 new-trial motion challenged excessiveness and was denied without comment.

The Second Circuit applied CPLR §5501(c), evaluated comparative awards and the uniqueness of the images and earnings evidence, and directed a new trial unless Gasperini accepted $100,000. It did not simply enter an unconditional $100,000 judgment. Its findings about at most fifty uniquely valuable transparencies and the photographer's earnings are appellate assessments at issue, not newly adopted Supreme Court findings. The case concerns compensatory damages; BMW's distinct punitive-damages constitutional limits cannot be substituted for the presented Erie and Seventh Amendment questions.

## Medtronic v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic

Sources: [Eleventh Circuit, 56 F.3d 1335](https://openjurist.org/56/f3d/1335/lohr-v-medtronic-inc), especially paragraphs 13–17, 30–31 and the claim-specific conclusion; objective narrative in [518 U.S. 470](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-754.ZO.html), describing the November 30, 1982 FDA letter and complaint. The lower opinion was retrieved as primary opinion text through the web tool; its post-divergence Supreme Court citations describe the lower court's reasoning and do not determine simulated law.

The Activitrax pacemaker and its Model 4011 lead entered through substantial equivalence, not premarket approval. The lead's 1982 FDA clearance permitted marketing subject to general controls and expressly did not endorse safety. Lora Lohr was implanted in 1987; the pacemaker failed in December 1990, requiring emergency surgery. Her physician attributed the failure to a likely lead defect. The complaint alleged negligence and strict liability, including design, manufacturing and warning theories; her husband claimed loss of consortium. Allegations are not proven defects or ultimate liability.

After removal on diversity grounds, the District Court ultimately dismissed the entire complaint as preempted. The Eleventh Circuit restored negligent-design and strict-liability theories while sustaining preemption of negligent manufacture and failure to warn. It rejected a parallel-federal-duty argument after expressly exercising discretion to reach that argument despite its first appearance in the reply brief (paragraphs 30–31). It therefore is not left unreviewed by an assumed reply-brief forfeiture. Each Supreme Court docket must identify which portion of that mixed judgment is addressed.

Section 360k(a) begins with its subsection (b) exception and addresses a State or political subdivision's requirement respecting a human-use device that is different from or additional to a requirement applicable under the federal chapter and relates to safety/effectiveness or another matter included in the federal requirement. The inquiry must keep state-duty identification, federal requirement, difference/addition, and subject connection distinct. Section 360h(d)'s saving text concerns compliance with an order under its own section; it is not an unrestricted exemption from §360k. The §808.1(d) regulation's text and its enumerated qualifications must be read if used. No PMA-approved device, §360k(b) exemption order or general federal cause of action is established in this case.
