## Standards and Tests selection: Interim restraint and orderly review in related bankruptcy proceedings

### Interim restraint and orderly review in related bankruptcy proceedings

**Governing question:** Collateral displacement of an operative §105 restraint in a proceeding actually related to Chapter 11.

**Current rule:** In a related proceeding supported by an actual bond, retained collateral and reorganization connection, §1334(b) supplies nonexclusive subject-matter jurisdiction; §105 supplies remedial authority, not independent jurisdiction. Section 157(c)(1)’s limit on final noncore adjudication does not eliminate sufficient authority for an interlocutory preservation restraint. Where actual relatedness, sufficient interim authority, substantial support for the applicable restraint and a concrete relief-and-review mechanism are established, a party must challenge it through the issuing proceeding and authorized review rather than execute contrary to it elsewhere while it remains effective.

Statutory withdrawal of reference retains its own conditions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims

### Exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims?

**Current rule:** Under §1251(a), a federal district court cannot grant relief on an actual controversy between two States, even if joined to private litigation or this Court previously declined an original action. Such a complaint must be dismissed for want of jurisdiction. An otherwise lawful private-title action may determine a boundary incidentally without binding the States on their sovereign boundary; a State's intervention seeking no relief against another State is not barred on that ground. Original-forum discretion does not transfer exclusive jurisdiction to another court.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury.

**Authority by component:**

Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

### Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver

**Governing question:** What instructions govern capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver?

**Current rule:** Dusky's standard governs capacity to stand trial, plead guilty and choose to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of proceedings. A competent person's actual waiver must separately be knowing, intelligent and voluntary. Further competence determination is necessary when circumstances give reason to doubt present competence, not automatically for every plea or waiver. Existing competence findings do not settle informed, uncoerced relinquishment; then-effective treatment of state findings remains independent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

### Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review?

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No comprehensive formula, ratio, mandatory list of factors, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. Austin leaves truly innocent ownership's constitutional protection unresolved; Alexander's independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Required commercial connection and distinct economic-class regulation

### Required commercial connection and distinct economic-class regulation

**Governing question:** The commerce connection required for the original school-zone possession prohibition and the distinct armed-vehicle-taking class.

**Current rule:** A stand-alone local school-zone possession prohibition without an enacted commerce connection cannot be sustained by a generalized chain through crime costs, education and productivity to the national economy. Courts cannot save the unambiguous original §922(q)(1)(A) offense by adding an unexpressed commerce element. This bounded rule does not categorically exclude noneconomic intrastate conduct or require formal legislative findings in every case. Regulation of an economic vehicle-taking class within an interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme supplies a distinct sufficient commerce ground for original §2119, whose enacted particular-vehicle interstate-history requirement must also be proved.

**Authority by component:**

Justice Kennedy’s opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas: five direct votes for this bounded ground and affirmance. The Chief Justice’s additional six-route framework has no additional joins and is not the Court’s exhaustive test. No Marks synthesis is needed.
- The possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element: [The possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Lopez_merits_1995-04-26.md#public-projection), 1995-04-26. Kennedy’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas: five direct votes for the no-insertion disposition and the precise reservation. - The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection: [The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md#public-projection), 1995-04-27. ## Standards and Tests selection: RFRA review of prospective enforcement

### RFRA review of prospective enforcement

**Governing question:** Application of original RFRA to threatened enforcement, including preexisting laws and commercial religious exercise.

**Current rule:** The claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The inquiry applies to threatened future implementation of a preexisting law. Commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and labeling a law antidiscrimination does not itself justify its application. Direct transactional and dignitary injury and legally material alternatives or exemptions may be considered; availability of alternative housing is not an automatic answer to direct injury.

**Authority by component:**

- RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication: [RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Church_of_Scientology_Flag_Service_Organization_Inc_v_City_of_Clearwater_merits_1994-12-05.md#public-projection), 1994-12-05. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that RFRA governs, but does not join the opinion or its disposition of these adverse determinations.
- RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws: [RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Swanner_v_Anchorage_Equal_Rights_Commission_merits_1995-03-27.md#public-projection), 1995-03-27. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five direct rationale and judgment votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer dissent from the vacatur. Their agreement that RFRA governs does not turn their distinct application into Court law.

**Present operation effective:** Church of Scientology v. Clearwater, December 5, 1994; Swanner, March 27, 1995, clarifies the commercial-participation and application-specific limits without changing the burden sequence; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary standing and appropriate-relief limits remain. RFRA protects religious belief and preserves the Establishment Clause; this statutory inquiry does not replace the constitutional free-exercise or establishment standards. A remand under it establishes neither an exemption nor failure of the government’s eventual showing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause.

**Authority by component:**

- Content classification and five-Justice injunction standard: [Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — The decree as confined is not categorically content based; A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Madsen_v_Womens_Health_Center_Inc_merits_1994-06-30.md#public-projection), 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part II-A(2), joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this application while accepting the separate actual-concert principle. O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.
- Removal of invalidated predicates and lawful remedial discretion: [Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — Relief removes invalidated predicates; Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Madsen_v_Womens_Health_Center_Inc_merits_1994-06-30.md#public-projection), 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all eight other Justices: nine at the invalidated-predicate and no-evasion level. This agreement does not settle the permissible content of a new decree. O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight. Stone-Zsela dissents from the refusal to require his bounded post-refusal replacement.

- Coverage boundary retained in applying the speech-injunction rule: [Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — An injunction binds responsible parties and actual concert, not a viewpoint](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Madsen_v_Womens_Health_Center_Inc_merits_1994-06-30.md#public-projection), 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part II-A(1), joined by all eight other Justices: nine. The three dissenters on operational neutrality join only this distinct coverage principle.

**Present operation effective:** Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc, 1994-06-30.

**Exceptions and limits:** The particular distances and hours adjudicated are applications, not universal safe harbors. The decision does not require a personal buffer zone, decide the reserved independent conduct-language claims, or transplant Casey’s abortion-regulation framework into the advocacy inquiry. Coverage remains confined to responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert or participation; viewpoint, association or independent advocacy alone does not establish concert.

## Standards and Tests selection: Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

### Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

**Governing question:** Enforceability of a plea-discussion agreement permitting impeachment of the defendant’s own inconsistent testimony.

**Current rule:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that statements may impeach the defendant’s own inconsistent trial testimony. Absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, that limited waiver is enforceable. Supported ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness and agreement-scope objections receive individual review. Inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority by component:**

- A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable: [A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Mezzanatto_merits_1995-01-18.md#public-projection), 1995-01-18. Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-18; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Then-Rule 410 and parallel Rule 11(e)(6) protect against use against the participating defendant in civil or criminal proceedings of withdrawn guilty pleas, no-contest pleas, related Rule 11 or comparable state-proceeding statements, and prosecutor plea discussions resulting in no guilty plea or a withdrawn plea. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion already introduced and fairness requiring contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires oath, an on-record statement and counsel’s presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. The waiver holding decides no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing or other-person-testimony use, no independent perjury-use term and no universal waiver of fundamental protections. Independent constitutional and evidentiary limits remain; the concurrence’s additional Government-proof requirement does not control.

**Holdings navigation:** [A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#a-knowing-and-voluntary-impeachment-waiver-is-enforceable).

## Standards and Tests selection: Direct enterprise engagement in interstate commerce

### Direct enterprise engagement in interstate commerce

**Governing question:** The direct-engagement alternative under §1962(a).

**Current rule:** An enterprise engages in interstate commerce when it directly participates in interstate acquisition, production or distribution of goods or services. Actual cross-border procurement and repeated interstate recruitment and movement for enterprise work, together with carriage across state lines of the enterprise’s output, can establish that operation without proving that the particular enterprise substantially affects interstate commerce.

**Authority by component:**

- The mine’s own interstate operations satisfy the enacted enterprise-commerce condition: [The mine’s own interstate operations satisfy the enacted enterprise-commerce condition](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Robertson_merits_1995-05-01.md#public-projection), 1995-05-01. Justice Breyer’s opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: nine direct joins in the rule, cumulative application and bounded remedy.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-01; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The affecting-commerce alternative and its constitutional reach remain undecided. No procurement-only sufficient rule or attribution of every proprietor’s private act to the enterprise is adopted. Section 1962(a)’s separate income, participation and investment elements remain.

**Controlling construction:** The separate open-market securities-investment qualification requires no intent to control, participate in control or assist control; postpurchase aggregate holdings of the purchaser, immediate family and racketeering or unlawful-debt accomplices below one percent of any outstanding class; and no legal or actual power to elect a director. It is not an exemption for ordinary ownership and operation of an enterprise.

### Liens and claim valuation

#### Dewsnup v. Timm, 502 U.S. 410 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-741.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether §506(d) reduces an allowed real-property lien to collateral value in the presented Chapter 7 case

**Holding:** In the Chapter 7 setting presented, §506(d) does not reduce a lien on real property to its current judicially determined value when the claim is allowed under §502 and secured by that lien. Section 506(a)'s valuation for treatment of a claim does not, on these facts, compel avoidance of the lien's excess under §506(d); other statutory settings and express exceptions remain outside the decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; five of eight participating Justices control the statutory construction and affirmance.

**Material application:** The debtor sought to reduce the lien securing an allowed debt of approximately $120,000 to the established property value of $39,000. Section 506(a) continues to value secured and unsecured amounts for claim treatment; that valuation does not automatically avoid the excess lien here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt the Tenth Circuit's abandonment or estate-interest rationale, determine estate administration, or decide other subsections, express statutory exceptions, reorganization-specific restrictions, constitutional taking questions, or future-valuation questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit judgment is affirmed and the dismissal with prejudice remains in place; the allowed lien is not reduced under §506(d) to the property's judicially determined value.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Long v. Bullard*, 117 U.S. 617 — the ordinary survival of liens through personal discharge remains an interpretive premise, not immunity from every express avoidance provision.
- *Johnson v. Home State Bank*, 501 U.S. 78 — applied for the distinction between extinguished personal liability and a surviving claim against property.
- *Farrey v. Sanderfoot*, 500 U.S. 291 — lien survival informs the Code's operation subject to its actual avoidance provisions.
- *United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235 — preserved; the Court finds contextual uncertainty here rather than displacing the priority of clear statutory text.
- *United Savings Association v. Timbers of Inwood Forest Associates*, 484 U.S. 365 — §506(a)'s valuation function remains operative; it does not decide this §506(d) question.
- *Union Bank v. Wolas*, simulated December 11, 1991 — its §547(c)(2) rule remains intact; no lien-avoidance rule is drawn from its treatment of debt maturity.

### Bond execution and bankruptcy restraints

#### [Celotex Corp. v. Edwards](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Celotex_Corp_v_Edwards_merits_1995-04-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1504.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### The operative June injunction reaches this proposed bond enforcement

**Holding:** The June 13, 1991 order expressly continued the §105 restraint and required relief from the issuing bankruptcy court before collection on bonds whose appeals had concluded when the bankruptcy petition was filed. Its coverage reaches the Edwards bond proceeding authorized in May 1992; this conclusion does not depend on an automatic §362 stay, estate ownership of the matured bond, or a finding of contempt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's June-order coverage portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rule 65.1 retains its motion procedure and surety-jurisdiction function; it does not itself answer the effect of another court's applicable injunction.
- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: preserves the distinction between the operative order and objections to its validity; no sanction or culpability finding is imported.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 105(a) permits orders, process, or judgments necessary or appropriate to carry out title 11. A provision allowing a party in interest to raise an issue does not preclude the court from acting on its own when necessary or appropriate to enforce or implement orders or rules or prevent abuse of process. This is remedial authority, not an independent subject-matter jurisdiction grant or an override of §157. The June order distinguishes bonds during an ongoing appellate process, which that court regarded as estate property subject to §362, from bonds after a creditor succeeds through an authorized completed appeal and bonds whose appeals ended by the petition date. Its latter two categories require relief from the continued §105 restraint. The Court's coverage holding does not adopt every premise of the first category, decide §362's scope, or find that every completed bond remains estate property.

##### Immediate execution on this bond is related to the Chapter 11 case

**Holding:** Whether the Edwardses may immediately execute against Northbrook on this supersedeas bond is at least related to Celotex's Chapter 11 case under §1334(b). The bond, retained insurance-settlement collateral, and reorganization connection establish actual nonexclusive subject-matter relatedness; this holding does not establish exact depletion, final core authority, the outer limits of relatedness, or an independent jurisdictional grant under §105.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's actual-relatedness portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, November 8, 1994: preserves the distinction between procedural power and unpresented underlying merits; its holding does not independently determine bankruptcy relatedness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1334(b) grants original but nonexclusive district-court jurisdiction over civil proceedings arising under title 11, arising in a title 11 case, or related to one, notwithstanding an Act of Congress conferring exclusive jurisdiction on a court other than the district courts. It differs from §1334(a)'s original and exclusive jurisdiction over cases under title 11 and the then-applicable §1334(d)'s exclusive jurisdiction, in the district where the case begins or is pending, over the debtor's property wherever located as of commencement and property of the estate. The 1994 amendments do not apply to this bankruptcy commenced in 1990; the later property designation (e), new jury provision, and changed appellate-panel/election provisions supply no rule here.

##### Section 157 permits this interim restraint without a final core determination

**Holding:** In this related proceeding, §157(c)(1)'s restriction on a bankruptcy judge's final noncore adjudication does not deprive that judge of authority to enter this interlocutory restraint preserving the matter for proper determination. That sufficient interim authority defeats the asserted collateral-displacement ground without deciding final core status, all-parties consent, every equitable predicate, or the ultimate propriety and duration of the §105 injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's interim-authority portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: extends its interim-preservation reasoning to this substantial bankruptcy authority question without treating arguable authority as final legality.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: preserves distinct statutory functions and qualifying review routes; it does not independently validate the injunction or decide §157 classification.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, March 1, 1995: preserves attention to the actual authorized judicial act; its appellate holding is not a grant of bankruptcy injunction power.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 157(a) permits a district court to refer title 11 cases and proceedings arising under title 11, arising in such a case, or related to one to its bankruptcy judges. Under §157(b)(1), referred core proceedings arising under title 11 or in a title 11 case may be heard and determined, with appropriate orders subject to §158 review. The bankruptcy court called its bond-property determination core under §157(b)(2)(A), (G), and (O); that is its classification, not this Court's final adoption. Those clauses concern estate administration; motions to terminate, annul, or modify the automatic stay; and other proceedings affecting liquidation of estate assets or adjustment of debtor-creditor or equity-security-holder relationships, except personal-injury tort and wrongful-death claims. Section 157(b)(3) places the classification question before the bankruptcy judge on the judge's own motion or timely party motion and forbids a noncore classification solely because state law may affect the outcome. It does not make the classification unreviewable.

For a noncore but related proceeding, §157(c)(1) permits hearing and proposed findings and conclusions submitted to the district court; the final order belongs to the district judge after consideration and de novo review of the matters timely and specifically objected to. De novo review is not automatically required for every unobjected proposition. Section 157(c)(2) separately permits district-court referral for hearing, determination, and orders with all parties' consent, subject to §158 review. The record establishes neither consent nor an affirmative refusal; neither is presumed. Section 157(b)(5) requires the district court to order personal-injury tort and wrongful-death claims tried in the district where the bankruptcy is pending or where the claim arose, as the district court where the bankruptcy is pending determines. Section 157(b)(2)(B) includes allowance or disallowance of claims or estate exemptions and estimation for plan confirmation in its core category, but excludes liquidation or estimation of contingent or unliquidated personal-injury tort or wrongful-death claims for distribution. These distinct limits remain intact. No new tort trial or liquidation is authorized here.

##### This still-operative restraint must be challenged through orderly relief and review

**Holding:** The actual §1334(b) relation, sufficient interim §157 authority, substantial support for this applicable restraint, and concrete relief/review mechanism require a challenge through the issuing proceeding and authorized review rather than contrary execution elsewhere while the restraint remains effective. The holding preserves the prior authorities' jurisdictional and frustrated-review reservations and does not validate the injunction's ultimate merits or any contempt sanction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's qualified collateral-review portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: applies orderly relief and review with substantial-jurisdiction and duration limits; preserves the different consequences for criminal punishment and civil remedies after invalidation.
- Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307: applies orderly-challenge reasoning while preserving its transparent-invalidity, frivolous-pretense, and frustrated-timely-review reservations; no announcement of knowing defiance, notice finding, or sanction is imported.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, March 1, 1995: preserves the requirement of an actual authorized review route; ordinary power over one proceeding does not confer appellate power over another court's order.

**Limits and questions not reached:** United Mine Workers permits temporary preservation of a substantial jurisdictional question and expressly excludes a frivolous and insubstantial jurisdictional claim. Its distinct unreversed-order formulation concerns a court with jurisdiction over subject matter and person. Its orders remain operative until expiration or appropriate relief. After invalidation, remedial civil relief falls with an erroneously issued injunction, and still more clearly with an order beyond jurisdiction; criminal punishment may have a different survival rule, subject to independent procedural and remedial objections. Compensation, coercion, and punishment are not interchangeable. This case imposes none of them and finds neither contempt nor knowing personal violation.

Walker preserves its reservation for transparent invalidity or only a frivolous pretense to validity and its different constitutional posture for timely challenge met by delay or frustration. It does not define a universal exception or hold that any objection automatically licenses disregard. No such frustrated timely challenge is established here. Its treatment of Green does not create a general ban on jurisdictional objections. Its clear-notice, announced-defiance, two-day timing, and Alabama expedited-review facts are not imported. The present explicit motion, debtor-burden, and statutory review mechanisms supply the concrete basis for orderly challenge; no unconditional immediate appellate remedy is presumed.

##### The execution authorization must yield while the creditors retain qualified relief and review routes

**Holding:** The contrary execution authorization must be set aside while the §105 restraint remains effective; the creditors may seek relief in the issuing bankruptcy proceeding, where Celotex bears the burden to justify continuation, and use an authorized review route. Section 158 preserves its finality and leave requirements, Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route, and the decision neither dissolves the injunction nor orders permanent restraint or payment of the bond.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's execution-and-review-remedy portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: applies coexistence of §158(d) and an independently qualifying §1292 route, preserving each route's conditions.
- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: preserves the order's force only while operative and the availability of appropriate appellate or other relief.
- Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307: retains the significance of meaningful access to orderly relief; no unconditional immediate-appeal right is inferred.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The issuing bankruptcy court may entertain a request to vacate or modify the restraint; its June order places the burden on Celotex to establish that continuation is warranted. The Court has settled the supplied §1334(b) relation and sufficient interim authority for this collateral posture. Its reservation of direct challenge does not reopen those conclusions as wholly undecided. Remaining lawful coverage questions, final §157 allocation and procedure, ultimate statutory injunction power, particular evidence, and duration remain available through proper proceedings. The lower court's possible avoidance, collateral-contract, and reorganization inquiries are not completed adjudications of avoidance recovery or exact estate loss on this bond.

Under the applicable §158(a), final bankruptcy orders go to the district court for the district where the bankruptcy judge serves, and interlocutory review requires leave: here the Middle District of Florida. Section 158(d) permits Eleventh Circuit review of qualifying final appellate decisions, not immediate circuit review of every interlocutory action. No bankruptcy appellate panel with the necessary institutional and consent predicates is established. Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route from a district court's bankruptcy appellate order. Section 1292(b), when invoked, requires written district-court findings that a controlling question of law presents substantial ground for difference of opinion and that immediate appeal may materially advance termination, application within ten days, and circuit permission; the application itself does not stay proceedings. No finding that these predicates have already been met is made.

Section 157(d) withdrawal of reference is separate. The district court may withdraw for cause on its own motion or timely party motion; withdrawal on timely motion is required when resolution requires the specified consideration of both title 11 and other federal laws regulating organizations or activities affecting interstate commerce. No withdrawal motion or qualifying finding is invented. Ordinary review and withdrawal are not interchangeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The bond-execution authorization is reversed and remanded for its removal while the §105 restraint remains effective. The creditors may seek relief in the issuing proceeding, where Celotex must justify continuation, and use qualified review routes. No permanent injunction, dissolution, bond payment, automatic §362 stay for all sureties, estate ownership, contempt or alteration of the tort judgment is ordered.

### Exclusive original jurisdiction and private title litigation

#### Mississippi v. Louisiana, 506 U.S. 73 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1158.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### An interstate complaint belongs to the exclusive original forum

**Holding:** Section 1251(a) denies a federal district court jurisdiction to grant relief on an actual controversy between two states, even when the complaint is joined to private litigation and this Court previously declined an original action. The appellate judgment is reversed insofar as it adjudicates Louisiana’s complaint against Mississippi, with instructions to direct dismissal of that complaint for want of jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous controlling holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California v. Arizona, 440 U.S. 59 — applied; this Court is the exclusive federal forum for a state’s claim against another state.
- Illinois v. City of Milwaukee, 406 U.S. 91 — distinguished as to different defendants and applied to preserve original-forum discretion.
- Ohio v. Wyandotte Chemicals Corp., 401 U.S. 493 — preserved; discretion does not grant another court exclusive interstate jurisdiction.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 — preserved; case-specific discretion does not negate §1251(a).
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 — distinguished; its original-forum exercise and separate objections supply no district-court authority here.

##### Private title questions remain distinct from binding interstate relief

**Holding:** Section 1251(a) does not bar an otherwise lawful private-title action merely because deciding title requires locating a state boundary; such adjudication does not bind the states on their boundary. Louisiana’s intervention that seeks no relief against Mississippi is unaffected, and the remaining title claims return for a determination whether the existing record permits fair resolution or additional proceedings are necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous controlling holding and remand limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Durfee v. Duke, 375 U.S. 106 — applied; adjudication between private parties does not bind the states on their boundary.
- California v. Arizona, 440 U.S. 59 — applied to the actual interstate controversy; it does not withdraw otherwise lawful private-title jurisdiction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no interstate boundary, private title, accretion, avulsion or sovereign acquiescence issue. Party agreement or convenience cannot create district-court jurisdiction over the interstate complaint.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the interstate adjudication and direct dismissal of Louisiana’s complaint against Mississippi for want of jurisdiction. Remand the remaining title claims to determine whether the record permits fair resolution or further district proceedings are needed; Louisiana’s nonadverse intervention is unaffected.

### Jury determination of criminal elements

#### [United States v. Gaudin](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Gaudin_merits_1995-06-19.md)

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. - Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

### Competence and informed waiver

#### Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-725.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### A common capacity standard and a separate actual waiver

**Holding:** Dusky supplies the constitutional mental-capacity standard for standing trial, pleading guilty and choosing to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings. A competent defendant may relinquish the rights only through a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver; competence does not establish that the particular choice was informed and uncoerced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402: the functional competence standard governs each presented choice.
- Faretta v. California, 422 U.S. 806: legal skill is distinct from competence to choose representation.
- Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238, and Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: actual informed and voluntary waiver remains separately required.
- Westbrook v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 150: additional waiver inquiry preserved; a higher constitutional capacity quantum is not required.

##### Existing findings do not eliminate the distinct inquiry on remand

**Holding:** A further competence determination is required when circumstances give the court reason to doubt present competence; no automatic hearing is required for every plea or waiver. Reversal of the higher-standard ruling leaves the existing claims about adequate inquiry and informed, voluntary relinquishment for consideration under the correct standards and the applicable pre-AEDPA treatment of state findings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** States may provide further safeguards. Execution competence and a distinct standard for actually conducting a trial without counsel remain open. Medication does not itself establish incapacity, and earlier findings are neither ignored nor conclusive after material change; reliable retrospective review and actual waivers remain for consideration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the higher-standard basis and remove the associated conditional-writ direction; remand under proper capacity, inquiry, waiver and pre-AEDPA state-finding rules. No automatic validation or restoration of the ex-wife murder death sentence already reduced to life.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375, and Drope v. Missouri, 420 U.S. 162: reasonable doubt concerning present competence triggers inquiry.
- Medina, June 22, 1992, and Riggins, May 18, 1992: their limited rules are preserved, not enlarged into conclusive findings here.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37: the Court may decline to introduce an unraised Teague defense.

### Excessive fines and criminal RICO forfeiture

#### Alexander v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1526.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the traditional-prior-restraint classification and independent First Amendment examination in *Alexander v. United States*, June 28, 1993, No. 91-1526, see First Amendment — Postconviction forfeiture of expressive enterprises.

##### Whether punitive criminal RICO forfeiture requires a distinct Excessive Fines inquiry

**Holding:** A criminal RICO forfeiture extracting property as punishment is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause independently of the validity of the convictions or the permissibility of the prison term. The lower court must examine the extent of the exaction in relation to the offenses supporting it; it may not reject that inquiry simply because the sentence is less than life imprisonment without parole.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt coverage and remand. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy accept the alternative excessiveness remand without joining Part III or adopting a proportionality formula.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves actual excessiveness to the lower court, with appropriate record development. The inquiry is not reduced to counting the seven titles adjudged obscene, and it may not presume that every asset or dollar of lawful business is criminal activity. No numerical cap, comprehensive proportionality formula or binding list of excessiveness considerations is adopted. The independent First Amendment inquiry remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate approval of the challenged forfeiture and remand for an Excessive Fines examination independent of the First Amendment inquiry. All nine accept this remand, but only the six Part III Justices adopt its coverage rationale; Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy accept alternative relief without joining that rationale. No final excessiveness finding, dollar cap or return of property is ordered; the convictions remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S. 257: its distinction between private awards and governmental punishment supports coverage of this criminal forfeiture; its private-award holding remains intact.
- Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S. 277: no rule about imprisonment displaces the separate Excessive Fines inquiry; the Court adopts no general prison-sentence holding here.
- Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993, is an uncoordinated same-day decision, not an earlier source of this holding. No logical priority or adoption of its separate writings is inferred.

### Plea-discussion impeachment waivers

#### [United States v. Mezzanatto](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Mezzanatto_merits_1995-01-18.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1340.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Holding:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only; his different proof requirement is not controlling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.

### Speech injunctions and remedial scope

#### [Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Madsen_v_Womens_Health_Center_Inc_merits_1994-06-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-880.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

For the other questions in Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 1994-06-30, No. 93-880, see Federal Courts — The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review; Federal Courts — An injunction binds responsible parties and actual concert, not a viewpoint; Federal Courts — The independent conduct-language claims remain undecided; Federal Courts — Relief removes invalidated predicates; Federal Courts — Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone.

##### The decree as confined is not categorically content based

**Holding:** As authoritatively confined to named responsible parties and actual concert with notice, this injunction addresses established interference with access, traffic, medical care and residential privacy rather than abortion advocacy as such; defendant-specific selection does not make it categorically content or viewpoint based. Each restraint still requires independent constitutional scrutiny, and a materially different viewpoint-based application receives no approval.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A(2), joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this application while accepting the separate actual-concert principle.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content; defendant-specific relief still requires scrutiny.
- *Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC*, June 27, 1994: applies its operation-and-justification distinction without importing its cable-specific result or plurality evidentiary application.
- *Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, Inc.*, March 24, 1993: preserves the rule that a benign objective alone does not cure a speech-selective burden.

##### A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary

**Holding:** A content-neutral injunction burdening protected expression must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve the corresponding significant governmental interest. Its precise terms require a closer fit than ordinary review of generally applicable neutral time, place and manner rules; neither an injunction label nor past misconduct permits suppression of unrelated lawful advocacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: distinguishes generally applicable neutral regulation from this direct injunction; its ordinary rule remains intact.
- *Carroll v. President and Commissioners of Princess Anne*, 393 U.S. 175: applies its demand for precision in speech-burdening injunctive relief.
- *NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.*: applies the relation between unlawful conduct, responsible persons and permissible relief.
- *Califano v. Yamasaki*, 442 U.S. 682: applies the limitation of equitable relief to what the established violation requires.

##### The qualified public access arrangement survives

**Holding:** The public portion of the 36-foot clinic clearance, with its retained south shoulder, east shoulder subject to the five-foot setback and no-embankment rule, and prompt-crossing exception, burdens no more speech than necessary on this record to secure driveway access and traffic after narrower obstruction commands failed. It remains enforceable at that qualified scope, without creating a generally permissible clinic-buffer distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five on geographic fit. Stevens adds a vote to leave the arrangement intact only against the categorical challenge; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the expressive exclusion.

**Material application:** The southern boundary is the south edge of Dixie Way's pavement, leaving the unpaved south shoulder available subject to lawful restrictions. On the east, the U.S. Highway One shoulder remains usable, but defendants may not ascend the embankment and must remain five feet from the clinic's east line. Pending painted lines, prompt crossing uses an eight-foot strip directly from Dixie Way's south to north edge, with its western line coextensive with Highway One's western right-of-way line; once marked, the crosswalk governs. Within the buffer no other pavement entry is allowed. Crossing permits no stopping, hesitation, picketing, congregation, demonstration or traffic impediment. This is an existing crossing dimension, not a personal-separation order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Carroll*: applies precision to the access boundary and actual crossing and shoulder permissions.
- *Claiborne Hardware*: confines the expressive burden to supported access relief.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*, June 13, 1994: applies attention to usable alternatives without creating an entitlement to every preferred location.

##### The private-property extension lacks the necessary remedial connection

**Holding:** The north/west extension of the 36-foot exclusion cannot suppress otherwise lawful expression on adjoining private land without a demonstrated connection between that full exclusion and the access or operational harm to be remedied. Florida's approval of that extension is reversed; removing the government's expressive restriction neither authorizes trespass nor adjudicates owners' property rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on their stricter grounds; Stevens declines distinct geographic review.

**Material application:** The north/west owners and invitees were excepted from the entry prohibition, while the other expressive restrictions still applied when they acted in concert. Removing the governmental expressive exclusion adjudicates neither permission to enter nor private property boundaries.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies the requirement that relief correspond to the established wrong.
- *Carroll*: applies precision to the unsupported geographic extension.
- *Ladue*: preserves practical channel analysis; it supplies no right to use another person's property.

##### The qualified medical-noise restriction survives

**Holding:** Paragraph (4)'s noise restriction is valid in its medical-operation context from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery periods, as to the listed and other sounds within earshot of patients inside the clinic. Its affirmance rests on the treatment interference established here, not disagreement with a message, and adds no decibel, intent or newly drafted volume threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on strict direct-restraint and adequate-unlawful-predicate grounds, despite accepting fit under the Court's assumed standard; Stevens declines merits.

**Material application:** The covered sounds are singing, chanting, whistling, shouting, yelling, bullhorns, auto horns, amplification equipment and other sounds within the earshot of patients inside the clinic, in the lower court's medical-operation context. Both the 7:30 a.m.–noon Monday–Saturday window and the surgery-or-recovery condition apply; neither is replaced by a newly invented sound threshold.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: applies the distinction between controlling sound's physical effects and suppressing its content; the heightened injunction rule governs this remedy.
- *Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement*, June 19, 1992: preserves the distinction between a neutral harm and burdens resting on hostility to a message.
- *Carroll*: applies precision through the time, treatment and patient-location limits.

##### The prohibition on observable images is too broad

**Holding:** The prohibition on all images observable to patients inside the clinic, even within the stated morning surgery-and-recovery periods, burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be removed. Patient vulnerability or distress at a message does not establish a general power to prevent visible advocacy; independently lawful threat or intrusion relief remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens declines merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville*, 422 U.S. 205: applies protection against broad suppression merely because images are visible to unwilling viewers.
- *Boos v. Barry*, 485 U.S. 312: applies the distinction between communicative emotional effects and neutral physical harms.
- *Carroll*: requires precision beyond a categorical visual prohibition unsupported by the demonstrated need.

##### Peaceful initial approach cannot depend on advance invitation throughout 300 feet

**Holding:** Paragraph (5)'s requirement of an indication of interest before any physical approach to a person seeking clinic services within 300 feet burdens more speech than necessary and must be removed. This holding protects peaceful initial advocacy, not a right to accompany, encircle, surround, threaten, obstruct or force communication upon a person who declines it, and approves no universal application of disputed harassment or abuse terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-E, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens dissents from reversal of this physical-approach condition.

**Material application:** The clause regulates physical approach, not every word or sign within 300 feet. Invited nonthreatening conversation and literature were permitted when a service-seeker approached or inquired, but never inside the 36-foot zone. Protections against accompanying, encircling, surrounding, harassment, threats and physical or verbal abuse of a declining person retain only their independently lawful scope; reversal neither invalidates every conduct protection nor approves every disputed use of those labels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies protection of persuasion and the connection between misconduct and relief.
- *Boos*: preserves political approach while allowing properly grounded protection against threats and coercion.
- *Carroll*: requires the restriction to distinguish protected initiation from the conduct justifying restraint.

##### Residential privacy does not justify this territorial exclusion

**Holding:** The 300-foot residential prohibition on approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstrating and amplification sweeps beyond the supported protection of a targeted home and must be removed. Its advance-invitation qualification does not restore general neighborhood expression; distinct lawful obstruction, trespass, threat and properly focused residential protections are preserved without selecting a replacement distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-F, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six on this bounded ground. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal under their stricter direct-restraint account; the judgment is unanimous.

**Material application:** Paragraph (6)'s separate bans on blocking entrances, exits or driveways and impeding a street providing sole access to the residences' street are distinct from the territorial ban. The separate invited-contact provision allows willing contact outside the clinic buffer, with residential invitation communicated from a distance before contact, not obtained by an uninvited knock or doorbell. Invitation may be revoked, requiring immediate termination and departure. Retained language must conform to reversal and cannot recreate the invalid territorial exclusion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Frisby v. Schultz*, 487 U.S. 474: applies its distinction between focused home picketing and general neighborhood expression; its narrow residential protection remains.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*: applies practical attention to lost channels while preserving the distinction between one's own home and intrusion on another's.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Florida judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded for a conforming decree. The qualified public-access arrangement and medical-noise restriction remain; the private extension, images prohibition, initial-invitation requirement and residential territorial ban must be removed, including dependent use under paragraph (9). The predecessor and independently lawful unreviewed terms are not automatically dissolved. The court may consider precisely fitted lawful conduct relief, but no replacement injunction, personal distance, new sound threshold, damages or contempt judgment is commanded.

### Private conspiracies and clinic obstruction

#### Bray v. Alexandria Women’s Health Clinic, 506 U.S. 263 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-985.

**Decided:** January 13, 1993.

##### Private conspiracy requires purposeful interference with a privately protected right

**Holding:** A purely private conspiracy under the deprivation clause of §1985(3) must aim at interfering with a right protected against private as well as official encroachment; incidental effects are insufficient. Clinic obstruction applied to interstate and local patients alike does not establish that interference with interstate travel was a conscious objective merely because substantial numbers of patients crossed state lines.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. Souter separately agrees with rejection of this theory without a formal join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88 — applied; coverage of private conspiracies retains purposeful, invidiously discriminatory deprivation requirements.
- United Brotherhood of Carpenters & Joiners v. Scott, 463 U.S. 825 — applied; a private conspiracy must target a privately protected right.
- United States v. Guest, 383 U.S. 745 — applied by analogy; injury to a traveler differs from purposeful interference with interstate travel.

##### Constitutional abortion liberty does not itself supply the private-right element

**Holding:** Constitutional protection of abortion choice against governmental interference does not itself create a right against the purely private obstruction proved here. Without necessary state involvement or another right protected against private encroachment, the deprivation clause cannot sustain this theory merely because the conspiracy deliberately seeks to prevent abortions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters of this distinct sufficient ground addressing the alternative privacy theory. Souter separately accepts Scott’s private-right limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott, 463 U.S. 825 — applied; the underlying constitutional guarantee’s state/private distinction remains.
- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — preserved as carried forward and modified by Casey; no direct private-obstruction cause follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992 — preserved; its six-step state-regulation framework does not change §1985(3)’s elements.
- Great American Federal Savings & Loan Assn. v. Novotny, 442 U.S. 366 — applied; §1985(3) does not itself create the substantive right.

##### Federal fees fall while independent state relief requires examination

**Holding:** The existing §1988 fee-and-cost award cannot stand on the rejected §1985(3) liability and is vacated. The injunction returns for examination of its state trespass/public-nuisance foundations, jurisdiction and lawful scope; federal merits failure alone neither sustains it nor requires its dissolution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. Souter agrees to remove the present federal basis but would additionally direct consideration of hindrance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Farrar v. Hobby, December 14, 1992 — distinguished; enforceable federal nominal relief differs from reversal of the only adjudicated federal liability basis.
- Gibbs, 383 U.S. 715 — applied; pendent adjudication and support for particular relief remain distinct.

##### The unadjudicated hindrance theory is not resolved here

**Holding:** The Court does not adjudicate liability under §1985(3)’s separate clause concerning conspiracies to prevent or hinder constituted state authorities from giving or securing equal protection. That theory was not adjudicated below or fairly included in the granted deprivation-clause questions, and no finding establishes the required purpose to hinder state protection; the remand does not direct liability under that theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part V, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters control scope and mandate. Ultimate substantive requirements of the separate clause remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido, April 1, 1992 — applied; a distinct claim outside the grant differs from additional argument on a presented claim.
- Kamen v. Kemper Financial Services, Inc., 500 U.S. 90 — applied at that scope; additional legal arguments do not erase claim limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Women’s status as a qualifying class, invidious sex-based animus, the full nonracial class boundary, a categorical rule for equal intrastate obstruction and statutory authority for an injunction on a successful federal claim remain unresolved. Scalia Part III has four votes and supplies no alternative holding. The hindrance theory receives no merits ruling; a later properly pleaded and supported claim is not barred on its merits by this disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse deprivation-clause liability, vacate the $27,687.55 federal fee-and-cost award and remand for the injunction’s state trespass/public-nuisance grounds, pendent jurisdiction and lawful scope. Federal merits failure does not automatically dissolve the injunction or dismiss the entire action for lack of jurisdiction. The five-Justice mandate directs no hindrance adjudication.
