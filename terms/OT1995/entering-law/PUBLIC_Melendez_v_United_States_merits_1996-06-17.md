## Event

**Melendez v. United States, 518 U.S. 120, No. 95-5661 — June 17, 1996.** Argued February 27, 1996, on certiorari to the Third Circuit, 55 F.3d 130. The question is whether a Government substantial-assistance motion limited to departure below the Guidelines range also authorizes a sentence below the statutory minimum.

The Guidelines range was 135–168 months; the separate statutory floor was 120 months. The Government's October 7 letter requested a §5K1.1 departure below the Guidelines range, consistent with a conditional cooperation promise in the May 10 plea agreement. It did not request below-minimum relief. The sentencing judge imposed 120 months and five years of supervised release on November 23, 1993. The Third Circuit affirmed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Third Circuit's judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Breyer.

The 120-month sentence and five-year supervised-release term remain unchanged. The Court orders no resentencing or departure below the statutory floor. Independent statutory, constitutional or plea-enforcement claims are not adjudicated by implication and remain subject to their own lawful procedures.

## Holdings

### I. Statutory-minimum relief requires Government authorization of that relief

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all participating Justices except Stevens: eight direct joins.

**Operative rule:** Section 3553(e) requires the Government to request or consent to a sentence below the statutory minimum to reflect substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another person who committed an offense; useful assistance alone and Commission policy cannot supply that authorization. The request may express the necessary authorization in substance without a separate document, prescribed formula or citation to §3553(e).

**Controlling explanation:** The motion condition attaches to the statutory relief Congress makes available. Reading any acknowledgment of assistance as authorization to disregard the statutory floor would detach the Government's request from that relief. The court decides the appropriate reduction only after the relevant authority exists; this does not give the prosecutor power to dictate the precise extent of an authorized statutory departure.

Section 3553(e) also requires the authorized sentence to accord with Commission guidelines and policy statements under §994. Section 994(n) directs the Commission to reflect the general appropriateness of lower sentences for substantial assistance, including sentences below statutory minima. Neither provision empowers the Commission to replace Congress's motion condition. Wade preserves the motion prerequisite, while reserving the independent operation now at issue. Stinson makes explanatory commentary authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to a federal statute, inconsistent with the Guideline or a plainly erroneous reading of it; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Neal likewise preserves distinct statutory and Guidelines measures within their respective fields.

The requirement concerns the substance of authorization, not the number of papers. Nor is it declared an Article III jurisdictional limit. Wade's existing review for unconstitutional purpose, including race or religion, and refusal unrelated to any legitimate governmental end remains; useful assistance alone establishes neither. Discovery or a hearing still requires a substantial threshold showing of improper motive; useful assistance, with or without generalized accusations, is insufficient. Its particular corrective remedies remain unresolved. No such refusal claim is decided here.

### II. The present Guidelines do not convert this limited request into statutory permission

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six direct joins.

**Operative rule:** The existing §5K1.1 provisions permit a Guidelines assistance departure without making every such departure conditional on authorization to sentence below an applicable statutory minimum. The motion actually made here requested Guidelines relief alone, so it did not authorize a sentence below 120 months and the sentence must be affirmed.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5K1.1 permits departure from the Guidelines on the Government's appropriate assistance request. Application note 1 addresses below-statute sentencing under the circumstances of §§3553(e) and 994(n); it preserves the statutory condition rather than treating a limited request as authorization for different relief. The Government's letter requested reduction from the Guidelines minimum, and the plea agreement promised no particular sentence. Neither assistance's value nor the letter's mere existence supplies an unexpressed below-floor request.

The contrary reading combines two different proposals. A Guidelines-only request cannot be converted into statutory authority Congress conditions on a request for that relief. A system conditioning Guidelines departures on the kind of motion required for statutory relief presents a different question. The Court concludes that the existing provisions do not impose that condition; whether the Commission could lawfully adopt such a system remains open. Text and structure resolve this case, so Granderson's residual lenity does not change the result merely because interpretations differ.

Once proper authority exists, the court determines the appropriate reduction for stated reasons. The nonexclusive assistance considerations include significance and usefulness with attention to the Government's evaluation; truthfulness, completeness and reliability; nature and extent; resulting injury, danger or risk to the defendant or family; and timeliness. Not all five must be proved. Assistance remains distinct from acceptance of responsibility, and the Government's assessment receives substantial weight, especially when value is difficult to assess. Those standards inform lawful reductions without requiring the Court to assign different direct referents to §5K1.1's two sentences. They do not independently authorize crossing the statutory floor.

The Court does not decide a particular reduction, safety-valve entitlement, drug-quantity issue or plea withdrawal. It also declines the distinct plea-interpretation theory: the statutory question is resolvable without new contract findings, and no basis for exceptional reach is established. This is nonreach, not a merits rejection or finding of intentional waiver.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Wade v. United States, May 18, 1992:** Its Government-motion prerequisite and constitutional refusal-review rules remain. The Court resolves the reserved relationship between the actual statutory and Guidelines requests without selecting a refusal remedy or changing Wade's discovery threshold.
- **Stinson v. United States, May 3, 1993:** Applied to authoritative commentary within constitutional, statutory and Guideline-consistency limits; commentary cannot supply authority contrary to Congress's condition.
- **Neal v. United States, January 22, 1996:** Applied for the distinction between enacted statutory rules and the Commission's delegated measures; its different LSD question did not previously decide this motion issue.
- **United States v. Granderson:** Residual lenity remains available after ordinary interpretation leaves the required ambiguity; disagreement alone does not establish it here.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp., February 21, 1995:** Permission to consider an included legal argument does not require adjudicating a distinct factual plea-promise theory. No new categorical bar to late arguments is adopted.
- **Santobello v. New York, 404 U.S. 257:** Enforcement of an established plea promise remains available on its own requirements; no promise breach or remedy is decided here.

## Law After Decision

Under the present provisions, Guidelines assistance relief and statutory-minimum relief retain distinct authorization requirements. Substance controls whether the Government has authorized the latter; a mandatory second paper is unnecessary. This motion left the statutory floor in force. The Commission's possible adoption of a different coordinated-request system, independently preserved plea claims and constitutional refusal review remain within their stated reservations. No earlier decision is overruled.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring.** Souter joins the Court's opinion because it does not make a sentence-by-sentence division of §5K1.1 necessary to the result. He reads both sentences as addressing Guidelines departures. The Application Notes, given policy-statement force by §1B1.7, extend the assistance standards to authorized statutory departures and fulfill §994(n)'s direction. That route preserves Congress's separate authorization requirement and the Court's reading of the existing Guidelines. It gives no support to automatic statutory permission, a jurisdictional label or assistance-only entitlement to discovery.

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** Stevens believes the Commission intended a unitary assistance system, and therefore does not join the Court's construction of its intent. Congress nevertheless placed the statutory-minimum exception behind the Government's authorization of that relief. The Commission cannot convert this Guidelines-only request into permission to disregard the floor. His objections to mandatory minima do not change that statutory constraint, so he affirms. His ground neither supplies a join to Part II nor combines with the dissent's different conditioning theory to establish another controlling rule.

**Breyer, joined by O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The dissent accepts Congress's statutory condition and joins Part I. It reads the Commission's Guidelines as requiring the same kind of motion for a Guidelines assistance departure as would authorize a statutory departure. The identical assistance language, cross-referencing commentary, §994(n)'s delegation and the analogous Rule 35(b) structure support that coordination. On this reading, the lower courts cannot accept the motion as sufficient to reduce 135 months to the statutory floor while treating it conclusively as insufficient for statutory relief without examining the coordinated requirement.

The dissent accepts the letter's actual terms and does not claim the Commission can override Congress. It would remand to determine whether this motion sufficed for both departures or for neither, not declare that it necessarily authorizes a sentence below 120 months. It directs no amount and adjudicates no unpresented Government request to increase the existing sentence. Actual preservation and procedural limits would govern any ensuing relief; no new cross-appeal or breach finding is supplied.
