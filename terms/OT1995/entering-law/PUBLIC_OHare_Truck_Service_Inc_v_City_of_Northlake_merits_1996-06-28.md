# Public decision reading copy

## Event

O'Hare Truck Service, Inc. v. City of Northlake, No. 95-191. Argued March 20, 1996; decided June 28, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 47 F.3d 883, under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1).

The First Amendment question concerns removal from an existing recurring towing-referral relationship for refusing requested political support and supporting the incumbent mayor's opponent. The separate procedural-due-process judgment rejecting a property entitlement is not before the Court for reopening.

The case was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6). O'Hare alleges longstanding participation in a police rotation list, assurances concerning satisfactory service, refusal of a requested contribution, support and posters for an opposing candidate, and removal at the reelected mayor's direction. Those allegations are accepted for the motion, not established as true after factfinding. Northlake neither paid O'Hare nor entered a direct towing-services contract; vehicle owners paid. The governmental benefit was recurring access to controlled referrals.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment dismissing the First Amendment claim is reversed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the qualified affiliation, speech and causation rules. The separate due-process disposition remains undisturbed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: First Amendment dismissal reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The claim may proceed beyond categorical contractor exclusion. The Court establishes neither actual retaliation nor municipal attribution, personal liability, a property right or a damages amount. It orders no restoration to the list, permanent referrals, evidentiary hearing or trial and decides no new immunity question. Summary judgment remains available under ordinary standards on an appropriately developed record.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. The seven-Justice opinion directly supports the complete qualified framework below; no combination of competing rationales is necessary.

## Holdings

### Political affiliation in an existing recurring service relationship

**Controlling proposition:** Government may not withdraw an existing independent-contractor or regular service-provider relationship because the provider refuses partisan support or supports an opponent, unless the government demonstrates that political affiliation is an appropriate requirement for effective performance of the actual work. That protection reaches the pleaded recurring towing referrals even without a direct municipal purchase, employment relationship or property entitlement.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion and the seven-Justice coalition identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Elrod and Branti protect against withdrawal of governmental work for an impermissible partisan condition. Rutan confirms in the employment setting that the constitutional question is not confined to formal tenure or a narrow category of benefits. Perry separates an entitlement to receive a benefit from the government's authority to withhold it for a forbidden reason. The recurring relationship alleged here supplies the concrete benefit and retaliation risk needed for the extension.

Branti's exception depends on function, not a label. A genuinely policymaking or confidential role can justify a political requirement when the government shows why allegiance is appropriate to effective performance. Calling someone a contractor, policymaker or confidential worker does not itself make that showing. General patronage benefits, rewarding allies and dislike of opposing support do not alone satisfy it.

The pleaded towing work formulates no policy, occupies no confidential political role and does not speak for the administration; no appropriate partisan qualification is shown at this stage. The City's control over referrals can impose an economic penalty even though customers pay the bills and the provider has other business. Legitimate safety, responsiveness, reliability, economy and integrity requirements remain available. But a generalized service concern cannot contradict the accepted allegation that political retaliation caused removal. The Court decides neither the truth of that allegation nor every initial bid, later allocation or commercial dispute.

### Distinguishing affiliation demands from speech disputes

**Controlling proposition:** The functional affiliation rule does not replace Pickering's analysis of public-concern speech or materially mixed speech-and-affiliation claims. Courts must identify the developed claim and apply the appropriate inquiry: whether political allegiance is genuinely required for the work, or whether protected citizen speech and the public's interests outweigh the government's legitimate service interests in the actual relationship.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** An asserted requirement of partisan allegiance poses a different question from the effect of particular expression on performance. Branti's specific appropriate-affiliation condition cannot be replaced by an undifferentiated claim of managerial discretion. Conversely, the presence of political association does not predetermine every dispute involving criticism, statements or their effects on services.

Pickering supplies the service-adjusted balance for the speech inquiry, with Connick's attention to content, form and context in identifying public concern. The courts below may determine in appropriate further proceedings how the refusal of support and the affirmative electoral expression relate in the developed claim. That reservation does not reinstate categorical contractor exclusion or establish a special constitutional investigation code. NTEU's broad prospective restriction and Waters's limited genuine-ground propositions retain their actual scopes; neither mechanically decides this pleading case. The controlling affiliation extension is complete with its qualifications even though particular mixed applications remain for development.

### Causation, defenses and relief

**Controlling proposition:** The claimants must establish the relevant protected conduct and show that it was a substantial or motivating factor in the removal. Under Mt. Healthy, the City may prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the same decision would have occurred absent that conduct; a hypothetical permissible reason is insufficient. Governmental attribution, individual defenses and remedies retain their ordinary independent requirements.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion applies Mt. Healthy's substantial-or-motivating-factor and same-decision sequence.

**Controlling explanation:** The First Amendment does not guarantee permanent referrals or insulate deficient performance. It forbids a causally effective unconstitutional condition, subject to proof that a lawful decision would have occurred anyway. Neutral service, safety, cost, responsiveness, reliability and integrity reasons remain available when supported by the actual record. Their possible existence cannot defeat the pleaded retaliatory reason by assumption.

Waters and Settle preserve the distinction between a genuine reason and an imagined sufficient one without establishing a new comprehensive mistake-of-fact rule here. Monell preserves separate municipal-attribution requirements; the constitutional extension does not establish the mayor's actual direction as a fact or determine personal liability. The remand leaves immunity and damages questions for lawful resolution and does not reopen the separate property/due-process holding. If the developed record presents no genuine material dispute and otherwise warrants judgment, summary judgment remains available; a trial is not the inevitable consequence of reversal.

### Limits and questions not reached

This decision concerns an existing recurring relationship, not an initial unsuccessful bidder or every governmental allocation. It creates no property entitlement, general employee status for unrelated purposes, criminal bribery ruling or right to deficient performance. It does not decide what every hypothetical reduction in referrals would require, or establish liability, immunity, attribution or relief from allegations alone. The qualified affiliation rule and the mixed-speech inquiry remain distinct.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Elrod and Branti:** Their protection against impermissible affiliation conditions is extended to the existing contractor or regular-provider relationship, retaining the government's functional appropriate-affiliation exception.
- **Rutan:** Its employee-benefit extension supports examining the reason for government action rather than confining protection to tenure; it did not itself previously resolve this recurring-provider setting.
- **Perry v. Sindermann:** Applied to distinguish lack of a property entitlement from freedom to impose an unconstitutional condition.
- **Pickering and Connick:** Supply the distinct speech/public-concern inquiry for an appropriately developed speech or mixed claim, adjusted to actual service interests rather than displaced by a universal affiliation test.
- **Mt. Healthy:** Applied without changing the claimant's motivating-factor burden or the City's preponderance same-decision defense.
- **Waters and Settle:** Their genuine-ground and actual-cause distinctions remain in force; no new complete factual-error or investigation framework is adopted.
- **National Treasury Employees Union:** Its advance-restraint rule remains distinct from this individual referral removal.
- **Monell:** Its independent governmental-attribution requirements remain necessary; reversal does not establish municipal liability.

## Law After Decision

An existing contractor or recurring service provider may challenge withdrawal of the relationship for refusing partisan support or adopting an opposing allegiance. Government must justify any asserted political qualification by the actual work, while retaining neutral service criteria and a proved same-decision defense.

The absence of direct municipal payment or a property entitlement is no categorical defense to the First Amendment claim. Speech or materially mixed disputes still require the appropriate service-adjusted analysis. The decision establishes the qualified rule and permits further proceedings, without adjudicating the pleaded motive or conferring permanent work.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia's dissent, joined by Thomas.** They would affirm because the Court should not extend public-employment patronage restrictions to this nonemployment allocation of commercial referrals. Their Rutan and National Treasury Employees Union positions favor preserving the traditional political discretion surrounding governmental work, recognizing contractors' separate business opportunities and leaving procurement rules principally to political institutions. They question the claim that loss of a commercial benefit necessarily coerces belief in the same way as employee patronage and warn that constitutional litigation would burden routine allocation and service judgments.

They also object that distinctions between affiliation and speech become difficult to administer when refusing support, displaying campaign material and criticizing officials overlap. A conditional insistence on actual causal proof and legitimate service decisions does not amount to a join in the majority's extension. Their position leaves existing employee doctrine and separate restrictions on sovereign suppression of advocacy intact. They accept the allegations for the motion and do not find that O'Hare provided deficient service, that the City had a lawful actual motive or that any criminal offense occurred. Lack of a property entitlement is not their sole ground, and the separate due-process disposition remains outside review.

## Procedure After Action

The First Amendment claim returns for further proceedings under the qualified affiliation framework, any appropriate speech analysis and the ordinary causation, attribution, defense and remedy rules. The courts below may decide the developed case through lawful summary judgment or other appropriate proceedings; no particular hearing, trial, restoration or monetary award is directed. The separate procedural-due-process disposition is undisturbed.
