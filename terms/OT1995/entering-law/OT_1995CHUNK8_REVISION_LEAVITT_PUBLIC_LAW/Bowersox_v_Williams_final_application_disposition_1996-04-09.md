## Public Projection

## Event

Bowersox v. Williams, application No. A-828; 517 U.S. 345. October Term 1995. Final application disposition, April 9, 1996, after the Court's separate interim order that day. Missouri seeks vacatur of the Eighth Circuit execution stay concerning Doyle J. Williams.

The third federal petition was denied January 11, with adoption of Magistrate Judge Hays's report and denial of amendment. A certificate of probable cause issued; rehearing en banc was denied. The March 8 appellate stay continued through submission and further order, with argument scheduled May 13. Execution had been scheduled for April 10; no exact hour or execution occurrence is established. The application now returns for decision. No transmission time, completed supplementation, response, compliance or elapsed processing interval is presumed.

## Participation

Justice O'Connor, the Eighth Circuit Justice, presents the application and refers it to the Court. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Missouri's application is **granted**. The Eighth Circuit's **March 8 execution stay is vacated**. No substitute Supreme Court stay or fixed grace period is entered. This action resolves the application, not the habeas appeal or entitlement to the writ.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Grant and vacate, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Vacatur removes the restraint imposed by the March 8 appellate stay. It does not direct execution, establish that execution occurred, dismiss the appeal or finally adjudicate an unpresented claim. The dissenters would deny the application, leaving the appellate stay governed by its existing terms and subject to lawful modification or dissolution, without a new Supreme Court stay or guaranteed protection through May 13.

## Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor gives her signed final explanation as Circuit Justice. It is the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–V, joined throughout by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Those five adopt every controlling proposition below at the limited application level.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer each file a signed dissent. Their shared substantial Hicks due-process question does not command a majority. No inter-Justice joinder in those separate statements is stated.

## Holdings

### I. The identified grounds do not sustain this successive-petition stay

**Controlling proposition:** A stay pending a second or successive federal habeas petition requires substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Applying that standard to the identified claims and bounded assumptions below, the Court finds no ground sufficient to maintain the challenged stay and vacates it under abuse-of-discretion review.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I and Parts II–V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, and Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320, supply the successive-petition standard and review of the actual basis for protection. The petition's number alone decides nothing. The certificate, argument setting and denied rehearing describe appellate consideration but do not establish substantial grounds automatically; the unexplained stay does not establish their absence either. The Court's conclusion rests on the particular assessments that follow.

The first federal petition's nonexhaustion dismissal was not a merits denial. The second proceeding's actual merits adjudication matters to the repeated instruction ground. This application uses neither Barefoot's distinct initial-certified-appeal rule nor its separate certiorari-stage test. Delo v. Blair's equal division supplies no Court rationale. No universal duty to explain appellate stays is imposed, and no habeas merits judgment is entered here.

### II. The reported penalty-cause account supplies no substantial basis for overturning the no-causation determination

**Controlling proposition:** A preference for death that leads a prisoner to withhold permission for a sentence challenge does not, by itself, establish an objective factor external to the defense that prevented presentation. On the identified account, this cause issue does not supply a substantial available ground for maintaining the stay.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, with the same five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467, distinguishes an external impediment actually preventing presentation from inexcusable neglect; intentional abandonment is not necessary to abuse. Williams's contrary evidence that prison conditions caused his preference remains relevant. But accepting the reported preference does not itself establish preventive impairment. The district's reported no-causation finding, conditions litigation and representation that legitimate sentence challenges had been raised reinforce the limited assessment.

The Court makes no credibility determination about unreproduced expert opinions and no incapacity or independent ineffective-assistance merits finding. The 1990 appellate opportunity bears on diligence without creating a categorical obligation to request remand. The Court decides neither undisclosed mitigation nor constitutional prejudice. McCleskey's requirement of clear, particular State abuse pleading before the prisoner's responsive burden remains intact; no particular pleading defect or concession is found from an unavailable filing.

### III. Prior disagreement about the first-degree instruction does not establish a substantial federal ground

**Controlling proposition:** Where genuine, evidence-supported noncapital verdict choices are supplied, Beck does not require every supported lesser-offense instruction. Williams's ordinary second-degree murder and manslaughter options defeat the asserted all-or-nothing premise; old trial evidence and prior judicial disagreement do not independently require redetermination or sustain this stay.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, with the same five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Schad v. Arizona, 501 U.S. 624, Part III, supplies this limit while expressly preserving the requirement that an alternative be genuine and supported by evidence. The reported homicide evidence and disputed premeditation give Williams's supplied noncapital homicide options practical work. The Court does not treat a nominal, unsupported option as sufficient.

Both trial parties requested the first-degree felony-murder instruction. The transport, confinement, search for seclusion and impeachment of Morgan remain a real evidence dispute, alongside the contrary prior account and witness-elimination finding. Morgan's testimony is not made conclusive. The issue was previously adjudicated on its merits. Sanders v. United States, 373 U.S. 1, retains constrained reconsideration discretion and the applicant's ends-of-justice burden, but discretion alone creates no federal merit. The old evidence and dissent do not overcome Schad. No new evidence, changed Missouri law or separately current first-degree equal-protection claim is supplied.

### IV. The distinct second-degree constitutional theories do not sustain the stay on the bounded assumptions

**Controlling propositions:** For this assessment the Court assumes the omitted second-degree felony-murder instruction was available under then-applicable state rules, was refused differently from materially similar cases, and presents a procedurally available claim. With genuine noncapital choices retained and no separate defect in them posited, that limited inconsistency does not present a substantial arbitrary-deprivation claim under Hicks. Separately, the assumed difference in treatment alone does not establish substantial unconstitutional unequal administration.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, with the same five-Justice coalition, separately adopting the due-process and equal-protection conclusions.

**Controlling explanation:** Schad answers the Beck demand for an additional option, but does not alone resolve the other constitutional theories. Hicks v. Oklahoma, 447 U.S. 343, protects a state-created opportunity against arbitrary deprivation. It involved removal of the jury's statutory sentencing discretion through an invalid mandatory rule. The bounded omission here leaves supported lawful noncapital homicide choices and supplies no separate defect in that decision process. The Court does not equate every state instructional inconsistency with destruction of the protected opportunity in Hicks.

State-law availability does not itself establish a mandatory instruction duty. On equal protection, the Court accepts the assumed similarity and different treatment; it neither requires a protected class nor finds discriminatory purpose disproved. It declines to infer the additional federal wrong from that limited inconsistency alone. No broader immunity for arbitrary state adjudication is announced. The two theories and the prior first-degree ground remain distinct, and the assumptions determine no ultimate habeas merits judgment.

### V. The proportionality allegation and proposed amendment supply no substantial ground here

**Controlling proposition:** The allegation of approximately two hundred missing comparison files does not itself establish arbitrary deprivation of Missouri's mandated proportionality-review opportunity. Introducing that distinct statewide comparative-review dispute after completed discovery and preparation of the magistrate report, when the district court was ready to rule, supplies an apparent discretionary justification for declining expansion; elapsed years alone would not.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part V, with the same five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Pulley v. Harris, 465 U.S. 37, rejects an invariable federal right to comparative proportionality review. It does not displace Hicks or immunize arbitrary performance of a state-created duty. The state opinion reports mandated review and an actual comparison considering the crime and defendant. Even accepting the missing-files allegation, its number alone does not establish deprivation of that opportunity. The Court finds neither that every file was irrelevant nor that stated review invariably satisfies due process.

Foman v. Davis, 371 U.S. 178, requires liberal amendment when justice requires, while permitting a justifying reason apparent from the record or declared by the court. Habeas Rule 11 permits civil rules only when appropriate and consistent with the habeas Rules. The new subject and completed adjudicative work together supply the apparent justification here. No bad faith, specific prejudice, early discoverability or actual district rationale is attributed. A meritorious newly discovered deprivation could warrant amendment despite the stage, but no such predicate is established by the reported allegation. The Court adopts no categorical amendment deadline.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Barefoot and Stokes:** Applied to the actual successive-petition stay; certification and scheduling do not replace substantial grounds. Barefoot's initial-certified-appeal protection and separate certiorari test retain their distinct triggers.
- **McCleskey and Sanders:** Applied to distinct omitted-new-ground and previously adjudicated-ground inquiries. Clear, particular State abuse pleading precedes the prisoner's burden; cause, actual prejudice and any applicable miscarriage exception remain distinct. Reconsideration discretion is not an obligation to reopen.
- **Beck, Hopper and Schad:** The evidence must permit a rational lesser conviction and acquittal of the greater; Schad permits genuine supported noncapital alternatives without requiring every lesser offense. An unsupported nominal option is insufficient.
- **Hicks, Pulley and Foman:** State-created opportunities remain protected against arbitrary deprivation; neither ordinary state-law error nor incomplete comparative files automatically establish it. Amendment remains liberal but discretionary for an apparent or declared justification.
- **Gomez, McFarland and Lonchar:** Independent stay equity, the actual statutory preparation opportunity and conditional protection for identified process remain effective. None supplies an unidentified further obligation or necessary additional period here. Seriousness and irreversibility alone do not compel late protection; proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction remain relevant.
- **Schlup and Sawyer:** Their gateways are not satisfied. Schlup requires reliable new evidence, evaluated with the whole probative record, showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict; possible acquittal or ordinary prejudice is insufficient. Sawyer requires clear and convincing proof that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find death eligibility; discretionary mitigation alone is insufficient. Neither gateway establishes an underlying violation or automatically grants relief. The available second-degree claim needs no gateway.
- **Delo v. Blair:** Equal division supplies no controlling rationale.

Pre-AEDPA Rule 9(b) and §2244(b) retain their actual conditions. Rule 9(b) distinguishes repeated grounds after prior merits determination from new grounds whose omission constituted abuse. Section 2244(b)'s refusal permission follows a federal denial after a material factual evidentiary hearing or legal merits hearing; its exception requires both an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground. Neither permission becomes mandatory dismissal. No delay-prejudice bar, exhaustion ruling or undisclosed default is added. No precedent is overruled or new §2251 construction adopted.

## Law After Decision

The Court applies the existing successive-petition stay standard to the identified grounds and expressly bounded second-degree assumptions. The five-Justice conclusions govern this application assessment; they establish neither a categorical bar to further federal review nor a final habeas merits disposition. The four dissenters' contrary Hicks assessment is noncontrolling.

The application is completed, and the March 8 stay no longer restrains execution. The habeas appeal remains pending. This ruling grants no writ, creates no further protection, directs no execution and establishes no execution occurrence. The earlier interim action remains a separate completed order.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Sandra Day O'Connor, signed final Circuit Justice explanation and Opinion of the Court.** I grant the application for the reasons in Parts I–V. The identified cause, repeated-instruction, distinct second-degree and amendment grounds do not satisfy the substantial-grounds standard. The available noncapital options answer the Beck issue; the assumed state-rule inconsistency does not add the federal deprivation shown in Hicks. My explanation removes the challenged stay without deciding entitlement to the writ or directing execution.

**Justice Antonin Scalia.** I vote to grant the application and vacate the March 8 stay. I adopt O'Connor's Parts I–V, including their separate grounds and limits.

**Justice Anthony M. Kennedy.** I vote to grant the application and vacate the March 8 stay. I adopt O'Connor's Parts I–V, including their separate grounds and limits.

**Justice David H. Souter.** I vote to grant the application and vacate the March 8 stay. I adopt O'Connor's Parts I–V, including their separate grounds and limits.

**Justice Clarence Thomas.** I vote to grant the application and vacate the March 8 stay. I adopt O'Connor's Parts I–V, including their separate grounds and limits.

**Justice John Paul Stevens, dissenting.** I would deny the application. A State's creation of a particular jury opportunity is not made immaterial by a different lawful noncapital option. Schad controls the minimum Beck safeguard; the available second-degree claim raises the distinct Hicks question whether inconsistent withholding of the state-created option was arbitrary. The reported intent and kidnapping dispute gives that question potential consequence. It warrants appellate consideration without establishing a violation, a writ or an automatic successive-petition stay. I need not decide an independent equal-protection ground or the other claims.

**Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg, dissenting.** I would deny the application. Genuine noncapital alternatives answer Beck but do not conclusively answer arbitrary deprivation of the separately available state-law opportunity under Hicks. On the assumed availability and inconsistent treatment, that due-process question is substantial enough for meaningful appellate examination. I find no proved violation or independent equal-protection ground and rely neither on the certificate alone nor on unknown papers. The existing stay would remain subject to lawful modification, without a new Supreme Court stay or guaranteed duration.

**Justice Stephen G. Breyer, dissenting.** I would deny the application. The available Hicks question could affect the conviction or sentence on the reported intent and kidnapping evidence. Absence of an all-or-nothing choice under Schad does not conclusively resolve the alleged arbitrary withholding of a further state-created jury opportunity. That concrete issue, rather than uncertainty or the certificate, supplies substantial grounds. Ultimate relief remains undecided; I require no new hearing, gateway or protection through every appellate step and need not decide the other claims.

**Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** I would deny the application. The available second-degree claim presents a substantial question whether Missouri arbitrarily withheld a state-created jury opportunity when it refused an available instruction differently from materially similar cases. The reported dispute about intent and the kidnapping gives that question potential consequence. Hicks protects against arbitrary deprivation of a state-created opportunity. Schad establishes that genuine noncapital verdict options satisfy Beck's minimum safeguard; it does not conclusively resolve this distinct due-process issue. Availability alone does not establish a mandatory instruction duty, and the assumed inconsistency does not prove a constitutional violation. The question is substantial enough for the existing appeal, although ultimate relief remains undecided. Denial would leave the appellate stay governed by its own terms and subject to lawful modification. I would issue no new Supreme Court stay, guarantee no particular duration, and adopt no independent ground on the other claims.
