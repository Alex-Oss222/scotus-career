## Public Projection

## Event

Stutson v. United States, 516 U.S. 193, No. 94-8988. Petition-stage grant, vacatur, and remand, January 8, 1996. On petition for certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit's unexplained affirmance of the refusal to excuse a late criminal notice of appeal. Counsel misdirected the filing, which arrived one working day late. The question is whether Pioneer and the Government's changed position warrant reconsideration of excusable neglect under Rule 4(b). The criminal merits are not before the Court.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate. The petition poll is not disclosed.

## Public Action

The Court grants leave to proceed in forma pauperis, grants certiorari, vacates the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, and remands for reconsideration in light of Pioneer. Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. No petition vote tally is announced.

## Judgment & Remedy

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated. That court must determine the proper effect of Pioneer under Rule 4(b) and arrange any necessary District Court findings. The order does not accept the late notice, require a merits appeal, find excusable neglect, reverse the conviction, or alter the sentence.

## Opinion Topology

The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stevens also files a concurrence, without an additional join. Scalia files a dissent, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

### The unresolved excusable-neglect premise warrants reconsideration

**Operative rule:** Section 2106 permits a limited return for reconsideration where the combination of a potentially important governing formulation, the Government's changed legal position, a one-working-day misdirected filing, and an unexplained appellate affirmance leaves a substantial question whether the correct excusable-neglect inquiry was performed. These circumstances warrant reconsideration of Rule 4(b) in light of Pioneer; no one circumstance, including a Government concession, makes vacatur automatic.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These seven directly adopt this case-specific procedural ground and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Pioneer Investment Services construed excusable neglect in Bankruptcy Rule 9006(b)(1) to include mistake and carelessness, while requiring an assessment of all relevant circumstances and attributing freely chosen counsel's conduct to the client. Its possible relevance matters because the Government originally opposed using that formulation for criminal appeals and now accepts its relevance. The unexplained affirmance leaves uncertain whether the appellate court considered it.

The short delay and changed position do not prove an erroneous judgment. Pioneer was already available; the court below may have considered it and denied relief on a lawful ground. But that possibility does not remove the concrete value of reconsidering a legal premise that can determine whether appellate review is available. Section 2106 permits that procedural step while leaving actual excusability to the receiving court. Neither administrative convenience nor Government agreement alone supplies the reason.

The Eleventh Circuit must determine Pioneer's proper effect under Rule 4(b) and arrange any necessary District Court findings. Relevant circumstances include prejudice, delay and its effects, the reason for delay and its reasonable controllability, and good faith; no factor is invariably decisive under Pioneer. Counsel attribution remains. The Court does not decide that all Rule 4(b) applications are identical to bankruptcy enlargement, transplant Rule 9006(b)(2)–(3)'s separate restrictions, find excusable neglect, accept the late appeal, or adjudicate conviction or sentence.

**Precedent treatment:** Pioneer supplies the potentially consequential formulation at its actual Chapter 11 scope and retains counsel attribution and its rule-specific limits. Henry's distinction between reconsideration and merits relief is applied.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates Limited Partnership, 507 U.S. 380:** Applied as the potentially relevant excusable-neglect formulation, preserving its Chapter 11 scope, all-circumstances inquiry, counsel attribution, and rule-specific enlargement limits. No complete Rule 4(b) merits extension is decided.
- **Henry v. City of Rock Hill, 376 U.S. 776:** Its distinction between reconsideration and final merits adjudication is applied.
- **Alvarado v. United States, 497 U.S. 543:** Independent judicial justification remains necessary; changed Government advocacy does not dictate the disposition.

## Law After Decision

This record requires renewed consideration of the appellate-timeliness ruling. A short delay is not automatically excusable, a Government confession creates no automatic GVR, and an unexplained judgment is not conclusively presumed erroneous. The lower court retains responsibility for Rule 4(b), actual neglect, and the availability of appellate review.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court and adds that case-specific, totality-of-the-circumstances discretion is a proper part of certiorari administration. The combination of an uncertain review premise and the potential loss of appellate consideration supports obtaining a fresh lower-court judgment without deciding excusability. This position creates no entitlement for every short delay and no exception to attribution of counsel's conduct. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny. Pioneer was available when the lower courts acted, and an unexplained affirmance may have applied its approach and still rejected the excuse. The Government concedes a point of law concerning relevance, not error in the judgment. Its changed argument therefore establishes neither new intervening law nor a defective judicial premise. The dissent accepts vacatur without a merits determination for an intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment. It does not foreclose plenary review or a justified error-correcting disposition. None of those grounds is established here. Uncertainty about which ground sustained a judgment, or a desire for better reasoning, does not itself justify vacatur; the proper exercise of §2106 requires more than the possibility that reconsideration could change the result.

## Procedure After Action

Proceedings resume through the Eleventh Circuit under the limited remand. A lawful renewed denial remains available; no merits appeal or criminal relief is guaranteed.
