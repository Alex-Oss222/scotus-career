## Public Projection

## Event

Carlisle v. United States, No. 94-9247. Argued January 16, 1996; decided April 29, 1996. October Term 1995. On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Sixth Circuit's judgment, 48 F.3d 190, reversing a postverdict judgment of acquittal for lack of authority under Rule 29.

The jury returned a marijuana-conspiracy guilty verdict and was discharged July 13, 1993. Under the then-operative Rule 45(a), the seven-day period excluded intervening weekends, making July 22 the deadline. Counsel filed July 23. The Government timely objected. No trial motion, reserved decision or extension fixed within the seven days is established.

The judge initially denied acquittal on sufficiency grounds, then reconsidered at the October 14 sentencing hearing and entered acquittal October 28. He acknowledged the late filing and relied on lack of Government prejudice and prevention of injustice. The two assessments drew different inferences from the same trial conduct; neither establishes factual innocence. The Sixth Circuit decided only authority, not disputed evidentiary sufficiency, and directed reinstatement of the verdict and sentencing.

That mandate was implemented before this review: sentence was imposed June 27, 1995. The July 7 amended judgment vacated the earlier judgment under Rule 35(c), readjudged guilt and retained June 27 as the sentence-imposition date. The present issue is authority to grant the late acquittal, not a new guilt or sentencing determination.

## Participation

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision. No Justice is recused or otherwise nonparticipating.

## Public Action

The Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed, 7–2.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices Stevens and Kennedy would reverse the no-authority ruling and remand for appellate consideration of the Government's disputed evidentiary-sufficiency ground. They would not finally restore acquittal before that review.

The late acquittal remains unauthorized. The reinstated conviction and already-entered sentencing judgment are undisturbed on the issue reviewed. No duplicate sentencing, new trial, release or custody-status determination is ordered. Independently authorized and properly presented sufficiency, sentencing or collateral claims retain their lawful posture; none is adjudicated or automatically reopened here.

## Holdings

### I. Rules 29 and 45 do not authorize this new, untimely postverdict acquittal

**Controlling proposition:** With the Government's timely objection preserved, the district court lacked authority to grant Carlisle's new postverdict motion outside Rule 29(c)'s period. Rule 45(b) excludes ordinary excusable-neglect enlargement beyond Rule 29's own conditions. This is a limitation on the requested motion remedy, not a loss of federal criminal subject-matter jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** The applicable Rule 29(c) permits a motion within seven days after jury discharge or within further time fixed during that period. It authorizes setting aside a guilty verdict on such a motion and requires no earlier trial motion. Rule 29(a) requires acquittal, on a defendant's or the court's motion after the evidence closes, if the evidence cannot sustain conviction. Rule 29(b) permits reserving a timely trial motion and deciding it later on the evidence when reserved. These provisions supply different authorized paths; none was followed here.

Rule 45(b)'s general enlargement power expressly excepts action under Rules 29, 33, 34 and 35 except to the extent and under the conditions those Rules state. A one-day delay, attorney error or absence of Government prejudice therefore cannot itself supply power to extend this period.

The strongest contrary reading relies on Rule 29(a)'s lack of an express terminal phrase and the judge's responsibility not to enter judgment on insufficient evidence. But reading that provision to authorize the identical new acquittal without the postverdict conditions would make those conditions ineffective. The Court relies on the integrated text, not a caption alone. Sentencing supplies no substitute deadline in the Rule.

The decision leaves open a court-initiated action within the permitted period, reconsideration of a timely motion, valid reservation and Government forfeiture of its objection. Neither an earlier denial nor the judge's later changed evidentiary appraisal created a timely motion or reservation here. Enforcing this vehicle's conditions does not decide that the trial evidence was sufficient.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith's rejection of an unrestricted judicial substitute for a restricted new-trial motion supports the structural reasoning, although its postappeal posture differs. Pioneer permits excusable-neglect flexibility only where authorized and preserves specific enlargement restrictions. Neither case supplies a general extension here.

### II. General rules and inherent authority cannot supply the same act that the specific Rules foreclose

**Controlling proposition:** Rule 2, Rule 57 and inherent supervisory authority do not authorize this otherwise prohibited postverdict acquittal. The asserted separate pre-sentence power cannot circumvent Rules 29(c) and 45(b) by changing the label attached to the same relief. The holding does not abolish inherent judicial authority generally.

**Authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 2 directs just determination, simplicity, fairness and elimination of unjustifiable expense and delay. It guides interpretation; it does not displace a specific restriction. Former Rule 57 allowed consistent practice where the Rules made no provision. This case presents a governing provision, not a procedural gap.

The December 1, 1995 amendment retains consistency with federal law and national and local rules. Its protection against nonwillful violations of local form requirements does not excuse a national Rule 29 deadline. Its actual-notice protection concerns requirements outside federal law, federal rules and local rules. The amendment governs pending proceedings only insofar as just and practicable; the Court neither applies it automatically to the 1993 conduct nor derives an enlargement power from it.

The dissent's distinct retained-power argument is substantial: a trial judge traditionally protects against legally insufficient convictions while the case remains before the court. But the asserted authority here would deliver precisely the relief the specific postverdict mechanism restricts. Bank of Nova Scotia requires supervision to remain consistent with governing rules. Williams's bounded supervision of a materially distorted grand-jury presentation survives; its prejudice-based, rule-consistent remedy does not authorize this conflicting exercise.

Fallen likewise does not supply an attorney-error exception. There the ill, effectively unrepresented prisoner did all reasonably possible to transmit a timely notice. That treatment of timely efforts differs from excusing counsel's admitted late motion. Existing inherent powers are not denied, and hypothetical Article III limits on withdrawing them are not presented. The Court decides the claimed power in this rule structure, not every judicial correction before judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Bank of Nova Scotia and Williams preserve bounded, rule-consistent supervision. Fallen is distinguished on its timely-effort premise. Lonchar preserves process genuinely required by law without an additional equitable substitute; it establishes no still-owed proceeding requiring this late motion.

### III. The alternative writ and due-process theories do not require this acquittal or a second trial

**Controlling proposition:** Neither the All Writs Act nor due process supplies authority for the untimely acquittal on these facts. The Government may obtain restoration of the existing verdict without a second factfinding trial. The ruling preserves, but does not adjudicate or guarantee, independently available review of properly presented claims.

**Authority:** Part III of the same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1651 permits necessary or appropriate writs in aid of jurisdiction consistent with the usages and principles of law. Morgan's extraordinary coram nobis remedy is not an alternative deadline for counsel's late motion in an ongoing criminal case. No independent exceptional predicate is established here.

Nor is this a case of a predeadline judicial assurance inducing lateness. The judge's later reconsideration supplies no such assurance. The Court need not decide every judicial-misinformation case to reject ordinary counsel error as that different circumstance; Harris Truck Lines and Thompson do not establish the missing predicate.

Jackson protects against conviction without legally sufficient evidence, but does not itself require this additional late district-court vehicle. Carlisle had the opportunities provided by the Rules; a missed motion is not automatically informed waiver of every other claim. Remaining appellate and collateral channels depend on their own authority, preservation, proof and procedural conditions. Plain-error review is not declared equivalent to ordinary sufficiency review, and no ineffective-assistance remedy is promised. AEDPA's new conditions and unresolved application questions must be respected, rather than describing collateral relief as unlimited.

Finally, Wilson permits the Government's appeal because reversal restores the jury's existing verdict without subjecting Carlisle to another trial. That consequence does not decide sufficiency, which the Sixth Circuit left unresolved. Reinstatement and sentencing have already occurred, so affirmance orders neither repetition nor release. The dissent's requested sufficiency-review remand follows from its different authority construction, not from a sufficiency determination made by this Court.

**Precedent treatment:** Morgan's extraordinary remedy is distinguished; Jackson's substantive protection and Wilson's no-second-trial rule remain intact. Olano's forfeiture/waiver distinction and conditioned correction survive, without converting appellate power into district-court enlargement or supplying a new collateral holding.

## Precedent Treatment

The Court applies the specific-rule limits while preserving Williams's bounded supervisory authority and Bank of Nova Scotia's consistency requirement. Smith's rule-structure reasoning is applied with its later procedural posture distinguished; Fallen's protection of timely efforts is not converted into ordinary excusable neglect. Pioneer, Olano and Libretti preserve the separate inquiries into authorized correction, procedural default, actual waiver and lawful punishment.

Lonchar and Cooper require meaningful performance of legally required process but establish no additional Rule 29 route. Markman's reserved civil motion and Holly Farms' distinct statutory exception do not change this criminal deadline. Morgan, Jackson and Wilson retain the scopes explained above. No precedent is overruled; Manypenny's reconsideration of a timely motion is not decided to govern this new late motion.

## Law After Decision

On these facts, Rules 29(c) and 45(b) bar the new late acquittal, and general practice rules, inherent supervision, coram nobis and due process do not supply the same relief. The rule is not a subject-matter-jurisdiction holding or a determination of guilt or evidentiary sufficiency. Timely motions and reservations, possible timely court action, Government forfeiture and materially different judicial-assurance circumstances remain outside the decision.

Any separately available federal collateral proceeding must account for AEDPA, enacted April 24. Section 2255 now contains a one-year period measured from the latest specified event: finality; removal of an unlawful governmental filing impediment; a newly recognized Supreme Court right made retroactive on collateral review; or discovery of supporting facts through due diligence. A second or successive motion requires appellate certification for qualifying newly discovered evidence sufficient, if proved and considered with the whole record, to establish clearly and convincingly that no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt, or for a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by the Supreme Court. The amended appellate-certificate provisions require the specified substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identification of qualifying issues.

These are statutory conditions, not a determination that a particular later Carlisle proceeding satisfies or is governed by them. Chapter 154's pending-case clause does not resolve every other transition question. The June 1995 dismissal of an early §2255 motion as premature establishes no subsequent-motion classification, deadline, custody predicate or entitlement here. No new collateral action or remedy is ordered.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justice Kennedy.** They would reverse the no-authority ruling and remand for appellate consideration of the Government's disputed sufficiency ground. Their source of power is the court's retained pre-sentence judicial authority to prevent judgment on legally insufficient evidence, informed by the acquittal duty recognized in Rule 29(a). They do not treat that subdivision alone as an unrestricted postverdict motion remedy.

They accept that Rules 29(c) and 45(b) foreclose the defendant's late-motion entitlement, but do not read those provisions as withdrawing the judge's distinct authority while the case awaits sentence. Smith's completed-appellate setting makes its finality concern materially different. Manypenny's timely-motion holding does not itself decide this further question, but recognizes judicial correction within a continuing proceeding. Williams supplies an example of bounded supervision; it is not a license to disregard a controlling rule. On their reading, no rule withdraws the particular power asserted here.

The Court's strongest objection is that this construction allows the same relief the time limit restricts and substitutes sentencing for the specified deadline. The dissent answers that the source and function differ: a lost party motion does not compel entry of a judgment the judge concludes lacks legal evidentiary support. The position is confined to that pre-sentence responsibility, not a one-day, no-prejudice or general fairness exception, and it does not reopen completed criminal judgments indefinitely.

The dissent does not decide that the evidence was insufficient or that Carlisle was factually innocent. The Government contested the sufficiency ruling, and the Sixth Circuit did not resolve it. Acquittal therefore could not finally be restored without that review. The dissent orders neither a new trial nor release and does not rely on an unrestricted collateral remedy or automatic judicial-assurance exception.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment below is affirmed on the authority question. The unauthorized acquittal is not restored; the existing conviction and completed sentencing judgment remain undisturbed at this review. No new proceeding, sentencing repetition, special mandate date or further Supreme Court action is prescribed. Independently authorized review remains subject to its own conditions and actual procedural posture.
