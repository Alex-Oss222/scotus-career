## Civil Rights

### Abortion emergency care

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203

**Holding:** Section 3203 survives this facial challenge under the prospective construction that compliance with the Act cannot create a significant threat to the patient's life or health. Its good-faith clinical standard permits prompt treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset, without waiting for catastrophic deterioration; the ruling does not adjudicate every other postviability restriction or every degree of clinical risk.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3203 defines medical emergency through the physician's good-faith clinical judgment: a condition necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or one in which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The significant-threat interpretation and application to the three obstetric conditions are the Third Circuit's construction, not additional statutory words or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court interpretation. Section 3205(c)'s separate disclosure defense is not an emergency-delay exception. A materially different authoritative construction or enforcement remains challengeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Facial relief against the emergency definition is denied under the prospective protective construction. No separate vagueness judgment is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: applies its attention to a medical rule's workable, prudent construction; does not adopt every part of its fractured abortion analysis.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton: their retained necessary-care protection is stated separately at its own proposition-level coalition; this unanimous statutory construction does not create unanimous adherence to their entire reasoning.

##### Emergency explanation under §3205(b)

**Holding:** Section 3205(b) requires an explanation of the medical indications supporting emergency abortion before the procedure only if possible. It does not condition necessary immediate care on completing an explanation or obtaining advance permission, and it survives the facial challenge on that limited operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** When medical emergency compels an abortion, the physician shall inform the woman, prior to abortion if possible, of the medical indications supporting the judgment that abortion is necessary to avert death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The statutory text appears in the appendix at 505 U.S. 903–904.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The explanation requirement survives with its express if-possible qualification; it creates no advance-permission or treatment-delay condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permission for appropriate informed-consent administration while keeping interference with the treatment decision distinct.

##### The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c)

**Holding:** A physician is protected from liability for failing to furnish §3205(a)'s information if the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. This statutory disclosure defense does not supply a general waiting-period waiver, authorize delay of necessary emergency treatment or replace §3203's emergency protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The paragraph identified by the District Court and Third Circuit as §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for failure to furnish subsection (a)'s information when the physician proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. The defense concerns the physician's reasonable belief, not proof that feared injury actually occurred. Its enacted preponderance burden does not displace the constitutional justification allocation. It does not excuse every unfavorable reaction or inconvenience.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enacted disclosure defense remains available without becoming a general waiting waiver or a substitute for prompt emergency treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: ordinary informed-consent administration remains permissible subject to the actual statutory and constitutional limits; no general proof rule is derived from it.

### Abortion informed consent

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Accurate material medical information

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(1)(i)–(iii)'s accurate, medically material information concerning the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks may be required for informed consent. Content is sustained independently of the separately invalidated exclusive routine-delivery and fixed waiting requirements, with the statutory disclosure-health defense preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Material application:** Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter additionally apply the complete framework: separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded medical information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation. The other four Justices join the bounded medical-content permission without adopting the complete framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. False or exaggerated risk descriptions and ideological assent are outside the sustained content. Concrete misleading implementation remains challengeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The content ruling is affirmed separately from invalid timing and routine-delivery exclusivity. Accurate, material content and appropriate clinical responsibility survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to ordinary voluntary and informed consent.
- Akron and Thornburgh: their protection against misleading or coercive communication survives; they do not forbid this isolated accurate medical content.

##### Notice of available free materials

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(2)(i) may require truthful notice that state materials describing fetal development and alternatives exist, that the patient has a right to review them, and that a copy will be supplied free if she chooses. The duty requires notice, not acceptance, reading or endorsement of the materials or listed agencies.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The judgment is 8–1: White, Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on their broader reasonable-regulation grounds; Blackmun dissents from the mandatory notice.

**Material application:** Separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The clinician may correct the directory, criticize a listed agency or recommend another. Findings of omissions and misinformation by some organizations do not establish that the availability notice itself is false. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The concise notice survives, with no compulsory acceptance, reading or endorsement; the fixed timing and exclusive routine-speaker conditions do not.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to relevant informed-choice information.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified as to categorical exclusion of this concise accurate notice; their protections against falsehood, coercion and actual burdens remain.

##### Notice of potentially available medical assistance

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(2)(ii) may require concise accurate notice that medical assistance benefits may be available for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care, with more information in state materials. It does not promise eligibility or payment, and remains subject to the disclosure-health defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The judgment is 8–1: White, Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on their broader reasonable-regulation grounds; Blackmun dissents from the mandatory notice.

**Material application:** Separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. The performing or referring physician may delegate the ancillary notices to a qualified physician assistant, health-care practitioner, technician or social worker. No entitlement to benefits or general permission to impose every truthful financial fact is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conditional notice survives without a payment guarantee or the invalid fixed interval.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to relevant information supporting voluntary choice.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified for this concise conditional assistance notice; no general permission for compulsory advocacy follows.

##### Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) may require accurate notice that the father is liable to assist in supporting the child even if he offered to pay for an abortion; the statute provides, “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No police report is required. Legal liability cannot be represented as guaranteed payment, and the omission permission changes no support obligation. This holding governs the disclosure alone; a married patient's separate question of notifying her spouse of the pregnancy is governed by the spousal-notice holding below, not by this provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The judgment is 8–1: White, Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on their broader reasonable-regulation grounds; Blackmun dissents from the mandatory notice.

**Material application:** Separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rape-related omission is permitted, not required, whether the assailant is the spouse or another person. Failure to report spousal assault within §3209's incorporated ninety-day period does not remove this separate omission permission. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The accurate notice survives with the omission permission; no support obligation is extinguished and collection is not guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to truthful consent-related information.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified for accurate concise paternal-liability information with the statutory permission; their anti-coercion and accuracy protections remain.

##### Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials

**Holding:** Section 3208's publication duties and section 3205(a)(3)'s provision of a copy when the patient chooses to review it are sustained at their objective, accurate and nonjudgmental statutory scope. The patient need not accept, read or endorse the materials, and the ruling does not establish that every publication or listed agency actually complies.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3208 requires easily comprehensible English, Spanish and Vietnamese materials within sixty days after the chapter becomes law, annual updates and legible type. A geographically indexed directory identifies public and private pregnancy, childbirth, dependent-child and adoption services and contacts, including phone numbers; the Department may instead provide a toll-free twenty-four-hour number supplying that list and local service information. Fetal information covers probable anatomy and physiology in two-week increments from fertilization to full term and relevant survival information; realistic pictures state dimensions and match the patient's stage. Information must be objective, nonjudgmental and scientifically accurate, including common abortion methods and risks, possible detrimental psychological effects and childbirth risks. Free copies in appropriate numbers are available to any person, facility or hospital requesting them. The materials state that coercion is unlawful, that abortion without informed consent or a private medical consultation may expose the physician to civil damages, that paternal support liability remains despite an offer to pay for abortion, and that adoptive parents may pay prenatal, childbirth and neonatal costs. Financial statements do not guarantee availability or collection. Findings of directory defects and misinformation by some organizations do not establish universal falsity or compliance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Publication and provision of a copy if chosen are sustained. Acceptance, reading and ideological assent are not required, and clinicians may supplement or criticize the material.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: supports information serving voluntary choice.
- Thornburgh: distinguished at voluntary publication/receipt scope; misleading or coercive implementation remains reviewable.

##### Certification of lawful receipt

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(4) may document actual receipt of lawful information without ideological assent or certification of unchosen reading. Its particular mandatory ancillary, timing and physician-delivery links stand or fall with the separately counted duties; a nine-Justice agreement on basic documentation is not a nine-Justice vote for every retained prerequisite.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by the other eight Justices: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. The basic receipt principle is unanimous. Ancillary dependencies survive 8–1, with Blackmun dissenting; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter join the five-Justice ancillary rationale, while White, Scalia and Thomas concur in those judgments. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter sever both timing and exclusive-delivery dependencies, each 5–4.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The patient certifies in writing before the procedure that required lawful information was supplied. Receipt is not agreement; no certificate may demand unchosen reading, compliance with invalid timing or an exclusive routine speaker. Particular duty coalitions remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Retain accurate documentation of surviving information duties. Remove timing-dependent and exclusive-speaker-dependent forms and sanctions, each on the five-Justice invalidation coalition. Ancillary dependencies survive on their separately stated five-Justice rationale and eight-vote judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to written consent documentation, with receipt distinguished from assent.

##### Physician delivery of medical information

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(1) is invalid insofar as it excludes qualified personnel from communicating routine consent information under appropriate physician responsibility. Accurate content remains required; individualized diagnosis, clinical assessment and professional responsibility are not eliminated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from invalidation. The judgment is 5–4.

**Material application:** The record establishes meaningful added demands from physician-only delivery of information trained personnel can competently communicate. Qualified communication with physician availability and responsibility is a concrete practicable alternative the State has not adequately answered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever exclusive routine physician delivery and its dependent certification and sanctions; preserve accurate content, individualized diagnosis, clinical assessment and applicable professional qualifications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Akron: its protection against unnecessary exclusive physician delivery is retained and applied.
- Ohio v. Akron: parental notification does not independently resolve adult clinical counseling.
- Danforth: permits informed consent without validating every staffing restriction.

##### The mandatory 24-hour interval

**Holding:** The compulsory twenty-four-hour interval in §3205(a)(1)–(2) is invalid under the meaningful-interference branch. The State has not substantiated the justification and fit of a uniform interval or adequately answered practical, substantially less burdensome means of securing informed and voluntary consent. Timing-dependent certification and enforcement are severed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4; no finding that every application is a substantial obstacle is required for this distinct ground.

**Material application:** The demonstrated additional delay, travel, expense and scheduling burdens are meaningful interference. Individual counseling, questions and additional time when needed or requested supply practical alternatives the State has not adequately answered. An emergency exception does not justify burdens on patients outside its terms, and voluntary waiting does not justify universal compulsion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the fixed interval and timing-dependent certifications and enforcement; preserve lawful consent, voluntary reflection and time required by genuine clinical need or unresolved consent. No finding that every application is a substantial obstacle is necessary to this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Akron: its fixed-waiting protection is retained and applied.
- Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron: parental-involvement decisions do not supply a justification for universal adult reconsideration.
- Roe and Doe: the protected decision and necessary timely care remain independently binding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the compulsory twenty-four-hour interval creates a substantial obstacle to the practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population, it is independently invalid. No compelling-interest assertion can rescue an established obstacle.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same five directly adopt this obstacle ground and the complete framework. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from invalidating the interval.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling at the stated substantial-obstacle scope.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The Court does not find that every application of the interval creates a substantial obstacle. Invalidation of the compulsory interval as a whole rests independently on the meaningful-interference ground above; this ground applies where the additional obstacle condition is established. It requires no further or different relief beyond removal of the interval and dependent certification and enforcement. Roe and Doe preserve the protected decision and timely necessary care; Akron's fixed-waiting protection remains applied.

### Abortion reporting and public disclosure

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Confidential referring-source identification

**Holding:** The referring-source component of §3214(a)(1) is invalid insofar as it demands identification solely because a physician, agency or service made a referral. The remaining identification duties and otherwise lawful inquiries based on actual clinical responsibility are preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4.

**Material application:** Evidence shows referrers would stop providing referral information if identified even under accepted confidentiality. The State has not justified blanket identification over performing-provider records and targeted inquiry tied to actual clinical responsibility or suspected misconduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever identification solely because of referral. Retain performing-physician, required concurring or second-physician and facility identification and otherwise lawful targeted investigation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: useful confidential reporting remains permissible; confidentiality does not answer every demonstrated access burden.
- Thornburgh: actual deterrence and information demanded must be examined separately.
- Brockett and Pennsylvania severability law: remove the referring-source component without reopening independently lawful identification duties.

##### Confidential reporting of medical necessity under §3214(a)(8)

**Holding:** Section 3214(a)(8) may require a confidential account of the physician's basis for invoking §3211(b)(1)'s medical-necessity predicate. Recording that judgment does not establish a new substantive necessity test, require advance approval, adjudicate every other postviability restriction or create an offense merely because another physician later disagrees.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3214(a)(8) requests the basis for the judgment that an abortion under §3211(b)(1) was necessary to prevent death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. That predicate concerns the referenced rule for abortion at twenty-four or more weeks, not merely a viability/gestational-age result. The individual report excludes patient names; actual confidentiality safeguards are accepted in this litigation. These terms appear in §§3214(a) and (a)(8); the confidentiality findings are stated in the lower-court reporting discussions. The accepted §3214(e)(1)–(2) safeguards make them nonpublic, require a common pleas court good-cause order for law-enforcement access, and bar statistical publication identifying any physician, facility or patient. Concrete materially different administration remains challengeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The bounded confidential report remains lawful. It creates no advance permission, unnecessary test, new culpability rule or professional immunity, and does not adjudicate unappealed age-result or underlying ascertainment duties.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permissibility of appropriately directed confidential reporting rather than inferring invalidity from the existence of documentation.
- Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists: its attention to actual burdens and disclosure consequences is preserved; it does not automatically invalidate this distinct confidential medical-basis field.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming meaningful interference from this documentation, the State has substantiated the bounded report's fit to review of the particular medical-necessity judgment invoked under §3211(b)(1); no identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately supplies that particular information. The field concerns clinical work already performed, preserves confidentiality and requires no additional testing or advance permission.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; these five directly adopt the complete-framework application. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the bounded reporting permission without joining this additional ground.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling as stated for this particular field and record.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Bare totals or an unexplained checkbox do not adequately perform this function. The inference that written reasons necessarily chill lawful clinical judgment does not establish invalidity here; confidentiality alone is not decisive. This ground sustains the same bounded reporting duty and changes neither the underlying treatment rule nor its culpability requirements.

##### Notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason reports

**Holding:** The notice-given and reasons-for-nonnotification components of section 3214(a)(12) are invalid and severed because they enforce or reproduce section 3209's unconstitutional disclosure condition. The independent confidential marital-status question remains, and no report or certification may restore the invalid notice obligation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse approval of both notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason fields in No. 91-744; sever them and dependent enforcement under Act 64 and Pennsylvania law, preserving independent confidential marital status.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: confidential-reporting permission does not validate enforcement of an invalid spousal condition.
- Brockett: supports relief confined to the unconstitutional component; state severability law governs the surviving text.

##### The independently severable marital-status field

**Holding:** Section 3214(a)(12)'s confidential inquiry whether the patient was married is sustained and severed from its invalid notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason questions. The field supplies demographic information without requiring spousal disclosure or an explanation for withholding it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The accepted §3214(e)(1)–(2) safeguards make them nonpublic, require a common pleas court good-cause order for law-enforcement access, and bar statistical publication identifying any physician, facility or patient. Concrete materially different administration remains challengeable. No general governmental entitlement to intimate information is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Retain only the independently operative marital-status field. Forms and enforcement may not reconstruct spousal notice or require a reason for withholding it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to confidential demographic reporting.
- Brockett: supports component-limited relief, implemented through Act 64 and Pennsylvania severability law.

##### Public facility and affiliation reports

**Holding:** Section 3207(b)'s compulsory public-release condition is unconstitutional as applied to the plaintiff facilities and their covered reports. Confidential collection and otherwise lawful official access and public-expenditure disclosure remain. The cause is remanded to identify the protected reports and conform the injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would sustain public release. The 5–4 holding supplies plaintiff-specific relief, not automatic statewide facial invalidation.

**Material application:** Supported risks of harassment and withdrawal from medical-assistance participation establish meaningful interference for these plaintiffs. Receipt of funds, including for other services, does not adequately justify exposing the broader affiliation reports. Public expenditure and funded-service disclosure together with confidential examination provide concrete alternatives the State has not adequately answered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3207(b)'s release trigger uses State-appropriated funds received in the twelve calendar months before a request to inspect or copy. Reports of nonfunded facilities are available only to law-enforcement officials and the State Boards of Medicine and Osteopathic Medicine for official duties. The collected information includes facility name/address, parent, subsidiary and affiliated entities and commonly owned or controlled entities; collection and immediate updating remain distinct from public access. This holding does not establish public disclosure of patient identities, automatic statewide invalidity or immunity from financial accountability.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse public-release approval as applied to plaintiff facilities and remand to identify and protect their covered reports and conform the injunction, including dependent enforcement. Preserve confidential collection, lawful official access and independently lawful public-expenditure disclosure. Extending relief to other providers requires an adequate evidentiary and procedural basis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: confidential health administration remains distinct from public release.
- Thornburgh: actual disclosure burdens matter; institutional affiliation information is not equated with patient identities.
- Brockett: relief is confined to the proven applications, preserving severable collection and lawful accountability.

##### Public quarterly totals and trimester reports

**Holding:** Section 3214(f)'s compulsory public-release condition is unconstitutional as applied to the plaintiff facilities' covered quarterly reports on this record. Confidential submission of quarterly and trimester totals remains. Public reporting of actual State expenditures is not invalidated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4.

**Material application:** Harassment and medical-assistance withdrawal risks establish meaningful interference for the plaintiffs. The State has not adequately justified disclosure of complete abortion totals over public expenditure/funded-service information combined with confidential examination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3214(f) requires quarterly totals and totals by trimester when the facility performed an abortion during the quarter. Its public-access lookback covers State-appropriated funds received in the twelve calendar months before filing that report. A declaration of no qualifying funds requires confidentiality unless other evidence causes the Department to conclude funds were received. This differs from §3207(b)'s inspection-request clock. Institutional totals are not equated with patient identities, and statistical trimester categories do not revive the constitutional timetable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse as to plaintiff facilities' covered public-release reports and remand for a conforming injunction. Preserve confidential collection, official oversight and lawful spending disclosure; broader relief requires an adequate evidentiary and procedural basis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: permits bounded health reporting, with public release analyzed separately.

### Child-welfare statutory enforcement

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort v. Ash, 422 U.S. 66 (1975) — applies its inquiry into an implied private remedy, with congressional intent decisive.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11 (1979) — applies its requirement of congressional intent to create the particular remedy asserted; statutory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992, decision — preserves the distinction between the existence of a cognizable action and appropriate relief within it; no Title IX holding is narrowed.

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. The reporting ground therefore cannot independently sustain the challenged caseworker injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-A, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this separate statutory holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wright, 479 U.S. 418 — applies the need to identify a privately enforceable statutory right; its distinct rent holding remains in force.
- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. Rejection of this distinct statutory vehicle does not decide private enforcement of §671(a)(16).

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the ground rejecting direct implied enforcement of the reporting claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains open as to enforceable right, implied action, violation and additional lawful relief. No constitutional claim is revived or nationwide three-day deadline imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort, 422 U.S. 66, and Transamerica, 444 U.S. 11 — apply the congressional-intent requirement to the distinct implied reporting action.
- Franklin, February 26, 1992, decision — applies its action/remedy distinction without changing the established Title IX action or relief available within it.

### Equal protection and corporate venue

#### Burlington Northern Railroad Co. v. Ford, 504 U.S. 648 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-779.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The Montana Supreme Court consolidated the Ford and Thomas L. Johnson venue appeals; both transfer denials are reviewed in this docket.

##### Whether Montana's resident-corporation home-county preference denies equal protection to this nonresident corporation

**Holding:** Montana's venue distinction satisfies equal protection as applied to Burlington, which is incorporated outside Montana and has its actual headquarters in Texas: the State may give greater weight to a resident corporation's home-county convenience while allowing plaintiffs to choose a county against this nonresident corporation. Ordinary rational-basis review permits incorporation to approximate headquarters location when the challenger has not shown that the resulting overinclusion or underinclusion makes the venue rule irrational.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this ground. Stone concurs separately in the unanimous judgment without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** Burlington is incorporated in Delaware and actually headquartered in Texas. Its principal Montana office does not have the same relationship to corporate litigation convenience as an actual home county. Incorporation provides an administrable, imperfect headquarters approximation whose mismatch Burlington has not shown irrational.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No suspect classification or fundamental right to a preferred county is involved. A foreign corporation actually headquartered in Montana, separately presented commerce objections, personal jurisdiction and the FELA merits remain undecided. The Court adopts neither FELA recovery policy nor hypothetical federal venue as its ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both transfer denials are affirmed. Ford's and Johnson's suits may proceed in Yellowstone County without transfer to Hill County; no new venue factfinding or liability determination is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Railroad Retirement Board v. Fritz, 449 U.S. 166 (1980): applied; reasonable legislative accommodation of competing interests need not be uniquely correct or perfectly precise.
- Bain Peanut Co. of Texas v. Pinson, 282 U.S. 499 (1931): applied for latitude in ordinary venue arrangements, subject to equal protection.
- New Orleans v. Dukes, 427 U.S. 297 (1976), Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp., 426 U.S. 794 (1976), and Lindsley v. Natural Carbonic Gas Co., 220 U.S. 61 (1911): applied for permissible imprecision in rational classifications.
- United States v. Sperry Corp., 493 U.S. 52 (1989), and Dallas v. Stanglin, 490 U.S. 19 (1989): applied to ordinary rational-basis review where neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right is involved.
- Metropolitan Casualty Ins. Co. v. Brownell, 294 U.S. 580 (1935), and G. D. Searle & Co. v. Cohn, 455 U.S. 404 (1982): applied for reasonable corporate-location assumptions and the challenger's failure to show an irrational classification on this record.
- Power Manufacturing Co. v. Saunders, 274 U.S. 490 (1927): distinguished; Arkansas's materially different place-of-business classification does not invalidate Montana's more closely fitted headquarters approximation. Its equal-protection holding is not overruled.
- American Motorists Ins. Co. v. Starnes, 425 U.S. 637 (1976): its doubts about Power's continuing force are acknowledged without treating them as an overruling; it also supports reviewability of this final venue determination under §1257(a).
- Citizens & Southern National Bank v. Bougas, 434 U.S. 35 (1977), Denver & Rio Grande Western Railroad Co. v. Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen, 387 U.S. 556 (1967), and Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612 (1964): applied for venue's function of accommodating the parties' competing interests in the place of trial.

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently, Burlington cannot obtain relief on the objection that incorporation imperfectly substitutes for actual corporate headquarters, because a rule using actual headquarters would still treat this Texas-headquartered defendant as a nonresident. The Court does not decide the equal-protection claim of a foreign corporation whose actual headquarters is in Montana.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this ground. Stone concurs separately in the unanimous judgment without a formal opinion join.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The alternative addresses this asserted defect and this Texas-headquartered challenger; it does not create a categorical rule insulating incorporation classifications or govern every facial challenge. The Montana-headquartered foreign-corporation application remains open. Correcting the alleged incorporation/headquarters mismatch would still leave Burlington a nonresident and supplies no right to transfer either action.

- Roberts & Schaefer Co. v. Emmerson, 271 U.S. 50 (1926): applied to bar relief based on a classification defect whose correction would not benefit this challenger.
- United States v. Raines, 362 U.S. 17 (1960): applied by analogy for the usual limit on invoking unconstitutional applications to others; no categorical exception or new facial-challenge rule is announced.

### Individual-capacity liability

#### Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-681.  
**Decided:** November 5, 1991.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. **Material application:** The Court of Appeals construed the damages claims as personal claims against Hafer. Her authority to discharge employees supplies the state-law connection without converting the claims into claims against Pennsylvania.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and entitlement to reinstatement remain unresolved; no immunity standard changes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kentucky v. Graham supplies the distinction between litigation capacities. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. Rhodes confirms possible personal liability of executive officers; Monroe v. Pape supplies the under-color-of-law rule for misuse of official power. Ex parte Young's prospective-relief channel is unchanged.

### Minor consent and independent authorization

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### One-parent informed consent and independent judicial bypass

**Holding:** Section 3206's one-parent informed-consent structure survives this facial challenge with its emergency and family-substitution provisions and an effective independent judicial route. A minor may seek court authorization without first approaching a parent; maturity, capacity and actual informed consent, or alternatively her best interests, support authorization through confidential, expedited proceedings with counsel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unemancipated minors under eighteen ordinarily need their own informed consent plus one parent's. Only the mother is required where the father participated in incest. If both parents are deceased or unavailable within reasonable time and manner, a guardian may consent; after divorce the custodial parent's consent suffices; if neither parent nor guardian is available within reasonable time and manner, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. Parent/guardian considers only the child's or ward's best interests. Medical emergency excuses compliance. A minor may elect court without first requesting parental consent. Maturity/capacity plus actual consent and best interests are alternative judicial routes. Counsel is provided absent private counsel or a knowing intelligent waiver. Trial ruling is due within three business days of application; appeal ruling within five business days of filing. Proceedings and records are confidential. The separate adult-incompetence text presents no developed independent challenge here. These are the terms of §3206(a)–(f) and (h), including the provisions quoted by the Third Circuit. The statutory safeguards sustained here are not all declared constitutionally indispensable. No developed adult-incapacity claim or present failure to supply the bypass's required operation is decided. Emergency care cannot await parental or judicial proceedings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The one-parent structure and independent bypass survive the facial challenge; no special parental-attendance proceedings are ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bellotti v. Baird: applies the distinct maturity/informed-consent and best-interests routes rather than merging them into cumulative conditions.
- Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft: follows its sustention of parental consent supported by effective expedited judicial safeguards.
- Hodgson v. Minnesota and Ohio v. Akron Center: apply their treatment of usable independent bypass without converting parental responsibility into an adult spouse's authority.

##### Accurate information used for informed parental consent

**Holding:** Section 3206 may use accurate material medical information and concise, nonmisleading notices concerning available materials, possible assistance and paternal support to inform a parent's consent. This sustention creates no independent parent-specific waiting period, physician-only parental-delivery command or compulsory clinic visit; optional materials need not be read or endorsed, and the paternal-support provision states: “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No police report is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 7 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The district court used §3205 to interpret informed parental consent at 744 F. Supp. 1382–1384. That is a lower-court construction, not an express §3206 incorporation of every §3205 timing/delivery mechanism. Assistance may be available, not guaranteed. Paternal liability includes the father who offered to pay for abortion; “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” This permission has no law-enforcement reporting condition and does not guarantee collection. Materials are furnished if chosen, without required acceptance, reading or ideological assent. The §3206 emergency, incest, unavailability, custody and bypass terms remain as stated in the preceding holding.

Under the Court's separately counted five-Justice waiting and exclusive-delivery holdings, those invalid requirements cannot survive through incorporation into parental consent. This conforming consequence does not add the dissenters from those judgments to their rationale coalitions. The parental-content holding creates no additional parental timing, speaker or attendance obligation. Stone concurs in the judgment only; Blackmun opposes mandatory ancillary content. The seven-Justice rationale does not import the separate waiting or exclusive-delivery conditions into §3206. Materials remain optional; assistance and collection are not guaranteed. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The incorporated accurate ancillary content is sustained. Invalid waiting and exclusive-speaker conditions cannot survive through incorporation, on their separately counted five-Justice judgments; no additional parent-specific visit is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota and Ohio v. Akron Center: apply the distinct permissible parental-guidance setting and actual bypass safeguards.
- Akron and Thornburgh: categorical opposition to accurate concise assistance or alternative notices merely because they favor childbirth does not control this narrow factual-content permission; other delay and medical-delivery questions remain separately counted.

### Municipal liability and public employment

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

### Prisoners’ rights

#### Hudson v. McMillian, 503 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6531.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.

##### Whether limited injury defeats the established nontrivial malicious beating of a restrained prisoner

**Holding:** A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner violates the Eighth Amendment even when the resulting physical injuries are limited, where the established conduct satisfies even *Whitley*’s demanding malicious-and-sadistic inquiry. Injury remains relevant evidence and bears on compensation; accidental harm or a merely technical battery is not enough.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this rule and its application. Blackmun’s concurrence in the judgment is not counted as a join. O’Connor’s Part B, joined by White, Kennedy and Souter, has only four votes for extending *Whitley* to every prison excessive-force claim. No *Marks* aggregation supplies that universal rule.

**Material application:** Hudson was handcuffed and shackled; the undisturbed findings established unnecessary repeated blows and supervisory condonation. Dental and facial injuries confirmed that the force was not trivial. The findings satisfied even *Whitley*’s heightened culpability inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No comprehensive mental-state standard for every force setting, psychological-injury-only rule, immunity decision, or exhaustive rule for unusual forms of cruelty is adopted. Medical-care, confinement-conditions, legitimate-discipline and compensation rules remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand to the Fifth Circuit for further disposition consistent with the undisturbed findings and without its significant-injury bar. No increase above the existing $800 award, immunity ruling or prison-management injunction is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Reversal and remand, 7–2: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Whitley v. Albers*, 475 U.S. 312, is applied as a sufficient ground on these findings; its disturbance rule is not extended universally. *Wilson v. Seiter*, 501 U.S. 294, is distinguished: its objective-deprivation requirement does not become a significant-injury floor for this beating. *Estelle v. Gamble*, 429 U.S. 97, supplies the prohibition of unnecessary and wanton pain while retaining the distinct serious-medical-need and deliberate-indifference requirements.

##### Whether an assumed isolated, unauthorized assault sustains affirmance on this record

**Holding:** The undisturbed finding of supervisory condonation and the limits of the granted question prevent affirmance on an assumed characterization of this beating as an isolated, unauthorized assault.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part C of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six-Justice case-specific rejection of the alternative ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court announces no general definition of punishment or general rule governing isolated, unauthorized conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative characterization supplies no ground for preserving the Fifth Circuit’s judgment; the remand must respect the intact supervisory-condonation finding.

#### [Sandin v. Conner](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Sandin_v_Conner_merits_1995-06-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1911.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty

**Holding:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient: courts must examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and the meaningful restraint, without making state codification necessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is sufficient and nonexclusive. Mandatory procedures, routine transfers and brief inconveniences do not automatically create entitlements; direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration interests remain. No atypical-and-significant-hardship test controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hewitt v. Helms: narrows mechanical reliance on mandatory wording while retaining genuine substantive-predicate liberty protection; no wholesale methodology replacement controls.
- Kentucky Department of Corrections v. Thompson: applies distinction between substantive constraints and procedural directions alone.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves significant prison liberty and context-sensitive procedural protection.
- Meachum v. Fano: distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized management from a genuine protected constraint.
- Vitek v. Jones; Washington v. Harper: preserve distinct directly protected, qualitatively different restraints; this state-entitlement rule is nonexclusive.
- Farmer v. Brennan; Helling v. McKinney: distinguish Eighth Amendment conditions liability from this procedural-liberty inquiry.

##### The reviewed summary judgment and remand

**Holding:** The summary judgment rejecting the reviewed liberty-and-process claim cannot stand on a mechanical reading of Hawaii’s regulatory words or dismissal of genuine punitive constraints. The district court must determine whether the rules genuinely constrain authority, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due; the Court makes no liability or ultimate protected-interest finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court and remedial disposition; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg join the bounded rule-and-remand ground. Four concurring Justices separately would find the interest already established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-evidence language is not rewritten as an express acquittal command; the whole misconduct, impartial-adjudication, evidence and sanction scheme matters. Punitive labels alone do not establish liberty; similarity to separately authorized administrative or protective custody alone does not defeat a punitive constraint. Later expungement supplies no retroactive process and discretionary parole consequences are not inevitable sentence extension. Wolff permits refusal of witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or safety or correctional hazards. Neither the four concurring votes finding an established interest nor the dissent's four-vote replacement framework controls under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff: applies witness qualifications and the separate process inquiry once protected liberty is implicated.
- Hewitt; Thompson: require whole-scheme substantive analysis rather than a one-way mandatory-word inference.
- Meachum: preserves ordinary authorized custody without making every punitive restraint legally unconstrained.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit in the reviewed respect, leaving reversal of officials' summary judgment effective, and remand for genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and due process determinations. The Court finds neither ultimate protected liberty nor liability; unrelated amended-complaint claims and defenses are not resolved.

### Psychiatric confinement

#### Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the continuing illness-and-danger basis of insanity commitment; initial verdict-based commitment and rejection of an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling remain intact.
- O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975): applied to the requirement that confinement end when its authorizing basis disappears; the Court does not equate a conditional-release recommendation with a finding of harmlessness.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the required relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose.

##### Whether Louisiana may preserve psychiatric custody without new adequate grounds and proceedings

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Material application:** A counseled hearing about danger alone cannot supply the absent illness predicate. Ordinary civil commitment requires clear and convincing proof of present illness and dangerousness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Vitek v. Jones, 445 U.S. 480 (1980): applied to psychiatric institutionalization's distinct liberty deprivation and need for appropriate grounds and procedures.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the need for an independently authorized commitment proceeding after the initial basis expires.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418 (1979): applied unchanged to the clear-and-convincing burden for ordinary civil commitment; that burden is not made universal for every preventive custody channel.
- Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107 (1966): relied on for the ordinary protections attending a new civil commitment after the prior detention basis ends; no separate majority equal-protection holding is created here.

##### Whether indefinite psychiatric confinement may rest on failure to prove nondangerousness after mental illness ends

**Holding:** Louisiana's scheme permitting indefinite psychiatric confinement of a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee because he has not proved nondangerousness violates due process. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure the absence of a carefully limited substantive authority; the Court leaves materially narrower preventive regimes undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-C, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient rejection of Louisiana's asserted alternative preventive justification. The holding adopts no new general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness proof standard.

**Material application:** Periodic review does not cure an indefinite scheme without carefully limited substantive authority. Salerno’s focused pretrial regime does not justify this post-acquittal confinement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): distinguished because its confined pretrial detention authority and safeguards do not authorize Louisiana's indefinite post-acquittal psychiatric restraint.
- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the absence of authority to punish an insanity acquittee as a convicted offender.

### Qualified immunity

#### Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1440.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Who decides qualified immunity from an arrest-without-probable-cause claim and what record governs at summary judgment

**Holding:** Qualified immunity from a claim of arrest without probable cause is a legal question for the judge under ordinary summary-judgment constraints. The court must determine whether a reasonable officer could believe the arrest lawful on the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; choosing the “more reasonable” interpretation is not the test, and a generalized security concern alone does not establish a reasonable belief that the offense's elements were satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; these five Justices supply the controlling majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** The agents could consider assassination references, a reported gesture, oral statements, the confusing interview, and refusal to elaborate. They also had the letter's repeated attribution of the supposed plot to an outside group, bearing on whether Bryant communicated a serious threat under §871 or reported an imagined danger. The Ninth Circuit's comparison of the relative reasonableness of interpretations and its general assignment of legal immunity to a jury were erroneous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither guilt, innocence, bad faith, nor a constitutional violation. It neither directs immunity nor orders an automatic trial. The separate warrant ruling remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is vacated and the case remanded. The remand court must specify any supported dispute over material historical facts and decide immunity promptly under the correct standard.

**Material judgment:** Vacatur and remand are supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter, 6–2. White would direct immunity; Stevens would affirm, and Scalia would reverse and direct immunity. These differing remedial positions do not enlarge the five-Justice controlling rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow, Anderson v. Creighton, and Malley supply protection for reasonable mistakes and early judicial resolution when supported facts permit it. Watts supplies the contextual distinction between a true threat and protected expression. Mitchell, Beck, and Liberty Lobby are applied and preserved. No qualified-immunity rule is revised.

### Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Viability is individualized and medically supported; no fixed week or conclusive calendar presumption is adopted. Necessary care includes relevant physical and mental health and cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable treatment, not official approval of the patient's reasons. The holding does not decide constitutional fetal personhood, general public funding, conscience claims, every postviability restriction or admission of a particular drug.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The retained liberty governs the separate provision-specific dispositions. Its six supporters do not all join the complete framework, which has five supporters.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

The adopted inquiry has the following complete operative scope:

1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violates the protected baseline and cannot be justified later in the inquiry. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe, suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons or a legislative definition declaring all noncompliant care unsafe.
2. Identify actual function and purpose. Safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration and financial accountability may supply permissible functions. Truthful persuasion and a preference for childbirth are permissible; moral disapproval, punishment or deterrence through blocked access are not independent justifications. Obstructive purpose established objectively from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish it. Medical and deliberative justifications require support addressing their actual functions.
3. Challengers must establish a concrete, nontrivial additional interference attributable to the duty, including demonstrated expense, appointments, delay, provider-capacity loss, clinical risk, coercion or disclosure consequences. Compare the system with and without that obligation, or the identified challenged package, holding other law and circumstances constant. Consider cumulative operation without double counting. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding injury, retain that group throughout, and use evidence-supported subgroups where appropriate. Do not average burdens over all pregnancies or define the group only as successful claimants. Poverty, distance, work, transportation and abuse matter through their causal connection to the State's added condition; exceptions, bypasses and providers count only if usable. No universal monetary, distance or time cutoff applies. Reliable evidence and reasonable predictions may establish prospective interference without waiting for lost access. Meaningful interference triggers review but does not itself establish invalidity.
4. Apply the protections in sequence. A violation of the baseline, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle ends the inquiry in invalidity. An obstacle seriously obstructs practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and successful compliance by some does not establish access for others. No compelling interest rescues an established obstacle. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; identify the severe intrusion and why the first inquiry did not resolve it. The protections may overlap, but an obstacle cannot be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable. For remaining meaningful lesser interference, the State must substantiate its permissible justification and the fit of the additional duty. If challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it, including any inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequence. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice: no particular legislative finding, new study in every case, scientific certainty, numerical benefit calculation or disproof of every hypothetical alternative is required. A court may not substitute its preferred policy once justification and fit are established. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary legal requirements remain. Challengers bear duty, population, causation and trigger; the State bears the applicable justification, tailoring or fit burden. Ordinary proof rules for historical facts and separate statutory defenses do not change.
5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication. Assess medical reasons against competent evidence, accepted standards, individual circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement has serious evidentiary weight but no association holds a veto; neither a bare clinical assertion nor unsupported official preference is conclusive. Necessary care must remain timely, without an unnecessary committee veto or documentation used as prior permission. Duties must be definite and applicable culpability requirements respected; reasonable later professional disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense. Fraud, malpractice and misconduct remain enforceable. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material. Concise factual assistance notices need not entail ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not itself justify burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Analyze useful confidential records separately from public disclosure and enforcement of invalid conditions.
6. Identify the particular violation and tailor relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under severability law and address dependent certifications, reports and sanctions expressly. Another patient's ability to comply does not defeat an individual claim. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it; voluntary compliance in some cases alone does not justify compulsion. Otherwise limit relief to proved applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not dispense with causation, scope or justification analysis. Cumulative-burden relief addresses the challenged contribution, not all existing regulation. Replacement of the timetable neither dissolves injunctions nor authorizes every challenged duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a direct five-Justice rule; no Marks synthesis or addition of Kennedy's separate approach is required. An independent numerical facial-review threshold is not adopted. The spousal-notice holding supplies its distinct provision-specific affected-group analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The complete framework replaces the independent trimester timetable effective June 29, 1992. Existing injunctions must be conformed to the actual component judgments, not automatically dissolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe and Doe: preserve the protected decision and clinical safeguards while replacing the independent timetable.
- Carey and Akron: support protection against serious and unjustified interference; the general lesser-burden substantiation rule is expressly adopted here.
- Marks: no aggregation of dissenting rationales is necessary because five Justices directly join the complete rule.
- Ohio v. Akron: its facial-review formulation is qualified at the scope expressly stated in Step 6 and the spousal-notice holding.

##### Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation

**Holding:** Roe's protected reproductive liberty, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection are retained because renewed disagreement with the constitutional judgment supplies no sufficient justification for withdrawing that settled protection. The Court may distinguish or alter a separable regulatory rule for a demonstrated legal reason, but such treatment does not silently overrule the liberty that the rule implements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional foundation, each regulatory proposition and each coalition remain distinct. Changed membership, continuing disagreement or criticism of one regulatory implementation does not silently withdraw the protected liberty. No wholesale overruling of Akron or Thornburgh follows; their actual treatment appears with the affected duties.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arizona v. Rumsey — Applies the requirement of a special justification beyond a belief that a constitutional precedent was wrongly decided.
- Roe v. Wade — Reaffirms its protected-choice foundation, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health safeguard; regulatory implementation receives separate treatment.
- Griswold v. Connecticut and Eisenstadt v. Baird — Their protection of individual reproductive choice remains a doctrinal foundation for retaining Roe.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Retains the connection between protected reproductive choice and meaningful access to its exercise; its existence undercuts a claim that Roe's foundation has been doctrinally displaced.
- Doe v. Bolton and Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Their clinical-judgment, consent and third-party-control rules demonstrate separable applications of the protected liberty and remain subject to the specific holdings stated here.
- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health and Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists — No wholesale overruling follows from this stare-decisis holding; each actual qualification, retained protection or changed rule is identified in the provision-specific holdings.

##### The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation

**Holding:** The independent trimester timetable is displaced together with adoption of the complete six-step replacement. Five Justices adopt that integrated holding. All nine reject continuation of the former timetable in result, but the other four do not join the replacement standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The separate result of rejecting the former timetable has nine supporters; only these five adopt its integrated replacement.

**Material application:** The former first-trimester reservation of decision and effectuation to patient and physician and the later health-regulation timing rule are displaced. The viability boundary and necessary-care protection remain. Gestational age may substantiate a particular safety duty when evidence links it to medical risk, but crossing a trimester boundary does not independently change the previability standard.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The integrated replacement takes effect with the complete framework; existing injunctions remain subject to ordinary conforming relief. Statistical use of trimester categories remains distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe: independent trimester timing restrictions displaced; protected choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health care retained.
- Doe: individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and psychological health remain protected.
- Webster: its criticism of rigid timing supports reconsideration of implementation, without treating its fractured opinions as an earlier complete overruling.

### School desegregation decrees

#### Freeman v. Pitts, 503 U.S. 467 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 89-1290.
**Decided:** March 31, 1992.

##### When supervision may be withdrawn from completed parts of an unfinished school-desegregation decree

**Holding:** A court may withdraw supervision from a completed part of a school-desegregation decree while retaining jurisdiction and supervision elsewhere only after the district shows full and satisfactory compliance and elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable. The court must assess demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree and whether retaining control is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: all eight participating Justices support this limited rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The district bears the release showing; good faith must be demonstrated through conduct toward the whole decree. Whether retained control is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere is part of the inquiry, beyond a bare no-obstruction test. Jurisdiction and active authority over remaining violations continue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eleventh Circuit’s categorical simultaneous-compliance restriction and remand, 8–0; Thomas did not participate. The court must identify issues properly open, secure findings on whole-decree good faith and remedial connections, and define any released obligations and retained supervision. This Court releases no component, orders no general retrial of settled facts, and finds no final systemwide compliance. Each party bears its own costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430 — applied: the affirmative duty and operational inquiry continue across the unfinished decree.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 — applied: equitable flexibility remains tied to effective repair of the violation.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 — clarified for partial withdrawal: good faith and practicable elimination must be assessed in that setting.
- Milliken v. Bradley, 433 U.S. 267 — applied: restoration of local control remains consistent with completion of the constitutional remedy.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992 — preserved within its consent-modification scope; it does not replace the Green/Dowell release inquiry.

##### What causal showing and factual review govern partial withdrawal

**Holding:** The district bears the burden of showing that imbalance for which it disclaims remedial responsibility is not traceable to the constitutional violation; genuinely independent demographic change does not require perpetual racial balancing. Existing factual findings receive clear-error review, but findings on one question do not decide unexamined causal or remedial connections; the remand must resolve properly open issues and define the obligations released and retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: eight votes; the separate opinions’ disputed factual applications are outside this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither racial imbalance alone nor demographic movement automatically establishes a violation or completed remedy. Properly settled factual findings receive clear-error review; unexamined causal or remedial links remain open. No majority adopts the separate writings’ disputed factual applications or a revised causation presumption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eleventh Circuit’s categorical simultaneous-compliance restriction and remand, 8–0; Thomas did not participate. The court must identify issues properly open, secure findings on whole-decree good faith and remedial connections, and define any released obligations and retained supervision. This Court releases no component, orders no general retrial of settled facts, and finds no final systemwide compliance. Each party bears its own costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pasadena Board of Education v. Spangler, 427 U.S. 424 — applied with its independent-cause and completed-remedy limits.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 — preserved: the district’s causal showing and violation-linked remedy remain required.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 — applied: good faith requires demonstrated compliance, not an assurance alone.

#### [Missouri v. Jenkins](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Missouri_v_Jenkins_merits_1995-06-12.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1823.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

##### Violation-linked salary support is permissible on the preserved findings

**Holding:** A desegregation court may fund instructional and noninstructional salary support reasonably necessary to deliver educational measures repairing established segregation-caused injury, without treating low salaries as a separate constitutional wrong. These challenged orders are sustained on their independently sufficient intradistrict educational-repair and personnel-necessity findings; incidental voluntary attraction does not negate that ground, and the broader magnet foundation is not reopened.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this holding and affirm both reviewed salary judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The independently sufficient educational-repair findings sustain instructional and critical support-staff costs. November salary findings were not challenged as clearly erroneous; distinct December labor-market and support-staff challenges received that review. No automatic future increase, general educational-improvement guarantee, unproved interdistrict remedy or reopening of the broader magnet foundation follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Milliken v. Bradley (Milliken II), 433 U.S. 267, is applied to violation-linked educational repair and necessary implementation. Swann is applied to equitable means bounded by the adjudicated injury. Milliken I, 418 U.S. 717, retains the interdistrict-violation limit; it is not extended into a categorical support-staff exclusion.

##### Partial release requires the complete Freeman showing

**Holding:** Partial withdrawal may occur only after the defendant seeking release proves full and satisfactory compliance and practicable elimination of the relevant vestiges, whole-decree good faith shown by conduct, and the necessary causal and remedial connections; retained control necessary or practicable for compliance elsewhere and nonobstruction of remaining relief must be assessed. Missouri’s proof of installing programs does not make that complete showing, so continued funding and denial of partial release are affirmed; low scores alone establish neither continuing constitutional injury nor completed compliance, and national parity is not an indispensable release condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the complete existing Freeman rule and its present-record application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Program installation and spending alone do not establish practicable elimination or whole-decree good faith. Maximum educational potential and every program goal are not release requirements. No consensus, reviewer endorsement or Rufo changed-circumstances condition is added; properly supported later release remains possible.

The State and district’s respective responsibilities must be assessed separately; one defendant’s conduct does not establish the other’s compliance. The defendant bears the release showing and the showing that imbalance for which it disclaims responsibility is not traceable to the violation. Genuinely independent demographic change imposes no perpetual balancing duty once the required work is completed. Racial imbalance alone proves neither liability nor completed remedy. Partial release may, rather than must, follow a satisfactory showing. The court must examine whether retaining control is necessary or practicable for compliance elsewhere, avoid obstructing remaining relief, and define released duties, remaining obligations and retained supervision. Existing factual findings receive clear-error review; findings on one issue do not decide unexamined connections. There is no all-components-at-once restriction or good-intentions substitute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Freeman v. Pitts, 503 U.S. 467, is applied at the completed Court decision’s operative scope: permission, release and causal burdens, whole-decree conduct, retained control and nonobstruction. Dowell is applied to temporary relief and dissolution. Rufo retains its consent-modification scope and supplies no changed-circumstances condition for fulfilled school obligations.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both Eighth Circuit judgments sustaining the challenged salary orders and continuing quality-education obligation and denying present partial release. Preserve the actual fifty/fifty allocation and joint-and-several obligations within the orders' terms and the prior salary settlement. No reimbursement, clawback, rollback, new taxation, supervisory body or additional review condition is ordered.

### Section 1983 immunity

#### Wyatt v. Cole, 504 U.S. 158 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-126.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether private litigants using state replevin, garnishment or attachment machinery receive Harlow immunity from suit

**Holding:** Private litigants who invoke state replevin, garnishment, or attachment procedures do not receive the objective immunity from suit recognized in Harlow merely because their qualifying joint action may be attributable to the State under §1983. Whether such defendants have a distinct good-faith or probable-cause defense, whether the plaintiff bears additional elements, and the allocation of any such burden remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy: six of nine participating Justices adopt the proposition covering replevin, garnishment and attachment. Stone joins the opinion only as applied to private litigants invoking state replevin machinery, including its stated reservations; seven adopt that narrower proposition. He takes no position on its extension to the other procedures. Souter and Thomas dissent. The six full joins suffice for the broader rule; no fractured-decision analysis is required.

**Material application:** Private commercial use of compulsory process and representation in that use do not establish a public office whose discretionary performance needs immunity from litigation. Attribution under Lugar and eligibility for Harlow immunity remain separate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The availability, elements and burdens of any independent good-faith or probable-cause defense remain open, as do each defendant’s attribution and liability. Public-official immunity, Hunter’s Rule 56 administration and Hafer’s individual-capacity rule remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The private-immunity dispositions for Cole and Robbins are reversed, 7–2, and remanded for each defendant’s remaining §1983 predicates and legally available defenses. Attorney status alone settles neither Robbins’s attribution nor immunity. No damages, liability determination or new-trial command issues; official defendants’ dispositions and fee matters outside the grant are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922 (1982) — answers its reserved immunity question while preserving its two-part attribution inquiry; joint action does not itself establish immunity or either defendant's liability.
- Owen v. City of Independence, 445 U.S. 622 (1980) — applies its historical and functional inquiry; immunity is not assumed for every person reached by §1983.
- Pierson v. Ray, 386 U.S. 547 (1967) — distinguishes its police good-faith protection from automatic private immunity from suit; no private merits defense is adjudicated.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982) — declines to extend its objective public-official immunity to these private litigants; eligible officials' protection remains unchanged.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985) — applies its distinction between immunity from litigation and a defense to liability; it does not supply private eligibility.
- Malley v. Briggs, 475 U.S. 335 (1986) — uses its complaining-witness historical analogy without turning analogous tort elements into Harlow immunity.
- Dennis v. Sparks, 449 U.S. 24 (1980) — preserves its refusal to transfer judicial immunity to private joint actors; the independent reliance theory also fails for the reasons stated here.

### Spousal control of reproductive choice

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Spousal notice and effective control over the protected decision

**Holding:** Section 3209 is invalid because its compulsory notice and certification condition gives spouses practical control over protected previability decisions for a large fraction of the independently defined women whom it requires to disclose against their wishes; voluntary consultation does not cure that operation. Its exceptions do not adequately protect that group, so the notice requirement and dependent enforcement are severed and enjoined; no general numerical facial-review formula is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Material application:** For the five framework Justices an established substantial obstacle independently ends justification. Kennedy joins the notice-specific effective-control and affected-group rationale but not the complete framework. No compelling-interest assertion rescues that obstacle and no universal numerical facial formula follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The affected population is married women compelled to notify against their wishes after the actual exceptions, defined independently of the constitutional conclusion. The record establishes effective obstruction for a large fraction of that group without assigning a percentage. Under §3209, a signed substitute statement may certify that the spouse is not the father; diligent effort failed to locate him; pregnancy resulted from reported spousal sexual assault under §3128; or there is reason to believe notice would likely cause bodily injury to the woman by the spouse or another individual. Medical emergency independently excuses notice. Statements need no notarization but must warn that false statements are punishable. The incorporated assault involves sexual intercourse or deviate sexual intercourse through force, threat of force preventing resistance by a person of reasonable resolution, or an unconscious spouse. The victim or her agent must personally report to an agency with jurisdiction within ninety days of commission, not discovery of pregnancy or the abortion request. That statutory clock is distinct from district finding 297's explanation of its practical narrowing and is not independently adjudicated as a criminal-law rule. The exceptions do not generally cover economic or psychological coercion, threats to children or others, publicity, divorce or custody retaliation. Failure to qualify for one substitute does not establish safety; another substitute may remain available. None of these reporting conditions applies to the separate rape-information omission permission in §3205(a)(2)(iii). Independent spousal speech, equality and generalized privacy grounds are not reached by a controlling rationale.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Third Circuit in No. 91-902. Sever and enjoin notice, its certifications and dependent enforcement, while preserving voluntary consultation. The invalid enforcement includes professional discipline and the knowing-violation civil action available to the spouse who is the father, with resulting damages, $5,000 punitive damages and prevailing-plaintiff fees. Remove dependent notice-report fields through the separately stated judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 (1976): extended from formal spousal consent to notice proved to give effective control; voluntary consultation remains lawful.
- Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502, 514 (1990): its no-valid-applications formulation is qualified for this notice condition; voluntary disclosure by unaffected women does not defeat the demonstrated facial defect.
- Bellotti II, Ashcroft and Hodgson: distinguished because parental responsibility and effective bypass for minors do not establish marital authority over an adult.
- Roe and Doe: supply the protected decision and its life-and-health boundary.

### Statutory discrimination remedies

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

### University desegregation

#### United States v. Fordice; Ayers v. Fordice, 505 U.S. 717 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1205 and 90-6588.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the school-supervision holding applied here, see Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992, No. 89-1290, under Civil Rights — School desegregation decrees.

##### The continuing duty in a formerly dual university system

**Holding:** A State that maintained a legally segregated university system does not discharge its affirmative dismantlement duty merely by adopting good-faith, facially neutral policies. It must eliminate policies traceable to that system that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; the established duty does not require fresh discriminatory intent for each such remnant, and current racial imbalance alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Scalia does not join the system-wide rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Current racial imbalance alone is insufficient. New policies unconnected to the established dual system remain subject to ordinary equal-protection analysis. No general Title VI disparate-impact cause or heightened proof quantum is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is vacated in both consolidated dockets. The courts below must assess traceable policies' current effects, educational justification, practical elimination and interaction. If the State's duty remains undischarged, Equal Protection and the related Title VI duty are violated and appropriate remedial proceedings must follow. Existing findings are not set aside merely because the governing legal standard was wrong.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, and Brown II, 349 U.S. 294: applied; university systems share the affirmative duty to dismantle established legal segregation.
- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430: applied to effective dismantlement, with voluntary university attendance and legitimate institutional differences preserved.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237: applied; good faith and practicable removal of vestiges remain distinct requirements.
- Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992 simulated decision: applied within its actual causal and practicability limits; its school-decree holding is not replaced or mechanically transplanted.
- Bazemore v. Friday, 478 U.S. 385: distinguished; genuinely unfettered voluntary club choice does not establish removal of inherited university constraints.
- Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp., 429 U.S. 252: preserved for a new violation involving policies not rooted in the established dual system.

##### The State's burden and assessment of interacting policies

**Holding:** The State bears the burden of demonstrating that it has dismantled its established dual system; a court may not shift that burden to plaintiffs by demanding that they disprove discharge. The court must examine the current effects and practical alternatives of challenged inherited policies, including their interaction, and must tie any remedy to the violation rather than infer liability or relief from racial identifiability alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Scalia does not join the system-wide rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown II, 349 U.S. 294: applied; the State retains the burden of showing discharge of its established duty.
- Green, 391 U.S. 430: applied; effectiveness is assessed in actual operation, not by formal policy labels.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1: applied; the scope of the violation bounds equitable relief.
- Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992 simulated decision: applied; causal burdens, practicable compliance and remedial interdependence are retained.
- Milliken v. Bradley, 418 U.S. 717, and 433 U.S. 267: preserved; remediation must address the proven violation and its consequences.

##### Differential and ACT-only admissions requirements require further justification

**Holding:** Where present admissions requirements derive from deliberately exclusionary criteria and continue segregative effects, the State cannot establish dismantlement merely by attributing their later retention to neutral educational purposes. On this record it must adequately justify the differential thresholds and ACT-only method under the continuing-duty standard or replace the unjustified, practicably removable remnants; the Court does not invalidate every use of the ACT or prescribe a substitute score.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Scalia agrees only with further admissions review on a distinct ground and does not supply a ninth rationale join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown and Green: applied; inherited discriminatory admission barriers require effective dismantlement rather than a new neutral label.
- Dowell and the simulated Freeman: applied; practical removal and causal attribution govern the continuing compliance inquiry.
- Florida ex rel. Hawkins v. Board of Control, 350 U.S. 413: applied to equal access to higher education; no new test-score formula follows.

##### Remand for the full structure without a predetermined institutional remedy

**Holding:** The corrected inquiry must examine unnecessary program duplication, inherited mission assignments, continued institutional organization, and other properly challenged features including affected funding, with their combined segregative effects and educational justifications. Neither racial identifiability nor fiscal inefficiency alone requires closing or merging a university, and improved funding alone cannot establish dismantlement while unlawful barriers remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Scalia does not join the system-wide rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Additional funding merely to preserve publicly financed separate enclaves is not an entitlement; funding required for actual dismantlement remains for remedial findings. Racial identifiability and fiscal inefficiency alone do not require closure or merger.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand encompasses unnecessary duplication, mission assignments, continued institutional organization, affected funding and other properly challenged features. No closure, merger, fixed funding award, compulsory assignment or numerical balance is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Green and Swann: applied to effective, violation-linked structural relief rather than a numerical enrollment command.
- Milliken, 418 U.S. 717, and 433 U.S. 267: applied to connect practical educational relief to proven consequences.
- Alabama State Teachers Association v. Alabama Public School and College Authority, 393 U.S. 400: confined to the issues necessarily affirmed concerning the justified new campus; no blanket university-compliance safe harbor is recognized.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992 simulated decision: left intact in its consent-decree-modification setting; it supplies no different liability or automatic termination standard here.

### Voting Rights Act preclearance

#### Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-711 and 90-712.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Presley concerns the Common Fund Resolution in No. 90-711; Mack concerns the Unit System in No. 90-712. One opinion supplies the same controlling grounds for both appealed noncoverage judgments.

##### Whether the Common Fund Resolution and Unit System require §5 preclearance

**Holding:** Section 5 does not require preclearance of these reallocations of governing authority because they do not alter voting procedures, candidacy, the electorate, or the method of selecting the relevant offices and have no direct relation to voting. This ruling does not resolve whether an extreme transfer could effectively replace an elective office with an appointive one, nor does it exempt a change merely because it occurs after an election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The Common Fund Resolution replaces individual district road-fund authority with collective commission control. The Unit System places road operations under an appointed engineer while retaining substantial authority, including appointment and budget powers, in the elected commission. Neither changes the relevant selection mechanism or electorate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The listed electoral categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. Effective replacement of an elective office with an appointive office remains unresolved. The Court decides no racial-purpose or discriminatory-effect question, §2 claim, Title VI claim, or constitutional claim; the unappealed Road Supervision ruling is undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appealed noncoverage judgments are affirmed. Neither challenged resolution requires §5 preclearance or is enjoined under §5 on these grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Allen v. State Board of Elections*, 393 U.S. 544 — broad coverage of changes affecting electoral choice remains; elective-to-appointive selection is distinguished from these retained elected offices.
- *Perkins v. Matthews*, 400 U.S. 379 — changes to the electorate and voting arrangements remain covered; coverage remains distinct from discrimination merits.
- *Dougherty County Board of Education v. White*, 439 U.S. 32 — candidacy restrictions directly related to electoral choice remain covered.
- *McCain v. Lybrand*, 465 U.S. 236, and *City of Lockhart v. United States*, 460 U.S. 125 — changes in electoral arrangements remain within §5; neither decides ordinary internal governing duties.
- *NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission*, 470 U.S. 166 — practical changes to election timing or procedure require proper review; postelection timing alone supplies no exemption here.

##### Whether the Attorney General's interpretation permits coverage without the statutory connection to voting

**Holding:** The Attorney General's interpretation of §5 is entitled to substantial consideration but cannot extend preclearance to governing changes that lack the statutory connection to voting. The proposed significant-transfer/different-constituencies test cannot substitute for that connection in these appeals.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The rejected test concerns significant transfers of authority between officials responsible to substantially different constituencies. That formulation can reach governing responsibilities without identifying any change in voting and therefore cannot substitute for the required connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The distinction between §5 coverage and review of discriminatory effect remains intact. Administrative expertise retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Board of Commissioners of Sheffield*, 435 U.S. 110 — administrative interpretation retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837 — preserved; deference does not authorize exceeding the statute's scope.
- *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130 — coverage and discriminatory-effect review remain separate inquiries.
- *South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301 — §5's remedial function is preserved; it does not by itself establish coverage of every governing change.

### Prevailing parties and reasonable fees

#### Farrar v. Hobby, 506 U.S. 103 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-990.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility

**Holding:** A civil-rights plaintiff who obtains an enforceable nominal-damages judgment against the defendant from whom fees are sought is a prevailing party under §1988. The judgment must directly benefit the plaintiff by changing the defendant’s legal obligation; its nominal amount does not defeat eligibility.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices support this controlling proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 — applied; nominal relief vindicates the established procedural violation without presumed compensatory injury.
- Hewitt v. Helms, 482 U.S. 755 — distinguished; favorable language without enforceable relief differs from a payment judgment.
- Rhodes v. Stewart, 488 U.S. 1 — distinguished; ineffective declaratory relief differs from a judgment directly benefiting the estate.
- Texas State Teachers Assn. v. Garland Independent School District, 489 U.S. 782 — applied and clarified; magnitude affects amount, not eligibility where nominal relief is enforceable.

##### Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review

**Holding:** When the court of appeals denies fees solely because a nominal-damages plaintiff is ineligible, correction of that error does not itself determine a reasonable amount. On this record, the fee judgment is vacated and remanded for preserved reasonableness objections and an explained Hensley assessment of actual success, allowing reduction to zero without automatically reinstating the prior award or requiring proportionality to damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices control the remedy and stated fee principles.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424 — applied; actual success, unrelated unsuccessful work and a reasoned discretionary assessment govern amount.
- City of Riverside v. Rivera, 477 U.S. 561 — applied within its authority; no mechanical proportionality requirement, without guaranteed fees for nominal relief.
- Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87 — applied; the court must determine a reasonable statutory fee in the circumstances.
- City of Burlington v. Dague, June 24, 1992 — distinguished; contingency enhancement under the two environmental statutes does not decide this eligibility or remand question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Eligibility does not guarantee a positive fee. Actual success, exclusion of unrelated unsuccessful work and an explained Hensley assessment govern; no damages-to-fees ratio or invented public benefit is permitted. The decision does not categorically forbid affirmance on a sufficient alternative fee ground in another case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the fee judgment and remand for preserved reasonableness objections and lower-court review as necessary. The prior $280,000 award is not automatically restored; the result may be a reduced award or zero.

### Private conspiracies and clinic obstruction

#### Bray v. Alexandria Women’s Health Clinic, 506 U.S. 263 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-985.

**Decided:** January 13, 1993.

##### Private conspiracy requires purposeful interference with a privately protected right

**Holding:** A purely private conspiracy under the deprivation clause of §1985(3) must aim at interfering with a right protected against private as well as official encroachment; incidental effects are insufficient. Clinic obstruction applied to interstate and local patients alike does not establish that interference with interstate travel was a conscious objective merely because substantial numbers of patients crossed state lines.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. Souter separately agrees with rejection of this theory without a formal join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88 — applied; coverage of private conspiracies retains purposeful, invidiously discriminatory deprivation requirements.
- United Brotherhood of Carpenters & Joiners v. Scott, 463 U.S. 825 — applied; a private conspiracy must target a privately protected right.
- United States v. Guest, 383 U.S. 745 — applied by analogy; injury to a traveler differs from purposeful interference with interstate travel.

##### Constitutional abortion liberty does not itself supply the private-right element

**Holding:** Constitutional protection of abortion choice against governmental interference does not itself create a right against the purely private obstruction proved here. Without necessary state involvement or another right protected against private encroachment, the deprivation clause cannot sustain this theory merely because the conspiracy deliberately seeks to prevent abortions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters of this distinct sufficient ground addressing the alternative privacy theory. Souter separately accepts Scott’s private-right limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott, 463 U.S. 825 — applied; the underlying constitutional guarantee’s state/private distinction remains.
- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — preserved as carried forward and modified by Casey; no direct private-obstruction cause follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992 — preserved; its six-step state-regulation framework does not change §1985(3)’s elements.
- Great American Federal Savings & Loan Assn. v. Novotny, 442 U.S. 366 — applied; §1985(3) does not itself create the substantive right.

##### Federal fees fall while independent state relief requires examination

**Holding:** The existing §1988 fee-and-cost award cannot stand on the rejected §1985(3) liability and is vacated. The injunction returns for examination of its state trespass/public-nuisance foundations, jurisdiction and lawful scope; federal merits failure alone neither sustains it nor requires its dissolution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. Souter agrees to remove the present federal basis but would additionally direct consideration of hindrance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Farrar v. Hobby, December 14, 1992 — distinguished; enforceable federal nominal relief differs from reversal of the only adjudicated federal liability basis.
- Gibbs, 383 U.S. 715 — applied; pendent adjudication and support for particular relief remain distinct.

##### The unadjudicated hindrance theory is not resolved here

**Holding:** The Court does not adjudicate liability under §1985(3)’s separate clause concerning conspiracies to prevent or hinder constituted state authorities from giving or securing equal protection. That theory was not adjudicated below or fairly included in the granted deprivation-clause questions, and no finding establishes the required purpose to hinder state protection; the remand does not direct liability under that theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia Part V, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters control scope and mandate. Ultimate substantive requirements of the separate clause remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido, April 1, 1992 — applied; a distinct claim outside the grant differs from additional argument on a presented claim.
- Kamen v. Kemper Financial Services, Inc., 500 U.S. 90 — applied at that scope; additional legal arguments do not erase claim limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Women’s status as a qualifying class, invidious sex-based animus, the full nonracial class boundary, a categorical rule for equal intrastate obstruction and statutory authority for an injunction on a successful federal claim remain unresolved. Scalia Part III has four votes and supplies no alternative holding. The hindrance theory receives no merits ruling; a later properly pleaded and supported claim is not barred on its merits by this disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse deprivation-clause liability, vacate the $27,687.55 federal fee-and-cost award and remand for the injunction’s state trespass/public-nuisance grounds, pendent jurisdiction and lawful scope. Federal merits failure does not automatically dissolve the injunction or dismiss the entire action for lack of jurisdiction. The five-Justice mandate directs no hindrance adjudication.

For Bray v. Alexandria Women’s Health Clinic, January 13, 1993, No. 90-985, concerning federal merits failure and pendent jurisdiction, see **Federal Courts — Federal merits failure and pendent jurisdiction**.

### Pension withdrawal liability and fair adjudication

#### Concrete Pipe & Products of California, Inc. v. Construction Laborers Pension Trust for Southern California, 508 U.S. 602 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-904.
**Decided:** June 14, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Concrete Pipe & Products of California, Inc. v. Construction Laborers Pension Trust for Southern California, June 14, 1993, No. 91-904, see Property and Economic Rights — Pension withdrawal liability and takings.

##### The preliminary assessment is not an adjudication

**Holding:** The interested plan sponsor’s withdrawal-liability assessment and demand are enforcement acts, not an adjudication. Due process permits that preliminary demand because the dispute first receives adjudication before a neutral arbitrator, under the lawful arbitral burdens stated separately below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, The preliminary assessment is not an adjudication portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Tumey, 273 U.S. 510 — interested adjudication forbidden; the sponsor is not the adjudicator here.
- Ward, 409 U.S. 57 — appellate review does not automatically cure initial biased adjudication; that premise is absent here.
- Withrow, 421 U.S. 35 — preliminary and adjudicative functions distinguished.

##### The factual presumption assigns ordinary persuasion

**Holding:** In neutral arbitration, §1401(a)(3)(A) places on the employer the burden to disprove the challenged factual determination by a preponderance. It does not add appellate deference to the sponsor’s factual determinations, a clear-and-convincing requirement or a conclusive presumption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, The factual presumption assigns ordinary persuasion portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (8 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Crowell v. Benson, 285 U.S. 22 — constitutionally sound construction used within the interpretive choice the text permits.
- Section 1401(c) — judicial review presumes arbitral findings correct unless rebutted by a clear preponderance; no transfer of that standard into arbitration.

##### Actuarial rebuttal retains both statutory routes

**Holding:** Under §1401(a)(3)(B), a contesting employer may rebut the actuarial calculation by a preponderance showing either that assumptions and methods were unreasonable in the aggregate, considering plan experience and reasonable expectations, or that the actuary made a significant error in applying them. The professional and statutory constraints make this presumption constitutionally permissible here; fraud need not be proved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, Actuarial rebuttal retains both statutory routes portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Section 1393 — statutory constraints on actuarial assumptions and methods applied.
- Section 1401(a)(3)(B) — aggregate unreasonableness OR significant application error preserved as separate routes.
- Connolly, 475 U.S. 211 — pooled-plan structure provides context, not an irrebuttable actuarial presumption.

##### This withdrawal allocation is rational

**Holding:** Allocating a proportionate share of unfunded vested-benefit costs to this withdrawing employer is rational in light of its participation in the pooled pension undertaking. Neither private contribution limits nor the absence of a one-to-one match between its contributions and benefits to its own employees defeats this substantive-due-process application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, This withdrawal allocation is rational portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arbitral factfinding and actuarial judgments remain distinct. Under the applicable §1393(a)(1) route, assumptions and methods collectively must be reasonable and represent the actuary’s best estimate of anticipated plan experience; another reasonable estimate alone proves no defect. Judicial review under §1401(c) separately presumes arbitral facts correct unless rebutted by a clear preponderance. Defined-benefit classification is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm enforcement of this award under independent arbitration and the stated statutory construction; no second arbitration is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Usery v. Turner Elkhorn, 428 U.S. 1 — rational retrospective economic allocation applied.
- PBGC v. R. A. Gray, 467 U.S. 717 — pension-protection allocation sustained within actual statutory setting.

### Equality and procedure in civil commitment

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. **Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

### Prosecutorial investigation and press statements

#### Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7849.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor who performs ordinary investigative work to obtain evidence identifying a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication in that function; later courtroom use does not transform the earlier work into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate’s role remain protected, and neither all pre-indictment acts nor all post-indictment acts receive a categorical classification.

### Civil statutory temporal reach

#### [Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Landgraf_v_USI_Film_Products_merits_1994-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

### Civil-rights amendments and earlier conduct

#### [Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Rivers_v_Roadway_Express_merits_1994-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-938.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Section 101’s new contractual coverage does not govern the earlier conduct

**Holding:** Section 101’s expansion of §1981’s making-and-enforcing-contracts coverage to include making, performance, modification, termination, and enjoyment of all benefits, privileges, terms and conditions of the contractual relationship does not govern conduct completed before November 21, 1991: it adds substantive legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. This provision-specific rule does not eliminate a claim already covered by old §1981 or decide a separate dispute over subsection (c)’s protection against nongovernmental discrimination and impairment under color of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; these five adopt the complete explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same temporal result through their separate relevant-activity and textual-command method.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, decided earlier April 26, 1994: applied to §101 independently of §102, preserving the provision-specific temporal inquiry and its five-Justice controlling explanation.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988), and Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to new substantive consequences for completed events; no constitutional bar against sufficiently directed civil retrospective legislation is adopted.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): distinguished in its collateral litigation-fee setting, not overruled.
- Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164 (1989): applied as the governing construction of the earlier text within its scope; an old-law enforcement claim is not extinguished by refusing the later expansion.

##### Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach

**Holding:** When Congress expands statutory liability in response to a judicial interpretation, its corrective purpose or use of a new definition does not alone make the amendment govern earlier conduct; sufficient temporal direction remains necessary under Landgraf, and Congress may supply that direction within constitutional limits. Judicial construction of the earlier statute remains distinct: Harper governs application of that construction to open cases, and a defendant need not prove actual reliance on a later-issued judicial decision before invoking the enacted amendment’s limited temporal reach.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Patterson: retained as the old-text construction relevant to the temporal question; Congress’s prospective expansion supersedes its narrower coverage for conduct governed by the amendment, without overruling it as the construction of earlier law.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): applied to distinguish judicial retroactivity from later legislation; the lower court’s selective Chevron Oil balancing is not adopted.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished because its express substitution of law for identified pending disputes supplied direction absent here.
- Frisbie v. Whitney, 9 Wall. 187 (1870), and Freeborn v. Smith, 2 Wall. 160 (1865): distinguished as instances of directed curative legislation; neither establishes automatic retrospective reach for a corrective amendment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress's prospective expansion supersedes Patterson's narrow coverage for conduct governed by the amendment while leaving it the construction of earlier text. The Court does not reconsider Patterson, endorse the lower court's whole-Act generalization or selective Chevron Oil balancing, or extinguish an old-scope enforcement claim. The distinct §1981(c) question remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit judgment is affirmed and the case remanded, 8–1, preserving the existing old-law contract-enforcement jury remand. That remand retains Lytle's protection against preclusion by later Title VII bench findings; it is not §102's new jury option. No discrimination finding, damages or dismissal of the preserved claim is ordered, and the unreviewed related matters are not reopened.

### Disability education and parental reimbursement

#### [Florence County School District Four v. Carter, 510 U.S. 7](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Florence_County_School_District_Four_v_Carter_merits_1993-11-09.md)

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Appropriate parental placement and equitable reimbursement

**Holding:** When the public offer violates IDEA and the parents' unilateral private placement provides an education otherwise appropriate for the child under the Act, §1415(e)(2) permits equitable reimbursement even if that placement lacks state approval or does not satisfy every requirement in §1401(a)(18)'s definition of a publicly provided free appropriate education. ### Equal protection in jury selection

#### [J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/JEB_v_Alabama_ex_rel_TB_merits_1994-04-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1239.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

**Holding:** The State may not strike a prospective juror because of sex or the assumption that sex itself predicts competence or impartiality. Under the equal-protection requirement that a sex classification substantially serve an important governmental objective, a generalized prediction that men will favor the alleged father does not justify this exclusion; genuine individual, sex-neutral reasons remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the rule and explanation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment and independently supports the operative prohibition on individual-right grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190 — applied: sex classifications require an important objective and substantial relationship.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718 — applied to unjustified sex stereotypes and equal protection of men.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended to purposeful sex-based state peremptories; its racial rule remains intact.
- Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 — applied for the excluded juror’s individual interest and the litigant’s ability to challenge discriminatory selection.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614, and Georgia v. McCollum — relied on for juror equality within selection; the private-party sex-strike question is not decided.
- Taylor v. Louisiana, 419 U.S. 522 — distinguished: no proportionally representative petit jury is required by this decision.

##### Staged proof and the remand

**Holding:** A party challenging a peremptory strike as purposeful sex discrimination must first make the required prima facie showing; the striking party must then give a genuine sex-neutral explanation, which need not justify a challenge for cause. The trial court decides intentional discrimination with the ultimate burden on the objector; if Alabama cannot sustain the challenged strikes under that inquiry, the verdict must be set aside and a new trial held before a lawfully selected jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s six-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the procedure and conditional remedy; Kennedy independently agrees with the operative inquiry and remand, producing seven votes for reversal and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — its staged purpose inquiry is extended without requiring a cause-level explanation or an explanation for every strike.
- Georgia v. McCollum — its distinction between production and the objector’s ultimate persuasion burden is preserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The case involves State strikes, not an adjudication of private-party sex strikes. No proportional petit jury, cause-level explanation for every strike or stricter general sex-classification rule is required. A genuinely neutral reason correlated with sex is not automatically forbidden, while a neutral label does not defeat a supported pretext finding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Court of Civil Appeals is reversed and the case remanded, 7–2. Apply the staged selection inquiry and order a lawfully selected new trial if the strikes cannot be sustained. No paternity finding, jury quota or automatic determination of pretext is made.

### Qualified-immunity review

#### [Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Elder_v_Holloway_merits_1994-02-23.md)

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. ### Conviction-related damages and accrual

#### [Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Heck_v_Humphrey_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-6188.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record

**Holding:** A state prisoner in Heck's position, in custody with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, has no presently cognizable §1983 damages claim when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that outstanding conviction or sentence, unless the judgment has undergone qualifying invalidation. The rule decides that setting; it does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide plaintiffs who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity or whose custody has ended.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): its release-channel distinction informs the new, bounded damages prerequisite; its holding is not misdescribed as already deciding damages-only claims.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974): preserves civil relief compatible with the operative conviction and custody judgment.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): remains the rule against general §1983 exhaustion; this decision addresses present cognizability instead.

##### Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite

**Holding:** For the in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim described in Part I, coordination of §1983 with the available habeas channel supports favorable invalidation as a prerequisite, with the common-law malicious-prosecution analogy implementing that coordination. This is a new, limited restriction on §1983, not conversion of the damages complaint into habeas, a general exhaustion rule, or adoption of every malicious-prosecution element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join Part II.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: extends statutory coordination to the particular damages claim while preserving the distinct relief and conditions of habeas.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves its permissible civil-remedy category; factual overlap with prosecution does not remove a claim from that category.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguishes general exhaustion from the newly adopted prerequisite; exhaustion alone cannot satisfy it.
- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978): applies common-law remedial guidance as an aid to construing the federal remedy, not a wholesale importation of a state tort.

##### Qualifying invalidation and deferred accrual

**Holding:** The claim barred under Part I becomes cognizable only when the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. That claim accrues only upon such invalidation; exhaustion, unsuccessful review, or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation, and neither immediate accrual with equitable tolling nor a preservation stay is required for this unaccrued claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt the four invalidation routes and the accrual consequence for the barred claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus: applies its common-law-informed approach to the federal remedy without imposing additional constitutional-liability elements.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: preserves the distinction between exhaustion and existence of a cognizable damages claim.

##### Independent injuries, claim-specific analysis and this dismissal

**Holding:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity falls outside this prerequisite even if it concerns the same investigation or trial; courts must compare the civil claim's elements with the basis of the conviction, and compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury. On the accepted characterization of Heck's presented action, dismissal without prejudice stands on lack of present cognizability, without deciding misconduct, immunity, personal responsibility, preclusion, damages, or entitlement to release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt this boundary, application and remedy.

**Material application:** The lower courts' characterization that Heck's action necessarily attacked the conviction was not disputed in the petition or principal merits brief; the late reply established no separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand. An unlawful-search claim can remain compatible with a conviction through independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error, but those possibilities establish neither automatic admissibility of every search claim nor compensation for conviction injury without invalidation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves the distinction between compatible civil relief and relief dependent on invalidating custody.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: distinguishes its opportunity-only claim; the existing §1983 holding remains intact.
- Carey v. Piphus: applies the requirement of actual compensable injury without adjudicating the amount or merits here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity remain outside the question resolved. No universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action, automatic state-law preclusion, automatic habeas conversion or pending-prosecution abstention rule is adopted. Custis's distinct no-meaningful-review reservation in the ACCA channel remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal without prejudice is affirmed unanimously for lack of present cognizability. The barred claim has not accrued, so its limitations period is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet. A later otherwise proper action may follow qualifying invalidation, subject to remaining requirements; no stay, release, automatic conversion to habeas, liability, immunity or damages determination is ordered.

### Prison officials’ duty to protect

#### [Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Farmer_v_Brennan_merits_1994-06-06.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7247.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Holding:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Material application:** The official need not foresee the precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. No defendant is found to have the required knowledge, responsibility or causal role; reasonable but unsuccessful protection remains outside deliberate indifference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Holding:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Proposition-level authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Holding:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Proposition-level authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and summary judgment without an express-warning prerequisite. Discovery may be allowed or denied on a proper basis, and renewed summary judgment remains available. Knowledge, responsibility, causation, reasonable response, capacities and immunity remain individualized. No trial, damages or placement injunction is ordered; prospective relief must fit any established continuing danger.

### Abortion access and provision-specific review

#### [Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Fargo_Womens_Health_Organization_v_Schafer_merits_1995-02-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1712.
**Decided:** February 13, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements (The access claims return for a supported assessment and prompt interim consideration).

##### The complete Casey standard governs the access and facial challenge

**Holding:** Casey's complete six-step standard governs the challenged previability access duties, without Salerno's independent no-valid-applications barrier or an independent numerical facial-review threshold. Courts must identify the duty and protected baseline, function and purpose, attributable interference and the affected class, the applicable substantive protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and relief corresponding to the proved violation; finding no substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Material application:** Telephone communication by the authorized physician or agent remains relevant; no statutory additional personal visit is found. The physician retains responsibility for the gestational determination, while an agent may obtain history and convey that determination. The patient's written certification and its receipt by the physician or agent precede the abortion, not necessarily the visit by twenty-four hours. Notice that materials are available does not require accepting, reading or endorsing them; copies must be furnished if chosen. Assistance may be available, but payment is not guaranteed. A support obligation is not a promise of collection. Statutory freedom to comment or dissociate concerns the printed materials; the lower court additionally permits comment on assistance and paternal-support statements.

Casey's qualified information rulings cannot be imported without their limits. Pennsylvania separately protects omission of information upon proof by a preponderance of a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect physical or mental health; the feared injury need not occur, and that defense does not waive the waiting period. Pennsylvania also permits, but does not require, omission of paternal-support information in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition. North Dakota's supplied text contains neither provision. Their absence requires attention to supported and preserved compulsory-information applications; it neither inserts these exceptions into North Dakota law nor establishes automatic invalidity. No independent new speech claim or parental-involvement challenge is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): applies the complete six-step rule, its proof allocation, broad necessary-care safeguards and tailored-relief qualification unchanged; Pennsylvania's particular interval result is not an automatic North Dakota disposition.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): does not supply an independent all-applications barrier overriding Casey in this abortion-access challenge; its operation outside this setting is not decided.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 497 U.S. 502 (1990): its rejection of speculative additional duties remains relevant, while its older facial formulation remains subject to Casey's actual qualification; a hypothetical second visit is not treated as enacted law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The complete operative standard is retained without change; see Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, June 29, 1992, Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent, the previability-regulation holding. Baseline violations, obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently invalidate a duty; remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring, and remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit and an answer to identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. No universal numerical threshold, mandatory new study or cheapest-policy requirement is added. Causation, the stable affected population, usable exceptions, clinical and communication safeguards, and relief matched to proved violations remain necessary. Pennsylvania's particular interval result supplies no automatic North Dakota disposition.

##### The abortion definition excludes an unintended diagnostic termination

**Holding:** Section 14-02.1-02(1), defining abortion as termination of human pregnancy with an intention other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus, identifies a purposeful pregnancy termination. Diagnostic amniocentesis followed by an unintended pregnancy loss is not brought within that definition merely because the diagnostic purpose is neither of the two stated exclusions; this reading defeats the preserved facial notice and enforcement objection without a borrowed culpability default.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is not immunity for diagnostic treatment, negligence or other misconduct; it does not decide a completed prosecution, civil award, general knowledge-of-illegality requirement or actual-knowledge minimum for every statutory element. It does not resolve the emergency definition or every sanction attached to consent duties.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): applies its contextual definition analysis without transplanting its federal scienter construction or treating fault as a universal cure.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies the separate notice and minimum-enforcement-guidance requirements; distinguishes an undefined official compliance demand from this bounded medical act and purpose.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves its requirement to examine the particular trigger and fault separately; the present ground rests on the category's own words rather than fault addressed to a different element.

##### The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion

**Holding:** The preserved facial vagueness challenge to section 14-02.1-02(7) fails because its specified outcomes and timing, assessed through the physician's prospective best clinical judgment, give notice and enforcement guidance: the pregnancy must require immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay must create grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Gravity, immediacy and major bodily function call for a professional forecast, not certainty, a numerical prognosis or waiting until the threatened harm occurs; this holding does not adjudicate the distinct claim that the exception's substantive breadth fails to protect particular necessary care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Clarity is not a ruling that this restricted emergency category satisfies every Casey life-and-health requirement. Casey's broader necessary-care protection, including individualized physical and mental health and care before catastrophic deterioration, remains law. The emergency undue-burden claim was not pressed below and is not decided here. The federal appellate significant-threat construction is not enacted text or an authoritative North Dakota Supreme Court construction; it does not license deleting immediate, irreversible or major from the statute. The present notice ground stands independently of that broader construction and of any presumed default fault rule.

Section 14-02.1-03(1)'s emergency exception excuses that subsection's ordinary requirements, but requires an explanation of the medical indications before the abortion if possible and written certification of those indications. The conditional explanation is no advance-permission requirement, and certification supplies no authority to delay necessary treatment. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish all elements of a criminal offense or civil damages; disagreement may remain relevant under actual applicable law. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct are not immunized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971): applies the intelligibility of professional judgment at the stated scope; its D.C. statute's life-or-health construction and prosecution burden do not become North Dakota law.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973): applies its recognition of clinical judgment without importing Georgia's broader statutory construction or creating professional immunity.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): distinguishes its conflicting viability and treatment duties, while retaining independent scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability for error.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies minimum enforcement guidelines and distinguishes undefined official satisfaction from the stated clinical criteria; no universal all-applications bar is adopted.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): preserves broad necessary-care safeguards and conditional emergency explanation; its Pennsylvania construction was not a separate emergency-vagueness holding and does not determine this distinct claim.

##### A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself

**Holding:** Section 12.1-02-02 alone does not establish default willfulness for the silent misdemeanor clause of section 14-02.1-11, and neither that default nor the latter section's separately expressed willful rule-or-regulation infraction supplies the criminal or civil elements of every chapter violation. Rejection of that additional rationale does not defeat the independently sufficient definition holdings or establish facial invalidity of the general penalty merely because its first sentence lacks an express culpability term; actual element-specific fault and liability remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The misdemeanor clause covers a chapter violation for which no other penalty is specifically prescribed. The distinct infraction clause concerns a willful violation of a promulgated rule or regulation. The latter's express term does not amend the former. The independent civil remedy provides $10,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an abortion without the required informed consent, and $5,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an attempted abortion without compliance. The Court enters no liability determination or award under either provision and creates no civil scienter defense. No separate facial judgment on an unpresented civil-damages theory is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, 455 U.S. 489 (1982): applies contextual notice, enforcement and scienter principles, with greater clarity required for criminal sanctions and protected conduct; its commercial all-applications formulation does not govern without its premise.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves the separation of an intelligible clinical standard from fault concerning an erroneous assessment; no particular universal fault level is supplied.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): preserves its context-specific distinction between definition and fault; no universal knowledge element or scienter cure is inferred.
- City of Dickinson v. Mueller, 261 N.W.2d 787 (N.D. 1977), and State v. North Dakota Education Association, 262 N.W.2d 731 (N.D. 1978): apply their title limitation when assessing the asserted default; no abortion-specific state construction is attributed to either case.
- State v. Goetz, 312 N.W.2d 1 (N.D. 1981): distinguishes definition of an expressly enacted willfulness term from importing default fault; its securities-law instruction and knowledge-of-illegality discussion do not settle these elements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Access review is vacated and remanded, 6–3; the abortion-definition, emergency-definition and associated limited general-penalty rejections are each affirmed, 8–1. The District Court must promptly consider interim protection under the ordinary standard and current record. No dissolved injunction is automatically restored, no final injunction or hearing is ordered, and no emergency-access claim unpressed below or new parental or speech claim is decided.

### Police force and municipal responsibility

#### [Plakas v. Drinski](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Plakas_v_Drinski_merits_1995-01-09.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1824.
**Decided:** January 9, 1995.

##### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 (1989): applies objective reasonableness under the complete contemporaneous circumstances from the reasonable officer's on-scene perspective, with allowance for tense and rapidly developing events; no subjective-malice or hindsight test is substituted.
- Tennessee v. Garner, 471 U.S. 1 (1985): preserves the prohibition on deadly force against a nondangerous fleeing person and the serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning conditions; this supported immediate armed attack differs from nondangerous escape.
- Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991): preserves the supported nonmovant account and complete contemporaneous record at summary judgment; this case neither selects immunity nor uses Hunter as notice predating its decision.
- Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994): preserves consideration of relevant law on a preserved immunity issue, ordinary record limits and conduct-date notice; no immunity disposition or mandatory merits-first sequence is added.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earlier-beating theory was not pleaded and lacks the asserted causal showing; the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. Gilmere's different provocation theory is neither approved nor disapproved. The Court does not decide a case in which the threat ended before the shot or a supported safe alternative makes the force unreasonable. The merits ground supplies no general immunity for tactical decisions.

##### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

**Holding:** A municipality's §1983 liability requires a cognizable federal deprivation attributable to municipal policy or training, the applicable culpability and a causal connection; officer personal immunity is not municipal immunity, and reasonable final force alone does not answer every independent municipal-created-danger theory. The county is entitled to summary judgment here because the actual equipment-and-training contention establishes neither a constitutional equipment-list duty nor a developed independent deprivation with the required causal and culpability showing; a purely derivative force theory also lacks a predicate when the force itself is reasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the county judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserves municipal policy/custom responsibility and causation for a federal deprivation; employment or vicarious responsibility alone is insufficient.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): applies the required failure-to-train culpability and causal connection; it does not create a substantive right to every device or training method.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992): applies the separation between deprivation and municipal attribution; its voluntary-employment holding does not decide every affirmative municipal injury.
- City of Los Angeles v. Heller, 475 U.S. 796 (1986): applies to a derivative theory resting solely on constitutionally reasonable force; it does not supply a universal bar to a distinct municipal wrong.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993): preserves ordinary municipal pleading and distinct Rule 56 proof; it neither relaxes Monell/Canton elements nor supplies personal immunity to a municipality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment for Drinski and Newton County is affirmed on the presented force and municipal claims. No trial, damages or personal-immunity ruling is ordered.

### Equal athletic opportunity and sex-conscious restructuring

#### [Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kelley_v_Board_of_Trustees_of_the_University_of_Illinois_merits_1995-05-22.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-783.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Program-wide participation opportunity

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies lawful delegated interpretation; authorizes no quota contrary to statute.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144: applies reasonable regulatory interpretation within its terms.

##### Independent constitutional scrutiny

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applies intermediate scrutiny to sex-conscious treatment.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applies genuine-objective and connected-disadvantage requirements.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547: its racial-treatment analysis supplies no changed sex standard or federal-compliance immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the University on the distinct Title IX and equal-protection claims. No reinstatement, damages or remand is ordered, and no broad section 1983 preclusion rule is adopted.

### Federal remedial racial classifications

#### [Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Adarand_Constructors_Inc_v_Pena_merits_1995-06-12.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The inherited federal remedial standard sustains this challenge

**Holding:** Under Metro Broadcasting, a benign federal racial classification supported by Congress must serve an important governmental objective and be substantially related to that objective; Fullilove retains its supported judgment recognizing Congress's remedial authority at the scope actually sustained there. Applying that meaningful review of purpose, fit and burdens, the Court affirms the judgment rejecting this preserved challenge to the subcontractor compensation arrangement, without deciding every certification route or conferring a general exemption on measures called benign.

**Proposition-level authority:** Merits portion of Justice Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this rule and bounded application, not merely the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Metro's actual federal standard and Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): retains the important-objective/substantial-relationship standard for benign federal classifications at its actual scope.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains its supported remedial judgment and congressional-competence recognition; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): distinguished as state/local remedial classification law; its standard is not displaced or extended to erase Metro here.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to evidence, remedial fit and burdens; remedial purpose alone supplies no exemption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment rejecting the preserved constitutional challenge, 5–4. No remand, program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract follows.

##### Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims

**Holding:** Qualified immunity does not impose a categorical requirement of direct evidence of unconstitutional motive. A plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements through legally competent direct or circumstantial proof and satisfy ordinary procedural burdens; specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general abolition of heightened specificity rules or new clear-and-convincing burden is adopted. The December 22 episode may independently fail for inadequate personal-involvement support. Pell and Turner retain prison-access limits and create no unrestricted press-conference entitlement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 — preserved as the objective clearly-established-law defense, distinguished from the underlying motive element.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635 — applied to particularized notice and appropriately tailored discovery.
- Siegert v. Gilley, 500 U.S. 226 — distinguished; its constitutional-injury ruling established no direct-only proof rule.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County, 507 U.S. 163 — used by analogy only; its individual-immunity reservation remains.

Independently sustainable episode-specific judgments and lawful discovery limits remain available. No trial, unlimited discovery, retaliation finding or damages award is ordered. The Fifth Amendment dismissal and Title III matters remain undisturbed.

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

### Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

**Governing question:** How equal protection governs a State’s use of sex in peremptory jury selection.

**Current rule:** The State may not strike a juror because of sex or a stereotype that sex predicts competence or impartiality. Sex classifications must substantially serve an important governmental objective; a generalized prediction of sympathy does not suffice. The objector first must make the required prima facie showing, after which the striking party must produce a genuine sex-neutral explanation, without having to establish cause. The court decides purposeful discrimination with the ultimate persuasion burden on the objector. A correlated individual characteristic may be a genuine reason but cannot serve as pretext or proxy. Established discriminatory selection requires a new trial before a lawfully selected jury, not a proportionally composed jury.

**Authority by component:**

- Prohibition, staged proof and conditional new-trial remedy: [J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B — Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes; Staged proof and the remand](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/JEB_v_Alabama_ex_rel_TB_merits_1994-04-19.md#public-projection), 1994-04-19. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the rule and explanation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment and independently supports the operative prohibition on individual-right grounds. Blackmun’s six-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the procedure and conditional remedy; Kennedy independently agrees with the operative inquiry and remand, producing seven votes for reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B, 1994-04-19.

**Open question:** The sex-strike rule for private parties is not decided. The earlier racial-strike rules, including the criminal-defense rule, retain their separate scope.

## Standards and Tests selection: Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

### Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

**Governing question:** Title IX accommodation and constitutional review during public-university athletic restructuring.

**Current rule:** Title IX does not guarantee a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity. Effective accommodation may be shown by any one of substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex’s interests and abilities; other regulatory duties remain independently binding. A public university’s sex-conscious restructuring separately must serve an actual important objective and be substantially related to it. Genuine fiscal or programmatic change may preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality, but ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient. Stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than demonstrated need are forbidden.

## Standards and Tests selection: Partial withdrawal of school-desegregation supervision

### Partial withdrawal of school-desegregation supervision

**Governing question:** Release of completed portions of a school-desegregation decree while obligations elsewhere remain unfinished.

**Current rule:** A court may withdraw supervision from a completed component while retaining jurisdiction and supervision elsewhere only after the school district shows full and satisfactory compliance and elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable. The court must assess demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree and whether retaining control of that component is necessary or practicable to obtain compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy. The district bears the burden of showing that imbalance for which it disclaims responsibility is not traceable to the constitutional violation. Genuinely independent demographic changes do not require perpetual balancing after required remedial work is completed. Existing findings receive clear-error review, but a finding on one question does not decide unexamined causal or remedial relationships. Withdrawal orders must define obligations released, remaining duties and retained supervision.

**Authority by component:**
- Partial-release conditions, burden and integrated assessment: [Freeman v. Pitts](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Freeman_v_Pitts_merits_1992-03-31.md), March 31, 1992, holding 1, “Partial withdrawal under an unfinished decree.”
- Causation, clear-error review and defined retained obligations: the same record, holding 2, “Causation and the limits of the remand.” Stone for White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter controls; Thomas did not participate.

**Present operation effective:** Freeman, March 31, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Racial imbalance alone establishes neither a present constitutional violation nor entitlement to release. Neither good intentions alone, an automatic demographic exemption nor categorical simultaneous compliance across all components replaces the specified showing. Rufo's consent-decree modification standard is distinct and imposes no additional changed-circumstances prerequisite for finding a school-decree obligation fulfilled. Separate opinions' specific factual applications and proposed changes to causation presumptions do not control.

**Holdings navigation:** [Freeman v. Pitts, 503 U.S. 467 (1992) — School desegregation decrees](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#freeman-v-pitts-503-us-467-1992).

### Violation-linked educational salary support

**Governing question:** Salary funding in a decree remedying established school segregation.

**Current rule:** A desegregation court may require instructional and noninstructional salary support reasonably necessary to deliver educational measures repairing established segregation-caused injury without finding low salaries themselves a separate constitutional wrong. ## Standards and Tests selection: Dismantling a formerly segregated public university system

### Dismantling a formerly segregated public university system

**Governing question:** Discharge of a State's affirmative duty after it maintained a legally segregated university system.

**Current rule:** Good faith and facially neutral policies alone do not discharge the duty. The State must eliminate policies traceable to the dual system that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices. No fresh discriminatory intent is needed for each such remnant; current racial imbalance alone is insufficient. The State bears the burden of demonstrating dismantlement. Examine present effects, educational justification and practical alternatives, including interacting admissions policies, program duplication, mission assignments, institutional organization and properly challenged funding features. A later neutral purpose does not by itself remove an inherited policy's continuing effects. Tie remedies to the established violation and practicable removal of its consequences: neither racial identifiability nor fiscal inefficiency alone requires closure or merger, and improved funding alone does not establish dismantlement while unlawful barriers remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Continuing duty, burden, combined-effects inquiry and violation-linked remedies: [United States v. Fordice and Ayers v. Fordice, holdings 1–4](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Fordice_and_Ayers_v_Fordice_merits_1992-06-26.md), June 26, 1992; White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. The related Title VI disposition uses that same established discrimination duty.

**Present operation effective:** *Fordice*, June 26, 1992, specifying the established dismantlement duty in higher education.

**Exceptions and limits:** New policies without roots in the dual system remain subject to ordinary equal-protection analysis. No proportional-enrollment command, universal test-score ban, closure order, general Title VI disparate-impact cause or new heightened proof quantum follows. Freeman's separate school-decree rules and Rufo's consent-decree modification rule retain their distinct functions.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Fordice; Ayers v. Fordice, 505 U.S. 717 (1992) — University desegregation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-fordice-ayers-v-fordice-505-us-717-1992).
