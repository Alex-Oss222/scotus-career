# Public law reading copy

## Event

Attwood v. Singletary, 516 U.S. 297, No. 95-6710. Decided January 22, 1996. The Court considers financial leave for the present certiorari petition, a proposed prospective noncriminal filing condition, and disposition of the petition. Attwood challenges Florida counsel-signature restrictions while alleging inability to obtain counsel. The attached state order records extensive abusive litigation; his claims about the restrictions' effects on custody and mistreatment remedies remain allegations.

## Participation

No nonparticipation is noted.

## Public Action

The motion for leave to proceed in forma pauperis is granted. The petition for a writ of certiorari is denied. No new prospective filing condition is imposed. The Court announces no petition poll.

## Judgment & Remedy

The present petition is accepted without prepayment and denied. No payment interval is necessary, no future noncriminal fee-and-form condition issues, and no judgment on the underlying federal or state claims is entered. Denial of certiorari does not affirm the state court's reasoning or approve its counsel-signature requirement.

## Opinion Topology

Ginsburg writes the Court's explanation concerning financial leave and prospective control, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs in the result with a separate explanation. The certiorari denial supplies no merits opinion or disclosed poll.

## Holdings

### This particular application supports financial leave

**Operative rule:** Supported inability to prepay and an arguable present petition warrant financial leave on this record; prior volume and state abuse do not themselves establish the particular frivolousness or malice needed for the proposed Rule 39.8 denial. Granting leave does not establish the truth of the allegations, entitlement to certiorari, or future financial eligibility.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. The same eight Justices adopt the following prospective-control holding. Stone-Zsela agrees with the result on his separately stated grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The signed poverty statement and accompanying financial answers support this application despite partly illegible account details. The Court adopts no precise asset balance. The petition raises a concrete concern that mandatory counsel signatures, coupled with an alleged inability to obtain counsel, impede access including claimed custody-related relief.

Martin distinguishes an arguable current submission from one actually established as frivolous. Anderson likewise separates financial leave for an access claim from obstacles to obtaining the requested writ. The state's findings of extensive abuse remain relevant, but do not decide this distinct challenge to the restriction's practical operation. Zatko continues to permit denial of a particular filing upon the required showing. The Court accepts no general right to unlimited fee-free litigation and decides no claim of constitutional invalidity here.

**Precedent treatment:** Martin and Anderson are applied to the particular submission; Zatko's power to deny an actually frivolous or malicious filing remains.

### The proposed future condition lacks its separate supporting showing

**Operative rule:** A prospective fee-and-form condition on a category of future filings requires support tied to the applicant, that category, and the inadequacy of treating individual submissions. Extensive state litigation alone does not establish that showing for the proposed condition on future noncriminal certiorari petitions in this Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The restriction would preserve paid and criminal routes. That limited scope matters, but does not prove its necessity or fit. The established state abuse does not itself demonstrate abuse of this Court's relevant category or why particular-filing treatment is inadequate. Martin, Day, Sassower, and Anderson require those distinctions rather than a prediction that every unwritten claim will be frivolous.

The Court declines the condition on this record. It establishes no universal warning prerequisite, numerical threshold, or obligation to exhaust every conceivable lesser measure. McDonald and Sindram retain their authority for properly supported targeted controls. Ordinary neutral filing rules and sanctions for proved misconduct remain available.

**Precedent treatment:** Martin, Day, Sassower, and Anderson supply the distinct prospective showing; McDonald and Sindram retain their bounded process-protection authority.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Zatko v. California:** Preserves supported denial of a particular frivolous or malicious filing; prior losses alone do not make this challenge frivolous.
- **Martin v. District of Columbia:** Applied to separate the current petition's arguability from the independent justification for prospective control.
- **In re Anderson:** Applied to distinguish supported financial leave and actual access concerns from entitlement to a writ or future control.
- **Day and Sassower:** Their individualized, category-specific prospective-control requirements remain and are applied here.
- **In re McDonald, 489 U.S. 180, and In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177:** Targeted protection of the judicial process remains available on an adequate record; neither dictates the proposed order without its supporting predicates.

## Law After Decision

The filing ruling applies the existing current-submission and prospective-control requirements. It creates no categorical immunity from supported controls and no right to discretionary Supreme Court review. The certiorari denial decides none of the access, habeas, counsel-availability, or mistreatment questions.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the result.** He favors granting leave upon the established financial showing and promptly disposing of the present petition without attaching a disability to future litigation. In his view, equal access to federal process cannot be traded away merely for docket convenience; volume warrants efficient scrutiny of each paper, not a presumption that every unwritten claim is frivolous. Rule 39.8 permits a fee denial when justified but does not require that intermediate step before ordinary certiorari disposition.

His constitutional position seeks stronger protection against predictive, identity-based exclusion than the Court's record-specific ground establishes. His preference for direct screening is an institutional judgment, not an empirical finding about administrative cost. He preserves ordinary neutral rules, jurisdiction, sanctions for proved misconduct, and valid finality doctrines. No other Justice joins this explanation, and it does not turn denial of the petition into a ruling on the underlying claims.

## Procedure After Action

This petition is concluded. No payment deadline, additional Court warning, counsel appointment, release order, or new prospective restriction follows. Any different later filing remains subject to the ordinary rules and governing law.
