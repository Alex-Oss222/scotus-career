## Public Projection

## Event

**United States v. International Business Machines Corp., 517 U.S. 843, No. 95-591 — June 10, 1996.** Argued March 18, 1996, on certiorari to the Federal Circuit. The question is whether the Export Clause permits application of the general foreign-insurance premium tax in 26 U.S.C. §4371 to insurance of goods in export transit.

For 1975–1984, IBM paid assessments concerning foreign policies covering its exported products and sought refunds. The Federal Circuit, 59 F.3d 1234, affirmed the Court of Federal Claims' ruling that this application was unconstitutional. The policies attach to identified shipments; their premiums reflect both cargo value and shipment-specific risks. Section 4371 is not confined to exports.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. Justice Stevens takes no part in consideration or decision.

## Public Action

The Federal Circuit's judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Kennedy, Ginsburg.

The judgment requiring refund of the challenged unconstitutional assessments stands within the claim and amount properly preserved. The Court neither determines a new refund amount nor invalidates section 4371 in all its applications.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer. Justice Kennedy dissents, joined by Justice Ginsburg.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, with the six direct joins identified above, controls the rule, application and limits below.

### The general tax cannot reach this export-transit insurance

**Operative rule:** A federal tax on goods in export transit does not escape the Export Clause merely because it applies without discrimination to other transactions. Thames & Mersey remains controlling for premiums on insurance of goods during that transit, so section 4371 cannot be applied to the insurance at issue here; the rule does not exempt every activity or expense that indirectly affects exporting.

**Controlling explanation:** The Constitution expressly forbids federal taxes or duties on articles exported from a State. Fairbank protects export bills of lading, and Hvoslef protects charter arrangements for export carriage; those cases prevent taxation of the protected export transaction through its integral documents. Thames & Mersey directly applies that protection to insurance of goods during the export voyage.

The Government's contrary argument rests on nondiscrimination and changes in other tax doctrines. Those doctrines answer different constitutional questions. Michelin concerns the States' Import-Export restriction and goods no longer in transit. Washington Stevedoring concerns a service tax and reserves direct taxation of goods in transit. Neither converts the federal Export Clause into a prohibition only of discriminatory taxes. Itel preserves the direct-export question while sustaining a distinct local container transaction; Jefferson Lines concerns a local retail sale under the Commerce Clause. Fulton requires comparison of actual taxable events within that Clause, rather than transferring its standard to different constitutional text.

The insurance here covers the identified outward shipments. Risk as well as cargo value affects the premium, but that does not distinguish the export-insurance category already decided in Thames & Mersey. The Government's asserted interclause developments do not justify overruling that directly applicable precedent. Its decision to press nondiscrimination, while declining to advance a separate goods-versus-services challenge, also counsels against undertaking a comprehensive reconsideration of that boundary here. An included legal argument may be considered; the Court is not required to decide it.

The Court retains the distinction between taxing exports and imposing ordinary burdens before exportation or on genuinely distinct or tangential activities. It leaves fuller classification of other services open. Applying the existing insurance rule is sufficient to preserve this refund judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Thames & Mersey Marine Insurance Co. v. United States, 237 U.S. 19:** Retained and applied to export-transit insurance; its directly governing rule is not displaced by nondiscrimination alone.
- **Fairbank v. United States, 181 U.S. 283:** Applied as the export-document precedent supporting the Clause's protection of the export transaction.
- **United States v. Hvoslef, 237 U.S. 1:** Applied as the export-carriage precedent supporting the Clause's protection of the export transaction.
- **Michelin Tire Corp. v. Wages, 423 U.S. 276:** Distinguished by its state-tax setting and goods outside export transit; it does not replace the federal Clause's rule.
- **Department of Revenue v. Association of Washington Stevedoring Cos., 435 U.S. 734:** Distinguished as a service-tax decision reserving direct goods-in-transit taxation.
- **Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston:** Distinguished by the separate local container transaction and its reserved direct-export question.
- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc.:** Its transaction-specific Commerce Clause rule remains effective in its own setting and supplies no federal Export Clause transformation.
- **Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner:** Its transaction-specific Commerce Clause rule remains effective in its own setting and supplies no federal Export Clause transformation.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Its permission to consider an included argument remains discretionary. The Court announces no new review-scope restriction.

## Law After Decision

The Export Clause continues to protect the export-insurance application governed by Thames & Mersey even under a generally applicable, nondiscriminatory federal tax. No comprehensive immunity for export-related services or incidental exporter costs is adopted. No earlier decision is overruled.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would reach the narrower argument that the tax falls on a purchased indemnity service rather than the exported articles. Itel and Jefferson Lines illustrate the need to identify the actual taxable transaction. The stipulated premium reflects shipment risks as well as cargo value; that relationship does not make the service identical to the goods. The dissent would expressly overrule Thames & Mersey to the extent its commercial-integration rationale treats this genuine insurance-premium tax as a tax on exports.

The dissent regards the narrower issue as fairly included in the question presented and sufficiently supported by the stipulated record despite the Government's emphasis on nondiscrimination. It would reverse and remand without validating all export-associated taxes, creating a nondiscrimination safe harbor for an actual goods tax, or determining a new assessment amount. Its service classification does not command a majority.

## Procedure After Action

The refund judgment remains in force subject to ordinary mandate procedures. No new trial, tax computation or further Supreme Court proceeding is ordered.
