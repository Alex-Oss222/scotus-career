## Law After Decision

Section 7.4001(a) defines §85 interest to include payment compensating a creditor or prospective creditor for extending credit, making a credit line available, or a borrower's default or breach of a condition of credit. Its examples include numerical periodic rates, late fees, insufficient-funds fees, overlimit fees, annual fees, cash-advance fees and membership fees connected with credit extension or availability. It ordinarily excludes appraisal fees, insurance premiums and commissions guaranteeing repayment, finders' fees, document-preparation or notarization fees and credit-report fees.

Under (b), a national bank may charge the highest interest permitted to a home-state chartered or licensed lender, subject to home-state provisions material to the applicable loan class; the small-loan example retains loan-size restrictions without requiring the bank to obtain that state license. Under (c), the federal definition does not alter definitions or calculations used solely for state law. Home-state-permitted late fees may be charged interstate as federal interest even when home-state law excludes those fees from its own interest calculation. Under (d), a bank in a State denying corporate borrowers a usury defense may charge such a borrower the agreed interest rate.

These provisions take effect April 1, 1996. The instrument supplies no separate late-fee retroactivity or pending-case transition rule. Judicial treatment of the interpretation in a case involving earlier transactions and the validity of particular fees remain to be decided in the proper proceeding. Non-interest-charge rules are distinct from §7.4001.
