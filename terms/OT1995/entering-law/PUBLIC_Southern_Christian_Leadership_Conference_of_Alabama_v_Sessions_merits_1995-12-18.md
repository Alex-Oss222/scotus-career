# Public law reading copy

Authority: [Record](../records/Southern_Christian_Leadership_Conference_of_Alabama_v_Sessions_merits_1995-12-18.md)

## Event

Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions. Merits decision, December 18, 1995. On review of the en banc Eleventh Circuit judgment of June 14, 1995, 56 F.3d 1281, affirming the rejection of the Section 2 claim after a bench trial. The questions concern the application of Section 2 to the challenged at-large trial-judge elections, including a proper comparison, actual minority electoral opportunity, and the linkage between judges' electoral and territorial jurisdictions. The challenge covers ten identified judicial circuits and district-judge elections in four counties, not all Alabama judicial elections. No argument date or separate Supreme Court docket designation is established here.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. No Justice is recused or otherwise nonparticipating.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is vacated, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the opinion of the Court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The Court of Appeals must reconsider the independent no-dilution ground under the correct legal standards, with any properly necessary proceedings in the district court, before imposing relief. Supported subsidiary historical findings remain. If a violation is proved, the lower courts must consider lawful, practical correction and genuine judicial-administration interests without categorical racial-partiality assumptions. The Court imposes no present liability decree, map, number of judgeships, injunction, election delay, deadline or guaranteed election of Black candidates.

## Opinion Topology

Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor dissents, joined by Kennedy, on the trial-record failure-of-proof ground. Thomas dissents, joined by Scalia, on statutory noncoverage. The four dissenters seek the same affirmance but do not share a substantive rationale or join one another's writings.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt each holding and the remand. The two dissenting grounds supply no additional joins, and no Marks synthesis is needed.

### 1. The opportunity determination must use the statutory results inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2 reaches these at-large judicial elections and requires proof of unequal opportunity for minority voters to elect their actual preferred candidates under the applicable Gingles conditions and the totality of circumstances; it requires neither a Black preferred candidate nor proof that voters or legislators acted from racial motives. The challenged jurisdictions must be evaluated on their actual electoral opportunity, with contextual treatment of candidate preference, incumbency, appointments, partisanship, qualifications and electoral success; neither raw success elsewhere nor the absence of discriminatory enactment resolves the claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judgment's independent no-dilution ground requires reconsideration because its legal framing affects the opportunity inquiry itself. Chisom and Houston Lawyers establish that judicial elections fall within Section 2. Gingles supplies the required causal conditions: a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, with special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate considered. Those conditions do not alone establish liability. De Grandy requires the further totality inquiry and distinguishes meaningful opportunity from candidate race, numerical success and a duty to create every feasible district. The appellate discussion of voting because of candidate race and nondiscriminatory enactment cannot substitute a racial-motive prerequisite for the statutory inquiry. Nor can an aggregate account obscure the actual challenged electoral units. The State correctly invokes the trial court's consideration of both experts, varied elections, party affiliation and incumbency. Supported subsidiary findings remain, but deference to those findings does not settle whether the ultimate conclusion uses the correct legal criteria. The Court neither chooses the plaintiffs' expert nor categorically excludes white-only or statewide contests. Such evidence may inform local preference and opportunity in context. The remand corrects the governing premises; it does not find dilution or presume that the necessary conditions establish it.

### 2. Judicial structure warrants concrete consideration, not categorical remedial immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 comparison and remedy in judicial elections must be legally grounded, practicable and fitted to the governmental task, with genuine weight given to the linkage between the electorate and the jurisdiction served, the qualified-candidate pool and judicial administration. Those interests do not categorically defeat a proved violation or every possible correction, and racial awareness in territorial remedial design alone does not establish unconstitutional racial partiality; any independently cognizable constitutional injury remains subject to its own rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Houston Lawyers treats the State's jurisdiction-electorate linkage as legitimate and relevant, not an exemption from Section 2. Holder requires a reasonable comparison with independent legal and practical roots, rather than a size or structure selected solely to produce desired racial winners; it does not demand a uniquely ideal arrangement. Comparison, proof of dilution and relief remain separate. A possible subdistrict is not enough to establish liability, and the statute does not authorize automatic restructuring simply because another electoral arrangement can be imagined. But the lower court's alternative remedy ground cannot stand insofar as it treats independent judges, jurisdictional linkage or race-aware boundaries as necessarily incompatible with relief. Shaw and Miller require an independently cognizable deprivation beyond territorial racial design alone. They do not presume that voters' preferred judges would dispense racially partial justice or serve only members of one race. Those decisions leave actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation and inferior civic status fully reviewable under their own standards. The court below must evaluate concrete administration and candidate-pool concerns and lawful alternatives if a violation is proved. The Court selects no plan and decides neither the constitutionality nor practical adequacy of any particular new district.

### 3. Reconsideration preserves findings and the plaintiffs' burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the challenged jurisdictions under the correct opportunity, comparison and remedy standards, preserving supported subsidiary historical findings and the plaintiffs' burden to prove each required condition and ultimate unequal opportunity. Relief may be ordered only for an established violation and must be lawful and practicable; if the preserved facts do not establish dilution under the corrected inquiry, judgment may again be entered for the State.

**Controlling explanation:** The judgment rested on two independently stated grounds, so a flaw confined to remedies would not justify vacating the separate no-dilution determination. The Court identifies legal error affecting that determination as well as the categorical remedial premises. Reconsideration must therefore begin with the independent opportunity ground, not with an assumed liability finding. Gingles and De Grandy preserve the plaintiffs' causal and totality burdens, while Holder keeps a grounded comparison distinct from both liability and relief. The remand returns through the Eleventh Circuit for reconsideration and any properly necessary district-court proceedings. It requires no new statistical formula, additional expert studies, automatic liability trial, particular judicial structure or guaranteed electoral result.

### Limits and questions not reached

The decision concerns circuit-judge elections in the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Tenth, Thirteenth, Fifteenth, Twentieth, Twenty-Third and Twenty-Sixth Circuits and district-judge elections in Jefferson, Mobile, Montgomery and Russell Counties. A statewide plaintiff class does not expand the challenge to all forty Alabama circuits or every judicial office. The constitutional-purpose claims are not revived. No proportional-results entitlement, maximization duty, universal evidentiary exclusion, additional constitutional-injury element for Section 2, particular population denominator, new statistical method or burden shift is adopted. Cumulative voting was mentioned in the lower dissent, not proposed as a preserved remedy; no entitlement to its adoption is decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Chisom v. Roemer, 501 U.S. 380:** Applied to preserve Section 2 coverage of judicial elections; the two-Justice contrary view does not alter that rule.
- **Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas, 501 U.S. 419:** Applied to independent trial judges and the legitimate but nonconclusive linkage interest; coverage, proof and remedy remain distinct.
- **Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30:** Its causal conditions, special-circumstance qualification and totality inquiry remain required; no racial-motive condition, automatic-liability rule or universal statistical method is added.
- **Holder v. Hall:** Applied in its controlling form to a legally and practically grounded comparison and a remedy fitted to a proved violation; its rules do not require a uniquely ideal structure or grant categorical immunity to existing arrangements.
- **Johnson v. De Grandy:** Its local-opportunity, candidate-preference and totality rules are applied, including the rejection of maximization, proportional-results entitlement and conclusive numerical safe harbors; meaningful success remains evidence.
- **Shaw v. Reno:** Its rejection of a territorial-design-only constitutional claim is applied; actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation and legally inferior civic status remain independently cognizable.
- **Miller and consolidated cases:** Applied to reject racial predominance alone as a constitutional defect or proof that an officeholder represents only one race; the decision adds no constitutional-injury gate to Section 2 and decides no particular remedial map here.

## Law After Decision

Section 2's existing judicial-election coverage and results inquiry remain governing. This decision requires reconsideration of these challenged elections under actual candidate preference and jurisdiction-matched opportunity, and it rejects the categorical remedial premises used below. It does not establish dilution in any jurisdiction, prescribe a new proof method or adopt the lower dissent's liability findings. Legitimate linkage, candidate-pool and administrative interests retain concrete weight; territorial racial design alone supplies neither constitutional liability nor an additional statutory element. The separate noncoverage view remains noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Kennedy.** They would affirm on the independently sufficient failure-of-proof ground under the governing Section 2 results inquiry. Houston Lawyers permits judicial-election coverage while retaining legitimate institutional considerations, and De Grandy requires proof of ultimate unequal opportunity rather than automatic liability from polarization or a feasible additional district. The trial court evaluated both experts, several kinds of elections, actual candidate preferences, party affiliation and incumbency. In their view the Court of Appeals upheld permissible contextual factfinding rather than applying a racial-intent requirement or numerical safe harbor. They agree that legal error would defeat factual deference, but find no error warranting reconsideration of this independent ground. They do not defend presumed racial partiality, categorical structural immunity, or the proposition that all proposed remedies are unconstitutional. Kennedy's earlier broader objection to judicial vote-dilution coverage is not the ground he selects here. Neither joins Thomas's noncoverage rationale.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** They would affirm on their separate statutory view that Section 2 does not reach this judicial vote-dilution claim. Their preserved Holder and De Grandy positions reject dilution coverage; Scalia's Chisom and Houston Lawyers dissent supplies earlier judicial-election-specific support. They acknowledge that the Court's contrary coverage holdings govern unless changed, but continue to urge their construction rather than join the majority's comparison and totality framework. They do not join O'Connor's trial-record application merely because they seek the same decree. Their opinion supplies no additional votes for an evidentiary proposition, categorical remedial rule or present change in the Court's coverage law.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Eleventh Circuit for reconsideration and any properly necessary district-court proceedings. The first task is to determine whether the independent opportunity conclusion survives the corrected legal standards. A lawful, practicable remedy is considered only if a violation is established. Supported historical facts are preserved, and the State may prevail again if the plaintiffs' required proof is insufficient. No immediate electoral change or special timetable is ordered.
