COMPLAINT 


Now come the plaintiffs, O’ Hare Truck Service, Inc. 
(hereinafter “O’ Hare”) and John A. Gratzianna and complain 
of the defendants, City of Northlake (hereinafter “Northlake”), 
Mayor Reid Paxson (hereinafter “Paxson”), Police Chief 
Seymour Sapoznik (hereinafter “Sapoznik”) as follows: 

JURISDICTION AND VENUE 

I. This action is brought pursuant to 42 U.S.C. 

Section 1983 in that plaintiff has been deprived of his nights 


14 


secured by the First and Fourteenth Amendments to the United 
States Constitvsiun, to wit, freedom of speech and due pro- 
cess of law. The jurisdiction of this Court is invoked pursu- 
ant to 28 U.S.C. Sections 1331, 1334 and 2201. Venue lies in 
the district court for the Northern District of Illinois pursuant 
to 28 U.S.C. Section 1391, because all relevant acts occurred 
within this district. 
PARTIES 

2. The plaintiff, O’ Hare Truck Service, Inc. is an 
Illinois corporation with its principal place of business in the 
City of Northlake, Cook County, Illinois. O’ Hare Truck Ser- 
vice, Inc., in addition to other services, provides towing ser- 
vices at the request of municipalities throughout Cook and 
DuPage counties. O’ Hare Truck Service, Inc. is owned and 
operated by plaintiff John A. Gratzianna. 

3. The City of Northlake is a duly constituted 
municipal corporation and body politic, located in Cook 
County, Illinois. 

4. Paxson is, at all relevant times herein, the 
Mayor of the City of Northlake and its principal policy maker. 
He is sued in his official and individual capacity. 


15 


5. Sapoznik is, at all relevant times herein, the 
chief of Police of the City of Northlake. He gained and main- 
tains his position as Chief of Police by appointment of defen- 
dant Paxson. He is sued in his official and individual capac- 
ity. 

COUNT 1 - FREEDOM OF SPEECH 

6. The plaintiffs provide towing services to mu- 
nicipalities. When a need arises for the towing of a vehicle, 
as a result of an accident, mechanical breakdown, abandon- 
ment or other reason, plaintiff is contacted by the municipai- 
ity in which the vehicle is located and asked to provide tow- 
ing services. The plaintiffs earn a substantial part of their 
incomes from fees paid by municipalities and private indi- 
viduals as a result of requests for towing services by various 
municipalities. 

7. In order to procure towing services under the 
circumstances described above, the Northlake Police Depart- 
ment has maintained and continues to maintain a towing ro- 
tation list (hereinafter “rotation list”). When the need for tow- 
ing services arises in Northlake the police department con- 


tacts the towing service which is next in line on the rotation 


16 


list and requests towing services. 

8. It has been the policy and practice of Northlake 
and the Northlake Police Department since 1965 and before 
to request towing services from the rotation list. 

9. The plaintiff O’ Hare has been on the Northlake 
rotation list since 1965 and has provided towing services at 
the request of Northlake since 1965 as a result of its place- 
ment on the rotation list. 

10. ‘In 1993 defendant Paxson ran for reelection 
to the position of Mayor of Northlake. 

11. Although plaintiff John A. Gratzianna was 
solicited for campaign funds by Paxson’s campaign commit- 
tee, he refused to contribute to Paxson’s reelection campaign. 

12. __ Instead, John A. Gratzianna openly supported 
Paxson's opponent for the office of Mayor. Campaign post- 
ers for Paxson’s opponent were displayed at plaintiff O’ Hare's 
place of business. 

13. In retaliation for the activities described in 
paragraphs |! and 12, defendant Sapoznik, upon information 
and belief, at the direction of defendant Paxson, removed 
plainuff O'Hare from the Northlake rotation list on or about 


17 


July 8, 1993. 

14. Asadirect and proximate result of defendants’ 
removal of plaintiff O’ Hare from the towing rotation list the 
plaintiffs have lost substantial income. 

15. At all relevant times herein it has been the 
policy and practice of Northlake, as set by defendant Paxson, 
to use the powers and authority of Northlake to retaliate against 
political opponents of defendant Paxson. 

16. The actions of defendants Paxson and 
Sapoznik as described in paragraph 13 occurred in conform- 
ance with and as a result of the policy and practice of Northlake 
as described in paragraph 15. 

17.  Asadirect and proximate result of the defen- 
dants’ removal of plaintiff O’ Hare from the towing rotation 
list the plaintiffs have suffered a chilling effect upon the ex- 
ercise of their constitutional right of freedom of speech and 
have suffered harm and injury. 

18. The actions of the defendants as described 
herein were taken intentionally and with callous indifference 
to plaintiffs’ federal constitutional rights.

grant the following relief: 

A. A preliminary and permanent injunction re-

B. A declaratory judgment stating that defendants 
have violated plaintiffs’ federally protected rights; 

ce. Judgment for compensatory damages in favor 
of the plaintiffs and against the defendants in an amount to be

D. Judgment for punitive damages in favor of the 
plaintiffs and against defendants Paxson and Sapoznik in an 
amount to be set by the jury in this matter; 

E. An award for reasonable costs and attorney's 
fees as authorized by 42 U.S.C. Section 1988; 

F. For such other relief as this court deems just.

21. _—=‘The plaintiffs were not provided notice of 
O’Hare’s removal from the tow rotation list; the plaintiffs

12(b)(6) MOTION TO DISMISS

through their attorneys, FRATERRIGO, BEST & BERANEK, 
and respectfully request dismissal with prejudice of plain- 
tiffs’ Complaint pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 
12(b)(6). In support thereof, defendants state as follows: 

l. Plaintiffs allege a First Amendment claim in 
count I and a due process claim in count II arising from ter- 


mination of plaintiffs’ towing services by the CITY OF 


23 


NORTHLAKE. Plaintiffs allege termination of towing ser-

2. Under the Doctrine of Qualified Immunity, 
“public officials performing discretionary functions are pro- 
tected against suits from damages unless their conduct vio- 
lates clearly established statutory or constitutional rights of 
which a reasonable person would have known.” Doe vy. 
Bobbitt, 881 F.2d 510, 511 (7th Cir. 1989) (citing Harlow v. 
Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, 818 (1982)). 

3. To determine whether the right allegedly vio- 
lated was “clearly established” the right must be identified in 
a particularized sense with respect to the circumstances of 
the alleged violation. Warlick v. Cross, 969 F.2d 303, 309 
(7th Cir. 1992). 

4 The contours of the right must be sufficiently 
clear that a reasonable official would understand that what he 
is doing violates that right. Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 
635 (1987). 

5. Plaintiff has the burden of showing that the 
constitutional right was clearly established. Rice v. Burks, 
999 F.2d 1172, 1173 (7th Cir. 1993) (citing Rakovich v. Wade, 
850 F.2d 1180 (7th Cir.), cert denied 488 U.S. 968 (1988)). 

6. At the times alleged in plaintiffs’ Complaint

the state of First Amendment and Fourteenth Amendment law
