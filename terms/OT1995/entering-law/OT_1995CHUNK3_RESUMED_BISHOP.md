## Standards and Tests selection: Required commercial connection and distinct economic-class regulation

### Required commercial connection and distinct economic-class regulation

**Governing question:** The commerce connection required for the original school-zone possession prohibition and the distinct armed-vehicle-taking class.

**Current rule:** A stand-alone local school-zone possession prohibition without an enacted commerce connection cannot be sustained by a generalized chain through crime costs, education and productivity to the national economy. Courts cannot save the unambiguous original §922(q)(1)(A) offense by adding an unexpressed commerce element. This bounded rule does not categorically exclude noneconomic intrastate conduct or require formal legislative findings in every case. Regulation of an economic vehicle-taking class within an interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme supplies a distinct sufficient commerce ground for original §2119, whose enacted particular-vehicle interstate-history requirement must also be proved.

**Authority by component:**

- The original possession offense lacks the required commerce connection: [The original possession offense lacks the required commerce connection](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Lopez_merits_1995-04-26.md#public-projection), 1995-04-26. Justice Kennedy’s opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas: five direct votes for this bounded ground and affirmance. The Chief Justice’s additional six-route framework has no additional joins and is not the Court’s exhaustive test. No Marks synthesis is needed.
- The possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element: [The possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Lopez_merits_1995-04-26.md#public-projection), 1995-04-26. Kennedy’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas: five direct votes for the no-insertion disposition and the precise reservation. No Justice’s conditional willingness to consider the indictment issue is counted as a separate holding deciding it.
- The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection: [The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md#public-projection), 1995-04-27. Justice O’Connor's opinion of the Court, joined on this ground by Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the judgment on a distinct instrumentality ground and does not join the economic-class rationale.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-04-27; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Remote movement alone and generalized social costs independently establish neither holding. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope; appropriate inclusion of intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regime remains possible. Garcia’s generally applicable regulation of state activity is undisturbed. No exhaustive six-route test, automatic vehicle-instrumentality rule, Second Amendment holding, amended-statute ruling or independent indictment ruling follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment

### Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment

**Governing question:** Fault and punishment under the offense-date versions of §§2119 and 924(c).

**Current rule:** Original §2119 requires knowing armed participation in taking or attempting to take the covered vehicle from another’s person or presence by force and violence or intimidation. Aiding and abetting requires intentional assistance, not presence, association or awareness alone. The Government must prove the enacted vehicle-history nexus; no profit motive, interstate-resale purpose, knowledge of federal law or later intent-to-kill-or-seriously-injure element is added. Offense-date §924(c) expressly authorizes an additional consecutive term even though firearm possession is part of the original carjacking definition, but use or carrying during and in relation to the qualifying predicate must independently be established.

**Authority by component:**

O’Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer, directly adopts the complete construction and its bounded application. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the knowing-participation and fair-notice propositions and rejection of a general legal-knowledge requirement; no additional Stone join is attributed to the separately stated intentional-assistance formulation. His separate conclusions concerning knowledge of the vehicle's interstate history and permanent deprivation have no additional joins and are not holdings of the Court.
- Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking: [Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md#public-projection), 1995-04-27. O’Connor's opinion, joined on this statutory holding by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct votes. The holding sustains the reviewed forty-one-month carjacking terms and additional sixty-month firearm terms, not an unreviewed weapon enhancement.
- Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution: [Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md#public-projection), 1995-04-27. ## Standards and Tests selection: Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

### Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

**Governing question:** Which circumstances determine custody for Miranda warnings.

**Current rule:** Custody turns on the objective circumstances confronting the person questioned and whether a reasonable person in that position would understand freedom of movement to be restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest. An officer’s uncommunicated view of the person as suspect or witness does not enter the calculation. Beliefs conveyed by words or conduct may affect the objective setting to the extent they would affect that reasonable understanding. Communicating suspicion does not itself establish custody, and an officer’s private intent cannot substitute for the objective inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective arrest-equivalent restraint and communicated beliefs: [Stansbury v. California — Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Stansbury_v_California_merits_1994-04-26.md#public-projection), 1994-04-26. The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Present operation effective:** Stansbury v. California, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** [Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#custody-turns-on-the-circumstances-communicated-to-the-person-questioned).

## Standards and Tests selection: Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel

### Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel

**Governing question:** What police must do after a valid waiver when a suspect may be presently asking for counsel.

**Current rule:** After a valid waiver, a statement that a reasonable officer would understand in context as a possible present request for counsel requires a pause in substantive questioning and neutral clarification. Unexpressed wishes and unrelated references to a lawyer do not trigger that duty. While the request remains unresolved, substantive interrogation may not resume; clarification may not discourage counsel through pressure, promises or repeated persuasion. A clear request requires cessation under Edwards, and later answers cannot retroactively erase it. Further interrogation then requires counsel or the suspect’s own initiation followed by a valid waiver.

**Authority by component:**

- Possible-request trigger and lawful neutral clarification: [Davis v. United States — I. A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification; II. The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Davis_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-24.md#public-projection), 1994-06-24. Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part. Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.
- Clear invocation and prohibition on retrospective erosion: [Davis v. United States — III. A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Davis_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-24.md#public-projection), 1994-06-24. Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Present operation effective:** Davis v. United States, 1994-06-24.

**Open question:** Prewaiver statements, an independent military Fifth Amendment claim and §3501 are outside this holding.

**Holdings navigation:** [A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#a-reasonable-possible-present-request-requires-neutral-clarification); [The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-actual-clarification-and-ensuing-statements-were-lawful); [A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#a-clear-request-requires-cessation-and-cannot-be-undone-by-later-answers).

### Commerce power and armed vehicle taking

#### [United States v. Harris](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Criminal Procedure — Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment (Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content; Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking; Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution).

##### The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection

**Holding:** Congress may apply original §2119 to the armed forcible acquisition of the vehicle proved here as regulation of the economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme. The statute's separately enacted requirement that the particular vehicle have been transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce must be proved; neither remote movement alone nor generalized crime, insurance, or productivity costs independently supplies this holding's constitutional ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's commerce portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a distinct ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: applies its retained economic-class boundary and distinguishes its inadequate stand-alone possession/downstream-effects chain; no missing element is supplied.
- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111 (1942): applies aggregation within a regulated economic class rather than demanding a substantial effect from each individual taking.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146 (1971): applies Congress's authority over the economic class despite an individual instance's local character.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: preserves its distinction between actual commercial operation and remote object history; prior manufacture alone is not the constitutional ground here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and each defendant's forty-one-month Count One term plus consecutive sixty-month Count Two term are affirmed on all three presented challenges. No merger, vacatur, concurrent conversion, sentence increase, resentencing, new trial or exploratory remand is ordered.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection — Commerce power and school-zone firearm possession

Authority: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`.

### Commerce power and school-zone firearm possession

#### [United States v. Lopez](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Lopez_merits_1995-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The original possession offense lacks the required commerce connection

**Holding:** The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition cannot sustain this prosecution on the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy. Its stand-alone regulated class and enacted elements supply no adequate commercial connection, and that generalized downstream-effects theory cannot supply one; the holding does not categorically exclude all noneconomic intrastate conduct from federal regulation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of all noneconomic intrastate conduct or universal formal-findings requirement is adopted. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense, Elections Clause standard or Reconstruction Amendment standard.

##### The possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Because the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the independent indictment defect that would arise if a permissible construction made a nexus an element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

### Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment

#### [United States v. Harris](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Constitutional Structure — Commerce power and armed vehicle taking (The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection).

##### Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content

**Holding:** Original §2119 requires knowing armed participation in taking or attempting to take the covered vehicle from another's person or presence by force and violence or intimidation; aiding-and-abetting liability requires intentional assistance in that taking, not mere presence, association, or awareness of another's plan. Read with that ordinary criminal fault, the statute gives fair notice of the charged conduct and defeats the presented lack-of-fault and vagueness challenges without adding a profit motive, interstate-resale intention, knowledge of federal law, or the later intent-to-kill-or-seriously-injure element.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor and all seven other Associate Justices adopt the complete knowing-participation and intentional-assistance rule. Stone-Zsela additionally joins knowing participation, fair notice and rejection of a general legal-knowledge requirement, but not the separately stated intentional-assistance formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994: applies contextual factual culpability despite silence while retaining its distinct regulated-characteristic holding.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: applies contextual criminal-fault construction without transplanting its depiction elements or deciding every jurisdictional-knowledge question.
- United States v. Shabani, November 1, 1994: preserves the distinction between knowing participation and mere association; its conspiracy holding does not itself define this separate offense.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its specific willfulness and known-illegality language rather than converting it into a universal ignorance-of-law defense.
- Lopez: preserves its refusal to insert an absent commerce element; that limit does not eliminate ordinary criminal-fault construction of the enacted taking offense.

##### Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking

**Holding:** The offense-date §924(c) authorizes its additional, consecutive punishment for the original §2119 carjacking predicate even though firearm possession is integral to that predicate's definition. The Government must still establish firearm use or carrying during and in relation to a qualifying federally prosecutable predicate; this cumulative-authorization holding does not equate possession with every §924(c) condition or determine universal element overlap.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on cumulative statutory authorization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ratzlaf: preserves residual lenity after ordinary construction; the inclusive, additional, and consecutive directions leave no residual ambiguity for this pairing.
- Smith v. United States, June 1, 1993: preserves the separate firearm-use and relation requirements; a predicate's possession element does not automatically establish all §924(c) elements.
- Deal v. United States, May 17, 1993: distinguishes its conviction-sequence question, which does not itself decide cumulative punishment under these two statutes.

##### Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution

**Holding:** Applying Missouri v. Hunter, the clearly authorized §2119 and §924(c) convictions and consecutive punishments imposed in this single prosecution do not violate the Double Jeopardy Clause even assuming overlap under Blockburger. This holding applies the governing same-trial precedent to the clear statutory direction and neither decides universal element overlap nor changes the rules for successive prosecutions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on the bounded application of Hunter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Missouri v. Hunter, 459 U.S. 359 (1983): applies its governing same-trial rule to the clear cumulative authorization without reconsidering its constitutional foundation.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299 (1932): applies its construction role rather than treating possible overlap as an absolute bar to this expressly authorized same-trial punishment.
- Whalen v. United States, 445 U.S. 684 (1980): preserves the presumption against cumulative punishment absent contrary legislative intent and distinguishes the clear direction here.
- United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993: preserves its successive-prosecution holdings, including the continued operation of Grady.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 (1990): remains governing law for its essential-conduct successive-prosecution setting; this single prosecution does not invoke or displace that rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no independent instruction, indictment, preservation, harmlessness, sufficiency, or intradistrict-venue question. It determines neither knowledge of the vehicle's interstate history nor an independent permanent-deprivation requirement by a majority. No later carjacking intent amendment, later firearm tier, new short-barrel classification, serious-injury or death enhancement, or different charging pair is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and each defendant's forty-one-month Count One term plus consecutive sixty-month Count Two term are affirmed on all three presented challenges. No merger, vacatur, concurrent conversion, sentence increase, resentencing, new trial or exploratory remand is ordered.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection — Custodial interrogation

Authority: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`.

### Custodial interrogation

#### [Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Stansbury_v_California_merits_1994-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5770.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned

**Holding:** Miranda custody exists upon formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from all objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee’s position would understand them; an officer’s uncommunicated view that the person is or is not a suspect, and the interviewee’s undisclosed subjective belief, have no independent role. An officer’s words or conduct conveying suspicion matter only to the extent they affect that objective understanding: communicated suspicion is not automatically custody, and undisclosed beliefs bearing on a witness’s credibility do not thereby become independent custody factors.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): applied to the custodial-interrogation trigger; no new exception to warnings is created.
- Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), and California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983): applied for the arrest-equivalent restraint standard; station questioning and suspect status alone remain insufficient.
- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984): applied to exclude uncommunicated intentions from the objective custody inquiry.
- Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976): applied to distinguish investigative focus from custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual custody, independent coercion, admissibility, harmlessness and capital punishment are not decided. An undisclosed belief bearing on witness credibility does not become an independent custody factor. Withrow and Powell's different review and remedy rules remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded to reconsider custody without the private investigative-focus criterion. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release or resentencing is ordered; ultimate custody and any properly reached consequences remain for the state court.

#### [Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Davis_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1949.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Holding:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. ### Davis v. United States, No. 92-1949

Merits decision, June 24, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

512 U.S. 452. Argued March 29, 1994; decided June 24, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Military Appeals, 36 M.J. 337, affirming the military conviction. The question is whether, following a valid waiver, an ambiguous counsel reference requires cessation or clarification and whether the actual questioning complied. Davis seeks reversal for admission of statements; the United States seeks affirmance.

Davis was convicted by general court-martial of unpremeditated murder under Article 118. His sentence included life confinement, dishonorable discharge, forfeiture of all pay and allowances, and reduction to E-1. The Navy-Marine Corps Court of Military Review affirmed September 16, 1991; the Court of Military Appeals affirmed March 11, 1993. The separate October 20 interview and physical-evidence issue is not reopened.

On November 4, 1988, naval investigators gave oral and written Article 31 and counsel advice and obtained a valid written waiver. About ninety minutes into questioning, Davis said, “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer.” The credited account shows an actual interruption of offense questioning, neutral clarification and a voluntary disclaimer that he was asking for counsel. After questioning resumed and a later break, agents briefly reminded him of his rights; they did not repeat all warnings or obtain a new written waiver. Davis later clearly said that he wanted a lawyer before saying anything else, and the interview stopped. The military judge credited the agents over Davis's differing account; the Court makes no new credibility finding.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participated at argument and decision. No Justice is recused.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Court of Military Appeals, 9–0. A five-Justice majority requires neutral clarification of a reasonable possible present request for counsel after waiver; the actual clarification and final cessation satisfied that rule.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Controlling proposition:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a valid waiver does not permit officers to exploit expressed uncertainty about whether the suspect now wants counsel. Miranda protects an effective choice during custodial questioning, and Edwards makes a request for counsel consequential. Smith distinguishes genuine initial ambiguity from an already clear request that later answers cannot erase. Barrett requires attention to the choice actually communicated; McNeil requires an expression concerning assistance during custodial questioning, rather than an unrelated counsel interest. Those decisions leave this postwaiver ambiguity question open. The contrary clear-request-only rule offers simplicity, but both approaches require officers to assess words in context. A short neutral clarification protects the choice without treating every reference as an irrevocable invocation. It also respects the initial waiver by permitting resumption when the suspect voluntarily clarifies a wish to proceed. The Court adopts this additional safeguard as the rule resolving the presented ambiguity question, not as a conclusion that the investigators' compliance alone logically compels it. Prewaiver conduct and unrelated lawyer references remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### II. The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Controlling proposition:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the military judge's credited account rather than resolving the competing testimony anew. Davis received oral and written advice and executed a valid initial waiver. His first statement, “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer,” expressed genuine uncertainty in its context. The investigators stopped offense questioning and asked whether he was requesting counsel, explaining that questioning would stop if he was. He voluntarily disclaimed a request. Barrett supports respecting the choice actually expressed, and Smith does not prohibit genuine clarification where no initially clear request has occurred. The later break brought a brief reminder of rights, not repetition of all warnings or a new written waiver. The Court does not depend on an invented second waiver. The argument for complete termination at the first remark would disregard the established distinction between uncertainty and a clear invocation. Because the actual exchange was limited and neutral, it satisfied the Court's clarification rule and also fell within the permissible questioning accepted by the concurring Justices.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### III. A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Controlling proposition:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court preserves the distinction between deciding whether a request was made and deciding whether questioning may continue after one. Edwards supplies the cessation rule and its narrow routes for further communication; an earlier waiver does not authorize police to disregard a later invocation. Smith prevents officers from manufacturing uncertainty by relying on answers obtained after an initially clear request. Neither principle requires a suspect to use technical language. The inquiry concerns what the words reasonably communicate in their actual setting. Davis's first remark was uncertain on the credited record, so neutral clarification was permissible. His later statement that he wanted a lawyer before saying anything else was a clear request, and the agents stopped. The Court therefore has no ensuing interrogation to exclude on that ground. This application does not decide a different case involving pressure after invocation, the validity of an unpresented later waiver, or Sixth Amendment attachment; it leaves those questions to their governing rules.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

#### Scope and questions not reached

The Court decides postwaiver invocation within the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules below. It does not independently resolve the Fifth Amendment's direct operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, adequacy of the initial warnings, an invalid initial waiver, or the separate October 20 interview/physical-evidence dispute. The Government did not invoke 18 U.S.C. §3501 below or rely on it as its merits ground. The exact rule has five direct joins, and the actual application and clear-request protections have nine; no Marks aggregation is used.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

The holding-specific treatments above govern. No precedent is overruled. Miranda's counsel protection is developed for genuine postwaiver ambiguity; Edwards and Smith retain their clear-request safeguards. Barrett and McNeil retain their distinct limits concerning communicated choice and the relevant counsel interest. Withrow remains the rule for Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims; Stansbury remains the objective custody rule. Neither resolves this distinct invocation issue by itself.

Effective June 24, 1994, a valid initial waiver does not permit continued substantive questioning through words reasonably understood as a possible present request for counsel. Officers must pause and clarify neutrally, maintaining the pause while that reasonable uncertainty remains. A clarified voluntary choice to proceed permits resumption, while a clear request invokes Edwards. Unrelated lawyer references and unexpressed wishes do not trigger the new duty. No prescribed legal phrasing is required.

The military framework is accepted as applied below and by the parties. Direct Fifth Amendment operation in the military, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct and the unpressed §3501 theory remain undecided. The four-Justice optional-clarification position is noncontrolling. Actual lawful clarification and honoring a clear final request independently support the unanimous affirmance.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, joins the actual-clarification application and clear-request protections but rejects a mandatory pause for an uncertain reference. Following a knowing and voluntary waiver, these Justices would require words clear enough that a reasonable officer would understand counsel to be requested. Their rule demands no legal terminology or prescribed formula. McNeil requires communication of the particular custodial-counsel interest, and Edwards's cessation protection follows invocation rather than a possible undisclosed wish. The initial waiver remains significant until the suspect objectively communicates a changed choice. Limited clarification is often sensible and is lawful here, but its practical value does not itself establish a legal duty. These Justices accept Smith's prohibition on using later answers to undo a clear request and do not approve coercive persuasion disguised as clarification. They accept the credited first ambiguity, the actual neutral exchange and voluntary disclaimer, and cessation upon the final clear request. They do not rely on a new waiver, decide prewaiver conduct, or adopt the unpressed §3501 theory. Their no-duty rule has four votes; Part I's mandatory rule controls.
