## Holdings

### I. Each individual financial submission supports leave

**Holding:** L. Shieh's separately signed declarations establish financial eligibility for each of the three motions on this record. The grants rest on each application's actual showing and create neither an irrebuttable-affidavit rule nor derivative relief for the corporation appearing in two captions.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 39.1 requires the prescribed motion identifying other-court financial applications and their dispositions, with a Form 4 notarized affidavit or §1746 declaration. Its exception for counsel appointed by a United States district or appellate court under the Criminal Justice Act or another applicable federal statute requires citation of that statute; no exception is invoked here.

Each dated declaration reports no current employment, no income during its own preceding twelve months, no cash/accounts or listed valuable property, and former April 1989 employment at approximately $7,500 gross monthly. Former earnings are not present resources. The blank dependents line claims no dependent expense but does not negate those answers; the separate California fee grant does not control this Court.

The August 1995 English document appended to each petition describes annual gross income often reaching several million dollars. That recent statement supplies a substantial reason to consider clarification for each motion. It identifies no dated receipts during the reporting periods or presently available funds or assets, however, and its alleged interruption of client representation prevents treating it as an unambiguous disclosure of accessible wealth. The later specific sworn answers adequately address the relevant questions here. The Court neither authenticates that document nor finds its account false.

Attwood supports an adequately established individual showing; Jones's reservation arose from unestablished financial particulars, unlike the declarations before the Court. Particular further inquiry remains permissible when needed. Under Rowland, an entity may seek relief through its own authorized actual-means showing, with lawful representation still required; Shieh's declarations decide no entity application. These financial grants alone establish no entitlement to certiorari or relief.

### II-A. Kakita's particular notice and reopening objection is not disposed of by its other defects

**Holding:** The present showing does not establish a basis for the proposed Rule 39.8 denial in No. 95-7587. This refusal depends on the particular petition's identified nonservice/reopening contention, not on immunity for its broader allegations.

**Authority:** Part II-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 39.8 permits, but does not require, denial when the Court is satisfied that the particular covered filing is frivolous or malicious. Zatko preserves supported denials; Martin distinguishes arguable current content from combined sufficient defects. Denton's factual screen does not permit ordinary disbelief to resolve genuine disputes and does not itself supply the complete Rule 39.8 standard.

Kakita's no-review and complete-ban accounts conflict with the October 27 order, which recites review of the opening brief and record and bars additional submissions in the identified closed dockets. The appeal concerns denials of reopening, not unrestricted direct review. The foreign-criminal and misconduct assertions establish no underlying facts.

This petition nevertheless identifies an allegedly unserved summary-judgment motion and district order and connects that nonservice to reopening. The review recital and closed-docket direction do not answer that specific contention. Missing underlying papers establish neither service failure or preserved error nor a conclusive independent defeat of the objection. Martin, Anderson, Attwood and Jones require that particular assessment. Merely using notice language would not protect an otherwise demonstrated frivolous filing; the identified motion, order and asserted reopening ground matter here. No independent malice finding supplies a denial ground. The Court finds neither unlawful procedure nor entitlement to review.

### II-B. The separate challenge to the prospective appellate order presents its own notice question

**Holding:** The Court declines the proposed Rule 39.8 denial in No. 95-7588 on the present showing. The petition's concrete show-cause and notice contention is not resolved by the visible order's retained filing routes or recital of an opportunity to respond.

**Authority:** Part II-B, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** This petition alleges nonreceipt in Taiwan of the October 27 show-cause order, challenges the fourteen-day response period and documents a January 17 request for the missing paper before the January 22 petition. The fax evidence establishes the request, not failed mailing or the truth of the alleged telephone account.

The December 13 order recites show cause and failure to respond. Its visible paragraph (1) excludes counsel-represented and district-certified nonfrivolous appeals from the pro se notice-of-appeal restriction. Paragraph (5) permits a reasoned lifting request no earlier than January 1, 1998, and continues the order until further order. These qualifications contradict an absolute-ban account; they do not establish the adequacy of notice. The missing middle page and absent original show-cause/service materials also prevent a complete account of the permission procedure.

Anderson, Attwood and Jones require assessing the particular procedural issue apart from the broad allegations. The established adverse features do not dispose of it sufficiently to support the proposed denial. This ruling adopts no actual-receipt requirement, general fourteen-day rule or finding that the restriction is unlawful. Rule 39.8 remains available upon its particular showing; the Court neither validates the petition's other theories nor grants certiorari.

### II-C. Krieger's own nonservice claim must be assessed within its reopening posture

**Holding:** The Court declines the proposed Rule 39.8 denial in No. 95-7589 on the present showing. Krieger's identified nonservice and reopening argument requires its own assessment, independently of Kakita and the different show-cause claim in No. 95-7588.

**Authority:** Part II-C, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual appellate ruling concerns denial of reopening, so the petition's de novo summary-judgment theories do not enlarge the review available. Its foreign-proceeding, treaty, discovery, preclusion and amendment assertions establish no entitlement. The no-new-arguments ruling and exaggerated allegations are substantial grounds to scrutinize this petition.

But its own body alleges nonservice of the summary-judgment motion and district order and invokes Rule 60(b)(6) and Molloy as grounds for reopening. The October 27 order cites Molloy for abuse-of-discretion review and Backlund for refusing vacatur when no new arguments are raised. Those references do not themselves establish a conclusive answer to the identified notice ground. The March 3 order records denials of reconsideration, discovery and amendment, not the missing papers' contents or service facts.

As Martin, Anderson, Attwood and Jones require, the Court separates this concrete contention from defects elsewhere. Citation of Rule 60 alone would not establish arguability; its connection to the identified allegedly unserved papers supplies the distinction here. The Court accepts neither the nonservice allegation nor Shieh's proposed legal construction, and announces no treaty, discovery, amendment or foreign-proceeding entitlement. No established malice independently supports denial. Financial access remains distinct from certiorari and underlying relief.

### III. The future condition lacks its separate supporting showing

**Holding:** A prospective condition on future noncriminal certiorari petitions requires support tied to the applicant, the relevant category and the inadequacy of treating particular submissions. The proposed new fee-and-form condition lacks that showing on this record and is declined.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine Justices. The Court does not adopt the concurrence's additional strong presumption.

**Controlling explanation:** Martin, Day, Sassower, Anderson, Attwood and Jones require a distinct prospective inquiry. Lower restrictions and repeated litigation warrant scrutiny, but do not themselves establish abuse of this Court's relevant category or why individual treatment is inadequate. The present record establishes no sequence of this Court's earlier Shieh denials, warnings or failed limited responses that supplies the missing connection. That is a limit of the record, not a universal warning prerequisite.

Retaining paid and criminal routes narrows the proposal but does not justify it. Screening consumes real resources, and a properly supported targeted condition remains lawful under McDonald and Sindram within their distinct settings. Every conceivable lesser measure need not first fail. Here the necessary applicant/category/inadequacy showing remains absent, while the current papers illustrate why their particular procedural content requires assessment.

The Court imposes no numerical threshold, categorical immunity or new rule for extraordinary writs or habeas. It vacates no existing lawful restriction and preserves ordinary neutral filing requirements, valid finality and jurisdictional rules, and sanctions for proved misconduct.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Zatko v. California:** Preserves supported denial of a particular frivolous or malicious filing; identity and earlier losses do not establish the necessary present defect.
- **Martin v. District of Columbia:** Applies its distinction between particular current papers and its separate prospective-control inquiry; the different current-paper outcomes remain meaningful.
- **Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25:** Preserves the clearly-baseless factual threshold and prohibition on resolving genuine disputes through ordinary disbelief. Its district-court statutory screen is not treated as the complete Rule 39.8 test.
- **Rowland v. California Men's Colony, 506 U.S. 194:** Preserves an entity's own authorized actual-means showing and lawful-representation requirements; personal financial leave does not resolve corporate eligibility or means.
- **Day, Sassower and In re Anderson:** Apply their current-paper and applicant-specific, category-specific prospective limits without creating a universal warning or exhaustion rule.
- **Attwood v. Singletary, 516 U.S. 297:** Applies its adequate financial-showing, particular-access-content and separate prospective-ground distinctions. Its certiorari denial is not extended to these petitions.
- **Jones v. ABC-TV, 516 U.S. 363:** Applies the same separate inquiries while distinguishing its unestablished financial particulars from Shieh's three supported declarations. Its pending financial treatment is not a rule requiring reservation here.
- **In re McDonald, 489 U.S. 180, and In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177:** Preserve bounded authority for properly supported targeted controls; they do not supply missing predicates for this proposal.

## Law After Decision

Financial eligibility, present-paper screening, future filing controls and certiorari remain separate inquiries. The Court applies existing law to three individually supported financial motions and three distinct procedural contentions. It grants neither blanket future eligibility nor derivative entity relief, and finds neither the underlying allegations nor notice violations proved.

No new future noncriminal-certiorari condition issues on this record. Supported Rule 39.8 denials and properly justified targeted controls remain available. The concurrence's stronger presumption is noncontrolling, and all three petitions await separate consideration.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs while joining the Court's opinion. Financial leave provides an opportunity for consideration, not endorsement of a petition or entitlement to certiorari. Each of these declarations and papers satisfies the conditions for that opportunity; neither one adequate affidavit nor one defective allegation can resolve the other motions.

He would apply a strong presumption against predictive personal exclusions and require demonstrated inadequacy of ordinary paper-specific controls before contemplating exceptional future restrictions. A history of unsuccessful submissions does not establish the contents of an unwritten petition, and a potentially cognizable future claim should not be excluded merely because of its author's identity. Past filings can justify close scrutiny; they do not supply a presumption that an unexamined paper is malicious or frivolous.

That stronger position goes beyond the Court's refusal of the unsupported condition here. He does not turn the prior filing decisions into a universal prohibition or claim that individual screening is costless. Targeted sanctions for proved misconduct, valid jurisdictional and finality rules, financial eligibility and ordinary filing requirements remain distinct. The three grants imply no underlying relief or disposition of certiorari. No other Justice joins this additional presumption.
