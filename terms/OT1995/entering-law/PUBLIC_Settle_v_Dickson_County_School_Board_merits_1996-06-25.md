## Event

Settle v. Dickson County School Board. Decided June 25, 1996, on review under 28 U.S.C. section 1254(1) of the Sixth Circuit's May 8, 1995 judgment, No. 93-6207, 53 F.3d 152, affirming defendants' summary judgment on the student's free-speech claim.

During March 1991, ninth-grade teacher Dana Ramsey assigned a paper teaching research, synthesis and writing. Students could choose or change subjects on a list through March 22; later changes required permission, and outlines were due March 26. Brittney Settle first listed Drama, changed over the weekend, and tendered The Life of Jesus Christ without prior discussion or permission. Ramsey rejected it and the later April 3 proposal, A Scientific and Historical Approach to Jesus Christ. Settle received zero after declining a different assignment. The petition additionally reports a later completed paper tendered without being read; its full contents are not reproduced in the cited materials.

The six reported explanations remain distinct:

1. Settle had not obtained advance permission for the change.
2. Her strong belief might prevent dispassionate research or make ordinary criticism of grammar and organization seem criticism of religion.
3. Personal religious beliefs were considered inappropriate classroom dialogue.
4. Her existing knowledge might permit a quick outline without new research.
5. Ramsey believed law prohibited religious issues in class.
6. She believed sources about Jesus ultimately derived from the Bible and failed the four-source requirement.

The district memorandum reports a following-day opportunity to submit another topic without penalty and reproduces Ramsey's statement that procedure and topic both contributed. The petition reproduces her answer that she would also have rejected the topic as an original choice, adding the same answer for Buddha or Muhammed. Actual source permissions included two books and two encyclopedias or resources, magazine or pamphlet substitutes, and outside materials producible on request.

The question is whether those grounds and the contrary evidence permit summary judgment under genuine pedagogical limits and ordinary causation rules. No independent religion-clause or equal-protection claim is decided.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate in the decision.

The Court vacates the Sixth Circuit's judgment and remands, setting aside the reviewed free-speech summary judgment.

## Holdings

### I. Curricular authority must rest on genuine, legitimate pedagogy

**Operative rule:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the rule, explanation and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazelwood preserves substantial authority over the lesson taught, maturity, quality, research and method. Tinker does not entitle a student to replace a genuine assignment with expression of her own choosing. The Court applies that pedagogical principle here without assuming that a paper submitted only to a teacher carries public school endorsement or creates an open forum.

Lamb's Chapel, Rosenberger and Good News distinguish legitimate program boundaries from exclusion of an otherwise admitted religious perspective. Their forums and benefits differ from this graded assignment; they inform the distinction without requiring acceptance of every religious topic. A school may select the knowledge, method or perspective taught and distinguish objective inquiry from personal testimony or devotional advocacy. Today's rule does not impose universal viewpoint neutrality on every curricular selection.

Epperson preserves educational authority while denying constitutional immunity to curriculum decisions and distinguishing academic study from religious instruction. Lee and Sherman likewise do not make all consideration of religion unlawful. The asserted blanket legal prohibition therefore cannot be justified simply as an honest academic mistake. Ordinary genuine factual or evaluative errors may remain within legitimate pedagogical discretion; a legally impermissible categorical rule is different.

The complete board policy permits objective, respectful academic treatment, including religious literature, music, drama and art intrinsic to learning, with balanced, comprehensive thematic treatment that neither promotes tenets nor demeans belief. It also accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief in responses and assignments. Both parts matter. Neither establishes automatic topic approval or substitutes a policy dispute for the presented federal claim.

### II. The affirmative record leaves a material causal dispute

**Operative rule:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The approval violation is supported, and no religious exemption excuses it. But the documented next-day alternative without penalty, the mixed explanation and the original-choice answer permit dispute over whether that violation independently governed the result. The answer concerning Buddha and Muhammed matters in full; it does not establish exclusively anti-Christian hostility.

The concern about dispassionate research and ordinary criticism can be academic, but the student's faith alone does not conclusively prove inability to research or accept evaluation. The classroom-dialogue explanation may distinguish objective work from testimony, or may exclude religious perspective as such. Familiarity can justify requiring new inquiry, but does not itself establish that this project involved none. These applications and their causal roles remain disputed.

The asserted legal ban is not governing law. The Bible-source explanation must be assessed against actual permission for secondary sources, substitutes and producible outside materials. A primary-source-only or school-library-only rule cannot be added to sustain judgment. Other accepted religiously inflected subjects provide relevant context, but their described content does not establish identical method, source, belief or approval circumstances.

Waters preserves the distinction between a genuine permissible account and an explanation merely available after the fact. Its full employment balance and investigation framework do not govern school assignments. Mt. Healthy likewise distinguishes actual motivating cause from a proved same-decision defense: identifying authority to make a lawful decision does not prove that decision would have occurred, while an impermissible factor does not defeat a properly proved defense automatically.

The strongest affirmance argument preserves genuine objective-research topic control. The Court accepts that authority but cannot treat its disputed application as established here. The affirmative testimony and permissions warrant vacatur; incomplete source reproduction is not evidence of litigant default or an independent reason for relief. The Court finds neither pretext nor final liability and leaves ordinary adjudication of remaining grounds available.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The legitimate-pedagogy principle now has this bounded application to a teacher-only research assignment: instructional authority remains substantial, but it does not insulate a categorical religious reason or conclusively resolve a supported dispute about actual cause. Neither content selection nor a student's religious commitment decides the claim by itself.

Courts must preserve genuine academic requirements, apply ordinary summary-judgment inferences and maintain Mt. Healthy's separate burdens. They do not manage every research judgment, require perfect factual accuracy or impose an independent constitutional duty to investigate each proposed topic. The decision establishes no general religious-topic entitlement, classroom forum, curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule, compelled grade or final liability.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Sixth Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the free-speech claim under the stated rules. Defendants may establish genuine curricular grounds, a properly supported same-decision defense and other actually available defenses. The remand does not guarantee a trial or foreclose another legally sufficient disposition.

Graduation does not establish mootness of every claim in light of the reported damages demand and requested academic-record correction, but no broad injunction for future students follows. The district did not decide qualified immunity; the opposition's presumption of abandonment is not a waiver finding. Actual capacity, municipal attribution and relief must be addressed as properly presented. Any individual conduct-date notice inquiry concerns March-April 1991; later decisions cannot themselves establish notice then. No immunity award or denial, damages sum, grade change or additional Supreme Court event is ordered.
