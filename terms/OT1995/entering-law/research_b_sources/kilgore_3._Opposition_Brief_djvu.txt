COUNTERSTATEMENT OF THE CASE 


The pickup truck in ques- 
tion was parked in a private 
driveway. Police saw defendant 
(owner) and Kelly Kilgore walk 
"toward" the truck, not “to” it. 
Police then lost sight of the 
couple for up to five minutes. 
There was no known entry or 
touching of the truck. Out- 


buildings were adjacent to it. 


After a team of at least three 
police raided property, and 
took the defendant into custody, 
and had all residents and every- 
thing under control, they 
searched the truck and found 
cocaine in the bottom of a 
McDonald's cup not in plain 


view.

The Supreme Court of 
Pennsylvania suppressed the 
cocaine because there were no 
exigent circumstances justifying 
searching the truck without a 
warrant. 

Petitioner is attempting 
to torture this decision into 
saying that the police may never 
make a warrantless search of an 


vehicle .-

REASON FOR DENYING WRIT 


COMMONWEALTH'S ARGUMENT TURNS THE 
HOLDING OF THE PENNSYLVANIA 
SUPREME COURT INTO A SWEEPING 
ABROGATION OF THE AUTOMOBILE 
EXCEPTION TO THE WARRANT REQUIRE- 
MENT, WHICH IT IS NOT. BECAUSE 
OF ITS UNIQUE FACTS, THIS CASE 
FALLS OUTSIDE THE AUTOMOBILE 
EXCEPTION IN THAT IT DOES NOT 
INVOLVE A CONSENT SEARCH, PLAIN 
VIEW, SUDDEN ENCOUNTER AND STOP, 
OR SEARCH INCIDENT TO ARREST. 
VEHICLES ARE NOT PER SE SEARCHABLE 


WITHOUT A WARRANT. 


Most of the cases Commonwealth 
cites involve sudden encounter and 


stop (Johns, Thomas and Ross), or 


exigent circumstances (Carney) or 


plain view (White). The present 


5 

case simply falls outside the 
recognized exceptions to the 
Warrant requirement, and there- 
fore a warrant is required. 

Commonwealth is attempting 
to make the automobile exception 
apply to all automobiles, per se, 
regardless of circumstances. 
An automobile is not a talisman 
in whose presence the Fourth 


Amendment fades away. 


CONCLUSION 
The writ should be denied.
