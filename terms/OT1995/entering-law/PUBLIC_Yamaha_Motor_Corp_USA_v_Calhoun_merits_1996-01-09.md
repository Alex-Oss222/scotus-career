## Event

Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun, 516 U.S. 199, No. 94-1387. Argued October 31, 1995; decided January 9, 1996. On certiorari to the Third Circuit, 40 F.3d 622. A child died in a jet-ski accident in Puerto Rico's territorial waters. The parents invoked admiralty and diversity and alleged negligence, strict liability, and implied warranties. The District Court's certified partial-summary-judgment order treated federal maritime law as displacing state death remedies; both sides obtained permitted interlocutory appeals. The questions are the scope of review of that order and whether state wrongful-death and survival remedies remain available to this nonseafarer.

## Participation

## Holdings

### Review follows the certified order, including its anterior displacement question

**Operative rule:** When a court of appeals permits an interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b), its jurisdiction reaches issues fairly included within the certified order, rather than only the particular question formulated for certification. Here the maritime displacement question is fairly included because it is the premise of the appealed partial-summary-judgment order; this authority reaches no unrelated order in the lawsuit.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These eight directly adopt the rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition on this component and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written certification that an order involves a controlling question of law, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that immediate review may materially advance ultimate termination. A timely application within ten days allows the court of appeals to permit review in its discretion; an application alone does not stay proceedings without an authorized stay order. The certified object of review is the order.

The District Court relied on federal displacement when it granted Yamaha partial summary judgment against future earnings and punitive damages but denied it against loss of society and support or services. Both parties sought certification and obtained permitted appeals. The Third Circuit could examine the anterior displacement issue, which both sides briefed and which underlies the damages rulings, even though the certified question described recoverable damages under a federal maritime cause.

Swint v. Chambers County Commission rejected adding a separate, otherwise nonappealable county defense to the officers' collateral-order appeal for convenience. It preserved §1292(b)'s distinct authorized route. Here that route was actually followed. The Court neither enlarges collateral-order jurisdiction nor grants appellate power over every issue or order associated with the case.

**Precedent treatment:** Swint's limit on unauthorized connected review is preserved and distinguished from this permitted appeal of a certified order.

### State death remedies survive in this nonseafarer territorial-waters case

**Operative rule:** Moragne's federal maritime wrongful-death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state wrongful-death and survival remedies for a nonseafarer killed in territorial waters when no applicable federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule supplies an exclusive recovery regime. This rule concerns persons outside the Jones Act seaman and pertinent federal maritime-employment classes; it does not enlarge remedies within Congress's occupied seaman or high-seas fields.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the remedies rule, its limits, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Moragne v. States Marine Lines supplied a federal wrongful-death action to remove a gap in maritime protection. Adding that remedy did not require withdrawal of state death remedies traditionally available in territorial waters. The Court assumes without deciding that Moragne extends to this nonseafarer and includes a survival action; even on those assumptions, the federal remedy is not an exclusive damages code here.

Miles v. Apex Marine Corp. aligned the remedy for a seaman with Congress's employment-related compensation choices. Offshore Logistics v. Tallentire preserves the Death on the High Seas Act's exclusive regime within its geographical field. Neither source extends that occupied field to a child who was neither a seaman nor a covered longshore worker and who died in Puerto Rico's territorial waters.

Yamaha's appeal to uniformity cannot replace those statutes' coverage conditions. Damages are substantive, so American Dredging's separate protection of state forum administration does not alone decide the issue. The relevant ground is the established coexistence of territorial-water death remedies outside a conflicting federal regime, not a general permission for state law to alter characteristic maritime rights.

The Third Circuit therefore correctly rejected displacement as the basis for Yamaha's partial-summary-judgment motion. The Court does not select Pennsylvania or Puerto Rico law, determine what either wrongful-death or survival law permits, or award any category or amount of damages. Liability standards, defect, negligence, causation, and punitive liability remain unresolved.

**Precedent treatment:** Moragne is applied as an added federal remedy without automatic displacement. Miles and Offshore Logistics retain their statutory boundaries. American Dredging is distinguished as a forum-practice decision, not an independent validation of substantive damages.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35:** Its collateral-order and separate-defense limits remain; the Court applies the distinct authorized §1292(b) route to issues within the certified order.
- **Moragne v. States Marine Lines, 398 U.S. 375:** Its federal death remedy does not automatically oust state remedies here. Extension to nonseafarers and inclusion of a survival action are assumed, not decided.
- **Miles v. Apex Marine Corp., 498 U.S. 19:** Its congruence with congressionally prescribed seaman remedies remains; that limitation is not transferred to this uncovered territorial-water death.
- **Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207:** Its protection of DOHSA's exclusive high-seas regime is preserved and distinguished geographically.
- **American Dredging Co. v. Miller, 510 U.S. 443:** Its state-forum practice holding remains distinct; substantive damages do not survive merely because a State labels them procedural.

## Law After Decision

A permitted §1292(b) appeal includes issues fairly within its certified order, not unrelated orders. State death and survival remedies remain available in this nonseafarer territorial-waters category subject to actual federal displacement. No general rule validating every state maritime remedy or liability standard is announced.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit's remand remains operative. The District Court must determine the applicable Pennsylvania or Puerto Rico law and its consequences in the ordinary course. Recoverable damages, liability standards, and actual liability remain for adjudication; the Court prescribes no award.
