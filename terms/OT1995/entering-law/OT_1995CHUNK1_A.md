## Standards and Tests selection: Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

### Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

**Governing question:** Assessment and consequence of suppressed favorable evidence.

**Current rule:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses in the whole record, including defense preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently meet the standard; the defendant need not show that acquittal is more likely than conviction or that remaining evidence is legally insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence held by police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires. Personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Once a material Brady violation is established, a second duplicative prejudice or harmlessness inquiry cannot defeat relief by reassessing the same nondisclosure.

**Authority by component:**

- Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction: [Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kyles_v_Whitley_merits_1995-04-19.md#public-projection), 1995-04-19. Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for the rule, application and conviction-level relief. The dissent accepts collective assessment but disputes materiality on this record.
- The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team: [The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kyles_v_Whitley_merits_1995-04-19.md#public-projection), 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. The dissent’s consideration of the police-held material does not supply additional votes for an affirmative organizational rule.
- A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing: [A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kyles_v_Whitley_merits_1995-04-19.md#public-projection), 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for this claim-specific ground. The dissent finds no material violation and adopts no independent harmlessness ground.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-04-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This case-team responsibility does not impute every government office’s knowledge or require unrestricted investigation or open-file discovery. Lawful corroboration, impeachment and investigation-related uses matter collectively; every nondisclosure is not structural error. Independent habeas gates and defense diligence concerning known evidence remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent legal review, contemporaneous notice and preservation limits: [Elder v. Holloway — Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Elder_v_Holloway_merits_1994-02-23.md#public-projection), 1994-02-23. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. ## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: [Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Johnson_v_Jones_merits_1995-06-12.md#public-projection), 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: [Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kimberlin_v_Quinlan_merits_1995-06-12.md#public-projection), 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-12; the component authorities identified above.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

## Standards and Tests selection: Individual-capacity liability for official conduct under §1983

### Individual-capacity liability for official conduct under §1983

**Governing question:** Whether use of official authority prevents a state officer from being a §1983 person when sued individually.

**Current rule:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority; sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by the officer personally. The plaintiff must prove the officer's own federal violation under color of law, and applicable personal immunities remain available. The capacity in which the officer is sued, rather than whether the conduct was official, distinguishes this action from a claim against the State or an official-capacity damages claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Personhood, capacity distinction, personal-payment immunity limit, and retained defenses: [Hafer v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for preserved Doyle error and the preserved personal-intent instructional category properly available on federal habeas.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. For preserved Doyle error properly before a federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the verdict, considering the whole record. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. The same State-borne Chapman inquiry governs a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that category. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ.

**Authority by component:**

Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas: [Brecht v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

### Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

**Governing question:** When a capital defendant must be allowed to explain the only lawful alternative to death.

**Current rule:** When the State puts future dangerousness in issue and the only lawful alternative to death is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. No single verbal formula is required. Speculative clemency does not make ordinary parole available. On direct review, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that denying the explanation did not contribute to the jury’s selection of death; where it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it continues to seek death. The error is not classified as structural.

**Authority by component:**

- Trigger, effective means and error remedy: [Simmons v. South Carolina — Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case; The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Simmons_v_South_Carolina_merits_1994-06-17.md#public-projection), 1994-06-17. Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary. Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Present operation effective:** Simmons v. South Carolina, 1994-06-17.

**Open question:** The decision does not establish the instruction requirement when the State has not raised future danger or when the legally available alternatives materially differ.

**Holdings navigation:** [Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#parole-ineligibility-must-be-available-to-answer-the-states-future-danger-case); [The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-error-is-not-harmless-on-this-record-and-requires-a-new-penalty-proceeding-if-death-is-pursued).

## Standards and Tests selection: Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

### Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

**Governing question:** Constitutionally invalid aggravation in a weighing State, whether used directly by the judge or transmitted through a jury recommendation carrying legally required great weight.

**Current rule:** Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure a constitutional weighing error, although an invalid weighing factor does not automatically negate death eligibility if valid eligibility findings remain. The state judicial system must provide genuine reweighing without the invalid factor where state law authorizes it, or constitutional harmless-error review proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not affect the sentence. Bare factor counting, proportionality review or a harmless-error label supplies neither cure; no particular verbal formula is indispensable if the substantive finding is actually made. A jury's inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction can infect the final death sentence when the judge must give the resulting recommendation great weight, even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. Correction must address that transmitted influence. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor disturbance of guilt follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Distinct weighing error and eligibility, with lawful correction routes: [Stringer v. Black, “A weighing error needs a constitutionally adequate cure”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Stringer_v_Black_merits_1992-03-09.md), March 9, 1992; Kennedy for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor.
- Actual judicial reliance, substantive cure and no magic-words requirement: [Sochor v. Florida, “The judge's documented coldness reliance requires correction” and “The existing appellate explanation does not cure the error”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Sochor_v_Florida_merits_1992-06-08.md), June 8, 1992; respectively the eight-Justice Part III-B-1 and six-Justice Parts III-B-2/IV, applying Stringer, Clemons, Parker and Chapman without changing their correction standard.
- A legally influential recommendation and correction of its transmitted effect: [Espinosa v. Florida, “A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator” and “Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Espinosa_v_Florida_summary_merits_1992-06-29.md), June 29, 1992; per curiam joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Espinosa*, June 29, 1992, extending the rule to the recommendation's legally required influence. The earlier direct-weighing rule remains supplied by *Stringer*, March 9, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** On the harmless-error route the State bears Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. That requirement is distinct from authority to perform a genuine reweighing and from collateral-review gateways.

**Exceptions and limits:** States retain latitude in assigning sentencing roles, but must give constitutional guidance to an influential recommendation. No factor-specific juror finding is presumed, and constitutional permission for a kind of appellate cure does not establish that state law authorizes it. Independent preservation, default and habeas barriers remain intact.

**Related current rules:** “Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations” preserves the distinct inference boundary for a legally available but evidentially unsupported jury factor. “Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims” governs access to review of otherwise barred claims, not correction of an available claim's weighing error.

**Holdings navigation:** [Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992) — Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#stringer-v-black-503-us-222-1992); [Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992) — Capital sentencing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#sochor-v-florida-504-us-527-1992-1); [Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992) — Capital sentencing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#espinosa-v-florida-505-us-1079-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

### Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

**Governing question:** Whether an omitted Teague defense must be entertained when first raised in Supreme Court merits briefing.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted it below and in its certiorari opposition and first asserted it in merits briefing, the Court may decline consideration. The permission is discretionary, not a mandatory waiver rule. A consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review presents the distinct fair-inclusion setting addressed in Caspari; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

## Standing State selection: 3. Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. ### Collective disclosure materiality and prosecution responsibility

#### [Kyles v. Whitley](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kyles_v_Whitley_merits_1995-04-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7927.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction

**Holding:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently satisfy that standard, and the defendant need not show acquittal more likely than conviction or that the remaining evidence is legally insufficient; the established suppression here meets the standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83: applies the duty to disclose material favorable evidence to the established suppression.
- United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: applies reasonable-probability materiality to the combined defense counterfactual, including lawful impeachment and preparation uses.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: distinguishes its gateway showing; it supplies no additional innocence prerequisite for this Brady claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collective inquiry includes legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. It does not excuse lack of diligence about evidence already known to the defense, require open-file discovery or make every nondisclosure structural error.

##### The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team

**Holding:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady and Bagley: apply their trial-disclosure protection to information held by police acting for this prosecution.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: distinguishes its confined grand-jury disclosure and dismissal rules; those limits remain effective within their own setting.

##### A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing

**Holding:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bagley: treats materiality as a defining element of the suppression violation, not an initial screen before a second duplicative prejudice test.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: preserve their actual category-specific holdings and distinguish them from claim-defining Brady materiality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and remanded for a conditional habeas writ requiring release unless Louisiana retries Kyles within a reasonable time fixed by the District Court. No acquittal, immediate unconditional release, fixed number of days, penalty-only relief or adjudication of other claims is ordered.

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. **Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

### Constitutional trial error on habeas

#### [O'Neal v. McAninch](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/ONeal_v_McAninch_merits_1995-02-21.md)

##### Chapman governs the preserved personal-intent instructional claim

Actual harmlessness is not decided.

### Capital sentencing responsibility and rebuttal

#### [Romano v. Oklahoma, 512 U.S. 1](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Romano_v_Oklahoma_merits_1994-06-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9093.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### Another jury's death decision cannot displace the present jury's responsibility

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments forbid the State to present an irrelevant death sentence imposed by another jury in circumstances that substantially invite the present capital jury to treat its own choice of death as already made elsewhere. This contextual rule extends Caldwell's protection beyond an inaccurate description of legal responsibility; it requires more than a speculative possibility of influence, preserves independently admissible prior-conduct evidence, and leaves open whether a focused instruction can prevent the distortion in a different record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Responsibility portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices adopt this rule and its application to the Sarfaty penalty proceeding.

**Material application:** The Thompson judgment disclosed another jury's death selection and execution command despite an offered stipulation to conviction. Its truth, the appeal notice and correct general instructions did not specifically withdraw the suggestion that the decisive choice had already been made. The Court finds the contextual constitutional distortion, not actual abdication by an identified juror.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its responsibility protection is extended to an official disclosure of another jury's death selection that substantially invites displacement of the current choice; the Court does not pretend the earlier facts already decided this question.
- Darden v. Wainwright, 477 U.S. 168 (1986): its guilt-stage setting and remarks increasing responsibility are distinguished; its separate fundamental-unfairness standard remains intact.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401 (1989): its procedural-default holding is preserved; its description of the ordinary misstatement claim does not preclude the extension adopted here.
- Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227 (1990): its collateral-review new-rule holding is preserved; no nonretroactivity bar enters this direct review.

##### Earlier aggravator reweighing does not resolve the responsibility error

**Holding:** The state appellate court's deletion of the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighing of the remaining three do not themselves resolve the distinct constitutional error in presenting another jury's death selection. Its judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under the responsibility holding; the Court establishes no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and a first constitutional harmless-error inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Common disposition and prior-reweighing portion of Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five adopt this limited proposition and appellate vacatur/remand; their different further-remedy positions are not a single holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority determines whether the existing sentence may be retained following a new constitutional harmlessness determination or whether a new penalty hearing is required whenever the State continues to seek death. No independently sufficient ordinary due-process holding, general constitutional evidence code, or Court-wide ruling on the validity of capital punishment is adopted. No actual juror mental state, inevitable execution, harmlessness finding, automatic life sentence, acquittal or release is found or ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its independent-responsibility protection identifies an error distinct from an invalid aggravator; earlier reweighing does not automatically answer it.
- Johnson v. Mississippi, 486 U.S. 578 (1988): the invalid-prior-conviction problem remains distinct from the responsibility error; removing the prior-violent-felony aggravator does not decide the latter.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Oklahoma appellate judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded under the responsibility rule. The Court has no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and an initial constitutional harmless-error inquiry. It neither separately declares the trial sentence automatically void nor declares it enforceable; guilt and the independent Thompson proceedings remain untouched. No replacement sentence, release or execution order issues.

#### [Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Simmons_v_South_Carolina_merits_1994-06-17.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9059.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

##### Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue in a capital sentencing proceeding and the only legally available noncapital sentence is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity for the defendant to inform the jury of that ineligibility through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. A direction to disregard parole and give life imprisonment its ordinary meaning does not satisfy that requirement on this record; the rule neither requires a particular script nor promises immunity from clemency or future legislation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The operative alternative was life without ordinary parole under §24-21-640's second-or-subsequent violent-conviction rule following separate sentencing for the prior conviction; offenses within one day or one continuous course count as one. The eligibility premise is uncontested here, not a rule that every life sentence excludes parole. The State may still prove danger to inmates or guards and offer truthful information about commutation or pardon.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977): its opportunity-to-answer principle supplies the due-process foundation for responding to material sentencing information.
- Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986): its future-danger rebuttal principle is applied to a fixed legal sentencing alternative; its independent mitigation rule remains intact.
- California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983): distinguished; permission to give truthful commutation information does not authorize withholding this responsive fact about ordinary parole.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their effective-mitigation rules remain controlling within their scope; neither is treated as having already decided this separate legal-information question.

##### The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued

**Holding:** Under Chapman's direct-review standard, the State cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt that barring the parole-ineligibility explanation did not contribute to this jury's selection of death, given the State's future-danger argument, the complete exclusion of the legal answer and the jury's express parole inquiry. The death-sustaining judgment is reversed and the case remanded for a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; a lawful noncapital disposition remains available, and the convictions and separate sentences are undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no independently sufficient Eighth Amendment instruction rule. It does not determine an entitlement to parole information where the State has not raised future danger, settle a disputed parole-eligibility question, require a fixed form of words, guarantee against clemency or future legislation, or announce automatic reversal for every omission. The State may pursue a lawful noncapital disposition instead of another capital hearing. No guilt retrial, acquittal, release, invalidation of the separate sentences or general prohibition of capital punishment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied; the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, and fails on this record.
- Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994: distinguished as an affirmative disclosure of another jury's death decision; this decision neither supplies a missing majority for its further-remedy positions nor changes that case's mandate.
- Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: preserves the distinction between correcting a capital sentencing defect and disturbing guilt; no automatic noncapital sentence follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sustaining judgment is reversed, 7–2, and the case remanded. Five Justices require a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; the State may instead pursue a lawful noncapital disposition. The existing death sentence cannot be retained by a further harmlessness finding on this omission. Guilt and the separate life and thirty-day sentences remain undisturbed.

### Capital sentencing and correction of weighing error

#### Richmond v. Lewis, 506 U.S. 40 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7094.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### An actual cure is necessary

**Holding:** When a death sentence in a State that weighs aggravation against mitigation rests on an unconstitutionally vague aggravating factor, remaining valid factors do not alone cure the error. The sentence may stand only after an authorized sentencer actually makes a constitutionally valid new sentencing determination, including lawful appellate reweighing, or the State establishes constitutional harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990), applied: lawful state appellate cure is permitted, automatic factor-count affirmance is not. Stringer v. Black (March 9), Sochor v. Florida (June 8) and Espinosa v. Florida (June 29), all 1992, applied at their controlling levels: actual weighing error requires actual constitutional correction. Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), preserves the State’s beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness obligation.

##### Arizona’s adjudication did not cure this sentence

**Holding:** On the parties’ premise that a majority of the Arizona Supreme Court had to cast valid curative votes, this sentence cannot stand: even assuming the two Justices retaining the narrowed aggravator lawfully reweighed, the two other affirming Justices relied conclusively on the remaining criminal history without a new balance of mitigation. The denial of sentence relief is reversed for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of the death sentence and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents. No rule demanding identical reasoning from every member of a state appellate majority is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990), and Lewis v. Jeffers, 497 U.S. 764 (1990), preserved: narrowing constructions and appropriately reviewed state applications may be valid, but do not supply a curative vote absent here. State v. Gretzler, 135 Ariz.42, 659 P.2d1 (1983), remains the state narrowing source; its proper application by Holohan and Hays is not decided. Clemons and the operative weighing-error decisions require the stated remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arizona is a weighing State. A general duty of independent review is not actual reweighing; no prescribed verbal formula is required, and a federal habeas court cannot perform the missing state sentencing balance. The validity of the Holohan–Hays narrowing and reweighing is assumed, not decided. The accepted state-majority premise establishes no requirement that every curative vote use identical reasoning.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the denial of sentence relief and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of death and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence. The conviction is untouched; no automatic life sentence is imposed.

#### Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992)

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.

#### [Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Caspari_v_Bohlen_merits_1994-02-23.md)

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401 (1989): its procedural-default holding is preserved; its description of the ordinary misstatement claim does not preclude the extension adopted here.
- Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227 (1990): its collateral-review new-rule holding is preserved; no nonretroactivity bar enters this direct review.

##### Earlier aggravator reweighing does not resolve the responsibility error

**Holding:** The state appellate court's deletion of the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighing of the remaining three do not themselves resolve the distinct constitutional error in presenting another jury's death selection. Its judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under the responsibility holding; the Court establishes no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and a first constitutional harmless-error inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Common disposition and prior-reweighing portion of Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five adopt this limited proposition and appellate vacatur/remand; their different further-remedy positions are not a single holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority determines whether the existing sentence may be retained following a new constitutional harmlessness determination or whether a new penalty hearing is required whenever the State continues to seek death. No independently sufficient ordinary due-process holding, general constitutional evidence code, or Court-wide ruling on the validity of capital punishment is adopted. No actual juror mental state, inevitable execution, harmlessness finding, automatic life sentence, acquittal or release is found or ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its independent-responsibility protection identifies an error distinct from an invalid aggravator; earlier reweighing does not automatically answer it.
- Johnson v. Mississippi, 486 U.S. 578 (1988): the invalid-prior-conviction problem remains distinct from the responsibility error; removing the prior-violent-felony aggravator does not decide the latter.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Oklahoma appellate judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded under the responsibility rule. The Court has no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and an initial constitutional harmless-error inquiry. It neither separately declares the trial sentence automatically void nor declares it enforceable; guilt and the independent Thompson proceedings remain untouched. No replacement sentence, release or execution order issues.

### Qualified-immunity review

#### [Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Elder_v_Holloway_merits_1994-02-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

## Verbatim Holdings selection: civil-rights / Prison officials’ duty to protect

Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md

### Prison officials’ duty to protect

#### [Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Farmer_v_Brennan_merits_1994-06-06.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7247.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Holding:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Material application:** The official need not foresee the precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. No defendant is found to have the required knowledge, responsibility or causal role; reasonable but unsuccessful protection remains outside deliberate indifference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Holding:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Proposition-level authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Holding:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Proposition-level authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and summary judgment without an express-warning prerequisite. Discovery may be allowed or denied on a proper basis, and renewed summary judgment remains available. Knowledge, responsibility, causation, reasonable response, capacities and immunity remain individualized. No trial, damages or placement injunction is ordered; prospective relief must fit any established continuing danger.

### Individual-capacity liability

#### Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-681.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. **Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and entitlement to reinstatement remain unresolved; no immunity standard changes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. ### Collateral appeals from municipal liability defenses

#### [Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Swint_v_Chambers_County_Commission_merits_1995-03-01.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1636.
**Decided:** March 1, 1995.

##### Ordinary municipal liability remains reviewable after final judgment

**Holding:** The denial of this municipal defendant's ordinary liability defense does not qualify for immediate collateral-order review under §1291 because the defense remains effectively reviewable after final judgment. The officers' distinct immunity from suit does not give the municipality a corresponding right to avoid trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. retains its cumulative collateral-order requirements. Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. is applied through its independently controlling adequate-final-review ground. Mitchell v. Forsyth is distinguished because the officials' immunity from suit is not the county's liability defense. Monell v. Department of Social Services supplies no municipal immunity from all trial.

**Independent alternative holding:** An order that expressly leaves the county's final-policymaker question open for further presentation and a later legal ruling does not conclusively determine that question under Cohen. This failure independently defeats collateral-order review; the mere denial of the pending motions does not resolve a question the court expressly reserves.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Part III, eight Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen is applied to the express reservation of the disputed question. Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay is applied to the requirement of a conclusive, rather than tentative, determination. Neither decision is enlarged into a rule admitting every later definitive ruling to immediate appeal.

##### A proper immunity appeal does not authorize this separate county appeal

**Holding:** A court of appeals may not decide a separate party's nonappealable liability defense merely because a related officers' immunity appeal is properly before it and resolving the defense would save litigation. The county attribution issue here is neither inextricably intertwined with nor necessary to meaningful review of the officers' qualified-immunity questions, and no other authorized appellate route is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part II, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abney v. United States and United States v. Stanley are applied to limits on adding otherwise unauthorized issues to a proper immediate appeal. Cohen and Digital Equipment remain the source of the separate collateral-order inquiry. Earlier examples of connected review are not overruled or converted into a general pendent-party jurisdiction doctrine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Part IV of Ginsburg’s opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices, expressly leaves undecided whether genuinely inextricable or necessary connected review is available at all and what its scope would be. Stone-Zsela’s affirmative view is separate and noncontrolling. Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written statement that an order involves a controlling question of law, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that an immediate appeal may materially advance the litigation's ultimate termination. Application must be made within ten days; the court of appeals may then permit the appeal in its discretion. Application alone does not stay District Court proceedings unless the District Judge, the court of appeals, or a judge thereof orders a stay. No qualifying certification or permission is established here. Section 1292(e) permits additional interlocutory-appeal rules prescribed under §2072; §2072(c) permits rules defining finality under §1291. Those are rulemaking authorizations, subject to §2072's prohibition on abridging, enlarging, or modifying substantive rights, and are not an operative rule allowing this appeal. No mandamus relief is sought or granted here; that distinct route retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Commission's appellate disposition under §§1983 and 1985(3) is vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss its interlocutory appeal. Its surviving federal claims return to their preappeal District Court posture without a liability or summary-judgment ruling. Officers' immunity dispositions and separately declined City and individual state-law appeals are undisturbed; no county state-law count remained.

#### [Johnson v. Jones](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Johnson_v_Jones_merits_1995-06-12.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-455.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

##### Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal

**Holding:** A denial of qualified immunity is not immediately appealable under §1291 insofar as it determines only whether the pretrial evidence presents a genuine factual dispute about the official's participation in the alleged conduct. Mitchell's immediate appeal remains available for the separable legal question whether the conduct, on the relevant assumed facts, violated clearly established law; the present excessive-force participation dispute does not meet that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A proper legal immunity appeal uses the district court's assumed facts. If unstated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bootstrap factual review. Genuine necessary connected review and future mixed-order questions remain at their reserved scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949): applies separateness as a cumulative collateral-order condition.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985): retains immediate legal immunity review on assumed facts, bounded against ordinary factual-sufficiency appeals.
- Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct (June 6, 1994): applies category and cumulative-condition discipline; public immunity importance does not erase separateness.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission (March 1, 1995): applies rejection of convenience-based appellate expansion; genuine necessary connected review remains at its reserved scope.
- Elder v. Holloway (February 23, 1994): preserves review of all relevant date-eligible legal authority on an authorized immunity issue; supplies no fact-reweighing jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the reviewed immediate excessive-force factual-sufficiency appeal. The circuit's separate false-arrest immunity reversal remains undisturbed. No participation, beating, evidence-sufficiency, liability, state-law claim or other defendant's liability is adjudicated.
