## Public Projection

## Event

Jones v. ABC-TV, No. 95-7186, 516 U.S. 363. Filing-management order, February 26, 1996. The Court considers the financial-leave motion accompanying Jones's petition for certiorari and a proposed condition on future noncriminal certiorari filings. The petition seeks review of the Eighth Circuit's August 8, 1995 summary affirmance of the district court's June 14 dismissal of a media-related civil action. Certiorari itself is not decided by this order.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participate. Justice Breyer takes no part; no reason is stated.

## Public Action

The Court maintains the financial-leave motion under consideration and reserves the financial-eligibility determination. It declines to deny the motion under Rule 39.8 on the present showing and declines the proposed future noncriminal-certiorari fee-and-form condition. The motion and petition remain pending. No refiling requirement or payment deadline is imposed.

## Judgment & Remedy

All eight participating Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg—support the pending treatment and refusal of the proposed present and prospective restrictions. This is not a grant or denial of financial leave or certiorari. No petition poll is announced. No new warning, sanction, Rule 33.1 payment/form condition, merits remand or alteration of an existing lawful filing order follows.

## Holdings

**Financial eligibility remains reserved.** Ginsburg's opinion, joined on this proposition by all seven other participating Justices, keeps the financial motion pending without finding eligibility or ineligibility. Rule 39.1 requires the prescribed motion and notarized affidavit or §1746 declaration, unless a qualifying appointed-counsel exception applies; no such exception is established here. The references to monthly benefits, copying loans and an accompanying affidavit do not establish the required financial particulars. Neither does an incomplete evidentiary showing establish that Jones can pay, failed to submit a required document or made a false statement. The pending treatment adds no refiling duty, deadline or default consequence. A later financial ruling requires its own supported basis.

**The present showing does not establish the Rule 39.8 predicate.** The same eight Justices decline a present denial for frivolousness or malice. The underlying media-agreement, compulsory-publication and derivative §1986 theories have substantial defects, and formal assignment and actual appellate review contradict broad allegations of self-assignment and no review. But the petition also identifies distinct objections to actual leave requirements affecting paid pro se filings and denial of requested appellate briefing. The defects in the media theories do not dispose of those objections. Martin, Anderson and Attwood require assessment of the particular paper; ordinary pleading failure is not automatically frivolousness. The Court finds neither that the access allegations are true nor that the lower restrictions, recusal ruling or appellate procedure were unlawful. A supported Rule 39.8 denial remains available; the Rule permits rather than compels that action. Refusing this proposed denial establishes neither indigence nor entitlement to review.

**The proposed future condition lacks its independent supporting showing.** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, applies Martin, Day, Sassower, Anderson and Attwood: a prospective condition needs support tied to the applicant, the relevant filing category and the inadequacy of individual treatment. The lower courts' abuse findings do not themselves establish those predicates for future noncriminal certiorari submissions here. Preserving paid and criminal routes narrows the proposal but does not supply the missing justification. The Court declines the condition on this record without categorically invalidating all prospective controls, requiring a warning in every case, fixing a numerical threshold or demanding exhaustion of every conceivable lesser measure.

## Precedent Treatment

Martin's present-paper distinction and separate prospective requirement, together with Anderson, Day, Sassower and Attwood, are applied. Zatko continues to permit supported denial of a particular frivolous or malicious filing. McDonald and Sindram retain their bounded authority for targeted protection of the judicial process. No prior filing-control holding is overruled or enlarged into a categorical immunity.

## Law After Decision

Current-paper assessment, financial eligibility, justification for future controls and certiorari remain separate inquiries. This order applies existing filing law, leaves the financial determination and petition pending, and creates no merits holding concerning the underlying suit or its procedural restrictions. The Court does not adopt the concurrence's broader future-filing rule.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in the financial and current-paper treatment and agrees that the prospective condition should be refused. He would require individual consideration of future ordinary civil-certiorari submissions by an otherwise financially eligible litigant. Rule 39.8 addresses a frivolous or malicious submission; past abuse does not establish the contents of an unwritten petition. Automatic fee denial based on identity and history risks excluding a later legitimate claim solely because its author cannot pay. Inherent docket authority should implement the Rules' current-paper inquiry, while permitting prompt rejection of a submission actually shown defective.

His position goes beyond the Court's record-specific ground. It concerns ordinary civil-certiorari fee treatment, does not decide every extraordinary-writ restriction, vacate unreviewed orders or establish unlimited fee-free litigation in every court, and preserves jurisdictional, timeliness, form and current-paper controls. Individual consideration requires no merits opinion, hearing or elaborate explanation for every filing. Financial eligibility must still be established; once it is established and current frivolousness or malice is not shown, he would grant financial leave and consider certiorari separately. The missing financial proof is not itself ineligibility, and the pending treatment grants neither review nor relief against the respondents.

## Procedure After Action

The financial-leave motion and petition remain pending. Financial eligibility is reserved for a supported later determination. No date for that determination, direction to refile, payment interval or terminal petition action is ordered. Future submissions remain subject to ordinary rules and governing law.

## Source Notes

The petition and its appended June 14 district memorandum, transfer order and August 8 Eighth Circuit order distinguish the rejected media claims from the particular filing and briefing objections. The petition references financial benefits and an affidavit but does not establish the affidavit's contents or present eligibility. The alleged proof in nineteen separate appendix volumes is not established by the record described here. The governing filing provisions are the Rules effective October 2, 1995, and 28 U.S.C. §1915 as then in force.
