## Public Projection

## Event

Bowersox v. Williams, application No. A-828; 517 U.S. 345. October Term 1995. Interim order, April 9, 1996, on Missouri's application to vacate an Eighth Circuit execution stay concerning Doyle J. Williams.

The third federal petition was denied January 11, following adoption of Magistrate Judge Hays's report; amendment was also denied. A certificate of probable cause issued. The March 8 appellate stay extends through submission and further appellate order, with argument scheduled May 13. Execution had been scheduled for April 10. No exact execution hour is established here. The available account describes a summary appellate order without articulated reasons.

## Participation

The application is presented to Justice O'Connor, the Eighth Circuit Justice, and by her referred to the Court. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Final disposition of the application is reserved for immediate examination of the actual application record. The Court orders:

1. The Clerk shall promptly circulate the application, any response and relevant papers already filed, and promptly transmit this order to counsel by appropriately speedy means.
2. Counsel shall immediately identify the operative appellate stay and later orders, the exact execution setting if known, and the grounds on which the challenged stay is defended or attacked. To the extent not already lodged and identified in this Court's file, they shall furnish the third petition, pertinent earlier dispositions, the magistrate report and objections, the January 11 ruling, proposed amendment and denial, and certificate and scope. Only papers material to the asserted grounds are required. Existing filings may satisfy this direction by identification; duplicative filing and an unrelated complete trial record are not required.
3. Initial supplementation shall conclude **two hours after the Clerk's prompt transmission**. Within that period counsel may identify a particular unavailable paper, explain its materiality and request an adjustment. Each side may promptly answer new material. Inability to produce or silence establishes no concession, abuse or insubstantiality; ultimate burdens remain unchanged.
4. The procedural reservation ends upon completion of immediate review or **four hours after transmission, whichever occurs first**. The still-pending application shall then return to the Court for immediate determination of the next lawful action. It shall return earlier when execution circumstances or completed submissions warrant. No automatic continuation follows; further judicial delay requires a separately supported action.
5. **No new Supreme Court execution stay issues.** This order neither enlarges, shortens nor finally approves the existing appellate stay. Expiration of the processing interval does not dissolve that stay, authorize execution, deny the application or adjudicate a habeas claim. The appellate stay remains governed by its own terms unless lawfully modified.

## Holdings

### I. Final disposition is reserved for immediate record examination

**Controlling proposition:** The Court reserves the State's application while immediately obtaining and examining the actual basis needed to decide it. This is an interim procedural action, not a determination that the appellate stay is lawful or unlawful or that any habeas ground warrants relief.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** A decision whether to disturb this execution stay requires the actual grounds and relevant procedural posture. The petition's number, a certificate and a scheduled argument do not identify those grounds. Nor does an unexplained appellate order establish their absence. Immediate examination separates the information needed for decision from the decision itself.

Delo v. Blair's equal division provides no controlling rationale. McFarland's preparation opportunity and Schlup's gateway require their own predicates. Lonchar does not establish a Williams claim, an unmet procedural obligation or a necessary interval of execution protection. The Court therefore makes no final substantial-ground finding and selects no eventual result. The parties' actual papers, existing judicial grounds and material remaining questions must determine the next action.

### II. Application management supports the specified short processing order

**Controlling proposition:** For this pending referred application, the Court directs circulation of existing papers and conditional supplementation of actually absent material on the two-hour/four-hour terms stated in the order. The deadlines regulate initial application processing and create no entitlement to a minimum delay or further habeas proceedings.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela does not join this exact implementation.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 22 governs the pending application and its referral. The Court may obtain the information necessary to consider that application; Barefoot's account of the Garrison submissions illustrates resolving an actual uncertainty about prior consideration through the papers. That procedural use grants no substantive habeas relief.

The order begins with what is already filed. A gap in an available source collection proves no omission from the Court's file, failure of service or failure of lower-court examination. Existing papers may permit immediate return without supplementation. Only actually absent, material documents require production, with an opportunity to identify an unavailable item and address new material.

The short maximum requires prompt attention to the State's request while permitting that examination. Two and four hours are this order's case-management terms, not periods prescribed by precedent. The four-hour endpoint returns the application for the next lawful decision; it guarantees neither adequate time for every merits inquiry nor any substantive result. The general motion-response period in Rule 21.4 does not apply simply because a Rule 22 application reaches the Court. Further Court-directed delay requires its own grounds and action.

### III. This processing order grants no additional execution protection

**Controlling proposition:** No independently established need for an additional Supreme Court execution stay supports the eight-Justice order on the present showing. The appellate restraint continues on its own terms, without new Supreme Court approval, shortening or extension.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by the seven other Associates. Stone-Zsela would grant the separate administrative protection stated below.

**Controlling explanation:** Missouri's pending application does not itself dissolve the appellate stay. Examination of that application can proceed under the existing restraint without treating unknown claims as substantial or finding a further habeas opportunity legally required. The need to determine the application therefore does not by itself establish a need for a second execution restraint.

A new administrative stay, a stay pending review and a final decision vacating an existing stay have different legal effects and require their respective grounds. The Court chooses none of those effects by setting a paper-processing interval. Rules 22–23 do not themselves manufacture substantive stay jurisdiction, and no new statutory injunction authority is decided. If later established circumstances require further action, its authority, basis, scope and ending event must be addressed then. The present order establishes no categorical prohibition on administrative protection and gives no permission to execute contrary to an operative stay.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, and Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320:** The successive-petition stay standard requires substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Actual procedural availability, prior dispositions and any independent merits ground matter. Neither petition count nor appellate silence substitutes for that inquiry. Barefoot's meaningful-review and necessary-stay rules for an initial certified appeal retain that setting; a certificate in this successive proceeding is not an automatic stay entitlement. Its certiorari-stage probability and irreparable-harm test is separate. Stokes's separate All Writs and heightened-deference reasoning is not promoted into a Court holding.
- **Delo v. Blair:** The equal division supplies no Court precedent or majority rationale.
- **Gomez and McFarland:** Independent stay equity and the State's enforcement interest survive; serious claims and irreversibility do not alone compel late relief. McFarland protects its actual statutory preparation opportunity while preserving denial for proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction. No new preparation entitlement is established here.
- **Schlup and Sawyer:** Crime innocence and death-eligibility innocence retain different thresholds. Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record, showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict; mere possibility or a reviewing judge's doubt is insufficient. Sawyer requires clear and convincing proof that absent constitutional error no reasonable juror would find death eligibility under state law; added discretionary mitigation alone does not suffice. Neither gateway establishes an underlying violation or automatically grants relief or a stay. No gateway is found satisfied.
- **Lonchar:** Its first-personal-petition dismissal correction and conditional necessary-process protection do not displace the successive-petition rule. Any necessary-process application requires identifying the obligation still due and inability to complete it lawfully in time; those predicates are not established here. Its restoration of a displaced district stay supplies no automatic remedy in this case.

The pre-AEDPA statutes and Rules remain applicable. Rule 9(a) requires delay-caused prejudice to the State's ability to respond and preserves its reasonable-diligence qualification; there is no enacted five-year presumption. Rule 9(b) requires the actual prior-merits/repeated-ground or abuse predicates. The then-effective §2244(b) subsequent-application permission follows a federal denial after an evidentiary hearing on a material factual merits issue or a hearing on a legal merits issue. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; the State's clear, particular abuse pleading precedes the prisoner's resulting burden. New evidence is not automatically a new ground. Exhaustion and its absence/ineffectiveness exceptions, default and independent merits grounds remain distinct. No current predicate is adjudicated.

The Court adds no new construction of §2251. No precedent is overruled and no final substantial-grounds standard is changed.

## Law After Decision

The order applies existing application-management authority to this pending request and fixes a short, conditional processing interval. It makes no change to the governing habeas, gateway or execution-stay standards and creates no automatic right to time for gathering every possible paper.

The State's application remains pending. The appellate stay retains its own force and terms; neither the two-hour supplementation limit nor the four-hour return point is an execution authorization or a new Supreme Court stay expiration.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** I join Part I's reservation of final disposition and immediate record examination. Execution before the Court can identify the lower court's substantial grounds would make review irretrievable. Lack of an explanation calls for one; it does not establish that a successive habeas claim is substantial.

I would direct the lower court to supply its stay order, reasons, identified claims and relevant successive-petition findings within two hours after service of the order, with concise party responses within two further hours. I would also administratively stay execution until the full Court resolves the dissolution application or twelve hours after service, whichever occurs first, unless the Court expressly extends protection. The Court must review the matter before expiration, and the Clerk must confirm actual notice to the executing authorities. Those are my proposed terms, not additional relief entered by the Court.

The short administrative interval would preserve the ability to decide the actual application. It would neither dissolve nor adjudge valid the Eighth Circuit stay. Delo v. Blair's equal division supplies no controlling rationale; McFarland's preparation rule and Schlup's gateway retain their own predicates. I select no final grant or denial. The Court's different implementation and decision to issue no additional execution stay are not my proposed disposition.
