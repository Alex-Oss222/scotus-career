## Public Projection

## Event

**Louisiana v. Mississippi et al., No. 121, Original — decree, December 4, 1995.** This original-action decree implements the October 31, 1995 resolution of exceptions to the Special Master's report concerning the boundary near Stack Island. The exceptions were argued October 3, 1995. No lower-court judgment is under review at this stage. The question is the precise implementation of the adjudicated boundary, the disposition of Louisiana's private-title cancellation prayer, and retention of jurisdiction.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. All nine participate; no case-specific nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The Court enters the implementing boundary decree, denies Louisiana's prayer to cancel the Houston Group's private title at the previously resolved standing scope, and retains original jurisdiction for implementation and enforcement. The unsigned decree announces no numerical vote or individual action lineup.

## Holdings

### The decree implements the boundary already adjudicated

**Operative rule:** An implementing decree in this original action must embody the former-channel boundary actually adjudicated on October 31. The authenticated twenty-two-call schedule above gives that boundary operative geographic form, subject to the stated distinction between the fixed and live reaches; it supplies no new survey finding or alteration of the earlier merits judgment.

**Authority:** The Court's decree implements Souter's October 31 Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. That prior unanimous holding controls the geographic entitlement and the requirement of an authenticated consistent description; the present decree supplies its specific relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The October 31 decision adopted the Master's former-channel recommendation but withheld an inconsistent numerical call. Appendix A expressly identifies Mississippi's counterclaim paragraph 5 as its source. That primary description confirms Point 3 at 32°48′47″ north latitude and 91°09′37″ west longitude. The match identifies the adopted schedule without redrawing the line or treating Mississippi's advocacy as Louisiana's consent. The expressly enumerated Point 22 remains part of the description despite an introductory reference to twenty-one points. Nebraska v. Wyoming distinguishes implementing an established entitlement from changing it; no new allocation or boundary theory is necessary here.

**Precedent treatment:** The October 31 decision is implemented, including its coordinate-authentication reservation. Nebraska's enforcement/modification distinction is applied without expanding it.

### Sovereignty and private ownership remain distinct

**Operative rule:** Denial of Louisiana's private-title cancellation prayer does not adjudicate competing rights of absent private owners or declare the Houston Group's title universally superior. The Court retains jurisdiction to implement and enforce the interstate decree through further lawful orders, without reopening resolved exceptions or enlarging private-title relief.

**Authority:** The Court's decree implements the private-title and retained-jurisdiction portions of the October 31 unanimous Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Mississippi v. Louisiana reserves an actual interstate controversy to this Court while preserving otherwise lawful private-title litigation. Louisiana's standing defect was left undisturbed in October; geographic precision supplies no new standing or representation of absent owners. Enforcement jurisdiction permits implementation of the line actually decided, not discretionary substitution of another boundary. The Court adopts no patent, deed-description, laches, prescription or acquiescence ground and announces no new island definition.

**Precedent treatment:** Mississippi v. Louisiana's separation of sovereign boundaries from private-title claims remains in force.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Louisiana v. Mississippi, October 31, 1995:** Implemented at its existing boundary, authentication, private-title and retained-jurisdiction scope; the unresolved numerical discrepancy is answered by the report's cited primary description.
- **Mississippi v. Louisiana:** Applied to preserve otherwise lawful private-title litigation while making the interstate boundary binding on the States.
- **Nebraska v. Wyoming:** Applied for implementation of an established entitlement; its distinct requirements for modification are unchanged.

## Law After Decision

The authenticated description now governs implementation of the adjudicated interstate boundary. The earlier island, active-channel and cessation rules remain unchanged, including the description's live-reach qualification. The decree resolves no general private ownership dispute and establishes no new boundary doctrine.

## Procedure After Action

The decree is entered in No. 121, Original. Original jurisdiction remains for implementation and enforcement by further lawful orders; the action is not dismissed. No new hearing, title-cancellation proceeding, cost award or later river-movement determination is directed.
