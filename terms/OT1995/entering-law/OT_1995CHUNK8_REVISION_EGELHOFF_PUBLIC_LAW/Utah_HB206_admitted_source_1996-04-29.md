Jane L., No. 95-1242. The Act adds section 76-7-310.5, amends sections 76-7-314 and 76-7-315, and states a separate legislative-intent limit.

## Holdings

The Act creates no holding of the Court. Its text prohibits knowing performance of the defined procedures after viability is determined under section 310.5, unless all other available abortion procedures would risk the woman's life or health. The section uses a physician's best clinical judgment based on the known particular pregnancy facts and reasonably available medical technology and information, assessing a realistic possibility of life outside the womb with or without temporary artificial support. Its definition of dilation and extraction excludes dismemberment before removal, suction curettage and suction aspiration. Its own third-degree-felony clause requires intentional, knowing and willful violation.

Amended section 314 separately retains intentional unauthorized-abortion liability, the pregnant woman's exemption from criminal liability for her own abortion, willful-violation penalties for the listed sections including sections 307, 308 and new 310.5, and misdemeanor treatment of other violations. Amended section 315 makes sections 302, 304(2), 305(2) and 310.5 inapplicable when a serious medical emergency leaves no time to comply. Section 4 confines the new viability method to section 310.5 and its purposes and disclaims effects on other statutory provisions and their litigation.
