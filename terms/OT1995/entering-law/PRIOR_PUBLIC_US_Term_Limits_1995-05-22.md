## Event

U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton / Bryant v. Hill; Nos. 93-1456, 93-1828. October Term 1994. Merits decision, 1995-05-22.

Argued November 29, 1994; decided May 22, 1995. Hill challenged section 3 of Arkansas Amendment 73. It excludes otherwise eligible congressional candidates from the general-election ballot after three House terms or two Senate terms. The trial court invalidated the congressional restriction and the Arkansas Supreme Court affirmed. Both consolidated dockets ask whether the service-based ballot restriction is an additional qualification. Article I sections 2-5, the Tenth Amendment, Powell and Storer enter; Montana concerns representation rather than qualifications.

Render form: full; the constitutional qualifications and ballot distinction requires the stated judgment and opinion topology.

Nine participating Justices supply a quorum; five votes are required for a judgment and direct controlling proposition.

House restriction: affirmance 6, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; reversal 3, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Senate restriction: the same named six and three. ## Opinion Topology

Stevens writes for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy concurs and joins the Court opinion. Thomas dissents, joined by O'Connor and Scalia. Each writing addresses both House and Senate restrictions; Kennedy's concurrence adds no judgment vote.

## Holdings

### States cannot add congressional qualifications through the ordinary ballot

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not deny an otherwise constitutionally qualified congressional candidate ordinary ballot access because of prior congressional service when that restriction adds a substantive personal qualification. The exclusive Article I qualifications cannot be supplemented indirectly through the ballot; genuine time, place and manner administration remains distinct.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes, in both consolidated dockets.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Arkansas cannot use prior congressional service to disqualify otherwise eligible candidates from the ordinary ballot. Powell establishes the fixed character of congressional qualifications and protects electors' choice of constitutionally eligible representatives against additional substantive exclusions. The national offices created by Article I do not become state offices subject to a reserved power to alter their qualifications. The Elections Clause allows administration of the manner of elections; it does not authorize a service-based disability on who may represent the people. Arkansas answers that a candidate may still seek write-in votes and that Powell concerned Congress rather than a State. But the State deliberately excludes the candidate from the ordinary ballot for a personal eligibility characteristic, achieving indirectly the additional qualification it lacks authority to impose directly. Storer preserves genuine election regulation rather than such additions. The Court leaves neutral filing, petition, support and ballot-management requirements, and Arkansas's separate state-office provisions, outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:**
- Powell, 395 U.S. 486: applies fixed congressional qualifications and elector choice to the State's service-based restriction; Congress's own exclusion was its original setting.
- Storer, 415 U.S. 724: distinguishes genuine electoral administration from added substantive qualifications; ordinary neutral election rules are preserved.
- Gregory, 501 U.S. 452: distinguishes state control of its own officers from qualification of federal representatives; its actual state-officer protection remains intact.

## Precedent Treatment

Powell's fixed national qualifications are applied to substantive state additions; Storer's genuine election-administration category remains. Gregory concerns state officers and supplies no reserved authority over qualifications for Congress. Montana and Lopez remain at their own apportionment and national/state allocation scopes; neither supplies a separate qualifications holding.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 22, substantive state additions to congressional qualifications, including this service-based ordinary-ballot restriction, are impermissible. Genuine neutral election administration remains available. No general judicial authority to require competitive elections or redesign Congress is recognized.

## Separate Writings

Kennedy emphasizes that national representatives derive their constitutional place from the people within the federal structure; States' authority over their own officers does not extend to altering that national representative relationship. He joins the common Article I holding. Thomas, joined by O'Connor and Scalia, reads Powell as limiting Congress, not the people's retained electoral authority within each State. He considers this ordinary-ballot limitation permissible while write-in eligibility remains. The dissent must defend the substantive service criterion as an exercise of retained authority, not merely invoke a procedural label.

## Procedure After Action

The Arkansas judgment stands in both consolidated dockets. Section 3's congressional service-based ballot exclusions remain unenforceable; ordinary lawful state election administration continues. No further Supreme Court procedural event is created.
