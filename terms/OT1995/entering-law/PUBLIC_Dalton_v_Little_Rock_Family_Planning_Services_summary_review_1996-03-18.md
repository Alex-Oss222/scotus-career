## Event

Dalton v. Little Rock Family Planning Services, No. 95-1025. Summary review on certiorari petition, March 18, 1996; October Term 1995. No oral argument was held. The Court reviews the Eighth Circuit's July 25, 1995 judgment, 60 F.3d 497, affirming the Eastern District of Arkansas, 860 F. Supp. 609. Rehearing below was denied September 22, 1995.

The first petition question concerns whether a Medicaid-participating State must fund medically necessary abortions for eligible women whose pregnancies result from rape or incest despite its contrary constitution. The second challenges the scope of the injunction and declaration against Arkansas Amendment 68. Review is limited to the second question.

Amendment 68 §1 prohibits public abortion funding except to save the mother's life. Section 2 declares a policy protecting unborn life from conception until birth to the extent the Federal Constitution permits. Section 3 excludes contraceptives from the amendment's operation and disclaims requiring an appropriation. Section 1 operates outside Medicaid as well as within it.

The published July 25, 1994 decree enjoined the amendment in its entirety for as long as Arkansas accepts Medicaid funds and separately enjoined inconsistent abortion-funding provisions of its Medicaid plan. An unpublished July 27, 1994 stay-denial order declared the amendment null, void and of no effect. The court of appeals sustained the broad relief on Arkansas inseverability grounds. The Arkansas appeal was heard with a Nebraska appeal below; only the Arkansas petition is decided here.

The applicable FY1995 Medicaid authority and conditions continue under Pub. L. 104-91 Title I. Section 101 retains the current-rate ceiling and the applicable lower-rate qualifications when House and Senate amounts differ or only one House has acted. Sections 102–105 preserve the pertinent appropriation's extent and manner, prohibit initiation or resumption of projects unavailable in FY1995, exclude newly proposed cross-account conditions absent from the applicable FY1995 Act, and cover obligations or expenditures during the authorized period. Unless Title I or the applicable appropriations Act otherwise provides, §106 ends the authority at the earliest of an appropriation for the covered activity, enactment of the applicable appropriations Act without that activity, or September 30, 1996. Section 110 makes the covered period begin December 16, 1995. The general continuing-resolution deadline is not this account's deadline. No amount of Medicaid funding is adjudicated.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate in the petition consideration and summary decision.

## Public Action

Certiorari is granted limited to the second question and otherwise denied. In a separate unanimous summary merits disposition, the Court vacates the judgment only insofar as it affirms invalidation or suspension of Amendment 68 beyond applications conflicting with the Medicaid obligation left unreviewed, and remands for corresponding declaratory and injunctive relief. The Court accepts the lower funding construction for this purpose without deciding it.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Partial summary vacatur and remand, 9–0, on the scope of relief. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This published count concerns the merits disposition only.

The lower courts must correct both the July 25 whole-amendment injunction and the July 27 blanket declaration. They must preserve the prohibition against using Amendment 68 to refuse medically necessary abortions for Medicaid-eligible rape/incest patients while Arkansas participates in Medicaid and the applicable federal obligation requires that coverage. The separate protection against inconsistent state-plan funding provisions remains operative to that extent. No section of the amendment may be used to evade that surviving protection.

Every severable nonconflicting application must remain outside the decree. The Court awards no damages or past reimbursement, orders no payment for every abortion, requires no continued state participation in Medicaid, and directs no new hearing or trial. No separate Nebraska judgment or independent constitutional challenge to the crime-victim program is decided.

## Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join both Part I, addressing the limited question and excessive reach of the decree, and Part II, specifying retained protection, temporal limits and remand. ## Holdings

### Relief must follow the established federal conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** When the established Medicaid conflict concerns particular applications of a state funding restriction, federal declaratory and injunctive relief must protect those applications without invalidating or suspending severable applications for which no conflict has been established. The July 25 injunction and July 27 declaration therefore cannot reach Amendment 68 in its entirety. The funding obligation underlying that conclusion is accepted without deciding the first petition question.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine participating Justices identified in Opinion Topology. The rule has nine direct joins and requires no combination of separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Supremacy Clause makes federal law controlling where the state rule conflicts with it. It does not eliminate every other operation of that rule. The lower courts' Medicaid construction, left unreviewed, supports protecting the specified eligible patients against contrary administration. It supplies no finding that every non-Medicaid public-funding application violates federal law. Harris v. McRae leaves the precise statutory funding issue open; denial of review supplies no answer to it.

The lower courts relied on Arkansas inseverability law and declined to write a new exception into the amendment. That objection does not justify this decree. Confining federal relief to conflicting applications leaves the enacted text in place; it neither creates a new state appropriation nor inserts an exception. Once §1 retains lawful applications, the premise that §§2–3 must lose every function because §1 has disappeared cannot support their blanket invalidation. Any genuinely remaining state-law severability question remains for the proper court. The State may not use another section to evade the Medicaid protection left standing.

NTEU permits sufficiently broad relief when the proved defect and an indivisible prohibition require it, while excluding unrelated claims from the decree. Here the Medicaid conflict does not establish a defect in every application. Madsen's remedial holdings likewise preserve lawful independent operation without imposing its speech-specific test on this case. The Court adopts no general rule against broad relief and no new facial-review formula. The crime-victim program illustrates §1's operation outside Medicaid; its funding source and independent constitutional validity are not decided.

### Prospective protection follows the applicable obligation

**Holding and operative rule:** The confined decree must preserve the adjudicated Medicaid protection while Arkansas participates and controlling federal law requires the coverage. It may not suspend unrelated applications merely because the State continues participating, regardless of a later change in the governing conditions. An actual lawful change may support appropriate modification; this decision declares no current lapse or automatic dissolution.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by the same nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The decree's continued reach must correspond to the federal obligation it enforces. Pub. L. 104-91 continues the relevant conditions under the account-specific qualifications stated above; locating that continuation establishes no intervening repeal or expiration. The present remand accordingly implements a decided scope correction while retaining the funding protection left unreviewed. A possible future change is not an event the Court can declare now. The receiving courts must express the actual obligation and its limits, without adopting a replacement funding policy or predicting a later legislative choice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither the underlying Title XIX/Hyde construction nor a constitutional entitlement to public abortion funding. It does not adjudicate the constitutionality of a direct abortion prohibition or foreclose a properly preserved constitutional challenge to a remaining application supported by a developed record. Casey's complete standard and Fargo's application remain unchanged; their access protections are not transformed into a funding entitlement. No independent standing, enforcement or jurisdictional issue drawn from the separate Nebraska litigation is decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Harris v. McRae, 448 U.S. 297:** Preserves its actual limits; the precise statutory funding question left open there is not decided here.
- **United States v. National Treasury Employees Union, February 22, 1995:** Applies its distinction between relief supported by the proved violation and relief for unrelated claims; its protection against the indivisible prohibition in that case does not establish invalidity of every Amendment 68 application.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc., June 30, 1994:** Applies the bounded remedial principle preserving independent lawful operation; its content-neutral-injunction speech test is not extended to Medicaid funding.
- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, and Fargo Women's Health Organization v. Schafer, February 13, 1995:** Leave their access, proof and remedy rules intact. Neither supplies a statutory or constitutional funding entitlement in this limited review.

## Law After Decision

The federal decree must reach the actual Medicaid conflict and preserve every severable nonconflicting application of Amendment 68. Its policy and no-appropriation provisions are not invalidated wholesale, but cannot defeat the surviving federal protection. No authoritative new construction of every Arkansas severability question results.

The lower funding ruling remains operative for the specified Medicaid-eligible patients. It does not become a Supreme Court holding through denial of the first question. Current funding conditions continue under their actual statutory qualifications; future relief depends on the applicable obligation rather than participation alone. No precedent is overruled, general facial-review rule adopted, or current injunction automatically dissolved.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court review is complete. The case returns through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court to conform the July 25 injunction and July 27 declaration to the conflict-specific protection stated above. The merits correction is final; the remand is for its implementation, with no reopening of the funding question ordered by this Court. Ordinary mandate procedures apply. No special mandate date, automatic hearing, guaranteed trial or later Supreme Court event is prescribed.
