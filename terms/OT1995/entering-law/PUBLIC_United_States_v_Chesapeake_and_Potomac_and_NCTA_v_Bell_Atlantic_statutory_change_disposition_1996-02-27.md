## Event

United States v. Chesapeake & Potomac Telephone Co. of Virginia, No. 94-1893, and National Cable Television Assn., Inc. v. Bell Atlantic Corp., No. 94-1900; 516 U.S. 415. Post-argument statutory-change disposition, February 27, 1996. Argued December 6, 1995. On writs of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 42 F.3d 181. The two dockets are disposed of together.

The Fourth Circuit affirmed a judgment declaring former 47 U.S.C. §533(b)'s telephone-company cable restriction unconstitutional. The amended District Court order enjoined enforcement against the named plaintiffs. After argument, Congress repealed the challenged provision. The immediate question is whether the litigation remains live and what disposition should precede any constitutional ruling.

The Government seeks vacatur and dismissal, asserting that repeal ends the prospective dispute and that ordinary cable service is immediately available even over telephone wires. Respondents contend that their proposed Alexandria open-video service remains impeded pending FCC rules; alternatively, they oppose vacatur because the Government procured repeal and seek to retain the injunction for a proposed Rule 60(b) clarification. The Government disputes the asserted continuing prohibition and treats the successor dispute as distinct. No clarification has been granted. The complaint does not expressly demand damages, and the Government describes it as seeking no retrospective relief.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate. The per curiam disposition does not disclose an individual lineup or numerical tally.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Fourth Circuit judgment in both dockets and remands to that court to consider whether the cases are moot in light of the Telecommunications Act of 1996.

## Judgment & Remedy

The same appellate vacatur and remand applies to Nos. 94-1893 and 94-1900. The Fourth Circuit must consider whether a live controversy survives. It may obtain appropriate further District Court proceedings if needed; this Court does not require a further remand. If no live controversy remains, the appellate court must direct appropriate dismissal and address the remaining orders under applicable vacatur doctrine. If a controversy survives, proceedings must remain confined to it.

The Court neither dismisses the actions now nor vacates the District Court judgment. It does not extend the old injunction to the new statute, guarantee amendment or relief, or affirm either statute's constitutionality.

## Opinion Topology

A per curiam opinion states the Court's limited procedural ground. No separate writing or individual join is announced.

## Holdings

**Intervening repeal requires reconsideration of the surviving controversy.** Under 28 U.S.C. §2106, the material statutory change and unresolved effect on these claims justify vacating the appellate judgment and remanding for consideration of mootness before constitutional adjudication. The per curiam opinion of the Court supplies authority for this case-specific disposition; it establishes neither final mootness nor a categorical entitlement to equitable vacatur.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 302(b)(1) repealed the provision adjudicated below. Church of Scientology requires attention to meaningful remaining relief; Northeastern Florida asks whether a successor actually continues the challenged injury. Neither makes repeal conclusive or treats every dispute about the same service as the same controversy.

The parties dispute the new regime's effect. Communications Act §653(a)(1), 47 U.S.C. §573(a)(1), permits a local exchange carrier to offer cable service through a complying open video system. Its separate permission for a cable operator or other person is qualified by permissible Commission regulations. Reduced regulatory burdens require certification of compliance with subsection (b) regulations and Commission approval. Section 651(a)(3), 47 U.S.C. §571(a)(3), applies ordinary Title VI to nonradio, non-common-carriage programming unless provided through an open video system with approved certification. The Commission has six months to complete the specified rulemaking, including reconsideration. It must publish notice of receipt of a certification and act to approve or disapprove it within ten days after receipt. Section 302(b)(3)'s termination of video-dialtone rules does not require termination of an already approved system. These distinctions establish neither a six-month blanket prohibition nor this proposed system's present compliance.

Bancorp separates authority to dispose of a judgment from equitable entitlement to erase it. Respondents' voluntary-surrender argument and the Government's repeal argument therefore require consideration in their actual setting. Today's appellate vacatur rests on the enacted change and unresolved threshold application, not on an assumed final mootness finding or automatic Munsingwear relief. The Fourth Circuit can assess the remaining controversy and determine what further proceedings are necessary. The former statute's constitutional merits, the successor scheme's constitutionality, ultimate treatment of the trial judgment, and entitlement to any proposed relief remain undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

- Church of Scientology of California v. United States, 506 U.S. 9: applies the requirement to examine meaningful partial relief, without establishing entitlement to relief.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656: applies the distinction between actual continuing injury and a replacement that ends it; the old injunction does not automatically extend.
- U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18: preserves the separate equitable inquiry. Its ordinary voluntary-settlement rule, exceptional-circumstances qualification and distinction for events outside the movant's choice remain intact.
- United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36: its judgment-clearing remedy does not establish the antecedent mootness finding missing here.
- Anderson v. Green, 513 U.S. 557: its lost-approval and contingent-program setting does not establish mootness or trial-judgment vacatur in these cases.
- Lawrence v. Chater, 516 U.S. 163: preserves independently justified reconsideration and the receiving court's judicial responsibility; its petition-stage rule is not mechanically extended to this disposition.

## Law After Decision

The repealed restriction cannot be treated as unchanged governing law. The appellate judgment no longer stands, and the Fourth Circuit must assess whether the original controversy and meaningful lawful relief survive under the enacted regime. The Court creates no new speech tier, successor-statute constitutional rule, or automatic rule awarding or denying vacatur after legislative change. Existing mootness and equitable-vacatur law remains applicable within its stated limits.
