## Standards and Tests selection: Integrated fair use of commercial parody

### Integrated fair use of commercial parody

**Governing question:** How §107 applies to claimed parody and its borrowing and market effects.

**Current rule:** Consider together purpose and character, the copyrighted work’s nature, the amount and substantiality taken, and effect on the potential market or value. Commercial parody may qualify without a dispositive presumption of unfairness. Parody’s particular borrowing justification requires reasonably perceptible criticism directed at the original; satire directed elsewhere lacks that justification merely as satire but may establish fair use under the factors. Transformation informs purpose without absorbing the other factors. Requesting or being denied permission does not defeat an otherwise fair use. Creative character remains relevant, though it contributes little to separating fair from unfair parody, which ordinarily uses recognizable expression. Quantity and qualitative importance must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; recognizable or central material is not excessive solely because it is central. Repetition and potential substitution remain relevant, with no fixed allowance. For transformative parody, commerciality alone creates no market-harm presumption. Examine substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including widespread comparable conduct; lost demand caused by criticism or loss of licensing the critical attack itself is not cognizable substitution. Supported nonparody derivative markets remain relevant.

## Standards and Tests selection: Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary

### Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary

**Governing question:** What instructions govern authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary?

**Current rule:** Commentary interpreting or explaining a Guideline is authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to a federal statute, inconsistent with the Guideline or a plainly erroneous reading of it. Commentary cannot amend contrary text; an earlier judicial interpretation does not alone defeat a later permissible Commission interpretation. Amendment 433's exclusion of unlawful firearm possession by a felon permissibly interprets crime of violence for defendants to whom it applies. Governing version and temporal application remain separate; the decision does not resolve them. Williams's distinct departure-error consequences remain operative.

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: [Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Johnson_v_Jones_merits_1995-06-12.md#public-projection), 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: [Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kimberlin_v_Quinlan_merits_1995-06-12.md#public-projection), 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-12; the component authorities identified above.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure

### Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Clergy-led prayer organized by public secondary-school officials for the official graduation program.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Independently sufficient sponsorship ground: [Lee v. Weisman, “Officially organized graduation worship is forbidden”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Lee_v_Weisman_merits_1992-06-24.md), June 24, 1992; Blackmun's Part II joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter.
- Independently sufficient pressure ground: *Lee*, “The school-directed ceremony also imposes impermissible religious pressure”; Kennedy's concurrence joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. Each rule has five direct supporters; neither depends on Marks or on treating Stone as joining Kennedy.

**Present operation effective:** *Lee*, June 24, 1992, for the identified secondary-school graduation practice.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision applies existing school-worship and Lemon principles at this recurring graduation setting without creating a replacement establishment test. Private prayer and genuinely private equal-access speech remain distinct. Adult legislative prayer, general accommodation, funding and denominational aid are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** [Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992) — Official school prayer](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#lee-v-weisman-505-us-577-1992-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

### Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

**Governing question:** When an otherwise discriminatory tax can compensate for a tax already borne by the corresponding intrastate activity.

**Current rule:** The State must identify the actual intrastate burden for which it compensates, show that the interstate charge roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and establish that the taxed events are substantially equivalent, reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies for one another. General income taxation and waste disposal are not such equivalent events. For a use tax said to equalize local sales taxes, the comparison must use the sales tax actually imposed in the affected locality. Statewide averages and burdens elsewhere cannot cure an excess there. Equal or lower use-tax burdens in other localities are not invalidated merely because an excess exists elsewhere; relief addresses the established inequality through lawful remedial procedures.

**Authority by component:**

- Three compensatory-tax predicates: [Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality — General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Oregon_Waste_Systems_Inc_v_Department_of_Environmental_Quality_merits_1994-04-04.md#public-projection), 1994-04-04. Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.
- Local comparison, bounded invalidity and relief: [Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman — The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality; Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Associated_Industries_of_Missouri_v_Lohman_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Blackmun agrees with the judgment without joining this full framework.

**Present operation effective:** Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman, 1994-05-23.

**Holdings navigation:** [General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#general-income-taxes-do-not-establish-a-compensatory-disposal-tax); [The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-compensating-burden-must-exist-in-the-affected-locality); [Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#relief-must-address-the-established-inequality-through-lawful-state-procedures).

## Standing State selection: 3. Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the [opening Standing State](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/13b19ee463d16fb4377d846e5b058599f886bed3/state/STANDING_STATE.md#3-standing-practices) and the [OT1993 Term Continuity Note](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/workspace/continuity.md#standing-practices). #### [American Dredging Co. v. Miller, 510 U.S. 443](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/American_Dredging_Co_v_Miller_merits_1994-02-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1950.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### State forum administration under the saving clause and Jones Act

**Holding:** A competent state court hearing this domestic Jones Act and accompanying in personam maritime action need not employ the federal doctrine permitting dismissal in favor of a more convenient forum; Louisiana may retain the action under Article 123(C). That choice changes neither federal maritime liability and remedies nor jurisdiction, and it decides no independent discrimination, Commerce Clause, or foreign-party question.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory/state-forum portion of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven Justices adopt this sufficient ground for affirmance. Stevens joins this portion and the judgment, but not the separate Jensen analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Missouri ex rel. Southern Railway Co. v. Mayfield, 340 U.S. 1: its state-forum choice for FELA actions carries through the Jones Act; its requirement of impartial local policy, without discrimination against federal claims or sister-state citizens, remains intact. A residence distinction that treats all nonresidents alike is not equated with citizenship discrimination.
- Bainbridge v. Merchants & Miners Transportation Co., 287 U.S. 278: applied to preserve state-court Jones Act venue rules; venue and discretionary dismissal remain distinct.

**Independent alternative holding:** Louisiana's refusal to dismiss this domestic in personam maritime action for convenience neither materially prejudices a characteristic feature of general maritime law nor interferes with its proper harmony and uniformity. Forum non conveniens governs forum administration rather than primary maritime obligations; a procedural label alone, however, does not protect a state rule that impairs a substantive maritime right or an integral burden or remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The Jensen portion of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stevens does not join it. Souter also supplies a separate close-classification qualification without withdrawing this join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen, 244 U.S. 205: applied and left in force; neither characteristic-feature protection nor necessary maritime uniformity requires the dismissal procedure here.
- Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501, and Piper Aircraft Co. v. Reyno, 454 U.S. 235: their federal convenience inquiry remains applicable in the courts governed by it, including the adequate-alternative-forum prerequisite, relevant private/public considerations, appropriate weight to forum choice, and abuse-of-discretion review; it is not imposed on this state court.
- Miles v. Apex Marine Corp., 498 U.S. 19: its statute-bounded uniformity of substantive maritime recovery is preserved, not converted into a uniform forum-management command.
- Sun Oil Co. v. Wortman, 486 U.S. 717: its context-sensitive substance/procedure distinction supports attention to legal function; it supplies no categorical maritime exemption for procedural labels.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns a competent state court and domestic in personam claims. Maritime liability, remedies, integral burdens, independent discrimination and Commerce Clause objections, and foreign-party controversies are not resolved by treating forum administration as procedural. Jensen remains in force.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Louisiana Supreme Court is affirmed, preserving its reversal of the convenience dismissal and remand. The injury claims may proceed in state court. No interstate transfer, federal convenience reweighing or liability award is ordered; the unpursued personal-jurisdiction objection is not decided.


## Verbatim Holdings selection: Campbell v.

Source: `state/holdings/business-and-commercial-law.md`.

#### [Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Campbell_v_Acuff_Rose_Music_Inc_merits_1994-03-07.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1292.

**Decided:** 1994-03-07.

##### Commercial parody and the integrated statutory inquiry

**Holding:** Section 107 requires the four fair-use factors to be considered together; commercial parody may qualify, and neither profit motive nor a parody label settles the inquiry or creates a dispositive presumption of unfairness. The work must reasonably permit perception of original-directed criticism to claim parody's particular justification for borrowing; satire directed elsewhere lacks that justification merely as satire but may still establish fair use under the actual factors, and requesting or being denied permission does not defeat an otherwise fair use.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417: its contextual statutory inquiry is applied; its commerciality language does not establish a universal presumption against sold criticism, and its home-time-shifting result remains intact.
- Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539: distinguished as commercial appropriation in a protected publication market; its four-factor method remains binding, without a categorical rule against commercial parody.

##### The original song remains protected creative expression

**Holding:** The published original song's creative nature weighs within §107's second factor and is not erased by the claim of parody, but it contributes little to distinguishing fair from unfair parody because parody ordinarily must use recognizable expressive works. Neither publication nor creativity categorically determines fairness, and the four factors remain part of one inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: its attention to creative character remains; its first-publication concern does not determine this published-song parody, and no general unpublished-work rule is decided.

##### The lyrical reference is justified; the extent of musical borrowing remains open

**Holding:** Under §107's third factor, both the quantity and qualitative importance of the borrowing must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; taking a recognizable opening or the original's core is not excessive solely because it is central. The brief identifying lyrical reference followed by substantially different critical lyrics takes no more than the parody reasonably requires, but whether the repeated musical borrowing is excessive remains for further consideration with purpose and potential substitution; no fixed allowance or sampling violation is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: qualitative substantiality remains important, but its application to valuable excerpts is distinguished where recognizable borrowing serves criticism; no automatic permission to take the whole or repeat the core follows.

##### Market substitution differs from harm caused by criticism

**Holding:** For this transformative parody, commerciality alone does not raise a presumption of market harm; §107 requires examination of substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including the effect of widespread comparable conduct. Harm because criticism diminishes demand and foregone licensing of the critical attack itself are not cognizable substitutive injury, but a supported nonparody rap-derivative market remains relevant and this record proves neither its injury nor its absence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sony, 464 U.S. 417: commercial market-harm language is confined to its context; no universal presumption follows for this transformative use.
- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: protection of legitimate existing and potential derivative markets remains; criticism injury and lost permission for criticism are distinguished from substitution.

##### The defense burden and limited remand survive correction of the presumptions

**Holding:** Fair use remains an affirmative defense, and defendants seeking summary judgment must establish it on a record permitting judgment as a matter of law; rejection of the appellate commerciality and harm presumptions does not shift that burden. The Sixth Circuit's adverse fair-use judgment is reversed and remanded without reinstating defense summary judgment, because justified musical taking and the potentially material nonparody derivative market remain unresolved; no final infringement, royalty, damages, or injunction ruling follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: applied to preserve the fair-use defense burden and the requirement of sufficient facts for a conclusive judicial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Transformation is neither indispensable to every fair use nor a substitute for the integrated four-factor inquiry. Requesting or being refused permission supplies no veto over otherwise fair use. No first-publication claim, bad faith from the disputed release/request sequence, independent sampling violation or constitutional immunity is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded without reinstating defense summary judgment. The justified extent of musical borrowing and the legitimate nonparody derivative market require further consideration. No final fair-use or infringement judgment, compulsory license, royalty, damages or injunction is directed; unappealed collateral claims remain undisturbed.

#### [NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. / Ludwig v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/NationsBank_Ludwig_v_Variable_Annuity_Life_Insurance_Co_merits_1995-01-18.md)

##### Reasoned approval of annuity brokerage falls within the banking power

**Holding:** The business of banking under 12 U.S.C. §24 Seventh is not exhausted by its enumerated activities, and the Comptroller's reasonable construction may include financial-intermediary agency sales of the fixed, variable and combined annuity products covered by this approval. The construction must remain within banking and its incidental powers and comply with enacted restrictions; this conditioned brokerage approval authorizes no underwriting, issuance or guarantee of the insurers' obligations and no unrelated commercial enterprise.

### Current-term authority: A_St_P_C_v_B_C_merits_1995-11-20

Source: `terms/OT1995/records/A_St_P_C_v_B_C_merits_1995-11-20.md`.

## Event

A. St. P. C. v. B. C., No. 94-7810. Decided November 20, 1995, on merits review of State in the Interest of A.C., 643 So. 2d 743 (La. 1994), Louisiana No. 93-CA-1125, on rehearing October 17, 1994. The question is whether procedural due process permits a bare-preponderance finding of parental sexual abuse to trigger La. Rev. Stat. 9:364(D)'s prohibition of all visitation and contact, or requires clear and convincing proof.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court resolves the submitted merits question and issues the judgment and mandate below.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** The Louisiana Supreme Court's judgment is affirmed on the federal proof-burden ground, 5–4. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would reverse that ground and remand.

The mandate returns the matter for state proceedings consistent with the heightened-proof requirement. It does not impose a new visitation arrangement, compel treatment or an admission, or decide whether abuse occurred. The Louisiana judgment separately invalidated, under state separation of powers, the command to give the abused child's therapist greater weight than other visitation testimony when that therapist is a licensed mental-health professional with training, experience and expertise in treating sexual-abuse victims; that state ruling remains undisturbed. Independently lawful child-protection measures remain available; no unrestricted contact is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author and joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join in full | Heightened statutory trigger, bounded affirmance and protective limits |
| Dissent | O'Connor; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join | Reject the categorical intermediate-proof requirement; reverse that ground and remand without approving the old notice defect |

## Holdings

### Clear and convincing proof must precede this statutory all-contact restriction

**Operative rule:** Before La. Rev. Stat. 9:364(D)'s severe restriction may be imposed, it must be proved by clear and convincing evidence that the parent sexually abused his or her child or children. A bare-preponderance finding cannot trigger mandatory loss of all visitation and contact until a contradictory (adversarial) hearing establishes both successful completion of a program designed for sexual abusers and that supervised visitation is in the children's best interests. The statutory treatment definition requires evaluation and psychotherapy specifically designed for perpetrators of family violence and conducted by licensed mental-health professionals.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices directly adopt the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews requires examination of the actual loss, the risk of error and the State's protective interest. The consequence here is complete exclusion from a child's life with no fixed endpoint, followed, if two conditions are met, only by supervised contact. Santosky explains why ordinary preponderance may allocate too much risk to a parent facing a grave destruction of family relations. Although the legal relationship survives here, the scope and potentially indefinite duration of this particular exclusion warrant intermediate proof before it begins. The Court extends that method to this statutory consequence, not to every custody or visitation decision.

Protection of the child remains essential. Existing authority for investigation, emergency intervention, temporary separation and supported supervised restrictions permits protective action under the requirements governing each measure while the severe statutory predicate is established. Clear and convincing proof is not a prerequisite to every protective act. The contrary view correctly identifies danger from erroneous permission, but it does not explain why imposing this complete, potentially indefinite regime requires only ordinary preponderance when those independent protections remain available. Foucha and Heller reinforce attention to the grounds and reliability of serious deprivation within their distinct commitment-law settings; they do not already prescribe this parental-contact rule.

The earlier bare-preponderance finding, entered without notice of these additional consequences, cannot supply the required predicate. That conclusion is not a finding that abuse did not occur. The Louisiana judgment is affirmed on this proof-burden ground; the Court neither rewrites the statute nor orders contact. Lawful state implementation, including any available conforming construction, remains for the state courts. No general periodic-review schedule, restoration procedure, treatment-admission condition, fee ruling or proof standard for lesser restrictions is created.

**Precedent treatment:** Mathews is applied to the specific deprivation; Santosky's error-allocation method is extended to this statutory cutoff while its termination holding remains intact. Foucha and Heller are applied only by the stated analogy; their distinct operative rules are unchanged.

## Law After Decision

Ordinary investigations, emergency intervention, temporary protection and lesser custody or supervised-visitation measures remain subject to their existing legal requirements. ## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor dissents, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The dissent would reverse the categorical requirement of clear and convincing proof and remand for proper consideration of the protective procedure. Santosky concerned complete and irrevocable termination; here parental status survives, and treatment followed by a judicial best-interests determination permits supervised contact. Under Mathews, the process must be assessed as a whole, including danger to a child from an erroneous decision permitting renewed exposure. The severity or duration of a restriction demands adequate process but does not automatically import termination's proof burden.

The dissent distinguishes Foucha's expired confinement predicate and Heller's unjustified disparity between commitment classes. Neither supplies this new categorical rule. All four recognize the serious contact loss and reject immediate reliance on the old finding entered without notice of these additional consequences. They do not validate every indefinite restriction, make periodic review a cure for every deprivation, find that abuse occurred, or direct enforcement on the defective notice premise. Their proposed reversal would leave those questions and lawful protective measures for further state proceedings.

## Holdings

### Ordinary parole availability defeats the asserted release-prediction entitlement

**Operative rule:** Simmons's right to explain legal parole ineligibility does not require a favorable prediction about eventual release when the noncapital alternative permits ordinary parole. The rejection of that asserted entitlement is affirmed; this conclusion neither categorically bars truthful parole evidence nor authorizes a misleading description of the lawful sentencing alternative.

**Authority:** Part I-A of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this eligibility-versus-prediction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Simmons v. South Carolina protects an effective opportunity to tell the jury that the only lawful noncapital sentence excludes ordinary parole when the State raises future dangerousness. That legal fact answers the State's sentencing case; speculative clemency does not change it. Here ordinary parole was legally available. A prediction that release is improbable therefore cannot be substituted for Simmons's absent predicate. The Court does not adopt the lower court's suggestion that danger to inmates universally defeats Simmons. The claim fails because of ordinary parole availability, without reaching harmlessness.

**Precedent treatment:** Simmons retains its legal-ineligibility rule and is distinguished on its express predicate, not confined by a new prison-danger exception.

### The proposed forecast-instruction extension is independently unavailable under Teague

**Operative rule:** Federal habeas relief on a proposed constitutional entitlement to a favorable parole-improbability instruction is unavailable here because no basis establishes that this different rule was dictated when Allridge's conviction became final before Penry in 1989, or that either then-existing Teague exception applies: rules excluding conduct, classes of persons, or punishments from the State's substantive power, or watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. This is an independent ground for rejecting the proposed extension, not a new rule governing accurate information about a fixed statutory minimum.

**Authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight directly adopt this alternative ground. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Teague requires claim-specific identification of law dictated at finality. Simmons's separate legal-ineligibility premise cannot establish a broader forecast right where ordinary parole remains available. The record supplies no prefinality requirement for that proposed prediction instruction. As Gilmore v. Taylor and Caspari v. Bohlen explain, the then-existing exceptions concern substantive limits on the State's power to punish and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. The requested forecast instruction regulates the jury's information; it does not remove Allridge's conduct, a class to which he belongs, or his punishment from the State's substantive power. Nothing establishes it as a watershed accuracy-and-fairness procedure; asserted usefulness in answering future-danger evidence does not itself meet that demanding category. Neither exception supplies a route to this requested extension. A properly presented claim about a fixed legal minimum would require its own preservation and retroactivity inquiry; this decision does not resolve such a claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Teague's dictated-at-finality requirement and its two then-existing exceptions are applied at the scope preserved in Gilmore and Caspari; neither exception is created, abolished, or generally narrowed. Simmons is not enlarged into a parole-forecast rule.

### Admitted mitigation must be assessed by its possible independent culpability significance

**Operative rule:** A court may not reject a preserved claim for effective consideration of admitted capital mitigation merely because the evidence fails to establish a uniquely severe permanent disability to which the crime is conclusively attributable. When the actual evidence supports material mitigation of culpability independent of deliberateness or future danger, the court must determine whether, considering the charge, evidence, and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that jurors could not give it effect while answering the special issues truthfully; mere possibility is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this correction of the reviewing rule and bounded remand, without finding an actual instructional violation.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham v. Collins requires an effective sentencing route for record-supported mitigation with independent culpability significance and rejects a uniquely severe permanent-handicap restriction. Its background holding does not require that the evidence lack all relevance to future danger. Johnson v. Texas applies the whole-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry: admission and partial future-danger relevance do not suffice if truthful special-issue answers prevent the supported mitigating use. Those rules preserve Jurek's conditional approval of the Texas scheme while enforcing Lockett and Eddings's requirement that relevant mitigation receive lawful effect.

The father testified about alleged mental illness and prior prison abuse. The lower court rejected the claim because his nonexpert account failed to establish the crime-attribution and severe-disability linkage. That categorical requirement is no longer a lawful reviewing premise. His lack of diagnostic expertise limits what the testimony proves; it does not erase admitted lay evidence or establish that no abuse occurred.

The competing contention—that the testimony may still be weak, unsupported, or adequately usable under the actual charge—remains for proper review. The Court has not found psychiatric illness, credibility, causation, material independent mitigating force, or the absence of an effective sentencing route. The jury need not assign favorable weight. No categorical additional special issue, facial invalidity, or new general mitigation category follows. Because the stated lower ground is legally insufficient, the rejection of this admitted-evidence claim is vacated and remanded.

**Precedent treatment:** Graham's youth and background rules and prefinality analysis are applied; its fractured positive-character component is not enlarged. Johnson and Boyde supply the functional reasonable-likelihood inquiry. Tuggle preserves the distinction between correcting an appellate rationale and finding a present entitlement to a writ.

### Unoffered medical evidence supplies no independent instruction entitlement

**Operative rule:** A defendant cannot obtain a mitigation instruction on the assumed content of medical evidence never presented merely because presenting it might also support future dangerousness. The independent instruction claim based on that unoffered evidence fails, while a supported challenge to an actual unconstitutional exclusion or legal impediment remains distinct and is not decided here.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight adopt the rationale; Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of this component in the judgment only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Delo v. Lashley permits a requirement of some evidence supporting a requested mitigating circumstance while protecting meaningful use of mitigation actually offered. Evidence carrying both mitigating and aggravating implications can create a difficult strategic choice. That concern alone does not establish the missing diagnosis, its content, admissibility, or mitigating significance, and does not require jurors to act on assumed proof. The separate admitted-evidence inquiry continues under Part II. This ruling does not compel a defendant to present evidence formally excluded by an unconstitutional rule, foreclose a supported challenge to an actual legal barrier, or adjudicate a new ineffective-assistance claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Delo's evidentiary predicate is applied without an expert-only rule, an adverse inference from silence, or a reduction of the State's aggravation burden. Graham's protection of supported mitigation remains intact.

### Correcting the reviewing standard does not itself require a sentencing writ

**Operative rule:** The admitted-evidence mitigation component returns for review of the actual evidence, charge, prefinality law, and any properly available defenses and error-effect inquiry; vacatur alone establishes neither an available violation nor entitlement to relief. If a defect ultimately requires correction, relief concerns punishment through adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt untouched; no present writ, hearing, release, stay, deadline, or execution authorization issues.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the bounded remand and reservations.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham's prefinality analysis bars treating its established functional rule as newly created merely because another defendant invokes it. A materially new extension nevertheless requires its own Teague inquiry. The documented instruction request proves that a request was made, not exhaustion or preservation of every reformulated theory. Applicable factual presumptions and genuinely available exhaustion, default, and other habeas defenses remain; no forfeited defense is revived and no settled issue reopened.

Tuggle corrects a discrete legally insufficient review ground without resolving the underlying violation or issuing a writ. That distinction governs this remand too. Brecht governs preserved Doyle error and O'Neal preserved personal-intent instructional error. Neither already selects a universal collateral harmlessness rule for capital mitigation. The Court reserves whether a separate error-effect inquiry is required here, its applicable standard, the actual effect, and ultimate relief. The complete charge, relevant testimony, claim-specific procedural rulings, and any properly preserved error-effect submissions are needed before further relief. Unreviewed Brady and lesser-offense claims are untouched.

**Precedent treatment:** Tuggle's review-versus-relief distinction is applied. Brecht and O'Neal retain their category-specific Chapman holdings; neither they nor Chapman's direct-review rule are extended by the Court to this category.

## Event

**American Life League v. Reno. Decided November 13, 1995.** On review of the Fourth Circuit's judgment in No. 94-1869, affirming dismissal with prejudice of the second amended complaint under Rule 12(b)(6). The questions concern congressional authority, speech and association, facial vagueness and overbreadth, constitutional religious exercise, and RFRA's distinct protection against threatened enforcement. The plaintiffs allege future prayer, counseling, demonstrations and peaceful physical obstruction; this is not a prosecution of adjudged violent conduct.

The challenged reproductive-service provisions of the Freedom of Access to Clinic Entrances Act, 18 U.S.C. §248, apply to conduct on or after May 26, 1994. Section (a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and either action because a person is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive health services, or action intended to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Section (a)(3)'s challenged branch separately covers intentional damage or destruction of a facility's property, or attempt, because the facility provides those services. The parallel worship branches are outside this review.

Interference means restricting freedom of movement; intimidation means reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another; physical obstruction means making ingress to or egress from a reproductive-service facility or place of worship impassable, or passage to or from it unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services relating to reproduction, pregnancy or its termination, provided in a hospital, clinic, physician's office or other facility. The inclusive facility definition covers those reproductive-service establishments and the building or structure in which the facility is located; it does not separately add grounds or worship premises. The passage provisions do not depend on that addition and create no indoor-only limit or outdoor immunity. A parent or legal guardian of a minor is exempt from **all penalties and civil remedies under §248**, including private damages and governmental or private injunctions, for the described activities insofar as they are directed **exclusively at that minor**. This grants no immunity under other law or constitutional parental veto.

The ordinary criminal maximum is one year for a first offense and three years after a prior conviction under the section, with the applicable title 18 fine or both. Exclusively nonviolent physical obstruction instead carries maxima of six months and $10,000 for the first offense, and eighteen months and $25,000 for a subsequent offense, with imprisonment, fine or both. If bodily injury results, imprisonment may reach ten years; if death results, any term of years or life. No criminal sentence is before the Court.

An aggrieved person's §248(a)(1) civil action is limited to persons involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. A court may award appropriate injunctive relief, compensatory and punitive damages, costs and reasonable attorney and expert fees. Before final judgment, the claimant may elect $5,000 per violation in lieu of actual compensatory damages. All of these remedies remain subject to the full parent/guardian exception. Federal enforcement requires the Attorney General's reasonable cause to believe a person or group is being, has been or may be injured by a violation; state Attorneys General may proceed on that basis as parens patriae for natural-person residents. Government civil relief may include injunctions and compensation; civil-penalty maxima are $10,000 for a first nonviolent obstruction and $15,000 for a subsequent one, or $15,000 and $25,000 for other first and subsequent violations. These are distinct from private elected damages and criminal punishment. The unchallenged §248(a)(2) branch concerns force, threats or obstruction of lawful religious exercise at a place of worship; its private action is limited to persons lawfully exercising or seeking that right or the entity owning or operating the place. Section (a)(3) separately addresses worship-property damage or destruction; these branches are described, not adjudicated.

Section 248(d) preserves First Amendment-protected expressive conduct, including peaceful picketing and demonstrations; creates no new remedy for interference with protected speech or religious exercise outside a facility, regardless of viewpoint, and limits no existing remedy; makes neither penalties nor civil remedies exclusive and preempts no state or local penalties or remedies; and does not interfere with state or local regulation of abortion or other reproductive services. ## Holdings

Every holding below retains the following enacted qualifications. A parent or legal guardian of a minor is subject to **no penalty or civil remedy under §248** for the described activities insofar as they are directed **exclusively at that minor**; this includes private damages and private or governmental injunctive relief. No FACE civil remedy survives for that excepted conduct. Other law is not preempted, but no equivalent state remedy, general parental immunity or constitutional veto is presumed.

The facility definition **includes** a hospital, clinic, physician's office or other reproductive-service facility and the building or structure in which it is located; it adds neither grounds nor worship premises. The separate physical-obstruction definition covers impassable ingress or egress, or unreasonably difficult or hazardous passage, **to or from** a service facility or place of religious worship. The worship branches remain unchallenged. These distinct provisions establish no property-line rule, new buffer, interior-only prohibition or immunity for all outdoor conduct. ### Congress may protect this medical-services market

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may regulate the challenged class of targeted force, threats, physical obstruction and facility destruction directly interfering with provision or receipt of reproductive health services in an interstate medical-services market. The absence of a separate interstate-crossing element for each actor does not defeat this class-based ground; direct regulation of private conduct also presents no compelled state legislation or administration.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes the commercial service being obstructed from the objector's ideological purpose. Lopez rejects the remote chain from stand-alone school firearm possession through education and productivity; it does not disable protection of an actual services market. Harris applies the retained economic-class principle, while preserving the particular-vehicle nexus Congress enacted there. Reported legislative evidence here concerns interstate patients, personnel and supplies, and actual loss of service through obstruction and closure. That connection is direct, rather than a general crime-cost theory. Local participation does not sever the regulated class from its commercial setting. Robertson confirms the significance of actual commercial operation without making every protester an interstate enterprise. The Court adds no element Congress omitted, treats no legislative statistic as conclusive, and adopts no exhaustive commerce formula. The separate persons-or-things-in-commerce theory and Fourteenth Amendment §5 ground need not be decided. New York's anti-compulsion rule remains intact: FACE regulates private actors rather than ordering States to legislate or administer a federal program.

**Precedent treatment:** Lopez (April 26, 1995) is distinguished at its inadequate downstream-effects chain; Harris (April 27, 1995) supplies the bounded economic-class ground; Robertson (May 1, 1995) remains a distinct direct-enterprise holding; New York v. United States (June 19, 1992) retains its anti-compulsion limit.

### The defined access restrictions survive the facial speech challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** FACE's challenged service-related force, threat, obstruction and intentional-property-damage provisions, with their physical-harm definitions, intent and alternative service-related motives, regulate access-related conduct without selecting a protected message. When that conduct is expressive, the provisions satisfy intermediate scrutiny because they advance substantial access and safety interests unrelated to suppression and do not burden substantially more speech than necessary, while leaving usable nonobstructive communication.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part II, joined by every other Justice: nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Both motive branches concern obtaining or providing services, not an antiabortion message or religious belief. Reproductive services include carrying pregnancy to term, counseling and referral as well as termination. Mitchell permits relevant motive to accompany independently proscribable conduct; it does not eliminate scrutiny of peaceful expressive obstruction. Under O'Brien and Ward, protecting passage and bodily safety is substantial and nonsuppressive. The law addresses impassable or unreasonably difficult or hazardous passage, force, threats and destruction, while preserving nonobstructive prayer, signs, leafleting, demonstrations and willing conversation. It imposes no numerical buffer, advance invitation requirement or compulsory silence. Turner requires examination of actual selection and fit; a benign purpose alone would not suffice. The connection between obstruction and denied access is direct here. Labor or environmental views receive no general exemption: coverage depends on the enacted purpose and physical predicates. The parent/guardian exception covers penalties and civil remedies only for conduct directed exclusively at the actor's own minor; it neither selects an abortion viewpoint nor permits general interference with other patients. Madsen's more demanding injunction standard and case-specific distances are not transplanted to this statute. Hurley protects private expressive composition, which is not compelled here. Discriminatory enforcement and materially different mixed-motive applications remain open.

**Precedent treatment:** Mitchell (June 11, 1993) supplies motive/conduct and protected-belief limits; O'Brien and Ward supply ordinary intermediate review; Turner (June 27, 1994) supplies classification and fit; Madsen (June 30, 1994) is distinguished as injunction review; Hurley (June 19, 1995) remains a private-expression holding.

### The presented substantial-overbreadth challenge fails

**Holding and operative rule:** The intentional-act and service-related-purpose requirements, defined physical access or bodily-harm predicates, and textually available protection for First Amendment expression do not reach substantial protected expression relative to FACE's legitimate sweep on this facial challenge. Political offense, successful persuasion and abstract belief alone are not statutory violations; protected expression cannot become punishable merely because it supplies evidence of a legally relevant motive.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part III-A, joined by every other Justice: nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** R.A.V. invalidates substantial protected coverage that permissible construction cannot cure. Its controlling rule concerns overbreadth, not the separate proposed content-selection theory within unprotected categories. FACE's enacted definitions restrict interference to movement and intimidation to reasonable apprehension of bodily harm. Section 248(d)(1) reinforces those available limits rather than supplying an unexplained assurance of validity. Mitchell distinguishes speculative future evidentiary chilling from a statute that directly punishes protected belief. Relevant statements remain subject to ordinary safeguards, and abstract belief is not itself an offense. True threats of force remain distinct from harsh political hyperbole. The Court announces no universal threat mental-state rule and adjudicates no uncharged statement; an application that actually punishes protected advocacy remains challengeable.

**Precedent treatment:** R.A.V. (June 22, 1992) is applied at its controlling substantial-overbreadth rule, with its distinct content-selection opinion left noncontrolling; Mitchell preserves relevant-motive evidence and the bar on abstract-belief punishment.

### The defined conduct is not facially vague

**Holding and operative rule:** FACE's movement, passage and reasonable-apprehension-of-bodily-harm definitions, together with intentional conduct and the required service-related purpose, provide ascertainable boundaries sufficient to reject the presented facial due-process vagueness claim. Mere offense, disagreement, resentment or persuasion does not satisfy those predicates.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part III-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this component's disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The challenged words operate within definitions of physical effects and an intentional service-related act, rather than an unrestricted prohibition of upsetting political debate. Whether passage is impassable or unreasonably difficult or hazardous is a conduct boundary; apprehension must concern bodily harm and be reasonable. Cameron supports the intelligibility of physical-obstruction restrictions in their statutory setting. R.A.V. did not decide vagueness, and its overbreadth holding is not substituted for this inquiry. Particular enforcement questions remain possible, but they do not establish that this enacted scheme lacks facially intelligible limits. No person's criminal liability or specific threatening statement is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Cameron v. Johnson is applied to the defined physical-obstruction setting; R.A.V.'s express vagueness reservation remains intact.

### Elected statutory damages are not categorically barred by the First Amendment

**Holding and operative rule:** For conduct outside the parent/legal-guardian exception, an otherwise eligible claimant may elect §248(c)(1)(B)'s $5,000 per violation before final judgment in lieu of actual compensatory damages; the First Amendment does not categorically require such violation-based statutory damages to equal proved actual loss. This rejects the categorical remedy challenge without deciding the reasonableness of that amount, a particular entitlement or award, the number of violations, punitive damages or a criminal sentence.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part IV-A, joined by all seven other Associates: eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejecting the categorical speech challenge without adding a statutory-damages theory.

**Controlling explanation:** Claiborne forbids charging a defendant for protected persuasion and the unlawful acts of others without the necessary attribution; it does not categorically forbid a legislatively selected substitute for actual compensatory loss caused by an attributable statutory violation. The election is permissive, occurs before final judgment and replaces actual compensatory damages, not every other available remedy. A hypothetical mixed campaign does not establish that the statute requires damages for its protected portion. No award exists to review here, and the amount's reasonableness was expressly outside the lower decision. Those questions remain distinct from the categorical contention. No private damages, elected sum, injunction or other FACE civil remedy is available when the actor is the minor's parent or legal guardian and the described activity is directed exclusively at that minor; no qualifying application is found here.

**Precedent treatment:** NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co. is applied to responsibility and protected persuasion, without extending it into a categorical ban on elected statutory damages.

### A damages defendant must be responsible for the violation

**Holding and operative rule:** FACE liability must rest on a defendant's legally attributable violation; shared belief, organizational membership, independent advocacy or another person's unlawful act alone does not establish that liability. Protected persuasion that reduces demand cannot itself supply the unlawful injury for an award.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part IV-B, joined by every other Justice: nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Claiborne preserves responsibility for unlawful conduct while protecting association and persuasion. Those requirements remain operative when a legislature permits statutory damages. An injunction's actual-concert principle is not a substitute for the legal predicates of a damages action. Madsen similarly does not permit an injunction to bind a viewpoint or independent advocate merely by association. The Court adjudicates no organizational attribution, civil conspiracy, actual-concert finding or individual award in this pre-enforcement case.

**Precedent treatment:** Claiborne's attribution rule is preserved; Madsen's responsible-party and actual-concert limits are retained within injunction procedure and do not become a new damages theory.

### The presented facial religious-targeting claim fails

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged text does not establish religious targeting or a substantial exemption for comparable general secular blockades: its predicates concern service-related acts and access, not religious identity. Its parent/guardian exception for penalties and civil remedies reaches conduct directed exclusively at that person's own minor and does not itself establish that outsiders may obstruct other persons' access on secular grounds.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part V-A, joined by all seven other Associates: eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the constitutional free-exercise disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Lukumi requires scrutiny of objective text, exemptions and operation, including comparable secular harm. Religious prominence among opponents does not alone prove targeting, just as a secular description of purpose would not defeat an actually discriminatory scheme. FACE does not exempt labor or environmental viewpoints from its enacted predicates; conduct with a different object may fall outside them. The limited own-minor relationship does not resemble the substantial secular underinclusion in Lukumi. The Court rejects the presented facial inference, without deciding discriminatory enforcement or declaring the exception irrelevant to RFRA's separate application-specific inquiry. Congress withheld even preventive FACE civil relief for qualifying own-minor conduct. The Court weighs that full distinction, not a lesser-penalty exception; it establishes no universal rule that family status defeats comparison and no fact about a plaintiff's qualification in a different encounter.

**Precedent treatment:** Lukumi (June 11, 1993) is applied at its objective-targeting and comparable-secular-harm holdings, without turning its noncontrolling legislative-purpose discussion into the governing test.

### The broader constitutional exemption is rejected on the pleaded frame

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Smith's constitutional neutral-law rule as bounded by Lukumi, the asserted religious obligation to engage in the covered physical obstruction supplies no constitutional exemption on the pleaded frame after the presented targeting and comparable-secular-exemption challenges fail. This conclusion does not dispose of RFRA, decide every different application or overrule any constitutional protection against discriminatory enforcement.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Part V-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct rationale votes. O'Connor and Souter reserve this broader question; Stone-Zsela supplies a disposition-only vote and no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith distinguishes a neutral, generally applicable conduct rule from a law singling out religious exercise, and Lukumi preserves the objective targeting and comparability inquiry. On this pleading, the asserted exemption from the defined conduct prohibition does not follow as a constitutional matter. The parent/guardian qualification is retained with its own-minor and exclusive-direction limits; it is not a general secular blockade entitlement. Congress has independently imposed RFRA's more demanding standard, which remains fully applicable and requires the separate disposition below. The Court neither decides RFRA's constitutionality nor substitutes this constitutional rule for its statutory burden sequence.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith remains the constitutional neutral-law rule; Lukumi retains its targeting and general-applicability limits; Clearwater and Swanner require the separate statutory inquiry.

### RFRA requires further adjudication of the prospective-enforcement claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The dismissal of the RFRA prospective-enforcement claim is vacated. The district court must identify each live claimant and threatened application, determine a genuinely contested substantial burden, and, if the burden is established, require the government to produce evidence and persuade that applying it to that person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means, considering concrete alternatives and legally comparable exceptions.

**Authority:** O'Connor's RFRA opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: five direct votes for the rule, its application and vacatur/remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Clearwater and Swanner require a distinct inquiry into the actual person and application. The Court assumes the sufficiently pleaded religious burden solely to review dismissal; the government disputes it and no sincerity or doctrinal-necessity finding is made. General appeals to access, health and safety, even with successful intermediate speech scrutiny, do not complete RFRA's least-restrictive-means showing. Reliable medical access and bodily safety carry compelling weight, and an accommodation may fail because it leaves the very access injury the statute prevents. But this generalized dismissal did not complete that showing for the claimants' actual conduct and legally material alternatives, including the parent/minor exception's complete exclusion of penalties and civil remedies, even preventive FACE injunctions. That exclusion defeats a premise of universal access enforcement and requires a genuine comparison; it does not itself establish that an outsider blockade and the exclusively parent-to-own-minor relation are comparable. No equivalent state remedy is presumed. The government bears production and persuasion; claimants do not bear a universal duty to prove an alternative. Remand neither requires a hearing whenever religion is asserted nor grants an entitlement to block a clinic or divert patients elsewhere. A claim may fail on its actual pleadings and a sufficient showing, or at the substantial-burden threshold. RFRA applies to later federal enactments unless expressly excluded by reference, and FACE contains no such exclusion. Ordinary standing, appropriate relief, religious-belief protection and the Establishment Clause remain. The statute stays in force; no injunction or final exemption follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Clearwater (December 5, 1994) and Swanner (March 27, 1995) are applied without changing their burden sequence or treating third-party harm as irrelevant; Smith does not replace RFRA; Lee and Roberts retain the weight of direct injury without creating a categorical exception to the statute.

## Holdings

### Use requires active employment of the firearm

**Operative rule:** Under the applicable §924(c)(1), a defendant uses a firearm only by actually and actively employing it as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment does not by itself satisfy use; firing or employment as a weapon is not required, and Smith's actual firearm barter remains use.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices named in Opinion Topology, supplies nine direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal of the use-based appellate affirmances.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress used a conduct word and separately referred to carrying. Reading use as every way a gun's availability may support a criminal enterprise would erase the difference between actual employment and possession. Smith holds that offering a firearm in exchange for drugs employs it in the transaction even though as merchandise. That example broadens the purposes for which a gun may be actively used; it does not eliminate the act of using it.

The Government's protective-availability and emboldening account addresses a real danger, but that danger cannot replace the conduct Congress specified. Bailey's weapon was stored in a locked automobile trunk; Robinson's was kept in her bedroom near drug proceeds. The trial theories and instructions permitted conviction through possession and availability rather than active employment. Those grounds cannot sustain the appellate judgments. Staples and Ratzlaf reinforce respect for the distinct enacted elements; they do not create an extra knowledge-of-illegality requirement or a universal presumption that every criminal ambiguity must be resolved before ordinary interpretation.

The Court provides no closed list of qualifying conduct and no comprehensive definition of carrying. It decides no retroactivity question and leaves distinct possession offenses untouched.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith's barter holding is applied and its unaddressed possession boundary resolved; Staples and Ratzlaf retain their offense-specific knowledge holdings without extension to a new mental element here.

### Connection to the offense cannot substitute for using or carrying

**Operative rule:** The Government must establish the use or carrying conduct independently of the requirement that it occur during and in relation to the predicate offense. A firearm's actual or potential facilitation may satisfy the required relationship under Smith, but it does not establish active employment under the use prong merely because the firearm is present or available.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Part I coalition directly adopts this distinct-elements proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith gives the relation requirement work by excluding an accidental or coincidental connection and requiring a purpose or effect concerning trafficking. Its actual exchange also supplies the separate act of employment. The two showings therefore coexist; one does not do the other's work. Harris likewise requires independent proof of §924(c)'s use or carrying even where the qualifying predicate itself includes a firearm. The Court applies that separation here without enlarging Harris's carjacking holding or deciding the outer boundary of sufficient facilitation.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith's relation rule and Harris's independent-conduct requirement remain in force; neither makes storage into use.

### The carrying alternative remains for proper consideration on remand

**Operative rule:** Both use-based appellate affirmances are reversed, and the D.C. Circuit may consider whether a legally sufficient and properly available carrying basis supports either reviewed firearm conviction. The presence of carrying language in the charges and instructions establishes no finding that every carrying element was lawfully determined, that the error was harmless, or that any particular remedial course is permissible.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight votes. Stone-Zsela supports remand but dissents from leaving the trial convictions available for that consideration without first vacating them.

**Controlling explanation:** The verified instructions submitted carrying in both cases, but they also treated possession and intended availability as sufficient without preserving the necessary conduct distinction. The Court decides the presented use issue; it does not decide an unexamined carrying theory from a charge label. The lower court must address the independent lawful requirements and procedural availability before relying on that alternative. Robinson's aiding-and-abetting submission likewise proves neither a substantive firearm offense nor intentional assistance by itself. Harris preserves the distinct requirement of intentional assistance; mere presence, association or awareness is insufficient.

The remand accordingly directs no carrying conviction, immediate acquittal or retrial. It neither determines waiver nor resolves sufficiency or harmlessness from instruction excerpts. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments remain undisturbed. The Court's refusal to order immediate trial-conviction vacatur is not a holding that either conviction is valid on carrying grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's independent-conduct and intentional-assistance requirements remain separate from the possibility of remand review; no new carrying or remedial rule supplants ordinary requirements.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

### Temporary preservation of a claimed setoff

A creditor's genuinely temporary refusal to pay a deposit debt while promptly seeking judicial relief to exercise a claimed setoff is not itself a setoff forbidden by §362(a)(7) when the creditor has not intended permanently to settle the mutual accounts or finally applied one obligation against the other. The act's legal character controls; a label or the absence of a bookkeeping entry alone does not establish that character.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Studley describes setoff as applying mutual debts against each other. The ordinary decision, implementing act and recordation sequence reflects the intention permanently to settle those accounts. Federal law determines whether a setoff has occurred under the Bankruptcy Code even if state law employs a different definition. Section 553 preserves qualifying nonbankruptcy setoff rights but creates none and leaves their exercise subject to the automatic stay. Section 542(b) excepts payment to the extent the debt may be offset under §553. Requiring immediate payment while treating every temporary preservation step as the completed offset would defeat that enacted relationship.

The bank imposed its hold October 2, 1991, and sought relief October 7, 1991; its application asked the bankruptcy court to authorize the later setoff. It did not purport finally to reduce the account by the loan balance. Five days is the record's interval, not a universal safe harbor. The rule permits neither an indefinite freeze nor a secret completed debit, and good faith creates no general stay exception. Whether the amount withheld exceeded the available offset is not decided. No fixed deadline, independent promptness cause of action, or new setoff entitlement is created.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Bank of Marin supplies the debtor-creditor character of an ordinary bank deposit: the depositor has a payment claim, not title to particular currency held in trust. The Court authorizes no indefinite restraint, refusal after adverse judicial direction or amount beyond legally available offset. Confirmation's effect under §1327 is not reached because that contention was not raised below. The later stay-relief/setoff order is not adjudicated anew.

**Precedent treatment:** Bank of Marin is applied to the deposit relationship. Whiting Pools and Celotex are distinguished at their statutory and procedural scopes.

## Holdings

### Legal immunity review accepts the properly assumed facts

An immediate qualified-immunity appeal may decide a separable legal question on the plaintiff’s properly supported assumed facts, but may not decide only whether the evidence is sufficient to establish those facts. A factual-sufficiency portion must be dismissed; the appellate court may not choose whose account is true or resolve disputed knowledge and causation by recasting those questions as law.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s Part I, joined by all eight other participating Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson v. Jones distinguishes an appealable immunity question from a dispute about the sufficiency of proof. Elder v. Holloway requires the appellate court to examine the relevant law, not to reconstruct a more favorable factual record for the officer. Here the officials may obtain review of the legal consequences of the properly assumed record. They may not secure findings that warnings were never given or that their inaction did not contribute to the abuse. The inconsistent January and February descriptions of Caplinger’s first notice are not resolved by this Court. No ruling that an identified item of evidence is credible, or that Doe has proved her claim, follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Johnson’s jurisdictional boundary and Elder’s legal-review rule are applied without expansion of collateral appellate jurisdiction.

### Knowing, causally effective acquiescence in a teacher’s sexual abuse is personal wrongdoing

A school supervisor violates a pupil’s bodily-integrity right when the supervisor actually knows of sexual abuse through school authority, or of a substantial risk of that abuse, deliberately and unreasonably disregards that known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to take protective action, and that culpable inaction causes injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury still occurs. Awareness may be proved circumstantially, but warning signs do not compel that finding and what an official merely should have known is insufficient. The properly assumed Lankford facts describe that personal knowing acquiescence, not negligence or liability merely for supervising the teacher; the relevant preconduct law gave fair notice of the wrong, so immunity does not bar the claim at this stage.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Breyer, controls by five votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Ingraham recognizes bodily security. The teacher's exploitation of his official authority and access supplies the pleaded governmental connection, although some acts occurred away from school. Monell forbids vicarious liability, and Rizzo requires a causal link to the supervisor's own conduct.

Ford limits responsibility to direction, participation or approval. Reimer and Vela reject liability merely for failing to adopt preventive policies while recognizing responsibility for affirmative unlawful policies causing injury. Chinchello reads Rizzo more narrowly as requiring more than awareness and inaction and describes other courts' inaction cases through communicated approval. These formulations are not interchangeable; they expose the danger of converting inadequate supervision into vicarious liability. Wanger and Bowen nevertheless recognize personal responsibility where the supervisor's own deliberately indifferent conduct or inaction causes the constitutional injury. The restricted conduct assumed here—actual awareness, deliberately unreasonable nonresponse within the principal's authority, and causal prolongation of abuse—falls within that principle. No finding that Lankford communicated approval to the teacher is made.

The dismissed earlier physical-conduct report, repeated warnings, and February valentine and suspected relationship support the assumed knowledge. The alleged failure to act meaningfully may have permitted continued injury. Those are legal-review assumptions, not findings; the earlier sexual contact, later intercourse and continued abuse remain distinct for causation. Negligent failure to discover misconduct, a state reporting breach alone, or supervisory office would not suffice.

For pre-May 1987 omissions, notice comes from earlier law. Jefferson's direct-restraint holding and Lopez's school-supervision analysis reinforce evaluation of the alleged later continuation, but their different settings and Lopez's rejection of liability on its facts remain material. Neither creates a general monitoring duty or supplies retroactive notice. Farmer's later actual-awareness formulation is only a current analogy. Doe must still prove awareness, unreasonable disregard and attributable injury.

**Precedent treatment:** Ingraham is applied to bodily integrity; Monell and Rizzo retain their personal-attribution limits. Hafer confirms individual-capacity liability for one’s own wrong. Collins’s reserved deliberate-injury setting is distinguished from its voluntary-employment omissions. Farmer is used only as a current analogy, not conduct-time notice.

### Caplinger is immune on the distinct notice question

On Caplinger’s own assumed information and responses, the conduct-time law did not clearly establish that his particular supervisory conduct violated Doe’s federal right. He therefore receives qualified immunity on the presented individual supervisory claim; the Court does not decide whether that conduct actually violated the Constitution or attribute the principal’s fuller knowledge to him.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer, controls by eight votes. Stone-Zsela concurs only in this component’s judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Elder requires the full relevant legal landscape, while Hafer requires individualized responsibility. Caplinger arrived in July 1986 and was not told the principal’s earlier history or the February valentine. His response to later reports included inquiries, an interview of Doe in which she denied sex, and warnings to the teacher; the promised wider meeting did not occur. Those facts cannot be replaced by an assumption that he knew everything Lankford knew. Nor does the Court resolve the lower opinion’s conflicting January/February notice descriptions.

The preconduct supervisory cases did not clearly define this particular combination of limited information, investigation and alleged inadequate follow-through as constitutional wrongdoing. Jefferson and Lopez, issued in May 1987, cannot illuminate earlier omissions retroactively and do not, for later conduct, equate this superintendent’s individual circumstances with the principal’s alleged knowing acquiescence. A broad duty to protect children does not answer the required particularized notice inquiry. Conversely, the judgment does not excuse an official merely because his notice came later: sufficiently clear personal knowledge and duty would require their own analysis. The constitutional merits remain reserved.

**Precedent treatment:** Elder and Hafer are applied to the superintendent separately. No broad immunity for school officials, no mandatory merits-first sequence, and no endorsement of his response as constitutionally adequate is established.

## Holdings

### Part I — Actual fraud requires actual and justifiable reliance

**Operative rule:** For the actual-fraud claim presented under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(A), the creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance. Justifiability turns on this recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances: there is no general duty to investigate, but a creditor cannot rely on known or personally obvious falsity, and a warning actually discovered may require inquiry under that recipient-specific standard.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt this rule and its limits. Scalia and Breyer do not join Part II or the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress used established common-law fraud language. As the interpretive method recognized in Darden illustrates, such terms carry their established legal content unless the statute indicates otherwise. Common-law deceit requires reliance, and Restatement §§537 and 540–541 distinguish justifiability from an ordinary prudent person's duty to investigate. Carelessness alone does not excuse intentional fraud; known or obvious falsity does. The recipient's actual capacities and encountered warnings therefore matter without creating a universal title-search or professional-advice requirement.

Subsection(A) excludes statements respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. Subsection(B) separately covers a materially false written statement of that kind, on which the creditor reasonably relied, that the debtor caused to be made or published with intent to deceive. Its express reasonable-reliance requirement does not import that broader inquiry into(A). Nor does the modifier's absence from(A) erase reliance from actual fraud. Objective unreasonableness may bear on whether claimed actual reliance is credible, but it is not an independent universal prudence requirement. This is general common-law content, not the tort law of a particular State.

Grogan's ordinary preponderance burden remains unchanged. Fraud, actual reliance, justifiability and the requirement that the debt be obtained by the specified conduct remain separate. The Court decides the actual-fraud claim before it; it does not independently classify a different claim involving only false pretenses or representation without actual fraud, or determine the amount of any nondischargeable debt.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden's common-law-term method is applied by analogy beyond employee classification; Grogan's proof burden is preserved, not heightened or reduced.

### Part II — The lower courts must apply that standard before a final debt determination

**Operative rule:** An adverse actual-fraud discharge judgment resting on the broader objective prudent-person investigation requirement cannot stand merely because the record contains individualized facts relevant to justifiability. The judgment is vacated and the case remanded for application of the correct standard to the existing findings, further findings as needed, and independent resolution of properly remaining statutory debt-nexus and extent questions.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the application and remedy. Scalia and Breyer dissent from this part and would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The bankruptcy court expressly asked what a prudent person should have investigated, including whether to consult an attorney, even while accepting a lack of sophistication. Its discussion of visits and reported ownership does not establish a separate application of the recipient-specific standard. The Court cannot resolve that uncertainty by treating the two standards as interchangeable. The dissent's reading of the same findings is substantial, but the express use of the broader requirement makes proper lower-court application necessary.

The existing findings of actual reliance, detriment and effective extension of credit are not erased by this legal correction. They also do not establish, without the separate statutory inquiry, that every dollar of the original note was obtained by the later concealment. The First Circuit must return the matter to the bankruptcy court for application of justifiability and proper treatment of the obtained-by and other remaining elements, with any further findings needed. Ordinary preservation rules remain for issues not resolved here. ## Holdings

### 1. Religious instruction on an admitted subject receives equal access

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school that opens an after-school period to comparable private instruction and activity concerning children's moral and character development may not exclude an otherwise eligible club because its approach to that subject is religious. The rule applies to this actual forum even if it is nonpublic; genuine subject, activity, scheduling, supervision, safety and access limits remain available when applied without viewpoint discrimination.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight Justices directly adopt this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the district has excluded a religious perspective from a subject it admits. Lamb's Chapel supplies the distinction between a permissible subject limit and an impermissible religious-viewpoint exclusion. The district court found that both the Scouts and this Club concern moral development. The Scouts' practical and secular activities and the Club's Christian instruction differ, but the district cannot make religious treatment of the admitted subject a disqualifying subject of its own. The policy's express exclusion of religious speech even within the Scout program confirms the viewpoint restriction. Prayer and Bible lessons do not erase the relevant comparison on this record. Perry and Cornelius preserve genuine forum limits; they do not permit that reclassification of an admitted perspective. Access after 6 p.m. does not cure discrimination in the 3–6 p.m. period available to comparable activities. The students' ages and the continuing extracurricular day require consideration of the actual arrangements, but do not make private religious instruction school speech. The parent-led, voluntary meetings, parental permission and express notice of private sponsorship establish the concrete private-use basis on which establishment concerns cannot justify this exclusion. The Court does not require every school to open every facility to every religious activity.

### 2. These actual private-use arrangements do not establish religion

**Holding and operative rule:** Allowing this Club equal access on the established forum terms does not violate the Establishment Clause: the forum serves a secular purpose, the Club's expression remains private in the actual context, and equal access creates neither governmental religious advancement nor excessive entanglement. Private sponsorship must be real; school selection, control, adoption, preferential treatment or obscured attribution could change the constitutional assessment, and neither neutral availability nor a disclaimer alone would cure official religious activity.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt the contextual explanation under existing establishment law. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join Part I's concrete rejection of the establishment defense but do not join Part II's full explanation; no additional joins are inferred.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the existing inquiry into purpose, effect and entanglement; it creates no new constitutional checklist. Lemon supplies that inquiry, and Lamb's Chapel applies it to equal access for private expression in a school. Chabad requires examination of actual governmental affiliation rather than reliance on a forum label. Here parents lead the meetings, students attend voluntarily with parental permission, and announcements disclaim school sponsorship. No current official direction of the Club's worship is established. The former board member's earlier involvement does not establish present control. Students are 11–15 and meetings occur while other school activities continue, but those contextual facts do not outweigh the established separation between the school and this private program. The available late bus adds convenience, not compulsory attendance. The secular purpose is to make facilities available for the admitted youth activities; permitting the religious perspective does not make the school its speaker. Ordinary neutral supervision and administration do not require school officials to direct or endorse the Club's religious instruction. ## Holdings

### Similarity must concern the challenged opportunity

**Operative rule:** In an equal-protection challenge to prison programs, whether prisoners are similarly situated must be assessed in relation to the treatment challenged. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resource choices may materially distinguish a particular program; general differences between entire institutions cannot categorically defeat every comparison of the benefits provided.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices named in Opinion Topology, controls this and the following two holdings unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Equality does not require identical institutions before any particular deprivation may be examined. The women's institution is smaller than the Nebraska State Penitentiary and houses prisoners with different sentence lengths and security characteristics. Those circumstances may explain particular choices; they do not establish that sex is irrelevant to every challenged opportunity. Hogan requires scrutiny of the actual sex distinction rather than a broad institutional label. Turner preserves attention to legitimate prison administration and resources; it does not exempt an actual sex classification from the governing equality rule.

The State correctly rejects a demand to duplicate the larger prison's entire program mix. But dismissal based on whole-institution dissimilarity is broader than that premise supports. The prisoners likewise cannot prove a constitutional injury merely by showing that another institution spends more or offers more programs. The Court vacates categorical dismissal while leaving the significance of concrete operational differences for the individual claims.

**Precedent treatment:** Hogan's real-justification inquiry and Turner's attention to actual operational constraints are applied together; neither becomes a rule of identical services or a prison-program immunity.

### Actual sex classifications and neutral decisions require distinct inquiries

**Operative rule:** If a challenged program decision actually classifies by sex, in its terms or administration, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the sex distinction. If the choice is genuinely sex neutral, plaintiffs must prove that it was made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and awareness of disparity alone do not establish that discriminatory purpose, though circumstantial evidence may do so.

**Authority:** The unanimous whole-opinion coalition identified above directly adopts both branches and their qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Craig and Hogan supply the scrutiny of actual sex classifications. Davis and Arlington Heights distinguish disparate effects from purposeful discrimination while permitting proof from circumstances. Feeney distinguishes a decision made because of its adverse consequences for a sex from one made despite them. Segregated housing does not itself decide which description fits each program choice; nor must plaintiffs produce a written admission before they may establish discriminatory administration.

Kelley confirms that a sex-conscious allocation remains subject to an actual important objective and substantial connection even when real program constraints exist. Its Title IX athletic routes are not prison rules. J.E.B. similarly rejects sex as a proxy for individual opportunity, but its jury-specific sequence of proof is not a new prison-program procedure. The Court preserves ordinary plaintiff burdens for neutral decisions and the State's justification burden for established classifications. It announces no impact presumption, least-restrictive-means requirement or right to a selected program.

**Precedent treatment:** Craig, Hogan, Davis, Arlington Heights and Feeney supply their distinct established functions. Kelley and J.E.B. inform sex-classification review without transferring their setting-specific statutory or procedural rules.

### The legal error requires further adjudication, not restored liability

**Operative rule:** The existing liability determination cannot stand merely on sex-separated housing and unequal programs without the required classification or purpose findings, but the absence of adequate findings under that mistaken approach does not justify dismissal through an overbroad comparator rule. The liability reversal is affirmed and the dismissal direction vacated so the preserved claims can be adjudicated under the proper standards.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices directly adopt this mixed disposition and its bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate majority and dissent differ over what the trial findings establish. The Court does not turn that disagreement into its own finding either of discrimination or of its universal absence. It instead separates whether a particular comparison is meaningful, whether the State used sex or acted with discriminatory purpose, whether the applicable justification is sufficient, and what relief follows. The district court's disparity premise is insufficient for immediate restoration; the appellate court's categorical dissimilarity premise is insufficient for complete dismissal.

The reviewed controversy remains limited to the pleaded program comparisons. Title IX, independent access-to-courts claims, other male institutions excluded by the denied amendment, unpleaded discriminatory funding, and any later final judgment are outside it. Gunter's individual responsibility and immunity, Clarke's official-capacity responsibility, damages and equitable relief remain separate and undecided. Judgments concerning other defendants are not reopened. Legally adequate existing findings may be used; additional proceedings are neither prohibited nor automatically required.

**Precedent treatment:** The ordinary classification and purpose authorities govern further adjudication; no new substantive liability or remedial test is substituted.

## Holdings

### A material agency development can justify reconsideration without a merits decision

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These six directly adopt the procedural rule and its application. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on his separately stated ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Henry v. City of Rock Hill distinguishes reconsideration from adjudication of the merits. Alvarado v. United States illustrates remand for judicial examination of a changed Government position; the Court must nevertheless supply its own reason for vacating a judgment. Section 2106 permits that limited remedy without a present determination that the claimant must prevail.

The agency now acknowledges examining the constitutional validity of state intestacy and paternity requirements at least in some circumstances. That qualified position bears sufficiently on the state-law premise of this benefits claim to warrant examination below. The lower court has not considered it. Its possible effect and the value of an initial lawful inquiry justify reconsideration even though this Court cannot determine whether the acknowledgment applies to this claimant or changes the ultimate result.

The objection that conditional executive advocacy proves no legal error is substantial, but the order makes no such finding and delegates no judicial judgment to the Government. ## Holdings

### Agreement permits waiver but cannot enlarge the forfeiture statute

A defendant may knowingly and voluntarily relinquish the forfeiture jury protection and admit facts supporting forfeiture. Consent does not authorize punishment beyond § 853: an admission may establish a statutory predicate, but an agreement cannot replace a predicate the statute requires or transfer an interest outside the defendant's lawful power of disposition.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I-B, joined by all eight other Justices, controls unanimously on these common propositions; the distinct application grounds and constitutional reservation appear below.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 853(a)(1) reaches property constituting or derived from proceeds obtained directly or indirectly from the violation. Paragraph (a)(2) reaches the defendant's property used or intended to be used, in any manner or part, to commit or facilitate it. For a continuing criminal enterprise, paragraph (a)(3) also reaches his interests in or claims against the enterprise and his property or contractual rights affording a source of control over it. Lawful acquisition alone therefore does not establish immunity.

Substitute property under § 853(p) is different. The defendant's act or omission must have made qualifying property unavailable through inability to locate it despite due diligence; transfer, sale or deposit with a third party; placement beyond the court's jurisdiction; substantial diminution in value; or commingling with other property that cannot be divided without difficulty. When those predicates are established, the court must order forfeiture of other property of the defendant up to the value of the qualifying unavailable property. Section 853(d)'s rebuttable presumption at trial requires the Government to prove by a preponderance both acquisition during the violation or within a reasonable time afterward and the absence of a likely source other than the covered violation. An all-assets promise alone proves neither condition.

Godinez distinguishes capacity from actual knowing and voluntary choice; Olano distinguishes intentional relinquishment from forfeiture by inaction. The limited waiver recognized in Mezzanatto does not dispense with understanding or authorize an unlawful sentence. The Court does not find every asset independently eligible, invalidate every asset, or infer private counsel's advice. The defendant's plea and agreement operate within the statute, and third-party rights retain their separate protection.

**Precedent treatment:** Godinez and Olano supply the capacity/choice and relinquishment/inaction distinctions. Mezzanatto's limited consent principle is applied without expanding its impeachment holding into unlimited sentencing authority.

### Former Rule 11(f) does not require a separate forfeiture factual-basis inquiry

Former Rule 11(f) requires a factual basis for the guilty plea to the substantive offense; it does not independently require a guilt-style factual-basis inquiry into the extent of criminal forfeiture imposed as part of the sentence under §§ 848 and 853. This rule-specific conclusion does not permit a court to exceed those statutes or require acceptance of a defective agreement.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I-A, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in affirmance on his separately stated statutory-record and informed-waiver grounds, without adopting an additional Rule 11(f) rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(f)'s inquiry protects the guilty plea to the offense. The separate notice and special-verdict requirements of Rules 7(c)(2) and 31(e) address criminal forfeiture without converting each property interest into an element of continuing-criminal-enterprise guilt. Libretti pleaded guilty to that offense, and the challenged determination fixes property included in its statutory sentence. The existence of forfeiture safeguards does not change the words or subject of Rule 11(f).

That distinction answers the argument that every stipulated asset required a new plea-style factual inquiry. It does not erase the difference between procedural inquiry and lawful punishment. The sentencing court still operates under § 853; a factual admission and existing evidence may supply its predicates, while consent cannot supply a power Congress withheld. Whether an independent duty apart from Rule 11(f) requires a particular further inquiry is addressed only to the limited extent necessary below. No new Rule 32.2 procedure or later amendment is applied.

**Precedent treatment:** The plea and waiver precedents retain their defined roles; this holding construes the then-applicable Rules 11,7 and 31 rather than enlarging their text through an analogy to guilt elements.

### The particular forfeiture determination is not an offense-element jury question

Under the §§ 848 and 853 scheme at issue, the determination of the extent of forfeiture following conviction is a sentencing determination, not an element of continuing-criminal-enterprise guilt requiring a Sixth Amendment jury determination. The holding concerns this statutory determination; it does not announce that every fact affecting punishment lies outside the jury guarantee.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls by five votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg leave the constitutional source of the forfeiture jury protection unresolved.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin requires the jury to decide each enacted criminal element, including materiality, but does not classify these property interests as elements of the substantive CCE offense. Rule 31(e) separately provides a forfeiture special verdict. The distinction between conviction and punishment recognized in McMillan, Cabana and Spaziano permits the particular statutory sentencing determination here without converting it into a new guilt element.

Austin's treatment of punitive civil forfeiture under the Excessive Fines Clause and Alexander's treatment of criminal forfeiture as punishment establish the bounded propositions those cases decide. Punitive character does not by itself transfer every procedural guarantee applicable to adjudication of guilt. The Court neither decides an Excessive Fines claim nor limits Gaudin's protection of actual offense elements. The four Justices reserving the constitutional issue do not vote to recognize a constitutional forfeiture jury right; their separate grounds make its source unnecessary to their dispositions.

**Precedent treatment:** Gaudin remains fully effective for enacted offense elements and is distinguished as to this sentencing determination. McMillan, Cabana and Spaziano supply the date-eligible guilt/punishment distinction. Austin and Alexander are applied only at their punitive-forfeiture scope.

### The affirmative agreement and courtroom assent waive the statutory special verdict

Libretti's represented agreement to forfeit his identified interests, signed jury waiver and express courtroom assent to ending the trial and proceeding to sentence waive the Rule 31(e) special verdict on this record. Former Rule 11(c) does not require an additional forfeiture-specific advisement, and Rule 23(a) does not require a new forfeiture-specific written waiver for this statutory sentencing verdict.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls by five votes. Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg also uphold the waiver on informed-relinquishment grounds, but do not join this part's broader sufficient-waiver formulation. Stevens does not independently resolve the waiver application because his statutory remand ground disposes of his vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Libretti did more than remain silent or enter an unexplained plea. He signed the represented agreement, expressly accepted it in court, and was told the trial would end and sentencing would follow. The agreement's actual admissions and scope matter; competence alone and a signature alone do not establish knowing surrender of every sentencing issue. Earlier trial references to the forfeiture verdict reinforce the record without requiring an assumption about private advice from counsel.

Godinez permits a competent defendant's choice but preserves the separate requirement that it be knowing and voluntary. Olano distinguishes that choice from an unpreserved objection. Mezzanatto does not make every plea a waiver of every protection. A judge may explain the special verdict expressly and must not affirmatively mislead the defendant or leave evident confusion uncorrected. Supported challenges to coercion, voluntariness, understanding or agreement scope remain distinct. The absence of a new rule-specific warning at this plea hearing therefore does not require the proposed remand; neither the right's statutory source nor practical convenience alone supplies the waiver.

**Precedent treatment:** Godinez, Olano and the limited Mezzanatto holding are applied to affirmative, represented assent. Their protections against unsupported or involuntary waiver remain intact.

### This record does not require the demanded wholesale new factual-basis proceeding

The trial evidence, sentencing materials, agreement referring to § 853 and the judge's response to Libretti's preserved objection provide a case-specific basis for rejecting the demanded new forfeiture factual-basis proceeding. The Court leaves the precise extent of any independent duty outside Rule 11(f) open and makes no new asset-by-asset adjudication under § 853(a) or (p).

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five votes. Scalia and Thomas do not join this additional policy and record appraisal. Stone-Zsela concurs on his stated record-limited statutory ground; Stevens would require independent assurance of the order's lawful scope on remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The judge heard trial evidence, received sentencing materials, considered an agreement expressly tied to § 853, acknowledged the statute's limits and answered Libretti's personal factual-basis objection by referring to evidence already heard. The decision therefore did not rest solely on an unexplained promise to surrender everything. The court of appeals' broad view of what agreement could authorize is not adopted as a source of statutory power.

The later April 2 order contains actual adverse findings and third-party return orders. In the Wyochem/Porter discussion, it found the Government had not carried the stated substitute-property burden and left tracing or other claims for further hearing. The district court found that the ring was bought with legitimate funds and described Porter as an innocent owner; it found no drug-transaction nexus for the childhood accounts. It ordered return of the ring and the parents' funds and interest in those accounts. These particular findings and returns do not replace Section 853(n)'s statutory criteria with a general innocent-owner defense. The defendant-specific reopening was ineffective after his appeal; that jurisdictional limit did not erase the court's separate § 853(n) jurisdiction or all its third-party findings.

The expressly designated $8,416.23 General Chemical distribution check in December paragraph NN is distinct from the Columbia IRA in paragraph Q and Porter's $1,333.45 claim. An express substitute-property designation is not proof of every § 853(p) predicate. The appellate caveat for an employer pension or profit-sharing interest made inalienable by law, or a valid spendthrift trust created by another, is conditional; it does not establish that the distributed check or IRA had that status. These distinctions defeat neither statutory limits nor the bounded procedural affirmance. The Court orders no automatic property return, new hearing for every asset or reopening of valid third-party relief.

**Precedent treatment:** The lawful-authority limit is preserved alongside the narrow procedural holding; the Court does not adopt a consent-created forfeiture power or decide the full independent-duty question urged in dissent.

## Holdings

### Waiting incident to completed-assignment return is neither duty nor rest

**Operative rule:** Waiting genuinely incident to transportation from a completed duty assignment to final release falls within 49 U.S.C. §21103(b)(4)'s return-transportation interval and counts as neither on-duty time nor required off-duty rest. Time engaged in or connected with movement of a train remains duty time under §21103(b)(2), as does any other service for the carrier during a twenty-four-hour period in which the employee is engaged in or connected with train movement under §21103(b)(3); transportation to a new duty assignment remains on duty.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the rule, its limits, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Transportation describes the undertaking of returning the crew, including awaiting the vehicle that carries it. Nothing in the specific return provision confines that undertaking to minutes of physical movement. The general reporting-to-final-release rule in §21103(b)(1) must leave room for the express return category in §21103(b)(4). Otherwise the general language would defeat the exception Congress placed beside it.

The unions emphasize that waiting crews remain subject to the railroad's direction and may become fatigued. Those circumstances do not distinguish the wait from the return journey Congress expressly excluded from duty. Nor does the exclusion declare the time restorative: Congress also withheld off-duty credit. The separate provisions counting actual service and certain interim rest as duty continue to govern their own predicates. An employer's transportation label cannot remove work from those provisions.

MCI Telecommunications requires an independent determination of the statutory boundary; Town & Country permits agency construction only within the statutory range. The enacted categories resolve this question without reliance on an agency's litigation position or administrative history. No general deference doctrine is changed.

The question concerns waiting after assignment completion without further train-movement responsibility or other service. It does not require a judicial maximum waiting duration, a finding that waiting causes no fatigue, or a decision about a particular worker's compensation, penalty, or rest entitlement. The Seventh Circuit's classification is affirmed.

**Operative qualifications:** Section 21103(a)'s ordinary eight-consecutive-hours-off requirement within the preceding twenty-four hours, and its ten-consecutive-hours-off requirement after twelve consecutive hours on duty, remain. Section (c)'s distinct emergency permission for a wreck or relief crew allows up to four additional hours in twenty-four for emergency-related work and ends when the track is cleared and the line reopened; that permission is not invoked here. Sections (b)(5)–(6) count interim rest outside a designated terminal, or shorter than four hours at a designated terminal, as duty. Section (b)(7) separately excludes an interim period available for at least four hours of rest at a place with suitable food and lodging facilities when a casualty, track obstruction, act of God, or derailment or major equipment failure prevents the employee from reaching the designated terminal; for derailment or major equipment failure, the cause must have been unknown and unforeseeable to the carrier or its officer or agent in charge of that employee when the employee left the designated terminal. Those predicates are neither found nor excused by this decision.

**Precedent treatment:** MCI's statutory-boundary inquiry and Town & Country's bounded agency-construction rule remain intact; neither supplies an independent deference ground or a contrary classification here.

## Holdings

### A navigation-channel shift does not transfer a continuing island between States

When a river divides around a continuing island, an established interstate boundary remains on the island's original side even if the principal downstream navigation channel shifts to its other side. Gradual erosion and accretion may move the still-running boundary channel on that original side; once it ceases running and becomes stagnant, later filling does not continue moving that fixed boundary.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The ordinary thalweg rule locates a river boundary in the main downstream navigational channel and accommodates gradual erosion and accretion. Missouri v. Kentucky supplies the island exception that prevents a navigation shift alone from transferring an established island. Indiana v. Kentucky confirms that altered flow or filling does not alone transfer sovereignty, while its distinct cession basis remains intact. Arkansas v. Tennessee supplies the distinction between gradual movement of the active old channel and its eventual cessation. These rules do not make the boundary geographically immobile at the earlier navigation shift or classify every divided-channel change as an avulsion.

The Master found that the disputed area derives from Stack Island, previously on Mississippi's side, and that erosion on its east and accretion on its west moved the island toward the Louisiana bank. The inherited exception preserves Mississippi sovereignty on that supported continuity premise. Louisiana's exception A incorrectly treats the navigation channel and the continuing boundary channel as necessarily identical. The Court overrules it without deciding a general rule for newly formed islands or avulsive severance of mainland.

**Precedent treatment:** Missouri, Indiana and the two Arkansas decisions retain their distinct island, channel and cessation functions; no universal island-stability maxim replaces them.

## Holdings

### Paid union organizing does not categorically defeat employee status

**Operative rule:** A worker who performs or seeks ordinary compensated service for an employer is not excluded from NLRA §2(3) merely because a union also pays the worker to organize. After independent examination of the statutory boundary and compatible common-law service, the Board permissibly treats union pay and organizing obligations as consistent with employee status; no rule requires coverage of every relationship regardless of actual control or a statutory exclusion.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; all nine directly adopt the statutory rule, application, limits and reversal with remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2(3)'s broad language and express exclusions contain no paid-organizer exclusion. That observation must be tested against the common-law relationship, not substituted for it. Darden and Reid require attention to service and control; ordinary agency principles permit service to two principals when serving one does not require abandonment of compatible service to the other. A union's organizing assignments and power to direct departure do not themselves eliminate the contractor's control over how electrical work is performed while employment lasts.

At Chevron's first step, the statute does not unambiguously impose the employers' categorical exclusion. MCI requires that judicial inquiry before deference. Within the resulting statutory range, the Board reasonably regards compatible work and organizing as simultaneous service. Health Care's permissible functional construction supports respecting the actual statutory predicates. Lechmere addresses access by established nonemployees; assuming these applicants are outsiders would beg the present question. Phelps Dodge confirms that hiring protection cannot be avoided merely because a person is seeking work.

The resolutions' duty to organize and leave on union notice makes the employers' control argument substantial, but does not establish that every designated worker cannot perform assigned electrical work. This Court makes no finding of actual incompatibility, sabotage or refusal to perform the work. Organizing, union allegiance and protected concerted activity cannot themselves be labeled disqualifying misconduct. Otherwise unprotected misconduct, including dishonesty, sabotage or refusal to perform the work, may support lawful action under the applicable rules. Employee status does not settle qualifications, discrimination, lawful work-time restrictions or relief. The categorical exclusion is reversed, and those remaining questions return for proper adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:** Chevron and MCI govern the sequence and boundary of permissible construction; Darden/Reid inform compatible common-law service; Health Care supplies a limited functional-construction analogy; Lechmere is distinguished at the antecedent classification stage; Phelps Dodge preserves hiring protection.

### Existing statutory exclusions and individual-remedy requirements remain intact

**Operative rule:** The paid-organizer holding adds no exemption from §2(3)'s existing exclusions and supplies no individual unfair-labor-practice or monetary award. The definition is not confined to an employee of a particular employer unless the subchapter explicitly provides otherwise. The Act still excludes agricultural labor, domestic service in a home, employment by a parent or spouse, independent contractors, supervisors, employment by a Railway Labor Act employer and employment by a person outside its employer definition; its coverage of a worker whose work ceased in connection with a current labor dispute or because of an unfair labor practice retains the condition that the worker has not obtained other regular, substantially equivalent employment.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court adopts these limits on the status ruling and remedy; no independent determination that a particular exclusion applies is made.

**Controlling explanation:** Rejection of one categorical bar is not unconditional enforcement of every Board determination. The two employers' roles differ; Ameristaff hired and referred Hansen, who was later discharged, while ten other applicants were not interviewed. Neither the two full-time officials nor all nine other union members can be treated as identical in every respect. The court of appeals must consider properly preserved remaining issues, including disputed discrimination inferences, qualifications, work-time restrictions and other lawful defenses. Any compliance inquiry must determine how many applicants would actually have been hired and the relief authorized for them. No automatic ten-person backpay award, amount, reinstatement schedule or bargaining-unit ruling follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden's insistence on the actual relationship and Phelps Dodge's hiring protections are preserved without converting legal eligibility into proof of every liability or remedy element.

### 1. The opportunity determination must use the statutory results inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2 reaches these at-large judicial elections and requires proof of unequal opportunity for minority voters to elect their actual preferred candidates under the applicable Gingles conditions and the totality of circumstances; it requires neither a Black preferred candidate nor proof that voters or legislators acted from racial motives. The challenged jurisdictions must be evaluated on their actual electoral opportunity, with contextual treatment of candidate preference, incumbency, appointments, partisanship, qualifications and electoral success; neither raw success elsewhere nor the absence of discriminatory enactment resolves the claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judgment's independent no-dilution ground requires reconsideration because its legal framing affects the opportunity inquiry itself. Chisom and Houston Lawyers establish that judicial elections fall within Section 2. Gingles supplies the required causal conditions: a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, with special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate considered. Those conditions do not alone establish liability. De Grandy requires the further totality inquiry and distinguishes meaningful opportunity from candidate race, numerical success and a duty to create every feasible district. The appellate discussion of voting because of candidate race and nondiscriminatory enactment cannot substitute a racial-motive prerequisite for the statutory inquiry. Nor can an aggregate account obscure the actual challenged electoral units. The State correctly invokes the trial court's consideration of both experts, varied elections, party affiliation and incumbency. Supported subsidiary findings remain, but deference to those findings does not settle whether the ultimate conclusion uses the correct legal criteria. The Court neither chooses the plaintiffs' expert nor categorically excludes white-only or statewide contests. Such evidence may inform local preference and opportunity in context. The remand corrects the governing premises; it does not find dilution or presume that the necessary conditions establish it.

### 2. Judicial structure warrants concrete consideration, not categorical remedial immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 comparison and remedy in judicial elections must be legally grounded, practicable and fitted to the governmental task, with genuine weight given to the linkage between the electorate and the jurisdiction served, the qualified-candidate pool and judicial administration. Those interests do not categorically defeat a proved violation or every possible correction, and racial awareness in territorial remedial design alone does not establish unconstitutional racial partiality; any independently cognizable constitutional injury remains subject to its own rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Houston Lawyers treats the State's jurisdiction-electorate linkage as legitimate and relevant, not an exemption from Section 2. Holder requires a reasonable comparison with independent legal and practical roots, rather than a size or structure selected solely to produce desired racial winners; it does not demand a uniquely ideal arrangement. Comparison, proof of dilution and relief remain separate. A possible subdistrict is not enough to establish liability, and the statute does not authorize automatic restructuring simply because another electoral arrangement can be imagined. But the lower court's alternative remedy ground cannot stand insofar as it treats independent judges, jurisdictional linkage or race-aware boundaries as necessarily incompatible with relief. Shaw and Miller require an independently cognizable deprivation beyond territorial racial design alone. They do not presume that voters' preferred judges would dispense racially partial justice or serve only members of one race. Those decisions leave actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation and inferior civic status fully reviewable under their own standards. The court below must evaluate concrete administration and candidate-pool concerns and lawful alternatives if a violation is proved. ## Holdings

### The unresolved excusable-neglect premise warrants reconsideration

**Operative rule:** Section 2106 permits a limited return for reconsideration where the combination of a potentially important governing formulation, the Government's changed legal position, a one-working-day misdirected filing, and an unexplained appellate affirmance leaves a substantial question whether the correct excusable-neglect inquiry was performed. These circumstances warrant reconsideration of Rule 4(b) in light of Pioneer; no one circumstance, including a Government concession, makes vacatur automatic.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These seven directly adopt this case-specific procedural ground and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Pioneer Investment Services construed excusable neglect in Bankruptcy Rule 9006(b)(1) to include mistake and carelessness, while requiring an assessment of all relevant circumstances and attributing freely chosen counsel's conduct to the client. Its possible relevance matters because the Government originally opposed using that formulation for criminal appeals and now accepts its relevance. The unexplained affirmance leaves uncertain whether the appellate court considered it.

The short delay and changed position do not prove an erroneous judgment. Pioneer was already available; the court below may have considered it and denied relief on a lawful ground. But that possibility does not remove the concrete value of reconsidering a legal premise that can determine whether appellate review is available. Section 2106 permits that procedural step while leaving actual excusability to the receiving court. Neither administrative convenience nor Government agreement alone supplies the reason.

The Eleventh Circuit must determine Pioneer's proper effect under Rule 4(b) and arrange any necessary District Court findings. Relevant circumstances include prejudice, delay and its effects, the reason for delay and its reasonable controllability, and good faith; no factor is invariably decisive under Pioneer. Counsel attribution remains. The Court does not decide that all Rule 4(b) applications are identical to bankruptcy enlargement, transplant Rule 9006(b)(2)–(3)'s separate restrictions, find excusable neglect, accept the late appeal, or adjudicate conviction or sentence.

**Precedent treatment:** Pioneer supplies the potentially consequential formulation at its actual Chapter 11 scope and retains counsel attribution and its rule-specific limits. Henry's distinction between reconsideration and merits relief is applied.

## Holdings

### The ordinary covered-remand bar applies in bankruptcy removals

**Operative rule:** When a removed case is remanded to the State court from which it came because of a timely raised defect in removal procedure or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, §1447(d), read with §1447(c), bars appellate review even if removal also invoked §1452. An order remanding a case removed pursuant to §1443 may be reviewed on appeal or otherwise; that then-applicable exception is not implicated here. The rule does not declare every bankruptcy remand unreviewable.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices identified in Opinion Topology, supplies nine direct votes for this holding and its application. The two concurrences add no limitation to those joins and no independent majority ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Thermtron requires reading the review restriction with the grounds listed in §1447(c). A procedural-remand motion must be made within thirty days after removal; lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is a distinct covered ground. The district court remanded this action for untimely removal, and the remand request was timely raised. The actual stated ground thus falls within the ordinary bar. Gravitt makes clear that an asserted error in a covered remand ground does not open an appeal to decide its correctness.

Rice applies the ordinary remand-review restriction across removal statutes absent contrary congressional direction. Section 1452(b) separately permits equitable remand and restricts review of the decisions it covers. It grants no entitlement to review forbidden by §1447(d). Germain requires giving overlapping statutes effect where they do not positively conflict. The specialized bankruptcy provision can perform its additional work without displacing the ordinary restriction. Petitioner's exclusivity theory mistakes an additional statutory regime for a repeal of a compatible rule.

The Sixth Circuit therefore properly dismissed the attempted appeal. The Court does not determine the ultimate scope of equitable grounds under §1452(b), the merits of bankruptcy jurisdiction or removal, the rent claim, or reviewability of remands outside the present statutory grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Thermtron supplies the covered-ground boundary; Rice supplies application across removal statutes; Gravitt preserves the restriction despite asserted error; Germain supplies coexistence rather than implied exclusivity.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Rice, 327 U.S. 742:** Applied to the ordinary restriction's operation across removal statutes absent contrary direction.
- **Gravitt v. Southwestern Bell Telephone Co., 430 U.S. 723:** Applied to prevent review of a covered remand merely because it is alleged to be erroneous.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, concurring.** They join the Court's opinion with the express understanding that the present covered-ground holding does not decide reviewability of a remand under Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill. Thermtron held both that a particular unauthorized remand was improper and that the ordinary review bar did not cover remands outside §1447(c). Cohill later recognized discretion to remand remaining state-law claims after elimination of the sole federal claim, despite the absence of express statutory authorization. Cohill did not decide whether §1447(d) bars review of that distinct remand. Neither today's affirmance nor these two Justices' reservation resolves that issue in either direction.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurring.** They also find an independent review bar in §1452(b). Equity there means fairness or appropriateness, not the former division between legal and equitable pleading. Failure to remove promptly fits that meaning. Making a statutory time bar reviewable while equivalent delay labeled laches is immune would make appellate jurisdiction depend on a label. Hernandez v. Brakegate supplies the modern sense of equity; Sykes v. Texas Air explains the incoherence of label-dependent review. The chapter's structure and the 1977 House report's parallel treatment of remand review support a supplementary bankruptcy-remand regime, while Timbers directs attention to the coherent statutory scheme.

They give an alternative explanation as well: even if a procedural or jurisdictional impediment were called legal rather than equitable, equity may describe the choice to remand rather than dismiss. Section 1452(b) permits that fair disposition without requiring the impediment itself to fit an old equitable category. Their position concerns remands of claims related to bankruptcy. It does not insulate lack of subject-matter jurisdiction in a retained case: a decision not to remand may lack a separate immediate appeal while jurisdiction remains open to district-court reconsideration and eventual review through an otherwise proper channel. ## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author and joins | Scope |

## Holdings

### Ultimate Miranda custody requires independent federal review

**Operative rule:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would cause a reasonable person to understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question requiring independent federal habeas review. Section 2254(d)'s presumption for factual determinations does not attach to that ultimate custody conclusion; historical events and supported credibility findings remain subject to the statute's own conditions.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt the classification, statutory limits and vacatur/remand. Thomas dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller's functional approach asks what the respective courts must decide, not whether a conclusion can be described as an inference. State courts determine what happened: the setting, words, conduct and credible accounts. The further question applies a national constitutional boundary to those established events. Independent application gives the objective arrest-equivalence rule in Berkemer and Stansbury consistent legal content without retrying the events.

Patton and Witt show that some assessments under legal standards remain factual because their subject matter and observational advantages support that allocation. They do not make Miranda custody presumptively factual. Nor does Miller turn every ultimate inference or mixed issue into a question reviewed anew. Custody's objective legal function supplies the distinction here. Wright established no general rule requiring acceptance of a reasonable state legal decision; Withrow allows this Miranda claim while retaining its ordinary habeas limits.

The federal courts applied the factual presumption to the ultimate noncustody conclusion. The judgment must therefore be vacated and the case remanded for independent application to the established circumstances. Voluntary arrival, accusations, duration, setting, assurances of freedom to leave and actual departure must receive their proper significance together. Private police intentions or later surveillance allegations do not become communicated custody facts by assertion. The Court decides neither ultimate custody nor suppression, harmlessness, an independent coercion claim, retroactivity or entitlement to release; no abandoned procedural default is restored.

**Precedent treatment:** Miller's functional fact/law distinction is applied to Miranda custody; Berkemer and Stansbury's objective standard is preserved; Patton and Witt retain their distinct factual classifications; Wright and Withrow retain their actual limits.

### The statutory protection of historical factfinding remains unchanged

**Operative rule:** Under the governing §2254(d), a competent state court's determination of a factual issue after a merits hearing in a proceeding involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by a written finding, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia, is presumed correct subject to the statute's exceptions. Excluding ultimate custody from that presumption neither establishes an exception for any historical finding here nor permits disregard of supported credibility determinations.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts this boundary on independent review; it makes no case-specific finding that a statutory exception applies.

**Controlling explanation:** The presumption yields when the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears, or the respondent admits that: (1) the factual dispute's merits were unresolved; (2) the factfinding procedure did not afford a full and fair hearing; (3) material facts were inadequately developed; (4) the state court lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter or applicant; (5) an indigent applicant was unconstitutionally denied appointed counsel; (6) the applicant lacked a full, fair and adequate hearing; or (7) the proceeding otherwise denied due process. The eighth exception applies when the pertinent state record is produced as the statute provides and, considered as a whole, does not fairly support the factual determination.

At a federal evidentiary hearing, once due proof of the state factual determination is made, the applicant bears the burden to show its error by convincing evidence unless a circumstance in(1)–(7) is shown, otherwise appears or is admitted, or the federal court concludes under(8) that the pertinent state record as a whole lacks fair support. Those conditions and allocations remain intact. A ruling on papers does not itself prove failure of the statutory hearing requirement. The remand corrects classification of the ultimate legal application, not any established historical fact, and creates no categorical right to a new evidentiary hearing or writ.

**Precedent treatment:** Section 2254(d)'s existing factual framework and Miller's respect for historical facts are preserved; no replacement general deference rule is adopted.

- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420, and Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318, April 26, 1994: the objective arrest-equivalence standard is preserved; communicated circumstances matter and undisclosed police intentions do not independently establish custody.
- Patton v. Yount, 467 U.S. 1025, and Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412: their factual classifications remain in their distinct settings; use of a legal standard alone does not decide classification.

Section 2254(d)'s factual presumption, all eight exceptions and its conditional convincing-evidence burden remain applicable to historical findings. Neither review classification nor remand establishes custody, a constitutional violation, harmlessness or a right to the writ.

## Holdings

### A surviving aggravator does not dispose of the independent assistance claim

The challenged Ake and sentence-review component must be vacated for further consideration; removing future dangerousness as an aggravator does not itself correct lost assistance in answering evidence the jury heard.

The Fourth Circuit treated surviving vileness as sufficient without examining the assumed loss of assistance. Lawful eligibility does not establish absence of an effect on that choice. Relevant prosecution evidence must be considered, and no favorable missing expert testimony or actual influence may be invented. Virginia's nonweighing character preserves the distinct eligibility finding; it does not dispose of this independent claim.

Brecht covers preserved Doyle error and O'Neal preserved personal-intent error; neither already decides this Ake category. ## Holdings

### Review follows the certified order, including its anterior displacement question

**Operative rule:** When a court of appeals permits an interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b), its jurisdiction reaches issues fairly included within the certified order, rather than only the particular question formulated for certification. Here the maritime displacement question is fairly included because it is the premise of the appealed partial-summary-judgment order; this authority reaches no unrelated order in the lawsuit.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These eight directly adopt the rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition on this component and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written certification that an order involves a controlling question of law, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that immediate review may materially advance ultimate termination. A timely application within ten days allows the court of appeals to permit review in its discretion; an application alone does not stay proceedings without an authorized stay order. The certified object of review is the order.

The District Court relied on federal displacement when it granted Yamaha partial summary judgment against future earnings and punitive damages but denied it against loss of society and support or services. Both parties sought certification and obtained permitted appeals. The Third Circuit could examine the anterior displacement issue, which both sides briefed and which underlies the damages rulings, even though the certified question described recoverable damages under a federal maritime cause.

Swint v. Chambers County Commission rejected adding a separate, otherwise nonappealable county defense to the officers' collateral-order appeal for convenience. It preserved §1292(b)'s distinct authorized route. Here that route was actually followed. The Court neither enlarges collateral-order jurisdiction nor grants appellate power over every issue or order associated with the case.

**Precedent treatment:** Swint's limit on unauthorized connected review is preserved and distinguished from this permitted appeal of a certified order.

### State death remedies survive in this nonseafarer territorial-waters case

**Operative rule:** Moragne's federal maritime wrongful-death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state wrongful-death and survival remedies for a nonseafarer killed in territorial waters when no applicable federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule supplies an exclusive recovery regime. This rule concerns persons outside the Jones Act seaman and pertinent federal maritime-employment classes; it does not enlarge remedies within Congress's occupied seaman or high-seas fields.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the remedies rule, its limits, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Moragne v. States Marine Lines supplied a federal wrongful-death action to remove a gap in maritime protection. Adding that remedy did not require withdrawal of state death remedies traditionally available in territorial waters. The Court assumes without deciding that Moragne extends to this nonseafarer and includes a survival action; even on those assumptions, the federal remedy is not an exclusive damages code here.

Miles v. Apex Marine Corp. aligned the remedy for a seaman with Congress's employment-related compensation choices. Offshore Logistics v. Tallentire preserves the Death on the High Seas Act's exclusive regime within its geographical field. Neither source extends that occupied field to a child who was neither a seaman nor a covered longshore worker and who died in Puerto Rico's territorial waters.

Yamaha's appeal to uniformity cannot replace those statutes' coverage conditions. Damages are substantive, so American Dredging's separate protection of state forum administration does not alone decide the issue. The relevant ground is the established coexistence of territorial-water death remedies outside a conflicting federal regime, not a general permission for state law to alter characteristic maritime rights.

The Third Circuit therefore correctly rejected displacement as the basis for Yamaha's partial-summary-judgment motion. The Court does not select Pennsylvania or Puerto Rico law, determine what either wrongful-death or survival law permits, or award any category or amount of damages. Liability standards, defect, negligence, causation, and punitive liability remain unresolved.

**Precedent treatment:** Moragne is applied as an added federal remedy without automatic displacement. Miles and Offshore Logistics retain their statutory boundaries. American Dredging is distinguished as a forum-practice decision, not an independent validation of substantive damages.
