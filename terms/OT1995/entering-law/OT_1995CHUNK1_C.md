## Standards and Tests selection: Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

### Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

**Governing question:** Whether a State may continue psychiatric confinement under insanity-acquittee authority after conceding that the acquittee is no longer mentally ill.

**Current rule:** The State may not continue that confinement solely because the acquittee cannot prove harmlessness. The acquittal supplies neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric authority after the illness predicate disappears. Continued psychiatric custody requires constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing its own proper grounds; ordinary civil commitment requires current mental illness and dangerousness proved by clear and convincing evidence, and additional procedure cannot supply a missing substantive ground. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure an indefinite psychiatric regime requiring a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee to prove nondangerousness. Any independently lawful restraint must rest on its own authority and procedures; the holding does not prescribe a universal preventive-detention burden or scrutiny tier.

**Authority by component:**
- Ended predicate and absence of punitive authority: [Foucha v. Louisiana, “1. The insanity judgment no longer authorizes this psychiatric confinement”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Foucha_v_Louisiana_merits_1992-05-18.md), May 18, 1992; White Part II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).
- Appropriate new grounds and procedures; ordinary civil-commitment burden: Foucha, “2. Psychiatric custody requires appropriate grounds and procedures,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (five). Addington's existing burden is retained within that channel.
- Limits of the asserted preventive alternative: Foucha, “3. Public safety does not sustain this unbounded dangerousness regime,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).

**Present operation effective:** Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992.

**Open question:** Materially narrower preventive regimes, maximum duration of an otherwise lawful commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses remain undecided. The equal-protection plurality supplies no controlling rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

### Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern equal protection of commitment proof and family participation?

**Current rule:** Kentucky's clear-and-convincing burden for adult intellectual-disability commitment, compared with reasonable-doubt proof for mental illness, fails rational-basis review because the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not justify the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Lawful equalization and severability must be addressed; Addington's federal minimum is unchanged and no national reasonable-doubt requirement or automatic release follows. Separately, continuing family knowledge rationally supports guardians' and immediate relatives' party participation for lifelong developmental disability. Such participation is not categorically unfair where the adult has counsel and effective opportunity to contest evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives cannot replace statutory grounds, control decision or suppress the adult's position. Heightened scrutiny remains undecided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded modification and distinct notice authority: [MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co — The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/MCI_Telecommunications_Corp_v_AT_and_T_merits_1994-06-17.md#public-projection), 1994-06-17. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co, 1994-06-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Limited deferral or waiver is not categorically foreclosed, but no particular waiver is approved. Failure of the special-circumstances-or-conditions predicate is a four-Justice alternative, not a separate controlling holding. No universal major-questions doctrine is created; the statute’s permissible flexibility remains available within the actual delegation.

**Holdings navigation:** [The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-modification-power-does-not-permit-this-replacement-of-mandatory-filing).

## Standards and Tests selection: Professional direction and genuine NLRA supervisory authority

### Professional direction and genuine NLRA supervisory authority

**Governing question:** How §2(11) distinguishes skilled patient-care direction from supervisory status.

**Current rule:** The Board may distinguish a professional employee’s skilled direction of care from genuine authority in the employer’s interest to perform any of §2(11)’s listed supervisory functions: hire, transfer, suspend, lay off, recall, promote, discharge, assign, reward, discipline, responsibly direct, adjust grievances or effectively recommend such action. The authority must involve independent judgment and not merely routine or clerical action. No hiring-or-firing power is indispensable, and genuinely held authority may qualify despite infrequent exercise. Professional judgment may be independent judgment, but skilled care alone does not establish the required supervisory authority. This is a permissible distinction, not a mandatory professional exemption or a holding that patient care never serves the employer’s interest.

**Authority by component:**

- Permissible functional distinction and all statutory predicates: [NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America — The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/NLRB_v_Health_Care_Retirement_Corp_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. All five directly adopt the permissible-construction ground; Stone-Zsela's proposed mandatory construction appears only in his concurrence.

**Present operation effective:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, 1994-05-23.

**Holdings navigation:** [The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-board-may-distinguish-skilled-direction-from-genuine-supervisory-authority).

## Standards and Tests selection: Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

### Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

**Governing question:** Which circumstances determine custody for Miranda warnings.

**Current rule:** Custody turns on the objective circumstances confronting the person questioned and whether a reasonable person in that position would understand freedom of movement to be restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest. An officer’s uncommunicated view of the person as suspect or witness does not enter the calculation. Beliefs conveyed by words or conduct may affect the objective setting to the extent they would affect that reasonable understanding. Communicating suspicion does not itself establish custody, and an officer’s private intent cannot substitute for the objective inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective arrest-equivalent restraint and communicated beliefs: [Stansbury v. California — Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Stansbury_v_California_merits_1994-04-26.md#public-projection), 1994-04-26. The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Present operation effective:** Stansbury v. California, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** [Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#custody-turns-on-the-circumstances-communicated-to-the-person-questioned).

## Standards and Tests selection: Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

### Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims?

**Current rule:** A full and fair state opportunity does not itself bar federal habeas review of a claim that a conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda; Powell's Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction does not extend to that claim. Ordinary habeas gates, merits and harmlessness remain. A court may not grant relief on an independent postwarning involuntariness theory neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent when the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of custody and warnings alone is not consent; later presentation remains subject to pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review: [Withrow v. Williams — A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/withrow_merits_1993-04-21.md), 1993-04-21. Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation: [Withrow v. Williams — An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/withrow_merits_1993-04-21.md), 1993-04-21. ## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

### ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

**Governing question:** Classification as an employee under ERISA's circular employee definition.

**Current rule:** ERISA incorporates general common-law agency, rather than any particular State's law or a substitute based on benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship, including control over the manner and means of work, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, integration into the regular business, the hiring party's business, benefits and tax treatment. No factor, contractual label or expectation of benefits is decisive. Employee classification precedes the resulting vesting or forfeiture inquiry; this construction does not extend to statutes with broader express definitions, including the FLSA's suffer-or-permit definition.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory construction and all-incidents inquiry: [Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Nationwide_Mutual_Insurance_Co_v_Darden_merits_1992-03-24.md), March 24, 1992, “ERISA employee status follows general common-law agency”; Souter's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Darden, March 24, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** [Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992) — ERISA employee classification](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#nationwide-mutual-insurance-co-v-darden-503-us-318-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy

### Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy

**Governing question:** When the Board may balance a nonemployee organizer's need for entry against the employer's property rights.

**Current rule:** Nonemployee organizers must demonstrate that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before balancing may begin. Greater convenience or improved organizing success does not establish that threshold. The Board may assess actual obstacles through its expertise, but it may not replace the controlling statutory inaccessibility requirement with universal balancing that treats alternatives as merely another factor. The inquiry requires neither organizing success nor exhaustion of every imaginable contact technique. Employee solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, and lawful public-property advocacy remain governed by their distinct rules; genuine isolation by living or working location remains within the inaccessibility inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Antecedent inaccessibility, communication-versus-success distinction, and rejection of universal *Jean Country* balancing: [Lechmere v. NLRB — Nonemployee access requires demonstrated inaccessibility before balancing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Lechmere_v_NLRB_merits_1992-01-27.md), January 27, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lechmere*, January 27, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** [Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, 502 U.S. 527 (1992) — Nonemployee organizing access](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#lechmere-inc-v-nlrb-502-us-527-1992).

## Standing State selection: 3. Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. ### Psychiatric confinement

#### Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the continuing illness-and-danger basis of insanity commitment; initial verdict-based commitment and rejection of an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling remain intact.
- O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975): applied to the requirement that confinement end when its authorizing basis disappears; the Court does not equate a conditional-release recommendation with a finding of harmlessness.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the required relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose.

##### Whether Louisiana may preserve psychiatric custody without new adequate grounds and proceedings

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Material application:** A counseled hearing about danger alone cannot supply the absent illness predicate. Ordinary civil commitment requires clear and convincing proof of present illness and dangerousness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Vitek v. Jones, 445 U.S. 480 (1980): applied to psychiatric institutionalization's distinct liberty deprivation and need for appropriate grounds and procedures.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the need for an independently authorized commitment proceeding after the initial basis expires.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418 (1979): applied unchanged to the clear-and-convincing burden for ordinary civil commitment; that burden is not made universal for every preventive custody channel.
- Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107 (1966): relied on for the ordinary protections attending a new civil commitment after the prior detention basis ends; no separate majority equal-protection holding is created here.

##### Whether indefinite psychiatric confinement may rest on failure to prove nondangerousness after mental illness ends

**Holding:** Louisiana's scheme permitting indefinite psychiatric confinement of a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee because he has not proved nondangerousness violates due process. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure the absence of a carefully limited substantive authority; the Court leaves materially narrower preventive regimes undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-C, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient rejection of Louisiana's asserted alternative preventive justification. The holding adopts no new general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness proof standard.

**Material application:** Periodic review does not cure an indefinite scheme without carefully limited substantive authority. Salerno’s focused pretrial regime does not justify this post-acquittal confinement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): distinguished because its confined pretrial detention authority and safeguards do not authorize Louisiana's indefinite post-acquittal psychiatric restraint.
- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the absence of authority to punish an insanity acquittee as a convicted offender.

### Equality and procedure in civil commitment

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S. 584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.


## Verbatim Holdings selection: administrative-law / Tariff-filing modification authority

### Tariff-filing modification authority

#### [MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/MCI_Telecommunications_Corp_v_AT_and_T_merits_1994-06-17.md)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

### Skilled employees and supervisory status

#### [NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, 511 U.S. 571](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/NLRB_v_Health_Care_Retirement_Corp_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1964.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority

**Holding:** The Board permissibly interprets NLRA §2(11), in the context of the Act's protection of skilled and professional employees, to distinguish direction incidental to skilled patient care from genuine supervisory authority exercised for the employer. On this record, its functional construction and supported determination that the four licensed practical nurses are employees must be sustained; no occupational exemption or independent judicial substitute for the agency's rationale is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. All five directly adopt the permissible-construction ground; Stone-Zsela's proposed mandatory construction appears only in his concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fall River Dyeing & Finishing Corp. v. NLRB, 482 U.S. 27 (1987): applies review for rational interpretation consistent with the Act and substantial evidentiary support.
- NLRB v. Yeshiva University, 444 U.S. 672 (1980): uses its distinction between ordinary professional duties and management, while preserving professional/institutional overlap and the separate managerial question.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves binding statutory boundaries; it does not settle this classification tension.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943), and National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: permit reliance on the agency's discernible actual rationale, not an invented substitute.
- Packard Motor Car Co. v. NLRB, 330 U.S. 485 (1947): distinguishes its pre-amendment employer-interest discussion from the later statutory definition.
- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, January 24, 1994: preserves Board judgment within an enacted delegation, without authorizing disregard of statutory text.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2(11)'s alternative authorities are hiring, transfer, suspension, layoff, recall, promotion, discharge, assignment, reward, discipline, responsible direction, grievance adjustment and effective recommendation of such action. Any one may suffice, even if rarely exercised, but exercise must serve the employer and require independent judgment beyond routine or clerical action. Hiring or firing is not indispensable. Professional judgment can be independent judgment exercised through genuine supervisory authority; it does not itself establish that authority. The sustained construction is permissible, not compulsory for all future Boards.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit's supervisory-status ruling is reversed and remanded, 5–4. Give effect to the supported Board employee-status determination and decide independently preserved remaining issues. No backpay award or finding of every unfair-labor-practice element is supplied.

### ERISA employee classification

#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

### Nonemployee organizing access

#### Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, 502 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-970.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

##### When nonemployee organizers may obtain access despite nondiscriminatory exclusion from private property

**Holding:** For a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy, nonemployee organizers must show that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before the Board may balance a need for entry against the employer's property rights; greater convenience or organizing success does not satisfy that threshold. This workforce's community residence, public approaches and substantial direct contacts defeat the asserted exception, while discriminatory exclusion, employees' own solicitation and genuinely inaccessible workforces remain governed by their distinct rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices control both the statutory rule and denial of enforcement.

**Material application:** Employees lived in the surrounding community; organizers used public approaches and obtained direct home-contact information for roughly one fifth of the workforce. Few authorization cards did not establish an inability to communicate. Board expertise remains relevant to actual access obstacles, within the judicially established statutory rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither organizational success nor exhaustion of every imaginable communication technique. Employees' own solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, lawful public-property advocacy, and genuinely inaccessible workforces retain their separate treatment. No new general deference doctrine or constitutional right of entry is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit judgment is reversed and enforcement denied. The Board's order is set aside insofar as it requires nonemployee-organizer access on these facts; no remand for a new balancing exercise is ordered, and the judgment permits no interference with employees' distinct organizing rights or lawful public activity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *NLRB v. Babcock & Wilcox Co.*, 351 U.S. 105 — applied and reaffirmed: employee and nonemployee activity differ, and demonstrated inaccessibility precedes balancing under a nondiscriminatory rule.
- *Republic Aviation Corp. v. NLRB*, 324 U.S. 793 — employees' own organizing rights remain distinct and are not narrowed here.
- *Central Hardware Co. v. NLRB*, 407 U.S. 539 — applied for the prior showing of a need for nonemployee access.
- *Hudgens v. NLRB*, 424 U.S. 507 — accommodation language is preserved within Babcock's nonemployee threshold, not treated as authorization for universal balancing.
- *Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Carpenters*, 436 U.S. 180 — applied for the heavy burden concerning alternative access and the separate discrimination route.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, and *Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc.*, 497 U.S. 116 — general deference remains; a later agency approach must respect a governing judicial statutory construction.
- *Jean Country*, 291 N.L.R.B. 11 — rejected insofar as its universal balancing replaces the antecedent inaccessibility inquiry for nondiscriminatory exclusion of nonemployee organizers; other contexts are not adjudicated.

### Custodial interrogation

#### [Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Stansbury_v_California_merits_1994-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5770.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned

**Holding:** Miranda custody exists upon formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from all objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee’s position would understand them; an officer’s uncommunicated view that the person is or is not a suspect, and the interviewee’s undisclosed subjective belief, have no independent role. An officer’s words or conduct conveying suspicion matter only to the extent they affect that objective understanding: communicated suspicion is not automatically custody, and undisclosed beliefs bearing on a witness’s credibility do not thereby become independent custody factors.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): applied to the custodial-interrogation trigger; no new exception to warnings is created.
- Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), and California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983): applied for the arrest-equivalent restraint standard; station questioning and suspect status alone remain insufficient.
- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984): applied to exclude uncommunicated intentions from the objective custody inquiry.
- Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976): applied to distinguish investigative focus from custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual custody, independent coercion, admissibility, harmlessness and capital punishment are not decided. An undisclosed belief bearing on witness credibility does not become an independent custody factor. Withrow and Powell's different review and remedy rules remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded to reconsider custody without the private investigative-focus criterion. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release or resentencing is ordered; ultimate custody and any properly reached consequences remain for the state court.

#### [Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Davis_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1949.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Holding:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Holding:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Holding:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides postwaiver invocation in the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules. It does not independently resolve direct Fifth Amendment operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, initial-warning adequacy, invalid initial waiver or the separate October 20 interview and evidence dispute. Section 3501 was not the Government's merits ground and supplies no alternative holding on construction, applicability or constitutionality. Withrow's collateral and coercion rules and Stansbury's objective custody inquiry remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court of Military Appeals is affirmed unanimously on the postwaiver invocation issue. No exclusion, new trial or remand follows; the separate October 20 dispute and any §3501 ground remain undecided.


## Verbatim Holdings selection: criminal-procedure / Wright v. West, 505 U.S. 277 (1992)

#### Wright v. West, 505 U.S. 277 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-542.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the actual larceny trial record satisfies Jackson's constitutional sufficiency requirement

**Holding:** On the trial record here, the quantity and recency of West’s possession of distinctive items from the same theft, considered with the jury’s permissible rejection of his incomplete account, allow a rational trier of fact to find the elements of larceny beyond a reasonable doubt; the exhausted Jackson insufficiency claim therefore warrants no habeas relief. The reviewing court must assess the evidence and permissible credibility choices favorably to the prosecution, without retrying guilt, shifting the State’s burden or treating possession as an invariably sufficient substitute for proof of theft.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas and Scalia in the principal opinion, White in his express endorsement of its sufficiency demonstration, O'Connor with Blackmun and Stevens in their concurrence, Kennedy in his concurrence, and Stone in his separate concurrence expressly adopt this same record-specific Jackson proposition: eight Justices. Souter is excluded because his ground is Teague-only. No complete opinion commands a majority, and no broader rule is synthesized under Marks.

**Material application:** More than fifteen distinctive items from the same theft were found in West's residence two to four weeks afterward, and the jury could reject gaps in his incomplete flea-market account. Affirmative possession evidence and permissible credibility choices together sustain the inference; disbelief alone supplies no missing element. The offense threshold was $100 and the prior felony was credibility material, not propensity proof of this theft.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new corroboration requirement, proof quantum, burden shift or categorical possession-to-theft inference is adopted. The facial validity of every possession inference, an independent instruction claim, the Elkins new-evidence claim and a general habeas legal-review standard remain undecided. The broader separate writings do not establish a new Teague rule or replace independent review with general acceptance of a reasonable state decision; Teague's existing operation, Miller's legal/fact distinction and the then-existing §2254(d) remain. Cosby's contextual considerations do not become a constitutional five-factor test. A Teague bar and merits denial are not combined under Marks.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's direction to issue a writ on insufficiency grounds is reversed. Remand preserves other properly remaining claims, including the Elkins claim, under their own requirements. No new trial, acquittal, release, expansion of custody or disposition of all habeas claims is ordered.

**Material judgment:** All nine Justices support reversal and remand; eight expressly support the narrow sufficiency holding and Souter supports the judgment on his separate noncontrolling Teague ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied unchanged to the actual record with the prescribed weight for rational jury inferences.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 — retained; the prosecution must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt and no burden shifts to West.
- Barnes v. United States, 412 U.S. 837 — permissive-inference principle retained as background, not an automatic equation of any possession with theft.

### Miranda habeas review and unpleaded coercion

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.
