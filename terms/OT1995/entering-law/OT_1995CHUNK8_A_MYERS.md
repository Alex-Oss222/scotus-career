## Criminal Procedure

### Appellate finality

#### United States v. Ibarra, 502 U.S. 1 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1713.  
**Decided:** October 15, 1991.

##### Whether a timely reconsideration motion renewing an abandoned suppression ground postpones appellate finality

**Holding:** A single timely motion asking a district court to alter a suppression ruling renders that ruling nonfinal for appeal purposes until the motion is decided, even when the motion renews a ground the Government earlier abandoned. The full appeal period begins upon disposition of the motion; the rule does not decide whether the renewed ground remains waived on the merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; unanimous eight-Justice participating Court.

**Material application:** The Government's January 30 notice of appeal was filed within thirty days after the January 3 denial of reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Bad-faith and successive motions, the substantive effect of abandonment, and the Fourth Amendment validity of the search are unresolved. Existing appeal periods and preservation rules are unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the appeal is vacated, and the case is remanded for further appellate proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** United States v. Healy supplies suspension of finality on a timely criminal rehearing request. United States v. Dieter forecloses an exception based on the kind or strength of the asserted error. Department of Banking v. Pink is distinguished because its filing sought no substantive alteration of adjudicated rights.

### Capital sentencing

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the preservation bar on the jury-heinousness instruction claim in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds.

##### Whether the judge's conscious-victim strangulation application supplies a sufficiently narrowed heinousness aggravator

**Holding:** A sentencing judge may apply an otherwise vague heinousness aggravator through an authoritative and sufficiently specific state narrowing construction. Florida's consistently recognized conscious-victim strangulation construction supplies adequate guidance here; inconsistent applications in other factual settings do not establish that this judge lacked guidance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens do not join this disposition because they would require correction of the separate jury defect.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated judicial application and record.

**Material application:** Florida's identified narrowing decisions address the fear and conscious suffering of strangulation. The victim's resistance, fear and strangulation support that concrete construction; the judge did not merely repeat the statutory adjectives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves neither every Florida heinousness decision nor the bare instruction given to this jury, and it does not decide a different victim's condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the preserved constitutional challenge to the judge's narrowed heinousness application; that factor supplies no basis for relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, and *Maynard v. Cartwright*, 486 U.S. 356, require meaningful narrowing. *Walton v. Arizona*, 497 U.S. 639, permits reliance on authoritative state constructions and presumes the judge's familiarity with them. *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, preserves a narrowed heinousness category without approving every later application.

##### Whether submission of a lawful but unsupported coldness aggravator establishes that a general advisory jury recommendation relied on it

**Holding:** Submitting a legally available aggravating circumstance without evidentiary support does not itself establish that a Florida advisory jury relied on it in a general death recommendation that identifies no aggravator findings. The asserted inference of jury-coldness error is rejected without a finding that the jury actually rejected the factor or resolution of a legally invalid instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices. Stone reserves this independent question, Blackmun and Stevens would include the submission in correction, and Scalia does not join this holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at this narrow factual-insufficiency inference and general advisory recommendation.

**Material application:** Cold, calculated and premeditated murder was a lawful aggravator, but proof of its heightened premeditation requirement failed. The jury identified no aggravator findings, so submission alone cannot establish its reliance. The judge's express coldness finding establishes a separate error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding makes no factual jury finding, does not assimilate every capital recommendation to a guilt verdict, does not decide that all instructional errors are harmless, and leaves broader capital-jury doctrine open. Legally invalid alternatives remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Griffin v. United States*, December 3, 1991, is extended narrowly through its factual-versus-legal distinction to this asserted inference from a general advisory recommendation. *Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359, and *Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298, retain their rules for legally invalid alternatives. *Estelle v. McGuire*, December 4, 1991, retains whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review, which neither removes the preservation bar nor supplies an automatic inference of factual reliance here.

##### Whether the judge's express weighing of the evidentially unsupported coldness aggravator creates constitutional error

**Holding:** When a Florida capital sentencing judge expressly weighs an aggravator that the state supreme court holds unsupported by the evidence, that invalid factor creates Eighth Amendment error in the weighing decision. Lawful reweighing, constitutional harmless-error review or resentencing is required; other valid aggravators do not themselves cure the error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B-1 of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices. Scalia rejects the constitutional weighing-error premise.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for documented reliance on an unsupported aggravator.

**Material application:** The judge expressly found and weighed heightened coldness, which Florida held unproved. Proof of ordinary murder premeditation does not establish this additional aggravating requirement. Surviving death eligibility therefore does not eliminate the actual weighing error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The convictions and mitigation findings are not disturbed. Removal of an aggravator does not itself require a life sentence or a new penalty jury. The no-mitigation finding does not mean no mitigating evidence was presented.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence requires a supported determination under *Chapman*, lawful reweighing if Florida law authorizes it, or resentencing. The separate holding below identifies why the existing appellate treatment fails to provide that correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, requires adequate correction of capital weighing error. *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, applies that principle to unsupported Florida aggravators and the actual sentencing record. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, supplies the correction principle for this documented reliance; its habeas retroactivity and default reservations do not bar direct review. *Zant v. Stephens*, 462 U.S. 862, is distinguished because its nonweighing setting does not make surviving eligibility an automatic cure. *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, supplies constitutional harmlessness with the State bearing the burden.

##### Whether Florida's proportionality discussion and surviving-factor count constitutionally cure the weighing error

**Holding:** A state appellate court affirming this sentence must substantively establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the invalid aggravator did not contribute to the sentence, or provide another authorized constitutional cure. Florida's existing proportionality discussion, count of surviving factors and mixed citations do not establish that inquiry; no verbal formula is either required or sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B-2 and IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; six Justices. White and Thomas accept the error framework but find the existing review sufficient; Scalia would affirm on a separate ground.

**Material application:** Proportionality compares sentences across cases; it does not itself determine whether this invalid factor influenced this sentencing balance. Three valid factors and no found mitigation may bear on harmlessness but do not establish the missing inquiry. The State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither undertakes reweighing nor determines the outcome of lawful correction. It requires no prescribed phrase and rejects a bare harmless-error label as sufficient. No automatic life sentence, mandatory new penalty jury, new mitigation finding or new habeas standard is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand to Florida for supported *Chapman* review, lawful reweighing if Florida law permits, or resentencing. The convictions remain intact; the state courts must choose and perform an adequate lawful correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, retains the State's burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, and *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, require corrective analysis of the sentence and actual record rather than proportionality alone. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, preserves the inadequacy of factor counting and proportionality as standalone cures.

#### Morgan v. Illinois, 504 U.S. 719 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5118.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether an automatic-death juror is disqualified from a capital sentencing jury

**Holding:** When a State provides a jury to decide a capital sentence, due process requires an impartial jury. A juror who would automatically impose death upon conviction regardless of the facts and required consideration of mitigation is subject to challenge for cause; if such a juror serves and death is imposed, that sentence cannot be executed.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** Illinois requires consideration of aggravation and mitigation. A juror who fixes death in advance regardless of the evidence refuses that task; the conclusion a qualified juror reaches after genuine consideration is different.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State need not provide jury sentencing, but a jury it supplies must be impartial. General support for capital punishment is not disqualifying, and the Court prescribes no weight for mitigation or required punishment. Witt's substantial-impairment standard remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sentence affirmance is reversed and remanded, 7–2. The conviction is undisturbed; no automatic life sentence or constitutional right to jury sentencing is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985), and Adams v. Texas, 448 U.S. 38 (1980): applied to views disabling lawful performance; their governing substantial-impairment rule is not replaced.
- Witherspoon v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 510 (1968): its impartiality principle applies to the opposite categorical disability; ordinary death-penalty views remain permissible.
- Ross v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 81 (1988): its automatic-death-juror principle is expressly adopted; its result rested on removal of the identified juror.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and the received mitigation line: retained as requiring a real opportunity for individualized consideration, not a prescribed result.

##### Whether requested inquiry must expose automatic-death views despite general assurances of fairness and obedience

**Holding:** On a capital defendant’s request, voir dire must adequately inquire into whether prospective sentencing jurors would automatically impose death regardless of lawful consideration of the facts and mitigation. General promises of fairness and obedience to instructions did not cover that subject here; the denied inquiry requires reversal of the death-sentence affirmance and remand, without disturbing the conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** Generic assurances can conceal a prospective juror's belief that automatic death is fair. One juror's volunteered disqualifying answer and removal do not show adequate subject coverage for the others. The defendant need not prove answers to an inquiry he was forbidden to make.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual bias is found in a seated juror. Trial judges retain control over wording and follow-up, and no fixed questionnaire or new proof quantum is required. Unrelated voir dire omissions, collateral review and retroactivity remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death sentence is set aside for lawful further sentencing proceedings without this defect. If Illinois uses a future capital jury, selection must comply with the inquiry rule; the Court does not require jury sentencing, order a guilt retrial or impose life imprisonment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985): its requirement of demonstrating bias through questioning supports the necessary opportunity to inquire.
- Mu’Min v. Virginia, 500 U.S. 415 (1991): distinguished; discretion over the detail of an inquiry does not authorize complete omission of this necessary subject.
- Lockhart v. McCree, 476 U.S. 162 (1986): its recognition of the opportunity to identify disqualified jurors is applied to the defendant’s corresponding challenge.
- Turner v. Murray, 476 U.S. 28 (1986): its protection against unacceptable capital-sentencing risk supports required subject coverage and sentence-only relief.

#### Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7390.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

##### A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator

**Holding:** When a weighing State requires its sentencing judge to give great weight to a jury's recommendation, an inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the final sentence even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. The especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel instruction given here lacks the guidance the Eighth Amendment requires; the judge's separate findings do not by themselves cure the recommendation's legal influence.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Material application:** The jury returned an 11–1 general death recommendation under the unelaborated instruction. The judge gave operative weight to four aggravators and found two mitigators. No factor-specific jury finding is inferred; the required legal weight of the recommendation supplies the indirect channel of error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), and Shell v. Mississippi, 498 U.S. 1 (1990): apply their requirement of meaningful guidance for a capital aggravating category; the instruction here is insufficiently narrowed.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992: applies the distinction between valid eligibility and constitutionally defective weighing; surviving valid aggravators alone do not remove the error.
- Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: applies its weighing-error rule while distinguishing its independent state preservation bar to a different jury claim and its separate factual-invalidity issue.
- Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367 (1988): applies the refusal to presume that jurors disregard an instruction's constitutionally defective route; no factor-specific verdict is invented.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990): preserves genuine judicial sentencing and the presumption that judges apply legal narrowing; distinguishes a system in which a separate unguided recommendation carries required weight.
- Baldwin v. Alabama, 472 U.S. 372 (1985), and Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447 (1984): preserve States' latitude in allocating sentencing responsibility; that latitude does not validate influential unguided weighing.
- Tedder v. State, 322 So.2d 908 (Fla. 1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988): supply the state-law requirement to give great weight to jury recommendations of life or death.
- Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989): rejects its premise that the jury's advisory designation alone makes this vague instruction constitutionally immaterial.

##### Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence

**Holding:** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic reduction to life, retrial of guilt, adjudication of unrelated aggravators, factor-by-factor jury finding, or collateral-review gateway rule follows. The petition's severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions are not granted or adjudicated in this limited summary action; no separate denial of them is invented. No prior Supreme Court decision is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court grants limited review and reverses the rejection of the sentencing claim. Remand requires lawful reweighing or Chapman review addressing the recommendation's influence, and new sentencing if no lawful cure is available. Guilt and the ungranted severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions remain outside the disposition; no separate denial or automatic life sentence follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: apply actual cure requirements to the recommendation's transmitted influence; no unreasoned harmlessness label suffices.
- Parker v. Dugger, 498 U.S. 308 (1991): applies review grounded in the real sentencing record and mitigation, not an assumed alternative record.

### Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity

#### Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6616.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is confined to the old-rule application established by Godfrey for this finality date. No Teague exception or unresolved procedural-default defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

##### What correction a constitutionally invalid aggravator requires in a capital weighing system

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine authorized reweighing and constitutional harmlessness are distinct corrective routes. The latter places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining valid aggravators or bare proportionality review alone do not cure the error; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 — distinguished: duplication in narrowing does not answer vague-factor weighting and cannot supply a prefinality reliance premise.

### Compelled medication and fair trial

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### Whether the unsupported treatment and unreconstructable trial risk require a new trial

**Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Material application:** The supported risk concerned attention, testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel as well as demeanor. Expert explanation cannot restore an altered conversation or response, and an unmedicated trial cannot reliably be reconstructed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

### Competency hearings

#### Medina v. California, 505 U.S. 437 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8370.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### Whether California may assign the defendant the preponderance burden at a competency hearing

**Holding:** Due process permits California to require a defendant asserting incompetence to prove it by a preponderance at an adequate competency hearing with counsel and access to psychiatric evidence. This allocation determines the outcome when evidence is evenly balanced; it neither authorizes trial of a defendant established to be incompetent nor excuses a hearing when constitutionally sufficient doubt arises.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy, White, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this proposition in the principal opinion; O'Connor and Souter expressly adopt the same California-specific burden and adequacy conclusion in O'Connor's concurrence in the judgment. Six support the exact operative proposition, although no whole opinion has five joins. The methods are not logical subsets yielding a broader Marks rule; the categorical exclusion of Mathews from criminal procedure has only four supporters and does not control.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated California preponderance-burden and adequate-hearing scope; no exclusive criminal due-process method controls.

**Material application:** The full hearing, counsel and psychiatric evidence supply constitutionally adequate protection despite the risk that incapacity may impair the defendant's ability to help prove it. No finding establishes actual incompetence or equipoise in Medina's hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dusky's competence definition, the duty to inquire, the prohibition on trial while incompetent and the prosecution's burden on offense elements remain unchanged. No higher proof quantum, insanity allocation, harmlessness of an invalid burden, retrospective feasibility or other trial error is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment; no new competency inquiry, retrospective hearing or trial is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375, and Drope v. Missouri, 420 U.S. 162, preserve necessary competency inquiry and the bar against incompetent trial without settling this persuasion allocation. Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402, preserves rational consultation and rational and factual understanding as the functional standard. Patterson v. New York, 432 U.S. 197, permits state procedural choices, but its exclusive-framework extension here has only four supporters. Ake v. Oklahoma, 470 U.S. 68, preserves necessary psychiatric assistance; Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319, is not categorically excluded. Leland v. Oregon, 343 U.S. 790, concerns insanity rather than present competence. In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358, preserves proof beyond reasonable doubt of offense elements. Foucha v. Louisiana and Riggins v. Nevada, both May 18, 1992, retain their psychiatric-custody and compelled-medication rules without deciding this burden; Wright v. West, June 19, 1992, supplies no replacement direct-review standard.

##### Whether the associated statutory presumption of competence may remain after sufficient doubt requires a hearing

**Holding:** California may retain its statutory presumption of competence insofar as it means that the party asserting incompetence must prove it by a preponderance. Sufficient doubt requiring inquiry does not itself prove incompetence or invalidate that burden-allocating presumption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy, White, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this interpretation in the principal opinion; O'Connor and Souter expressly adopt the same allocation and affirmance in their concurrence. Six support this narrow rule, without a controlling broader method or inference of additional evidentiary weight.

**Current force:** Remains controlling only as a restatement of the permitted persuasion allocation.

**Material application:** California's Supreme Court construed §1369(f) to allocate persuasion, rather than make the presumption psychiatric evidence. Precautions during the inquiry protect unresolved capacity without establishing its factual answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision approves no irrebuttable presumption, added evidentiary weight, increased proof burden or authority to proceed after a finding of incompetence. It makes no finding of actual incompetence or equipoise and supplies no harmlessness or retrospective-feasibility ruling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California judgment and challenged presumption remain in force at this limited scope; no new hearing or trial is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pate, 383 U.S. 375, is preserved and distinguished as governing the duty to inquire. Dusky, 362 U.S. 402, continues to govern the competence being determined; the presumption does not alter that definition.

### Conditional pleas

#### Doggett v. United States, 505 U.S. 647 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-857.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the Sixth Amendment scope and delay holdings in *Doggett v. United States*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-857, see Criminal Procedure — Speedy trial.

##### Whether the express conditional plea preserves the speedy-trial claim and defense-impairment argument

**Holding:** A guilty plea under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 11(a)(2), with the Government's express consent to reservation of the adverse speedy-trial ruling, preserves the claim and its defense-impairment argument for appellate review. Doggett's admission of guilt does not waive the claimed consequence of unconstitutional delay that his plea expressly reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy; six Justices adopt this threshold application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the expressly reserved claim and consented conditional plea.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unreserved objections and the factual admissions establishing guilt are not reopened. No new general exception to plea waiver is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Effective appellate review remains available; success on the preserved Sixth Amendment claim requires the dismissal and vacatur stated under Speedy trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rule 11(a)(2) is applied according to the express reservation and prosecutorial consent. United States v. Broce, 488 U.S. 563, is distinguished because an unconditional plea's consequences do not defeat this reservation. Strunk v. United States, 412 U.S. 434, supplies the consequence of success on the reserved claim.

### Confrontation and hearsay

#### White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6113.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions require prior production or proof of unavailability

**Holding:** Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; nine Justices endorse this rule.

**Material application:** The limited grant accepts the state-law exception foundations for the child's statements as its premise; the Court makes no finding that disputed foundations are satisfied. Their spontaneous and treatment settings supply evidentiary value that later trial testimony cannot recreate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, harmless-error holding, or ruling on state-law admissibility outside the granted premise is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed on the limited Confrontation Clause claim; no new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 — extended from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.
- *Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56 — preserved for its received confrontation framework; its former-testimony unavailability analysis is not universalized.
- *Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805 — preserved for constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions; no unsupported judicial assurance substitutes for its requirements.
- *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 — firmly rooted exceptions retain their established reliability significance.
- *Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836 — distinguished as trial-witness presentation cases; their safeguards remain intact.

##### Whether confrontation is categorically limited to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes

**Holding:** The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the proposed categorical narrowing. Scalia and Thomas withhold that join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that every hearsay statement violates the Constitution or decide the treatment of a formal investigative accusation on a disputed record. No new categorical testimonial rule replaces the received framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Mattox v. United States*, 156 U.S. 237 — retained as evidence of confrontation's substantive protection and its coexistence with established exceptions.
- *California v. Green*, 399 U.S. 149, and *Dutton v. Evans*, 400 U.S. 74 — preserved; the Court's treatment of out-of-court statements is not displaced by a formal-affidavit-only definition.
- *Inadi* and *Roberts* — applied as exception-sensitive confrontation decisions, not replaced with a new categorical testimonial rule.

### Detention credit

#### United States v. Wilson, 503 U.S. 329 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1745.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Who initially computes §3585(b) detention credit and when

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) does not authorize a district court to compute detention credit at sentencing; the Attorney General through BOP must make the initial computation after the federal sentence commences. Administrative review and proper subsequent judicial review remain available, so the calculation is not unreviewable discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A process capable of granting detention credit is distinct from McCarthy’s damages-only claim and compensation-incapable grievance procedure. The decision leaves proper administrative and judicial review available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Otte v. United States, 419 U.S. 43 — applied: Congress’s choice of verb tense informs statutory timing.
- Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation, 484 U.S. 49 — applied for the same temporal-text principle.
- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16 — applied as a rebuttable inference, overcome here by the rewritten text and administration scheme.
- Robertson v. Railroad Labor Board, 268 U.S. 619 — applied: a departure from long-established procedure is not lightly inferred.
- McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992 — distinguished: its damages-only process could not provide the requested relief, unlike a credit computation process.

##### Whether detention already credited to another sentence may also receive federal credit

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) forbids federal detention credit for time already credited against another sentence when the federal sentence begins. A defendant cannot avoid that condition merely because the federal sentencing hearing occurred before the other court awarded its credit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Tennessee credited 429 days before the federal term began, although after the federal sentencing hearing. The Court does not fix a numerical federal credit, define every form of official detention, or decide a challenge to a completed BOP calculation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 — applied: avoid an arbitrary statutory result unsupported by the text’s purpose.

#### [Reno v. Koray](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Reno_v_Koray_merits_1995-06-05.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-790.
**Decided:** June 5, 1995.

##### Official detention and federal conditional release

**Holding:** Time under a federal Bail Reform Act conditional-release order is not official detention under § 3585(b), even when the restrictions are severe, so Koray’s release period requires no conditions-based credit inquiry. This rule concerns the presented federal release/detention distinction, preserves qualifying uncredited detention and judicial review, and does not decide state custody or require physical BOP control for every creditable day.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven directly adopt the status rule and its application without an independently sufficient administrative-deference holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mandatory credit remains for qualifying detention resulting from the offense sentenced or another charge for which arrest occurred after that offense, if not credited against another sentence. BOP initial computation and exhausted judicial review remain. State custody, a universal physical-BOP-control requirement and the unpresented bail-choice notice question are not decided. Administrative practice does not supply an independent deference holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Wilson, March 24, 1992: applies BOP initial computation, no duplicate credit and subsequent lawful review; Wilson did not itself define official detention.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, June 24, 1994: distinguishes qualified controlling weight for an agency’s own-regulation interpretation from a program statement construing a statute; its own-regulation rule remains intact.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., June 17, 1994: preserves the statutory-boundary requirement; administrative practice does not independently rewrite the credit provision.
- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152: applies ordinary text and context before lenity; the Court finds no residual ambiguity in the presented federal-release category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Third Circuit's conditions-inquiry judgment and remand consistently with exclusion of this federal conditional-release period from section 3585(b) credit. No conditions-based credit inquiry, exact-day award, resentencing, immediate release or equality adjudication follows.

### Double jeopardy

#### United States v. Felix, 503 U.S. 378 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1599.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### Whether prior evidentiary use places a defendant in jeopardy for distinct uncharged substantive offenses

**Holding:** The use of evidence of an uncharged crime in one trial does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. The later Oklahoma substantive charges concern different acts from the Missouri attempt and are not barred merely because evidence of those acts helped prove intent in Missouri.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White's opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the distinct-prosecution rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Missouri attempt concerned different acts from the earlier Oklahoma offenses. Ordinary objections to the admissibility of particular evidence remain.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dowling v. United States, 493 U.S. 342 — applied: evidentiary use does not itself constitute prosecution for the described offense.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — applied and confined to essential-conduct analysis; its express rejection of a same-evidence test remains operative.

##### Whether overlapping substantive acts bar prosecution of the distinct conspiracy agreement

**Holding:** A conspiracy and its substantive objects are distinct offenses; prosecution of the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because the same acts help prove both. The overlapping Missouri conduct therefore does not bar this Oklahoma conspiracy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this rationale. Stevens and Blackmun concur only in the conspiracy judgment on a narrower ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady remains in force; no same-transaction requirement or new evidentiary exemption is adopted. A materially different single-incident successive prosecution remains for its own case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bayer, 331 U.S. 532 — applied: the agreement is distinct from its execution.
- Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 — applied: substantive crime and conspiracy remain separately punishable offenses.
- Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 — applied: conspiracy’s independent character survives overlapping proof.
- Garrett v. United States, 471 U.S. 773 — applied by analogy to the treatment of multilayered criminal conduct.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — clarified without overruling: it does not abrogate the established conspiracy/substantive-offense distinction.

### Entrapment and evidentiary insufficiency

#### Jacobson v. United States, 503 U.S. 540 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1124.
**Decided:** April 6, 1992.

##### Whether the Government proved predisposition independent of its inducement beyond a reasonable doubt

**Holding:** When the Government induces the charged crime and entrapment is at issue, it must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant’s predisposition existed before and independently of its cultivation; evidence obtained after contact may prove that earlier state of mind. Even giving the prosecution its reasonable favorable inferences, the lawful earlier purchase and responses generated during this twenty-six-month operation do not establish that independent predisposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes control.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Later-collected evidence may establish prior readiness; its collection date and investigative persistence do not alone decide entrapment. No knowledge-of-illegality element, reasonable-suspicion prerequisite, general bar on undercover opportunities, or objective misconduct defense is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and return the case for entry of acquittal. The prosecution may not retry the charge to supply missing proof of independent predisposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sorrells v. United States, 287 U.S. 435 — applied: the Government may offer opportunities but may not manufacture the disposition and prosecute its product.
- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: the record can establish entrapment as a matter of law despite an eventual willing transaction.
- United States v. Russell, 411 U.S. 423 — preserved: subjective predisposition remains the inquiry; no objective misconduct defense is substituted.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied: reasonable prosecution-favorable inferences remain subject to proof beyond reasonable doubt.
- Mathews v. United States, 485 U.S. 58 — preserved: a routine offered opportunity does not automatically support an entrapment instruction.

##### Whether insufficiency of proof overcoming entrapment permits retrial

**Holding:** Because the prosecution’s proof was legally insufficient to overcome entrapment, the proper disposition is acquittal rather than a new trial. Burks bars a second opportunity to supply the missing evidence of independent predisposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes support the remedial direction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This disposition rests on insufficiency, not an erroneous instruction or another correctable trial error. A separate outrageous-government-conduct claim and statutory amendments are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and return the case for entry of acquittal. The prosecution may not retry the charge to supply missing proof of independent predisposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Burks v. United States, 437 U.S. 1 — applied: insufficiency requires acquittal and bars retrial to repair the proof.
- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: matter-of-law entrapment supports termination, not a second prosecution opportunity.

### Evidence and jury instructions on habeas review

#### Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1074.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether admission of prior nonaccidental-injury evidence violates due process when the earlier abuser is unidentified

**Holding:** On federal habeas review, evidence of a child's prior nonaccidental injuries may be relevant to prove that the charged death was intentional even when the identity of the person who caused the earlier injuries is unknown. Admission for that nonidentity purpose does not itself violate due process, and a defendant's choice not to contest accident does not remove the State's burden to prove intentional killing.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined on this proposition by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The repeated injuries supported the inference of nonaccidental harm without themselves identifying McGuire as the abuser. The evidence-alone theory could not support habeas relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State-law admissibility alone is not reviewed. The Court does not decide a general constitutional prohibition of propensity evidence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis v. Jeffers and Pulley v. Harris exclude habeas relief for state-law error alone. Lisenba and Dowling preserve the fundamental-unfairness boundary. In re Winship supplies the prosecution's continuing burden to prove every element.

##### Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof

**Holding:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor join only the distinct prior-injury-evidence proposition.

**Material application:** The complete charge neither directed a finding that McGuire caused the earlier injuries nor shifted the prosecution's burden on identity or any other element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve every prior-act instruction or decide a general propensity-evidence rule. Because the controlling majority finds no constitutional error, it does not reach Teague, Rose, or Chapman or require harmless-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed. The habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cupp supplies whole-charge review, and Boyde supplies the reasonable-likelihood standard. Francis and Sandstrom are distinguished and preserved for mandatory presumptions or shifts in elemental persuasion.

#### Wright v. West, 505 U.S. 277 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-542.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the actual larceny trial record satisfies Jackson's constitutional sufficiency requirement

**Holding:** On the trial record here, the quantity and recency of West’s possession of distinctive items from the same theft, considered with the jury’s permissible rejection of his incomplete account, allow a rational trier of fact to find the elements of larceny beyond a reasonable doubt; the exhausted Jackson insufficiency claim therefore warrants no habeas relief. The reviewing court must assess the evidence and permissible credibility choices favorably to the prosecution, without retrying guilt, shifting the State’s burden or treating possession as an invariably sufficient substitute for proof of theft.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas and Scalia in the principal opinion, White in his express endorsement of its sufficiency demonstration, O'Connor with Blackmun and Stevens in their concurrence, Kennedy in his concurrence, and Stone in his separate concurrence expressly adopt this same record-specific Jackson proposition: eight Justices. Souter is excluded because his ground is Teague-only. No complete opinion commands a majority, and no broader rule is synthesized under Marks.

**Material application:** More than fifteen distinctive items from the same theft were found in West's residence two to four weeks afterward, and the jury could reject gaps in his incomplete flea-market account. Affirmative possession evidence and permissible credibility choices together sustain the inference; disbelief alone supplies no missing element. The offense threshold was $100 and the prior felony was credibility material, not propensity proof of this theft.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new corroboration requirement, proof quantum, burden shift or categorical possession-to-theft inference is adopted. The facial validity of every possession inference, an independent instruction claim, the Elkins new-evidence claim and a general habeas legal-review standard remain undecided. The broader separate writings do not establish a new Teague rule or replace independent review with general acceptance of a reasonable state decision; Teague's existing operation, Miller's legal/fact distinction and the then-existing §2254(d) remain. Cosby's contextual considerations do not become a constitutional five-factor test. A Teague bar and merits denial are not combined under Marks.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's direction to issue a writ on insufficiency grounds is reversed. Remand preserves other properly remaining claims, including the Elkins claim, under their own requirements. No new trial, acquittal, release, expansion of custody or disposition of all habeas claims is ordered.

**Material judgment:** All nine Justices support reversal and remand; eight expressly support the narrow sufficiency holding and Souter supports the judgment on his separate noncontrolling Teague ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied unchanged to the actual record with the prescribed weight for rational jury inferences.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 — retained; the prosecution must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt and no burden shifts to West.
- Barnes v. United States, 412 U.S. 837 — permissive-inference principle retained as background, not an automatic equation of any possession with theft.

### Former testimony

#### United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-872.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether Rule 804(b)(1) requires both opportunity and similar motive despite prosecutorial immunity power and asserted fairness

**Holding:** A party offering an unavailable witness's former testimony under Federal Rule of Evidence 804(b)(1) must establish that the opposing party had both an opportunity and a similar motive to develop that testimony; the Government's power to immunize the witness at trial and an appeal to adversarial fairness do not dispense with either requirement. Similar motive must be considered on the actual proceedings and arguments; this decision neither finds that condition satisfied here nor establishes a categorical exclusion of grand-jury testimony.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Stone concurs in the judgment on his separately stated compatible rule and qualifications; he is not a formal opinion joiner. Stevens dissents from the remand on actual-motive application, not on an exemption from the Rule.

**Material application:** The court of appeals dispensed with similar motive because the Government could immunize the witnesses at trial and did not fully consider the competing actual-motive accounts. Disclosure of grand-jury material to the defense is distinct from admitting it for its truth.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither actual similar motive nor ultimate inadmissibility and adopts no categorical exclusion of grand-jury testimony. Compelled immunity, other hearsay exceptions, independent preserved constitutional grounds, witness truthfulness and guilt remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the dispensation with similar motive and remand its actual satisfaction, admissibility and judgment consequences for Anthony Salerno, Vincent DiNapoli, Louis DiNapoli, Nicholas Auletta, Edward Halloran, Alvin O. Chattin and Aniello Migliore. Preserve independent appellate grounds; Ianniello's separate relief lies outside the writ. No categorical exclusion, unconditional reinstatement of every conviction or immediate retrial is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Green v. Bock Laundry Machine Co., 490 U.S. 504 (1989): applied for enforcing the enacted evidentiary rule rather than altering it for a preferred litigation result; no revision of Green's separate impeachment rule.
- Dennis v. United States, 384 U.S. 855 (1966): distinguished as a disclosure decision under Rule 6(e); its protection of access to relevant grand-jury material remains fully operative without creating automatic trial admissibility.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 (1984): its respect for the enacted evidentiary scheme remains consistent with this application; its distinct bias-impeachment rule is unchanged.

##### Whether offering independent contradictory evidence forfeits the Rule 804(b)(1) objection

**Holding:** When the Government offers independent evidence contradicting unavailable witnesses' grand-jury accounts, but does not present any version of those accounts to the trial jury, that act alone does not forfeit its Rule 804(b)(1) objection. Even assuming a privilege-forfeiture analogy is available, mere contradiction is not disclosure of the protected communication; this decision does not establish that every hearsay objection is a testimonial privilege.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices expressly reject the asserted forfeiture ground at this limited level of generality. This is a distinct controlling answer to the defendants' alternative admissibility theory, not a finding of ultimate inadmissibility.

**Material application:** The Government offered contractors, recordings and documents, without presenting any version of DeMatteis's or Bruno's grand-jury account to the trial jury. The selective-disclosure premise of the asserted United States v. Miller, 600 F.2d 498 (5th Cir. 1979), analogy is absent; that lower-court authority does not supply an adopted Supreme Court privilege rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The consequences of actual selective presentation of former testimony and any general equivalence between hearsay and testimonial privileges remain undecided. Rejection of this alternative admissibility theory does not determine actual similar motive or ultimate admissibility.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Actual motive and the resulting admissibility and judgment consequences remain for the seven-respondent remand; the independent-contradiction act alone supplies no forfeiture ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

The decision does not change White v. Illinois, January 15, 1992, or United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992. Their exception-sensitive confrontation and confined grand-jury presentation rules remain distinct from trial admissibility and do not create automatic admission.

### General verdicts

#### Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6352.  
**Decided:** December 3, 1991.

##### Whether factual insufficiency on one lawful conspiracy object requires reversal of a general verdict supported on another object

**Holding:** A general guilty verdict on a federal multiple-object conspiracy need not be set aside merely because the evidence is inadequate for one legally valid object when another object is supported sufficiently under the unchanged beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. This rule does not govern a legally invalid object, failure of proof on every route, an element-diluting instruction, or a properly presented independent unanimity defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined on the proposition by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; seven of eight participants. Blackmun's judgment-only concurrence is not counted as a join in this reasoning.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the multiple-object conspiracy verdict. *Sochor v. Florida*, decided June 8, 1992, extends the factual-versus-legal distinction only to rejecting an inference of reliance on a legally available but factually unsupported aggravator from a general Florida advisory death recommendation; it does not settle broader capital-jury error rules.

**Material application:** Both alleged §371 conspiracy objects were legally permissible. The Government conceded insufficient evidence connecting Griffin to the DEA object, but the IRS object had sufficient evidentiary support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The excluded legal-invalidity, all-routes-insufficiency, elemental-instruction, and independent unanimity questions remain unresolved. Preferred withdrawal of an unsupported route on timely request creates no independent constitutional reversal rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is affirmed; the supported IRS object sustains the conviction against this challenge. No retrial or acquittal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Turner v. United States is applied to factual insufficiency. Stromberg v. California and Yates v. United States are distinguished and preserved for legally forbidden verdict grounds. In re Winship preserves proof beyond reasonable doubt, and Jackson v. Virginia preserves the rational-factfinder sufficiency inquiry.

**Later-authority backlinks:** *Sochor v. Florida*, 504 U.S. 527 (1992), No. 91-5843, decided June 8, 1992, question concerning inferred jury reliance on the coldness aggravator; see Criminal Procedure, the Sochor entry.

### Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal

#### United States v. Williams, 504 U.S. 36 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1972.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Williams*, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1972, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (reviewability) and Criminal Procedure — Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal (disclosure duty and dismissal authority).

##### What known evidence a federal prosecutor must disclose to avoid materially distorting a grand-jury presentation

**Holding:** Before seeking an indictment, a federal prosecutor must present or otherwise disclose to the grand jury personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when its omission would materially distort the grand-jury presentation. This duty requires neither an investigative search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every possible defense; a violation alone does not establish entitlement to dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, adopt the personally-known/direct-negation duty. Stone’s separate merits opinion adopts that duty where omission materially distorts the presentation. Under Marks, these five judgment supporters establish the stated common requirement; the four-Justice formulation does not remove Stone’s material-distortion qualification from the controlling rule.

**Material application:** The duty concerns substantial evidence personally known to the prosecutor, not an investigative search or a preliminary trial of every defense. Misconduct and charging prejudice are separate inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns authority, not the accounting documents’ specific character. Affirmance does not establish that every intent-related item meets the duty or that the prosecutor fabricated evidence. Negligent discovery, state-grand-jury duties, constitutional disclosure, immunity and fabrication claims remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — its protective function and enforceable process limits support the confined duty; ordinary evidentiary freedom remains.
- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its misconduct and prejudice inquiries remain separate; violation does not automatically warrant dismissal.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — preserved for competence and sufficiency challenges to an otherwise lawful presentation; distinguished from the specified prosecutorial omission.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the judgment supporters’ shared direct-negation duty with the material-distortion qualification retained.

##### When a materially misleading omission permits supervisory dismissal without prejudice

**Holding:** Federal supervisory authority permits dismissal without prejudice when a prosecutor withholds personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense, the omission materially distorts the presentation, the evidence would plainly preclude a finding of probable cause, and the violation substantially influenced the indictment or leaves grave doubt that the indictment was free from that influence. This establishes the stated sufficient ground for dismissal; it requires neither a search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every potential defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s merits Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, supply the narrower judgment-supporting rule. Stone concurs in affirmance under the broader material-distortion formulation, covering known evidence substantially undermining an essential element with established Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, the four-Justice rule is the controlling logical subset: every known, directly negating omission that plainly defeats probable cause, materially distorts the presentation and causes the stated prejudice also satisfies Stone’s broader rule. The five judgment supporters together establish its force. Stone is not represented as formally joining the narrower qualification. The four-Justice and broader one-Justice applications are not combined to remove that qualification from the sufficient ground recognized here. Neither the plurality nor the five judgment supporters adopt an exclusive rule forbidding relief in every other circumstance.

**Material application:** The narrower rule adds evidence plainly precluding probable cause to the shared known-evidence, direct-negation and material-distortion requirements, and independently requires Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. The Government presented a categorical authority objection rather than renewed review of the particular evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a sufficient dismissal class, not an exclusive prohibition on relief outside it. The plurality does not find the accounting documents plainly defeat probable cause; the evidentiary application was outside the grant. Stone’s broader application does not become general law. Guilt, negligent discovery, state grand juries and constitutional disclosure remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its prejudice requirement is retained; limited supervisory power is extended to the specified known-evidence omission. Its evidence-reliability prohibition survives for lawful presentations.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — distinguished: the confined misconduct inquiry is not a challenge to the competence or sufficiency of evidence in an otherwise lawful presentation. Its hearsay and ordinary adequacy rules remain.
- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — preserved for grand-jury independence and freedom from trial evidence rules; no exclusionary-rule extension occurs.
- United States v. Mechanik, 475 U.S. 66 — its completed-trial harmlessness holding is not extended to erase a prejudicial violation challenged and resolved before trial; the charging-prejudice formulation adopted in Bank remains operative.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 — not transplanted into grand-jury proceedings; its trial disclosure duty remains unchanged.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the genuine logical subset of the five judgment supporters’ rules; the shared authority supports the sufficient dismissal ground, without creating a single majority merits opinion or an exclusive boundary on other relief.
- United States v. Page, 808 F.2d 723 — the judgment applying it in Williams is sustained against the categorical no-authority challenge; Page’s full disclosure formulation and any rule requiring every fact relevant to a possible defense are not adopted.

### Guidelines departure review

#### Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6297.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether reliance on a forbidden departure factor incorrectly applies the Guidelines

**Holding:** A departure based on a factor that the Commission has forbidden, or has already fully considered in the applicable range, rests on an incorrect application of the Guidelines under §3742(f)(1). A policy statement expressly prohibiting a sentencing action is authoritative guidance in applying the relevant Guideline, and an arrest record itself is not a permissible departure ground here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The policy-statement holding concerns an express prohibition guiding the relevant Guideline, not every policy statement for every purpose. Whether nonsimilar old convictions justify departure is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- No earlier Supreme Court decision is overruled; this is a construction of §§3553(b), 3742(f)(1) and the applicable §4A1.3 prohibition.
- Griffin v. United States, December 3, 1991 — kept distinct: unsupported facts under a valid legal theory do not establish the rule for a prohibited sentencing factor.

##### What showing permits affirmance despite reliance on an invalid departure ground

**Holding:** Once the challenger establishes reliance on an invalid departure factor, remand is required unless the reviewing court determines from the whole record that the district court would have imposed the same sentence without that factor. The challenger need not additionally prove that the error determined the sentence; the party defending the sentence must persuade the reviewing court that the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry asks whether this judge would have imposed the same sentence, not whether that sentence could lawfully have been imposed. Chapman’s constitutional proof standard is not adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(a) — applied as the governing harmless-error rule for this nonconstitutional sentencing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — not imported: the Court adopts no beyond-reasonable-doubt burden for this statutory error.

##### Whether departure reasonableness is separate from causal harmlessness

**Holding:** Even when an invalid departure ground was harmless under §3742(f)(1), a reviewing court must separately determine under §3742(f)(2) that the departure’s extent is reasonable in light of the valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court. A reasonable sentence that the district court could have imposed does not establish that the erroneous factor was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Review concerns valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court; comments used to select a sentence after departure do not become new departure grounds. The lawful use of nonsimilar old convictions and the ultimate reasonable magnitude remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S. 277 — applied for the primary sentencing role of the trial court, not to add a proportionality issue.

### International abduction and treaty limits on prosecution

#### United States v. Alvarez-Machain, 504 U.S. 655 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-712.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether the United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty bars prosecution following this officially sponsored, protested abduction

**Holding:** The United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty does not prohibit this abduction or make extradition its exclusive means of obtaining a defendant’s presence. Because the Treaty supplies no restriction barring this prosecution, the Ker–Frisbie rule permits trial notwithstanding the DEA-sponsored forcible transfer and Mexico’s protest; the treaty-based dismissal and repatriation order must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the treaty construction, application and remedy. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor dissent from the 5–4 reversal.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not authorize abduction, decide guilt or independent capture legality, foreclose every other remedy, or establish a universal bar to individual treaty enforcement. No extradition under the Treaty produced this transfer. The independently rejected outrageous-conduct theory is not reconsidered, and no separate Verdugo-Urquidez docket is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit treaty-based trial bar, appellate dismissal direction and repatriation disposition are reversed and remanded, permitting prosecution. No diplomatic return is ordered. The district order had discharged respondent and ordered repatriation without separately dismissing the indictment; the appellate characterization is not a new district disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ker v. Illinois, 119 U.S. 436 (1886): applied to trial authority after this official international abduction because the Treaty supplies no contrary limitation; not treated as affirmative authorization to seize.
- Frisbie v. Collins, 342 U.S. 519 (1952): retained and applied for the distinction between unlawful capture and the power to conduct a lawful trial.
- United States v. Rauscher, 119 U.S. 407 (1886): retained; its enforceable treaty limitation follows actual extradition on specified terms and does not make this different Treaty exclusive.
- Cook v. United States, 288 U.S. 102 (1933): distinguished; an established treaty restriction on governmental seizure controls there, and no comparable restriction is found here.
- Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S. 392 (1985), and Valentine v. United States ex rel. Neidecker, 299 U.S. 5 (1936): applied to begin with the Treaty’s terms and determine the adopted obligation rather than assume it.

### Official-right extortion

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6105.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### Whether Hobbs Act official-right extortion requires an independent demand or initiation by the official

**Holding:** Official-right extortion under 18 U.S.C. §1951 does not require that the public official initiate, demand or separately induce the payment when he obtains money to which he is not entitled knowing that it is given in return for official acts. The required interstate-commerce element and proof beyond a reasonable doubt remain.

**Holding:** A charge requiring a public official knowingly to accept money in exchange for a specific requested exercise of official power, while excluding a contribution standing alone even when the donor has pending business, satisfies McCormick’s exchange requirement on this record. **Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–II of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter; five Justices sustain the instruction and distinguish agreement from later performance.

**Holding:** Official-right extortion is not confined to obtaining payment by falsely asserting that law entitles the official to collect it. **Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices decide this construction. O’Connor does not reach it, and Stone does not join or supply an additional ground on it.

##### Whether Trevino preserved a pre-Batson equal-protection objection to racial strikes

**Holding:** A pre-Batson motion alleging a historical pattern of racial peremptory strikes, renewed after the challenged exclusions and carried forward on appeal with a Fourteenth Amendment reference, preserves the equal-protection objection despite imperfect labels. #### Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-372.
**Decided:** June 18, 1992.

For the State's standing to assert excluded jurors' rights in *Georgia v. McCollum*, June 18, 1992, No. 91-372, see Federal Courts — Standing to assert third-party rights.

##### Whether a criminal defendant's purposeful racial peremptory strikes are attributable to the State

**Holding:** A criminal defendant’s invocation of state peremptory authority to obtain judicial exclusion from a governmental jury is state action for that specific act, and purposeful racial exclusion violates the prospective juror’s equal-protection right. This attribution does not make defendants or defense counsel state actors in their ordinary representation, advice or investigation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; five of nine Justices adopt the rule and its limited attribution.

**Material application:** Georgia creates the peremptory power, summons and compensates jurors and judicially gives exclusion legal effect. Selection of the governmental criminal jury is the specific public function at issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary defense representation, advice and investigation remain private. The decision does not abolish peremptories or constitutionalize every defense act, and it decides no actual strike, criminal charge or nonracial classification.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pretrial Georgia judgment is reversed and remanded for selection and further proceedings under the constitutional prohibition; no conviction is reversed or particular juror ordered seated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 — extended from private civil peremptories to the specific criminal-defense selection act.
- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922 — applied for state-created authority and fair attribution; not displaced.
- Polk County v. Dodson, 454 U.S. 312 — distinguished for this delegated selection function; ordinary independent defense representation remains private.
- Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 — applied for the excluded juror’s equal-protection interest against purposeful racial exclusion.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended to purposeful racial exclusion by the defense during the state selection process.

##### What showing, explanation and persuasion burdens govern the State's objection to defense peremptories

**Holding:** When the State establishes facts supporting a prima facie inference of purposeful racial defense strikes, the defense must give race-neutral explanations and the judge must determine whether the State has proved purposeful discrimination; the ultimate persuasion burden remains with the State. Effective counsel and an impartial jury do not confer a right to racial exclusion, while genuine race-neutral challenges, inquiry into actual bias and appropriate protection of concrete confidential matters remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices. No general privilege-resolution rule or unconscious-bias test is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A strike against a minority juror alone does not require explanation. Genuine race-neutral challenges and meaningful inquiry into actual disqualifying bias remain available. A concrete privilege dispute, a general privilege-resolution rule and unconscious-bias safeguards remain undecided; confidential judicial consideration is possible without a categorical exemption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jury selection on remand must follow the ordinary Batson sequence; no purposeful discrimination is found in this event.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended with its purpose requirement, staged production and ultimate persuasion burden unchanged.
- Trevino v. Texas (April 6, 1992) — applied for ordinary Batson administration, without presuming discrimination.
- Ross v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 81 — applied to distinguish statutory peremptories from the impartial-jury guarantee.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 — applied; effective assistance does not authorize unlawful conduct.
- Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 — applied to reject presumed loyalties based on race.
- Ham v. South Carolina, 409 U.S. 524 — preserved for meaningful inquiry into racial bias, without treating race itself as disqualification.
- Morgan v. Illinois (June 15, 1992) — preserved for meaningful inquiry into disqualifying bias; no new assumption based on race follows.
- United States v. Zolin, 491 U.S. 554 — used for availability of confidential judicial consideration, not to decide a concrete privilege dispute here.

### Speedy trial

#### Doggett v. United States, 505 U.S. 647 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-857.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the conditional-plea holding in *Doggett v. United States*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-857, see Criminal Procedure — Conditional pleas.

##### Whether post-accusation defense impairment is protected when the accused remains free and unaware

**Holding:** Once an indictment or other official accusation triggers the Sixth Amendment, the speedy-trial inquiry must consider defense impairment even though the accused remains free and unaware of the charge. Absence of incarceration or anxiety removes those possible injuries on this record but does not exclude defense impairment from the Barker balance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy; six Justices adopt this scope rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for an operative official accusation.

**Material application:** Doggett's indictment remained pending throughout the challenged delay. His freedom and ignorance did not eliminate the independent interest in fair adjudication of that outstanding prosecution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No limitations period running from the offense or independent right to repose is created. Preaccusation delay, periods after dismissal and independently available due-process protection retain their separate treatment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The protected defense interest enters the balance that establishes the violation and dismissal consequence below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barker v. Wingo, 407 U.S. 514, supplies fair adjudication as an independent protected interest. Moore v. Arizona, 414 U.S. 25, rejects an indispensable actual-prejudice requirement. United States v. Marion, 404 U.S. 307, is distinguished as to preaccusation delay; United States v. MacDonald, 456 U.S. 1, as to periods following dismissal; and United States v. Loud Hawk, 474 U.S. 302, as to absence of an operative prosecution and justified interlocutory delay. Those decisions do not impose a categorical custody-or-anxiety restriction after accusation.

##### Whether extraordinary negligent delay and unrebutted defense impairment establish a violation requiring dismissal

**Holding:** Under Barker, extraordinary post-indictment delay from sustained governmental negligence can violate the Sixth Amendment without identified trial prejudice when the accused did not knowingly acquiesce and the resulting inference of defense impairment is not persuasively rebutted. The eight-and-one-half-year delay here, including approximately six avoidable years while Doggett lived openly, establishes the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy; six Justices adopt the rule, application and dismissal consequence.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated contextual scope under Barker; no fixed deadline or automatic violation from elapsed time alone is established.

**Material application:** Ordinary investigation could have found Doggett living openly, and accepted findings establish ignorance of the indictment before arrest. Prompt assertion after arrest supplies no knowing acquiescence. The Government's answers to particular alleged losses do not persuasively rebut the accumulated inference from prolonged neglect; the Court does not find the lost-tape or youthful-offender claims proved or attribute every foreign period to American fault.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Delay sufficient to trigger inquiry is distinct from the ultimate balance of duration, reasons, assertion and prejudice. Negligence weighs less than deliberate obstruction but increasingly against the Government as unjustified years accumulate. Diligence, justified foreign obstacles, knowing evasion or materially shorter neglect may yield different results. No universal transfer of the ultimate constitutional burden, preaccusation rule or independent right of repose is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit and remand through it for dismissal of the indictment and consequent vacatur of the conditional conviction and sentence. The violation is established; no additional trial-prejudice finding, retrial, sentence-only adjustment or new factual hearing is authorized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barker, 407 U.S. 514, is applied and specified for extraordinary negligent delay while preserving the distinct threshold and contextual balance. Moore, 414 U.S. 25, defeats a categorical actual-prejudice prerequisite. Loud Hawk, 474 U.S. 302, retains the relevance of justified delay and the insufficiency of possible prejudice alone. Strunk, 412 U.S. 434, requires dismissal and vacatur rather than sentence credit or a new trial.

### Capital sentencing and correction of weighing error

#### Richmond v. Lewis, 506 U.S. 40 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7094.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### An actual cure is necessary

**Holding:** When a death sentence in a State that weighs aggravation against mitigation rests on an unconstitutionally vague aggravating factor, remaining valid factors do not alone cure the error. The sentence may stand only after an authorized sentencer actually makes a constitutionally valid new sentencing determination, including lawful appellate reweighing, or the State establishes constitutional harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990), applied: lawful state appellate cure is permitted, automatic factor-count affirmance is not. Stringer v. Black (March 9), Sochor v. Florida (June 8) and Espinosa v. Florida (June 29), all 1992, applied at their controlling levels: actual weighing error requires actual constitutional correction. Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), preserves the State’s beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness obligation.

##### Arizona’s adjudication did not cure this sentence

**Holding:** On the parties’ premise that a majority of the Arizona Supreme Court had to cast valid curative votes, this sentence cannot stand: even assuming the two Justices retaining the narrowed aggravator lawfully reweighed, the two other affirming Justices relied conclusively on the remaining criminal history without a new balance of mitigation. The denial of sentence relief is reversed for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of the death sentence and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents. No rule demanding identical reasoning from every member of a state appellate majority is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990), and Lewis v. Jeffers, 497 U.S. 764 (1990), preserved: narrowing constructions and appropriately reviewed state applications may be valid, but do not supply a curative vote absent here. State v. Gretzler, 135 Ariz.42, 659 P.2d1 (1983), remains the state narrowing source; its proper application by Holohan and Hays is not decided. Clemons and the operative weighing-error decisions require the stated remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arizona is a weighing State. A general duty of independent review is not actual reweighing; no prescribed verbal formula is required, and a federal habeas court cannot perform the missing state sentencing balance. The validity of the Holohan–Hays narrowing and reweighing is assumed, not decided. The accepted state-majority premise establishes no requirement that every curative vote use identical reasoning.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the denial of sentence relief and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of death and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence. The conviction is untouched; no automatic life sentence is imposed.

### Collateral challenges to prior convictions

#### Parke v. Raley, 506 U.S. 20 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-719.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### Initial production burden

**Holding:** In Kentucky’s collateral challenge to a final counseled conviction used for sentence enhancement, due process permits the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and require the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. If the defendant refutes regularity, the Commonwealth must affirmatively establish constitutional validity under Kentucky’s procedure; a missing old transcript alone, without alleged official misconduct, does not establish invalidity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969), distinguished as direct review without weakening its valid-waiver requirement. Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 468–469 (1938), applied to collateral regularity. Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), preserved for constitutionally defective uncounseled convictions. Medina v. California (June 22, 1992), distinguished: its narrow competence-burden holding does not establish a controlling categorical ban on practical due-process analysis.

##### Measure of proof

**Holding:** Due process does not require the clear-and-convincing extrarecord proof demanded by the Sixth Circuit to establish validity of an old guilty plea in this collateral sentencing proceeding. Kentucky’s retention of the ultimate persuasion burden does not entail that heightened constitutional quantum; the fact of prior conviction and the validity inquiry remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin is not extended into a clear-and-convincing collateral proof requirement. Zerbst supports the distinction between constitutional validity and a universally heightened burden. The Sixth Circuit’s Dunn v. Simmons, 877 F.2d 1275 (1989), cannot govern insofar as it demands that constitutional quantum and shifts the entire production burden solely because no transcript survives.

##### The 1981 plea

**Holding:** Under then-applicable 28 U.S.C. §2254(d), a competent state court’s factual determination, evidenced by written findings, an opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State receives a presumption of correctness subject to the conditions and exceptions below; the ultimate federal question whether a plea was knowing and voluntary remains independently determined. On the established earlier warnings, contemporaneous 1981 charge form and jury warning, representation, education, unimpaired condition and testimony here, Raley’s 1981 plea was knowing and voluntary and the Sixth Circuit’s conditional new-hearing remedy is unwarranted. The presumption does not govern if the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears, or the State admits that (1) the state hearing did not resolve the merits of the factual dispute; (2) its factfinding procedure was inadequate to afford a full and fair hearing; (3) material facts were not adequately developed; (4) the state court lacked subject-matter jurisdiction or jurisdiction over the applicant; (5) an indigent applicant was denied appointed counsel in deprivation of his constitutional right; (6) the applicant did not receive a full, fair and adequate hearing; or (7) the applicant was otherwise denied due process in that proceeding. Under paragraph (8), the presumption also does not govern when the pertinent state record is produced and the federal court, considering that record as a whole, concludes that the factual determination lacks fair record support. At a federal evidentiary hearing, once the state factual determination is duly proved, and absent those circumstances, the applicant must establish its error by convincing evidence. That federal factual-review burden is distinct from Kentucky’s allocation and measure of proof of a prior plea’s constitutional validity. Inferences receive deference only when fairly supported by the facts; no presumption attaches to the ultimate federal legal conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422, 431–438 (1983), applied to supported historical inferences and independent legal review. Boykin and North Carolina v. Alford, 400 U.S. 25, 31 (1970), applied to an informed voluntary choice. No later habeas deference standard enters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ultimate federal validity of the plea remains independently determined. No universal collateral proof quantum or categorical prohibition on collateral review is adopted. Kentucky's separate proof of the fact of prior conviction beyond a reasonable doubt and prosecution burdens on the present offense remain. Earlier experience alone does not establish knowing waiver.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit conditional-writ judgment concerning the 1981 plea; the actual evidentiary showing warrants no new hearing. The unchallenged 1979 determination is undisturbed.

### Effective capital mitigation under Texas special issues

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

### Evidentiary predicates for capital instructions

#### Delo v. Lashley, 507 U.S. 272 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-409.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### An evidentiary predicate for the requested mitigating circumstance

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require a capital sentencer to receive an instruction on a particular mitigating circumstance when no evidence supports that circumstance; Missouri may require some supporting evidence for its no-significant-prior-criminal-history instruction while leaving the sentencer free to consider and give effect to mitigation actually presented. This production requirement neither requires exhaustive proof of a negative, compels the defendant to testify, authorizes an adverse inference from silence, nor reduces the State's burden to establish aggravating circumstances.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the rule and reversal of this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586; Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104; Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302: applied to preserve meaningful consideration of supported mitigation; they do not require this unsupported instruction.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993: preserved in its actual controlling scope; supported youth/background culpability evidence must receive effect, without presuming a distinct mitigating fact.
- Hopper v. Evans, 456 U.S. 605: applied by analogy for an evidentiary predicate, not for an obligation to submit unsupported alternatives.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639: applied to the permitted allocation of mitigation proof, subject to the State's undiminished aggravation burden; Missouri's lesser some-evidence requirement is all that is adjudicated here.

##### The proposed innocence instruction does not sustain this judgment

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a presumption of innocence of other offenses applies at capital sentencing, its existence does not automatically require an instruction; the relevant circumstances must create a genuine danger that jurors will attribute other criminal conduct to the defendant on a basis other than lawful evidence. The silent criminal-history record, undiminished reasonable-doubt instructions on aggravation, and absence of a circumstance creating that danger do not require such an instruction here; the Court does not decide the general existence or scope of that sentencing presumption.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices expressly adopt this alternative response. Stone-Zsela and Souter do not join or reach it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Taylor v. Kentucky, 436 U.S. 478: applied for the danger of a verdict based on extraneous considerations, while preserving its context-sensitive instruction rule.
- Kentucky v. Whorton, 441 U.S. 786: applied for the totality-of-circumstances inquiry and rejection of an automatic instruction requirement.
- Herrera v. Collins, January 25, 1993: not extended; its actual record-specific rejection of federal relief supplies no categorical rule extinguishing innocence protections for other purposes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Silence does not prove a no-significant-history mitigating fact. No actual history is found from material the jury did not hear; youth is a different mitigating circumstance. The alternative innocence-instruction holding assumes the protection without deciding its existence or full scope. Strickland merits and the independently rejected defaulted counsel claim are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse conditional habeas relief on the instruction ground and remand. No counsel-effectiveness or innocence finding, new stay or execution order issues.

### Fourth Amendment protection of possession

#### Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6516.

**Decided:** December 8, 1992.

##### A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy

**Holding:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a seizure under the Fourth Amendment even without a search, invasion of privacy or liberty, or criminal investigation. The family’s alleged forcible dispossession meets that threshold, but coverage alone does not establish unreasonableness or every element of §1983 liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 — applied; possession and privacy remain independently protected interests.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 — applied; seizure scrutiny does not require an accompanying search.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321 — applied; plain-view seizure remains subject to the Fourth Amendment.
- Adickes v. S. H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144 — applied to the summary-judgment joint-action inference, without a final conspiracy finding.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 — preserved; seizure coverage does not establish municipal liability.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992 — preserved; attribution does not settle immunity or a private merits defense.

##### A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim

**Holding:** A court may not reject an otherwise applicable Fourth Amendment claim merely because the same conduct also concerns a deprivation of property under the Due Process Clause. Each applicable constitutional protection must be examined on its own terms; this disposition does not adjudicate the family’s separate substantive-due-process claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 — applied; the specific Fourth Amendment source governs its covered conduct and is not displaced by a dominant-character theory.
- Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 — distinguished; postdeprivation procedural remedies do not answer the distinct seizure claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 — confined to its prison setting for the relevant coverage proposition; no rule excluding possession of a home follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private action alone is insufficient. Ultimate reasonableness, municipal policy, causation, immunity, damages and the separate substantive-due-process claim remain undecided. The holding addresses a home, without deciding coverage of every property category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse adverse summary judgment and remand the seizure claim; no final finding of unreasonableness or award of damages.

### Fugitive disentitlement and appellate review

#### Ortega-Rodriguez v. United States, 507 U.S. 234 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7749.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### A sufficient appellate connection is required

**Holding:** When a defendant's flight and recapture both precede a timely appeal, fugitive status alone does not justify dismissal: the sanction must respond to a sufficient connection between that misconduct and the appellate process. Courts may adopt reasonable rules for recurring classes of appellate disruption, and may dismiss for sufficiently established interference, inability to conduct meaningful review, or prejudice to a legally available retrial; retrial prejudice cannot justify dismissing a sufficiency appeal whose successful resolution would prohibit retrial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, qualifications and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Molinaro v. New Jersey, 396 U.S. 365: retained for flight connected to pending review; its disentitlement rationale does not establish automatic forfeiture for every completed preappeal flight.
- Estelle v. Dorrough, 420 U.S. 534: retained for deterrence and appellate order in its pending-appeal setting; it does not settle the distinct preappeal boundary.
- Smith v. United States, 94 U.S. 97, Bohanan v. Nebraska, 125 U.S. 692, and Eisler v. United States, 338 U.S. 189: their enforcement rationale remains applicable to fugitives beyond the reviewing court's effective control, a condition absent here.
- Allen v. Georgia, 166 U.S. 138: postappeal escape may support continued dismissal after recapture; that setting remains distinct.
- Thomas v. Arn, 474 U.S. 140: applied to require reasonable appellate supervision; reasonable circuit rules need not be identical.
- United States v. Holmes, 680 F.2d 1372: its automatic conviction-appeal forfeiture rule is rejected insofar as it dispenses with a sufficient appellate connection; its exception for absence beyond the defendant's control and its preservation of sentencing-error review do not cure that defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The issue is appellate supervision, not absence of a live controversy. District-court escape sanctions, appeal deadlines and disentitlement for flight during appeal remain available. No least-restrictive-sanction requirement, constitutional right to appeal or automatic review despite actual appellate disruption is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate dismissal and remand for the sufficient-connection inquiry, including any actual significance of separate codefendant appeals. The conviction appeal remains undecided.

### Ineffective assistance and displaced substantive law

#### Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1393.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to obtain a favorable application of a substantive rule subsequently rejected does not establish Strickland prejudice where the lost benefit is one the governing law does not recognize and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. This limited rule does not replace ordinary reasonable-probability analysis, excuse deficient performance, or require an additional showing of unfairness in the usual ineffective-assistance case.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): applied and clarified for loss of a subsequently rejected substantive rule; ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains the usual test.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986): applied by analogy for the distinction between a changed outcome and loss of a legally protected benefit; no finding of perjury by Fretwell is made.
- Kimmelman v. Morrison, 477 U.S. 365 (1986): distinguished; a procedural bar on direct habeas consideration does not eliminate a meritorious underlying claim, unlike the substantive premise here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 (1988): applied for constitutionally sufficient narrowing at the guilt stage despite duplication at sentencing.
- Collins v. Lockhart, 754 F.2d 258 (8th Cir. 1985), and Perry v. Lockhart, 871 F.2d 1384 (8th Cir. 1989): Collins's displaced double-counting rule supplies no cognizable benefit; Perry's rejection of that rule supplies the accepted litigation premise. Whether Collins could independently be revived is not decided.

##### Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry

**Holding:** Teague's limitation on new rules supporting collateral relief does not require a federal habeas court to disregard a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State when deciding whether an omitted objection caused cognizable Strickland prejudice. Performance remains assessed from counsel's perspective at the time, while the prejudice inquiry here does not preserve an entitlement to the rejected Collins rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989): distinguished from an affirmative entitlement to an obsolete rule; its collateral finality limitation is not made reciprocal.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): preserves the contemporaneous perspective for evaluating performance without mechanically fixing the prejudice inquiry to every favorable rule then available.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice remains the usual rule; no separate unfairness requirement is added in ordinary cases. Deficient performance is conceded. The Court does not revive Collins, decide the Solicitor General’s separate §2254(a) theory or determine the unresolved penalty-preparation claims. Thomas’s state/federal hierarchy observation is not a majority holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit grant of relief on the Collins-only ground. Unresolved independent claims retain their own treatment; no new sentence, stay or execution is ordered.

### Interstate detainers and trial-period commencement

#### Fex v. Michigan, 507 U.S. 43 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7873.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period

**Holding:** When a prisoner invokes Article III while serving a term of imprisonment and an untried charge in another participating State is the subject of a lodged detainer, the 180-day trial period begins only when the required written notice and request for final disposition have actually reached both the prosecuting officer and the appropriate court in that State. Delivery to the custodian alone does not start it; a necessary or reasonable continuance may be allowed for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present, Article VI(a) tolls the period while the prisoner is unable to stand trial as determined by the court having jurisdiction, and Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice when the applicable period is violated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six votes for this rule and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carchman v. Nash, 473 U.S. 716 (1985): applied for the compact’s federal-law character and attention to operative language; its exclusion of probation-violation detainers remains intact.
- Houston v. Lack, 487 U.S. 266 (1988): distinguished because a prisoner’s own appeal-filing deadline differs from commencement of the receiving State’s prosecution period; its mailbox rule remains in force within its setting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The custodian’s prompt forwarding and Article III(c) notice obligations remain; actual receipt does not excuse official delay. Separate notice remedies, constitutional claims and a continuance first sought after expiration are not resolved. There is no automatic grace period or bad-faith prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm: trial began 177 days after both required recipients received the papers, so Article III(a) supplies no dismissal with prejudice here.

### Joint trials, severance and claim preservation

#### Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6824.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Concrete prejudice and effective relief under Rule 14

**Holding:** For properly joined defendants, mutually antagonistic defenses alone do not require severance; the district court evaluates whether a joint trial presents a serious risk of compromising a specific trial right or preventing a reliable individualized verdict, and chooses effective relief in its sound discretion. Severance is not required for every showing of prejudice if instructions or other safeguards adequately cure the risk, but instructions are not presumed to cure every risk; Garcia, Soto and Martinez have shown no uncured risk requiring separate trials here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; eight Justices adopt the rule and its application. Stevens’s narrower concurrence supplies no join in this general formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200 — applied for joint-trial benefits and the ordinary effectiveness of instructions, not a universal cure.
- Bruton v. United States, 391 U.S. 123 — preserved: a specific confrontation risk may require exclusion, redaction or severance when instructions are inadequate.
- United States v. Lane, 474 U.S. 438 — applied only to Rule 14’s discretion over effective prejudice relief; no Rule 8 misjoinder ruling.
- Schaffer v. United States, 362 U.S. 511 — applied to adequate individualized jury instructions on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Serious risk may arise from another defendant’s evidence, markedly different culpability in complex proof or unavailable exculpatory evidence; the examples are not exhaustive. A better chance of acquittal alone is insufficient. Rule 8 joinder and independent confrontation rights remain distinct; review of effective Rule 14 relief is for abuse of discretion. Gloria’s sufficiency and collateral questions are not reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm on petitioner-specific grounds: Gloria’s claim is not reviewed; Garcia, Soto and Martinez receive no new trial because the individualized instructions adequately address the risks shown.

For Zafiro v. United States, January 25, 1993, No. 91-6824, concerning petitioner-specific appellate preservation, see **Federal Courts — Petitioner-specific appellate preservation**.

### Petty offenses and the jury-trial right

#### United States v. Nachtigal, 507 U.S. 1 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-609.

**Decided:** February 22, 1993.

##### Whether the national-park DUI penalties overcome the petty-offense presumption

**Holding:** An offense carrying no more than six months’ imprisonment is presumptively petty for the constitutional jury-trial right, but the defendant may overcome that presumption by showing that the additional authorized penalties, considered with the maximum incarceration, are so severe that they clearly reflect a legislative judgment that the offense is serious. For this national-park DUI scheme, the six-month imprisonment ceiling, $5,000 fine and alternative probation of up to five years with its authorized conditions do not rebut the presumption; the defendant has no constitutional jury entitlement on that basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by all nine Justices; all nine support both the complete Blanton rule and this application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Blanton v. North Las Vegas, 489 U.S. 538 (1989), applied: the six-month presumption remains rebuttable after considering additional authorized penalties; the governing legislature’s choices, rather than other jurisdictions’ practices, supply the relevant seriousness judgment.
- United States v. Craner, 652 F.2d 23 (9th Cir. 1981), displaced to the extent its jury-entitlement rule for this federal offense conflicts with Blanton as applied here. Delegated rulemaking does not remove Congress’s enacted imprisonment ceiling from the analysis.
No Supreme Court precedent is overruled. This decision establishes no universal rule that financial sanctions or probation can never overcome the petty-offense presumption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Authorized probation conditions, including treatment, community service, residence or movement restrictions and monitored home confinement, are considered; intermittent custody remains bounded by the offense’s imprisonment ceiling. Gain-or-loss-based alternative fines lack an established predicate on this record; a different triggered penalty package is not decided. No universal rule excludes fines or probation from rebutting the petty-offense presumption. The separate acquittal remains outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit jury-entitlement judgment and remand; other challenges to the conviction remain outside this decision.

### Presence at commencement of trial

#### Crosby v. United States, 506 U.S. 255 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6194.

**Decided:** January 13, 1993.

##### Trial cannot begin under the voluntary-absence exception without initial presence

**Holding:** Rule 43 does not permit a criminal trial to begin in a defendant’s absence merely because that absence is voluntary; its voluntary-absence continuation exception requires that the defendant was initially present when trial began. Crosby’s convictions from such a trial must be set aside and the case remanded for proceedings complying with the rule; no constitutional minimum or change to another enumerated exception is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine participating Justices adopt the rule and explanation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Diaz v. United States, 223 U.S. 442 — applied at its actual scope: voluntary departure permits continuation after initial presence, not commencement in absence.
- Taylor v. United States, 414 U.S. 17 — applied: no special warning is required for voluntary midtrial absence; its initial-presence premise remains essential.
- United States v. Crosby, 917 F.2d 362 — rejected on Rule 43: practical burdens and knowing pretrial flight cannot replace the rule’s express commencement condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other express Rule 43 exceptions and lawful independent flight sanctions are unchanged. The Court decides neither constitutional waiver nor a general harmless-error rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining Crosby’s convictions and remand for proceedings complying with Rule 43; no flight sanction or revised restitution award is entered.

### Protected silence and habeas harmless-error review

#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to this preserved Doyle claim with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman here; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. Exhaustion, preservation, other habeas gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

**Later-authority backlinks:** [O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/ONeal_v_McAninch_merits_1995-02-21.md): extends Chapman review to the preserved personal-intent claim arising from the combined instructions and prosecutorial argument; the State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden if a violation is found. The Doyle holding remains unchanged, and no general rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors is adopted. See Criminal Procedure — Constitutional trial error on habeas.

### Sentencing for trial perjury

#### United States v. Dunnigan, 507 U.S. 87 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1300.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Constitutional permission and the Guideline’s consequence

**Holding:** A sentence increase under U.S.S.G. § 3C1.1 for independently established trial perjury—willfully false testimony under oath or affirmation about a material matter—does not violate the defendant’s right to testify. Once the court properly determines that the defendant committed such perjury, the applicable Guideline requires its two-level obstruction adjustment; neither exercising the trial right nor a guilty verdict alone supplies that predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), applied to the right’s boundary: the right to present testimony includes no entitlement to perjury; the decision does not authorize assuming falsehood from an unsuccessful defense.
- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied: actual willful trial falsehood may inform sentencing; its rehabilitation discussion is not an exclusive justification and its warning against reflex increases remains operative.

##### Perjury predicates, independent findings and application

**Holding:** When a defendant objects to a § 3C1.1 increase based on trial testimony, the court must review the evidence and independently find willfully false sworn testimony concerning a material matter, excluding confusion, mistake and faulty memory. Separate clear findings for each element are preferable, but a single determination encompassing all the factual predicates is sufficient; the district court’s findings here meet that standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied with its safeguard intact: a sentencing response requires an actual assessment of deliberate false testimony, not an automatic consequence of conviction.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), left intact in its factual boundary: absence of a protected right to perjury does not establish that disputed testimony was perjurious.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No separate perjury prosecution or special verbal formula is required. Neither a guilty verdict nor rejection of a truthful defense establishes perjury. No general proof quantum, universal Guidelines-commentary rule or separate actual-obstruction element from summary-contempt law is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical constitutional prohibition and remand; the district court’s independent finding encompasses the required perjury predicates.

### Forfeited error on criminal appeal

#### United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1306.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: applies intentional relinquishment; right-specific waiver requirements remain.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies limited plain-error authority and ordinary prejudice requirement.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Atkinson, 297 U.S. 157: applies the independent fairness, integrity and public-reputation standard.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies discretionary, circumscribed correction.
- Sawyer, June 22, 1992: distinguished; capital actual-innocence gateway is not a universal limit on appellate correction.

##### Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone

**Holding:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 24(c) waivability, errors first clarified on appeal, outcome-independent categories, appropriate prejudice presumptions, the preserved-objection burden, and Rule 606(b) hearing limits remain undecided. Because substantial rights were not shown, discretionary correction of a prejudicial intrusion is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the alternate-presence new-trial ruling and remand for remaining appellate issues. Gray counts V–VII and Olano counts VI and VIII, reversed for insufficient evidence, remain untouched and are not authorized for retrial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Parker v. Gladden, 385 U.S. 363, and Remmer v. United States, 347 U.S. 227: apply the focus on prejudicial influence, preserving appropriate presumptions in other circumstances.
- Smith v. Phillips, 455 U.S. 209: applies rejection of automatic retrial for every compromising contact.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200: applies the ordinary instruction-following presumption, not an irrebuttable cure.
- Zafiro, January 25, 1993: consistent record-specific instruction analysis; no blanket rule that instructions cure intrusions.

### Personal Fourth Amendment rights

#### United States v. Padilla, 508 U.S. 77 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-207.
**Decided:** May 3, 1993.

##### Suppression depends on the defendant’s own protected interest

**Holding:** A defendant’s participation in a conspiracy neither creates nor defeats a personal Fourth Amendment claim: the defendant must show interference with his or her own protected property interest in a seizure or reasonable expectation of privacy in a search. The conspiracy exception is rejected, and the lower court must evaluate each reviewed defendant’s actual interests without reopening the unchallenged illegality of the stop.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Conspiracy neither supplies nor destroys an actual protected interest. The Court does not decide that every respondent lacks an interest or that all evidence must be admitted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for individual assessment of Donald and Maria Sylvia Simpson, Xavier Padilla, and Jorge and Maria Padilla. Strubbe’s unpetitioned loss and the unchallenged illegality of the stop remain untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alderman v. United States, 394 U.S. 165: applies express rejection of vicarious coconspirator rights.
- Rakas v. Illinois, 439 U.S. 128; United States v. Salvucci, 448 U.S. 83; Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies individualized protected-interest inquiry.
- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109: preserves distinct search and seizure interests.
- Soldal v. Cook County, December 8, 1992: consistent possessory/privacy distinction; home holding not enlarged into categorical vehicle standing.

#### [United States v. Wellons](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Wellons_merits_1995-05-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-496.
**Decided:** May 30, 1995.

##### Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement

**Holding:** Absence from a rental agreement does not by itself defeat a driver’s Fourth Amendment privacy claim when he possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud or comparable wrongful possession. Courts must evaluate the actual legitimate relationship, including the right to exclude, the owner’s retained rights and any effective termination of possession; renter permission alone does not establish privacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined in this proposition by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rental company's impoundment request may bear on whether possession remained legitimate at search time; its effect is not found here. Permission alone, title alone or the contract's name list alone does not settle the individual's privacy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rakas v. Illinois, 439 U.S. 128: applies its requirement of the claimant’s own legitimate interest, rejecting proxy rights while refusing a contract-name shortcut.
- Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies the circumstance-specific privacy inquiry; possession or property alone is not conclusive.
- Minnesota v. Olson, 495 U.S. 91: extends its recognition that an invited user can have personal privacy without title to this bounded inquiry; it supplies no automatic rental-driver privacy.
- United States v. Padilla, May 3, 1993: applies the individualized search/seizure inquiry; no automatic derivative rights are created.

##### Independent privacy in closed personal luggage

**Holding:** A person’s lack of vehicle privacy does not by itself extinguish a legitimate expectation of privacy in that person’s closed luggage inside the vehicle. Ownership or lawful control and measures preserving privacy must be established independently, and a valid warrant exception reaching the container may still authorize its search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined on this separate proposition by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Each claimed container requires its own ownership or lawful-control and privacy inquiry, including any disclaimer. The Court makes no ownership or container-specific probable-cause finding and does not restore Sanders's displaced automobile warrant rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Chadwick, 433 U.S. 1: preserves personal closed-luggage privacy as distinct from vehicle mobility, subject to the later applicable automobile rule.
- Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies the requirement of a demonstrated personal privacy interest; ownership or lawful control and privacy measures require assessment.
- California v. Acevedo, 500 U.S. 565: preserves automobile probable-cause authority to search containers within its scope; personal container privacy does not impose a categorical warrant requirement.
- Arkansas v. Sanders, 442 U.S. 753: retains container privacy only consistently with Acevedo’s displacement of its contrary automobile warrant rule; that displaced rule is not revived.

##### Remedy after rejection of the categorical grounds

**Holding:** When the reviewed categorical privacy grounds are erroneous but personal interests and preserved search justifications remain unresolved, the judgment must be vacated and remanded for those determinations before a suppression consequence is imposed. A recognized interest does not foreclose lawful probable-cause search, consent, standardized noninvestigatory inventory after lawful custody, or another preserved warrant exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt this case’s remedial holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The company's request is not itself lawful impoundment or consent to search every personal bag. A dog alert does not establish legality of the preceding detention or sniff or unlimited probable-cause scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California v. Acevedo, 500 U.S. 565: applies the distinction between an interest and a lawful probable-cause search reaching a container; no automatic suppression follows recognition of privacy.
- United States v. Padilla, May 3, 1993: applies its individual-interest remand discipline without deciding unproved exception predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the conviction affirmance and remand for the claimant's legitimate vehicle possession and personal closed-luggage interests, including permission, theft, fraud, comparable wrongful possession or effective repossession. Determine lawful impoundment; detention and dog-sniff legality and consequences; probable cause and container scope; lawful consent and scope; standardized noninvestigatory inventory; or another preserved warrant exception. Suppression depends on supported findings and law, with ordinary harmlessness if applicable. No acquittal, automatic exclusion, new exclusionary exception or resentencing is ordered.

### Reasonable-doubt instructions and structural error

#### Sullivan v. Louisiana, 508 U.S. 275 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5129.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### A charge vitiating the reasonable-doubt standard leaves no valid verdict for harmless review

**Holding:** When an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt charge vitiates the jury’s governing standard for all factual findings, the defendant has received no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. - In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358: proof-beyond-reasonable-doubt requirement applied.
- Duncan v. Louisiana, 391 U.S. 145: jury’s constitutional role applied.
- Cage v. Louisiana, 498 U.S. 39: conceded defect taken as the predicate, not relitigated.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18: actual-verdict inquiry preserved; no substitute hypothetical verdict permitted.
- Rose v. Clark, 478 U.S. 570: separable presumption error distinguished.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: structural-defect category applied.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991: contested-charge framework preserved; no reopening of the conceded defect.

### Firearm barter and the trafficking nexus

#### Smith v. United States, 508 U.S. 223 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-8674.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### Offering a firearm in exchange for drugs is use within the charged statute

**Holding:** Offering a firearm as consideration in a drug exchange constitutes using the firearm under the applicable §924(c)(1), even though the firearm is employed as merchandise rather than fired, brandished or otherwise employed as a weapon. The separate during-and-in-relation-to requirement must also be met; the statute is not satisfied by possession alone under this holding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

- Deal v. United States, May 17, 1993: conviction sequence remains distinct; it does not decide use.

##### The offered firearm has the required connection to the trafficking crime

**Holding:** The firearm must have a purpose or effect with respect to the trafficking crime, rather than being present by accident or coincidence; at minimum, it must facilitate or have the potential to facilitate that crime. **Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Stewart, 779 F.2d 538: facilitation formulation used as a minimum connection, not a universal sufficiency rule.
- United States v. Long, 905 F.2d 1572: facilitative relationship formulation applied within the proved exchange setting.

### Tactile recognition during a protective frisk

#### Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2019.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Minnesota v. Dickerson, June 7, 1993, No. 91-2019, see Federal Courts — Collateral consequences of diversion.

##### Authorized touch can supply probable cause without an additional search

**Holding:** An officer lawfully conducting a protective patdown may seize contraband when the authorized touching itself supplies probable cause of its incriminating character and the officer has lawful access to it. The officer may not manipulate or otherwise explore a nonthreatening object beyond the frisk to obtain that probable cause; immediate recognition requires probable cause, not certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Part II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

- Horton v. California, 496 U.S. 128: lawful access and immediately apparent incriminating character extended to lawful touch.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: probable cause may not be manufactured through a separate unauthorized search.
- Soldal v. Cook County, December 8, 1992: independent possessory protection preserved; no enlargement of a frisk follows.

##### Manipulation after excluding a weapon requires suppression here

**Holding:** When the accepted findings establish that the officer identified contraband only by squeezing and manipulating an object after determining that it was not a weapon, the examination exceeds Terry and cannot justify the seizure. **Proposition-level authority:** White’s Parts III and IV; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Probable cause must arise from authorized contact, with lawful access; exploratory touching is not a new exception. The diversion record’s identified legal uses, rather than an invented conviction, support review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm suppression on the accepted finding of manipulation after the weapon concern ended; no further evidentiary opportunity for the State is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry, 392 U.S. 1: protective authority ended when the object was known not to be a weapon.
- Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: information obtained by the additional exploration cannot validate it retrospectively.

##### Preventive protection does not require existing disease

**Holding:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation by proving deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm. He must prove both the objective gravity of the exposure—including seriousness, likelihood and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk—and the separate subjective requirement under Wilson, assessed against current conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, Preventive protection does not require existing disease portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The objective inquiry includes scientific risk and society’s unwillingness to tolerate involuntary exposure; Wilson’s subjective requirement remains separate. Transfer and actual implementation of the new smoking policy bear on current exposure, culpability and prospective need.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm and remand for proof of the future-risk claim; the current-symptom disposition and damages immunity remain intact. No violation finding, damages or prison-wide smoking ban.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle, 429 U.S. 97 — serious custodial needs and culpability retained.
- Hutto, 437 U.S. 678 — protection from unsafe conditions applied.
- Rhodes, 452 U.S. 337 — objective seriousness retained.
- Wilson, 501 U.S. 294 — deliberate indifference remains a separate requirement.
- DeShaney, 489 U.S. 189 — custodial safety obligation supplies context.
- Hudson, February 25, 1992 — nontrivial malicious beating rule remains distinct; no universal conditions standard imported.

### Effective consideration of youth mitigation

##### Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route

### Competence and informed waiver

#### Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-725.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### A common capacity standard and a separate actual waiver

**Holding:** Dusky supplies the constitutional mental-capacity standard for standing trial, pleading guilty and choosing to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings. A competent defendant may relinquish the rights only through a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver; competence does not establish that the particular choice was informed and uncoerced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402: the functional competence standard governs each presented choice.
- Faretta v. California, 422 U.S. 806: legal skill is distinct from competence to choose representation.
- Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238, and Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: actual informed and voluntary waiver remains separately required.
- Westbrook v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 150: additional waiver inquiry preserved; a higher constitutional capacity quantum is not required.

##### Existing findings do not eliminate the distinct inquiry on remand

**Holding:** A further competence determination is required when circumstances give the court reason to doubt present competence; no automatic hearing is required for every plea or waiver. Reversal of the higher-standard ruling leaves the existing claims about adequate inquiry and informed, voluntary relinquishment for consideration under the correct standards and the applicable pre-AEDPA treatment of state findings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** States may provide further safeguards. Execution competence and a distinct standard for actually conducting a trial without counsel remain open. Medication does not itself establish incapacity, and earlier findings are neither ignored nor conclusive after material change; reliable retrospective review and actual waivers remain for consideration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the higher-standard basis and remove the associated conditional-writ direction; remand under proper capacity, inquiry, waiver and pre-AEDPA state-finding rules. No automatic validation or restoration of the ex-wife murder death sentence already reduced to life.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375, and Drope v. Missouri, 420 U.S. 162: reasonable doubt concerning present competence triggers inquiry.
- Medina, June 22, 1992, and Riggins, May 18, 1992: their limited rules are preserved, not enlarged into conclusive findings here.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37: the Court may decline to introduce an unraised Teague defense.

### Successive prosecution after criminal contempt

#### United States v. Dixon, 509 U.S. 688 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1231.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection

**Holding:** A completed nonsummary criminal-contempt prosecution places the accused in criminal jeopardy; private initiation and an order’s protective purpose do not categorically remove that protection. Whether a later prosecution is for the same offense remains a separate inquiry; coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 194: criminal character of punitive contempt applied.
- Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range Co., 221 U.S. 418: punitive/coercive distinction applied.
- Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton, 481 U.S. 787: independent judicial enforcement authority retained, not a categorical exception from criminal protection.

##### Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again

**Holding:** When a criminal-contempt adjudication necessarily prosecutes the substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same substantive offense again. Dixon’s drug offense and Foster I’s November assault were necessarily prosecuted in the respective contempts and are barred; this shared proposition does not supply a common analysis of Foster II–V.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S. 161: successive greater/included-offense protection applied.
- Harris v. Oklahoma, 433 U.S. 682: necessary substantive offense cannot be erased by an abstract description of the first charge.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299: elements inquiry retained; disagreement over its broader application separately recorded.

##### Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law

**Holding:** Grady is not overruled: beyond Blockburger’s elements protection, a later prosecution is barred when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense for which the defendant has already been prosecuted. Mere reuse of evidence or a common episode does not suffice, and Felix’s independently punishable conspiracy holding remains; no five-Justice rationale determines Grady’s application to Foster II–V in this fractured decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady survives; evidentiary overlap and a common episode do not alone establish a bar. Summary contempt, coercive civil sanctions and separate sovereigns are not decided. Foster II–V have no controlling rationale, as stated separately below.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the bar for Dixon’s drug charge and Foster I (6–3), leaving the contempt judgments intact. Reverse the bar for Foster II–V and remand (5–4); threat charges remain subject to a properly established Ashe bar from actual acquittals and other defenses. Foster V may proceed without authorizing a barred simple-assault conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508: expressly retained; no overruling by this decision.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: conspiracy and evidentiary-overlap distinctions preserved, not recast as an overruling.
- Illinois v. Vitale, 447 U.S. 410: successive-prosecution concern beyond formal elements supports retention.
- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S. 161: finality and greater/included-offense protection retained.
- Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S. 436: independent issue-preclusion rule preserved for the remanded threat charges.

##### Whether Foster II–V may proceed under a common double-jeopardy rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse the bars to Foster II–V and remand, 5–4. No controlling rationale exists under Marks: the four-Justice elements-only rationale would overrule Grady, while Blackmun retains Grady but treats judicial-order enforcement differently. Those incompatible grounds are not logical subsets. The judgment permits these counts without creating a general contempt exception or displacing the separately majority-supported retention of Grady.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foster II–IV remain subject to any actual-acquittal issue preclusion established under Ashe and other preserved defenses. Foster V’s distinct assault-with-intent-to-kill charge may proceed; the judgment does not authorize a barred simple-assault conviction or disturb the contempt dispositions.

### Excessive fines and federal civil forfeiture

#### Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6073.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### These statutory forfeitures are covered fines

**Holding:** Forfeiture to the United States under 21 U.S.C.§§ 881(a)(4) and(a)(7) constitutes payment in kind to the sovereign at least partly as punishment and is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Its civil designation, proceeding against property, and concurrent remedial aims do not remove coverage; coverage does not establish excessiveness or import all criminal-procedure safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S. 257: government prosecution/receipt distinguishes this fine from private punitive award.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435: partly punitive function recognized by analogy; separate Double Jeopardy analysis not imported.
- One 1958 Plymouth Sedan v. Pennsylvania, 380 U.S. 693: lawful property is not contraband merely because used in crime.
- One Lot Emerald Cut Stones v. United States, 409 U.S. 232, and United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354: remedial customs/contraband characterizations distinguished, not abolished.
- Buena Vista, February 24, 1993: statutory owner defenses remain provision-specific; no § 881(a)(7) or constitutional innocent owner holding imported.

##### Historical in rem form does not negate punishment

**Holding:** The historical treatment of statutory in rem forfeiture includes punishment and does not establish categorical exemption from the Excessive Fines Clause for these forfeitures. The traditionally limited common-law innocent-owner defense does not prove that forfeiture was solely remedial; the constitutionality of forfeiting a truly innocent owner’s property remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peisch v. Ware, 4 Cranch 347: punitive understanding and stranger-removal qualification applied.
- The Palmyra, 12 Wheat 1: no prior personal conviction required, not an absence of punishment holding.
- J.W. Goldsmith, Jr.-Grant Co. v. United States, 254 U.S. 505, and Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht, 416 U.S. 663: punitive/deterrent features recognized; truly innocent owner question reserved.

##### Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection

**Holding:** The lower court must evaluate whether Austin’s actual forfeiture is excessive; statutory facilitation and coverage as punishment do not themselves decide that question. The Court adopts no comprehensive excessiveness test and does not limit the remand to instrumentality alone: the property’s connection to the offense may matter, and other relevant considerations may also be examined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage does not decide excessiveness, incorporate all criminal safeguards or establish state incorporation. Truly innocent ownership, taxation and solely compensatory recovery remain distinct; neither a comprehensive formula nor instrumentality alone is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for an excessiveness inquiry on a developed record. No automatic property return, value finding, ratio or fixed cap; the lower court’s concern was not a completed excessiveness determination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris, 492 U.S. 257: fine coverage principle applied without inventing a comprehensive proportionality formula.
- Calero-Toledo, 416 U.S. 663: statutory/historical connection not converted into a final excessiveness answer.

### Excessive fines and criminal RICO forfeiture

#### Alexander v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1526.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the traditional-prior-restraint classification and independent First Amendment examination in *Alexander v. United States*, June 28, 1993, No. 91-1526, see First Amendment — Postconviction forfeiture of expressive enterprises.

##### Whether punitive criminal RICO forfeiture requires a distinct Excessive Fines inquiry

**Holding:** A criminal RICO forfeiture extracting property as punishment is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause independently of the validity of the convictions or the permissibility of the prison term. ### Criminal statutory knowledge and lenity

#### [Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1196.

**Decided:** 1994-01-11.

##### Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful

**Holding:** For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

- Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943): applied for contextual interpretation of willfulness and permissible circumstantial proof.
- Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991): its recognition of a statutory known-duty requirement informs this distinct scheme; its tax rule is not made universal.
- Liparota v. United States, 471 U.S. 419 (1985): applied for context-sensitive knowledge requirements where apparently lawful conduct may otherwise be swept into a crime.
- Potter v. United States, 155 U.S. 438 (1894): the criminal statute's separate willfulness term receives operative effect rather than disappearing into other elements.

**Independent alternative holding:** If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152 (1990): applied to prevent policy or legislative materials from enlarging a crime beyond sufficiently clear text.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and Congress's responsibility to define criminal liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank's reporting duty and the customer's structuring prohibition are distinct. Section 5322(a)'s exception for §5315 and its regulations remains an exception to the penalty provision, not to §5324(3). No universal ignorance-of-law defense, requirement that proceeds be lawful or civil-forfeiture rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment for both Waldemar and Loretta Ratzlaf is reversed and remanded. Waldemar's conspiracy, four structuring and interstate-travel counts and Loretta's conspiracy and interstate-travel counts require count-specific implementation of the erroneous instruction; no count is declared automatically invalid. ### Custodial interrogation

#### [Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Stansbury_v_California_merits_1994-04-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5770.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned

**Holding:** Miranda custody exists upon formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from all objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee’s position would understand them; an officer’s uncommunicated view that the person is or is not a suspect, and the interviewee’s undisclosed subjective belief, have no independent role. An officer’s words or conduct conveying suspicion matter only to the extent they affect that objective understanding: communicated suspicion is not automatically custody, and undisclosed beliefs bearing on a witness’s credibility do not thereby become independent custody factors.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): applied to the custodial-interrogation trigger; no new exception to warnings is created.
- Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), and California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983): applied for the arrest-equivalent restraint standard; station questioning and suspect status alone remain insufficient.
- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984): applied to exclude uncommunicated intentions from the objective custody inquiry.
- Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976): applied to distinguish investigative focus from custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual custody, independent coercion, admissibility, harmlessness and capital punishment are not decided. An undisclosed belief bearing on witness credibility does not become an independent custody factor. Withrow and Powell's different review and remedy rules remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded to reconsider custody without the private investigative-focus criterion. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release or resentencing is ordered; ultimate custody and any properly reached consequences remain for the state court.

#### [Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Davis_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1949.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Holding:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Holding:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Holding:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides postwaiver invocation in the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules. It does not independently resolve direct Fifth Amendment operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, initial-warning adequacy, invalid initial waiver or the separate October 20 interview and evidence dispute. Section 3501 was not the Government's merits ground and supplies no alternative holding on construction, applicability or constitutionality. Withrow's collateral and coercion rules and Stansbury's objective custody inquiry remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court of Military Appeals is affirmed unanimously on the postwaiver invocation issue. No exclusion, new trial or remand follows; the separate October 20 dispute and any §3501 ground remain undecided.

### Drug-paraphernalia classification and knowledge

#### [Posters ’N’ Things, Ltd. v. United States, 511 U.S. 513](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Posters_N_Things_Ltd_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-903.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Objective design and primary likely use identify the covered articles

**Holding:** Former §857(d) covers equipment, products or material primarily intended or designed for manufacturing, compounding, converting, concealing, producing, processing, preparing, injecting, ingesting, inhaling or otherwise introducing an unlawfully possessed controlled substance into the body: objective design and primary likely use by customers in the actual commercial setting can establish that classification, including a demonstrated subset of dual-use goods. Section 857(e)'s considerations are permissive and nonexclusive; §857(f) independently exempts persons authorized by local, State or Federal law to manufacture, possess or distribute such items, and items that in the normal lawful course of business are imported, exported, transported or sold through the mail or by other means and traditionally intended for tobacco use, including pipes, papers and accessories.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined in this part by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree that the objective likely-use ground is sufficient here, without joining an exclusive objective construction of the intended-use branch.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, Hoffman Estates, Inc., 455 U.S. 489: applies its distinction between objective manufacturer design and relevant retail marketing; classification remains tied to the article and commercial context.

##### Knowing the likely illicit use is required; desiring it is not

**Holding:** A conviction under former §857(a)(1) requires knowing use of the Postal Service or another interstate conveyance as part of a scheme to sell articles the defendant knows are likely to be used with illegal drugs; actual factual knowledge is required, not merely what a reasonable retailer should have known. The prosecution need not prove the defendant desired the drug use, knew the statutory legal classification, or knew a particular customer's eventual use; the instructions here adequately conveyed the necessary knowledge.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined in this part by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree that the objective likely-use ground is sufficient here, without joining an exclusive objective construction of the intended-use branch.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246, and United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422: apply the presumption of culpability and knowledge of probable consequences.
- United States v. Bailey, 444 U.S. 394: distinguishes knowledge from a necessary purpose to achieve a consequence.
- Hamling v. United States, 418 U.S. 87: distinguishes factual knowledge of material's character from knowledge of its legal classification.
- Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994: its earlier effective decision concerns known machinegun characteristics; it does not decide §857's intended-use branch or impose legal-knowledge requirements.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its text-specific willfulness holding.
- United States v. Granderson, March 22, 1994: preserves residual lenity after ordinary interpretation; no unresolved ambiguity requires the proposed purpose element here.

##### The statute gives adequate notice in this application

**Holding:** Former §857 is not unconstitutionally vague as applied to petitioners' demonstrated drug-paraphernalia operation: the statutory descriptions, article-specific and commercial evidence, independent exemptions and required actual knowledge provide adequate notice and constrain enforcement. Hypothetical marginal sales by other retailers do not establish this as-applied challenge, and no First Amendment overbreadth question is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined in this part by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree that the objective likely-use ground is sufficient here, without joining an exclusive objective construction of the intended-use branch.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Grayned v. City of Rockford, 408 U.S. 104, and Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352: apply fair-notice and constrained-enforcement principles to the actual statutory application.
- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, Hoffman Estates, Inc., 455 U.S. 489: applies its contextual as-applied analysis and the role of scienter; hypothetical marginal uses remain unadjudicated.

##### Private seller purpose alone does not replace the objective classification

**Holding:** Under the primary-intended-use branch, an unmanifested private desire by the seller that an otherwise lawful article be used with drugs does not by itself substitute for the objective classification of that article in its commercial circumstances. Marketing, instructions and display can establish a drug-oriented subset of dual-use goods, but one customer's proposed misuse alone does not establish primary likely use by customers generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the objective application and other controlling parts but does not join this additional exclusive proposition. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject the exclusion of an independently sufficient seller-intent route.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, Hoffman Estates, Inc., 455 U.S. 489: applies the design/marketing distinction to an objective commercial classification while reserving marginal applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The cocaine aiding-instruction/presumption contention was not raised or addressed below; laundering, investment and proceeds questions were omitted from the petition; cocaine-evidence sufficiency was not developed in merits briefing. Each is unreviewed for its own reason. The permissive evidence factors, authorization exemption and traditional-tobacco exemption do not become mandatory elements or findings that every store item is covered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit is affirmed on the reviewed questions, unanimously in result. No conviction or sentence is altered and no remand, resentencing or acquittal is ordered. The knowledge instructions are adequate; affirmance does not rest on harmless omission of an element.

### Federal presentment and confessions

#### [United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 511 U.S. 350](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/United_States_v_Alvarez_Sanchez_merits_1994-05-02.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1812.

**Decided:** 1994-05-02.

##### The relevant presentment duty precedes the measurement of delay

**Holding:** In a federal or District of Columbia prosecution, detention solely on state charges does not itself start §3501(c)’s federal-presentment period; the period concerns delay in performing the duty to bring the person before an officer empowered to commit defendants for federal or District of Columbia offenses. Any law-enforcement officer can hold a person on a basis or arrangement engaging that duty, but suspicion of a federal offense, ordinary interagency notification or a state label alone does not decide it; the Court does not make formal federal charges or arrest indispensable in every possible arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the same bounded rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McNabb v. United States, 318 U.S. 332 (1943), and Mallory v. United States, 354 U.S. 449 (1957): used to identify the federal-presentment setting; their complete relationship with §3501 is not decided.
- Anderson v. United States, 318 U.S. 350 (1943): preserved as the relevant improper-working-arrangement authority; the effect of §3501 on its rule and the full range of qualifying arrangements remain open.
- Stansbury v. California, decided April 26, 1994: distinguished; objective Miranda custody does not itself establish the separate federal-presentment duty.

##### The limited statutory protection does not decide all confession admissibility

**Holding:** Section 3501(c) prevents exclusion solely for delay in presentment when the trial judge finds the confession voluntary, its weight is left to the jury, and it was made within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the six-hour limit does not apply when the judge finds the longer delay reasonable considering the means of transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. The Court does not decide the treatment of confessions obtained more than six hours after relevant federal arrest outside that proviso, the general relationship among subsections (a), (b) and (c), or §3501’s entire relationship to Miranda.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this limited statutory explanation. Ginsburg’s concurrence, joined by Blackmun, emphasizes the reservation without changing either Justice’s full join or supplying a separate controlling answer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993): preserved in its distinction between Miranda and independent coercion; no new collateral-review rule is announced.
- Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975), and County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): their constitutional rules remain unchanged; the unpreserved alternative is not adjudicated here.
- Powell v. Nevada, decided March 30, 1994: preserved with its separate timing and remedy questions; it does not excuse the missing presentation of this alternative below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported district-court no-collusion finding is procedural posture, not an adopted correctness finding. Section 3501's effect on Anderson, the full range of improper arrangements, post-six-hour confessions outside the proviso, and the statute's general relationship to Miranda remain unresolved. A waiver does not settle independent coercion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and remanded on the state-detention aggregation error. No admission or suppression of the confession, reinstatement of conviction, retrial, acquittal or release is directed. The Gerstein/McLaughlin alternative, unpresented below, is not adjudicated or affirmatively reopened.

### Firearm-characteristic knowledge

#### [Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States, 511 U.S. 368](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Beecham_and_Jones_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-16.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-445; Jones included under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### The law of the convicting jurisdiction governs the conviction exclusion

**Holding:** Section 921(a)(20)'s direction to use the law of the jurisdiction where the proceedings occurred governs the related exclusion for expungement, set-aside, pardon, or restoration of civil rights, so state restoration alone does not remove a federal conviction as a §922(g) predicate. The enacted exclusion remains subject to its qualification that a conviction is not excluded if the pardon, expungement, or restoration of civil rights expressly provides that the person may not ship, transport, possess, or receive firearms; the Court decides which sovereign's law governs, not whether a particular federal restorative mechanism exists or has been satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous; all nine Justices join the complete rule and both affirmances.

**Material application:** State restoration alone does not remove either federal predicate. Jones's two restored West Virginia convictions remain excluded; the decision does not revive them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court creates no federal restoration process and does not decide a particular federal pardon, the availability or requirements of §925(c) relief, the civil-rights bundle, automatic versus affirmative restoration, or whether federal habeas qualifies as a set-aside. It enters no guilt finding or independent ruling on the linked false-statement count's elements, materiality or intent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Fourth Circuit judgments are affirmed, 9–0 each. Jones's indictment-reinstatement and remand disposition remains operative; Beecham receives no new sentencing or restoration order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): applied to reserve lenity for unresolved penal ambiguity after interpretation; competing proposed readings alone do not trigger it.
- United States v. Granderson, decided March 22, 1994: its contextual interpretive approach and residual role for lenity remain intact; its distinct sentencing ambiguity does not establish ambiguity here.
- Jarecki v. G. D. Searle & Co., 367 U.S. 303 (1961): its neighboring-terms principle supports, without mechanically compelling, the shared sovereign reference.
- Dickerson v. New Banner Institute, Inc., 460 U.S. 103 (1983): the intervening statutory choice-of-law text governs; its former federal-law definition is not used to bypass the enacted provision.

The contrary separate-clause reasoning of Geyler and Edwards is rejected on sovereign selection. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### [Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Staples_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1441.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun

**Holding:** To convict a defendant under 26 U.S.C. §5861(d) for possessing an unregistered machinegun in the circumstances presented here, the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics bringing the rifle within the complete-weapon machinegun definition—a weapon that shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger; circumstantial evidence may establish that knowledge. It need not prove knowledge of nonregistration, the registration requirement, or illegality, and the omitted-element instruction requires reversal and remand without a finding of evidentiary insufficiency or an order of acquittal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246 (1952): applies the presumption of criminal fault despite statutory silence.
- United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422 (1978): applies contextual examination before dispensing with fault.
- United States v. Freed, 401 U.S. 601 (1971): distinguishes knowledge of the object's regulated characteristics from knowledge of nonregistration; its registration holding remains intact.
- United States v. Balint, 258 U.S. 250 (1922): preserves the bounded public-welfare category without extending its narcotics construction automatically to this rifle.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its express-willfulness and knowledge-of-illegality rule; no universal legal-knowledge requirement follows.
- United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., June 8, 1992: leaves its making rule and limited parts-package disposition unchanged; neither decides this possession element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate frame/receiver, conversion-part, conversion-parts and one-person-controlled assembly-parts branches of §5845(b) remain undecided. Section 5845(a)'s antique exclusion and its separate exclusion for Secretary-designated collector's items unlikely to be used as weapons remain; the collector exclusion does not encompass machineguns or destructive devices and depends on manufacture date, value, design and other characteristics. No exclusion is established for this rifle. No Second Amendment, facial validity, ultimate knowledge or error-review result is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit is reversed and remanded, 7–2, for the required factual-knowledge instruction. No acquittal, mandatory retrial or new sentence is ordered; properly available sufficiency and error-review questions retain their own requirements.

### Habeas nonretroactivity and double jeopardy

#### [Schiro v. Farley, 510 U.S. 222](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Schiro_v_Farley_merits_1994-01-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7549.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### Late presentation of nonretroactivity

**Holding:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted that defense in the lower courts and in its opposition to certiorari and first raised it in merits briefing, the Court may decline to consider it; the Court exercises that discretion here and reaches the presented double-jeopardy claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens also agree with merits reach, but their agreement is unnecessary to the opinion's controlling authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Collins v. Youngblood: applied for the nonjurisdictional character of nonretroactivity and the resulting discretion.
- Teague v. Lane: its substantive nonretroactivity rule and contemporary exceptions are left unchanged; they are not applied to dispose of this case.

##### The initial sentencing phase is not a second prosecution

**Holding:** Considering the intentional-killing aggravator in the initial sentencing phase of this prosecution did not itself place Schiro in jeopardy a second time. Acquittal-like capital determinations remain protected, but the jury's advisory recommendation against death was not a final acquittal of a statutory aggravator or sentence under this scheme.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Stroud v. United States and United States v. DiFrancesco: applied to distinguish initial sentencing from successive prosecution.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital determinations; distinguished from this advisory recommendation and initial sentencing.
- United States v. Dixon and Grady v. Corbin: successive-prosecution protection remains in force; this case does not supply its later-prosecution predicate.
- Sochor v. Florida: its distinction between an unparticularized recommendation and an actual aggravator finding remains intact.

##### The verdict did not necessarily determine intent for Schiro

**Holding:** Assuming issue preclusion can operate between guilt and sentencing in one prosecution, the claimant must show from the pleadings, evidence, charge and other relevant record that the jury necessarily decided the disputed ultimate fact in his favor. Schiro did not make that showing: the Count II felony-murder conviction and blank alternative forms could rationally reflect selection of one murder verdict without rejecting knowing or intentional killing.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ashe v. Swenson: applied to the necessary determination of an ultimate fact on the full record.
- Green v. United States: implied-acquittal protection preserved; its result does not control these alternative forms and instructions.
- Dowling v. United States: applied for the claimant's burden to identify an issue actually and necessarily determined; no new evidentiary rule is adopted.
- Griffin v. United States and Sochor v. Florida: their limits on inferring specific findings from general verdicts and recommendations remain distinct from actual acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither a universal forfeiture rule for Teague nor a decision that every advisory capital recommendation is an acquittal is adopted. General availability of issue preclusion between guilt and sentencing is assumed rather than decided. No affirmative finding of intent or independent jury-sentencing ruling is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's denial of the presented habeas grounds is affirmed, 7–2. No new factfinding, writ or resentencing is ordered; neither conviction nor sentence is disturbed on these claims.

#### [Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Caspari_v_Bohlen_merits_1994-02-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1500.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido: applied to distinguish subsidiary legal arguments from distinct questions; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate requirements.
- Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.: preserved; an omitted independent question does not become reviewable merely through notice, logical priority or ordinary economy. This confined same-rule habeas limitation is distinguished from its separate intervention determination.
- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

### Habeas review and counsel conflicts

#### [Burden v. Zant, 510 U.S. 132](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Burden_v_Zant_summary_merits_1994-01-10.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8836.

**Decided:** 1994-01-10.

##### An appellate court cannot defer to a finding that was never made

**Holding:** A federal habeas judgment resting on deference to a district-court finding that the district court never made must be reversed when that mistake leaves the actual conflict claim unadjudicated. Absence of formal transactional immunity does not establish absence of an informal nonprosecution agreement; on remand the courts must determine whether counsel had an actual conflict that adversely affected his performance under Cuyler v. Sullivan, without presuming those predicates from joint representation alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants support this narrow record-correction rule and remand. No fractured-opinion synthesis is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Burden v. Zant, 498 U.S. 433 (February 19, 1991): the earlier mandate and required treatment of the state factual determination remain binding.
- Cuyler v. Sullivan, 446 U.S. 335 (1980): applied as the remand standard; actual conflict and adverse effect are required, and once both are proved no additional outcome-prejudice showing is necessary.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): its ordinary outcome-prejudice requirement is not added to a proved Sullivan violation; no ineffective-assistance merits finding is made.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not determine the informal agreement, actual conflict, adverse effect, ultimate habeas relief or the §2254(d)(3) factual-development exception. Formal transactional immunity and an informal nonprosecution agreement remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for the actual-conflict and adverse-effect inquiry on the accurate record. Joint representation alone does not establish either predicate; a proved Sullivan violation requires no separate outcome-prejudice showing. No immediate writ or unconditional release is ordered.

### Military judicial appointments and impartiality

#### [Weiss v. United States, 510 U.S. 163](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Weiss_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-19.md)

### Parole consideration and prospective relief

#### [Cavanaugh v. Roller](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Cavanaugh_v_Roller_merits_1993-11-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1510.

**Decided:** 1993-11-30.

For the other questions in Cavanaugh v. Roller, 1993-11-30, No. 92-1510, see Federal Courts — A live claim for the outstanding hearing; Civil Rights — Section 1983 permits this opportunity-only claim.

##### The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record

**Holding:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining the intervening review is established. This binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence, and ultimate parole discretion; the prisoner need not prove that the omitted hearing would have produced release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns compulsory loss of every alternate ordinary hearing, not every procedural disadvantage. The State's separate medical power requires a condition associated with life expectancy of no more than one year and is no substitute for general intervening consideration; it has no established application here. The ten-consecutive-years report before release is distinct from hearing frequency. Individual postponement supported by findings, meaningful interim-review systems, voting rules, suitability criteria, abolition of parole and the reach of extraordinary clemency remain undecided. Neither a general probability formula nor proof of the omitted hearing's outcome is required. Act No. 184's January 1 transition does not itself establish later mootness or supersession of this federal holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): extends its protection of a legally available avenue to reduced imprisonment to the binding loss of this mandatory consideration opportunity; no vested entitlement to release is required.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies its distinction between binding adverse legal changes and flexible guidance; unchanged outer limits or a possibly identical outcome are not sufficient answers.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): applies its punishment-related limitation and rejects a free-standing rule against every procedural disadvantage; no broader substantial-rights theory is restored.
- Dobbert v. Florida, 432 U.S. 282 (1977): distinguishes permissible procedural alteration from the binding withdrawal of a meaningful opportunity for earlier release on this record.
- Griffin v. State, 315 S.C. 285 (1993): treats its adoption of Roller's analysis and overruling of Gunter v. State, 298 S.C. 113 (1989), as confirming state authority, not an independent substitute for this Court's federal analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's prospective-relief judgment is affirmed, 6–3. Its remand for a declaration restoring annual reconsideration remains in force; the district court may issue an injunction only if necessary to secure consideration. No release, damages, immunity reconsideration, contempt finding or specific hearing date is ordered. The unappealed official- and individual-capacity damages dismissals remain undisturbed.

**Later-authority backlinks:** [South Carolina Act No. 184, effective January 1, 1994](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/South_Carolina_Act_184_effective_source_1994-01-01.md): offense-date classification and savings/transition terms; no later mootness, completed hearing, judgment vacatur or displacement of the federal holding is established. [California Department of Corrections v. Morales, April 25, 1995](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/California_Department_of_Corrections_v_Morales_merits_1995-04-25.md): applies the protected-opportunity rule to the retrospective three-year postponement supported by individualized findings on the present record. It does not decide every possible interim-review system; the original compulsory biennial-review holding remains unchanged. See Criminal Procedure — Retrospective loss of parole consideration.

### Probation revocation and lenity

#### [United States v. Granderson, 511 U.S. 39](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/United_States_v_Granderson_merits_1994-03-22.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1662.

**Decided:** 1994-03-22.

##### The former proviso requires two to six months’ imprisonment on this record

**Holding:** When controlled-substance possession triggers former §3565(a)'s mandatory probation-revocation proviso on an ordinary record with an initially available zero-to-six-month Guidelines imprisonment range and no established lawful departure, the replacement imprisonment term must be at least one-third of the range's six-month maximum and no more than that maximum: two to six months here. The sixty months of probation actually imposed do not become sixty equivalent prison months; materially different original ranges and downward-departure situations are not resolved by this calculation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; six Justices support this complete construction, independently of Scalia's and Kennedy's judgment concurrences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. R. L. C., 503 U.S. 291 (1992): its recognition of a lawful Guidelines ceiling informs the distinct revocation context; its juvenile minimum and interpretive divisions are not transplanted.
- Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992): preserved for actual lawful departure grounds; no departure is invented to sustain twenty months.
- Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (1993): preserved; commentary cannot override a federal statute or resolve the proviso by contrary text.

**Independent alternative holding:** After examination of the former proviso's text, sentencing structure and companion enactment, any remaining genuine uncertainty about the Government's proposed conversion of sixty probation months into a twenty-month prison minimum must be resolved against that greater punishment. This residual-lenity ground independently rejects the Government's construction; it neither makes every competing reading ambiguous nor supplies a universal lowest-number rule for different sentencing records.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same six Justices adopt this independently sufficient reason to reject the Government's twenty-month construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and clear legislative authorization of punishment.
- Bifulco v. United States, 447 U.S. 381 (1980): applied to genuine uncertainty about the authorized penal consequence.
- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): preserved; interpretive disagreement alone does not activate lenity.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: its residual-lenity principle is applied to this distinct statutory ambiguity, without importing its offense-specific knowledge requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materially different original ranges and probation following a substantial downward departure remain unresolved, including the denominator and any distinct lenity question. An actual lawful departure is necessary; no later statutory amendment or probation-to-prison exchange rate is applied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit is affirmed, 8–1. Its vacatur of twenty months' imprisonment and release order remain operative, and revocation remains intact. No new custodial sentencing, renewed probation, substitute fine or separate supervised-release change is ordered; the unrevoked fine's payment status is undecided.

### Prompt judicial probable-cause review

#### [Powell v. Nevada, 511 U.S. 79](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Powell_v_Nevada_merits_1994-03-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8841.

**Decided:** 1994-03-30.

##### McLaughlin applies to a case still pending on direct review

**Holding:** A criminal defendant whose judgment was not final when McLaughlin issued receives that decision's prompt-probable-cause-review rule on direct review, even if the arrest or prosecution began earlier and even assuming the decision announced a new rule. The prosecution's commencement date does not permit withholding the governing rule; applying it does not itself determine the remedy for a violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's direct-review portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt this proposition. Thomas separately opposes vacatur on a remedial ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Griffith v. Kentucky, 479 U.S. 314 (1987): applied directly to the nonfinal criminal judgment; the commencement-date cutoff is rejected.
- County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): applied to Powell without deciding whether it announced a new rule.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): its equal-application principle and separation of genuine remedy questions are preserved.

##### The four-day interval requires justification, with remedy separately determined on remand

**Holding:** A judicial probable-cause determination within forty-eight hours of a warrantless arrest generally complies with the prompt-review requirement, but the arrestee may prove that the actual delay was unreasonable—for example, because it served to gather evidence justifying arrest, arose from ill will or occurred for delay's own sake, without limiting the challenge to those examples; after forty-eight hours the government must prove a bona fide emergency or other extraordinary circumstance, and intervening weekends or the administrative convenience of combining procedures do not alone excuse the delay. Powell's four-day interval therefore requires that justification inquiry; the Court vacates for consideration under this rule and properly available remaining grounds without deciding suppression, causal product, attenuation, preservation, good-faith reliance or harmlessness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's timing-and-remand portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt this bounded disposition and reservation of remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975): applied to neutral judicial review, while preserving that unlawful detention alone does not void a conviction.
- County of Riverside v. McLaughlin: applied with all timing qualifications and the government's post-forty-eight-hour burden unchanged.
- New York v. Harris, 495 U.S. 14 (1990): retained as a distinct protected-interest and remedial analogy; its home-entry facts do not settle this statement's causal source.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796 (1984), and United States v. Crews, 445 U.S. 463 (1980): causal-product principles remain available without a new application finding here.
- Illinois v. Krull, 480 U.S. 340 (1987): its separate statutory-reliance defense remains available for lawful consideration, without a merits ruling on its predicates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A police declaration does not perform neutral judicial review or restart the clock. Personal appearance is separate. A state-law arraignment waiver does not itself waive federal prompt-review rights; a prearrest statement alone does not establish federal harmlessness. Suppression, causal product, attenuation, properly available preservation, good-faith reliance and harmlessness remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Nevada Supreme Court judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 8–1. Timing and lawful justification must be addressed before any necessary remaining remedial issues. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release, damages or capital resentencing is ordered by this disposition.

### Proof beyond a reasonable doubt

#### [Victor v. Nebraska and Sandoval v. California, 511 U.S. 1](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Victor_v_Nebraska_and_Sandoval_v_California_merits_1994-03-22.md)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 92-8894 (Victor) and 92-9049 (Sandoval).

**Decided:** 1994-03-22.

##### The whole charge must preserve proof beyond a reasonable doubt

**Holding:** A reasonable-doubt instruction violates due process when, read with the charge as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that the jury understood it to permit conviction without proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every element. No particular verbal formula is required; if the complete proof standard is constitutionally vitiated, Sullivan requires relief without appellate reweighing of guilt evidence, while the question whether a court must give a definition when none is given remains open.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's shared-standard portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine adopt the standard. Sullivan supplies the existing remedial rule; no defective charge is found by the Court here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 (1970): applied to preserve proof beyond reasonable doubt for every element.
- Holland v. United States, 348 U.S. 121 (1954), and Cupp v. Naughten, 414 U.S. 141 (1973): applied for whole-charge review without a prescribed script.
- Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991): the reasonable-likelihood rule is applied unchanged.
- Cage v. Louisiana, 498 U.S. 39 (1990): preserved for complete instructions that dilute the burden; no isolated-word rule is inferred.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana, 508 U.S. 275 (1993): its structural-error consequence remains unchanged and is not triggered by the Court's validity findings.

##### Sandoval’s evidence-linked charge satisfies due process

**Holding:** Sandoval's complete charge creates no reasonable likelihood of conviction below the reasonable-doubt standard because its moral-certainty language requires an abiding conviction after consideration of all evidence and its other instructions confine findings to received evidence and exclude sentiment, passion, prejudice and public feeling. References to moral evidence and possible or imaginary doubt are sustained only in that context, not as detached approval of those expressions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Sandoval portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same instruction ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. McGuire: applied to the full Sandoval charge and its actual contextual restraints.
- Cage v. Louisiana: distinguished on the interaction and effect of the complete instructions; its burden-protection rule remains binding.
- In re Winship: applied without changing the prosecution's elemental burden.

##### Victor’s evidence and abiding-conviction requirements sustain the complete charge

**Holding:** Victor's complete charge creates no reasonable likelihood of conviction on insufficient proof because it requires an abiding conviction after full, fair and impartial consideration of the evidence, restricts doubt to evidence or its absence, and keeps the burden on the State throughout. Its moral-certainty terminology therefore does not detach conviction from proof; this holding concerns the complete instruction and does not make correct boilerplate an automatic cure for contradictory instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Victor evidentiary/abiding-conviction portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; these six Justices directly support this sufficient contextual ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. McGuire: applied to the complete Victor charge.
- Cage v. Louisiana: distinguished on the full charge's operation, without narrowing the required degree of proof.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: remains controlling if a charge vitiates the burden; evidence strength supplies no alternative affirmance ground.

##### Victor’s additional contextual explanations do not authorize a lesser burden

**Holding:** In Victor's complete charge, actual and substantial doubt is contrasted with mere possibility, bare imagination and fanciful conjecture; the grave-transaction hesitation explanation further conveys a real doubt rather than an unusually large one. The strong-probabilities sentence is permissible here only because it expressly requires probabilities strong enough to exclude every reasonable doubt and operates with the charge's evidence and abiding-conviction requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's additional Victor contextual portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices support this additional explanation. Ginsburg does not join it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Holland v. United States: applied to assess the complete explanation rather than isolate its probability or hesitation language.
- Cage v. Louisiana: distinguished by the contextual qualifications actually supplied here; the constitutional burden remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holdings sustain the two complete charges in context. No national script, isolated-word ban, general approval of moral-certainty language or evidence-strength cure is adopted. Ginsburg's Victor join extends to the six-Justice evidentiary ground, not the additional five-Justice hesitation/probabilities explanation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sandoval's instruction judgment is affirmed unanimously; Victor's is affirmed 6–3. The California judgment and Nebraska denial of postconviction instruction relief remain undisturbed on the reviewed questions. No new trial, release or additional sentencing proceeding is ordered.

### Sentencing and collateral attacks

#### [Custis v. United States, 511 U.S. 485](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Custis_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5209.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing

**Holding:** Under 18 U.S.C. §924(e), a defendant may not use the federal sentencing proceeding to litigate these constitutional attacks on operative state convictions for ineffective assistance and involuntary counseled pleas. The statute does not supply a general collateral-review procedure; the distinct exception for an unconstitutional complete denial of appointed counsel remains, as do objections to whether the enhancement itself satisfies its statutory requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct joins to this statutory ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lewis v. United States, 445 U.S. 55 (1980): applies the distinction between existing conviction status and an unresolved collateral challenge, without extending its civil-disability rationale to every sentencing defect.
- Beecham v. United States, May 16, 1994: preserves the convicting-jurisdiction selector for §921(a)(20), without adding an ACCA collateral-review procedure.

##### The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require the ACCA sentencing court to hear Custis's ineffective-assistance and involuntary-counseled-plea attacks, but a conviction obtained through complete denial of appointed counsel in violation of the constitutional entitlement, without valid waiver, may be challenged there. Custis may pursue available review of the state convictions; successful invalidation permits an application concerning the enhancement through an authorized procedure, without a promised reopening or particular result.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six Justices join O'Connor's separate constitutional ground and its express remedial reservation; the three dissenters would allow the present attacks under their statutory construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (1963): preserves the constitutional appointed-counsel entitlement and valid-waiver boundary.
- Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), and United States v. Tucker, 404 U.S. 443 (1972): preserve the exception for unconstitutional complete counsel deprivation; they do not require the broader sentencing forum sought here.
- Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992: preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful collateral proof rules without deciding the availability of this forum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 921(a)(20)'s business-regulation exclusions and state-misdemeanor exclusion for offenses punishable by two years or less remain distinct from collateral review. A conviction expunged, set aside, pardoned or followed by civil-rights restoration is excluded under the convicting jurisdiction's law unless the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No removal event is established here. Expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit is affirmed, 6–3, leaving the 235-month sentence intact against these attacks. Original-conviction review and a possible authorized application after successful invalidation remain available under their own rules. No stay, automatic reopening or fixed sentence reduction is ordered.

### Uncounseled misdemeanors at sentencing

#### [Nichols v. United States, 511 U.S. 738](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Nichols_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-06.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8556.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### This Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor is permissible

**Authority of the judgment:** The Sixth Circuit's sentence is affirmed, 5–4, by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. No complete controlling rationale exists under Marks. The express shared permission below does not make Souter a fifth vote for general overruling of Baldasar or for the plurality's warning rule.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** An uncounseled misdemeanor conviction valid under Scott because no imprisonment was imposed may supply the otherwise authorized criminal-history point in the Guidelines setting presented here: the later prosecution is counseled and the sentencing framework provides a genuine opportunity to contest reliability, relevance and significant overrepresentation through its lawful procedures, including a permitted §4A1.3 departure. This limited permission neither makes a departure mandatory nor adopts a general rule for a scheme that conclusively requires enhanced incarceration without that opportunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, permits this application under a broader valid-conviction rule; Souter's concurrence in the judgment expressly permits this Guidelines application on the narrower reliability-and-opportunity ground. Five Justices support the stated permission at this level; no complete rationale has five joins, and Souter supplies no fifth vote to overrule Baldasar generally.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under the applicable November 1990 Guidelines, §4A1.1(c) supplies one point per otherwise includable prior sentence outside (a) and (b), capped at four points; (a) gives three for imprisonment over one year and one month, and (b) two for imprisonment of at least sixty days not within (a). A prior sentence follows adjudication of guilt for conduct outside the instant offense. Section 4A1.2(e) counts over-one-year-and-one-month imprisonment imposed within fifteen years of offense commencement or producing incarceration during that period; other sentences count if imposed within ten years; older sentences outside those rules are excluded. Commencement includes relevant conduct; separate juvenile periods are not decided. Note 5 expressly counts DUI rather than minor traffic offenses; no other offense-classification or timing objection is presented. Note 6 excludes already reversed, vacated or constitutionally invalidated convictions without forbidding lawful consideration of underlying conduct. Section 4A1.3 permits departure on reliable information that the category significantly overrepresents seriousness or future-crime likelihood, using a lower category as reference, but not below Category I's lower limit on that ground. A prior arrest record itself cannot support the departure inquiry. Nichols made no such departure request. Point arithmetic is not optional, departure is not mandatory, and no independent proof of the DUI conduct or hypothetical departure is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott v. Illinois, 440 U.S. 367 (1979): preserves the actual-imprisonment boundary for the original appointed-counsel requirement; the fine-only conviction is treated as valid.
- Baldasar v. Illinois, 446 U.S. 222 (1980): its particular judgment and fractured reasoning remain; only four Justices vote for general overruling, and the Court permits the present Guidelines application without deciding a conclusive mandatory-enhancement scheme.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 (1977): does not convert Blackmun's original-invalidity rationale in Baldasar into five votes for the valid-but-unavailable rationale, or make four present overruling votes five.
- Stinson v. United States, May 3, 1993: preserves commentary's authority subject to constitutional, statutory and Guideline limits; commentary does not itself answer the constitutional objection.
- Custis v. United States, May 23, 1994: distinguishes the ACCA collateral-attack channel from this challenge to a Scott-valid conviction's subsequent use; the counsel-deprivation exception is unchanged.
- Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992, and Godinez v. Moran, June 24, 1993: preserve their distinct proof/regularity and actual-waiver rules; neither establishes waiver or nonindigency here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete rationale commands five votes under Marks. Souter's express permission for the defined Guidelines application is within the plurality's broader permission, but the four do not adopt his conditions as universally necessary. Baldasar's judgment and fractured authority remain; four votes do not overrule it or establish the plurality's broad future-enhancement-warning rule. This is not Custis's challenge to constitutional validity, and no waiver or nonindigency finding is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed, 5–4. The 235-month sentence and eight-year supervised-release term remain undisturbed; no DUI-point deduction, vacation of the Georgia fine, new hearing, release or fixed 25-month reduction is ordered. The waiver and indigency alternatives are not decided.

### Capital selection factors

#### [Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, 512 U.S. 967](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Tuilaepa_v_California_and_Proctor_v_California_merits_1994-06-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-5131 (Tuilaepa) and 93-5161 (Proctor).

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### Relevant individualized selection does not require fixed labels or numerical weights

**Holding:** On the unchallenged eligibility premise presented here, the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit consideration of understandable, relevant subjects for individualized penalty selection without requiring every consideration to be a yes-or-no factual element, exclusively aggravating or mitigating, or assigned a fixed numerical weight. This rule does not authorize forbidden or wholly irrelevant adverse considerations, exclusion of effective mitigation, or disregard of a separately established selection error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly adopt this bounded rule; Stevens and Ginsburg do not join Part I's additional framework discussion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful eligibility narrowing and individualized selection perform distinct functions under Gregg, Woodson and Lowenfield. Arave's authoritative limitation on an eligibility aggravator remains necessary in its setting. Eligibility does not cure a later unconstitutional weighing factor under Stringer and Espinosa. Romano's separate-death-decision responsibility rule and Simmons's future-danger/parole-ineligibility rebuttal rule retain their distinct operation. This decision neither extends nor retreats from those rules and does not approve the entire California capital scheme.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant individualized selection after unchallenged narrowing; it supplies no exemption for forbidden adverse considerations.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved; relevant mitigation must have effective consideration, without guaranteed favorable weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to contextual review and individualized moral judgment, not mechanical factor counting.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: distinguished in application; their effective independent-mitigation requirement remains fully operative.

##### The circumstances-of-the-crime factor survives both presented challenges

**Holding:** Section 190.3(a), directing consideration when relevant of the circumstances of the crime of conviction and the existence of special circumstances found true under §190.1, identifies an understandable and relevant selection subject and is not unconstitutional on the facial and instructional theories presented by either petitioner. The Court affirms each judgment on that ground without adjudicating §190.2 eligibility or any independently preserved claim of actual forbidden use.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight find no error on each docket's factor-(a) challenge; none of these joins is a harmlessness vote.

**Material application:** Both factor-(a) challenges fail without harmless-error review. The judgment does not decide unrelated guilt-phase comments, every other factor, racial discrimination, the lawfulness of treating absence of a minor-accomplice role as aggravation, or the whole California eligibility scheme. No actual juror mental state is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), and Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988): distinguished; their vague pejorative aggravators differ from identification of the actual crime's circumstances.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; relevant individualized consideration does not require an exclusive aggravating or mitigating label for every circumstance.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to each complete reported charge; no ordinary word or boilerplate direction automatically cures an actual contradiction.

##### The violent-criminal-activity factor is sufficiently definite in Tuilaepa

**Holding:** Section 190.3(b), when relevant, permits consideration of the presence or absence of criminal activity involving use or attempted use of force or violence, or an express or implied threat to use force or violence. As construed to concern punishable offenses involving force or threatened force against a person, this is an understandable selection subject and defeats Tuilaepa's presented vagueness challenge; Proctor presents no factor-(b) question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly sustain factor (b) on no-error grounds; Blackmun dissents on this factor as well as the others.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The trial instruction's proof burden is a record fact, not a new statutory or universal constitutional rule. This holding supplies no finding about an unreported act, no general permission to use unreliable or forbidden evidence, and no factor-(b) ruling in Proctor.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant offender conduct without requiring a uniform assigned weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied; individualized sentencing is not mechanical factual counting.

##### Age is a relevant contextual subject, not an automatic aggravator

**Holding:** Section 190.3(i)'s direction to consider, when relevant, the defendant's age at the time of the crime is sufficiently understandable on Tuilaepa's presented challenge and requires no universal numerical weight or exclusive aggravating or mitigating label. The absence of youth does not automatically aggravate the crime, and the possibility of improper argument does not establish a facial defect or a proved as-applied violation; Proctor presents no age-factor question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight adopt the rule and no-error application; Part VI gives the corresponding affirmances without a new penalty hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no constitutional error on any presented factor challenge and orders no new penalty hearing. It makes no harmlessness finding and adds no alternative cure holding. If an independent properly presented claim establishes a weighing error, Stringer and Espinosa still require genuine state-authorized reweighing or constitutional harmless-error review with the State carrying Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden, and a new sentencing proceeding if no lawful cure exists. Nothing here resolves Romano's distinct further-remedy disagreement or alters Simmons's case-specific mandate. Guilt remains outside these sentencing questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; contextual relevance does not require an exclusive label or numerical weight.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved as protection for meaningful mitigation.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their independent-culpability-mitigation rules remain binding; the different California mechanism supports a different no-error application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both California judgments are affirmed, 8–1, on no-error grounds for the presented factor challenges: (a) in both cases and (b) and (i) only in Tuilaepa. No new penalty hearing or harmlessness determination follows. Guilt, the unchallenged eligibility premise and independently available properly presented claims of actual error are not adjudicated; no execution or date is ordered.

### Capital sentencing responsibility and rebuttal

#### [Romano v. Oklahoma, 512 U.S. 1](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Romano_v_Oklahoma_merits_1994-06-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9093.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### Another jury's death decision cannot displace the present jury's responsibility

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments forbid the State to present an irrelevant death sentence imposed by another jury in circumstances that substantially invite the present capital jury to treat its own choice of death as already made elsewhere. This contextual rule extends Caldwell's protection beyond an inaccurate description of legal responsibility; it requires more than a speculative possibility of influence, preserves independently admissible prior-conduct evidence, and leaves open whether a focused instruction can prevent the distortion in a different record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Responsibility portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices adopt this rule and its application to the Sarfaty penalty proceeding.

**Material application:** The Thompson judgment disclosed another jury's death selection and execution command despite an offered stipulation to conviction. Its truth, the appeal notice and correct general instructions did not specifically withdraw the suggestion that the decisive choice had already been made. The Court finds the contextual constitutional distortion, not actual abdication by an identified juror.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its responsibility protection is extended to an official disclosure of another jury's death selection that substantially invites displacement of the current choice; the Court does not pretend the earlier facts already decided this question.
- Darden v. Wainwright, 477 U.S. 168 (1986): its guilt-stage setting and remarks increasing responsibility are distinguished; its separate fundamental-unfairness standard remains intact.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401 (1989): its procedural-default holding is preserved; its description of the ordinary misstatement claim does not preclude the extension adopted here.
- Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227 (1990): its collateral-review new-rule holding is preserved; no nonretroactivity bar enters this direct review.

##### Earlier aggravator reweighing does not resolve the responsibility error

**Holding:** The state appellate court's deletion of the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighing of the remaining three do not themselves resolve the distinct constitutional error in presenting another jury's death selection. Its judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under the responsibility holding; the Court establishes no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and a first constitutional harmless-error inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Common disposition and prior-reweighing portion of Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five adopt this limited proposition and appellate vacatur/remand; their different further-remedy positions are not a single holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority determines whether the existing sentence may be retained following a new constitutional harmlessness determination or whether a new penalty hearing is required whenever the State continues to seek death. No independently sufficient ordinary due-process holding, general constitutional evidence code, or Court-wide ruling on the validity of capital punishment is adopted. No actual juror mental state, inevitable execution, harmlessness finding, automatic life sentence, acquittal or release is found or ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its independent-responsibility protection identifies an error distinct from an invalid aggravator; earlier reweighing does not automatically answer it.
- Johnson v. Mississippi, 486 U.S. 578 (1988): the invalid-prior-conviction problem remains distinct from the responsibility error; removing the prior-violent-felony aggravator does not decide the latter.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Oklahoma appellate judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded under the responsibility rule. The Court has no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and an initial constitutional harmless-error inquiry. It neither separately declares the trial sentence automatically void nor declares it enforceable; guilt and the independent Thompson proceedings remain untouched. No replacement sentence, release or execution order issues.

#### [Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Simmons_v_South_Carolina_merits_1994-06-17.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9059.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

##### Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue in a capital sentencing proceeding and the only legally available noncapital sentence is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity for the defendant to inform the jury of that ineligibility through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. A direction to disregard parole and give life imprisonment its ordinary meaning does not satisfy that requirement on this record; the rule neither requires a particular script nor promises immunity from clemency or future legislation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The operative alternative was life without ordinary parole under §24-21-640's second-or-subsequent violent-conviction rule following separate sentencing for the prior conviction; offenses within one day or one continuous course count as one. The eligibility premise is uncontested here, not a rule that every life sentence excludes parole. The State may still prove danger to inmates or guards and offer truthful information about commutation or pardon.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977): its opportunity-to-answer principle supplies the due-process foundation for responding to material sentencing information.
- Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986): its future-danger rebuttal principle is applied to a fixed legal sentencing alternative; its independent mitigation rule remains intact.
- California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983): distinguished; permission to give truthful commutation information does not authorize withholding this responsive fact about ordinary parole.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their effective-mitigation rules remain controlling within their scope; neither is treated as having already decided this separate legal-information question.

##### The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued

**Holding:** Under Chapman's direct-review standard, the State cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt that barring the parole-ineligibility explanation did not contribute to this jury's selection of death, given the State's future-danger argument, the complete exclusion of the legal answer and the jury's express parole inquiry. The death-sustaining judgment is reversed and the case remanded for a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; a lawful noncapital disposition remains available, and the convictions and separate sentences are undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no independently sufficient Eighth Amendment instruction rule. It does not determine an entitlement to parole information where the State has not raised future danger, settle a disputed parole-eligibility question, require a fixed form of words, guarantee against clemency or future legislation, or announce automatic reversal for every omission. The State may pursue a lawful noncapital disposition instead of another capital hearing. No guilt retrial, acquittal, release, invalidation of the separate sentences or general prohibition of capital punishment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied; the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, and fails on this record.
- Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994: distinguished as an affirmative disclosure of another jury's death decision; this decision neither supplies a missing majority for its further-remedy positions nor changes that case's mandate.
- Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: preserves the distinction between correcting a capital sentencing defect and disturbing guilt; no automatic noncapital sentence follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sustaining judgment is reversed, 7–2, and the case remanded. Five Justices require a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; the State may instead pursue a lawful noncapital disposition. The existing death sentence cannot be retained by a further harmlessness finding on this omission. Guilt and the separate life and thirty-day sentences remain undisturbed.

### Contempt classification and criminal safeguards

#### [International Union, United Mine Workers of America v. Bagwell, 512 U.S. 821](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/International_Union_United_Mine_Workers_of_America_v_Bagwell_merits_1994-06-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1625.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### The surviving fixed public fines are criminal punishment

**Holding:** Substantial fixed fines imposed for completed out-of-court violations, unmeasured by proved loss and offering no postimposition compliance-based means to avoid the assessed liability, are criminal punishment in the circumstances presented here. Advance announcement of the schedule does not make these fines civil; genuinely compensatory relief and conditional coercion remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt the classification and application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compensation must correspond to proved loss. Genuine coercion may use prospective per-day sanctions avoidable through present compliance, confinement terminable by performance, or a fixed fine suspended on a real compliance condition. The contemnor must have the relevant ability to comply. The Court does not require cancellation of every already accrued per-day burden upon eventual compliance. Wording a command affirmatively or negatively does not itself settle classification; this decree contained both kinds of obligations. No clause-by-clause severance is undertaken. The Court does not displace the distinct classification of legislative civil schemes or resolve every discovery or litigation-management sanction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range Co., 221 U.S. 418 (1911): applied to distinguish remedial performance from punishment for completed violations.
- United States v. Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258 (1947): preserved; a suspended fine with a genuine later compliance condition differs from these fixed liabilities.
- Shillitani v. United States, 384 U.S. 364 (1966): preserved; conditional coercion depends on a continuing lawful opportunity to perform the required act.
- Hicks v. Feiock, 485 U.S. 624 (1988): applied; federal constitutional classification follows the proceeding and relief's substance, not its civil label.

##### Necessity for enforcement does not excuse criminal procedure on this record

**Holding:** The need to enforce this continuing decree does not justify imposing serious punitive sanctions for dispersed out-of-court conduct without the criminal safeguards those sanctions require. The scale of the liabilities, complex obligations and extensive retrospective factfinding reinforce the criminal classification; neither complexity nor amount alone supplies a universal civil/criminal test.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Eight Justices adopt this additional bounded procedural explanation. Ginsburg joins the common classification and remedy but not this account.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Existing direct-contempt powers remain subject to their own limits; directness alone does not remove the jury right when the punishment is serious. This case involves indirect contempt. No actual judicial bias is found, no mechanical complexity rule is adopted, and no independent ground validates or invalidates the underlying labor injunction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 194 (1968): applied; the enforcement interest does not dispense with the jury for serious criminal contempt.
- Shillitani v. United States, 384 U.S. 364 (1966): distinguished as continuing coercion directed to an identifiable act the contemnor could perform.

##### Serious criminal contempt requires a jury and these civil assessments cannot stand

**Holding:** These approximately $52 million public fines constitute serious criminal contempt and require applicable criminal safeguards, including a jury. Proof beyond a reasonable doubt before a judge does not replace that protection; the judgment sustaining collection through these nonjury civil adjudications is reversed and the case remanded, without directing a new prosecution or reviving any vacated fines.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the jury rule and bounded reversal/remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No jury waiver or harmlessness finding is made. The vacated initial $642,000 fines, including the former $424,000 suspension, and the vacated approximately $12 million Company assessments remain outside the surviving judgment. No substitute compensation, new fine amount, conviction, prosecution, specific mandate date, substantive-excessiveness ruling or wholesale invalidation of the dissolved injunction is ordered. Petty criminal contempt may lack a jury requirement but remains criminal and retains its applicable safeguards.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 194 (1968): applied; serious criminal contempt requires a jury, while the petty-offense qualification remains distinct from the civil/criminal classification.
- United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993: its rule for completed nonsummary criminal contempt and its retention of Grady remain intact; no future prosecution or defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the surviving approximately $52 million public fines is reversed and remanded unanimously. Collection cannot rest on these civil nonjury adjudications. Any further criminal proceeding needs its own lawful basis and all applicable defenses; no prosecution is directed or future double-jeopardy question decided. Vacated initial and private fines are not revived.

### Double jeopardy and punitive taxation

#### [Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, 511 U.S. 767](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Department_of_Revenue_of_Montana_v_Kurth_Ranch_merits_1994-06-06.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-144.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### The surviving drug assessment is a second punishment

**Holding:** The Double Jeopardy Clause forbids Montana from collecting this surviving drug assessment in a separate proceeding after criminal punishment for the same offense, on the preserved same-offense premise of this litigation. The combined operation of criminally conditioned drug liability, arrest-linked reporting, collection subordinate to fines and forfeitures, an extraordinary charge and taxation of confiscated contraband makes this exaction punishment; no single feature, fixed rate ratio or general tax-cost ceiling establishes the rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the combined-operation rule and affirm disallowance as applied.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision concerns the surviving harvested-marijuana claim, approximately $181,000 in principal. Independent state-law rejection of plants, oil and tar remains undisturbed. Only Richard pleaded to possession; the other five pleaded to conspiracy. The Court declines Montana's unpreserved contrary contention rather than deciding their plea terms or equating the offenses. Calling that contention standing does not establish a jurisdictional defect in this bankruptcy controversy. No general rule resolves the reverse sequence, separate sovereigns, every civil forfeiture, a confidential tax collectible independently of arrest, or all taxation of unlawful conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Benton v. Maryland, 395 U.S. 784: applies the Double Jeopardy Clause to the State through the Fourteenth Amendment.
- North Carolina v. Pearce, 395 U.S. 711: applies the protection against repeated punishment without deciding sanctions within one proceeding.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435: extends substance-over-label examination to this unusual tax; its civil-penalty accounting method is not a universal ceiling for taxation.
- A. Magnano Co. v. Hamilton, 292 U.S. 40: applies its recognition that penal operation can deprive a nominal tax of its ordinary revenue character.
- United States v. Sanchez, 340 U.S. 42: distinguishes this combined criminal-process-dependent levy; ordinary high or deterrent taxes remain valid within that decision.
- Helvering v. Mitchell, 303 U.S. 391: preserves genuine remedial tax additions and distinguishes this subsequent punitive exaction.
- United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242: civil labeling does not answer whether the particular second exaction constitutes punishment under the retained Halper rule; the general civil/criminal framework is not overruled.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: preserves the distinction between conspiracy and its substantive objects; no offense identity is inferred from evidentiary overlap.
- United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993: retains its successive-prosecution holdings and Grady; those rules do not themselves decide the unpreserved plea-specific issue.
- Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993: preserves its Excessive Fines coverage rules without importing a partly-punitive fines test as this Double Jeopardy rule.
- Alexander v. United States, June 28, 1993: preserves its separate Excessive Fines and expressive-burden inquiries; neither supplies the governing test here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The disallowance of the surviving harvested-marijuana assessment is affirmed, including the lower judgment's remittance consequence for sums already recovered. No new exact principal, penalty, interest or refund total is fixed. Independent state-law rejection of the other drug components and the criminal judgments remain undisturbed.

### Insanity-verdict consequences and jury instructions

#### [Shannon v. United States, 512 U.S. 573](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Shannon_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8346.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The Act does not require a commitment-consequences instruction

**Holding:** When insanity is properly raised in a federal criminal trial, 18 U.S.C. §4242(b) requires the prescribed verdict choices, but §§4242–4243 do not require an instruction explaining the commitment and release consequences of a verdict of not guilty only by reason of insanity. The mandatory initial commitment and subsequent judicial procedures do not guarantee any period of hospitalization and do not transfer disposition decisions to the guilt jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six of nine participating Justices directly adopt this statutory construction.

**Material application:** Section 4242(b)'s verdict direction operates upon the prescribed insanity notice and a motion by the defendant or Government or on the court's own motion. The Court does not classify Shannon's offense under §4243(d)'s different release burdens or decide the constitutionality of the commitment provisions. Mandatory initial commitment supplies no guaranteed hospitalization period or assurance that complete recovery is the only release route.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rogers v. United States, 422 U.S. 35 (1975): preserves the ordinary separation of guilt determination from consequences; it does not impose a statutory insanity-consequences charge.

##### Federal trial practice does not require the requested explanation here

**Holding:** A nonsentencing federal jury ordinarily must decide guilt and the insanity defense without regard to verdict consequences; proper submission of insanity and a defendant's request do not alone require a commitment explanation as federal supervisory practice. An appropriate accurate corrective instruction may be required to remedy a particular trial misstatement or error, such as a prosecutor or witness telling the jury that an insanity acquittee will go free, but no such event is reported here, so refusal of Shannon's requests was not error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the ordinary rule, corrective qualification and no-error application; none relies on an alternative harmless-error finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rogers, 422 U.S. 35: applies the guilt/disposition separation while preserving correction of a communication capable of distorting the jury's proper task.
- United States v. Olano, April 26, 1993: applies the ordinary instruction-following premise at its qualified scope; its forfeited-error rule does not decide a preserved-error remedy here.
- Simmons v. South Carolina, June 17, 1994: distinguishes the due-process right to answer a State-raised future-danger argument at capital sentencing; that holding and its separate grounds remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is affirmed, 6–3, on no-error grounds. The Court does not adopt a categorical denial of corrective-instruction authority or make a harmless-error finding. No new trial, insanity verdict, commitment, release or remand is ordered.

### Statements against penal interest

#### [Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Williamson_v_United_States_merits_1994-06-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5256.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Each material assertion must be against the declarant's interest in context

**Holding:** When an unavailable declarant's statement is offered under Rule 804(b)(3) as a statement against penal interest, each offered material assertion must, in the circumstances when made, so tend to expose that declarant to criminal liability that a reasonable person in that position would not have made it unless believing it true; a generally self-inculpatory account does not carry an independently neutral, noninculpatory or self-serving assertion into the exception. This is contextual assessment, not grammatical atomization: an apparently neutral detail or identification of another participant can qualify if its actual effect sufficiently incriminates the declarant, while neither joint inculpation nor custody alone establishes admissibility or exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. These seven participating Justices adopt the same rule, contextual qualification and explanation. Justices Kennedy and Thomas favor a different construction; their judgment votes add no support to this rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Salerno, decided June 19, 1992: applies its requirement that the selected hearsay exception's actual conditions be satisfied; its former-testimony and similar-motive holdings remain unchanged and do not themselves resolve Rule 804(b)(3)'s unit.
- Lee v. Illinois, 476 U.S. 530 (1986): draws on its warning about selective unreliability in an accomplice's accusation; does not turn its confrontation analysis into automatic evidentiary exclusion or decide the constitutional claim here.

##### Vacatur preserves the distinction between admissibility error and relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with the assertion-specific Rule construction, without a controlling determination that all of Harris's account qualifies or all of it fails; any properly available harmless-error argument must be considered under the standard applicable to the error actually found. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos place the applicable harmlessness burden on the Government and require the reviewing court, considering the whole record, to have fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, with grave doubt preventing a harmlessness finding; Chapman instead requires harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt only if constitutional trial error is actually reached and found, a new trial follows if error requiring relief is established, and neither acquittal nor automatic retrial follows from rejecting whole-account admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven Justices adopt these shared judgment-consequence limits while retaining the application disagreements described below. All nine Justices vote for vacatur and remand, but Kennedy and Thomas concur in that judgment under their different Rule construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record, substantial-influence inquiry to any established preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error; no present harmlessness result is reached.
- United States v. Olano, decided April 26, 1993: distinguishes its ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error; neither forfeiture nor intentional waiver is inferred here.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves the Government's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness burden for a constitutional trial error if one is reached and found; does not apply that standard merely because the Rule was violated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling application rationale under Marks classifies the whole Harris account; agreement on vacatur does not settle admissibility of particular assertions. The Confrontation Clause claim, firm-rootedness and a nationwide prosecution-side corroboration requirement remain undecided. Rule 804(b)(3)'s express corroboration sentence concerns penal-interest statements offered to exculpate the accused. The separate Harrell/Taggart circuit requirement remains applicable on its own terms, without becoming national law. Neither corroboration nor unavailability substitutes for genuine penal adversity; the abandoned coconspirator theory is not revived and no other exception is held satisfied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded unanimously, subject to the seven-Justice Rule construction and error-consequence limits. No common classification of every assertion or present harmlessness finding is made. Properly available harmlessness arguments, independently applicable corroboration and the separately preserved constitutional question retain their own requirements. The abandoned coconspirator theory is not revived; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, with no acquittal or unconditional retrial ordered.

### Advisory jury recommendations in capital sentencing

#### [Harris v. Alabama](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Harris_v_Alabama_merits_1995-02-22.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7659.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

##### The Constitution does not require numerical weight for advisory jury sentencing advice

**Holding:** The Eighth Amendment does not require a State to prescribe a numerical or mathematical weight for a jury's advisory sentencing recommendation. That proposition does not resolve whether judicial selection remains adequately guided and reviewable, whether relevant mitigation was considered, or whether a distinct substantive constraint on overriding life advice is constitutionally required.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, June 30, 1994, are applied for relevant individualized selection without fixed numerical weights, preserving their limits on forbidden considerations. Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, retain effective consideration of relevant mitigation; neither is converted into a numerical scheme.

##### Alabama’s consideration rule and this reviewed sentencing record satisfy the admitted challenge

**Holding:** Where Alabama requires consideration of an unwaived advisory recommendation but assigns final sentencing to the judge, the Constitution does not additionally require Tedder's particular substantive override standard. On Harris's actual individualized findings and appellate review, the absence of a prescribed weight or separate comparative formula for rejecting the life recommendation establishes no constitutional error; this ruling does not excuse disregard of the recommendation, mitigation or a proved independent sentencing defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447, is applied to judicial final sentencing; Tedder v. State, 322 So. 2d 908, is not made a federal constitutional command. Espinosa v. Florida, June 29, 1992, retains its rule concerning invalid-factor advice carrying required great weight, without deciding every Alabama advice error. Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994, retains its contextual protection against an irrelevant separate death decision distorting responsibility. Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, continues to require a lawful cure for an invalid aggravator; no such error is found on this question. Tuilaepa/Proctor preserves independent selection-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Supreme Court is affirmed, 7–2, on the admitted sentencing challenge. No penalty rehearing is ordered, and the guilt judgment is undisturbed. Unanimity on the absence of a numerical-weight requirement does not establish unanimous approval of the sentence.

### Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment

#### [United States v. Harris](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Harris_merits_1995-04-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Constitutional Structure — Commerce power and armed vehicle taking (The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection).

##### Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content

**Holding:** Original §2119 requires knowing armed participation in taking or attempting to take the covered vehicle from another's person or presence by force and violence or intimidation; aiding-and-abetting liability requires intentional assistance in that taking, not mere presence, association, or awareness of another's plan. Read with that ordinary criminal fault, the statute gives fair notice of the charged conduct and defeats the presented lack-of-fault and vagueness challenges without adding a profit motive, interstate-resale intention, knowledge of federal law, or the later intent-to-kill-or-seriously-injure element.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor and all seven other Associate Justices adopt the complete knowing-participation and intentional-assistance rule. Stone-Zsela additionally joins knowing participation, fair notice and rejection of a general legal-knowledge requirement, but not the separately stated intentional-assistance formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994: applies contextual factual culpability despite silence while retaining its distinct regulated-characteristic holding.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: applies contextual criminal-fault construction without transplanting its depiction elements or deciding every jurisdictional-knowledge question.
- United States v. Shabani, November 1, 1994: preserves the distinction between knowing participation and mere association; its conspiracy holding does not itself define this separate offense.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its specific willfulness and known-illegality language rather than converting it into a universal ignorance-of-law defense.
- Lopez: preserves its refusal to insert an absent commerce element; that limit does not eliminate ordinary criminal-fault construction of the enacted taking offense.

##### Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking

**Holding:** The offense-date §924(c) authorizes its additional, consecutive punishment for the original §2119 carjacking predicate even though firearm possession is integral to that predicate's definition. The Government must still establish firearm use or carrying during and in relation to a qualifying federally prosecutable predicate; this cumulative-authorization holding does not equate possession with every §924(c) condition or determine universal element overlap.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on cumulative statutory authorization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ratzlaf: preserves residual lenity after ordinary construction; the inclusive, additional, and consecutive directions leave no residual ambiguity for this pairing.
- Smith v. United States, June 1, 1993: preserves the separate firearm-use and relation requirements; a predicate's possession element does not automatically establish all §924(c) elements.
- Deal v. United States, May 17, 1993: distinguishes its conviction-sequence question, which does not itself decide cumulative punishment under these two statutes.

##### Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution

**Holding:** Applying Missouri v. Hunter, the clearly authorized §2119 and §924(c) convictions and consecutive punishments imposed in this single prosecution do not violate the Double Jeopardy Clause even assuming overlap under Blockburger. This holding applies the governing same-trial precedent to the clear statutory direction and neither decides universal element overlap nor changes the rules for successive prosecutions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on the bounded application of Hunter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Missouri v. Hunter, 459 U.S. 359 (1983): applies its governing same-trial rule to the clear cumulative authorization without reconsidering its constitutional foundation.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299 (1932): applies its construction role rather than treating possible overlap as an absolute bar to this expressly authorized same-trial punishment.
- Whalen v. United States, 445 U.S. 684 (1980): preserves the presumption against cumulative punishment absent contrary legislative intent and distinguishes the clear direction here.
- United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993: preserves its successive-prosecution holdings, including the continued operation of Grady.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 (1990): remains governing law for its essential-conduct successive-prosecution setting; this single prosecution does not invoke or displace that rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no independent instruction, indictment, preservation, harmlessness, sufficiency, or intradistrict-venue question. It determines neither knowledge of the vehicle's interstate history nor an independent permanent-deprivation requirement by a majority. No later carjacking intent amendment, later firearm tier, new short-barrel classification, serious-injury or death enhancement, or different charging pair is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and each defendant's forty-one-month Count One term plus consecutive sixty-month Count Two term are affirmed on all three presented challenges. No merger, vacatur, concurrent conversion, sentence increase, resentencing, new trial or exploratory remand is ordered.

### Collective disclosure materiality and prosecution responsibility

#### [Kyles v. Whitley](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Kyles_v_Whitley_merits_1995-04-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7927.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction

**Holding:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently satisfy that standard, and the defendant need not show acquittal more likely than conviction or that the remaining evidence is legally insufficient; the established suppression here meets the standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83: applies the duty to disclose material favorable evidence to the established suppression.
- United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: applies reasonable-probability materiality to the combined defense counterfactual, including lawful impeachment and preparation uses.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: distinguishes its gateway showing; it supplies no additional innocence prerequisite for this Brady claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collective inquiry includes legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. It does not excuse lack of diligence about evidence already known to the defense, require open-file discovery or make every nondisclosure structural error.

##### The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team

**Holding:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady and Bagley: apply their trial-disclosure protection to information held by police acting for this prosecution.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: distinguishes its confined grand-jury disclosure and dismissal rules; those limits remain effective within their own setting.

##### A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing

**Holding:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bagley: treats materiality as a defining element of the suppression violation, not an initial screen before a second duplicative prejudice test.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: preserve their actual category-specific holdings and distinguish them from claim-defining Brady materiality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and remanded for a conditional habeas writ requiring release unless Louisiana retries Kyles within a reasonable time fixed by the District Court. No acquittal, immediate unconditional release, fixed number of days, penalty-only relief or adjudication of other claims is ordered.

### Constitutional trial error on habeas

#### [O'Neal v. McAninch](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/ONeal_v_McAninch_merits_1995-02-21.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7407.
**Decided:** February 21, 1995.

##### Chapman governs the preserved personal-intent instructional claim

**Holding:** For a preserved constitutional trial violation, properly available on federal habeas, in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor's statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant's own intent required for the offense, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict. Kotteakos's substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt framework does not govern this claim; the standard for other reserved categories is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. The broader Part III extension has no controlling coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: extended from preserved Doyle error to the preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category; its original rule survives, while other reserved categories remain unresolved by the Court.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied with the State's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden and actual-verdict contribution inquiry intact.
- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): not extended to replace Chapman for this constitutional claim; its existing operation for genuinely nonconstitutional trial error remains unchanged.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 (1970): applied as the constitutional requirement that the prosecution prove the elements of the charged offense; no new personal-intent element is imposed on offenses that do not require it.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993: preserved as the distinct rule for a defect vitiating the jury's governing reasonable-doubt standard; the assumed error here is not declared structural.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt a general Chapman rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors. Proof of the violation, preservation, exhaustion, independent habeas gates, structural-error rules and a prejudice element defining another constitutional claim remain separate. Actual harmlessness is not decided.

##### The proper standard requires renewed review, not an automatic writ

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's substantial-influence assessment with a prisoner prejudice burden does not supply the required Chapman determination; its challenged judgment must be vacated for review of the combined claim under the correct rule. If an available constitutional violation is established and the State fails to prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the existing record that it did not contribute to the verdict, the writ shall issue for the affected conviction or convictions unless the State retries O'Neal within a reasonable time; no immediate writ or present retrial deadline is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. The broader Part III extension has no controlling coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: its whole-record remand and conditional-relief distinction are extended to this instructional claim, without automatic reinstatement of a prior writ.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied to the existing trial record; a weaker substantial-influence finding is not treated as the required finding beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991, and Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California, March 22, 1994: their whole-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry remains the rule for identifying an ambiguous instruction's constitutional defect; the Court does not decide that antecedent application here.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: its gateway for otherwise barred claims remains independent; neither passage through that gateway nor an innocence showing is invented for this claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is vacated and remanded, 7–2, with Chapman directions supported by five Justices. Review concerns the combined personal-intent instructional/argument claim, with District Court proceedings as appropriate. A lawful no-error ruling or a proper assumed-error/Chapman-harmlessness ruling permits denial of the writ. Otherwise the conditional writ issues for the affected convictions unless the State retries within a reasonable time; no present deadline, immediate release or reinstatement of the earlier conditional order is directed.

### Court-employee warrant-record errors

#### [Arizona v. Evans](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Arizona_v_Evans_merits_1995-03-01.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1660.
**Decided:** March 1, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds (Federal review of the suppression judgment).

##### A qualifying court-employee error and objectively reasonable reliance

**Holding:** The federal exclusionary rule does not require suppression for a clerical warrant-record error attributable to a non-law-enforcement court employee when the arresting officer's reliance on the apparently current warrant information is objectively reasonable. The rule does not validate the arrest, rest on subjective innocence alone, resolve police-generated or mixed errors, or excuse reliance made unreasonable by known or sufficiently evident defects in the information system.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Leon is extended to the bounded court-employee setting while retaining its objective limits; Sheppard supports reasonable reliance despite judicial clerical error. Calandra and Krull preserve the separate remedial inquiry. Whiteley's rule concerning the invalidity of originating information remains; its treatment of violation and suppression as synonymous does not determine this post-Leon remedy. Hensley found no constitutional violation and did not settle the distinct suppression question here.

##### The rule requires a limited remand, not an admission order

**Holding:** Because the trial court expressly left the source of the warrant-record error unresolved, reversal of Arizona's categorical federal exclusion rule requires remand for proper application of the court-employee/objective-reliance rule. The rejected intermediate attribution inference, the street officer's ordinary response to the reported warrant, and the clerk's frequency estimate do not establish sole court fault, a reliably maintained system or entitlement to final admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Leon's distinction between rule and application governs. Dickerson preserves the importance of supported findings under the governing search rule; it does not supply a computer-error exception or authorize an appellate finding on a question the trial judge left unresolved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Leon retains its exclusions for an affidavit knowingly or recklessly misleading the magistrate, abandonment of the neutral judicial role, an affidavit so lacking in probable-cause indicia that reliance is entirely unreasonable, and a facially deficient warrant on which an officer cannot reasonably rely. Its rule assumes proper execution within the warrant's scope and considers relevant officers who obtain or supply information as well as the executing officer. The present court-employee rule adds no immunity for police recordkeeping, mixed responsibility, deliberate disregard of defects or objectively unreasonable system reliance. The Court decides neither who caused this entry to remain nor any universal error frequency, statewide maintenance standard, guilt, civil damages, renewed state-law ground or general database-safeguards test. Full Arizona statutory text is not construed beyond the categories quoted and treated in the lower opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Six Justices reverse and remand Arizona's suppression judgment; one would affirm and two would dismiss. The source of the error remains unresolved and must be considered under the bounded court-employee/objective-reliance rule. No final admission, conviction, sole-court-fault finding or renewed independent state-law ground is ordered.

### False statements and statutory court coverage

**Holding:** Under the offense-date §§6 and 1001, a federal court is neither an enumerated executive department nor an agency within the statute's contextual meaning; the department definition's branch-wide contextual alternative does not extend this statute to the court, and the distinct agency definition does not do so either. **Operative remedy or transition:** The affirmance of Counts V–VII is reversed and remanded for lawful vacatur of those §1001 convictions. Counts I–IV and VIII–X remain undisturbed. ### Plea-discussion impeachment waivers

#### [United States v. Mezzanatto](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Mezzanatto_merits_1995-01-18.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1340.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Holding:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only; his different proof requirement is not controlling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver ruling and resulting new-trial order are reversed, 7–2, for ordinary implementation of the limited waiver. No mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction or sentencing ruling is ordered. If preserved nonconstitutional error is found, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that it did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and Olano's forfeited-error burden is not substituted.

### Prior consistent statements and evidentiary error

##### Substantive prior consistent statements require the premotive relationship

**Holding:** Under original Federal Rule of Evidence 801(d)(1)(B), a prior statement offered substantively to rebut an express or implied charge of recent fabrication or improper influence or motive must have been made before the particular charged fabrication, influence, or motive arose. - Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993): applies its distinction between incompatible common-law additions and compatible interpretive guidance; no expert-evidence factor is transferred to this Rule.

##### Alternative admissibility and ordinary error review remain for remand

**Holding:** Reversal of admission under Rule 801(d)(1)(B) leaves the Court of Appeals to decide other preserved grounds of admission and, if the statements are not admissible on such a ground, the consequences under ordinary harmless-error rules. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness, and neither immediate retrial nor acquittal follows solely from the Rule-construction error.

- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record substantial-influence standard to preserved nonconstitutional error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves its beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard only for constitutional trial error actually reached and found.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): distinguishes forfeited error from this preserved construction question; no new preservation finding is made.

Then-Rule 803(24) applies to a statement not specifically covered by any of the foregoing exceptions and requires equivalent circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness, a material fact, greater probative value on the offered point than other evidence obtainable through reasonable efforts, service of the Rules' general purposes and the interests of justice, and advance notice affording a fair opportunity to prepare, including the intended statement's particulars and the declarant's name and address. The Court finds no such condition satisfied or unsatisfied. Rehabilitation-only use, other properly limited nonhearsay purposes, and factbound questions outside the admitted temporal issue remain undecided.

### Retrospective loss of parole consideration

#### [California Department of Corrections v. Morales](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/California_Department_of_Corrections_v_Morales_merits_1995-04-25.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1462.
**Decided:** April 25, 1995.

##### The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here

**Holding:** Applying the 1981 California hearing-deferral provision to Morales’s 1980 offense violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because the actual three-year deferral withdraws offense-date mandatory annual consideration during a period when parole is legally available, without an established ordinary substitute for the meaningful intervening opportunities. The separately reasoned prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that initial prediction do not preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances, or a materially mistaken forecast; relief restores consideration, not release.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: applies its opportunity rule to this reserved individualized setting; its original holding and rejection of proof of the omitted hearing’s outcome remain in force.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies protection of a legally available avenue to reduced custody and the distinction between guaranteed benefits and discretionary alternatives; it supplies no right to parole.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged outer limits and a possibly identical result.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): preserves the punishment boundary; no free-standing rule against every retrospective procedural disadvantage is adopted.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): accepts its separate-reasons construction, permitted factual overlap and initial review; its state-court federal characterization does not displace Cavanaugh or establish ordinary later reopening.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1981 amendment retains annual review as the default. It permits a next hearing no later than three years after denial only for a prisoner convicted, in the same or different proceedings, of more than one offense involving the taking of a life, when the Board finds that parole cannot reasonably be expected at a hearing during the intervening years and states its bases. Jackson requires reasons separately directed to postponement, permits their supporting facts to overlap the present-unsuitability reasons, and establishes review of the initial choice. It establishes no generally available later reopening procedure. The rule concerns Morales's actual maximum deferral, not a proved cohort-wide practice; his eligibility arose during the postponed interval.

##### The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes

**Holding:** A prisoner challenging retrospective withdrawal of a parole-consideration opportunity must show the earlier legal entitlement, its retrospective actual withdrawal, and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and proof that the omitted hearing would actually have produced parole is unnecessary. After that showing, the State must substantiate any replacement mechanism it invokes by identifying its legal basis and establishing its practical availability and timely capacity to provide the relevant consideration; the prisoner retains the ultimate burden of establishing an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: preserves meaningful opportunity loss and the absence of an actual-release prerequisite; this decision expressly clarifies substantiation of an asserted replacement.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies its refusal to assume discretionary alternatives compensate for guaranteed benefits; it did not itself prescribe this parole evidentiary direction.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): confines review and conceivable advancement to what the opinion establishes; initial review does not become later reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns Morales’s demonstrated meaningful loss under the challenged 1981 provision. It does not invalidate every individualized postponement, require an interim hearing in every possible individualized scheme, or constitutionalize every scheduling detail. Maximum deferral is permission, not a statutory command or a proved near-uniform cohort practice. The Court neither requires wholly different facts for the two decisions nor treats the initial forecast as unreviewable.

No universal probability threshold, general presumption transferring constitutional proof to the State, numerical response deadline, mandatory filing procedure, suitability finding, release date, damages award, or right to favorable parole is adopted. Adequacy of a demonstrated materially different arrangement, later amendments and their transition or mootness effects are not decided. The false-information and sentence claims are not reopened. Unresolved credit and matrix calculations establish neither release entitlement nor legal impossibility of earlier release.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's hearing-frequency judgment is affirmed, 5–4, preserving its remand for offense-date annual consideration. Lawful suitability standards and release discretion remain; no parole, release date or damages are ordered. Rejected false-information and sentence claims remain undisturbed.

### Thermal examination of a closed home

#### [United States v. Pinson](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Pinson_merits_1995-05-08.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-164.
**Decided:** May 8, 1995.

##### The targeted examination of this closed home's heat patterns is a search

**Holding:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home constitutes a Fourth Amendment search when, as here, its reported heat-pattern information reveals otherwise unobservable information concerning concealed domestic operation. External reception and the absence of an image identifying a person or object do not defeat protection; investigative purpose or use of equipment alone is insufficient, and the Court adopts no general rule governing every sensor or exterior emission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347: applied; privacy protection does not require physical penetration of the home.
- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III: applied to the reported concealed domestic information; its protection does not depend on the information arriving as an ordinary visual image.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: applied by analogy to the additional examination of concealed information and its rejection of an unimportant-information exemption; no physical manipulation is found here.
- Florida v. Riley, 488 U.S. 445: distinguished as naked-eye observation through actual openings; its opinions supply none of this record's missing altitude or public-flight facts.
- California v. Greenwood, 486 U.S. 35: distinguished; purposeful placement of garbage for collection does not establish surrender of domestic information conveyed by heat.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: distinguished; the narrow contraband-information character of the sniff is absent from this heat examination.
- Dow Chemical Co. v. United States, 476 U.S. 227: distinguished; the outdoor industrial photographs do not authorize this examination of a closed dwelling.
- Minnesota v. Dickerson, decided June 7, 1993: consistent with the distinction between information obtained through authorized observation and additional unauthorized probing; no tactile search is presented.

##### No warrant exception justifies this scan on the established record

**Holding:** This search of protected home information required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied. The July 25 scan had no warrant, and no consent, exigency, or other recognized exception is established; the subsequent warrant for physical entry does not retroactively authorize the scan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347: applied; protected surveillance is subject to the warrant requirement and its established exceptions.
- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III: applied; the warrant presumption for concealed home information retains recognized exceptions, including true exigency, none established here.

##### Correct the preserved search ruling without reopening unpresented warrant questions

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as its rejection of the preserved suppression claim and affirmance of the conviction rest on the categorical no-search ground, and the case is remanded for lawful disposition of that claim under the corrected search rule. Pinson's failure to challenge affidavit sufficiency on appeal remains operative: the mandate directs no new excision-and-probable-cause hearing, revives no waived or forfeited argument automatically, and permits further warrant, suppression, or reliance questions only to the extent properly available under the existing record and ordinary preservation and remedial law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part IV: retained as the distinct possibility of sufficient untainted warrant information; no determination of Pinson's affidavit or entitlement to a new hearing follows.
- United States v. Olano, decided April 26, 1993: retained; intentional waiver differs from forfeiture. If Rule 52(b) is actually invoked, error, plainness, ordinarily outcome-affecting prejudice, and the separate discretionary inquiry concerning fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings must be satisfied as applicable. ### Coordination of sentences for related conduct

#### [Witte v. United States](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Witte_v_United_States_merits_1995-06-14.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6187.
**Decided:** June 14, 1995.

For the other questions in Witte v. United States, decided June 14, 1995, No. 94-6187. see Criminal Procedure — Double jeopardy and relevant-conduct sentencing (Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here).

##### Future sentence coordination remains conditional and separate

**Holding:** The possibility of an overlapping later sentence does not itself bar this prosecution, and any sentence following conviction must satisfy the then-applicable coordination provisions and independent constitutional limits. Under received § 5G1.3, subsection (a) first requires the instant sentence to run consecutively to the undischarged term if the instant offense occurred during service of that term, including work release, furlough or escape, or after sentencing for that term but before service began; only if (a) does not apply does subsection (b) require concurrency when the earlier offenses were fully taken into account in the instant offense level; in all remaining cases, policy statement (c) requires consecutiveness to the extent necessary for reasonable incremental punishment, with the commentary's approximation task, not an automatic total or repeated departure.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, conditional coordination discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this statutory and remedial proposition. Lawful departure authority may address unusual circumstances, including substantial-assistance effects, but permits rather than commands repetition of the earlier departure. Coordination cannot cure a valid successive-prosecution bar.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harris — distinguished from successive prosecution; its one-prosecution authorization rule remains unchanged.
- Felix, Dixon and Grady — preserved; statutory sentence coordination cannot cure a valid prior-prosecution bar.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit reversal of pretrial dismissal, permitting the cocaine indictment to proceed without finding guilt or fixing a sentence. The prior marijuana-attempt judgment remains intact. No 292-month later sentence, time-served finding, credit award, repeated substantial-assistance departure or 144-month aggregate cap is imposed.

### Double jeopardy and relevant-conduct sentencing

#### [Witte v. United States](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Witte_v_United_States_merits_1995-06-14.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6187.
**Decided:** June 14, 1995.

For the other questions in Witte v. United States, decided June 14, 1995, No. 94-6187. see Criminal Procedure — Coordination of sentences for related conduct (Future sentence coordination remains conditional and separate).

##### Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here

**Holding:** Consideration of relevant uncharged conduct in selecting a sentence within the authorized statutory range for the offense of conviction does not itself constitute prosecution or independent punishment for the uncharged offense. Witte's marijuana-attempt sentence therefore does not bar the distinct cocaine indictment under the retained Grady rule; this holding does not approve a separate disguised penalty, punishment beyond statutory authority or disregard of independent sentencing safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, constitutional-characterization discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this rationale. Scalia and Thomas affirm only in the judgment on the narrower distinct-prosecution ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The prior judgment imposed 144 months after substantial assistance within the five-to-forty-year statutory range; 292–365 months was the Guidelines calculation. Actual cocaine aggravation does not make that judgment an independent cocaine penalty. No acquitted-conduct rule, statutory-maximum increase rule, complete sentencing-jury test or same-transaction bar is adopted. The plea-agreement contention remains outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Williams v. Oklahoma, 358 U.S. 576 — applied to consideration of other conduct when fixing lawful offense-of-conviction punishment.
- McMillan v. Pennsylvania, 477 U.S. 79 — applied within statutory authority; independent due-process and jury limits remain.
- United States v. Felix — applied by analogy for the difference between use of conduct and prior prosecution; no same-evidence bar is created.
- United States v. Dixon and Grady v. Corbin — expressly preserved; retained essential-conduct protection has no prior-cocaine-prosecution predicate here.
- Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch — distinguished as a distinct punitive exaction under its combined-operation analysis, not overruled.
- United States v. R.L.C. — preserved: statutory sentencing authority is not replaced by the Guidelines range.
- United States v. Harris — confined to authorized cumulative punishment in one prosecution; it does not cure a valid successive-prosecution bar.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit reversal of pretrial dismissal, permitting the cocaine indictment to proceed without finding guilt or fixing a sentence. The prior marijuana-attempt judgment remains intact. No 292-month later sentence, time-served finding, credit award, repeated substantial-assistance departure or 144-month aggregate cap is imposed.

### Interception notice and obstructive purpose

#### [United States v. Aguilar](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Aguilar_merits_1995-06-21.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-270.
**Decided:** June 21, 1995.

For the other questions in United States v. Aguilar, decided June 21, 1995, No. 94-270. see Criminal Procedure — Obstruction of judicial proceedings (A known nexus to pending judicial work); First Amendment — Official confidential access and obstructive speech (The as-applied speech objection).

##### Disclosure of a known application and obstructive purpose

**Holding:** Section 2232(c) requires knowledge of an actual federal application or authorization under chapter 119, purpose to obstruct its interception, and notice or attempted notice of that possible interception to a person. Expiration of the identified authorization does not alone establish a defense; the statute does not require successful interference or that authorization remain formally in force at disclosure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s §2232(c) portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices support this construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An actual application or authorization, rather than an imaginary application, is necessary. No knowledge of subsequent orders is imputed; the warning need not reach only a target, and official notice duties do not extend interception authority. Liability decades later and limits following official inventory notice remain open. Gaudin's jury-element allocation is preserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Staples; Ratzlaf: apply offense-specific knowledge analysis without grafting statutory-illegality knowledge onto this offense.
- Russell: preserves the distinction between an obstructive endeavor and completed success, without replacing §2232(c)’s actual application and purpose predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of section 1503 Count 8 for insufficient charged judicial nexus. Reject the automatic expiration ground for section 2232(c) Count 6 and remand for preserved knowledge-instruction, harmlessness and other unresolved claims. Reinstatement remains conditional on those determinations and ordinary sentencing; no present reinstatement or commanded retrial follows. Acquitted or dismissed counts and the Government's sentencing appeal are not adjudicated.

### Jury determination of criminal elements

#### [United States v. Gaudin](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Gaudin_merits_1995-06-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt to facts establishing guilt.
- Sinclair v. United States: overruled to the extent it assigns a criminal pertinency element to the judge; unrelated propositions are not displaced.
- Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions. Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.

### Obstruction of judicial proceedings

#### [United States v. Aguilar](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/United_States_v_Aguilar_merits_1995-06-21.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-270.
**Decided:** June 21, 1995.

For the other questions in United States v. ##### A known nexus to pending judicial work

**Holding:** Section 1503 requires a relationship in time, causation or logic between the endeavor and a pending judicial or grand-jury proceeding, including knowledge that the conduct is likely to affect that proceeding. An unsuccessful endeavor can qualify, but false statements to investigators do not suffice merely because they might later testify.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s §1503 portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Breyer; six Justices adopt this statutory nexus and application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The conduct must have the natural and probable relation to interference, with knowledge that it is likely to affect the proceeding. No categorical subpoena condition, wholesale section 1512 repeal of witness obstruction or privilege to lie is adopted. Direct grand-jury lies and knowingly channeled statements remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pettibone v. United States: applies known pending-proceeding and nexus requirements.
- United States v. Russell: preserves punishment of unsuccessful endeavors but does not eliminate the judicial object.
- Ratzlaf v. United States; Staples v. United States: preserve offense-specific mens rea construction; their distinct elements do not replace those enacted here.
- Hubbard v. United States: distinguishes §1001 entity coverage from these obstruction counts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of section 1503 Count 8 for insufficient charged judicial nexus. Reject the automatic expiration ground for section 2232(c) Count 6 and remand for preserved knowledge-instruction, harmlessness and other unresolved claims. ### Student searches and athletic drug testing

#### [Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1994/records/Vernonia_School_District_47J_v_Acton_merits_1995-06-26.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-590.
**Decided:** June 26, 1995.

For the other questions in Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton, decided June 26, 1995, No. 94-590. see Federal Courts — Scope of appellate disposition (Federal correction requires reconsideration of the Oregon ground).

##### Urine testing is a search; athletic participation is no waiver

**Holding:** Collection and chemical analysis of a student's urine are Fourth Amendment searches. Enrollment or voluntary athletic participation does not waive that protection; the school and athletic setting instead informs the reasonableness inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt the search and no-waiver rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Voluntary participation informs expectations but is not consent dispensing with constitutional reasonableness. Unrelated medical investigation and ordinary police use are not authorized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied; urine collection and chemical analysis each constitute searches.
- New Jersey v. T.L.O., 469 U.S. 325: applied; school warrant and probable-cause adjustments preserve constitutional reasonableness rather than extinguishing protection.

##### This interscholastic-athlete testing program is reasonable

**Holding:** In this documented school-athletic setting, objective seasonal and random urinalysis is reasonable without individualized suspicion because the protective safety need, athletic privacy expectations, limited collection and drug information, confidentiality and restricted counseling or athletic consequences together justify the intrusion. The holding concerns this interscholastic program and record; it creates no authority to test every student, conduct unrelated medical searches or use results for ordinary law enforcement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the rule and application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual policy qualifications below bound this interscholastic-athlete holding. All-student programs, other extracurricular populations, materially more intrusive practices and other uses remain undecided. No universal exhaustion of less intrusive alternatives, confirmed drug catastrophe or quantified efficacy showing is required. Stone-Zsela's necessity/proportionality proposal and the suspicion-centered dissent are not law.

**Material application:** The challenged policy concerns interscholastic athletes, not all extracurricular participants or the separate voluntary all-student program. Testing occurs initially each season and by weekly random selection of about 10 percent of competing athletes. A student draw with adult witnesses conceals selected identities and leaves no individualized official selection discretion. Boys remain clothed with backs toward a monitor, whose presence permits hearing and sometimes observing production but not viewing genitals. Girls produce in enclosed stalls with a monitor outside who can hear. These are actual privacy intrusions. Initial refusal excludes the athlete for the current season.

The basic laboratory targets are amphetamines, marijuana and cocaine; occasional District-requested LSD testing also occurs. Alcohol and steroids are not tested. Anonymous laboratory numbers, restricted school-result access, one-year retention and no police transmission or suspension, expulsion or general discipline confine the use. Prescription verification permits a prescription copy OR doctor's authorization. Failure to verify combined with a positive result triggers positive-test procedures, not automatic exclusion merely for missing verification. The written policy permits a second test and directs no action if it is negative; testified operation supplies confirmation of positive results. The Court does not rewrite written permission into a mandatory statutory retest rule. Parents are notified and a hearing follows confirmed positives. First offense permits counseling with six weekly tests OR current-and-next-season athletic suspension. Written second-offense consequences are current-and-next-season suspension, while testimony describes a counseling/weekly-testing alternative in practice. Third offense excludes the current and next two seasons. Retesting occurs before the next eligible season.

The trial findings establish increased drug use and discipline difficulties, athletes leading drug groups, observed impaired safety performance and previously attempted educational and deterrent responses. A suspected drug-related wrestling injury is not a toxicologically established causal finding. No finding establishes that Acton uses drugs, that every athlete caused the problem, or that the program has a quantified efficacy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New Jersey v. T.L.O., 469 U.S. 325: applied for school responsibilities and contextual reasonableness; its reservation of suspicionless searches is resolved only for this program.
- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied; a substantiated protective need can justify suspicionless testing after attention to actual privacy burdens and safeguards.
- National Treasury Employees Union v. Von Raab, 489 U.S. 656: applied; special-needs balancing is contextual, not confined to a universally required catastrophic-risk category.

##### The facial prescription-verification objection does not invalidate the policy

**Holding:** The prescription-verification provision permits a prescription copy or a doctor's authorization and does not require personal delivery to teachers or coaches. On this facial policy challenge the Court may consider a confidential means of compliance and does not presume the most intrusive implementation; the rule does not approve every specimen form, nonprescription disclosure, physician-name demand or actual personal-disclosure practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this limited facial rule. Stone-Zsela does not join it. It independently answers this objection, not the entire testing challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A confidential method, including sealed laboratory delivery, is textually possible, not found to have been used. Confidentiality does not erase the information-privacy intrusion. The complete specimen form is not established and actual more intrusive implementation remains undecided; no separate surviving disclosure claim is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied at the narrow medication-information context; no general approval of unrelated medical disclosure follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for independent reconsideration of the Oregon constitutional question after correction of its federal premise. No unconditional reinstatement of final dismissal, athletic-eligibility remedy, damages, refund, drug finding, program redesign or reopening of the rejected school-authority issue follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

**Authority by component:**

- Strong prudential presumption and independent-question boundary: [Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp — The intervention question was not fairly included and no exception is warranted](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Izumi_Seimitsu_Kogyo_Kabushiki_Kaisha_v_US_Philips_Corp_DIG_1993-11-30.md#public-projection), 1993-11-30.
