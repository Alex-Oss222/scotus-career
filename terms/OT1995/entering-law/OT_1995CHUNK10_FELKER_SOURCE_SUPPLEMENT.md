## Original request and distinct claims

The petition's pp.1–2 invokes **28 U.S.C. §§2241, 2254(a), and 1651(a)**. It asks for a stay so the Court may consider and grant original habeas relief and conduct appellate review of the Eleventh Circuit action. Its explanation under Rule 20.4 is that authorization to proceed in district court was refused. It alleges state exhaustion through Georgia's May 2 action; exhaustion is an assertion to assess, not a new finding by this preparation.

1. **Reasonable-doubt claim, petition pp.10–21.** Felker attacks the final guilt instructions' use of “moral certainty,” together with the prosecutor's related voir-dire questions. He claims the combined language diluted proof beyond a reasonable doubt under *Winship* and *Cage*. He invokes the retroactivity of *Cage*, argues that the claim became available only when *Sullivan* demonstrated retroactivity, and argues that a prima facie authorization inquiry must not become final adverse merits adjudication. He alleges that his attempted first federal filing preceded *Sullivan*. The Eleventh Circuit responds that *Cage* existed before the 1993 first petition, that *Sullivan* preceded formal docketing, and that an amendment remained possible before the August 6, 1993 answer. This is a dispute about legal availability and filing/amendment history, not a finding that the trial charge is new evidence.
2. **Trial-process/evidence constitutional claim, petition pp.21–32.** He contends that permitting state crime-laboratory employee Warren Tillman, a nonphysician, to supply the asserted critical time-of-death testimony denied due process and Eighth Amendment reliability. The petition describes this as a claim warranting a newly announced constitutional rule of a fundamental kind; it is distinct from an assertion that an already-announced new Supreme Court rule authorizes a successive petition. It relies on *Ake*, *Ford*, and *Eddings* and asserts that deficient forensic work undermined the conviction. It does not make every disagreement with an expert independently sufficient for relief.
3. **Independent innocence claim, petition pp.21–31.** He expressly asserts that he is innocent of murder and that executing him would violate the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments. He presents this as substantive habeas relief and also as a reason AEDPA is unconstitutional if it precludes that relief. This claim must not disappear into the procedural innocence gateway. The petition cites historical *Herrera*; only the actual simulated *Herrera* rule may govern.
4. **Innocence as gateway.** The same medical evidence is also offered to overcome procedural barriers to the independent trial-constitutional claims. The lower opinion examines that gateway separately from a possible freestanding innocence claim. Actual simulated *Schlup*, the new statutory conditions, and a freestanding claim are distinct inquiries with different requirements; no conclusion in one automatically establishes the others.
5. **Statutory/constitutional challenges.** The original petition challenges the review bar, prima facie requirement and “previously unavailable” language, compelled state-court deference, and asserted restrictions on new or innocence claims under Article III/separation of powers, the Supremacy Clause, and the cited Amendments. These arguments do not establish that every Title I provision is necessarily applied to Felker or requires decision. The neutral inventory question and proper reach must be separately respected.
6. **Stay.** A stay of execution is expressly requested in the combined original petition and conclusion. Jurisdiction to receive original habeas, entitlement to habeas relief, review of gatekeeping, and temporary execution relief are different components. The petition's then-current scheduled execution date is not proof of a present June warrant, expiration, execution, or entered stay. No historical Supreme Court order is admitted by this extraction. Control must supply the actual current procedural premise before describing continuation or dissolution of an existing stay.

## Record propositions and countervailing evidence

The petition relies on an alibi beginning after November 25, 1981 and on expert affidavits of **Werner Spitz** and **Jonathan Arden**, reproduced at length in the petition. They opine that death occurred approximately three to five days before discovery of the body on December 8, 1981; they criticize Tillman's forensic observations and qualifications. Their opinions are allegations/evidence to evaluate, not judicial findings of innocence or falsification. Arden also challenges inferences of binding and sexual trauma; those supporting observations do not themselves add an unpresented claim or a separately established sentencing disposition.

The petition's assertion that Tillman supplied the only time-of-death evidence consistent with guilt is contradicted by the lower opinion's quotation of **Dr. James Q. Whitaker**, trial transcript 997–998, JA 19–20. Whitaker favored a time near disappearance approximately fourteen days earlier, while allowing that three to five days was possible. The lower opinion recounts his medical and pathology qualifications and examination of evidence. The lower opinion also recounts the victim's disappearance, abandoned car, clothing, call from Felker's home, and other circumstantial evidence. These are attributed published record recitations. The lower opinion's conclusion that the combined evidence defeats relief must not be substituted for independent application of simulated law.

The original petition says Tillman changed an earlier time estimate, but the lower opinion separately says his lack of physician credentials was developed on direct and cross-examination at trial and no misstatement of those credentials was alleged. A change of expert estimate is not automatically established perjury; an expert's criticism is not itself a judicial finding of false testimony. The original affidavits expressly list Whitaker's testimony among materials reviewed; the medical dispute must be assessed with that testimony, not with an assumption that only Tillman supported the State's chronology.

The lower opinion says the constitutional instruction and nonphysician-testimony grounds were not raised in the first federal petition and separately gives pre-AEDPA abuse and merits reasons. It also discusses innocence as an independent constitutional theory, expressing uncertainty about its presentation below, and points to Georgia extraordinary-new-trial procedure under Ga. Code §§5-5-40 and 5-5-41. The **original Supreme Court petition is explicit** about independent innocence; uncertainty in the lower opinion must not become absence of the original claim. The lower court's statements about cause, availability, state process, and evidentiary sufficiency remain lower-court rulings or characterizations, not new waivers or Court findings.

## Complete-charge source and exact qualifications

JA 26–36 provides the charge, including transcript 1102–1103: the State bears the burden from beginning to end to prove every essential fact beyond reasonable doubt; criminal proof is distinguished from civil preponderance; the charge uses “beyond a reasonable doubt and to a moral certainty” and “Moral and reasonable certainty is all that can be expected in a legal investigation.” It defines reasonable doubt by evidence, lack of evidence, or conflict in evidence, contrasts reasonably entertained doubt with vague, fanciful, or farfetched doubt, and orders acquittal if minds remain wavering, unsettled, and unsatisfied. At 1105–1106 it requires circumstantial evidence to exclude every other reasonable theory and forbids conviction on suspicion, conjecture, or possibility. At 1108–1109 it again directs acquittal for reasonable doubt. Expert opinions are not binding and are to be disregarded if their assumed facts are not found true. The full charge is available for the simulated *Victor* complete-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry.

The lower opinion also records the prosecutor's voir-dire “moral and reasonable certainty” questions and a subsequent judicial instruction that arguments of counsel are not evidence and do not bind the jurors. Whether those statements and the complete final charge answer the presented claim is a legal question; neither isolated-word invalidity nor an automatic curative-boilerplate rule is supplied by this extraction. No new challenge to other charge language is inferred.

## Statutory distinctions and jurisdiction sources

- **§2244(b)(1):** a claim presented in a second or successive §2254 application that was presented in a prior application shall be dismissed.
- **§2244(b)(2):** a new claim in such an application shall be dismissed unless **(A)** it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral cases **by the Supreme Court**, **or (B)** both due-diligence unavailability of the factual predicate **and** facts which, if proven and viewed with all evidence, establish by **clear and convincing evidence** that **but for constitutional error** no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. The two requirements of (B) are cumulative. A circuit holding of retroactivity is not the statute's stated Supreme Court condition.
- **§2244(b)(3)(A)–(D):** before filing in **district court**, apply to the appropriate court of appeals; a three-judge panel acts; authorization requires a prima facie showing; the court shall act within 30 days. **(E)** makes authorization grants/denials not appealable and not subject to rehearing petitions or certiorari petitions. **(4)** separately requires the district court to dismiss an authorized claim unless the applicant establishes the statutory requirements. Authorization does not finally determine entitlement.
- **§2241(a)–(b):** expressly authorizes the Supreme Court and its Justices, district courts, and circuit judges to grant writs within their jurisdictions; the Supreme Court/Justice/circuit judge may decline and transfer to a district court with jurisdiction. Title I leaves the text of §2241 untouched. Distinguish this original-application source from §1254 certiorari review and from §2244(b)(3)(E)'s gatekeeping-review bar; preserving a source of jurisdiction does not itself decide the substantive successive restrictions or extraordinary-writ discretion.
- **§2254(b), (d), (e):** the enacted exhaustion alternatives (available state remedies exhausted, absence of process, or ineffectiveness), permission to deny unexhausted claims on merits, express-only state waiver, state-merits adjudication conditions, presumption of factual correctness, and failed-development hearing restrictions have separate triggers. Section 2254(e)(2)'s alternative new-law/new-fact requirement combines with its separate clear-and-convincing constitutional-error/no-reasonable-factfinder condition. Do not replace all of these with one generic “AEDPA restriction.” Exact enacted text is in the linked Title I extraction.
- **§2254(a)** remains the federal-law custody predicate. **§2242/Rule 20.4(a)** requires reasons for not applying to the district court; the petition must state how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, and show exceptional circumstances warranting this Court's discretionary power and absence of adequate relief in another form or court. Respondent's event-date brief quotes the rule at pp.18–19. “Rarely granted” does not mean jurisdiction absent.
- **FRAP 22(a)** as amended directs applications to district court and transfers circuit-judge applications; it does not expressly name the Supreme Court in that transfer direction. Constitutional Article III appellate character and §2241's statutory direct-filing channel should not be confused with Article III original jurisdiction limited to enumerated cases.
- **Chapter 154:** enacted §107(c) expressly supplies pending-case reach for that chapter. The Eleventh Circuit states at JA 5 n.1 that no showing of Georgia's qualification had then been advanced, and expressly excludes §107 from its analysis. Do not assume qualification or a chapter-154 automatic stay. The different Title I provisions require their own temporal analysis under simulated *Landgraf*.
