## Channel and judgment scope

No.95-865 concerns three consolidated interlocutory appeals. The Court of Federal Claims granted **partial summary judgment on contractual liability**, with damages still undetermined. The Federal Circuit accepted certified interlocutory review under §1292 and affirmed en banc. Supreme Court review is under §1254(1); the absence of a final damages award does not itself defeat that channel. The Court of Federal Claims' contract jurisdiction rests on §1491(a)(1); that statute does not create the substantive promise. The alternative takings claims were not reached below. Upholding contractual liability would leave damages and remaining proceedings to the trial court; it would not itself award a stated amount, invalidate FIRREA, or issue an injunction against Congress. An adverse contract ruling would require attention to the unadjudicated alternatives and actual scope of remand.

The lower court found an implied-in-fact agreement in Winstar and express agreements in the others. The **en banc court held Winstar also had an express agreement** on the documents and therefore did not reach implied-in-fact formation as an independent basis. Do not describe the en banc judgment as finding only an implied Winstar promise.

## Authority and risk allocation

The agencies did not purport to prevent Congress from enacting laws. Whether an authorized agreement shifts the **financial consequences** of later change is separate from whether Congress can legislate and the regulators must apply FIRREA. A demand for damages still requires a proved promise and authorized assent; calling a remedy damages cannot alone establish either.

The relevant statutory sources include former **12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(3)**, authorizing FSLIC “to make contracts,” and the transaction-effective **§1729(f)** assistance powers. Glendale's filed brief reproduces the 1976 contract authority and then-effective §1729(f) text at appendix A1–A2, including assistance to prevent default, merger assistance and guarantees against merger loss, and the liquidation-cost limit with the essential-community-service exception. It identifies the pertinent amendment as the **1978** Financial Institutions Regulatory and Interest Rate Control Act, notwithstanding its citation to the 1982 Code Supplement. Winstar/Statesman's brief cites the 1988 §1729(f)(2)(A),(3) assistance authority and §1725(d). The Board resolutions independently record the transaction-specific statutory determinations and approval. These are actual authority sources; no missing-delegation finding is made.

The Government's merits brief, pp.36–43, argues that general contracting/regulatory power does not specifically delegate a power to bind future legislative regulation; it invokes *Home Telephone*, *Stone v. Mississippi*, *Merrill*, and *OPM v. Richmond*. Respondents answer that the authorized undertaking exchanges merger assistance/accounting benefits for assumption of liabilities and carries damages when the Government changes the promised treatment; they do not require an agency delegation to bar Congress from legislating. These competing descriptions must be tested against the actual contracts and *Hercules*, rather than assuming that the word “contract” settles future-law risk or that regulation erases every negotiated obligation.

**FIRREA and regulation:** 12 U.S.C. §1464(t) imposes uniformly applicable capital standards and constrains supervisory goodwill. The lower opinion distinguishes tangible capital (no goodwill), phaseout of goodwill from core capital, and limited qualifying-goodwill amortization for risk-based capital. It separately attributes the treatment of **capital credits** as qualifying supervisory goodwill to **OTS regulation, 12 C.F.R. §567.1(w)**; FIRREA did not expressly use “capital credits” to impose that exact treatment. Do not attribute the agency's treatment verbatim to Congress. The Government defends the reforms as public and general; respondents contend that the particular impairment bears directly on the obligations undertaken and that assumed risk defeats impossibility. Neither statutory validity nor regulatory compliance is equivalent to contractual discharge.

## Supported decisional alternatives and entering law

Source-supported legal questions include: (1) whether each writing set creates the alleged promise under ordinary contract interpretation; (2) whether unmistakability is required for the particular undertaking/remedy and, if so, whether the documents satisfy it; (3) the scope of actual authority and reserved sovereign powers; (4) whether the relevant government act is public and general under *Horowitz*, and whether ordinary contract risk allocation or impossibility supplies discharge; and (5) whether the partial-summary-judgment record permits judgment or requires bounded further proceedings. A remand is not automatic because originals are unavailable to an operator—the record documents here are recovered. Nor may a court use “general fairness” to invent a promise.

The actual [*Hercules* public projection](PUBLIC_Hercules_Inc_v_United_States_merits_1996-03-04.md) was read. Souter's controlling opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg, requires an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact agreement; it does **not** bar all consequential damages or invalidate genuine warranties because possible damages were uncertain. Breyer's dissent joined by O'Connor accepts authority and an actual undertaking as essential while seeking a contextual/remedial inquiry on that different record. Stevens did not participate there; do not invent a prior vote for him. This continuity bears on contractual method, not on a predetermined Winstar commitment.

Other date-eligible authorities include *Lynch v. United States*, 292 U.S.571 (1934); *Perry v. United States*, 294 U.S.330 (1935); *Horowitz v. United States*, 267 U.S.458 (1925); *Bowen v. Public Agencies Opposed to Social Security Entrapment*, 477 U.S.41 (1986); *Merrion v. Jicarilla Apache Tribe*, 455 U.S.130 (1982); *United States Trust Co. v. New Jersey*, 431 U.S.1 (1977); *Sutton*, 256 U.S.575; *Federal Crop Insurance Corp. v. Merrill*, 332 U.S.380; and *Home Telephone & Telegraph Co. v. Los Angeles*, 211 U.S.265.
