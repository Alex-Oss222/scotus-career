## 1. Winstar Assistance Agreement, section 10 — Accounting Principles

JA 108–109, complete section:

> § 10 Accounting Principles. Except as otherwise provided, any computations made for the purposes of this Agreement shall be governed by generally accepted accounting principles as applied on a going concern basis in the savings and loan industry, except that where such principles conflict with the terms of this Agreement, applicable regulations of the Bank Board or the CORPORATION, or any resolution or action of the Bank Board approving or adopted concurrently with this Agreement, then this Agreement, such regulations, or such resolution or action shall govern. In the case of any ambiguity in the interpretation or construction of any provision of this Agreement, such ambiguity shall be resolved in a manner consistent with such regulations and the Bank Board’s resolution or action. If there is a conflict between such regulations and the Bank Board’s resolution or action, the Bank Board’s resolution or action shall govern. For the purposes of this section, the governing regulations and the accounting principles shall be those in effect on the Effective Date or as subsequently clarified, interpreted, or amended by the Bank Board or the Financial Accounting Standards Board (“FASB”), respectively, or any successor organization to either. If there is a conflict between what is required by the FASB and what is required by the Bank Board, the Bank Board’s interpretation and requirements shall govern.

## 2. Winstar July 13, 1984 letter — enumerated terms and reservation

JA 123–124a. The letter is from the Federal Home Loan Bank Board to the Board of Directors, United Federal Savings Bank, Windom, Minnesota; it is signed for the Board by Secretary J. J. Finn. The substantive letter text, including the provisions referenced by the reservation, reads:

> In connection with the merger of Windom Federal Savings and Loan Association, Windom, Minnesota (“Merging Association”), into United Federal Savings Bank (“Savings Bank”), this is to confirm the understanding that:
>
> 1. For purposes of reporting to the Board, Savings Bank may show, as a direct credit to its net worth, the cash contribution made by the FSLIC pursuant to Section 4(a)(1)(A) of the Assistance Agreement to be entered into among Winstar Corporation, Savings Bank, and the Federal Savings and Loan Insurance Corporation (“FSLIC”);
>
> 2. For purposes of reporting to the Board, the value of any intangible assets resulting from accounting for the merger in accordance with the purchase method may be amortized by Savings Bank over a period not to exceed 35 years by the straight-line method;
>
> 3. For purposes of reporting to the Board, any discount arising from the adjustment of Savings Bank’s mortgage loan portfolio to its fair value shall be credited to income by the level yield method assuming contractually required payments will be received for 15 years, and the remaining balance will be repaid at the end of the 15th year or by other methods of applying the level yield method as described in R memoranda or other accounting documents acceptable under generally accepted accounting principles; and
>
> 4. The limitation of 50%, on Savings Bank’s net income available for payment of dividends, as set forth in the Dividend Limitation Stipulation and Bank Board Resolution No. 84-363, is waived for its fiscal years beginning on July 1, 1985, July 1, 1986, and July 1, 1987. During Savings Bank’s fiscal year beginning on July 1, 1985, it may pay as dividends up to 60% of its net income. During Savings Bank’s fiscal year beginning on July 1, 1986, it may pay as dividends up to 70% of its net income. During Savings Bank’s fiscal year beginning on July 1, 1987, it may pay as dividends up to 60% of its net income. But in no event may Savings Bank by the payment of dividends reduce its net worth below the level required by § 563.13 of the Insurance Regulations for institutions insured for twenty years or longer.
>
> The accounting forbearances in the preceding paragraphs 1 through 3 are not acceptable in any financial statements which are required to be prepared in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles.
>
> The forbearances or waivers extended by this letter do not relieve Savings Bank of its continuing obligations to maintain records of its reserve and net worth condition and to report its financial condition in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements.
>
> This letter does not and shall not be construed to constitute forbearance or waiver by the Board or the FSLIC with respect to any regulatory or other requirements other than those encompassed within the preceding paragraphs 1 through 4. Other than actions to enforce the regulatory requirements waived in accordance with paragraphs 1 through 4 and the statutory provisions authorizing imposition of the waived requirements, insofar as such requirements are waived, the Board and the FSLIC expressly reserve all of their statutory rights and powers with respect to Savings Bank, including, without limitation, those under Section 5 of the Home Owners’ Loan Act of 1933, as amended, and Sections 406 and 407 of the National Housing Act, as Amended.

The other numbered provisions are reproduced to identify the actual scope of the reservation's cross-reference; their reproduction does not add claims or a request for relief.

## 4. Statesman Assistance Agreement, section 20 — Compliance with Law

JA 406–407, complete section, including its qualification:

> Section 20. Compliance with Law. The ACQUIRING ASSOCIATION shall, and shall cause its subsidiaries, Affiliates, and service corporations to, comply in all material respects with all applicable statutes, regulations, orders of, and restrictions imposed by the United States or any state, municipality, or other political subdivision, or by any agency of the foregoing public units, regarding the ownership of its properties and conduct of its business, including, without limitation, all applicable statutes, regulations, orders, and restrictions relating to equal employment opportunities, employment retirement income security, and environmental standards and controls; provided, however, that nothing contained in this § 20 shall require the ACQUIRING ASSOCIATION, or any of its subsidiaries, Affiliates, or service corporations, to comply with any law so long as the applicability thereof shall be contested in good faith.

This text is not a finding that a particular contest was made, was in good faith, or satisfied any applicable procedure. It is the contractual text only.

## 5. Ordinary-duration clauses and other termination triggers

Winstar, JA 110–111, complete section 13(a):

> § 13 Termination of Agreement.
>
> (a) Except as otherwise specifically provided in this Agreement, this Agreement shall terminate two years following the Effective Date or on such other date to which the parties or their successors agree in writing. However, if a receiver is appointed for the purpose of liquidating the RESULTING ASSOCIATION, or the RESULTING ASSOCIATION ceases to be an association the accounts of which are insured by the CORPORATION, this Agreement and all of the CORPORATION’s obligations under it shall terminate upon the effective date of such occurrence.

Section 13(b), also at JA 111, separately addresses indemnification for described liabilities/claims and related litigation expenses after ordinary termination, conditioned on the Resulting Association's continued compliance with the specified covenants and on the claim being in litigation at termination; it provides for recoveries to be paid over subject to the referenced limits. This note does not extend that separate indemnity to a claim it does not identify. The source image contains its full text.

Statesman, JA 405, complete section 19(a). The filed scan reads “in § 16 elsewhere,” without an added “or”:

> Section 19. Termination of Agreement.
>
> (a) Except as otherwise specifically provided in § 16 elsewhere in this Agreement, this Agreement shall terminate five (5) years following the Effective Date or on such other date which the parties or their successors agree in writing, provided that if a receiver is appointed for the purpose of liquidating the ACQUIRING ASSOCIATION, or if the ACQUIRING ASSOCIATION ceases to be an association the accounts of which are insured by the CORPORATION, this Agreement and all of the CORPORATION’s obligations under it shall terminate upon the effective date of such occurrence.

Sections 19(b)–(d), JA 405–406, separately address the Special Reserve Account balance, qualified continuing indemnification, and pending submissions received on or before the termination date. Section 19(c)'s continuation is expressly subject to the terminating proviso in 19(a). Section 16, JA 402, contains the separately defined unapproved-Sale termination provision and its named affiliate-transfer exclusion. Those source references are provided to prevent reading the ordinary five-year sentence as the entire termination scheme; no sale, liquidation, loss of insurance or resulting contractual cutoff is newly found in this note.
