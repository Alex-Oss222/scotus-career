## 1995-10-16 — Doe v. Taylor Independent School District; Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the Caplinger judgment.** The Chief Justice joins Parts I and II but would resolve Caplinger’s component on the merits. Each official must be assessed on his own information and response. Caplinger’s later and more limited information, inquiries, interview and warnings, despite their alleged deficiencies, do not amount on the assumed facts to the knowing disregard potentially shown against Lankford. The Chief Justice does not rely on the absence of close precedent, impute the principal’s knowledge, resolve the January/February conflict, or establish a general right to an adequate school investigation. This ground is not the Court’s Caplinger holding.

Hafer’s personal-responsibility rule and Elder’s complete review do not eliminate that specificity problem. Farmer and other later formulations cannot provide retrospective notice. The dissent assumes the pleaded facts for this legal inquiry; it neither endorses abuse nor holds that every omission is beyond §1983.

## 1995-10-23 — Hodge v. Jones; Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins the prospective mootness holding but would reject the damages claim because the asserted constitutional deprivation is absent on this record. ## 1995-10-30 — Tuggle v. Netherland; No. 95-6016; reported identifier 516 U.S. 10.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring; O'Connor and Souter join only the proposed Chapman rule for a preserved, properly available capital-sentencing denial of required psychiatric assistance when the State presents psychiatric future-danger evidence.** The Chief Justice joins the Court because its surviving-aggravator ground independently requires vacatur whatever error-effect standard ultimately governs. He would also extend O'Neal to an available Ake denial of this kind: the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the whole penalty record that the denial did not contribute to the death sentence. O'Connor and Souter support that proposal within the preserved and properly available category just described; their joins do not extend to the Chief Justice's further remedial directions.

A psychiatric defense serves investigation, cross-examination, rebuttal, and mitigation. An unrebutted future-danger case can influence the whole sentencing choice after its aggravator is removed. The State's asserted evidentiary advantage arises from the very denial that makes the missing defense difficult to reconstruct; the defendant should not bear that uncertainty. This is a proposed extension, not a claim that O'Neal already governs all Ake errors. A surviving eligibility finding cannot replace examination of whether the decision to impose death was constitutionally reliable.

The Chief Justice assumes the 1985 penalty-stage violation for this review without relying on the withdrawn concession. Any renewed request or threshold challenge must be available under the existing rulings and preservation rules. If the violation stands, he would require review of the whole penalty record, including what competent assistance could have supplied for rebuttal and mitigation, without inventing testimony. If harmless under the governing standard, relief on this error should be denied; otherwise he would require a conditional writ allowing a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable District Court deadline or replacement of the death sentence by lawful relief. No deadline is fixed now. If the present submissions have not addressed the governing standard, he would permit briefing, including on his proposed Chapman extension. He would not order a new hearing now, declare harmlessness, classify the error as structural, order a guilt retrial, or decide an independent state-aggravator issue. These further directions are his own; the Court reserves the applicable standard and ultimate relief.

## 1995-11-07 — Libretti v. United States, No. 94-7427

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment and joining Part I-B.** The Chief Justice upholds Libretti's informed relinquishment of a forfeiture jury and leaves the right's constitutional source unresolved. A sentencing label alone cannot answer the constitutional question, and the actual waiver makes an answer unnecessary. Gaudin remains the rule for enacted elements; this disposition neither expands nor displaces it.

Section 853 fixes the court's power to take property. Actual admissions and existing evidence can establish that basis without another hearing for every item, but a promise to forfeit cannot authorize an otherwise forbidden sentence. Lawful source alone is not immunity where the property is an instrumentality, an enterprise interest or authorized substitute property. The existing orders, admissions and evidence support rejecting a wholesale new proceeding on the reviewed claims; those claims do not establish an identified, still-operative defendant-owned portion requiring vacatur. That is not a finding that every § 853(p) predicate was proved for every item or an endorsement of forfeiture by agreement alone. A demonstrated statutory defect in an identified portion would require its vacatur and lawful separation or further treatment; no prior final invalidity judgment would be necessary. The April adverse findings, effective third-party returns and defendant-specific postappeal jurisdiction limit retain their exact scope. The check, IRA and Porter claim remain distinct, and no conditional pension exclusion is applied without its predicate. Supported portions, the guilty plea and conviction are preserved.

**Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Justice Souter joins Parts I-A, I-B and III. He agrees that the right must be understood before it can be surrendered and resolves the case without deciding its constitutional source. Ordinary jury advice, understood with an indictment's forfeiture allegation, can cover the charged forfeiture question without reciting a rule number. Advice affirmatively suggesting that the jury right excludes forfeiture would present a different problem. The two earlier references to the jury's forfeiture function answer the possible misleading implication of the later guilt-focused explanation here; represented assent and the express end of trial then establish surrender. Godinez and Olano preserve that actual-choice requirement. He does not join the five's constitutional classification or broader sufficient-waiver formulation, require an automatic additional hearing, or infer private advice absent from the record.

**Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Justice Ginsburg joins Parts I-A, I-B and III. She requires awareness of the unusual forfeiture special-verdict right, and relies on the pretrial references to that jury function followed by Libretti's represented agreement and express assent to ending trial. A bare reading of a forfeiture allegation or broad signature need not suffice in another case. An explicit plea-hearing explanation is a readily available protection, but the omission of that additional warning does not require remand when the record otherwise establishes informed relinquishment. The limited Mezzanatto consent principle supports attention to the protection actually surrendered. She leaves the constitutional source unresolved and withholds the broader formulation in Part II; she neither finds a constitutional jury right nor equates statutory classification with knowing waiver.

**Stevens, dissenting, while joining Parts I-A and I-B.** Justice Stevens agrees that Rule 11(f) does not impose the requested forfeiture inquiry and that a known jury right may be surrendered. His dissent instead requires the sentencing court independently to assure itself that its punishment stays within § 853 where the record raises a concrete question about the forfeiture order's breadth. Bigelow and Ex parte Lange distinguish punishment exceeding statutory power from an arguably erroneous exercise of lawful power; Roberts illustrates why agreement alone cannot make an item forfeitable. A broad all-assets bargain cannot purchase authority Congress withheld.

The record supports portions of the order and expressly designates a substitute check. Those facts do not by themselves establish the basis for every additional interest embraced by the appellate all-assets rationale. The April court's affirmative adverse findings and its attempted defendant-specific further inquiry reinforce the distinction between assent and authority, even though the defendant's pending appeal defeated that reopening. Stevens would vacate the defendant's forfeiture disposition and remand for the statutory assurance he finds missing. He does not declare the check, IRA, Wyochem balance or every asset invalid, erase third-party orders or require automatic return. He leaves the constitutional question unresolved and does not premise remand on a finding of ignorance despite the jury notices. The remand would not automatically rescind guilt or preserve every benefit of a sentence-contingent bargain: if the court rejected an agreement governed by former Rule 11(e)(1)(C), Rule 11(e)(4)'s opportunity to withdraw would apply. That condition is not established merely by the existence of this forfeiture agreement.

## 1995-11-20 — A. St. P. C. v. B. C., No. 94-7810; October Term 1995; Judgment below: 643 So. 2d 743 (La. 1994).

Justice O'Connor dissents, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The dissent would reverse the categorical requirement of clear and convincing proof and remand for proper consideration of the protective procedure. Santosky concerned complete and irrevocable termination; here parental status survives, and treatment followed by a judicial best-interests determination permits supervised contact. Under Mathews, the process must be assessed as a whole, including danger to a child from an erroneous decision permitting renewed exposure. The severity or duration of a restriction demands adequate process but does not automatically import termination's proof burden.

The dissent distinguishes Foucha's expired confinement predicate and Heller's unjustified disparity between commitment classes. Neither supplies this new categorical rule. O'Connor's position preserves scrutiny of actual duration and adequate review; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas likewise reject transferring a proof rule across distinct protective settings. All four recognize the serious contact loss and reject immediate reliance on the old finding entered without notice of these additional consequences. They do not validate every indefinite restriction, make periodic review a cure for every deprivation, find that abuse occurred, or direct enforcement on the defective notice premise. Their proposed reversal would leave those questions and lawful protective measures for further state proceedings.

## 1995-12-05 — Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, No. 94-1530; 516 U.S. 124; October Term 1995.

**Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, concurring.** They join the Court's opinion with the express understanding that the present covered-ground holding does not decide reviewability of a remand under Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill. Thermtron held both that a particular unauthorized remand was improper and that the ordinary review bar did not cover remands outside §1447(c). Cohill later recognized discretion to remand remaining state-law claims after elimination of the sole federal claim, despite the absence of express statutory authorization. Cohill did not decide whether §1447(d) bars review of that distinct remand. Neither today's affirmance nor these two Justices' reservation resolves that issue in either direction.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurring.** They also find an independent review bar in §1452(b). Equity there means fairness or appropriateness, not the former division between legal and equitable pleading. Failure to remove promptly fits that meaning. Making a statutory time bar reviewable while equivalent delay labeled laches is immune would make appellate jurisdiction depend on a label. Hernandez v. Brakegate supplies the modern sense of equity; Sykes v. Texas Air explains the incoherence of label-dependent review. The chapter's structure and the 1977 House report's parallel treatment of remand review support a supplementary bankruptcy-remand regime, while Timbers directs attention to the coherent statutory scheme.

They give an alternative explanation as well: even if a procedural or jurisdictional impediment were called legal rather than equitable, equity may describe the choice to remand rather than dismiss. Section 1452(b) permits that fair disposition without requiring the impediment itself to fit an old equitable category. Their position concerns remands of claims related to bankruptcy. It does not insulate lack of subject-matter jurisdiction in a retained case: a decision not to remand may lack a separate immediate appeal while jurisdiction remains open to district-court reconsideration and eventual review through an otherwise proper channel. Their additional ground neither answers the separate Cohill question outside its statutory coverage nor commands a majority of the Court.

## 1995-12-11 — Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue; 28 F.3d 1501; Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** All three join Parts I and III, including the actual-forum viewpoint rule, the concrete conclusion that the establishment defense fails on these private-use facts, and the remedial limits. They withhold assent from Part II's comprehensive contextual Lemon explanation. Their ground is the distinction between genuinely private religious advocacy and governmental religious action, reflected in Kennedy's Mergens opinion and in the access reasoning of Rosenberger and Chabad. Voluntary attendance, parental choice and private leadership support access here; a religious message or possible subjective impression of endorsement does not alone permit suppressing private expression. They propose no unlimited forum, general financing entitlement or alteration of existing establishment law through this concurrence.

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the Eighth Circuit and leave the judgment for the school district in place. Perry and Cornelius permit a genuinely restricted forum, and his Mergens analysis treats the activity the school actually admitted as decisive. The Scout and athletic programs are secular activities related to the school's extracurricular program; the fact that both those programs and the adult-led Club concern moral development does not make them the same admitted activity. He acknowledges the policy's religious restriction and Lamb's Chapel's prohibition on viewpoint exclusion within an admitted subject, but finds the activity distinction sufficient for this Club's denial. The younger audience and continued extracurricular day reinforce the restricted character of the forum. His disagreement concerns that comparison, not a general exemption allowing schools to suppress religious viewpoints, a necessary finding that every Club meeting establishes religion, or rejection of Lamb's Chapel's governing rule.

## 1995-12-11 — Love v. Pepersack; OT1995-015; Fourth Circuit No. 94-1582, 47 F.3d 120. No separately established Supreme Court docket.

**O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas — plurality ground supporting due-process affirmance.** The four assume the state interest and accept the alleged deliberate, knowing and malicious arrest-only practice. They nevertheless find this eligibility-screening restriction insufficient to establish the separate substantive wrong. Unresolved dispositions concern the program's eligibility subject even when officials knowingly exceed statutory authority. Collins separates state duty from constitutional arbitrariness; Romein and Beach supply limited economic-rights analogies. The four adopt no benign-motive finding or legislative-classification burden for this executive act. They reject categorical cure through state review, an absolute-discretion entitlement rule and a finding that deadline pressure compelled the policy. Their bounded conclusion does not exempt every affirmative licensing act. Stone-Zsela does not join this application; it is not the Court's controlling rationale.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions and the personal-right, incorporation and necessary-acquisition holdings. He agrees with the due-process dismissal: unauthorized state administration does not by itself create a federal substantive wrong, state review corrected the state-law deprivation, and the incorporated Second Amendment supplies the specific protection for the asserted arms liberty. Substantive due process should not duplicate it. No separate entitlement to every lawful private transaction follows.

He would reverse the arms dismissal with constitutional infringement resolved, rather than vacate for an open application. In his view the guarantee centrally protects a law-abiding person's acquisition and keeping of a common firearm for lawful self-defense in the home. Nunn and Chandler support personal holders and defensive use while preserving regulation; Andrews supplies the acquisition incident, with its state-law and weapon-specific limits. Incorporation does not require reopening the Privileges or Immunities Clause.

His disqualification rule requires adjudicated conduct or incapacity genuinely connected to danger: conviction for a serious violent offense, a judicial dangerousness determination with adequate process, a domestic-violence protective order after notice and hearing, or adjudicated functional incapacity to possess safely. A bare diagnosis, accusation or uncontestable administrative label is insufficient. Convictions for murder, serious battery, armed robbery, violent domestic abuse, terrorism, planned unlawful force against government or comparable violence can support dispossession; arrests alone cannot. He does not settle every valid disqualifying conviction or restoration and duration rule.

On the pleaded record, Love's arrests were accusations; her identified obscene-show misdemeanor conviction established neither violence, dangerousness nor relevant incapacity. The Chief would hold that she could not constitutionally be denied an ordinary handgun solely because those arrests appeared in the files. The State may promptly investigate genuinely unresolved potentially disqualifying conduct under authority its law gives it. Missing dispositions do not become adjudications of guilt, and time pressure authorizes no judicially invented extra waiting period. Public safety does not supply a missing factual predicate.

His remand would resolve infringement while leaving personal responsibility, lawful relief and properly available defenses for ordinary proceedings. He decides no general public or concealed carrying rule, sensitive-place restriction, prohibited-weapon or magazine category, ordinary dealer licensing, recordkeeping, commercial regulation or storage rule. His affirmative application and adjudication-based disqualification framework receive no other Justice's join and are not the Court's law.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, and by Souter as to Part A only, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** All four join the procedural nonreach of immunity and the common state-law/federal-wrong and contracting propositions.

In **Part A**, all four would vacate due process and remand for assessment of the actual affirmative-policy claim. Zinermon and Stevens's Daniels concurrence distinguish substantive wrongdoing, complete when committed, from adequacy of procedure. Later correction alone cannot cure the former. The knowing-barrier allegations cannot be replaced by findings of necessary haste or official caution. Collins retains the need for a federal interest and substantive arbitrariness without completing this different application. An independent sufficient ground could permit affirmance despite erroneous reasoning, but these framing defects are material. The dissenters would return entitlement, constitutional sufficiency, attribution, defenses and relief for decision. They establish no violation, automatic discovery or trial, or damages entitlement. Malice and correction expense do not supply every element.

In **Part B**, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm arms dismissal under the specific state-nonapplication precedents. They acknowledge affirmative original evidence of individual keeping, acquisition and personal security, and the power to reconsider under Benton. Differing state clauses, military-suitable weapon boundaries, regulatory authority, the Reconstruction statute's equality and restoration setting, and Howard's privileges-or-immunities framing nevertheless fail, in their view, to justify this right-specific displacement of Cruikshank and Presser. Lewis's extant-felony setting is not a complete answer to Love. They adopt neither a collective-only rule, formal-enrollment condition nor state-correction cure. Souter does not join Part B; he joins the Court's arms holdings and bounded vacatur.

## 1996-01-08 — Lawrence v. Chater, No. 94-9323; 516 U.S. 163

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court's opinion and adds that certiorari administration properly permits a case-specific assessment of the totality of the circumstances. The value of obtaining the lower court's judgment about a potentially consequential development need not depend on first proving that the claimant will win. That discretion remains judicial and supplies no automatic rule accepting Government requests. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice would use §2106 when a material intervening development creates a reasonable possibility of a different result and reconsideration would conserve judicial resources. He does not adopt the Court's distinct reasonable-probability formulation. Henry separates this procedural return from a benefits award. The agency's changed position matters because it may unsettle the existing judgment, not because administrative deference supplies power to vacate. A strategic litigation reversal, an immaterial development, or a position outside lawful agency authority would not suffice, and remand may not predetermine the merits. Thomas Jefferson University and MCI supply cautions for the receiving court's independent examination of statutory authority, procedure, consistency, fair notice, and the position's lawful weight; they do not make the new position controlling. No other Justice joins this opinion.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny the petition. An intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment can justify vacatur without a merits determination. An established defect can also support a justified error-correcting disposition; plenary review is not foreclosed. Conditional executive advocacy of uncertain relevance supplies no sufficient ground here. The agency's qualified acknowledgment establishes neither its legal force nor its application to this claimant. A clearly cognizable agency change could qualify, but a desire to reassess an arguably valid decision or obtain better reasoning does not. The unproved predicates of the state amendment do not cure that defect. The dissent concerns judicial justification for this remedy, not a ruling that the agency can never examine constitutional validity.

## 1996-01-08 — Stutson v. United States, No. 94-8988; 516 U.S. 193

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the Court and adds that case-specific, totality-of-the-circumstances discretion is a proper part of certiorari administration. The combination of an uncertain review premise and the potential loss of appellate consideration supports obtaining a fresh lower-court judgment without deciding excusability. This position creates no entitlement for every short delay and no exception to attribution of counsel's conduct. No other Justice joins this supplemental explanation.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent would deny. Pioneer was available when the lower courts acted, and an unexplained affirmance may have applied its approach and still rejected the excuse. The Government concedes a point of law concerning relevance, not error in the judgment. Its changed argument therefore establishes neither new intervening law nor a defective judicial premise. The dissent accepts vacatur without a merits determination for an intervening factor legally bearing on the decision, clarification needed to establish jurisdiction where Michigan v. Long does not govern, or a confession of error in the judgment. It does not foreclose plenary review or a justified error-correcting disposition. None of those grounds is established here. Uncertainty about which ground sustained a judgment, or a desire for better reasoning, does not itself justify vacatur; the proper exercise of §2106 requires more than the possibility that reconsideration could change the result.

## 1996-01-16 — Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc., No. 94-2003; 516 U.S. 233.

**O'Connor's plurality, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg.** Section 102(b) leaves functions, procedures, and methods free, but an original selection and arrangement does not necessarily lose all protection merely because users employ it to operate software. Feist protects original arrangement while withholding ownership of the underlying material. Baker permits use of a system without establishing that every particular presentation of it is excluded. The plurality would reject the First Circuit's categorical treatment and distinguish any original expression from the unowned operation.

That view covers both implementations without declaring every command or all 469 choices protected. Compatibility matters to purpose and defenses, but does not automatically answer classification or fair use. ## 1996-06-24 — Lewis v. United States, No. 95-6465, 518 U.S. 322; October Term 1995.

### O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Souter and Thomas

The plurality would affirm because legislative seriousness is measured offense by offense. Each mail-obstruction count is petty under Blanton's full test, and joinder alone does not alter Congress's classification. The additional fine, assessment and probation package supplies no independent ground for treating either offense as serious.

Codispoti's aggregate actual-sentence analysis concerns contempt without a legislative maximum and the concentration of judicial power in that setting. Statutory crimes supply Congress's own seriousness measure. The plurality would therefore reject aggregation here without deciding whether the magistrate's cap was effective. The concurrent-sentence default does not establish that consecutive sentences were legally unavailable. This four-Justice rationale does not become Court law, and it announces no universal approval of prosecutorial evasion, new waiver or harmlessness rule.

Kennedy and Breyer reject a rule allowing unlimited cumulative imprisonment in one nonjury prosecution simply because each count is petty. In their view, a bench trial of joined petty charges constitutionally cannot yield aggregate imprisonment above six months. The magistrate's advance ruling made clear that this trial would remain within that limit; the constitutional analysis itself supplies the ceiling, without an invented independent statutory cap.

They distinguish an individually serious offense, which carries legislative stigma and a jury right that a short-sentence promise cannot remove, from the aggregate liberty loss created by multiple petty counts. Codispoti and Taylor are not irrelevant merely because they involved contempt. Here the advance announcement supports affirmance; eventual probation is not its retrospective justification. Their reasoning supplies no finding of charging abuse or rule for every sequence of separate prosecutions.

The Chief Justice first aggregates the exposure and then asks whether a lawful, irrevocable limitation actually removes serious punishment from the prosecution. The jury guarantee concerns the punishment the accused legally faces, not the labels on the counts.

Here the magistrate's unqualified announcement before trial operates with the constitutional prohibition on greater aggregate nonjury imprisonment. It binds the court through disposition and cannot be lifted after the jury demand is denied. That constitutional limit supplies enforceability; no separate statutory reduction, sentencing order or waiver is assumed. The other applicable penalties do not independently establish seriousness under Blanton.

An informal promise, revocable prediction, limitation imposed only after trial or cap omitting independently serious punishment would fail this analysis. Stone does not join categorical nonaggregation or rely on the probation actually imposed to cure a prior denial. His requirement of an effective advance cap remains distinct from Kennedy and Breyer's complete rationale.

Stevens and Ginsburg would reverse and remand because the joined prosecution authorized a year of imprisonment. The legislative measure remains controlling, but it must be applied to the prosecution as a whole. Blanton's single-offense facts did not require choosing between the offense and prosecution as the relevant unit.

Codispoti supports aggregation of serious confinement. Where contempt lacks a legislative maximum, actual punishment supplies a proxy; that feature does not confine the jury's protection to contempt. The right here attached when prosecution began. A judge's promise of leniency cannot alter the statutory exposure or remove that attached right, and the eventual probation terms do not cure its denial.

The dissent rejects the proposed distinction between the stigma of one serious offense and the serious liberty exposure produced by several petty counts. They do not rely on equalizing influence, reducing campaign costs or disliking expensive speech, and they do not extend their conclusion to every PAC, individual, press organization or nonprofit. The dissent would affirm enforcement under §441a(d), leave the penalty amount for the lower court and reject the requested facial invalidation without adjudicating every hypothetical coordinated transaction or finding an independent §441a(a)(2) violation.

## 1996-06-26 — Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc.; Nos. 95-754 and 95-886; October Term 1995.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join the design, parallel-duty and manufacturing/warning holdings and the formal FDA interpretation. They would leave open whether some other common-law duty may be expressly preempted by section 360k. Rejecting Medtronic's sweeping displacement argument does not require categorical immunity for tort law, but neither does it require affirmative inclusion beyond the duties actually resolved. Cipollone construed different statutory language and does not compel that additional step here. Their reserved position is not the Court's rule because five Justices directly adopt Part IV. Their agreement with the present application does not depend on a prediction that future common-law preemption must be rare.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I, II and IV. Substantial-equivalence clearance supplies no substantive design approval; genuinely equal state duties are not different merely because damages are available. But actual federal manufacturing and warning commands apply to this device even though they cover other devices. In their view, the statute requires applicability, difference or addition and subject connection, not an extra specificity prerequisite. The FDA cannot impose that extra condition against text they find clear. Section 808.1's recognition of court decisions and divergent substantive applications also defeats a categorical exemption based on general state-law form. They would affirm preemption of genuinely divergent manufacturing/warning duties, reverse the parallel-duty bar and remand to identify the actual obligations. They make no finding of compliance, breach or Florida entitlement and do not demand dismissal of an entire count that may contain a viable equal-duty theory.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I through IV and the judgment. Congress's express comparison controls: a damages duty can be a requirement, but clearance and the agency's presence do not supply a missing substantive federal comparator. Equal conduct does not become different merely because damages are available. His conclusion rests on that statutory ground, so he does not join Part V's independent reliance on agency interpretation. He neither rejects that ground as unlawful nor adopts a general tort exemption. The unresolved content of a particular pleaded duty can be identified through ordinary proceedings without requiring a new preliminary hearing in every products case; breach, causation and relief remain unproved.

## 1996-06-26 — United States v. Virginia, No. 94-1941; Virginia v. United States, No. 94-2107; 518 U.S. 515.

**Scalia, dissenting; no joins.** Scalia applies the existing important-objective and substantial-relationship standard but reaches the opposite conclusion on the accepted record. In his view, preserving VMI's integrated method and a real option within a diverse educational system is an important actual objective. The findings about the value of single-sex education and the changes coeducation would cause establish substantial fit. Intermediate scrutiny permits an imperfect fit; some women's ability to meet physical standards does not alone defeat it. He distinguishes Hogan because its permission for men to audit classes undermined the claimed need for exclusion, whereas VMI's residential and educational methods operate together. His position does not treat tradition as immunity, replace heightened scrutiny with rational-basis review, or exempt unlike institutions from comparison.

His primary disposition would reverse liability in No. 94-2107 and, consequently, vacate the compulsory remedy in No. 94-1941 insofar as it rests on that rejected liability, remanding to remove those dependent obligations. That would not prohibit Virginia from voluntarily continuing VWIL.

Separately, assuming liability remains established, he would affirm the existing VWIL approval with its monitoring requirements. He considers the subsidized leadership education, four-year ROTC, endowed support and fully credited alumni assistance meaningfully comparable despite different methods, engineering costs, credentials, resources and prestige. In his view, rejecting the whole alternative gives those differences more constitutional weight than intermediate scrutiny permits. His conditional approval requires faithful funding and alumni performance, the District Court's retained jurisdiction and six-month reports, and the appellate directions for qualified leadership, recruitment and promotion, near-term funding and continuing professional review; promises are not findings of completed performance. ## 1996-06-28 — Board of County Commissioners, Wabaunsee County v. Umbehr, No. 94-1654; October Term 1995.

**Scalia's dissent, joined by Thomas.** They would not extend public-employee retaliation doctrine to this commercial relationship. The traditional distinction between government employment and contracting, legislative responsibility for procurement and the contractor's separate business position counsel against judicial creation of this cause of action. Courts would otherwise have to evaluate political criticism, service judgments and lost commercial opportunities throughout government contracting, with litigation itself burdening administration. Their Rutan and National Treasury Employees Union positions support preserving that boundary rather than treating every economic government relationship as employment.

They acknowledge the existing employee-speech and unconstitutional-conditions precedents but reject the majority's proposed extension; a conditional preference for Pickering over universal strict scrutiny is not agreement that either protection must apply here. Direct sovereign regulation and government programs expressly supporting private expression present different questions. They would reverse the reviewed appellate ruling and leave official-capacity summary judgment in place on nonextension grounds. They do not find the commissioners' motives lawful, the criticism false or injury absent, and they do not reopen the individual-immunity or McClure judgments.

## 1996-06-28 — Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC; Alliance for Community Media v. FCC, Nos. 95-124 and 95-227; October Term 1995.

**Kennedy, joined by Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg, concurring in the judgment on (c) and dissenting on (a):** Congress itself selects protected content for loss of access protection. Legislative action is reviewable even when every resulting private refusal is not governmental. Turner supplies ordinary cable protection and the content inquiry; ACT and Sable require adequate means as well as a compelling purpose. A permission can deny willing adults access altogether and does not become tailored merely because a private operator chooses whether to use it. The policy safeguards in (a) do not cure that underlying selective veto; (c) does not even contain those same limits. Neither popular offensiveness nor moral disapproval alone supplies a sufficient governmental justification, and the protection extends to unpopular expression without ideological preference.

These Justices recognize Hurley's genuine private-presentation right, but reject assuming that every independently programmed access channel carries the operator's own message. A locally dedicated channel open to private speakers may support a forum analysis under Perry and Cornelius; their opinion does not find that dedication in every franchise, declare all PEG governmental or authorize local managers to censor protected expression at will. They join the Court's (b) explanation because the combined compulsory burdens fail the required means review. They reserve §506(a) and (b), lawful obscenity prohibitions, distinct unlawful-conduct questions and concrete editorial claims. They seek no unconditional carriage order. Their strict sufficient framework for (a)/(c) has three votes.

**O'Connor, dissenting on (c):** She accepts constitutional scrutiny of Congress's permissive law, but finds its accommodation of genuine independent editorial choice and protection of children sufficient against this facial challenge. Turner and Hurley give that editorial interest substantial weight. The permission does not itself order an unwilling operator to exclude speech, and the different origins of leased and locally arranged access do not, for her, justify the different constitutional result. She recognizes that (c) lacks (a)'s written/published-policy and reasonable-belief conditions; she does not silently supply them. Their absence does not persuade her that all applications of this uncoerced permission must fail. Actual governmental institutions, an established local obligation or coercive administration may present different claims.

Her position differs from Scalia and Thomas's independent private-editor ground: private choice does not end review of Congress's content selection. It also differs from her vote on (b), where willing communication encounters the government's integral compulsory structure. An ordinary age screen or subscription request alone is not condemned, but the operator-retained lockbox accommodation leaves that structure intact. She joins the Court's (b) invalidity and the four-Justice contextual (a) rationale, without joining a universal cable forum or a categorical absence of governmental action.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in the judgment on (a) and dissenting on (b)/(c):** Their permission analysis starts with the difference between a protected speaker's ability to seek a willing outlet and a constitutional right to force an unwilling private editor to carry the message. Scalia's Sable concurrence, their Turner positions and Hurley support that distinction. Congress's partial relaxation of imposed access duties is not itself a direct governmental ban on the programmer's speech. Statutory designation alone does not constitutionalize a forum or every former access entitlement. They reserve an actually governmental institution and independently established obligations without finding that any particular franchise lacks them; they decide neither the entire access regime's constitutionality nor a taking. This is a merits ground, not a denial of Article III standing. They do not join the plurality's contextual balancing as the sufficient reason to sustain (a) or (c).

They separately apply strict scrutiny to (b), which burdens transmission by a willing operator. Mixed and unpredictable schedules make ordinary subscriber-initiated blocking a less effective default protection; segregation leaves the other basic channels available while preserving a real adult election. A written request can reduce requests made by children without authorization, and thirty days is a compliance maximum, not a compulsory waiting period. Household controls remain usable after opt-in. They find that design sufficiently fitted despite its adult-access burdens, rather than requiring government to accept a materially weaker parental-assistance mechanism. They neither require a flawless alternative nor presume an official list, universal affordability, actual disclosure or implementation of the 1996 blocking laws. Thomas distinguishes ACT's mandatory broadcast-hours exclusion from the present adult election; Scalia's practical ACT concerns do not lower the governing standard. Their strict-(b) analysis has two votes.

## 1996-07-01 — Pennsylvania v. Labron, No. 95-1691; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, No. 95-1738; October Term 1995.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, dissenting from review and summary reversal:** Labron's opinion, read in the setting of Pennsylvania's decisions and Article I, §8, rests on an adequate independent state rule in their view. Its reliance on the Commonwealth's jurisprudence and White is substantive, not an incidental state citation accompanying an exclusively federal judgment. Federal search cases may describe the constitutional floor while the State imposes its own greater protection. They regard the state discussion, including the distinctions in White between automobile exigency and its separate Belton issue, and the state opinions' treatment of Pennsylvania protection as evidence that the judgment would remain independent of the federal answer. A disagreement within the state court over the wisdom or foundation of that rule does not itself make the ground federal.

Long's presumption begins with a judgment that fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven; these Justices would not extend it to a judgment they read as primarily state law merely because it lacks a particular verbal formula. Their actual Evans jurisdictional position reinforces the need for an affirmative basis for federal intervention. Cox finality cannot cure an adequate independent state ground. They therefore would deny Labron's petition and would not reach its federal automobile merits.

Kilgore is different: its express Fourth Amendment conclusion makes the federal ground much clearer. They nevertheless would deny discretionary review. The surrounding state jurisprudence makes renewed suppression on an independent Pennsylvania basis the expected result in their judgment, so a federal correction and remand would consume both courts' resources without a useful change in outcome. That is their reason for restraint, not an entered future state decision or a jurisdictional finding that Kilgore already rests exclusively on state law. Stevens's institutional caution in Carney supports examining whether a vehicle actually warrants intervention, rather than treating every asserted automobile error as requiring immediate review.

They do not turn the Lincoln's failed start into a new mechanical-incapacity holding or the farm's private location into an adjudicated curtilage claim. Nor do they adopt an extra federal exigency requirement as an affirmative merits position. Johns's distinction between custody and continuing vehicle-search authority remains consistent with their review objections. Their dissent concerns the authority and utility of deciding these cases, not a separately stated federal rule sustaining every suppression rationale below.

## 1996-07-01 — United States v. Winstar Corp., No. 95-865; consolidated Glendale, Winstar and Statesman contractual-liability disputes; October Term 1995.

**Souter's Part V, joined by Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer:** The unmistakability doctrine protects against an effective surrender of sovereign authority, but these particular financial undertakings leave Congress free to regulate. Lynch's recognition of government obligations and the distinctions in Bowen and Merrion require identifying what the promise and remedy would actually do. A demand for money does not automatically avoid the doctrine: repayment that effectively supplies a tax or comparable sovereign exemption can implicate it. Here, however, the authorized transaction allocated financial risk rather than immunizing institutions from regulation. The Government cannot obtain the acquired liabilities' assumption on the particular promised terms and then erase the ordinary contractual consequence merely by invoking its power to change law. These four distinguish the protected exercise of sovereign power from the limited obligation actually undertaken; they do not declare all damages suits outside unmistakability. Stone does not join this complete threshold account, and Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas instead apply the doctrine.

**Souter's Part VI, joined by Stevens and Breyer:** Independently of assumed risk, these three would reject the sovereign-acts protection where release from the Government's own contractual obligations is a substantial effect of the challenged measure rather than an incidental consequence of public regulation. The inquiry is objective. They do not require subjective bad faith, a predominant contractual-avoidance motive or predominant contractual effect. Inclusion in a broad financial reform statute does not itself make direct release from the promised accounting/capital performance incidental. The particular disallowance of that treatment has the required substantial effect for them. They add no quantitative industry-impact finding, condemn no legislative motive and do not classify every FIRREA provision as non-general. O'Connor withholds this ground; the other affirmers rely on the independent assumed-risk conclusion. The additional theory has three votes.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring:** Unmistakability applies and is satisfied. Sovereign contracting displaces the ordinary presumption that a promisor undertakes not to disable performance by its own acts; courts should not lightly infer a surrender or financial undertaking against legislative change. But the unmistakable subject of these bargains was the identified future accounting and capital treatment. The Government need not utter a second promise that it will keep the first before that actual promise has effect. Incorporation, priority, duration, reservation and compliance terms must all be read, not replaced by a general rescue-policy assumption. They find the particular risk assignment clear even under the sovereign-contract canon, while recognizing immediate regulatory permission could form a different bargain in another case.

Actual statutory assistance authority supports the financial commitments. No agency veto over Congress is needed, and no implied-in-law indemnity or universal governmental insurance is recognized. Clear assumption of this risk defeats the asserted sovereign-acts and impossibility discharge. They join the Court's contract, authority, common discharge and remedy parts, not the claim that unmistakability is inapplicable or the separate objective substantial-effect route. Damages still require proof under the individual contracts.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring:** Sovereignty determines whether Congress may change the law; the established contract determines who bears the financial consequences of withdrawing the promised treatment. These express authorized undertakings supply the relevant risk allocation. The doctrines protecting legislative authority from implied surrender cannot erase that actual monetary responsibility, but regulation alone would supply no contract. He joins the Court's common parts and its damages remand without choosing a complete threshold rule between the other affirmers' unmistakability accounts. He does not join the substantial-effect alternative or find bad faith. The result neither requires compensation for every legal change nor surrenders sovereign power or authorizes specific performance contrary to a valid statute. Causation, the losses covered and their amounts remain contract-specific.

**Ginsburg, dissenting from present liability affirmance:** The existence of written assistance arrangements and valuable regulatory permissions does not itself resolve the alleged promise to bear future legislative risk. Unmistakability is a construction rule that must inform identification of the undertaking before a court declares that risk was assigned. Immediate permission to use favorable accounting can be valuable consideration even if no longer-term protection against legislative change was promised. Describing the bargain as risk shifting and the remedy as damages does not answer that prior question.

She requires separate attention to Glendale's incorporation and assistance term, Winstar's reporting permission, regulatory-change and reservation language, and Statesman's distinct goodwill and express capital credit with their own conditions. She does not deny the actual $26 million credit wording or hold that no contract or authorized warranty can exist. Actual Hercules requires the real duty; it does not supply the further continuing promise merely because damages would be lawful if such a promise were proved. General agency authority and partial liability findings reached under an insufficient construction criterion do not settle that dispute.

She would vacate the present interlocutory affirmance and remand for bounded reconsideration under unmistakability for each writing set and each Statesman benefit. She would not order ultimate judgment for the Government, declare a specific delegation absent, adopt a broad sovereign-acts discharge, require a new trial or decide damages and takings. Her disagreement is with present affirmance of the asserted continuing risk undertaking, not a categorical denial of government contract liability or a refusal to recognize express terms.
