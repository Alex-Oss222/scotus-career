#### C. Issue positions

##### Issue 1: The organizing principle and the additional predominance question

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the admitted legal question once jurisdiction is established. Accept the premise that race predominated and ordinary criteria were subordinated. Stone does not avoid the question by imagining a predominantly nonracial legislative compromise. His answer applies simulated Shaw and explains the equality principle that makes its distinction defensible.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Equal protection secures persons' equal civic standing and participation in political institutions. Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency does not, by itself, deny those interests, even when traditional districting considerations are subordinated. Governmental exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, and other independently cognizable deprivations remain subject to their governing constitutional rules. This enumeration is illustrative, not exhaustive.

**Essential Reasoning:** Equal membership identifies what government owes each person. Antisubordination explains why the history and continuing operation of exclusion matter to that obligation: identical formal access can coexist with structures that impair participation, while attention to racial conditions can help remove barriers. Individual rights constrain corrective methods, and lawful authority constrains which institutions may act and what remedies they may impose. These are complementary parts of Stone's reasoning, not interchangeable tests or a license to select whichever rationale favors the policy under review.

Districting necessarily assembles residents into constituencies. Recognizing demonstrated racial voting conditions does not necessarily assign every resident a racial political identity, make voters of one race interchangeable, or create a representative legally accountable only to that race. The constituency includes all residents. This is why demographic composition alone does not establish unequal terms of membership. It is not because boundaries have no effects, because political institutions are exempt from personal equality, or because the government has described its purpose favorably.

Formal access is therefore a starting fact rather than a complete defense. A boundary can implement expulsion, unlawful dilution, or inferior civic treatment. Courts must examine the actual operation relevant to the asserted legal interest. Equal protection protects persons of every race; aggregate white political strength or a group's past dominance cannot answer an individual's present deprivation. The term civic membership expresses equal standing and does not limit the Equal Protection Clause to citizens.

**Boundary:** The present holding concerns territorial districting. It creates neither a universal constitutional action for unequal social outcomes nor a general replacement scrutiny tier for every governmental use of race. Antisubordination informs the common equality inquiry; it does not establish a separate immunity for measures called benign.

##### Issue 2: Historical constitutional arguments about separation, stereotyping, and representation

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does the racial design assumed here itself establish unconstitutional segregation, an official racial identity, or a legally inferior relationship to the elected representative? Stone answers no on these allegations and explains why each asserted inference fails. He does not simply rename the claim design-only and decline to confront it.

**Separation and stereotyping:** The strongest argument is that government itself assigns political identity when it deliberately sorts voters by race, so the assignment is already the unequal treatment. Stone accepts that imposed racial separation can be an injury without a second loss. He rejects the inference that every predominantly racial arrangement of a shared electorate necessarily has that character. The challenged demographic design does not itself make anyone ineligible to vote, associate, seek office, form a cross-racial coalition, or belong to the representative's constituency because of individual race. These features distinguish the asserted design from racial restrictions on institutional membership or participation. They do not insulate a geographically implemented restriction that actually imposes such inequality.

Recognition of observed voting patterns is different from prescribing a person's political beliefs or civic worth. Gingles permits relevant evidence of political behavior within its statutory inquiry; it does not authorize assuming that every person of a race thinks alike. Purpose evidence and official statements can expose an independently cognizable discriminatory practice. The State cannot rely on racial stereotypes as a justification for an actual burden. But the constitutional character of the governmental treatment must be explained, not conclusively inferred from the weight given to race in selecting boundaries.

**Representation:** A majority-minority district does not make its representative the legal representative of only minority residents. The prediction that the representative will neglect other residents does not follow necessarily from the racial composition or design of the district. A racially discriminatory denial of an otherwise available participation or constituent-service opportunity would require examination under the law governing that denial. No such practice is established by this reconstructed record. Conversely, the opinion creates no general right to command a representative's attention, policy agreement, or favorable vote.

**Status and expression:** Noneconomic harm can be a constitutional deprivation. Anderson's state-imposed racial ballot cues and McLaurin's restrictions within an institution refute any requirement that a plaintiff prove financial loss or a different election result. A status claim need not await an official's later denial of service if the challenged governmental act itself establishes the prohibited unequal status. What fails here is the assertion that predominant demographic design necessarily announces that residents have racially inferior standing. General disagreement with that design's perceived racial message, unsupported by an independently cognizable form of governmental inequality, does not establish the asserted violation.

**Application and limit:** On the stated premise, the historical separation, stereotyping, and representational arguments do not supply a distinct basis for invalidation. This is a substantive legal answer under the reconstructed record, not a finding that no conceivable injury existed in historical Georgia and not a dismissal of expressive harms as categorically unreal. An allegation's substance controls; a court must not reject facts establishing a recognized deprivation merely because the pleader also invokes racial predominance.

##### Issue 3: Exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, and civic status

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** An independent deprivation means one legally identifiable apart from predominance alone. It requires neither a second governmental act nor an additional downstream injury after unequal treatment is established. The challenged boundaries themselves can accomplish the wrong. Each claim retains its own elements, burdens, and remedy.

| Protected interest | What makes the constitutional issue legally distinct | Application to this reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| Racial exclusion | State action racially removes people from the franchise or equal institutional membership. Gomillion's municipal disenfranchisement supplies the concrete boundary example. | Predominant composition of a common congressional district does not alone establish expulsion or racial ineligibility. No such additional operative fact is supplied here. |
| Intentional vote dilution | A districting arrangement purposefully impairs protected voting opportunity under the applicable constitutional doctrine. Map evidence and history can help establish the claim. | No dilution is alleged in the reconstructed premise. That does not establish compliance with §2, whose statutory results inquiry does not depend on an added constitutional-purpose finding. |
| Materially unequal participation | Racial arrangements impair participation on equal terms despite nominal access. White and McLaurin demonstrate why formal eligibility can be insufficient. | No particular unequal participation practice is established. Being a racial minority within a district is not itself proof of such a practice. |
| Legally inferior civic status | Governmental treatment itself imposes a recognized racial inferiority or official discriminatory designation; resulting monetary or electoral loss need not be shown. Anderson illustrates official electoral treatment that can be unlawful without a changed winner. | The alleged inferiority cannot be presumed solely from predominant racial design. An actual status-imposing practice remains reviewable on its own facts and law. |

**Reasoning and history:** The Reconstruction Amendments and voting-rights enforcement responded to racial exclusion from political membership, including devices that preserved formal rights while frustrating their exercise. That history explains why meaningful participation and lawful correction matter. It does not prove this particular map effective, erase the rights of white residents, or turn equal membership into an entitlement to proportional seats. Recognizing four protected interests does not establish that four separate claims were pleaded, preserved, and tried in the historical Miller litigation. This brief answers their legal relationship to the reconstructed case without manufacturing those proceedings.

##### Issue 5: Voluntary inclusion, VRA obligations, and DOJ authority

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may take account of race in territorial districting to improve minority electoral opportunity even when no adjudicated prior violation or federal statutory command requires the precise district selected. Absence of such compulsion does not itself make the State's choice unconstitutional. The permission remains subject to every independently applicable constitutional and statutory limit and supplies neither a duty to maximize majority-minority districts nor an entitlement to proportional representation.

**Reasoning and Application:** The Constitution's protection of equal membership permits lawful efforts to make participation meaningful. Government need not wait until a particular response becomes judicially mandatory before considering obstacles to participation. The contrary inference would turn a statute's minimum obligations into a ceiling on constitutional permission. This conclusion rejects the asserted legal prohibition; it makes no finding that Georgia's policy actually achieved inclusion or that its historical predominant purpose was remedial.

**Historical preclearance question answered:** Compliance with a DOJ demand is not equivalent to compliance with the statute, and neither automatically establishes constitutional validity. Beer does not convert §5 into a requirement to create every possible majority-minority district. A federal administrator must act within delegated statutory authority, and independent judicial review of a properly presented constitutional claim cannot be surrendered to an administrator's approval. Conversely, the fact that DOJ could not lawfully require a particular district would not by itself establish that the State could not choose it.

**What this answers and what it does not:** Stone rejects the legal equivalence between constitutional permission, statutory duty, and an administrator's demand. He does not adjudicate the lawfulness of a particular historical objection letter on an unadmitted record or bypass Morris and the designated review channel. Nonretrogression does not eliminate the distinct statutory purpose inquiry. This opinion does not decide every relationship between §§2 and 5, the full reach of §5's purpose provision, or later disputes concerning federal enforcement authority. Those questions require their own statutory grounds and records. This reservation does not reopen the proposition actually answered here: lack of federal compulsion alone is no constitutional prohibition.

##### Issue 6: Individual justification, limits on authority, and consequences across settings

**Stone's Position:** Equal membership supplies a common constitutional purpose; the legal treatment, protected interest, institutional authority, and remedy determine how it applies. History helps identify subordination and the significance of corrective action. It cannot substitute for justification of an actual personal burden or authorize an institution to exceed its powers.

In contracting, a racial eligibility rule or incentive can affect a bidder's competitive opportunity directly. A lawful corrective objective does not erase that burden. Stone's separately selected Adarand position requires an important objective within lawful authority, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between means and objective, and justification of coverage and actual burdens, including concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. That remains his position, subject to the actual earlier adjudication; Miller does not adopt it by implication or create a benevolent-purpose exemption. His Stevens fallback in Adarand remains the separately authorized alternative for nonadoption of the replacement standard, not a means of excusing failure under an adopted rule.

An adjudicated school-segregation violation supplies a remedial predicate that voluntary districting does not require. Stone's Jenkins preference for effective correction, joint development with independent review, and a meaningful route to release addresses administration of that remedy. It creates no indefinite obligation to achieve ideal racial or educational outcomes. Equally, the absence of a decree does not itself prohibit every voluntary integration policy. An actual restriction on an individual's access must be justified under the law governing that restriction. A legitimate inclusion objective and an unlawful particular means can coexist.

**Lower-court direction:** Identify the challenged action and the source of the asserted right; distinguish jurisdiction from merits; determine the legally material facts and apply the governing burdens; assess any required justification; and confine relief to the established violation and lawful authority. These are directions for reasoned adjudication, not a mandatory factor count or a new branded test. Participation, transparent reasons, independent review, and revisable measures may improve institutional decisions. Miller does not make them universal constitutional prerequisites, automatic defenses, or substitutes for rights.

**Future-case boundary:** These propositions constrain later reasoning if adopted, but do not decide later outcomes. Standing, statutory causes of action, congressional enforcement power, agency authority, immunity, and remedial release each retain their governing sources. No future Vera, Boerne, Bossier, school-transfer, contracting, or crossover-district result is imported into the holding. Nor may a later court evade a proposition actually settled here merely by calling it reserved. The distinction is between applying this rule to a new claim and pretending that its already answered legal question remains open.

#### D. Precedent treatment and theoretical grounding

- **Simulated Shaw:** Apply its design-only holding, ordinary pleading protection, independent-injury qualification, and separate statutory review channel.
