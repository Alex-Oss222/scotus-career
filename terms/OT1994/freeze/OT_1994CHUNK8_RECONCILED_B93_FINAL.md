## Lawful paths

A: uphold the short waiting period under existing CentralHudson intermediate scrutiny. Protecting recent accident victims' privacy and the legal system's reputation are substantial interests; a temporally confined prohibition on an unrequested individualized invasion responds directly to the immediate-trauma privacy harm described in the Bar's predecision study and public submissions. Public advertising and later direct mail preserve meaningful channels. Required fit is reasonable rather than a least-restrictive-alternative command. Strongest counterargument: closed mail can be discarded, the solicitation is truthful, and public hostility to lawyers or advertising does not establish a harm caused by THIS communication in THIS period. A survives only if the privacy record supplies the missing connection; professional taste alone is insufficient.

B: invalidate the30day restrictions as applied to truthful noncoercive mailed solicitation. The record's broad disapproval and general traumatic vulnerability do not establish that simply receiving an identified letter causes the asserted harm or that a categorical30day period materially advances it. Shapero distinguishes discardable mail from the pressure of an in-person encounter. Strongest counterargument: government may rely on experience and reasonable predictive judgment, and advertising markings do not prevent unwanted direct intrusion during bereavement or injury. B cannot impose an empirical-study prerequisite, forbid any waiting period, or erase independent harassment/incapacity safeguards.

Lanphere and Rubin supply binding real-harm, material-advancement and reasonable-fit rules.
