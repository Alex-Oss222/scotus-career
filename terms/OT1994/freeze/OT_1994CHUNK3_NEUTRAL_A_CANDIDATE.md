## 25. O'Neal v. McAninch — No. 93-7407

**Argument:** October 31, 1994. **Scheduled event:** February 21, 1995. **Review:** Sixth Circuit reversal of federal habeas relief, O'Neal v. Morris, 3 F.3d 143 (August 20, 1993).

### Question and indispensable premise check

The admitted question concerns the consequence of a federal habeas court's grave doubt about whether constitutional trial error affected the verdict. The supplied packet embeds a further premise: that Brecht generally adopted the substantial-and-injurious-effect standard. **That premise is inconsistent with the controlling simulated Brecht.** The question cannot be processed merely by copying the historical lower court's account of Brecht.

Simulated Brecht, April 21, 1993, holds that for the preserved Doyle protected-silence error properly before the federal habeas court, the **State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict**. Collateral status alone does not replace Chapman with Kotteakos for that claim. The analysis is on the whole record; relief is required only when the State fails that standard, not automatically upon discovery of trial error. The holding does not resolve every class of constitutional habeas trial error or abolish independent procedural gates. White's controlling Parts II–III have five joins: Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, and Souter. See entering-law A, Brecht entry and the Standards and Tests entry **“Preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review,”** and the [public Brecht render](../../OT1992/output/OT_1992CHUNK5.md).

The present instructional claim is not itself Doyle error. A clean decision must identify whether the Chapman rule and rationale extend to this claim, or whether the preserved-Doyle boundary leaves a distinct harmlessness question. It may not announce that a universal Kotteakos rule is already binding. Until the admitted question is made coherent with that baseline, O'Neal is **not ready for a frozen merits commitment**.

### Record and lower-court reasoning

The [complete Sixth Circuit opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/3/html/0143-01.html) supplies the following facts and distinctions. O'Neal was convicted in Ohio of aggravated murder, aggravated robbery, and kidnapping after a joint trial. The victim was brought from Chicago to Cleveland, held at a bar, and later found dead from a blow and a gunshot. The prosecution offered participation evidence including possession of the victim's wallet, witness testimony about a confession, and physical evidence. These are the lower court's description of the record, not new findings of guilt.

The district court conditionally granted habeas relief based on confusing instructions and cumulative alleged errors. The Sixth Circuit reversed. It first rejected a waiver obstacle: although late objections to an initial magistrate report had been struck, the district court recommitted the matter and timely objections followed the second report. The court treated its waiver rule as nonjurisdictional and exercised interests-of-justice discretion. This history replaces the unsupported assertion that no threshold issue existed; it does not reopen an issue outside the admitted review.

The instructions referred to “defendants or codefendants or aiders and abettors” before discussing the specific purpose to kill. The charge twice directed separate consideration of each defendant. Applying Estelle/Boyde's reasonable-likelihood inquiry to the charge as a whole, the Sixth Circuit did not find the instruction by itself unconstitutional. The prosecutor then described the State's task as proving the crime and defendants' participation in a course of conduct that eventually led to it; the defense objected and the objection was overruled. The Sixth Circuit called that description a misstatement of Ohio culpability law. It nevertheless reasoned that even if the argument and instruction together produced constitutional error, the error was harmless.

In that alternative analysis, the Sixth Circuit placed on the petitioner a burden to show “actual prejudice,” using its historical understanding of Brecht. It pointed to evidence that O'Neal acted as a principal, rather than merely guilt through codefendants. **The opinion contains no express finding that the reviewing court was actually in equipoise.** The legal question about grave doubt must not be turned into an invented finding that this record produced grave doubt. Nor does an assumed constitutional error become an adjudicated structural defect. The lower court's other evidentiary and prosecutorial rulings are part of its judgment, not automatically additional admitted Supreme Court questions.

### Relevant law and individual writings

Simulated Estelle preserves the reasonable-likelihood, whole-charge inquiry for ambiguous instructions and the rule against relieving the State of elemental proof; it does not decide this harmlessness allocation. Sandstrom and Francis remain relevant to an actual mandatory-presumption claim. Sullivan's structural defect involving the absence of a valid reasonable-doubt verdict must be distinguished from an assumed trial error. Schlup's current-term innocence gateway permits merits review in its defined circumstances; it does not choose the burden on the underlying harmlessness issue.

The public Brecht writings provide unusually direct Justice-specific material. O'Connor joined Chapman and separately stressed accuracy and the costs of multiplying doctrinal categories, without denying all direct/collateral distinctions. Souter joined White's controlling rule and Part A of the continuity concurrence, not its broader Part B criticism. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would apply Kotteakos to the collateral claim based on finality and comity; it treated the challenged use as cumulative on that record and did not deny Doyle's constitutional status. Stevens separately preferred a demanding whole-record Kotteakos inquiry, not bare evidentiary sufficiency, and emphasized a prosecution obligation to explain harmlessness. These distinct grounds must remain distinct. No comparable date-eligible Ginsburg or Breyer writing on this exact allocation was established in this source pass; neither inherits a predecessor's vote.

### Candidate lawful paths for fresh validation

1. **Chapman path:** determine that the preserved constitutional instructional error comes within the rule or rationale governing Brecht; apply the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden. Grave uncertainty could not establish harmlessness under that burden. A remand may be needed to determine the assumed error and apply the correct standard; an automatic unconditional writ does not follow.
2. **Distinct collateral-error path:** explain why Brecht's preserved-Doyle holding leaves this class open, adopt or justify an applicable Kotteakos inquiry for this class, and then resolve how genuine grave doubt operates. The petitioner-burden argument invokes habeas's collateral posture and the need to demonstrate unlawful custody; the contrary argument invokes the nature of harmless-error review and the risk of affirming when the court cannot conclude the error was harmless. Either result requires a rule and record application, not an imported general Brecht premise.

A narrower disposition could remand because the lower court's legal premise differs from governing law without resolving every hypothetical equipoise question. The necessary remedy must specify what remains for the habeas court, preserve unreviewed gates, and distinguish a new trial opportunity under a conditional writ from an unconditional release order.

## 26. United States v. National Treasury Employees Union — No. 93-1170

**Argument:** November 8, 1994. **Scheduled event:** February 22, 1995. **Review:** NTEU v. United States, 990 F.2d 1271 (D.C. Cir. March 30, 1993), affirming an injunction against enforcement of the honoraria ban against Executive Branch employees.

### Question, parties, and record

The admitted controversy is the First Amendment validity of the broad honoraria ban as applied to employees' compensated expression, particularly expression unrelated to their jobs, and the permissible scope of relief. The certified class covers affected Executive Branch employees **below GS–16**. Peter G. Crane, an individual plaintiff, was GS–16 and outside that class. An all-Executive injunction therefore differs from class relief; neither party representation nor merits adjudication for every senior official may be assumed.

The [D.C. Circuit opinion, including the concurrence and dissent](https://static.case.law/f2d/990/html/1271-01.html), describes speech about religion, history, dance, art, and the environment. Many activities had no connection to the employee's duties, the payor's dealings with the employee, official resources, or government working time. Some plaintiffs' activities were closer to their official work. “Every plaintiff's speech was unrelated” would overstate the record.

Williams wrote for the court, joined by Randolph; Randolph concurred separately and Sentelle dissented. The majority used Pickering balancing, found a burden on protected outside expression, and held that the Government had not adequately connected the sweeping restriction to its asserted interests. It found no demonstrated breakdown of earlier safeguards supporting that breadth. The majority severed Executive employees' coverage, preserving the congressional and judicial applications, and affirmed the all-Executive injunction. It did not adopt every underinclusiveness theory. Sentelle defended a prospective response to corruption and its appearance, drawing on the GAO and commission materials, the permissible breadth of prophylaxis, Hatch Act authorities, and the statutory exclusions. He disputed both the constitutional conclusion and the judicial treatment of statutory scope. These are competing legal arguments, not factual proof that corruption was absent or inevitable.

### Statute in its own terms

The official [1994 United States Code, title 5 Appendix, Ethics in Government Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title5/html/USCODE-1994-title5-app-ethicsing.htm) supplies the pertinent text. Section 501(a)'s separate high-level noncareer outside-income ceiling is not the same provision or coverage as §501(b).

**Section 501(b):** “An individual may not receive any honorarium while that individual is a Member, officer or employee.”

**Section 505(2):** “The term ‘officer or employee’ means any officer or employee of the Government except any special Government employee (as defined in section 202 of title 18, United States Code).”

**Section 505(3):** “The term ‘honorarium’ means a payment of money or any thing of value for an appearance, speech or article (including a series of appearances, speeches, or articles if the subject matter is directly related to the individual's official duties or the payment is made because of the individual's status with the Government) by a Member, officer or employee, excluding any actual and necessary travel expenses incurred by such individual (and one relative) to the extent that such expenses are paid or reimbursed by any other person, and the amount otherwise determined shall be reduced by the amount of any such expenses to the extent that such expenses are not paid or reimbursed.”

Section 505(1) defines Member to include a Senator, Representative, Delegate, or Resident Commissioner; §505(4) defines travel expenses as transportation, lodging, and meals while away from residence or principal place of employment; §505(5) refers to §170(c) charitable organizations. Section 501(c) deems an honorarium paid instead on the individual's behalf to a charitable organization not received by that individual, subject to a **$2,000 maximum** and the prohibition on payment to an organization from which the individual or that individual's parent, sibling, spouse, child, or dependent relative derives financial benefit. Those qualifications are not a general employment-nexus exception.

Section 504(a) permits the Attorney General to bring a civil action and permits a civil penalty of not more than $10,000 or the compensation received, whichever is greater. Section 504(b) protects qualifying good-faith conduct in accordance with a written advisory opinion, including a materially indistinguishable factual situation, from subsection (a)'s sanction. These are enforcement and reliance provisions, not a power to disregard the substantive prohibition at will.

The definition's parenthetical makes the duties/status nexus express for a **series**. Whether an avoidance construction can extend that nexus to the whole definition without rewriting it is a distinct statutory question. It cannot be silently supplied as though Congress placed a general nexus limitation in §501(b).

### Entering law and Justice-specific materials

Pickering supplies the employee/citizen and employer-interest balance; Connick's public-concern concept is not limited to party politics or complaints about the workplace. Compensation restrictions can burden speech even when unpaid speech remains possible. The governing First Amendment slice also includes simulated Simon & Schuster and Turner: financial burdens and statutory classifications require analysis of their actual coverage and justification, and benign purpose does not answer every content-discrimination objection. Their different regulatory settings do not themselves establish the result here.

Simulated Waters, May 31, 1994, concerns discipline based on an employer's account of particular speech. O'Connor's full framework was joined by Stone, Souter, and Ginsburg. Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurred in the judgment with a broader genuine-ground position and no comparable constitutional investigation duty; honest belief still does not convert constitutionally protected speech into punishable speech. Stevens, joined by Blackmun, focused on actual speech, Pickering interests, causation, and immunity. The seven-Justice reasonable-and-genuine safe harbor and the six-Justice limit on an unreasonable false account must be kept distinct; Waters is not one majority opinion authorizing all prospective speech restrictions. Its Mt. Healthy causation remand likewise does not decide the present statutory fit. See the [public Waters render](../../OT1993/output/OT_1993CHUNK5.md) and the matching entering-law entries.

Thomas's own [NTEU preliminary-injunction opinion, 927 F.2d 1253 (D.C. Cir. 1991)](https://static.case.law/f2d/927/html/1253-01.html), predates divergence. It affirmed denial of interim relief for lack of irreparable short-term harm, emphasized expenses, charitable payments, and interim arrangements, and acknowledged possible longer-term reduction or elimination of expression. **It expressly did not decide the merits.** It is evidence of his treatment of interim injury and the statutory mechanisms, not a prior merits vote or an established recusal. No case-specific exclusion is supplied. No comparably decisive Breyer writing was established in this source pass.

### Two paths, strongest obstacles, and remedies

**Invalidity path:** A prospective ban imposes burdens on many employees and listeners without an adequate relation between the restricted compensation and official influence. The Government must justify the breadth it chose, and the existing nexus provisions and alternatives may bear on fit. The strongest contrary argument is that prevention of corruption and its appearance need not await individually documented misconduct and that administrable prophylaxis can properly exceed provable quid pro quo transactions. A decision accepting invalidity must answer that argument in terms of this law's coverage, record, and burden, rather than deny preventive authority categorically.

**Validity path:** Apply public-employment balancing with substantial regard for preventive administration and appearance concerns; find the compensation burden sufficiently limited by retained expression and statutory exclusions. The strongest contrary argument is that the statute sweeps across unrelated employees and expression without the public-employment nexus ordinarily explaining the Government's additional authority. Expense reimbursement does not reimburse the expressive labor whose compensation is forbidden.

On either path, separate **(1)** interpretation, **(2)** constitutionality for the plaintiffs and represented employees, and **(3)** remedy. A plausible narrowing construction may avoid some constitutional difficulty; an implausible relocation of Congress's parenthesis is legislative revision. Partial affirmance with narrower relief, reversal, or remand for the proper scope are available without pretending that an all-Executive injunction and relief for the class are equivalent. Do not invalidate the congressional/judicial applications or decide every high-level employee case without an independent basis. No penalty, damages award, or plaintiff-specific concession is established here.

**Source sufficiency:** The lower majority, separate writings, official statutory text, Thomas preliminary opinion, and actual simulated Waters are sufficient for bounded merits modeling. Rehearing materials are locally preserved but only partly inspected in this pass; no additional rehearing proposition is necessary to this candidate. Original affidavits or briefs are needed only if a proposed disposition turns on facts or concessions not reported in the published opinions.

## 27. Harris v. Alabama — No. 93-7659

**Argument:** December 5, 1994. **Scheduled event:** February 22, 1995. **Review:** Ex parte Harris, 632 So. 2d 543 (Ala. June 25, 1993), affirming Harris v. State, 632 So. 2d 503 (Ala. Crim. App. June 12, 1992; rehearing November 25, 1992).

### Neutral question and the actual sentencing record

The question is whether Alabama's judicial imposition of death after an advisory jury recommends life satisfies constitutional capital-sentencing requirements where the statute requires consideration but prescribes no particular weight for that advice. Whether this record provides a constitutionally meaningful basis for the displacement is **disputed**, not an established absence of reasons.

The [predecision Joint Appendix](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385013_0537/micro_IA40385013_0537%205.%20Joint%20Appendix.pdf), downloaded and read in full, reproduces the amended sentencing order at printed pages 1–8 and the lower opinions at pages 9–111. The relevant [Court of Criminal Appeals opinion](https://static.case.law/so2d/632/html/0503-01.html) and [Alabama Supreme Court opinion](https://static.case.law/so2d/632/html/0543-01.html) are independently available. Only the predecision record and lower reasoning support the following account.

Harris was convicted of murder for pecuniary consideration or pursuant to a contract or hire, §13A-5-40(a)(7). On July 13, 1989, the jury recommended life without parole **seven to five**. The court held a further sentencing hearing, imposed death on August 11, 1989, and entered the reproduced amended order on January 23, 1990.

The judge stated that he considered the guilt and penalty evidence, presentence material, the further hearing, counsel's contentions, and the jury's recommendation. He excluded the victim-impact material from consideration. He found **one** statutory aggravator, pecuniary gain under §13A-5-49(6), and no other aggravator; the findings described Harris as having planned and financed the killing and as a principal beneficiary. He found **one** statutory mitigator, no significant criminal history, and nonstatutory mitigation including testimony about family, work, church, and community. He rejected minor participation on the stated role findings.

The weighing passage says the aggravator far outweighed all nonstatutory mitigation. That wording generated an argument that the statutory mitigator had not been weighed. The lower court examined the order's separate statutory finding and the Rule 10(f) clarification hearing and concluded the statutory mitigator was considered. The candidate preserves both the wording problem and the lower finding; it does not resolve the constitutional weight question through an invented omission.

The order expressly says the court also considered the jury's advisory verdict. It does not quantify the weight assigned to that advice or separately give a comparative explanation specifically labeled as the reason for rejecting the life recommendation. It nevertheless contains substantive findings and a weighing conclusion. Calling this record wholly unexplained would be false. Whether its explanation is adequate for the jury's statutory role and constitutional review remains open.

The Court of Criminal Appeals rejected the contention that Tedder's demanding Florida override standard is constitutionally compulsory, relying on Ex parte Jones. It also performed the statutory review for arbitrary influence, independently weighed the aggravating and mitigating circumstances, and considered proportionality, including the different treatment of other participants and their roles/cooperation. The Alabama Supreme Court affirmed. Separate opinions concerning other trial issues do not automatically expand the admitted override question. Findings in these opinions are not fresh Supreme Court factfinding.

### Statutory command and its distinction from performance

[Ex parte Jones, 456 So. 2d 380 (Ala. 1984)](https://static.case.law/so2d/456/html/0380-01.html), quotes §13A-5-47(e):

> In deciding upon the sentence, the trial court shall determine whether the aggravating circumstances it finds to exist outweigh the mitigating circumstances it finds to exist, and in doing so the trial court shall consider the recommendation of the jury contained in its advisory verdict, unless such a verdict has been waived pursuant to section 13A-5-46(a) or 13A-5-46(g). While the jury's recommendation concerning sentence shall be given consideration, it is not binding upon the court.

The obligation to consider, the exception when the advisory verdict is waived under the cited provisions, and the nonbinding character of the advice all belong to the rule. The statutory command does not itself establish how faithfully a particular judge performed it. The judge's statement of consideration and the appellate findings are record evidence on performance.

The lower opinions and appendix also establish written sentencing findings, a presentence process, and appellate sentence review. This pass has not independently recovered the complete contemporaneous text of every subsection of §§13A-5-47 and 13A-5-53; no paraphrase here is offered as a verbatim substitute. If a proposed holding turns on an unquoted exception in those subsections, obtain that text before relying on it. This limited gap does not erase the reproduced sentencing order or the review actually performed.

### Entering law and individual grounds

Predivergence Spaziano permits judicial sentencing notwithstanding an advisory jury's recommendation; its Florida safeguards and the scope of its rationale must be confronted before treating either unrestricted override or mandatory jury control as established law. Tedder supplies the stricter Florida rule requiring facts favoring death so clear and convincing that virtually no reasonable person could differ. Ex parte Jones rejected the contention that the Federal Constitution required Alabama to adopt that precise rule. That state holding does not settle the federal question.

Simulated **Espinosa** holds that a vague aggravator cannot enter a capital weighing process through influential jury advice. Its seven-Justice coalition included Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas as well as Stone and Blackmun. It distinguishes the presumption about a judge knowing valid law from the problem of an inadequately instructed jury whose recommendation affects sentence. It does not prescribe a mandatory weight for every Alabama recommendation. Simulated **Stringer** requires an actual lawful cure for an invalid aggravator in a weighing scheme; factor counting or the mere existence of valid eligibility is insufficient.

Simulated **Romano**, June 13, 1994, must replace the historical account of that case. Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, and Ginsburg, held that the irrelevant separate death decision on that record substantially invited the present jury to surrender responsibility and required sentence relief. O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, dissented on the ground that Caldwell concerns misleading descriptions of legal responsibility and that the record's accurate information and instructions did not create the asserted defect. These different treatments of sentencing responsibility are direct individual materials; neither is a holding about Alabama override.

Simulated **Tuilaepa/Proctor**, June 30, 1994, permits relevant individualized selection after lawful eligibility without fixed numerical weights or mandatory labels for every factor. O'Connor's controlling selection discussion has eight joins; Stevens and Ginsburg join Parts II–VI but not the entire opening framework. Souter separately distinguishes factual clarity from nonpropositional moral judgment. Scalia's broader objection to additional post-eligibility regulation is his own separate position, not an assumed Thomas join. Meaningful mitigation and the prohibitions on irrelevant or constitutionally forbidden considerations remain. The authority forecloses equating absence of numerical weights with automatic invalidity, but does not itself decide what reasons a judge must supply for declining jury advice.

For individual analysis, Stevens's responsibility concerns, O'Connor's allocation and selection distinctions, Scalia's separate position, Kennedy's and Thomas's actual joins, Souter's Romano reasoning and Tuilaepa qualification, and Ginsburg's actual joins provide concrete materials. No equivalent Breyer capital-override position was established here; he must not inherit Blackmun's views. Complete predivergence Spaziano and Baldwin opinions are locally available but were not fully inspected in this pass, so this candidate does not supply a new individual-joinder assertion from them.

### Candidate lawful paths and remedy boundaries

**Sustain the sentence/framework:** Judicial sentencing remains permitted; the statute commands consideration of advice, the judge made individualized findings, and independent appellate review examined the sentence. Neither the lack of fixed weights nor the jury's life preference necessarily makes the process arbitrary. The strongest counterargument is that merely reciting consideration can leave the jury's prescribed role without an ascertainable effect and permit inconsistent overrides. The opinion must explain why the actual findings and review sufficiently address that concern.

**Require further protection or explanation:** A court could conclude that meaningful consideration and review require more than this order gives, or adopt a more protective override rule within the admitted issue. That would require explaining why Spaziano's permission for a judicial sentencer and Tuilaepa's rejection of fixed weighting do not answer the distinct jury-advice/review problem. The strongest contrary argument is that this record already contains individualized reasons and appellate weighing, and that federal law allows the State to assign final selection to the judge. A holding must identify the missing protection in concrete terms, not describe all findings as nonexistent.

Relief on a sentencing defect ordinarily concerns the death sentence and an appropriately bounded resentencing or review remand. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor retrial of guilt follows without an additional legal basis. An across-the-board jury-sentencing requirement, a Tedder-type override limit, and a narrower requirement of reviewable reasons are different rules and require different supporting analysis.

**Source sufficiency:** The actual order, statutory command, and appellate reasoning permit neutral analysis of this admitted issue. Unrecovered portions of the complete trial record become indispensable only if a proposed ground turns on a disputed fact not resolved in these sources. The question is not blocked simply because the entire original litigation file has not been reconstructed.

## 28. Jerome B. Grubart, Inc. v. Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co.; City of Chicago v. Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. — Nos. 93-762 and 93-1094

**Argument:** October 12, 1994. **Scheduled event:** February 22, 1995. **Participation:** Stevens and Breyer do not participate. **Review:** In re Complaint of Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co., 3 F.3d 225 (7th Cir. August 24, 1993), reversing dismissal of the limitation proceeding.

### Facts, allegations, and posture

The [complete Seventh Circuit opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/3/html/0225-01.html) describes Great Lakes' contract to replace clusters of bridge pilings, or dolphins, at five Chicago River bridges. Equipment operated from barges on the river. Claimants alleged that piling work near the Kinzie Street bridge damaged an underlying freight tunnel. The tunnel later breached on April 13, 1992, flooding downtown property. The opinion reports that maritime traffic was closed for more than a month. The flood and closure are record events; negligent vessel causation and ultimate liability remain allegations to be proved.

Great Lakes sought exoneration or limitation under 46 U.S.C. App. §§181–196 and impleaded Chicago for indemnity or contribution, alleging failures concerning disclosure, maintenance, and repair of the tunnel. Grubart presented a claim. The district court dismissed for lack of admiralty jurisdiction and alternatively for failure to state a limitation claim based on privity or knowledge and a personal-contract theory. The Seventh Circuit reversed both grounds and remanded. A decision on jurisdiction does not resolve either the casualty's merits or the owner's right to a cap.

### Governing rules and the reserved question

Section 1333(1) supplies original admiralty jurisdiction while preserving the saving-to-suitors qualification. The Admiralty Extension Act, 46 U.S.C. App. §740, extends admiralty to damage or injury caused by a vessel on navigable water even when consummated on land. As used in the lower opinion, ordinary proximate causation remains necessary; an invented immediate-impact requirement or fixed temporal/distance cutoff does not follow from the statute.

Executive Jet, Foremost, and [Sisson v. Ruby, 497 U.S. 358 (1990)](https://static.case.law/us/497/html/0358-01.html) supply the location and maritime-connection framework. Sisson examines the potential disruption of maritime commerce posed by the general type of incident and the substantial relationship of the activity giving rise to it to traditional maritime activity. Its marina storage/maintenance holding is not limited to collisions between commercial vessels. Gutierrez shows why ultimate land injury does not itself defeat the statutory extension.

**Sisson footnote 3 expressly reserves the situation involving instrumentalities with different relationships to traditional maritime activity.** The supplied packet's instruction not to require each defendant separately to undertake maritime work is therefore not already the answer supplied by Sisson. Whether the maritime activity of an alleged tortfeasor suffices in this mixed-actor casualty is part of the present legal analysis. A court may adopt that rule, but must support it and define its reach.

The Seventh Circuit characterized the incident by the vessel operation and damage in the waterway, not exclusively by the final flooded basements. It found potential disruption and a traditional maritime relationship in piling installation, including its functions for bridges, vessels, and navigation. It did not need to choose one exclusive purpose for every dolphin at the jurisdictional stage. It rejected a free-ranging policy-factor inquiry that would displace Sisson. The opposing characterization stresses the freight tunnel, remote land damage, and Chicago's nonmaritime conduct. Choosing a level of generality must neither erase the vessel's alleged causal role nor convert all activity involving floating equipment into maritime activity by definition.

The lower court separately held that corporate privity or knowledge and the relevant employee's managerial authority could not be conclusively resolved on the undeveloped record. It distinguished tort limitation from an asserted personal contractual obligation to Chicago. The identity/status of the contracting actor and the contract's scope may matter. The owner ultimately bears the statutory burden on lack of privity or knowledge after the relevant liability showing; jurisdiction supplies no finding satisfying it. The Seventh Circuit did not reach whether the Limitation Act independently supports jurisdiction because it found §1333 sufficient.

### Individual writings and actual simulated maritime law

O'Connor and Kennedy joined Sisson's majority. Scalia, joined by White, concurred only in the judgment and preferred a simpler rule for a wrong involving a vessel engaged in normal maritime activity on navigable waters; he opposed the additional case-by-case potential-disruption requirement. He did not thereby define every floating object as a vessel or resolve the independent Limitation Act ground. Thus the ordinary framework and Scalia's possible alternative cannot be merged into an invented unanimous rationale.

Simulated Gizoni concerns seaman status and the effect of LHWCA benefits, not this jurisdictional test. Its actual joins include O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. Simulated McDermott and Boca Grande unanimously settle defined maritime settlement/contribution issues and reserve independent indemnity or limitation questions; they are not a ruling that every mixed-party dispute is maritime. Thomas and Ginsburg's joins there supply their actual positions on those distinct questions, not a foreordained jurisdictional answer.

Simulated **American Dredging v. Miller**, February 23, 1994, is particularly useful to avoid conflating admiralty jurisdiction with exclusive federal adjudication. Scalia's controlling statutory treatment of a competent state forum was joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg. Its separate Jensen analysis had six joins, without Stevens. The decision allows the state forum's rule concerning forum non conveniens within its stated bounds; it does not abolish substantive maritime uniformity or alter admiralty jurisdiction. Souter's classification qualification remains his own. The candidate does not infer additional positions from Kennedy's or Thomas's nonjoins. These authorities are in the maritime entering-law volume.

### Two paths and disposition components

**Jurisdiction path:** The vessel operation on navigable water proximately caused the alleged land injury, the general incident had potential to disrupt maritime commerce, and the relevant activity had a substantial traditional maritime relationship. Explain why the mixed nonmaritime conduct does or does not alter that conclusion in light of Sisson's reservation. The strongest contrary argument is that overly broad descriptions collapse the connection requirement into the locality requirement. Scalia's alternative vessel-based analysis may independently support the judgment but is not an automatic join in Sisson's framework. Affirming permits the proceeding to continue; it does not establish negligence, indemnity, or limitation.

**No §1333 jurisdiction path:** Identify the specific missing locality/causation or maritime-connection element, using a defensible characterization of the incident and activity. The strongest contrary argument is that the statutory extension and the alleged vessel operation cannot be nullified by focusing only on where the damage finally occurred. If §1333 fails, address or remand the independent Limitation Act jurisdiction contention and any other supported jurisdictional channel before ordering a categorical dismissal.
