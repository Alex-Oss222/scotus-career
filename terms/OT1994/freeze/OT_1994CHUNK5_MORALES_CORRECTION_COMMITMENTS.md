# Morales — independently frozen enhanced provisional commitments

No. 93-1462; merits event 1995-04-25. Cutoff 1995-04-25. Read with `OT_1994CHUNK5_MORALES_CORRECTION_NEUTRAL.md`. This handoff precedes access to the historical Morales comparator and any current Stone supplement. It supersedes no earlier handoff by editing it: it is a fresh modeling product on corrected neutral sources and binding Cavanaugh. No historical authorship or lineup is used. These are decisional conclusions and adversarial tests, not invented conference dialogue.

## Common reach and bounded components

All eight associates reach the preserved Ex Post Facto Clause question in the admitted habeas posture. Cavanaugh's §1983 dispute supplies no current vehicle obstacle. No later application or completed relief establishes mootness. All leave the rejected sentencing and false-information claims undisturbed and decline to decide the five-year amendment. All distinguish hearing-frequency relief from a command granting parole. Every disposition below concerns the Ninth Circuit's ex post facto judgment and prospective crime-date consideration remedy alone.

## Justice Stevens

**Commitment:** Affirm the hearing-frequency judgment on a record-bounded opportunity ground. Annual consideration was legally available at the crime date; the three-year deferral actually removes two ordinary occasions, and no meaningful later ordinary review is established. The predictive finding is relevant but does not itself replace those occasions.

**Own authorities:** His join in Weaver, 450 U.S.24, protects opportunities for reduced custody without vested release rights; his join in Miller, 482 U.S.423, rejects proof of a necessarily lower sentence. His Collins concurrence, 497 U.S.52–61, distinguishes real protection from a capricious windfall. His published Cavanaugh join establishes a meaningful hearing opportunity, while retaining the individualized-postponement reservation.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Reverse by distinguishing an individualized prediction from Cavanaugh's automatic class-wide interval. Jackson's separate reasons and review make that a serious lawful path. Its strongest affirmative premise is that an agency can determine that another hearing is unnecessary. The objection is that this decision concerns a future period during which ordinary statutory suitability factors may change, not a finding of legal incapacity to release. Stevens does not treat gruesome facts as a constitutional substitute for later consideration or dismiss shared facts as unlawful duplication. Affirmance's strongest objection is overextension to harmless calendar changes; the response confines it to complete loss of the ordinary crime-date release avenue during entire intervening years on this record.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join the neutral allocation as applied, with claimant proving meaningful loss and the proponent supporting an asserted substitute; reject an actual-release probability prerequisite. Refuse a rule invalidating every scheduling adjustment, requiring wholly different postponement facts, or treating discretionary release as constitutionally guaranteed. A genuinely established timely ordinary substitute is a narrower lawful distinction, reserved rather than disproved categorically. Remedy: retain the lower crime-date hearing-frequency direction, without ordering release or a particular date.

## Justice O'Connor

**Commitment:** Affirm on the same bounded opportunity loss, with an explicit reservation that individualized postponement is not categorically unconstitutional. The present three-year prediction and initial-review safeguards do not establish continued access to a meaningful occasion for applying the unchanged release criteria to later developments.

**Own authorities:** Miller is her unanimous opinion distinguishing a binding adverse legal change from flexible guideposts despite unchanged outer limits. Her Collins join excludes a free-standing procedural-disadvantage test. Her Cavanaugh opinion protects the mandatory occasion without requiring proof of parole and expressly leaves individualized findings open. That reservation demands examination of California's actual protections, not automatic extension of Roller.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Reverse on California's narrow offense trigger, annual default, hearing protections, separately reasoned continuing-risk finding, and initial review. Jackson demonstrates an additional legal decision rather than mere administrative preference. The strongest objection to affirmance is that it would render Cavanaugh's reservation empty unless every valid postponement has reopening. The resolution is narrower: this record does not establish a materially equivalent retained ordinary avenue, while the earlier prediction alone is being asserted as conclusive proof that future hearings could not matter. No universal interim-hearing mandate is adopted. The objection to reversal is its need to equate reasonable prediction with the impossibility or legal irrelevance of later suitability. Miller and Cavanaugh prevent that shortcut.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join claimant's showing of crime-date opportunity, retrospective loss, and meaningful custody link, and require evidentiary support when the State invokes a substitute. This is not a general State burden to disprove constitutional harm. Refuse automatic invalidity based solely on any reduced frequency or an undefined substantial-rights rule beyond punishment. Minimum acceptable affirming formulation recognizes the separate predictive requirement and permitted overlap, and leaves proven distinct individualized systems open. Remedy: affirm only the opportunity remedy; no revisiting denial of parole or later law.

## Justice Scalia

**Commitment:** Reverse the hearing-frequency judgment and remand consistently with rejection of this ex post facto claim. Distinguish Cavanaugh at its express individualized-postponement reservation. California conditions postponement on a separately justified prediction at an actual hearing, subject to review, for a defined class; it does not automatically withdraw alternate hearings by class membership alone.

**Own authorities:** His Miller join distinguishes binding sentencing-range increases from discretionary guidance. His Collins join requires a crime-or-punishment change rather than every adverse procedure. His Cavanaugh Part B separate, joined by Thomas, requires further demonstrated punitive operation for hearing frequency; that view is noncontrolling, so the present rule cannot simply override the Cavanaugh majority.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Affirm because a reasoned prediction cannot restore an omitted legally mandatory opportunity and ordinary review remains unproved. The strongest objection to reversal is that release discretion was also unchanged in Cavanaugh. The permitted distinction is the additional legally constrained, individual, separately reviewable prediction, not the bare fact of discretion. Cavanaugh reserved that mechanism. The counterargument to affirmance is that it turns annual default into an immutable occasion irrespective of the lawful futility finding, effectively deciding the reservation without demonstrating this scheme's increased-punishment operation. On these facts he accepts the distinction.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join the ordinary proposition that a party invoking a factual mechanism must substantiate it. He does not join an allocation that, once retrospective omission is shown, requires the State to prove equivalent review or no harm as the only way to defeat the claim. He accepts Cavanaugh's no-proof-of-actual-release rule within its controlling circumstances; he does not require such proof here either, but requires a supported punitive operation beyond the legally controlled scheduling choice. No reopening finding or cohort statistic is needed to his sufficient ground. Refuse a conclusive assertion that statutory labeling immunizes punishment or that parole never falls within the Clause. Remedy removes the Ninth Circuit's frequency relief on this claim, leaving release discretion, other rejected claims, and later amendments untouched.

## Justice Kennedy

**Commitment:** Reverse and remand on a confined individualized-postponement distinction. Accept the Board's separately stated continued-risk reasons as relevant to the statutory period; do not decide that all future postponements, perfunctory reasons, or every later statute are constitutional.

**Own authorities:** His Collins join confines the federal prohibition to punishment rather than general procedural disadvantage. His Cavanaugh merits join protects the ordinary occasion in the absence of retained ordinary review, but the same published opinion reserves individualized findings. Both positions must be respected. Jackson's state-law construction supplies the concrete constraint absent from the unqualified withdrawal, not binding federal constitutional reasoning.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Affirm because the actual omitted occasions matter independently of present chances and future rehabilitation cannot be foreclosed by a prediction. That is the strongest contrary argument, and Kennedy does not answer it with a guaranteed nineteen-year matrix or an invented reopening mechanism. His sufficient distinction is a decision directed to why public-safety risk is likely to continue during the particular intervening period, separately explained and reviewable, with annual consideration retained unless statutory conditions are satisfied. Affirmance's strongest weakness is treating that additional judgment as legally meaningless despite the express Cavanaugh reservation. Reversal's strongest weakness is allowing the agency's conclusion to defeat the federal inquiry by fiat; its scope therefore leaves review of actual punitive operation and defective or categorical implementation available.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join the neutral evidentiary allocation as a method of identifying reliable premises, including requiring proof of an asserted timely substitute. Do not join a rule making equivalent interim review indispensable to every reasoned individualized postponement. No automatic State burden to prove no release is possible; no claimant requirement to prove actual release. Minimum accepted reversal recognizes Cavanaugh and confines the ground to these legally constrained reasons. Remedy: remove the ordered crime-date frequency relief on this claim and return for proceedings consistent with that holding, without a release ruling or merits reopening of rejected components.

## Justice Souter

**Commitment:** Affirm the record-specific frequency relief. A prediction at denial, even separately reviewable, does not supply an occasion to consider later suitability when the record proves actual withdrawal of ordinary annual opportunities and fails to establish ordinary timely reconsideration on developments.

**Own authority:** His published Cavanaugh merits join adopts the opportunity rule and the reservation of individualized scheduling. His prior published narrow approaches do not license a probability formula borrowed from unrelated doctrine. The relevant foundation is Cavanaugh's meaningful link to discretionary release, with Weaver and Miller as that opinion adopted them. His present application gives legal effect to both the reservation and the California requirement: initial individualized protections are relevant but insufficient here.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Reverse because separately stated predictive reasons plus initial review materially distinguish automatic withholding. The strongest argument is that a finding of likely continuing public-safety risk is a genuine additional statutory choice; Souter accepts that. Its remaining weakness is treating “not reasonable to expect” as a conclusive fact about later developments and construing review of the initial prediction as later reconsideration. Those propositions lack the necessary sources. The objection to affirmance is demanding an unchanged annual ritual even for demonstrably futile hearings. His answer confines the holding to the meaningful release avenue actually lost and does not decide how a demonstrably different schedule or retained ordinary mechanism operates.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join the neutral allocation in full as bounded to establishing reliable operational premises. Claimant retains the constitutional burden, satisfied here by mandatory opportunity, actual loss, and ordinary release dependency; claimant need not negate every imaginable collateral possibility. Refuse a rule treating mere permission for longer intervals or shared reasons as enough, or universal numerical thresholds of risk. A source-supported timely substitute would require a distinct application. Remedy: affirm crime-date consideration relief only, with no parole-date prediction or instruction that a later hearing must favor Morales.

## Justice Thomas

**Commitment:** Reverse and remand on the legally constrained individualized-postponement ground, without overruling Cavanaugh. No contrary provision-level vote is assigned on unpresented later enactments.

**Own authority:** His join in Scalia's Cavanaugh Part B distinguishes mandatory credits and binding sentencing ranges from discretionary release, and demands further punitive operation. That separate position is evidence of his reasoning, not law. Binding Cavanaugh answers its own fixed-withdrawal setting; its express reservation leaves this materially different statute open. Collins's punishment limitation remains binding, although Thomas was not a member when it was issued.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Affirm by extending Cavanaugh's opportunity principle to the two omitted occasions. The strongest objection to reversal is that a separately explained prediction is still a legal authorization to withhold consideration, and parole discretion could have been exercised during a hearing under former law. Thomas accepts that as a real argument rather than requiring vested rights. The distinguishing force is the actual individual hearing and statutory prediction directed to the intervening period, reviewed separately, rather than a universal loss imposed on every member of an offense class. The strongest objection to affirmance is treating that reserved mechanism as automatically unconstitutional without further demonstrated punitive operation beyond missed appearances.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Accept source substantiation of any claimed substitute; reject a constitutional burden shift under which unexplained absence of an interim mechanism itself establishes violation for every individualized schedule. Require a meaningful link to increased punishment rather than every potential procedural benefit; do not demand proof of what the parole vote would have been. Refuse reliance on unproved reopening or fixed matrix dates. Minimum compatible opinion distinguishes Cavanaugh expressly and keeps it binding within its scope. Remedy rejects the present ex post facto frequency claim; no release, ruling on later law, or re-adjudication of rejected claims.

## Justice Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Affirm the limited hearing-frequency remedy in the habeas posture. The legally guaranteed occasion is actually omitted; ordinary release depends on consideration, and no proved ordinary timely alternative survives. Her prior §1983 objection is absent here and changes neither the question nor her substantive premises.

**Own authority:** Her Cavanaugh separate conditionally agrees with the narrow constitutional conclusion and explains the distinction between rigid loss and guidance. Its discussion of her earlier Warren dissent identifies an opportunity to establish actual rigid operation, not a categorical invalidation of guidelines. The operative published writing supports examination of real administration. Here the statute and Morales's own maximum deferral establish the lost occasions; no system-wide maximum-deferral finding is supplied or required.

**Alternative and strongest objection:** Reverse on Jackson's separate predictive-decision protection or vacate for more evidence of administration. The strongest objection to affirmance is that Cavanaugh reserved individualized postponement and California provides a reasoned choice, not facially automatic delay. She recognizes that protection, permits factual overlap, and does not presume a system-wide practice. The obstacle to reversal is that a reviewable past prediction has not been shown to retain an ordinary later avenue. The objection to remand is that speculative substitute practice should not defeat otherwise demonstrated limited relief; existing record establishes this actual loss without a promised release date. She resolves that objection by affirming at this bounded scope rather than announcing facial invalidity of every permitted postponement.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join claimant's offense-date, loss, and meaningful-link showing; join substantiation of a replacement by its proponent without converting that support obligation into an ultimate State burden. Refuse the use of habeas posture to revive an actual-parole requirement rejected by Cavanaugh, or the use of Warren to invent factual findings. A legally/practically available and timely ordinary process would support a genuinely different case. Remedy leaves only the Ninth Circuit's crime-date frequency direction intact; no entitlement to release, general vehicle ruling, or later-amendment adjudication.

## Justice Breyer

**Commitment:** Vacate the ex post facto frequency judgment and remand for a limited assessment of the individualized scheme's legally authorized and actual operation under Cavanaugh. Reject both the lower court's language covering any retrospective reduction and the State's conclusive-prediction argument. Preserve consideration-only relief as an available remedy if the Cavanaugh injury is established; do not issue a final no-violation ruling on counsel's factual assertions.

**Authority and individual-source limit:** Breyer was not a participant in Cavanaugh, Weaver, Miller, or Collins. Their controlling rules bind him, but this context supplies no directly pertinent own prior ex post facto writing. No inherited Blackmun vote or ideology substitutes for one. The provisional conclusion rests on the particular legal reservation and sparse operational record, and remains explicitly subject to historical reconciliation's evidence rather than claiming documented personal adherence to an unsupplied opinion.

**Alternative and strongest objections:** Affirm on the actually lost meaningful opportunity; strongest reason is that a complete future occasion has been removed and the State cannot manufacture reopening. Reverse on legally constrained prediction; strongest reason is that the provision genuinely distinguishes current unsuitability from continuing risk, retains annual default, and limits postponement. The objection to remand is that missing data are neither always necessary nor a warrant to postpone established rights. His bounded response is that the reserved individualized distinction cannot be resolved by a broad logical equation between fewer hearings and greater punishment or by the opposite equation between prediction and no harm. Existing sources establish initial-review constraints but do not settle ordinary later reconsideration or actual release-timing claims; a limited inquiry can address that operation without speculative parole adjudication.

**Allocation/join barrier:** Join the neutral allocation, expressly leaving ultimate constitutional proof with claimant and excluding proof of actual parole as a prerequisite. A substitute's proponent must substantiate legal and timely practical availability. Refuse either automatic invalidation of every individualized delay or a universal equivalence requirement regardless of demonstrated operation. No cohort statistic, guaranteed release lag, or Board reopening practice is found. Minimum affirmance-compatible revision would establish why this particular loss remains meaningful notwithstanding the individualized constraint without treating unknowns as findings; minimum reversal-compatible revision would substantiate why the distinct mechanism removes the Cavanaugh injury without demanding proof of actual release. Remedy: vacate this component only; remand for bounded operation inquiry under the stated legal rule, with prospective annual-consideration relief available as justified, no immediate release, and other rejected claims undisturbed.
