The verifier read the complete filing text, including preliminary pages, body pages 1–29, and notes, checked the PDF text, and visually inspected the image containing printed pages 10–11. It did not merely infer the remainder of the filing from its opening pages. The filing is advocacy by amici, not a new party concession or a Supreme Court disposition.

Printed page **10**, quoting NASD Rule 21(f)(5), states:

> [T]he requirements of subparagraphs (f)(1) through (4) shall apply only to new agreements signed by an existing or new customer of a member after September 7, 1989.

Thus the rule uses **after**, not on or after. It covers new agreements signed after September 7 by existing customers as well as new customers; it does not merely classify customers by when they first opened an account. The quoted transition expressly covers subparagraph (f)(4). Printed page **7** says that agreements signed **on or before September 7, 1989** are outside Rule 21(f)(4). Printed page **12** applies the distinction to this agreement, executed in **1985**. The neutral packet independently reports October 1985 from the lower record. No new post-September-7-1989 agreement, amendment constituting such a new agreement, or renewal is established.

Printed pages **6 and 10** quote the pertinent part of Rule 21(f)(4), prohibiting an agreement condition that limits a party's ability to file a claim in arbitration or the arbitrators' ability to make an award. Page 6 reproduces that clause in reporting the lower analysis. The complete transition quotation is on **page 10**, not page 6 as the unverified temporal supplement's source pointer suggested. Pages 7 and 12 provide the express application. PDF image 10 contains printed pages 10–11; its visible rule number and date agree with the extracted text.

**Verified neutral conclusion:** Rule 21(f)(4) is not an operative regulatory prohibition governing this 1985 agreement. The contract's reference to institutional rules then in effect cannot silently discard an incorporated rule's own express temporal limitation. The later arbitration hearings do not by themselves transform the earlier agreement into a new agreement signed after the cutoff. On the established record, the regulatory prohibition cannot independently invalidate an otherwise effective contractual punitive-damages restriction or establish that these arbitrators possessed punitive authority.

## Sources that remain distinct

1. **Rule 21(f)(4), subject to (f)(5):** its operative regulatory force is unavailable here because of the agreement date. The amicus argues that it precludes inconsistent contractual restrictions in covered later agreements. The present correction does not decide every later agreement, announce a new general SRO-preemption holding, or establish that this particular 1985 contract excludes punitive damages.
2. **NASD Code of Arbitration Procedure §41(e):** the award-content requirement refers to a summary of damages and other relief. The brief discusses it on printed page 24. This is not express language granting punitive damages in every case. The absence of a remedial prohibition can bear on reading the institutional arbitration selection without becoming a freestanding affirmative entitlement.
3. **The Arbitrator's Manual:** page 24 reports its advice that punitive damages can be a permissible remedy. That explanatory guidance is not automatically incorporated binding contract text, is not Rule 21(f)(4), and cannot override an actual contractual restriction merely by being described as an NASD publication.
4. **Contract and FAA questions:** the general New York governing-law clause, the broad controversy submission, institutional selection, Garrity, and FAA §10(a)(4) remain the substantive interpretive dispute. Removing the inapplicable rule does not itself establish either reading of the complete contract. A contract-whole reading, a properly bounded review-of-award theory, and a construction enforcing an actual remedial restriction remain analytically distinct from regulatory compulsion.

The complete amicus confirms why the temporal correction does not exhaust the case: after disclaiming Rule 21(f)(4)'s application, it develops contract interpretation and arbitral-review arguments at pages 12–28. Its argument is evidence of an available litigating position, not proof of an arbitrator's unreported reasoning or a party's concession. Its references to post-divergence cases, including punitive-damages cases, are not substitutes for the repository's actual simulated law. No general delegation doctrine, constitutional waiver, damages-excessiveness ruling, or independently dispositive waiver is adopted here.
