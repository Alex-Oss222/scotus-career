### Constitutional status — special creation, governmental objectives and enduring majority appointment

When the Government creates a corporation by special law to accomplish governmental objectives and retains enduring authority to select a majority of its governing directors, the institution is governmental for purposes of the First Amendment; the statutory nonagency label cannot settle the constitutional obligations owed to persons affected by its actions. Here that authority is the permanent statutory governance arrangement, not a temporary investment or merely the current coincidence of government share ownership. The statutory creation, national transportation purposes and retained governmental majority selection satisfy the combined rule for Amtrak.

This bounded formulation makes precise the refreshed model's continuing-public-governance condition without enlarging it. The nine-member board is not composed of nine direct Presidential appointments: it includes the Secretary, five other direct Presidential selections under the statutory categories, two selections by the governmental preferred shareholder, and the president chosen inside the corporate structure. The permanent governmental majority does not depend on misdescribing those routes. Corporate officers may exercise delegated operating discretion without turning the governmental institution private. An additional external federal order to reject this particular advertisement is unnecessary once the institution itself satisfies the structural-status rule.

The combined rule does not make a federal charter, subsidy, regulation, public benefit, property ownership, ordinary appointment or temporary investment independently sufficient. Edmonson and McCollum illuminate government acting through legal machinery but do not make billboard advertising an exclusive public function. Hess addresses immunity and supplies no equivalence between denial of immunity and constitutional privacy. Red Cross concerns charter jurisdiction. Congress's classification retains its statutory operation; this decision creates no universal agency status, sovereign-immunity rule, power to bind the Treasury, tort-liability regime or charter-jurisdiction holding.
