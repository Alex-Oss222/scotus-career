## Threshold, scope and common legal premises

All eight accept the established merits-review channel from the final en banc judgment and reach Count II. Chabad itself requested access and was denied. That denial supplies the direct speech injury, the State the causal actor, and lawful access relief a possible remedy. Review does not depend on standing to object to someone else's religious display or on a reconstructed damages claim. The established merits posture supplies no new certiorari poll or invented grant date.

The eight-day Chanukah 1991 period has ended. All eight apply Pinette's unanimous short-duration and same-claimant-recurrence rule, grounded in Weinstein, together with Murphy v. Hunt's requirement of a reasonable expectation of recurrence. Chabad's repeated applications, continuing challenge and its confirmed representation at panel argument that it would apply again establish that expectation for this claimant. The full litigation cannot realistically be completed during the annual holiday. This is stronger than a generalized assertion that some organization may ask for a display. The reported statement is not a finding that a later application actually occurred. No universal promise, document or new-season application is required, and no seasonal dispute is automatically justiciable. The contrary mootness path fails on these concrete party-specific facts.

All eight were part of Pinette's threshold holding. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter's earlier Lee threshold joins supply additional eligible evidence for insisting on real exposure, without making Lee's different factual stipulation necessary here. For Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer no different personal rule is invented. All distinguish recurrence from relief: the exception does not issue or revive a 1991 permit, guarantee indefinite future access or settle an application under a later valid rule.

Count I, the outdoor plaza, is outside appellate review. Chabad conceded State summary judgment based on the neutrally enforced outdoor unattended-object rule. The model neither reverses that concession nor casts eight new merits votes to uphold the outdoor policy. Count II alone concerns the Rotunda, where that ban did not apply and no comparable neutral indoor prohibition is established. The disputed appeal is the en banc reversal of State summary judgment, not a detailed injunction already ordering access with or without conditions.

The Rotunda's actual policy and practice establish a designated forum for diverse private expression, including comparable temporary unattended displays. State ownership, the interior location and the presence of governmental speakers do not by themselves destroy that established forum classification. Perry gives genuine designated forums the relevant open-forum speech protection. A valid later closure or neutral operational restriction is possible but has not occurred by assumption. Religious content does not strip the private display of protection. All eight accept that a content-based exclusion requires the applicable compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring justification; Stevens finds it supplied here, while the other seven reject State summary judgment on different grounds.

Pinette's seven-Justice common holding rejects the categorical assertion that governmental proximity necessarily makes this kind of genuinely private access establishment. Its actual outdoor proposal and offered safeguard matter; it neither immunizes every nominal forum nor finds a completed sign adequate. Neither its four-Justice contextual rationale nor its three-Justice private-access rationale is a controlling composite under Marks. Lemon, Allegheny, Widmar and the actual Lamb's Chapel holding remain relevant. Kiryas Joel concerns governmental allocation, Lee preserves independent sponsorship and pressure grounds, and Zobrest supplies no aid-merits holding. No later law fills the attribution question.

The indoor Rotunda, fifteen-foot isolated symbol, eight-day duration, generally unattended presence and mixed governmental/private use create a substantial attribution question. The yellow greeting identifying Chabad does not expressly disclaim State sponsorship. The offer at argument to erect an appropriate disclaimer is a separate fact; no final words, dimensions, placement or effectiveness have been adjudicated. The State's Christmas tree would arrive after Chanukah, so no combined secular display is assumed. The proposed indoor display was not already installed. These facts distinguish the present question from both an abstract forum rule and review of a completed sign's performance.

## Stevens

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach Count II; reverse the en banc judgment and restore the district court's summary judgment for Georgia on that count. Leave Count I untouched. No new season-specific permit, general forum closure or removal order follows.

**Adopted establishment path:** The prominent, isolated religious symbol inside the central public area of the Capitol conveys governmental religious affiliation notwithstanding private title and ordinary admission of private speakers. His actual Pinette dissent treats governmental presentation as capable of producing that injury despite identification; the interior placement and government's own use make the concern stronger here. His Allegheny separate writing and simulated Lee and Kiryas Joel positions support independent sponsorship scrutiny. His Widmar concurrence and Lamb's Chapel joins protect identifiable nonsponsored access but do not establish an entitlement to every unattended object in this setting.

**Alternative access path and strongest objections:** The actual designated forum, comparable large unattended displays and first-come policy substantially support Chabad. Pinette now binds at its limited holding, even though he dissented there. Stevens distinguishes this core interior presentation rather than proposing to ignore Pinette or making ownership alone dispositive. A greeting identifying Chabad does not dispel the governmental affiliation. The offered express disclaimer and other attribution measures present the strongest narrower alternative: they could preserve speech without official sponsorship in many settings. He nevertheless concludes that this particular large isolated presentation in the Capitol's central space communicates an institutional association that identification alone cannot undo. That is a legal attribution conclusion on the described proposal, not a finding that a tested sign failed or that Chabad is incapable of private speech.

**Speech, tailoring and remedy:** He treats avoidance of that concrete attribution as sufficient to exclude this proposal and concludes that the offered attribution alternative would not cure it. No factual remand to measure an uncreated sign is needed under his adopted ground. A materially different location or presentation could present another application; he does not announce an exclusion of all religious events or displays throughout public property. He restores judgment for Georgia on Count II rather than affirming a new ban on every possible configuration.

**Join barriers:** He can join reachability, forum classification, protected religious speech, the scrutiny rule and temporal/scope limitations. He refuses a holding that State summary judgment fails here, a universal private-forum exemption or a direction to compel this display on an attribution promise. A contextual label alone does not resolve his objection; materially different presentation would be necessary. He does not adopt Ginsburg's distinct concern about what a future injunction must secure as his sufficient ground.

## O'Connor

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach; affirm the en banc reversal of State summary judgment on Count II and return for further proceedings consistent with a bounded contextual and narrow-tailoring decision. Do not affirm its complete categorical forum rationale or grant a retrospective permit.

**Adopted contextual path:** Her Allegheny concurrence and Mergens Part III require attention to the actual setting and private/governmental distinction. Her simulated Lamb's Chapel, Lee and Kiryas Joel positions preserve that inquiry. Most immediately, she joined Pinette's contextual Part IV and Souter's less-restrictive-attribution concurrence. The indoor location matters; a reasonable observer knows ordinary private use without being presumed to know every permit history. The State cannot establish the necessity of total exclusion merely from the Capitol setting where it actually admits diverse comparable private exhibits and the applicant is willing to clarify attribution.

**Alternatives and objections:** Scalia's broader path would make genuine neutral access independently sufficient. She rejects that categorical extension because actual government adoption can emerge despite formal eligibility. Georgia's strongest exclusion path relies on the isolated central symbol, the State's own speaking role and the identifying greeting's failure expressly to disclaim sponsorship. She takes that concern seriously: the greeting is not an adequate disclaimer by definition, and the offer is not a completed safeguard. But the district court omitted the separate question why effective clarification or appropriately identified private-display space could not avoid complete exclusion. On this summary-judgment record, Georgia has not demonstrated that all such measures fail. The alternative of sustaining its judgment therefore fails the State's burden; it does not follow that every proposed future sign succeeds.

**Remedy and joins:** The operative result is reversal of State summary judgment, followed by determination of actual relief. For this interior proposal, any eventual access decree must provide adequate dissociation where necessary to prevent the contextual endorsement; an appropriate clear and prominent disclaimer or an adequately identified private-display area are possible alternatives, not cumulative commands. No exact language or universal labeling rule is prescribed. The court must not assume the old greeting performs that function or treat the present affirmance as an unconditioned final access injunction. She joins the limited failure-of-categorical-defense proposition and the contextual/tailoring rationale. She refuses forum immunity, a finding that unspecified signage already works or an inflexible requirement that every religious display carry a sign. Preserving the actual remedial inquiry and its alternatives resolves her joins.
