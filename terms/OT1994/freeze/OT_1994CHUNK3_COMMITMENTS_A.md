## 26. United States v. National Treasury Employees Union — No. 93-1170

**Argument:** November 8, 1994. **Event cutoff:** February 22, 1995, common pre-day law. **Judgment under review:** 990 F.2d 1271, affirming an injunction covering all Executive Branch employees. The certified class covers affected employees below GS–16; named plaintiff Peter G. Crane is GS–16 and outside that class. Not every plaintiff's expressive activity is unrelated to official work.

### Statutory and record premises common to the commitments

Section 501(b) provides: “An individual may not receive any honorarium while that individual is a Member, officer or employee.” Section 505(2) covers government officers and employees **except special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202**. Section 501(a)'s separate outside-income ceiling for specified high-level noncareer officers is not decided.

Section 505(3) defines an honorarium as a payment of money or anything of value for an appearance, speech, or article, including a series **if the subject matter is directly related to official duties or payment is because of government status**. That parenthetical nexus qualification expressly concerns a series; it is not already a general nexus qualification governing every single appearance, speech, or article. The definition excludes actual and necessary travel expenses of the individual and one relative to the extent paid or reimbursed by another person and otherwise reduces the amount by those expenses to the extent not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses are transportation, lodging, and meals while away from residence or principal place of employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment instead made on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received by the individual, **only up to $2,000 for each payment**, and forbids this route to an organization from which the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child, or dependent relative derives financial benefit. It therefore preserves a charitable outlet, not compensation for the employee's expressive labor. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and permits a penalty no greater than $10,000 or the compensation received, whichever is greater. Under §504(b), a §503 entity may render a written advisory opinion. Protection from the subsection (a) sanction covers the recipient and another covered individual whose facts are indistinguishable in all material aspects **for action after issuance, in good faith, in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings**. Neither advisory availability nor charitable payment is a discretionary wholesale exemption from the substantive rule.

The lower record describes protected expression on subjects including religion, history, art, dance, and the environment. Many payments lack a duties, status, or payor-dealings connection; some activities are closer to official work. The GAO and commission materials support the Government's genuine corruption and appearance concerns. A conclusion that they do not justify this ban's reach is a judgment about constitutional fit, not a finding that corruption never occurs. Conversely, preventive authority is not proof that every unrelated payment causes disruption. Reimbursement does not replace payment for expressive effort, and the continued availability of unpaid speech does not establish zero speech burden.

All eight associates reach the admitted statutory/constitutional question and remedial scope. None adds a damages or civil-penalty adjudication, a congressional/judicial-employee ruling, or a constitutional ruling on §501(a). All decline to transplant the series parenthesis into every payment as an avoidance construction: the plausible-interpretation limit cannot be satisfied by writing a new general nexus rule into §505(3). This agreement on text is not agreement on validity or relief.

## 27. Harris v. Alabama — No. 93-7659

**Argument:** December 5, 1994. **Event cutoff:** February 22, 1995, common pre-day law. **Judgment under review:** Alabama Supreme Court affirmance, 632 So. 2d 543. All eight associates reach the admitted federal jury-advice/override question. They do not expand it to unrelated guilt or trial-presence issues discussed below.

### Actual record and controlling law

The jury recommended life without parole seven to five on July 13, 1989. The judge imposed death after a further hearing and entered the amended order reproduced in the predecision appendix. That order states that the evidence, presentence material, further hearing, counsel's contentions, and jury recommendation were considered. It excludes victim-impact material; finds one aggravator, pecuniary gain; finds no significant criminal history as statutory mitigation and further family, work, church, and community mitigation; and treats Harris as a principal planner, financier, and beneficiary rather than a minor participant. The wording of its weighing passage prompted a statutory-mitigator objection, but the appellate court, considering the order and Rule 10(f) clarification, found that mitigator had been considered. The appellate court also reviewed arbitrary influence, independently weighed the circumstances, and considered proportionality and the roles/cooperation of other participants.

These are record facts and lower findings. They are not an agreement that the override was constitutionally adequate. The judge did not assign a number to the recommendation or separately label a comparative explanation for rejecting it. The record nevertheless contains actual findings and weighing; no commitment may call it wholly unexplained or find ignored mitigation contrary to the stated lower findings.

Section 13A-5-47(e) requires the judge to determine whether found aggravation outweighs found mitigation and, in doing so, **shall consider** the advisory recommendation **unless waived under §13A-5-46(a) or (g)**. The recommendation is not binding. This case has an actual unwaived recommendation. The complete text of other sentencing subsections is not a premise for an additional rule here; their reported operation and the actual review are sufficient for the selected question.

Spaziano permits a judicial final sentencer notwithstanding contrary advice; Florida's Tedder protection is an important fact of that case but was not declared a universally compulsory federal formula. Actual simulated Tuilaepa/Proctor rejects required numerical weighting while preserving relevant individualized consideration, effective mitigation, and separate selection errors. Actual Espinosa concerns an invalid aggravator transmitted through advice to which state law assigns **great weight**; neither that factor error nor that statutory great-weight command is present here. Actual Romano invalidated a substantial invitation to surrender sentencing responsibility to an irrelevant separate death decision; it did not establish a universal jury-sentencing requirement. No harmlessness shortcut is used: the affirming commitments find no federal error on this admitted issue.

**Stevens.** Commit to H1, reject H2, and support H3 sentence vacatur/remand. ## 28. Grubart and City of Chicago v. Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. — Nos. 93-762 and 93-1094

**Argument:** October 12, 1994. **Event cutoff:** February 22, 1995, common pre-day law. **Judgment under review:** 3 F.3d 225, reversing both jurisdictional dismissal and the alternative pleading-stage limitation dismissal. Stevens and Breyer do not participate. Both dockets receive the same coordinated jurisdictional/remand disposition, with no inferred distinction in facts or grants.

### Common factual and legal premises

Great Lakes performed bridge-dolphin piling work from barges on the navigable Chicago River. The alleged vessel work damaged a freight tunnel that later breached on April 13, 1992, flooding land property; the lower opinion reports a river-traffic closure exceeding a month. These events support the jurisdictional characterization. Negligent proximate causation, Chicago's alleged contributing conduct, corporate privity/knowledge, contract scope, liability, and the limitation entitlement remain merits matters. The jurisdictional decision does not find them proved.

Section 1333(1) supplies admiralty jurisdiction with its saving-to-suitors qualification. The Admiralty Extension Act, then 46 U.S.C. App. §740, reaches injury caused by a vessel on navigable water even when consummated on land. Ordinary proximate causation supplies the necessary causal boundary; neither an invented immediate-impact rule nor a fixed six-month or distance limit controls. The reported barges served transportation functions and are not treated as vessels merely because any object can float.

Under Sisson, connection requires a general type of incident with potential to disrupt maritime commerce and an activity substantially related to traditional maritime activity. Damage by vessel-based piling operations to a structure beneath a navigable waterway has that potential; navigable-water repair/protection work carried out by vessels supplies the traditional relationship on these facts. The actual closure corroborates the apt description but is not made a necessary condition for jurisdiction in every case. Sisson reserved mixed instrumentalities. The present rule must therefore explain, rather than pretend already settled, why an alleged tortfeasor's substantial maritime activity can connect this casualty despite another alleged actor's nonmaritime conduct.

The five Sisson-framework associates adopt this bounded answer: when at least one alleged tortfeasor's activity is substantially related to traditional maritime activity and is an alleged proximate cause of the same potentially disruptive incident, another alleged contributing actor's nonmaritime conduct does not remove admiralty jurisdiction over that connected casualty. Mere joinder of a vessel owner, a wholly independent land event, or a conclusory reference to water is insufficient. The holding does not choose the governing substantive law for every claim or negate saving-to-suitors rights.

### Enhanced individual comparisons and join barriers

**O'Connor.** Commit to G1–G4 and the five-associate Sisson ground. Her actual Sisson majority join supplies direct acceptance of both connection inquiries; her American Dredging joins preserve the distinction between admiralty rights and permissible state forum administration. The strongest contrary path characterizes the incident as damage to land-based business premises through an old freight tunnel and stresses Chicago's nonmaritime omissions. She rejects that description as incomplete because the alleged operative cause is actual vessel-based work on a navigable waterway, with a foreseeable type of disruption to navigation. The mixed-actor extension is limited to the same allegedly caused casualty, not every claim against every defendant in a complaint. She will not join an opinion declaring that admiralty jurisdiction automatically displaces all state law or requires federal adjudication of every associated claim. Expressly reserving those matters and the owner's liability and cap resolves the barrier. For the alternative dismissal, the undeveloped corporate-role and contractual issues do not establish an unavoidable bar to every limitation claim; reversal of that dismissal is not an affirmative lack-of-privity finding.

**Scalia.** Commit to G1, G3, and G4 and to jurisdiction on a separate ground. His complete Sisson concurrence, joined there by White, would treat a tort involving a vessel engaged in normal maritime activity on navigable water as within admiralty without a further case-specific test of potential commercial disruption. His American Dredging opinion preserves the separate saving-clause/forum question. Vessel-based piling work in this navigable river satisfies his operative rule; the statutory extension prevents the location of ultimate land damage from defeating it. The strongest contrary path is that this formulation eliminates the independent connection safeguard and may sweep in fortuitously floating equipment. He retains the actual vessel and normal-maritime-activity conditions: not everything afloat qualifies, and this record's barges and work meet them. He need not dispute that the present incident could disrupt commerce, but he refuses to make that separate inquiry indispensable. He will not join G2's complete Sisson methodology as his governing test. A judgment opinion confined to the common jurisdictional result and limitation/merits reservation could obtain his agreement; no such agreement supplies a sixth associate join to the five's full framework. He leaves independent Limitation Act jurisdiction undecided.

**Kennedy.** Commit to G1–G4 and the Sisson ground. His own Sisson majority join supplies the two-part analysis. His actual American Dredging dissent, joined by Thomas, treats coherent maritime adjudication as a substantial federal interest even where a procedural device did not originate exclusively in admiralty. That uniformity position supports taking account of the connected vessel casualty, but does not itself decide jurisdiction or eliminate the saving clause. The strongest opposing argument is that Sisson reserved mixed activities and a broad characterization reduces connection to location. His answer preserves both real elements: vessel work of a traditional navigational kind and an incident capable of disrupting commerce. The City's alleged contribution does not divide this single causally connected casualty into incompatible jurisdictional descriptions. He will not join a rule covering unrelated land conduct solely because a vessel appears in the pleading, nor decide Chicago's liability or ultimate indemnity. Keeping the causally relevant activity and incident limitations resolves the barrier. He supports remand of corporate knowledge and contractual questions rather than assuming federal jurisdiction grants the owner a statutory cap.

**Souter.** Commit to G1–G4 and the Sisson ground. His actual American Dredging joins and separate qualification examine the relationship between the disputed rule and federal maritime interests instead of allowing a procedural/substantive label to do all the work. His Gizoni join likewise accepts claim-specific maritime statutory analysis without deciding this connection test. The adverse path here warns that “vessel work” and “disruption” can be descriptions chosen to guarantee jurisdiction. He requires the concrete operative activity and general incident to fit together: repair/protective piling work from actual barges in a navigable channel, alleged damage to the underwater structure, and a type of event capable of interfering with navigation. It is that combination, not a broad policy balancing test or the final basement damage in isolation, that supplies jurisdiction. He adopts the limited mixed-actor rule because the causally relevant maritime activity remains part of the incident. He cannot join an assertion that Sisson already resolved its express reservation or that any floating instrumentality automatically suffices. Acknowledging the present extension and retaining its causal bounds resolves that barrier. Liability, privity, personal-contract scope, and forum consequences remain distinct for remand.

**Thomas.** Commit to G1–G4 and the Sisson ground, not Scalia's separate categorical route. These are his own recorded positions. They support treating the genuinely maritime part of this casualty as legally significant, but do not establish a prior choice between the two Sisson approaches. He will not join a statement that he previously adopted Scalia's Sisson concurrence, or a free-standing rule that uniformity alone creates jurisdiction. **Ginsburg.** Commit to G1–G4 and the Sisson ground. Her actual American Dredging joins preserve a competent state forum's bounded administrative choices without abandoning substantive maritime rules; her McDermott/Boca Grande joins likewise separate defined substantive rights from reserved issues. Neither supplies an earlier vote on Sisson's mixed-actor question. Applying its existing inquiries, she finds jurisdiction based on the real maritime operation alleged to have caused this potentially disruptive incident. The best adverse characterization emphasizes remote land damage and the City's separate obligations. The Admiralty Extension Act and a causal, incident-based application of Sisson prevent those features from erasing the vessel operation; they do not permit jurisdiction by unrelated joinder. She requires the opinion to preserve the saving-to-suitors qualification, unresolved choice-of-law questions, and the distinction between federal competence and ultimate limitation relief. Removing categorical exclusive-forum or automatic-liability language resolves those join barriers. ### Alternative limitation dismissal and mandate

All six participating associates agree that the district court's alternative pleading-stage dismissal cannot stand on the stated undeveloped assumptions. The relevant employees' managerial authority and discretionary role, the corporation's privity or knowledge, the contracting actor's status, and the scope of the asserted personal undertaking require proper consideration. A personal contractual obligation to Chicago cannot simply be treated as a categorical bar to limitation of every tort claim. That conclusion neither extinguishes the contract defense nor promises that any particular contractual liability is limitable.

The proceeding returns for the necessary liability, contribution/indemnity, and limitation questions under their own governing rules. The owner bears its required showing concerning lack of privity or knowledge when the statutory sequence calls for it; the jurisdictional ruling satisfies no part of that merits burden. No cap amount, negligence finding, exoneration, liability allocation, damages award, or automatic injunction against other proceedings is ordered. Independent Limitation Act jurisdiction remains unnecessary to decide. The two dockets remain coordinated. No author or assignment is selected.
