## Facts and lower-court disposition

Walsh filed the federal FELA suit on May 9, 1989. He alleged that PATH negligence on October 6, 1986 caused a passenger train door to strike his right wrist and arm while he worked for PATH. These are allegations, not a Supreme Court finding of negligence or injury causation. His suit was filed within FELA's three-year period and more than one year after the alleged accident.

PATH moved under Rule 12(b)(1). The district court treated PATH as a state agency under Third Circuit precedent, including Port Authority PBA and Leadbeater. It separately rejected Walsh's arguments that New York and New Jersey had entirely waived immunity and that Congress had abrogated immunity through FELA. It read Feeney as finding federal-forum consent while preserving the conditions attached to consent. It applied N.J.S.A. 32:1-163 and N.Y. Unconsol. Law ?7107 as a one-year condition, concluded Walsh had not met it, and dismissed for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. It distinguished state-court FELA coverage from federal-forum immunity. Its references to historical Hilton must be compared with the actual current-law Hilton holding; the lower decision cannot import a historical Supreme Court holding after the divergence point.

The exact consent-statute text and possible exceptions are not reproduced by this opinion. No new tolling, waiver-by-litigation, later filing, state-court action or distinct preserved exception is established by it. The opinion does establish the specific timing predicate and separate consent/abrogation rulings in Walsh's action; it removes the former source gap as to whether the lower Walsh dismissal actually relied on failure to meet the one-year consent condition.
