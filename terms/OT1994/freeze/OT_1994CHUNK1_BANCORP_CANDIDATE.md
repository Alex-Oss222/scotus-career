## Other governing law

- **Article III and 28 U.S.C. § 2106:** Mootness ends power to adjudicate substantive bankruptcy rights. Section 2106 separately authorizes an appellate court to affirm, modify, vacate, set aside, reverse, or remand judgments lawfully brought before it; it does not command vacatur whenever a dispute becomes moot.

- **United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36, 39–41 (1950); Karcher v. May, 484 U.S. 72, 82–83 (1987):** Vacatur protects a litigant prevented from obtaining review through circumstances not properly charged to it. Munsingwear itself denied relief where the Government failed to seek vacatur. Karcher distinguishes a party’s voluntary abandonment of review. Their equitable rationale must be tested against consensual settlement.

- **Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc., 321 U.S. 671, 677–678 (1944):** An appellate court may make the disposition necessary to protect the parties’ rights after merits review becomes impossible. This is residual dispositional authority, not hypothetical jurisdiction over the merits.

- **Equitable allocation:** The applicant must justify the requested extraordinary alteration of an existing judgment. Voluntary causation, the availability of review, and the public interest in adjudicated law bear on that justification; no special numerical proof standard is supplied.

## E. ISSUE-BY-ISSUE LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Mootness, residual authority, and equitable vacatur

#### Question

Does settlement-caused mootness require or justify vacating the judgment under review?

#### Controlling reasoning below

The Ninth Circuit decided bankruptcy law before the settlement. It did not decide the later Supreme Court vacatur motion. The original merits judgment is therefore the object of the requested equitable relief, not a lower ruling on the current question.

#### Petitioner / Appellant / Excepting party

- **Proposed Rule and Application:** Bancorp argues that an unreviewable adverse judgment should be vacated under Munsingwear and that facilitating settlement is a sufficient equitable reason. Having obtained review should not leave it worse off merely because the parties resolve their dispute.
- **Requested Judgment and Limiting Principle:** Vacate and dismiss; limit the claim to a judgment whose review becomes moot while review is pending.
- **Principal Authorities:** Article III and 28 U.S.C. § 2106; United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36, 39–41 (1950); Karcher v. May, 484 U.S. 72, 82–83 (1987); Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc., 321 U.S. 671, 677–678 (1944); Equitable allocation; the published lower opinion identified in Section I.H supports the attributed position.
- **Necessary Concessions:** No express concession is attributed. As an analytical limit, this position remains subject to the independent threshold, factual, and remedial requirements identified above.

#### Respondent / Appellee / Opposing party

- **Proposed Rule and Application:** Bonner argues that voluntary settlement forfeits the losing party’s opportunity to obtain reversal, rather than entitling it to erase an adjudication. It also questions the Court’s power to act after the substantive controversy has ended.
- **Requested Judgment and Limiting Principle:** Dismiss without vacatur; distinguish involuntary mootness from voluntary settlement. The separate no-power contention would bar vacatur regardless of the equities.
- **Principal Authorities:** Article III and 28 U.S.C. § 2106; United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36, 39–41 (1950); Karcher v. May, 484 U.S. 72, 82–83 (1987); Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc., 321 U.S. 671, 677–678 (1944); Equitable allocation; the published lower opinion identified in Section I.H supports the attributed position.
- **Necessary Concessions:** No express concession is attributed. A favorable answer does not itself establish the separately reserved merits or remedial questions.

#### Strongest answer to petitioner

The appellant elected settlement rather than pursuing the available review. The public consequences of adjudication are not property that litigants may automatically cancel by agreement.

#### Strongest answer to respondent

Mootness cannot eliminate all power to dispose of the case; otherwise even the ordinary Munsingwear practice would be inexplicable. Lack of automatic entitlement need not imply an absolute prohibition on exceptional equitable relief.
