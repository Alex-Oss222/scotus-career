## 13. Tome v. United States, No. 93-6892 — January 10, 1995

### Reach, components, and entering law

All eight associates reach the admitted question under original Rule 801(d)(1)(B): whether substantive prior-consistent-statement admission to rebut the alleged fabrication or improper motive requires that the statement predate that asserted motive. They do not reopen the independently rejected confrontation, leading-question, or general fairness claims merely because the temporal question is before the Court. The asserted custody motive remains an allegation; no Justice finds fabrication, coaching, or guilt.

The rule requires the declarant to testify and be subject to cross-examination concerning the statement, consistency with that testimony, and the stated rebuttal purpose. Salerno (June 19, 1992), **Former testimony, similar motive and the Government's evidentiary objection**, requires satisfaction of the chosen Rule's actual conditions. Daubert (June 28, 1993), **Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702**, displaces incompatible common-law prerequisites while retaining compatible guidance. Williamson (June 27, 1994), **Assertion-specific declarations against penal interest**, requires context under its different exception and separates admissibility from appellate relief. None already decides Rule 801's temporal question.

### Adversarial comparison

**Premotive path:** A statement offered for the specified substantive use must precede the particular improper influence or motive it is offered to rebut. Repetition during the asserted motive does not answer the charge that the motive generated the account; the temporal rule implements the specific rebuttal condition rather than a general rule excluding every postmotive statement from evidence. Quinto, 582 F.2d 224, 233–234, and Guevara, 598 F.2d 1094, 1100, supply date-eligible circuit support. The strongest counter is that the Rule omits express timing language and circumstances can sometimes weaken an accusation despite an existing motive. The answer for this path is limited: other evidentiary routes and rehabilitation remain distinct, while general relevance cannot substitute for the particular condition governing substantive nonhearsay admission.

**Contextual-rebuttal path:** The text requires genuine rebuttal force but does not prescribe an invariant temporal condition. Timing is important; consistency alone is insufficient. The judge must identify circumstances furnishing probative force beyond repetition and apply Rules 402 and 403. The Tenth Circuit's treatment, Montague, 958 F.2d 1094, and Miller, 874 F.2d 1255, support this construction. Its strongest counter is that a statement made under the very motive challenged ordinarily does not rebut that motive, and a general relevance approach risks erasing the limiting clause. This path answers that objection by retaining an actual, identified rebuttal requirement; it does not treat all consistent statements as admissible.

## 14. Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church, assigned No. 93-1917 — January 17, 1995

### Components and competing paths

All eight associates reach both admitted components: the constitutional defense to this probationary minister's advancement/termination race, sex, and retaliation claims, and whether that defense defeats subject-matter jurisdiction. The lower-court docket is 93-2157. No Justice relies on an unestablished RFRA invocation or on a finding that discrimination occurred.

**Church-selection path:** Kedroff, 344 U.S. 94, 116, protects church government and selection of ecclesiastical authorities; Milivojevich, 426 U.S. 696, 708–725, bars civil reexamination of ecclesiastical decisions through a claim that internal procedures were violated. Young challenges her own advancement and retention as a minister. Compelled reinstatement or reconsideration would directly control that office; damages based on the same selection decision would impose civil liability for its exercise. The strongest counter is that race, sex, and retaliation are secular wrongs, §702 expressly permits religious preference rather than those forms of discrimination, and Jones v. Wolf allows neutral civil adjudication. The answer is claim-specific: §702 is not expanded; the independent constitutional limit follows because these requested liabilities review the selection itself and the church's ecclesiastical procedures, not an independent property or other secular obligation.

**Neutral-inquiry path:** Permit discrimination adjudication where secular facts suffice, reserving remedies that appoint clergy. Its strongest counter is that pretext inquiry and review of adherence to internal advancement procedures are the intrusion in this case; replacing reinstatement with money does not remove it. No distinct secular claim detached from the ministerial selection is established. This path therefore does not support relief on the supplied claim, though it prevents a general immunity extending to all church disputes.

**Jurisdiction fork:** Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678, 681–683, makes a substantial federal claim sufficient for jurisdiction apart from its ultimate legal success, with exceptions for immaterial claims made solely to obtain jurisdiction and wholly insubstantial or frivolous claims. Young's Title VII claim falls within §706(f)(3), 42 U.S.C. §2000e-5(f)(3), and §1331. Neither Bell exception is supported. The strongest counter is that ecclesiastical nonreviewability sounds like a limit on civil judicial power. The answer is that the court exercises jurisdiction to identify the federal claim and apply the constitutional defense; the defense supplies a substantive limitation, not the absence of every power to adjudicate. Rule 12(b)(1) and 12(b)(6) cannot be treated as interchangeable.

### Enhanced construction comparison

**Own-planting retention path:** The farmer-sales proviso reaches “such saved seed,” meaning seed genuinely retained for the farmer's own planting that becomes available for qualifying sale, rather than production undertaken as a step in marketing protected seed for growing purposes. Plans may change, and reasonably intended acreage expansion is not prohibited. The proviso's “without regard” phrase does real work: the qualifying sale of such retained seed is protected notwithstanding §2541(3); it does not turn the entire crop grown for reproductive sale into qualifying saved seed. “Marketing” is not limited to advertising campaigns, sales representatives, or extensive coordinated merchandising. The strongest counter is the grammar linking “for seeding purposes” to “obtained,” plus an express reproductive-sales privilege that should not be made illusory. This path gives that privilege actual work for qualifying retained seed and takes its purpose limitation from “such saved seed” and the retained infringement structure, not from a numerical cap or breeder-protection preference.

**Broader farmer-sales path:** The proviso expressly permits qualifying farmers to sell saved seed for reproductive purposes “without regard” to §2541(3). “For seeding purposes” modifies the authorized acquisition, not an express requirement that every later seed have been reserved only for one's own ensuing planting. The strongest counter is the independent force of “saved,” the opening reservation, and the prospect of routine reproductive production swallowing protection. The response is that the enacted seller/purchaser occupation requirements, authorized lineage, applicable state law, and retained restrictions define the privilege; courts cannot replace those with an unexpressed own-use ceiling. This path does not validate the Federal Circuit's crop-by-protected-crop, less-than-half formula: the statute addresses a person's primary farming occupation.

**Common notice boundary:** The statute says a qualifying §2543 act infringes no right under the chapter, subject to the specified opening exceptions. Section 2541(6) is not one of those exceptions. Accordingly an actually qualifying farmer sale is not independently made an infringement by that notice clause. That proposition does not establish that these sales qualify. For a sale outside §2543, Asgrow must establish §2541(6)'s own applicable elements, including dispensing propagable protected material without the notice under which it was received, within §2541's conditions. Liability is distinct from §2567 marking-related damages limits. Rejection of a broad exemption is not proof of notice infringement or a damages amount.

## 16. United States v. Mezzanatto, No. 93-1340 — January 18, 1995

### Reach and distinct components

All eight associates reach the legal enforceability of the reported knowing and voluntary advance waiver for impeachment of inconsistent trial testimony. They distinguish: the Rules' general prohibition; their express exceptions; whether the protection is legally waivable; whether assent was knowing and voluntary; agreement scope; preserved objection; harmlessness; and remedy. The only use adjudicated here is the described impeachment use. A promise concerning perjury or false-statement proceedings is not independently validated. Case-in-chief and derivative uses are outside the admitted question.

The then-existing Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) exclude the specified withdrawn guilty pleas, no-contest pleas, related plea-proceeding statements, and unsuccessful or withdrawn-plea discussions with the prosecuting attorney. The fairness exception concerns another statement from the same plea/discussion already introduced and contemporaneous consideration required by fairness. The criminal perjury/false-statement exception requires that the statement have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence. Ordinary impeachment does not fall within that exception merely because testimony is inconsistent.

### Enhanced two-path comparison

**Limited consensual-waiver path:** The Rules provide a defendant a protection without expressly forbidding its relinquishment. Brady, 397 U.S. 742, Ricketts, 483 U.S. 1, Peretz, 501 U.S. 923, and Rumery, 480 U.S. 386, support giving effect to an informed choice within their actual scopes, rather than inferring that every mandatory protection is unwaivable. The specific agreement, counsel consultation, and impeachment-only use matter. The strongest counter is that Congress's rejection of an ordinary impeachment exception and its bargaining-protection policy could be routinely defeated if waiver becomes the price of discussion. This path distinguishes admission without assent from enforcement of a limited informed agreement; the packet establishes no coercion or prosecutorial misconduct, and speculative universal effects do not establish that this particular bargain defeats the public policy. It preserves legal challenges to actual coercion, fraud, ignorance, scope, and independent evidentiary restrictions.

**No advance bargaining-entry waiver path:** The Rules' deliberately protected discussion space and carefully enumerated exceptions reflect an institutional interest in candid negotiation, not only an individual's privilege. Brooklyn Savings, 324 U.S. 697, establishes that consent cannot always displace a protection enacted for public purposes; Rumery's public-interest limitation prevents a universal waiver analogy. The strongest counter is the absence of an express no-waiver clause and the ordinary enforceability of many constitutional and statutory waivers. This path answers that the particular prospective condition defeats the function of the protected discussion before it begins; its ground is statutory policy and structure, not an invented finding that Mezzanatto was coerced or lacked capacity. It leaves agreements at other procedural stages and other uses undecided.

**Individual validity:** Godinez (June 24, 1993), **Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver**, separates capacity from actual knowing, intelligent, voluntary assent. It does not decide this protection's waivability. Olano (April 26, 1993), **Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)**, distinguishes valid intentional relinquishment from failure to object, but expressly preserves right-specific requirements. Salerno requires actual Rule conditions and rejects independent contradiction as selective disclosure; it does not decide express consensual waiver. These rules cannot be used to shortcut the statutory fork.
