## 22. Fargo Women's Health Organization v. Schafer, No. 93-1712

**Scheduled event:** Merits, February 13, 1995; user-established argument December 12, 1994. No. 93-1712 is the assigned Supreme Court docket, distinct from Eighth Circuit No. 93-1579. Review of 18 F.3d 526 (February 10, 1994), affirming summary judgment at 819 F. Supp. 862 (D.N.D. 1993). No grant date or order is invented.

### Questions, record, and disputed propositions

The granted question encompasses informed consent, the twenty-four-hour interval, definitions, the medical-emergency exception, facial review, and the pre-enforcement record and remedy. The actual challenges below concerned the new informed-consent provisions and emergency definition, and the preexisting abortion definition and general penalty. Parental-consent provisions were **not challenged**. The opinion expressly says the Organization pressed **vagueness**, not an undue-burden challenge to the medical-emergency definition. Treat preservation and the supplied question scope explicitly before expanding that component. A vagueness challenge to the emergency definition asks about fair notice and enforcement standards under the applicable date-eligible law; it is not converted into a general access challenge merely because emergency care also forms part of the protected baseline. A challenge to informed-consent obligations and delay may separately require the complete Casey access inquiry.

The district court issued a preliminary injunction on August 23, 1991 against informed consent and waiting provisions. The parties agreed to await Casey and later litigated summary judgment. The district court granted the State's motion, dissolved the injunction and dismissed the action. The Eighth Circuit affirmed. The supplied established merits posture does not require importing any other Supreme Court action in this litigation.

**Summary-judgment record:** The supplied lower-court account reports no district-court fact findings, but does report a substantial affidavit/deposition record submitted over a three-month period. The appellate majority assumed the nonmovants' factual assertions true, distinguished those from conclusory assertions, and held them legally insufficient under its two stated approaches. The dispute is not simply whether any evidence exists. It includes whether the majority's assumed facts and telephone construction dispose of every material burden under the governing law and whether additional findings or proceedings are needed.

Patients' travel distances, lodging costs, work and appointment constraints, protest-related exposure, and physicians' presence only on Thursdays and Fridays appear in the reported record. Do not assign numerical magnitudes or find the alleged burdens proved. The majority read the Act to allow information by telephone and written certification at the procedure, eliminating a statutory two-visit requirement. It therefore attributed remaining travel and clinic-day constraints to circumstances rather than the State's command. The competing question is whether the required interval nevertheless adds clinically or practically consequential delay or other interference to those circumstances, especially for a defined affected subgroup. That causal question requires the complete six-step rule below, not an assumption that all geographic difficulty is either attributable to the State or constitutionally irrelevant.

The lower court considered a March 1993 Attorney General opinion persuasive. It cited Johnson v. Baker, 21 N.W.2d 355, 364 (N.D. 1945), for an Attorney General opinion's force until contrary court ruling, and Svanes v. Grenz, 492 N.W.2d 576, 579 (N.D. 1992), for respectful consideration of persuasive opinions. The court independently adopted the same text construction. It expressly reserved a new challenge if the Attorney General or courts later required more than one in-person visit. That reservation is not a prediction of changed enforcement.

### Construction and qualifications that must remain separate

- The lower court permits the performing or referring physician or an agent to communicate the required information by telephone. The attending physician must exercise medical judgment to determine probable gestational age; an agent may collect the relevant history, including last menstrual period, and communicate the physician's determination. There is no categorical exclusive-physician delivery rule.
- Written patient certification and receipt by the physician or agent precede the abortion, but need not precede the woman's arrival by twenty-four hours. The duty does not compel acceptance, reading, or endorsement of state materials. Materials must be provided if she elects to view them; statutory state supply is free.
- “May be available” does not guarantee benefit eligibility or payment. A legal child-support duty is not a promise of collection. The express freedom to comment or dissociate concerns printed materials; the lower court additionally construes the Act to permit comment on assistance and paternal-liability statements. Distinguish enacted text from this interpretation.
- The North Dakota text supplied here contains **neither Pennsylvania's separate §3205(c) disclosure-health defense nor its rape-related permission to omit paternal-support information**. Do not import either into North Dakota. Their absence may matter when applying the entering Casey holdings, whose qualifications must be respected.
- Emergency exemption and clinical-judgment language do not require catastrophic deterioration. The lower court reads the purpose clause and clinical standard to mean compliance may not significantly threaten the woman's life or health. That is a protective lower-court construction, not extra enacted words. The “prior to abortion if possible” proviso qualifies the emergency explanation. Written certification is a separate obligation, not authority to delay necessary care.
- The challenged abortion definition has both a pregnancy-termination object and a purpose distinction. The providers' example was amniocentesis inadvertently resulting in termination. The lower court rejected applying the definition to an unintended termination by reading its terms and criminal fault requirements together. No claim about a particular completed prosecution is presented.

#### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

## North Dakota Supreme Court decisions on the provision's reach

These are the actual state opinions, retrieved from Harvard's Caselaw Access Project, not modern commentary. The HTML and extracted text are saved with the names given below. CAP metadata independently supplies the citations and dates.

**City of Dickinson v. Mueller, 261 N.W.2d 787, 789–790 (N.D. December 19, 1977).** Correct caption is *City of Dickinson*, not *State*. The Court, Erickstad writing with Sand, Paulson, Pederson, and Vogel concurring, held that §12.1-02-02(2) must be read with subsection (1)'s introductory title limitation and applies only to offenses described in Title 12.1. It therefore did not add willfulness to Title 5's prohibition on alcohol sales to minors or the corresponding municipal ordinance. The Court expressly reserved whether willfulness in the sense of conduct done voluntarily rather than under coercion could matter in other circumstances. [Primary text](https://static.case.law/nw2d/261/html/0787-01.html); `tmp/fargo_source_research/mueller.html` and `.txt`.

**State v. North Dakota Education Association, 262 N.W.2d 731, 733–734 (N.D. 1978), Part IV.** The Court rejected the requested default-willfulness instruction for a Title 16 election statute. Section 12.1-02-02 was part of the recodified criminal code and applied only to that code, not earlier unrepealed statutes outside it. The election statute contained no culpability requirement. [Primary text](https://static.case.law/nw2d/262/html/0731-01.html); `tmp/fargo_source_research/ndea.html` and `.txt`.

**State v. Goetz, 312 N.W.2d 1, 11–12 (N.D. October 15, 1981; rehearing October 23), issue thirteen.** The securities statute expressly used “willfully” but did not define it. The Court distinguished *Mueller* and *North Dakota Education Association*: those cases concerned attempts to import default culpability into outside-title statutes containing no culpability term. The trial instruction in *Goetz* used intentional or knowing conduct and omitted recklessness. That instruction imposed a greater burden on the State and did not harm Goetz; the Court did not need to decide whether it was erroneous. The opinion does not overrule the title limitation or make default willfulness applicable throughout the Century Code. [Primary text](https://static.case.law/nw2d/312/html/0001-01.html); `tmp/fargo_source_research/goetz.html` and `.txt`.

They predate both the present lower judgment and this event. No strict-liability conclusion follows automatically. A model relying on fault must identify the lawful source and its element-specific reach; a model relying independently on the definition's own terms or clinical construction must explain that sufficient ground. A bounded remand can leave the indispensable state-law question below without treating it as a bar to every other claim.

Section 12.1-02-02(3)'s conduct, attendant-circumstance, result, grading, defense and in-fact clauses remain separate even if a legally valid route makes the provision applicable. Its intentional/knowing distinction and recklessness alternative cannot be flattened into a knowing-only or bad-faith-only rule. Nor does subsection (5) create a general knowledge-of-illegality requirement. The dated enactment and earlier state applications are verified; complete intervening amendment continuity and Title 14 incorporation are not certified.


### Independent questions and the governing law for each

The matter presents separate legal objects. Keep (1) the content, delivery, interval and certification obligations; (2) the definition of abortion; (3) the definition of medical emergency; (4) each enforcement consequence; and (5) remedy and further proceedings distinct. A common appellate judgment does not turn them into one constitutional claim. The parental provisions are outside the presented challenge. ### Abortion-definition vagueness: two supported paths and their limits

The challenged definition concerns termination of a human pregnancy with an intention other than live birth or removal of a dead embryo or fetus. The providers' concrete illustration is amniocentesis followed by an unintended termination. Analyze the act being defined, the purpose language and the consequence separately. An intentional diagnostic act is not necessarily an intentional termination of pregnancy; conversely, the existence of an intentional medical act does not by itself answer the definition's grammatical reach.

**Path preserving the definition on its own terms.** A court may read the complete phrase as identifying intentional pregnancy termination with the stated purpose exclusions, and reject an interpretation that converts an unintended diagnostic complication into an abortion merely because the diagnostic aim was not one of those exclusions. The lower court's common-sense construction supplies an independent stated ground. X-Citement's contextual-definition reasoning supports examining the whole category; ordinary purposeful-termination language can supply notice without an extra Title 12.1 default. This path must explain why that is a permissible reading of these words and identify its consequence for the amniocentesis example. It cannot announce that diagnostic procedures are categorically immune from every properly defined offense or decide an unpresented completed prosecution.

**Strongest counterargument.** The phrase expressly states an intention other than two excluded aims; it does not separately say “intent to terminate.” If the law encompasses accidental loss during intentionally undertaken procedures, professional notice and chilling concerns remain, especially under criminal and punitive civil consequences. A protective reading cannot be asserted solely to avoid a constitutional problem if state law will not bear it. The disputed default-fault premise cannot substitute for explaining the actual text.

**Path requiring a bounded correction or remand.** If the text and lawful state-law construction do not securely dispose of accidental diagnostic termination, the Court may require reconsideration of this component with the actual culpability source, its element-specific reach and the independent textual construction identified. It need not invent a mental state or declare the entire Act void. The exact remand issue is whether and why the definition covers unintended pregnancy loss, and whether a legally available fault or construction rule cures any otherwise material uncertainty. If a facial defect is established as law without disputed facts, correspondingly confined relief may be considered instead; its actual scope and dependent duties must be stated.

**Strongest counterargument and narrower option.** No prosecution, inconsistent enforcement or concrete injury from a genuinely ambiguous application is supplied. The Court need not remand a merely strained hypothetical if the statutory definition itself clearly excludes it. Denial of this facial ground on that independent reading can leave actual materially different enforcement challengeable and coexist with remand of the access obligations. A decision on this definition does not resolve emergency vagueness, criminal culpability for all chapter violations, or the independent civil-damages scheme.

### Emergency-definition vagueness: two supported paths and their limits

The definition permits an immediate abortion, on the physician's best clinical judgment, where necessary to avert death, or where a twenty-four-hour delay creates grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. The challenged terms include grave, immediate and major bodily function. Whether the command is sufficiently definite, whether a protective interpretation is legally available, and whether later disagreement alone establishes a punishable violation are separate questions. “Best” and “good faith” are not identical enacted words; their relevant legal operation must be explained rather than assumed.

**Path sustaining the definition against the preserved facial attack.** Vuitch and Doe permit reliance on ordinary clinical judgment. The explicit reference to the physician's best clinical judgment, read with the statutory maternal-health purpose and the lower court's construction excluding significant threats from compliance, may give a competent physician a workable prospective standard. The text need not catalogue every emergency or prescribe a numerical probability. This path must retain the lower court's protective construction at its actual status, explain why the specified terms do not expose reasonable clinical judgments to uncontrolled enforcement, and treat any valid fault requirement only as additional support established by its own law. Casey's unanimous emergency-construction ruling is an analogy, not a prior vagueness judgment.

**Strongest counterargument.** North Dakota's grave-peril/immediate/irreversible-loss words are more specific restrictions than an unrestricted health judgment. **Strongest counterargument and narrower option.** The preserved attack may present only understandable medical terms and hypothetical disagreement, which Vuitch and Doe reject as a sufficient basis for facial invalidation.
