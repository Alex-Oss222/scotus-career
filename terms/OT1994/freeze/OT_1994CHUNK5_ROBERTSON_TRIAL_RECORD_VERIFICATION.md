## What the evidence accounts establish, and their limits

**Sales and retained nuggets.** The Government describes production of approximately $200,000–$290,000, most gold sold in Alaska, and about $30,000 of nuggets Robertson took outside the State (petition pp.4–5; merits pp.4–5). Respondent describes approximately $200,000 sold, all purchasers Alaska refiners, and about $30,000 retained by Robertson (merits p.3). The Ninth Circuit's opinion, reproduced in petition Appendix A p.9a, describes approximately $200,000 in production and says that, except for retained nuggets, none of the gold was sold outside Alaska. Its retained-nugget qualification must not be lost; the opinion does not establish that no gold physically left Alaska.

**Additional oral-argument acknowledgment.** At official argument pp.41–42, respondent's counsel described intrastate refiner sales and said there was an inference, but no evidence, of subsequent sales outside Alaska. At p.42 lines 2–15, he acknowledged the questioner's description that about $30,000, or 15 percent of output, was taken out of State, but expressly denied that this was an activity of the mine, characterizing it as Robertson personally taking gold rather than the mine selling gold outside Alaska. This is materially stronger support for physical carriage than the earlier briefs-only account. The percentage is an argument exchange counsel accepted, not an independently verified trial finding or a newly calculated output fraction. His attribution objection is preserved alongside his acknowledgment. Neither the acknowledgment nor his objection is substituted for the missing trial evidence about the transport's actual business purpose.

**Refiners and downstream commerce.** Government merits pp.20–21 and n.7 describe local sales to General Refining Corporation and Oxford Assaying and Refining Company, London spot-price linkage, and General Refining documents bearing Alaska, New York and Los Angeles addresses. The brief cites Tr.1004–1015, Tr.1011 and Government exhibits 259–262, 264 and 266. The exhibits and testimony are not reproduced in the available appendix. A refiner's multistate addresses and a world-market price do not trace this mine's gold into an interstate shipment. Respondent's argument acknowledgment supplies no agreed downstream tracing.

**Government's attribution argument.** Calling the retained-gold movement a shipment in the Government's notes establishes the Government's position. It does not supply shipping records, an outside-Alaska buyer, an interstate sale, a commercial delivery instruction, or testimony connecting the movement to ordinary mine operations. Ownership of a sole proprietorship does not by itself settle the operator's expressly required factual distinction between personal retention/carriage and business output shipment.

## Access limits and exact outstanding source need

Searches by the case name, docket, district case numbers and specific transcript/nugget terms did not locate an accessible separate trial transcript. The Archive inventory provides no such volume. Search results identifying opinions or briefs do not cure that absence. No PACER access, court-clerk retrieval, archival request, or physical inspection of court files was performed; this preparation does not claim the trial record is lost, sealed, universally inaccessible or incapable of proving the premise.

The missing sources are the actual district trial transcript, particularly Tr.774–784, 1722–1725 and 1906–1907, and, if material to tracing or commercial purpose, the identified refiner exhibits and related Tr.1004–1015/1011 testimony or equivalent authenticated primary transaction evidence. The district file is identified in the appendix as Southern District of California criminal case No.90-00925-01 / No.90-0925-N. Obtaining those materials would require access not established in this preparation. They must show whether, and in what meaningful amount, the **mine itself shipped its output interstate as part of ordinary business operations**. Physical carriage of retained nuggets, procurement and hiring, market linkage, and untraced downstream movement do not verify that particular premise.

**Renewed blocker:** Available documents support local refiner sales, retained nuggets, and an express argument acknowledgment that approximately $30,000 of gold left Alaska; they do not verify its shipment by the mine in ordinary interstate commercial operation. The specified underlying trial pages and transaction exhibits remain unavailable to this preparation. The required premise remains unverified, so Robertson is not adjudicated.
