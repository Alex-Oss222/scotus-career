## Wolens: exact terms of the separate recodified qualifications

The local official extract `../research/chunk2_b/statute_49_41713.txt`, from the 1994 United States Code, supplies these qualifications in full:

- Section 41713(b)(2): “Paragraphs (1) and (4) of this subsection do not apply to air transportation provided entirely in Alaska unless the transportation is air transportation (except charter air transportation) provided under a certificate issued under section 41102 of this title.” The exception to the Alaska exclusion must not be omitted if that exclusion is discussed as an operative rule.
- Section 41713(b)(3): “This subsection does not limit a State, political subdivision of a State, or political authority of at least 2 States that owns or operates an airport served by an air carrier holding a certificate issued by the Secretary of Transportation from carrying out its proprietary powers and rights.” This is a qualification concerning an airport owner or operator's proprietary powers and rights.
- The property-transportation provision in paragraph (4)(A) is subject to paragraph (4)(B). The latter preserves state motor-vehicle safety authority; highway route controls or limitations based on vehicle size or weight or the hazardous nature of cargo; and motor-carrier regulation of minimum financial responsibility relating to insurance requirements and self-insurance authorization. It also excludes transportation of household goods as defined in §10102. Paragraph (4)(C) states that paragraph (4) does not limit paragraph (1).

The extract's source and revision notes identify §1305(a)(1) as the predecessor of §41713(b)(1), and identify the later addition of paragraph (4). These notes do not establish that an otherwise unasserted qualification applies to the passenger-award claims. ## NationsBank: complete operative §92 conditions and provisos

The local official extract `../research/chunk2_b/statute_12_92.txt`, from the 1994 United States Code, confirms that §92 begins “In addition to the powers now vested by law” and authorizes qualifying national banks located and doing business in a place with no more than 5,000 inhabitants, measured by the last preceding decennial census, to act under Comptroller rules as an agent for an insurer authorized by the State where the bank is located. The authorized agency functions are soliciting and selling insurance and collecting premiums on the insurer's policies; fees or commissions may be agreed between the bank and insurer.

Its two operative provisos read: “That no such bank shall in any case assume or guarantee the payment of any premium on insurance policies issued through its agency by its principal” and “That the bank shall not guarantee the truth of any statement made by an assured in filing his application for insurance.” These express restrictions are separate from any inference about the permissibility of other activities in places exceeding 5,000 inhabitants. The clean digest's distinction between statutory text and the lower court's inference is supported by the text itself.
