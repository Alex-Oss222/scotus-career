## 29. Anderson v. Green, No. 94-197 — February 22, 1995

### Reach and component definitions

The supplied merits review concerns the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of a preliminary injunction against the first-year California benefit cap. The necessary federal approval was removed by Beno on July 13, 1994, before the reported July 28 petition. California acknowledges that link in its own dated submission and contests mootness on the ground of administrative reconsideration and possible recurrence. There is no issued replacement approval, its terms, a current lawful implementation threat, or other effective requested relief established by this record. The retained order protects a described present-and-future class; named plaintiffs' passage through their first twelve months is not the reason for the threshold result.

- **Current controversy:** dismiss the pending Supreme Court review as moot on the present record. A still-enacted cap and possible new approval do not establish a presently operative differential or a sufficiently concrete impending implementation controversy. No constitutional ruling is reached.
- **Timing and §2106:** Bancorp establishes dispositional authority after a judgment has been lawfully brought for review and subsequently becomes moot. That holding does not by itself decide this pre-petition chronology. Do not transform its sufficient rule into either an unexamined extension or an exclusive rule that dispositional power can never exist when mootness predates a petition.
- **Existing-judgment relief:** make no order vacating the Ninth Circuit judgment or district injunction and no direction dismissing the underlying complaint. Even assuming an otherwise available incidental power to alter those judgments, this record does not establish equitable entitlement to that additional action. The order is preliminary, the district action has not reached final merits judgment, and no unavoidable preclusive or other continuing injury requiring Supreme Court erasure is shown. The courts retaining the underlying proceeding can address the changed approval facts under their own lawful authority. This is not a finding that the injunction must remain operative indefinitely or that the underlying suit remains justiciable.
- **Reservations:** no ruling on a new approval, a future challenge, accrued monetary relief not established here, a universal pre-petition §2106 prohibition, or the constitutional validity of §11450.03. No unverified vacatur motion or party agreement is invented. The present disposition terminates this review only.

The remedy position is deliberately narrower than declaring §2106 wholly unavailable. A jurisdictional dismissal of this review is definite. The general power to erase lower judgments in every pre-petition mootness setting is not needed to decline additional relief on this record. The equitable conclusion does not assume jurisdiction to decide the constitutional merits.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Continue to constitutional merits:** California's strongest point is that it is actively pursuing administrative reconsideration and an injunction remains adverse to its ability to implement the same statute. Carroll recognizes a live dispute despite an expired order when continued activities and actual official reliance make the controversy persist. The counterargument is decisive here: Carroll had demonstrated continuing effects, while this cap cannot operate without a missing federal approval and no replacement has issued. Church of Scientology requires an identified available form of meaningful relief; it does not authorize a decision merely because a legal issue may return. Dickerson likewise depended on specified legal consequences. The possible scope of a new approval is not an established current controversy. All eight reject present constitutional adjudication, without rejecting a later supported challenge.

**Dismiss and vacate lower judgments:** loss of approval arose from an independent judicial proceeding, not California's settlement of the constitutional suit. Bancorp preserves the distinction between voluntary surrender and happenstance, making this a serious equitable argument. But its rule is not automatic vacatur whenever the party did not settle. Here the approval loss already existed when review was sought; the lower relief is preliminary; and the retained record establishes neither an irretrievable loss of a live appellate opportunity caused during this review nor a demonstrated need to erase a completed binding merits adjudication. Ordinary lower-court treatment of changed facts remains available subject to its own requirements. The selected path dismisses review and leaves any further judgment adjustment to an appropriate application and established authority, without deciding a blanket pre-petition power rule.

**Fact-development instead of dismissal:** obtaining an actual replacement approval could establish a different controversy. The present record contains none, and indefinite retention in anticipation of an unknown administrative decision would not identify a live question now. Dismissal does not prejudge what a dated later approval would mean. A new source showing an issued operative approval before February 22 would materially change the neutral facts and require return to preflight/remodeling, not a silent assembly assumption.

## 30. Gustafson v. Alloyd Co., No. 93-404 — February 28, 1995

### Questions reached and shared limits

Review reaches the statutory coverage issue presented by the negotiated agreement for the December 1989 stock acquisition. It does not decide actual falsity, materiality, purchaser ignorance, reasonable care, tender, recoverable damages, or a new limitations defense. The district court's summary judgment rested on a categorical scope rule and expressly left further substantive §12 objections unresolved. The Seventh Circuit vacated and remanded under Pacific Dunlop; it did not enter liability.

The common statutory text remains fully operative: §2's definitions apply unless context otherwise requires; §2(10) expressly includes written communications offering or confirming a security sale and contains its two distinct exceptions; §10 contains separate information, omission, filing, timing and Commission powers; §12(2) includes the specified exempt securities, but not §3(a)(2)'s class, and preserves purchaser knowledge, the seller's reasonable-care defense and the tender/damages distinction. No Justice treats sophistication or the negotiated true-up as an unlegislated categorical exemption. Those facts may matter to the still-open statutory elements and actual relief.

### Competing coverage paths

**Definition-based coverage:** the negotiated purchase agreement is a written communication offering or confirming the stock sale. Neither its private negotiation nor the stock's prior issuance removes it from the enacted definition. The definition's communication exceptions themselves distinguish a general prospectus from one meeting §10; §12(2)'s coverage of exempt securities also prevents equating every civil antifraud communication with a registered public offering. Section 10 prescribes the relevant statutory disclosure forms in its registration setting; it does not silently convert §12's express definition into a public-offering-only cause. That reading preserves each actual §10 condition rather than declaring §10 optional. The qualified definitions allow attention to their particular statutory use without replacing §12's terms by a general account of the Act's principal regulatory project.

Its strongest objection is that one defined word ordinarily retains a coherent meaning across the Act and §10 speaks in mandatory terms about prospectus content. Coverage cannot therefore rest merely on the proposition that every letter is a prospectus or that investor protection is desirable. The selected broad-scope associates answer with the actual definition, the §10-qualified exceptions, the registration/antifraud distinction, and the context proviso's operation at the statutory use in question. They do not adopt Pacific Dunlop's suggestion that the rest of the Act is categorically irrelevant to context.

**Offering-context limitation:** §§2 and 10 describe the prospectus as public-offering disclosure material; the list of notices, circulars and advertisements, the related oral-communication phrase, and the registration-content provisions give the broad words a specific statutory setting. A bilateral acquisition agreement falls outside that setting. This route can be confined to the agreement here without declaring every secondary distribution excluded regardless of its offering characteristics. Its strongest objection is that the legislature expressly included confirmations of sale, supplied carefully drawn communication exceptions, and extended §12(2) to exempt securities; public-offering limitation is not written into §12(2). The narrow-scope associates answer that the contextual proviso is itself enacted and that exempt public offerings can still explain the broader antifraud coverage without encompassing every negotiated contract.

**Oral communications and other remedies:** no independent oral misrepresentation is established as a separate basis to decide this appeal. Both routes require related treatment of substantively equivalent oral solicitation; neither adopts the anomalous rule that saying the contract aloud necessarily creates liability while the writing is exempt. Naftalin establishes §17(a)(1)'s own breadth and rejects importing §17(a)(3)'s purchaser limitation into it; it does not decide §12. Simulated Central Bank concerns bounded development of the received §10(b) action and expressly preserves Pinter's distinct express-statute construction. Its result cannot answer this §12 question by a general preference for more or less securities liability. Musick, Peeler likewise concerns a bounded incident of established shared liability.

## 31. Arizona v. Evans, No. 93-1660 — March 1, 1995

### Threshold, record, and separable remedial propositions

All eight exercise review of the federal question. The Arizona Supreme Court finally disposed of the State's suppression appeal after dismissal without prejudice; the supplied record identifies no continuing lower proceeding that must determine this suppression issue before that state judgment can be reviewed. This is a conclusion about this admitted review posture, not a general §1257 exception for every interlocutory suppression ruling. The majority's stated exclusion analysis rests on federal cases and a distinction from Leon. Its discussion of §13-3925 rejects the State's statutory characterization; it does not expressly adopt an independently sufficient state-constitutional suppression rule. Neither the dissent's account of state law nor citation of state cases supplies such a majority ground. Long, as preserved in the actual Sochor and Middlebrooks holdings, permits review of this federal/interwoven ruling. No additional state-law exception is construed from text that has not been recovered.

The warrant had been quashed; its entry remained in the database. The trial judge did not find whether court personnel, police personnel, or both caused that failure. The intermediate court's inference is not a substitute finding. For this review the associates accept the lower court's unlawful-arrest premise and address exclusion. They do not create a rule that an officer's subjective intent always controls the legality of an otherwise authorized traffic arrest. Nor do they reopen the rejected alternative discovery theory or hold the nonexistent warrant valid.

The following propositions separate the possible grounds:

- **Remedial inquiry:** Leon makes objective reasonableness and the exclusionary rule's relevant deterrent function a separate question from the underlying violation. The arresting officer's personal good faith alone does not decide the institution's recordkeeping issue.
- **Court-employee extension:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would hold that exclusion is not justified solely by an established negligent recordkeeping error of court staff functioning outside law enforcement when the arresting officer's reliance is objectively reasonable and no causally contributing police recordkeeping failure is established. The absence of a finding that this is such a case requires vacatur and remand. This does not decide police error, combined error, knowingly unreliable systems, or errors so pervasive that reliance cannot be objectively reasonable.
- **Narrower institutional application:** Souter and Breyer support vacatur and remand to determine the source, relevant recordkeeping responsibility and objective reliability of reliance. They accept nonexclusion for an established isolated court error in a reasonably maintained system where the relevant law-enforcement actors neither caused nor controlled the defect and exclusion would supply no appreciable corrective incentive. They refuse to make court payroll or the word “clerical” alone decisive. No such complete predicate has yet been found.
- **Administrative-function distinction:** Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm suppression. The existing judgment concerns a quashed warrant carried into an arrest by a failure of the ministerial warrant-record system, not a magistrate's legal judgment that probable cause justified a warrant. Excluding the resulting evidence can give those maintaining and using the arrest-record chain a reason to ensure that quashing orders are transmitted and acted upon. Identity of the particular clerk does not remove that function on this record. This is not a decision overruling Leon's protection for its judicial probable-cause setting or resolving every government computer error.

### Enhanced alternatives and remedies

**Affirm on administrative function:** its strongest support is the difference between an erroneous judicial decision and the failure to carry out a correct order quashing a warrant. The state opinion found that distinction sufficient and did not need to choose between competing fault inferences. The strongest objection is that Leon's deterrence rationale concerns the actors susceptible to the evidentiary sanction; court staff may have no law-enforcement stake, while an objectively reasonable officer cannot prevent a defect outside police control. Stevens and Ginsburg treat the arrest-record chain as a connected administrative operation whose accuracy can be improved by the incentive; the six remanding associates reject categorical irrelevance of who maintained and controlled it.

**Apply a court-clerk good-faith rule and reverse suppression outright:** Leon supplies the legal analogy, and the intermediate court inferred court error. But no trial finding establishes that premise; the Arizona Supreme Court expressly rejected treating the conflicting proof as settled. All six who accept some extension therefore refuse outright admission of the evidence or an instruction requiring denial of suppression. Their result is vacatur of the state supreme judgment and remand for proceedings under the adopted remedial distinction. No conviction is directed.

**Remand on a narrower institutional inquiry:** this avoids imputing all ministerial errors to police or categorically insulating all court records. Its counterargument is that record expansion may postpone a legal issue already sufficiently presented and adds factual demands beyond a simple court/police distinction. Souter and Breyer nevertheless regard responsibility and actual objective reliability as necessary to deciding whether the Leon analogy fits. The other four remanding associates can support the narrower nonexclusion proposition as a sufficient application without treating its additional facts as necessary in every case. Their broader employee-category proposition remains separately identified.

**Threshold dismissal for an independent state ground:** that route would be required if the majority actually supplied such a sufficient basis, as Middlebrooks did. It has not. Inferring independence from the mere presence of §13-3925 would evade the state opinion's federal distinction and transform a rejected statutory argument into an affirmative separate holding. All eight reject that inference. A later authoritative clarification would be new neutral material requiring proper review, not an assumed fact here.

## 32. Swint v. Chambers County Commission, No. 93-1636 — March 1, 1995

### Components and common disposition

All eight reach the appellate-power question and would vacate the Eleventh Circuit's county portion of the judgment, remanding with directions to dismiss the county's interlocutory appeal for want of appellate jurisdiction. That restores ordinary district proceedings on the county's surviving federal claims without deciding their merits. The individual qualified-immunity appeal dispositions are not disturbed by this county-jurisdiction decision. No appellate jurisdiction is created over the separately declined City or state-law appeals.

- **Collateral order:** the county's denial of summary judgment is not independently appealable under §1291. Its final-policymaker defense to liability is reviewable after final judgment; it is not the individual officers' immunity from suit. The cumulative Cohen requirements are not satisfied merely because a legal ruling could eliminate the county from trial. Digital Equipment's adequate-vindication alternative directly supports that conclusion without needing to decide every other collateral-order element.
- **Statutory routes:** no §1292(b) district certification and timely permitted appeal are established. That section requires the written controlling-question/substantial-disagreement/material-advancement determinations, application within ten days and appellate permission; application alone does not stay proceedings. Sections 1292(e) and 2072(c) supply rulemaking channels for additional categories, not an operative rule authorizing this particular appeal. They reinforce the need for lawful authority; their existence alone does not conclusively abolish every possible form of ancillary review.
- **Pendent power asserted here:** a proper individual-immunity appeal does not allow the court of appeals to decide a separate party's nonappealable liability defense merely for efficiency. The lower court's actual rationale was that county resolution would end its case. That is not necessity to resolve the officials' appeal.
- **Narrow reservation:** no decision whether some issue genuinely inseparable from, or necessary to meaningful resolution of, an independently appealable issue can be examined with it. Here the officers' alleged conduct, constitutional violation and clearly established law can be evaluated without deciding whether Alabama assigns final county policymaking authority to the sheriff. Shared facts do not make the county attribution issue indispensable. No general pendent-review category is affirmatively created by this reservation.
- **Remedy:** vacatur is limited to the county's §1983 and §1985(3) appellate disposition. The county is neither held liable nor granted summary judgment by this Court. There was no remaining county state-law count. The appellate merits discussion about Alabama authority supplies no Supreme Court holding or substitute district judgment.

### Enhanced two-path test

**Retain pendent review for efficiency:** the lower court's prior circuit authorities and the prospect of ending all litigation against the county offer the strongest argument. The official-immunity appeals properly place related events before the appellate court, and final-policymaker attribution may be a legal question. But those features do not make a separate liability defense effectively unreviewable after judgment or satisfy §1292(b). If eliminating a party were enough, ordinary summary-judgment denials would become appealable whenever an immunity question appears in the same order. All eight reject that rationale.

**Exclude all conceivable pendent review:** §1291, Cohen's cumulative conditions and Congress's express interlocutory/rulemaking channels strongly favor restraint. The strongest objection to a universal prohibition is that deciding a properly appealed issue may sometimes require addressing a connected question; the existence and limits of that necessity do not follow merely from the convenience rule used here. All eight therefore choose the narrower path: this county issue is independently resolvable and not required to decide the immunity issues, so the actual jurisdiction asserted fails. They reserve genuine necessity without recognizing it as a new jurisdictional category.
