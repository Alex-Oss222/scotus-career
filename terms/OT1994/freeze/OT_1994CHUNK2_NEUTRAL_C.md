## 21. McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., No. 93-1543

**Event:** Merits, January 23, 1995; argued November 2, 1994. Review of the Sixth Circuit's November 15, 1993 judgment, 9 F.3d 539, affirming summary judgment reported at 797 F. Supp. 604. Question: whether after-acquired evidence of employee wrongdoing bars all ADEA relief and, if not, its effect on available remedies.

### Record and posture

Christine McKennon worked for the Banner from May 1951 until October 31, 1990, principally as a secretary; the appellate opinion reports consistently excellent evaluations. She was 62 when discharged and sued on May 6, 1991. During her December 1991 deposition the Banner learned that, while secretary to its comptroller, she copied confidential documents accessible through that employment, removed copies from the premises, and showed them to her husband. The documents included payroll and financial records and management communications. She attributed the copying to concern about job security and a desire for protection in an anticipated discharge. The Banner then sent another termination letter stating it would have fired her immediately had it known.

The employer **assumed ADEA liability only for purposes of its summary-judgment motion**. Age discrimination is disputed in the underlying testimony and has not been adjudicated. The Sixth Circuit regarded the would-have-fired showing as undisputed: officers submitted affidavits, and McKennon acknowledged in deposition that the conduct would have caused termination. This is more specific than a showing that discharge was permissible. The judgment under review does not establish that every policy violation qualifies, that copying was the actual motive in 1990, or that discrimination is already conclusively proved for trial.

The Sixth Circuit applied the complete-remedy bar recognized in Summers v. State Farm, 864 F.2d 700 (10th Cir. 1988), and its own Johnson and Milligan-Jensen decisions. It treated the wrongdoing as eliminating compensable injury even though discovered after discharge, rejected a distinction between application fraud and employment misconduct, and rejected the claimed nexus to anticipated discrimination. Its footnote 7 separately recognized that genuinely protected opposition or participation under §623(d) could not become a lawful reason to escape liability, but regarded this copying and removal of confidential documents as unprotected. Do not turn that bounded ruling into a general rule against obtaining evidence of discrimination.

### Entering law and paths

The ADEA makes discharge because of age unlawful, 29 U.S.C. §623(a)(1). Section 626(b) incorporates specified FLSA enforcement provisions, deems amounts owing as a result of a violation unpaid wages, limits liquidated damages to willful violations, and authorizes appropriate legal or equitable relief to effectuate the Act's purposes, including employment, reinstatement, promotion, and unpaid-wage liability. Section 626(c)(1) provides an aggrieved person's civil action subject to termination of that individual right when the EEOC commences an enforcement action for that employee; no such EEOC action is established here. These textual remedial limits are separate from discrimination's liability elements.

The entering Hazen Paper holding requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; other impropriety is not automatically age discrimination. It preserves liability when an ostensibly different criterion is an age proxy or age also motivates the decision. Its Thurston willfulness rule requires knowledge or reckless disregard of the ADEA violation, not merely awareness that the statute may apply. St. Mary's Honor Center preserves Burdine's allocation and rejects automatic liability from disbelieving an employer's explanation; it supplies no new replacement persuasion allocation. Neither holding adjudicates after-acquired misconduct.

Supported alternatives requiring independent assessment:

1. Affirm the complete bar by treating the conceded legitimate basis for termination as defeating recoverable injury. The strongest objection is that an unknown ground did not cause the challenged act and a universal bar could remove both compensation for the earlier unlawful period and statutory deterrence.
2. Separate unlawful motivation from remedial entitlement: reverse the all-relief bar while allowing proved disqualifying wrongdoing to limit prospective relief and some backpay. The strongest objection is that an employee should not obtain continued pay or employment that lawful discovery would have ended. The actual cutoff, proof requirement, role of equitable circumstances, and treatment of distinct relief must be specified rather than borrowed from an unexamined general fairness rule.
3. Confine the decision to rejection or acceptance of the categorical summary-judgment ground and leave individualized remedy calculation for remand. If a broader remedial rule is adopted, identify why the record permits it. No amount, willfulness finding, front-pay award, reinstatement order, or final discrimination finding follows automatically.

Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Price Waterhouse, 490 U.S. 228 (1989), concern actual decisionmaking with lawful and unlawful considerations, and require careful treatment of the difference between a contemporaneous lawful reason and a later-discovered reason. The 1991 Title VII amendments must not be silently applied to this ADEA discharge. The October 1990 discharge predates those amendments.

## 22. Fargo Women's Health Organization v. Schafer, No. 93-1712

**Event:** Merits, February 13, 1995; user-established argument December 12, 1994. No. 93-1712 is the assigned Supreme Court docket, distinct from Eighth Circuit No. 93-1579. Review of 18 F.3d 526 (February 10, 1994), affirming summary judgment at 819 F. Supp. 862 (D.N.D. 1993). No grant date or order is invented.

### Questions, record, and disputed propositions

The granted question encompasses informed consent, the twenty-four-hour interval, definitions, the medical-emergency exception, facial review, and the pre-enforcement record and remedy. The actual challenges below concerned the new informed-consent provisions and emergency definition, and the preexisting abortion definition and general penalty. Parental-consent provisions were **not challenged**. The opinion expressly says the Organization pressed **vagueness**, not an undue-burden challenge to the medical-emergency definition. Treat preservation and the granted scope explicitly before expanding that component.

The district court issued a preliminary injunction on August 23, 1991 against informed consent and waiting provisions. The parties agreed to await Casey and later litigated summary judgment. The district court granted the State's motion, dissolved the injunction and dismissed the action. The Eighth Circuit affirmed. The supplied established merits posture does not require importing any other Supreme Court action in this litigation.

**Correction to the thin intake:** The case has no district-court fact findings, but it does have a substantial affidavit/deposition record submitted over a three-month period. The appellate majority assumed the nonmovants' factual assertions true, distinguished those from conclusory assertions, and held them legally insufficient under its two stated approaches. The dispute is not simply whether any evidence exists. It includes whether the majority's assumed facts and telephone construction dispose of every material burden under the governing law and whether additional findings or proceedings are needed.

Patients' travel distances, lodging costs, work and appointment constraints, protest-related exposure, and physicians' presence only on Thursdays and Fridays appear in the reported record. Do not assign numerical magnitudes or find the alleged burdens proved. The majority read the Act to allow information by telephone and written certification at the procedure, eliminating a statutory two-visit requirement. It therefore attributed remaining travel and clinic-day constraints to circumstances rather than the State's command. The competing question is whether the required interval nevertheless adds clinically or practically consequential delay or other interference to those circumstances, especially for a defined affected subgroup. That causal question requires the complete six-step rule below, not an assumption that all geographic difficulty is either attributable to the State or constitutionally irrelevant.

The lower court considered a March 1993 Attorney General opinion persuasive. It cited Johnson v. Baker, 21 N.W.2d 355, 364 (N.D. 1945), for an Attorney General opinion's force until contrary court ruling, and Svanes v. Grenz, 492 N.W.2d 576, 579 (N.D. 1992), for respectful consideration of persuasive opinions. The court independently adopted the same text construction. It expressly reserved a new challenge if the Attorney General or courts later required more than one in-person visit. That reservation is not a prediction of changed enforcement.

### Construction and qualifications that must remain separate

- The lower court permits the performing or referring physician or an agent to communicate the required information by telephone. The attending physician must exercise medical judgment to determine probable gestational age; an agent may collect the relevant history, including last menstrual period, and communicate the physician's determination. There is no categorical exclusive-physician delivery rule.
- Written patient certification and receipt by the physician or agent precede the abortion, but need not precede the woman's arrival by twenty-four hours. The duty does not compel acceptance, reading, or endorsement of state materials. Materials must be provided if she elects to view them; statutory state supply is free.
- “May be available” does not guarantee benefit eligibility or payment. A legal child-support duty is not a promise of collection. The express freedom to comment or dissociate concerns printed materials; the lower court additionally construes the Act to permit comment on assistance and paternal-liability statements. Distinguish enacted text from this interpretation.
- The North Dakota text supplied here contains **neither Pennsylvania's separate §3205(c) disclosure-health defense nor its rape-related permission to omit paternal-support information**. Do not import either into North Dakota. Their absence may matter when applying the entering Casey holdings, whose qualifications must be respected.
- Emergency exemption and clinical-judgment language do not require catastrophic deterioration. The lower court reads the purpose clause and clinical standard to mean compliance may not significantly threaten the woman's life or health. That is a protective lower-court construction, not extra enacted words. The “prior to abortion if possible” proviso qualifies the emergency explanation. Written certification is a separate obligation, not authority to delay necessary care.
- The challenged abortion definition has both a pregnancy-termination object and a purpose distinction. The providers' example was amniocentesis inadvertently resulting in termination. The lower court rejected applying the definition to an unintended termination by reading its terms and criminal fault requirements together. No claim about a particular completed prosecution is presented.

### Controlling Casey law

Use the complete text in the entering slice, Standards and Tests, **Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation**, present operation effective June 29, 1992. Its five direct supporters were Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; it is a full controlling rule, not a Marks synthesis. Kennedy joined retained protected choice and particular narrower holdings, not the full six steps. No successor inherits a predecessor's position.

1. Identify the duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement and protected baseline. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto, or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care is independently invalid.
2. Identify actual function and objective purpose. Truthful persuasion is permitted; an objectively established obstructive purpose independently invalidates. Legislative opposition to abortion alone does not prove that purpose.
3. Challengers establish concrete additional interference through a stable, duty-defined affected population and comparison with the system absent the challenged obligation or identified package. Cumulative effects count without duplication; reliable predictions can establish prospective injury. Exceptions and alternatives count only if usable.
4. Apply independent prohibitions first, including the substantial-obstacle bar. Remaining severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible justification and fit; the State must answer a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome adequate alternative. No new study, scientific certainty or numerical calculus is invariably required. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but other constitutional objections remain.
5. Protect timely clinical judgment and accurate, nonmisleading, materially relevant communication. Professional evidence has weight without categorical professional immunity; appropriate fault rules and adequate definiteness remain necessary. Accuracy alone does not justify burdensome delivery, timing or certification.
6. Match relief to proved invalid duty or application and preserve severable lawful requirements. An indivisible mandatory condition can fail when ordinary operation produces proved meaningful interference and insufficient justification. Some voluntary compliance does not cure compulsory injury; otherwise confine relief to established applications or groups. Dependent certifications and sanctions require express treatment.

The slice's verbatim text controls over this synopsis, including all burdens and qualifications. The Court's Casey decision invalidated the fixed Pennsylvania interval while preserving bounded content and other severable obligations. Accordingly, analogy to a supposed Supreme Court approval of that interval supplies no entering-law ground. Nor does absence of a substantial obstacle alone complete this Court's inquiry. The lower court's strict no-valid-applications approach cannot replace Casey step 6.

## 23. Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp., No. 93-1525

**Event:** Merits, February 21, 1995; argued November 7, 1994. Review of 12 F.3d 388 (2d Cir. December 27, 1993), reversing the district judgment reported at 811 F. Supp. 993. Question: whether Amtrak is a governmental actor for First Amendment purposes despite its statutory disclaimer.

### Record, judgment below, and remedy limits

Michael Lebron makes billboard works commenting on public affairs. In November 1992 he contracted with Transportation Displays, Inc. (TDI), Amtrak's advertising manager, for two months on the large “Spectacular” display at Penn Station, for $16,500 monthly. The lease reserved approval of copy and a termination right if Amtrak found it objectionable. Before signing, Lebron disclosed that his work was generally political but withheld its precise subject. The submitted photomontage criticized the Coors family's political activity and support for the Nicaraguan contras. Amtrak rejected it under a policy against political advertising on that sign.

After expedited discovery and trial on documentary submissions, the district court found governmental action and an unconstitutional speech restriction, and ordered two months' display after any stay ended. It did not reach the alternative contract claim. The Second Circuit stayed that order, then directed dismissal of the complaint on the federal-action threshold. It did **not** decide the First Amendment merits. It also directed leaving the contract dispute to state court; TDI's counterclaim concerning termination of the lease was not decided.

The majority did not dispute the findings of substantial governmental involvement in Amtrak's structure, financing and property. It treated the statutory designation as private as the starting point and required a sufficient connection between government and the challenged action, relying on Myron v. Conrail, 752 F.2d 50, and related employment cases. It regarded general funding, regulation, stockholding and board representation, without federal supervision of day-to-day decisions, as inadequate. Judge Newman's dissent distinguished direct political-content control from employment disputes; he nevertheless would not restore the mandatory-display injunction, considering damages or declaratory relief possible while disputing the sign's forum status and the appropriate scope of equity. That disagreement confirms that the actor threshold and speech remedy are separate questions.

### Statute, entering law, and live alternatives

The lower opinion quotes former 45 U.S.C. §541: Amtrak was to be operated and managed as a for-profit corporation and “will not be an agency or establishment of the United States Government.” The 1994 recodification, Pub. L. 103-272, supplies 49 U.S.C. §24301(a): Amtrak “shall be operated and managed as a for-profit corporation” and “is not a department, agency, or instrumentality of the United States Government.” Its revision notes map these phrases to §541 and state the stylistic revision. Do not treat recodification wording as a new constitutional adjudication.

The 1994 §24302 fixes a nine-member board: the Secretary of Transportation; Amtrak's president; three Presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed individuals representing specified labor, state-executive and business interests; two Presidential selections from commuter-authority nominees under the statutory conditions; and two preferred-shareholder selections. The governmental preferred shareholder supplies those two selections on the reported record. Amtrak's president is chosen within the corporate structure and chairs the board; do not collapse this into nine direct Presidential appointments. The lower court reports six Presidential appointments, two government selections as preferred shareholder, and the ninth selection by the board. The Secretary's ex-officio role and appointment of the Secretary explain the difference between that shorthand and the statute's detailed categories.

A federal corporation's statutory classification, statutory jurisdiction, constitutional actor status and sovereign immunity are distinct. The entering Red Cross jurisdiction rule does not decide constitutional status. The entering McCollum holdings concern attribution of a specified use of governmental machinery; they do not make every private beneficiary of public power a government entity. Current Hess examines whether a separate bistate public entity shares state immunity, not whether it must respect the First Amendment. Current FEC decided litigation authority and timeliness, without a controlling merits rule about governmental control.

**Path A:** Treat statutory creation, governmental purpose and continuing governmental control of corporate direction as sufficient for constitutional governmental status despite the classification clause. Strongest counter: Congress gave a distinct corporate form and left ordinary operating choices to the company; a rule based on financial aid or appointment alone risks swallowing private entities. The proposed criterion must distinguish permanent public control from subsidized or regulated private activity.

**Path B:** Accept separate private status and ask whether this particular refusal has a sufficiently close governmental nexus under Jackson, 419 U.S. 345, Burton, 365 U.S. 715, and the corporate precedents. Strongest counter: demanding an external command for every act of a government-created, government-directed institution may let the Government avoid constitutional obligations through incorporation.

**Path C:** Find attribution for this use of an institution's advertising resources, while preserving separate decisions about other activities. Strongest counter: the First Amendment applies to governmental action as such; varying entity status by the constitutional interest asserted requires an articulated doctrinal basis and must not merely prefer speech claimants.

If the threshold is resolved for Lebron, remand for the unadjudicated First Amendment merits, forum classification, contract issues and appropriate relief is available. Reinstating the district injunction needs a separate supported basis; actor status alone does not command display. If the threshold fails, dismissal of the federal claim and disposition of supplemental contract proceedings must fit the lower mandate. No damages amount is established.

**Preservation:** The federal-action claim was raised and decided; the precise distinction between an argument that Amtrak itself is governmental and an argument attributing a private actor's conduct requires the petition/appellate briefing if it becomes dispositive. The lower opinion itself relies expressly on §541 and corporate status. ## 24. Milwaukee Brewery Workers' Pension Plan v. Jos. Schlitz Brewing Co., No. 93-768

**Event:** Merits, February 21, 1995; argued December 5, 1994. Review of Jos. Schlitz Brewing Co. and Stroh Brewery Co. v. Milwaukee Brewery Workers' Pension Plan, 3 F.3d 994 (7th Cir. August 13, 1993), Nos. 92-3811 and 92-3894. The granted question concerns when computational interest begins under §1399(c)(1)(A)(i), not a new review of every allocation or fiduciary issue in the appellate opinion.

### Facts, prior proceedings, and exact surviving question

Schlitz left joint bargaining in January 1981 but remained in the pension plan until August 14, 1981. The Plan year coincided with the calendar year. The parties disputed attempted adoption of direct-attribution withdrawal liability; arbitration retained the presumptive method and the parties' actuaries agreed on approximately $23.3 million under assumptions not disputed in this case. The district court upheld the method but added interest for 1981, plus prejudgment interest on that added amount. The court of appeals preserved the method ruling, reversed the 1981 interest requirement and directed enforcement of the arbitral award in full.

The Plan calculated its disputed interest from December 31, 1980, the prior-year valuation date, while Schlitz was still a contributing employer. Schlitz made its first actual payment October 29, 1981. The statutory assumed first-payment date was January 1, 1982. Distinguish these three dates from the August 14 withdrawal, and distinguish interest within an amortization calculation from default interest and prejudgment interest on a disputed assessment.

The Seventh Circuit said the Plan had **not argued for partial-year interest between actual withdrawal in August and January 1982**. That intermediate date cannot be treated as a preserved alternative demand without establishing a lawful basis for reaching it. The appellate court viewed the prior-year valuation rule as simplifying calculation of underfunding, not making Schlitz indebted before withdrawal. It also declined to give controlling deference to an earlier PBGC litigation position absent regulation, ruling, established agency practice or assurance that the position remained the agency's. PBGC had not participated in that appeal. Do not present that limitation as proof that no later date-eligible agency filing exists.

The district judge's initial omission of the amount of prejudgment interest did not leave the reported appeal dismissed: the parties reached agreement, the October 20, 1992 order apparently accepted it, and payment had occurred. The court treated the judgment as appealable under Mallis despite the missing Rule 58 form. No present jurisdictional defect is established from that earlier clerical omission.

### Statute in terms and qualifications

Official 1994 29 U.S.C. §1399(c)(1)(A)(i):

> Except as provided in subparagraphs (B) and (D) of this paragraph and in paragraphs (4) and (5), an employer shall pay the amount determined under section 1391 of this title, adjusted if appropriate first under section 1389 of this title and then under section 1386 of this title over the period of years necessary to amortize the amount in level annual payments determined under subparagraph (C), calculated as if the first payment were made on the first day of the plan year following the plan year in which the withdrawal occurs and as if each subsequent payment were made on the first day of each subsequent plan year. Actual payment shall commence in accordance with paragraph (2).

Clause (ii) uses the assumptions for the most recent actuarial valuation in determining the amortization period. Subparagraph (B) limits ordinary liability to the first twenty annual payments when the computed period exceeds twenty years; (D) governs termination by withdrawal of every employer or substantially all pursuant to an arrangement and displaces that limit. No mass-withdrawal or twenty-year-cap dispute is supplied here. Subparagraph (C) calculates the annual amount, including its dated plan-election and partial-withdrawal qualifications; that annual-amount formula is not disputed in the supplied question.

Section 1399(b)(1) requires notice of liability and schedule and demand as soon as practicable after withdrawal. Paragraph (c)(2) starts actual payment no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal. Paragraph (c)(3) provides quarterly installments unless plan rules specify other intervals and imposes interest on overdue payments from due date to payment. Paragraph (c)(4) allows prepayment of outstanding annual payments plus accrued interest without penalty, subject to redetermination if the withdrawal later qualifies under the mass-withdrawal rule. Paragraph (c)(5) permits acceleration after default plus interest from the due date of the first untimely payment; statutory default includes nonpayment uncured sixty days after written notice and qualifying events defined in plan rules indicating likely inability to pay. Paragraph (c)(6) ordinarily uses prevailing-market rates under PBGC regulations, except the amortization assumptions in (c)(1)(A)(ii). None of these independently resolves the disputed starting date without construction of (c)(1)(A)(i).

### Entering law, paths, and scope

Concrete Pipe supplies lawful independent arbitration, ordinary rebuttal of factual determinations and distinct actuarial rebuttal routes; it does not decide this amortization starting date. The actual question is one of statutory law, and the lower parties agreed it did not require deference to the actuary's computation. Do not adopt the Seventh Circuit's broader gloss on §1401(c) in place of the exact entering Concrete Pipe rule. Current Brown confirms text/context before agency deference, without answering this different statutory calculation.

**Plan's path:** The withdrawal amount is valued at the preceding year-end and then amortized using ordinary actuarial methods; carrying that amount forward with interest makes its economic value constant. The assumed payment dates may regulate payment timing without shifting the valuation of the underlying debt. Strongest counter: the statute expressly assumes payments beginning with the following plan year and separates actual payment; capitalizing an extra year may charge for a period when contributions continued and no withdrawal obligation existed.

**Employer's path:** Use the following plan year's first day as the computational starting point and apply the statutory assumed first payment there, with interest thereafter between assumed payments. Strongest counter: ordinary amortization generally preserves present value, and one must explain how the first “as if” clause performs work rather than simply restating that no earlier obligation existed. The first assumed payment, annual amount, period, discount/interest assumptions and actual-payment obligation must remain distinct.

**Narrow disposition:** Resolve only the prior-year “gap” interest actually demanded, preserving the unrelated allocation-method and arbitral enforcement rulings. Any selection of an intermediate date must address the lower preservation fact above. No general ruling on collection-delay interest, default, prepayment, plan termination, mass withdrawal, or all PBGC interpretive instruments is necessary merely to answer the supplied question.
