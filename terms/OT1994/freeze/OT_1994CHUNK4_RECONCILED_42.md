## 42. Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — April 18, 1995

**Reconciled disposition:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas affirm the denial of §27A(b) reinstatement and join Stone-Zsela's opinion for the Court in full. Courts would independently determine eligibility and retain the securities merits, defenses, and remedies; Congress dictates no finding or liability.

**Alternative-path reconciliation:** The historical Stevens/Ginsburg reasons support the position that Congress restored a limited filing regime and left liability adjudication judicial; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg maintain that position in dissent, and Souter distinguishes an express statutory entitlement from reopening by judicial retroactivity alone. **Rule, remedy, and nonreach:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent. Finality is itself an Article III protection; it does not disappear merely because Congress leaves the underlying merits undecided. The August 13, 1991 dismissal became final without appeal before §27A(b)'s December 19, 1991 enactment, and the February 11, 1992 motion sought the vacatur and reinstatement the rule forbids. That eligibility and the merits would be judicially determined after reinstatement does not save the command, because the judicial act Congress sets aside is the completed dismissal itself. The Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed: the dismissal remains final, and the motion was properly denied. The rule leaves existing law in place: Congress may change the law while a case remains pending; may expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases, subject to other constitutional limits; may change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework; and may not require judges to adopt particular findings or impose predetermined liability. Relief from a final judgment on valid Rule 60(b) grounds or through other constitutionally permissible judicial procedures remains available. Subsection (a), litigation still pending on direct review, government waiver, due process, severability beyond withholding this application, fraud merits, and damages remain outside the necessary decision. No determination of the underlying securities claims is made.

**Precedent treatment:** Morgan Stanley's affirmance by an equally divided Court is not precedent and is neither applied nor distinguished. Robertson distinguishes genuine legislation from prescribed adjudication and left the final-judgment question open; the Court answers it for completed private judgments and leaves Robertson's pending-case rule intact. Klein protects independent adjudication, and McCullough protects completed determinations against legislative findings or declarations of liability; both remain in force and support the finality rule without being extended to every statute that affects a judgment. Beam's finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, the limit on legislative reopening. Wheeling Bridge permits new law to govern the future operation of continuing equitable relief and is distinguished; Sioux Nation concerns sovereign waiver of the Government's own defense and is distinguished. Chase Securities remains a due-process revival authority, not a controlling Article III answer.
