## Common record and permissible scope

The Government's principal request remains reversal of the constitutional judgment. Its alternative expressly protects **respondents and class members**, while removing nonparty Executive employees and proposing a nexus limitation on covered payments. Crane is a named respondent outside the below-GS–16 class. His absence from the class therefore supplies no reason, by itself, to exclude him from the alternative's respondent coverage.

No express Crane-specific merits concession appears. Neither a mandatory acceptance of the Government's whole alternative nor a jurisdictional bar to independently justified remedial action follows. The Court retains its responsibility to formulate lawful relief on the actually decided merits. It must distinguish three matters: the named respondent's existing judgment, the certified class, and senior nonparties whose merits were not represented. Preserving Crane's individual relief on this procedural ground creates no categorical rule for all GS–16 or higher employees.

For each Justice, the strongest argument for individual reconsideration is that a GS–16 employee's responsibilities might call for a different constitutional balance, and the Government sought full reversal. The answer on the **conditional** component is narrower: once the main theory has failed, the particular alternative actually sought does not ask to remove Crane; grade alone cannot convert its express respondents-plus-class formulation into class-only relief. A distinct record-supported legal reason for altering Crane's relief would have to be identified and adjudicated rather than attributed to that alternative.

## Precisely agreed proposition and limits

All eight associates support this conditional proposition: **When resolving the Government's alternative objection to relief extending beyond the represented parties, Crane remains a named respondent and is not removed or sent to separate reconsideration solely because the class is limited to employees below GS–16. Retaining his individual relief at the scope otherwise supported by the Court's merits and remedial ruling does not decide the constitutional claims of senior nonparties.**

This is not an eight-vote count for the final NTEU judgment or for invalidating the ban. A Justice who sustains the Government's main defense can still vote to reverse the underlying constitutional relief, including Crane's. The reconciliation context must combine this conditional position with each independently frozen main commitment, without treating agreement about what an alternative means as agreement that its triggering premise is satisfied.

No Justice finds an express substantive concession, a universal appellate waiver, or an independent jurisdictional disability. None commands a new Crane remand on grade alone. None imports the Government's proposed general nexus test as enacted statutory text or decides its ultimate severability validity in this checkpoint. Beneficiary scope and payment scope remain separate.
