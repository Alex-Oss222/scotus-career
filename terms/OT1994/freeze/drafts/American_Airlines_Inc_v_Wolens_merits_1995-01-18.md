## Controlling holdings

#### State consumer-fraud standards governing these airline benefits are preempted

**Controlling proposition:** The Airline Deregulation Act's transaction-period §1305(a)(1) preempts application of the Illinois Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act to the pleaded devaluation of earned airline-travel awards through changed redemption restrictions, because that claim enforces a state-imposed substantive standard directly related to airline rates and services. General applicability, a damages remedy and the general §1506 savings clause do not avoid that express prohibition; materially different duties with only tenuous, remote or peripheral connections remain outside this ruling.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, consumer-fraud portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Five of the eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule and explanation. Justices O'Connor and Thomas also vote to preempt this claim on their separate broader ground; Justice Stevens dissents on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged award restrictions concern the price and availability of airline transportation. Capacity controls govern how many seats passengers may obtain at an award level; the alleged restrictions on use of earned credits govern what travel those credits purchase. Morales supplies the rule that a generally applicable law can regulate airline rates and services when its application has the required connection. The Illinois court's description of frequent-flyer benefits as peripheral does not answer that direct relation. A damages action may enforce the same substantive command as prospective regulation, so the form of relief does not remove the conflict. Section 1506 preserves remedies outside the specific prohibition rather than undoing it. Cipollone's distinct cigarette provisions require attention to the duty and wording at issue, not a universal exception for claims called fraud. The Court decides this pleaded challenge without resolving the disputed retroactive application of particular blackout dates or declaring every airline deception claim preempted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., 504 U.S. 374: applies the controlling connection-or-reference rule and the specific-preemption/general-savings distinction to these award restrictions; its separate fare-marketing effects holding remains confined to that package, and its remote-effects reservation remains.
- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504: distinguishes its clause-specific preservation of general intentional falsehood; that cigarette holding supplies no universal airline-fraud exemption and retains its own scope.

#### An airline's actual voluntary bargain remains enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1305(a)(1) does not preempt ordinary court enforcement of an airline's self-imposed contractual obligations, including ordinary contract remedies, when the claim is confined to the undertaking the parties actually made. Courts may use ordinary contract law to identify and enforce that undertaking, but may not enlarge or reform it through a state policy external to the agreement; the pleaded contract claims survive dismissal only within that boundary.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, contract portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six of eight participants directly adopt this rationale; no synthesis of narrower separate opinions is required. Justices O'Connor and Thomas dissent on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that enforcing the parties' own bargain differs from prescribing its substantive terms. The airline chooses the obligation; ordinary adjudication determines what it promised and supplies the usual remedy for a proved breach. The Act's prohibition on state enactment or enforcement of governing legal standards, read with its savings clause, leaves room for that private ordering. Cipollone supplies a useful distinction between a voluntarily undertaken promise and a duty imposed by the State, although the airline clause requires its own construction. The contrary position correctly observes that contracts depend on law for enforcement. That observation does not make every privately chosen term a state-prescribed airline policy. The limitation is therefore substantive: a contract label cannot preserve an external fairness requirement. American's reservation of authority to alter its program may defeat the claimed promise, but its reach must be determined below. No binding promise protecting earned credits, breach, reliance, causation or damages is found here; the Court orders only the confined contract proceeding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Cipollone, 505 U.S. 504: extends the voluntary-source distinction by analogy to this independently construed airline clause; its narrower warranty ground does not become a blanket common-law exemption.
- Morales, 504 U.S. 374: preserves preemption of state-prescribed airline terms while resolving the contract-enforcement question that Morales left open; neither that case's injunction nor its fare-marketing effects holding decides the actual bargain here.
- Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. American Train Dispatchers Association, 499 U.S. 117: distinguishes the dependence of a contract on law for enforceability from the source of the substantive obligation under this statute; its construction of the distinct railroad immunity provision is undisturbed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The lower courts must determine the actual agreement, including whether American reserved authority to alter already earned credits, applicable ordinary defenses and any proved remedy. Good-faith and implied-term tools may identify the agreement but may not reintroduce external state rate policy; no particular such theory, punitive award or nonflight-benefit claim is adjudicated. The earlier rejection of injunctive relief remains undisturbed. No final fraud or contract liability, amount or precise retroactive blackout application is decided.
