## Loewenstein: separately identify every presented component

**Statutory classification.** The admitted question concerns the respondent's repo income and Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 under 31 U.S.C. section 3124(a). Actual Federal Reserve book-entry delivery, no separate promissory note, paired repurchase/resale promises, and a privately negotiated return remain facts; loan and purchase are disputed legal or economic characterizations. The taxpayer additionally invokes Frank Lyon Co. v. United States, 435 U.S. 561 (1978), arguing that independent business and regulatory reasons support respecting the sale-and-repurchase form. The State answers that the obligation producing the taxed return, rather than a transactional label or classification in another legal setting, controls this exemption. Neither position denies actual delivery. Frank Lyon's characterization argument does not by itself answer the different statutory immunity question.

**Discrimination.** The taxpayer separately contends that Nebraska discriminates against federal-obligation repos compared with repos involving state or local obligations. Its identified support is that Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 expressly addresses federal-obligation repos. The competing response is that naming the federal transactions does not establish favorable treatment of otherwise comparable state-obligation repo income. No statute, ruling or other state policy granting that asserted comparator exemption is identified in the supplied material. Preserve the constitutional claim independently of statutory classification and impairment of federal borrowing; assess the claim against its actual support. Do not invent the comparator exemption, treat it as conceded, or attribute an admission that discrimination would be lawful. The evidentiary limit is absence of an identified disparate policy in these materials, not a newly found fact about every Nebraska tax practice.

**Federal borrowing.** The taxpayer's argument that taxation burdens federal financing remains distinct. Peter Sternlight's 1986 affidavit addresses consequences if repos lose liquidity or flexibility or become unavailable to institutional investors. Its subject was purchase/sale versus secured-loan classification in bankruptcy and related settings. The reported Federal Reserve Bank of New York brief, pages 9-10, distinguishes that classification question from the economic effect of taxing repo transaction income. The 1984 congressional-hearing letter similarly confines the purchase/sale endorsement to bankruptcy. The taxpayer can argue that repo financing's practical role bears on federal borrowing; the State can answer that evidence of classification's effects does not establish the claimed effect of this tax. The evidence's actual subject must be respected without converting it into a finding that taxation can never affect borrowing. Rockford's remote-or-conjectural-burden limit remains available at its actual scope; no numerical economic threshold is supplied.

**Add-back argument and review scope.** At argument the taxpayer invoked Neb. Rev. Stat. section 77-2716(1)(e)(i): a Seller-Borrower who subtracts federal-security interest under section 77-2716(1)(a) might have to add back interest on indebtedness incurred to carry those obligations. The taxpayer uses that hypothetical to challenge the practical account of the exemption. The competing response distinguishes the respondent's repo income from a different taxpayer's deduction and add-back treatment. The Nebraska judgment did not cite the add-back provision; no Seller-Borrower assessment, respondent-specific application, tax amount or refund computation is established. Addressing the argument's bearing on the reviewed exemption is different from deciding the separate provision's construction or validity. No expanded grant is supplied. An independent add-back holding would require the actual term-effective provision, relevant assessment and presentation record, plus a supported basis for reaching that distinct question; they are not supplied by the hypothetical.

### Authority, remedy choice and account application

The legal question includes both availability and appropriateness of this specific injunction. The ICC invokes the express credit and enforcement provisions and argues that refusing the loss-of-discount injunction would leave the credit rules without effective enforcement in this bankruptcy setting. The trustee answers that the injunction circumvents the filed-rate command and that cease-and-desist measures, compliance injunctions and statutory sanctions remain available. The published October 1993 lower opinion records those alternatives and its balancing analysis. Independent analysis must therefore examine the actual request, conceded violations, statutory source, proposed alternative enforcement and review of remedy choice. It must neither stop at abstract power nor assume that statutory authority alone mandates the requested injunction.

Commercial Metals distinguishes its rejection of an implied consignor defense from public enforcement tools. Maislin rejects its particular negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy while leaving distinct statutory remedies for their own analysis. Current Reiter supplies enacted claims, recoupment and judicial procedure; Kmart concerns an effectively canceled tariff component, not equitable disregard of a valid rate. Current ABF preserves delegated remedial discretion and meaningful review with distinct majority and narrower concurrent grounds, but reviewed a Board remedial order: transferring its principles to an ICC request for a court-issued injunction requires attention to that difference. MCI keeps interpretation and deference within actual delegated authority. None supplies universal agency deference, judicial replacement of an agency's choice, or a prior answer to this injunction. Keep statutory interpretation, public-enforcement authority, review of remedy choice and ultimate bill application separately identifiable.

### Preserved operative qualifications

Authorized credit begins the day after bill presentation. The default is fifteen days including weekends and holidays; the permitted combined credit cannot exceed thirty days. Standard-credit service charges, published additional-period charges and published post-expiry charges are distinct. Post-expiry charges neither authorize delay nor eliminate the carrier's good-faith reassessment duty before extending credit again.

Section 1320.2(g)(1) permits reasonable, certain collection-expense damages through specified dollar or percentage charges or loss of an identified discount after a stated payment date no earlier than authorized-credit expiration. Under (g)(2), terms and conditions must be clear and avoid unlawful prejudice or unjust discrimination; claims must concern original, separate, independent bills, not aggregate balance-due demands for past shipments; clear clerical or ministerial errors such as nonreceipt, a lost payment check or misaddressing are excluded; damages cannot duplicate collection fees independently imposed by a bill of lading for the same transportation; and revised billing or notice must issue within ninety days after authorized credit expires. Section (g)(3)'s separate contractual bill-of-lading collection route remains distinct. Section 1320.3(c) requires original bill or accompanying notice of late-payment penalties, time limits, service/collection charges and discount terms. No concession establishes an unreported account amount or satisfies an unreported exception.

Section 11702(a)(4)'s exception for actions under a provision governing rate reasonableness or discrimination remains part of the enforcement inquiry. The earlier separate coded-rate component remains outside the reviewed credit-rule dispute. The pre-reform shipment regime governs; the Negotiated Rates Act or Trucking Industry Regulatory Reform Act cannot supply new relief without applicable transition text and a presented claim. Account-specific relief, separate private defenses, other statutory relief and estate distribution remain distinct from the challenged liquidated-damages class.
