## 29. Anderson v. Green

### Questions and actual posture

The reviewed judgment affirms a preliminary injunction against California's first-year AFDC benefit cap for bona fide new residents. The merits question concerns the interstate-migration/equal-protection limits on that differential. The antecedent question is what concrete implementation controversy and effective relief remain after loss of a federal approval that state law makes necessary to operate the provision. If merits review cannot continue, the separate question is the disposition of the injunction, appellate judgment, and underlying suit. No threshold answer or constitutional answer is presumed.

California's November merits brief seeks reversal of the constitutional injunction; its December reply argues that administrative reconsideration and likely recurrence keep the case live. The reply reports recipients' argument that the case is moot. The available direct recipients' submission has not been retained, so this packet does not attribute a specific proposed vacatur mandate to them. It is inaccurate to describe California as having conceded mootness or as itself requesting a threshold dismissal in place of its merits request.

### Established facts and the two distinct lower judgments

In [Green, 811 F. Supp. 516](https://static.case.law/f-supp/811/html/0516-01.html), January 28, 1993, the district court addressed families who were bona fide California residents but received less than otherwise comparable longer-term residents because of the benefits available in the State from which they moved. Section 11450.03(a) capped the first twelve months' payment at the former State's maximum. The court found the recipients likely to succeed under the interstate-travel/equal-treatment cases and found irreparable hardship. It rejected budget savings as justification for penalizing migration; it also rejected the offered classification under rationality review. Those are preliminary-injunction determinations, not a final adjudication of every claim.

The injunction pending judgment covers §11450.03 and its implementing regulations, including MPP EAS 89-402.4, All County Letter 92-98, and All County Information Notice 1-54-92 insofar as they determine benefits by prior residence. It orders notice to county welfare directors and requires no bond. The complaint sought declaratory and injunctive relief; no damages or accrued-payment claim is established in the retained materials. The opinion announces that the court will provisionally certify a class by separate order; footnote 4 describes the requested class of present and future applicants and recipients, and notes defendants' nonopposition. The injunction itself protects the described class. The retained opinion does not include the separate certification order or a later certification history. The announced class scope prevents assuming the controversy ended solely when a named plaintiff completed twelve months of residence. The [Ninth Circuit, 26 F.3d 95](https://static.case.law/f3d/26/html/0095-01.html), April 29, amended June 13, 1994, affirmed the preliminary injunction for the district court's reasons and retained panel jurisdiction.

In the separate [Beno v. Shalala, 30 F.3d 1057](https://static.case.law/f3d/30/html/1057-01.html), July 13, 1994, the Ninth Circuit vacated the Secretary's maintenance-of-effort waiver, identified in its disposition as the waiver of 42 U.S.C. §1396a(c)(1), and remanded through the district court for additional administrative consideration of the recipients' objections. The challenged project included grant reductions and work-incentive features. The court treated the legal merits as presented on the developed record despite the preliminary-injunction route. It found insufficient consideration of the statutory experimental purpose and the recipients' objections. It did not prohibit every possible future demonstration or adjudicate the human-subject and ADA claims. Footnote 47 expressly leaves the unchallenged 100-hour-rule and other income-disregard waivers in effect. This is an objective lower-court event; its APA analysis is not imported as this Court's controlling law.

The necessary link to §11450.03 is supported independently by California's own [November 14 merits submission, statutory text and pages 3–5](../sources/chunk3-b-neutral/Anderson_primary_excerpts.md). Subsection (b) states that the section shall not become operative until the necessary Secretary's approval to ensure continuing compliance with the identified federal programs. California identifies the original October 29, 1992 approval and acknowledges in footnote 3 that Beno removed the approval necessary for this section. Its plural shorthand there must not enlarge Beno's actual one-waiver disposition. The reproduced statute identifies Title IV and Subchapter 19 beginning at §1396; its printed/OCR title-number transcription is flagged in the source excerpt, rather than silently repaired. The federal-approval prerequisite is not in doubt on these submissions.

No replacement approval or its terms are established. California's December 28 reply invokes continuing administrative reconsideration, not an issued replacement. Its statement that Beno was only a preliminary ruling is a party characterization; Beno's own merits treatment and actual vacatur control the description of the lower event. A reported January argument concession is not necessary to the factual linkage established by these primary submissions, and no unverified transcript quotation is adopted here.

Chronology matters: Beno preceded California's reported July 28, 1994 petition filing. ### Entering law and live alternatives

Church of Scientology of California v. United States, November 16, 1992, establishes that some meaningful partial relief can preserve a controversy even when complete restoration is impossible. Minnesota v. Dickerson, June 7, 1993, separately requires attention to identified continuing legal consequences; it does not authorize invented consequences. Those principles require identification of actual remaining relief here, not the assumption that a still-existing statute is currently enforceable.

U.S. Bancorp, November 8, 1994, supplies the distinction between loss of authority to adjudicate merits and §2106 authority to make a just disposition of a judgment lawfully brought before the Court. It also makes vacatur an equitable question rather than an automatic consequence of mootness. Its ordinary refusal of vacatur following a losing party's voluntary settlement is distinct from loss of review through happenstance or unilateral action by a prevailing party. Identify the cause and timing of any loss of the controversy here. Do not turn Bancorp into either compulsory vacatur or a categorical prohibition on vacatur, and do not omit its lawful-review premise.

The retained [Carroll, 393 U.S. 175](https://static.case.law/us/393/html/0175-01.html), is California's pre-divergence recurrence authority. Carroll involved continued meetings and reliance on the expired restraint in an ongoing dispute. It requires comparison to demonstrated continued effects; it does not make every potentially recurring legal question live.

The live paths are: (a) identify an actual continuing implementation threat or other effective requested relief and decide whether merits review remains available; (b) find that the missing approval and absence of a presently operative differential prevent adjudication of the constitutional dispute, then separately determine jurisdictionally available and equitable treatment of the existing judgments and complaint; or (c) obtain further lower-court findings if the operative facts are material but cannot be resolved from the existing record. Mootness, prematurity of a possible new implementation, and equitable vacatur are different questions. Preserving a future challenge to a materially different approval is not a present ruling on that approval.

If a constitutional merits path remains available, the parties' competing positions are preserved: recipients invoke Shapiro and Memorial Hospital's protection against penalties on interstate migration and unequal treatment of bona fide residents; California relies on the temporary differential, continued receipt of former-State levels, the absence of total exclusion, fiscal need, and room for experimentation. The district court's likelihood findings do not settle those arguments for this Court. This packet does not authorize bypassing the threshold on an assumed replacement approval.

## 30. Gustafson v. Alloyd Co.

### Questions, transaction, and judgment below

The question is whether §12(2) of the Securities Act of 1933 reaches allegedly misleading representations in the negotiated agreement for the December 1989 purchase of substantially all the stock of a closely held corporation. The relevant stock had long been issued; there was no registration statement or conventional public offering for this negotiated acquisition. A transaction's private character and its secondary character are distinct descriptive axes, even though both are present here.

The purchase agreement contained financial representations, warranties, and an adjustment based on year-end results. The purchasers allege material misstatements in that agreement; sellers contest liability and rely in part on the negotiated true-up. Payment of an adjustment is not a finding that every federal remedy has been satisfied. Neither falsity, materiality, purchaser ignorance, the reasonable-care defense, nor the ultimate amount recoverable has been finally established for the federal claim.

The full lower orders are in the petition's [Appendix, pages 1–14](../sources/chunk3-b-neutral/Gustafson_lower_orders_App_1_14.txt), retained as lower-court material only. On May 29, 1992, the Northern District of Illinois granted sellers summary judgment on the federal claim because §12(2) did not cover this secondary private transaction. It dismissed the remaining contract count for lack of jurisdiction and treated purchasers' summary-judgment motion as moot. It rejected the specific Wind Point standing objection and the asserted limitations bar based on imputation of McLean's knowledge; it did not decide sellers' additional objections to the substantive §12 elements. Those rulings do not establish that §12 liability follows automatically if the categorical scope ruling changes.

On June 11, 1993, the Seventh Circuit vacated and remanded for further proceedings in light of [Pacific Dunlop Holdings v. Allen, 993 F.2d 578](https://static.case.law/f2d/993/html/0578-01.html). Its short order did not independently find misrepresentation or award rescission. Pacific Dunlop itself read §12(2) to reach secondary private transactions; its full majority opinion supplies the lower legal rationale and addresses the contrary Ballay interpretation. Treat its discussion of later-than-divergence Supreme Court cases through the actual simulated entering law, especially Rowland.

### Statutory provisions, including qualifications

The [1994 Code text of §2, 15 U.S.C. §77b](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/securities_77b.txt), begins with the qualification “unless the context otherwise requires.” Section 2(10)'s definition of prospectus includes any prospectus, notice, circular, advertisement, letter, or communication, written or by radio or television, offering a security for sale or confirming its sale. Its two exceptions are distinct. Under (a), the communication must have been sent or given after the registration statement became effective, must itself be other than a prospectus permitted under §10(b), and is excluded only if it is proved that a written prospectus meeting §10(a)'s requirements at the time of the communication was sent or given to the same person before or concurrently with it. The parenthetical excludes the §10(b) communication itself from this exception; it is not merely a limit on what accompanying paper suffices. Under (b), the communication must state from whom a written prospectus meeting §10 may be obtained and do no more than identify the security, state its price and who will execute orders, and give such further information as Commission rules permit under their terms and conditions. Neither exception is an unqualified exemption for private communications. This statute says context otherwise “requires”; Rowland construed the Dictionary Act's “indicates otherwise” proviso. Its actual contextual reasoning is relevant without silently equating the two formulations.

[Section 10, §77j](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/securities_77j.txt), prescribes registration-statement information, except to the extent otherwise permitted or required by §10(a), (c), (d), or (e). For securities other than those issued by a foreign government or political subdivision, §10(a)(1) permits omission of Schedule A documents numbered 28–32; for foreign-government or political-subdivision securities, §10(a)(2) permits omission of Schedule B documents 13–14. Under §10(a)(3), when use is more than nine months after registration effectiveness, information must be as of no more than sixteen months before use, so far as known to the user or obtainable without unreasonable effort or expense. Section 10(a)(4) permits omissions that Commission rules designate unnecessary or inappropriate in the public interest or for investor protection. Permission to omit never becomes an obligation to omit.

Section 10(b) requires the Commission to permit by rules, for §5(b)(1)'s purposes, a prospectus omitting or summarizing information from the §10(a) prospectus. Unless Commission rules otherwise provide, it is filed as part of the registration statement, but is not deemed part of it for §11 liability. The Commission may prevent or suspend use for required nonfiling or the provision's materially misleading or missing information, with notice and an opportunity for hearing, and must vacate or modify the order for good cause or filing/amendment complying with it. Section 10(c) allows required additional information; (d) allows classification by use, security, issue, issuer, or otherwise and prescribed form/content under rule conditions; (e) requires conspicuous placement and ordinarily body-sized type; (f) requires Commission filing of broadcast copies under its rules and permits required filing of forms and prospectuses used with registered securities. Those powers and requirements remain distinct from §2(10)'s exceptions. The full retained text controls any finer structural argument.

[Section 12(2), §77l](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/securities_77l.txt), applies to a person offering or selling a security, including exempt securities except §3(a)(2)'s class, through interstate facilities or mails, by means of a prospectus or oral communication containing a material false statement or an omission necessary to prevent misleading statements in their circumstances. The purchaser must not know of the untruth or omission. The seller bears the statutory burden of showing lack of knowledge and inability to discover the matter through reasonable care. Recovery is consideration with interest less income received upon tender, or damages if the purchaser no longer owns the security. Coverage alone therefore does not establish liability or an unconditional rescission award.

[Section 17(a), §77q](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/securities_77q.txt), separately prohibits, directly or indirectly in the offer or sale using interstate facilities or mails: (1) a device, scheme, or artifice to defraud; (2) obtaining money or property by a material falsehood or an omission necessary to prevent misleading statements in their circumstances; and (3) a transaction, practice, or course of business operating or that would operate as a fraud or deceit upon the purchaser. “Upon the purchaser” is in (3), not an unstated condition of (1). Section 17(c) disapplies §3's exemptions to §17. Its wording and enforcement setting differ from §12(2). [United States v. Naftalin, 441 U.S. 768](https://static.case.law/us/441/html/0768-01.html), applies §17(a)(1) to the fraud on brokers in the short-selling transaction there. It supplies a comparison of enacted coverage, not an already decided definition of prospectus in §12(2).

### Competing legal paths and entering-law constraints

The purchasers' path begins with the express §2(10) definition, including confirmations of sale, and §12(2)'s application to exempt securities. It distinguishes a registration exemption from an exemption from an express antifraud remedy, and argues that the written agreement communicates the offer or confirms the sale. Pacific Dunlop reasons that Congress distinguished registration language in §2(8), retained broader wording in §2(10), and allowed multiple §10 forms; overlapping §10(b) remedies do not necessarily narrow an express §12 action. The strongest objection is whether this construction gives insufficient effect to “prospectus” as a term used throughout the Act and to the registration-related structure of §10.

The sellers' path treats the Act's context as limiting the prospectus terminology to public-offering materials of the kind governed by §10, distinguishing a bilateral acquisition contract from such solicitation. It emphasizes consistent statutory terminology and differences between §§12 and 17. The strongest objection is the breadth of the enacted definition, particularly confirmation language, together with §12(2)'s express reference to otherwise exempt securities. Neither the practical sophistication of these parties nor a general investor-protection purpose supplies a substitute for resolving the statutory language. Both paths must address the oral-communication phrase and the relationship between it and prospectus; neither may silently make every oral transaction covered while exempting its written equivalent without a textual explanation.

The simulated Rowland, January 12, 1993, applies a statutory definition subject to actual incompatible context; it does not erase the contextual proviso or permit policy inconvenience alone to displace a workable definition. Its public separate writings are relevant to each participating Justice's approach to context, not an answer to §12. The simulated Musick, Peeler, June 1, 1993, permits contribution as a bounded incident of the received Rule 10b-5 action only after shared liability is established. The simulated Central Bank, April 19, 1994, retains a confined private affirmative-assistance action under §10(b); its different received-remedy rationale and preserved Pinter distinction must be honored. Do not substitute the historical Central Bank result, and do not infer a universal preference for broad securities liability from the simulated holding.

### Remedy and limits

## 31. Arizona v. Evans

### Questions and established record

First identify the basis of the Arizona Supreme Court's judgment for §1257 purposes. If review is available, decide whether exclusion is required for evidence obtained following an arrest based on a computer entry reporting a warrant that had already been quashed. Distinguish the Fourth Amendment violation from the exclusionary remedy, the officer's reliance from the institution's recordkeeping, and court personnel from police personnel.

The full majority and dissent in [State v. Evans, 177 Ariz. 201, 866 P.2d 869](https://static.case.law/ariz/177/html/0201-01.html), January 13, 1994, establish the following. An officer stopped Evans on January 5, 1991, for a traffic matter, learned of a suspended license, and testified that he would have issued a citation rather than arrested Evans absent the reported warrant. The warrant had been quashed weeks earlier. Marijuana was discovered during the arrest and vehicle events. The trial court suppressed; the State dismissed charges without prejudice in order to appeal. The state appellate proceedings concern that suppression ruling, not a final finding of guilt.

The trial judge made no express finding identifying the cause of the unremoved computer entry. The intermediate court inferred court-clerk error. The Arizona Supreme Court rejected reliance on that inference and regarded the clerk's institutional identity as immaterial to its exclusion analysis: ministerial recordkeeping can produce an unlawful arrest whether performed by police or court staff. It distinguished Leon's judicial probable-cause determination from failure to keep accurate records and emphasized incentives to maintain records implicating liberty. Do not restate the case as a found court-clerk error, a found police-clerk error, or proven systemic disregard.

The majority discusses federal exclusionary principles and Arizona's statutory good-faith provision, A.R.S. §13-3925. It rejects characterization of the error as the statutory reasonable judgmental mistake or technical violation. It does not expressly adopt an independent state-constitutional suppression holding. The dissent argues that Arizona's exclusion rule follows federal law, would apply Leon to court-clerk error, and would obtain a finding of the error's source if needed. The dissent's account is an argument, not an additional majority holding.

### Jurisdiction, remedy doctrine, and live paths

[Section 1257](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/statute_1257.txt) requires a qualifying final state judgment presenting the federal issue. Michigan v. Long supplies the federal/interwoven-ground rule and its adequate-independent-ground limitation. The simulated Sochor, June 8, 1992, preserves a clearly independent state preservation ground even when the state court alternatively addresses federal merits. The simulated Middlebrooks, December 13, 1993, respects an independently sufficient state constitutional ground supported by the original judgment and the state court's later clarification. Those holdings require examination of the actual basis here; citation of state authority alone neither establishes independence nor removes it. If the basis is materially ambiguous, identify exactly what clarification is necessary rather than inventing a state holding.

Leon is pre-divergence law. Its treatment of objectively reasonable reliance on a magistrate-issued warrant separates the exclusionary remedy from whether a search violated the Fourth Amendment. The competing applications here concern whether its institutional-deterrence reasoning reaches a quashed warrant left in a database, whether judicial recordkeeping differs from police recordkeeping, and whether exclusion could improve the challenged administrative practice. Good faith is objective; personal innocence of the arresting officer cannot alone establish every needed premise about the underlying process. Conversely, an unlawful arrest does not itself resolve Leon's remedial inquiry.

The paths retained are: application of Leon to adequately established court-employee error with reasonable reliance; suppression for ministerial error where exclusion supplies a relevant institutional incentive; and a narrower remand if the source of the error or the reliability of the recordkeeping system matters but is not found. A police-employee or mixed-source case may require a separate analysis. No factual basis establishes a recurring inaccurate system, warnings known to this officer, or a categorical entitlement to trust every computer report. A narrow resolution must identify the facts on which it depends and reserve any genuinely unresolved category.

The simulated Dickerson, June 7, 1993, separates a permissible limited search technique from its actual execution, affirms suppression on accepted findings of additional manipulation, and preserves the different views on whether those findings were sufficient. That public record supplies date-eligible individual approaches to remedial application and factual sufficiency, without deciding administrative error. Stevens's separate Leon writing, retained in full, independently emphasizes the distinction between a technical warrant-form problem and absence of substantive authority, the institutional incentives of exclusion, and avoiding broad remedial decisions when an antecedent ground can resolve the case. Those are his published premises; no current vote follows automatically.

### Exact limits and possible mandate

The full dated text of §13-3925 has not been independently recovered; the lower majority's quoted and applied portions suffice to identify its stated ground. A disposition turning on a different state statutory exception or the complete safe harbor requires the dated full text. No statewide systems audit, police training record, or trial-level finding of the source of the error is established. Those omissions do not prevent assessing a remand or a rule expressly conditional on supported facts. An appellate remedy must address the suppression judgment or the need for further findings; it may not declare the dead warrant valid or direct a conviction.

## 32. Swint v. Chambers County Commission

### Question and the components below

The presented issue is appellate power to review the County Commission's denial of summary judgment during an otherwise available interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal by individual officials. It is not a request to decide the county's ultimate liability or every individual-immunity ruling.

The full [Swint v. City of Wadley, 5 F.3d 1435](https://static.case.law/f3d/5/html/1435-01.html), November 3, 1993, concerns two raids on the Capri Club, in December 1990 and March 1991, and constitutional claims by owners, an employee, and a patron. The recited disputed facts are viewed favorably to plaintiffs on defendants' summary-judgment appeals; they are not final findings. The sheriff authorized the raids but was not present. Municipal, county, and individual defendants have distinct alleged responsibilities.

The Eleventh Circuit reviewed the individuals' qualified-immunity appeals, sustained the denial on Fourth Amendment and equal-protection claims, and granted immunity on due-process damages claims. Qualified immunity did not dispose of declaratory and injunctive relief. It expressly rejected independent collateral-order jurisdiction over the county because the county's defense could be reviewed after final judgment. It nevertheless exercised pendent appellate jurisdiction over the county claim for efficiency, since its resolution would eliminate the county from the litigation. It held the sheriff was not the county's final policymaker for the law-enforcement conduct at issue and found no independent basis to retain the federal conspiracy claim against the county. The county disposition thus covered the surviving §1983 and §1985(3) theories; no county state-law count remained.

The circuit declined analogous discretionary review of the City of Wadley on the insufficiently developed record and of individual state-law claims where the officers would face trial on federal claims in any event. These distinctions show the discretionary rationale actually used; do not replace it with a finding that every county issue was necessary to resolve the individuals' immunity.

### Statutory law and alternatives

[Section 1291](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/statute_1291.txt) supplies final-decision jurisdiction, subject to its own exceptions. Cohen's limited collateral-order class requires an order conclusively determining the disputed question, resolving an important issue completely separate from the action's merits, and being effectively unreviewable on appeal from final judgment. Those conditions are cumulative and applied to the legally relevant category, not merely this case's litigation burden. The simulated Digital Equipment, June 6, 1994, makes clear that litigation burden and asserted freedom from trial do not alone establish the required categorical importance or effective unreviewability. Its distinction between a private settlement and public-law immunity does not give a county the officials' qualified immunity or transform a liability defense into immunity from suit.

Mitchell's pre-divergence treatment of an official's qualified-immunity entitlement supports the available individual appeal on the appropriate legal question. O'Connor's separate writing, retained in full, identifies protection against trial as the feature that makes that official immunity unlike an ordinary defense. Stevens's separate writing there rests on the national-security statutory and immunity setting; it is not support for a free-standing pendent-appeal rule.

[Section 1292(b)](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/statute_1292.txt) supplies a separate channel: written district-court certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and the potential materially to advance termination; timely application within ten days; and the court of appeals' discretionary permission. An application does not itself stay the district proceedings. No qualifying certification or permission for this county appeal is established, and none may be supplied by inference from convenience.

Section 1292(e), effective January 1, 1993, authorizes rules providing for additional interlocutory appeals. [Section 2072(c)](../../../tmp/chunk3_b_sources/statute_2072.txt), added in 1990, allows rules defining finality for §1291 purposes. Section 2072(a) authorizes general practice, procedure, and evidence rules for district courts and courts of appeals; §2072(b) forbids those rules to abridge, enlarge, or modify substantive rights and states the effect of effective rules on conflicting laws. Section 1292(e)'s additional-appeal rules must be prescribed in accordance with §2072. These enactments bear on the lawful means of enlarging appellate categories. Their existence is not by itself an already adjudicated categorical ban on every form of pendent review.

The broad pendent path treats a properly appealed official-immunity question as allowing efficient resolution of a related county question. Its difficulty is identifying a lawful source of appellate authority when the county's own order fails the collateral-order test. The opposing path confines review to the qualifying appeal and requires a separate jurisdictional basis for the county ruling; its strongest narrower qualification is possible review of an issue genuinely inseparable from, or necessary to meaningful resolution of, the properly appealed issue. Test that necessity against this record, including the distinction between constitutional violation/clearly established law and attribution of conduct to the county. Shared facts and judicial economy are not synonyms for necessity. The Court can decide the actual asserted basis without deciding every conceivable pendent-review category.

### Remedy and uncertainty

If county appellate jurisdiction is absent, the remedy concerns the county portion of the appellate judgment and further proceedings in the proper forum; it does not itself hold the county liable, reverse every individual-immunity disposition, or decide Alabama policymaker law. If a jurisdictional basis exists, identify its trigger and the precise county issues it permits before reaching any merits component. The district court's unreversed components and the independent officer appeals must be traced separately. No new certification, final judgment, or inseparability finding is an established fact. The full lower opinion and governing statutory channels suffice to evaluate the jurisdictional alternatives; a new merits route beyond the presented appellate-power question requires independent scope and source support.
