## 74. Jenkins ? source findings, scope and definitive commitments

### Actual source facts, separated from advocacy

The original petition's questions concern continuation of education programs allegedly conditioned solely on test outcomes and salaries including noninstructional staff allegedly exceeding violation-linked relief. The merits brief reformulates its second question expressly in terms of an interdistrict remedial purpose and argues that both challenged orders permit review of that supporting legal purpose. Respondents object to reopening the entire magnet plan and unappealed causal findings. Neither position establishes the Supreme Court's scope by assertion alone.

The November1993 lower opinion, petition appendixA21?27, records that the State did not challenge the1992salary factual findings as clearly erroneous. It identifies necessity for implementing education/magnet programs and financial impairment from segregation in addition to attracting outsiders. December1993 opinion A30?35 records a distinct challenge to the national urban labor-market finding and rejects it under clear-error review, with nationwide recruitment evidence; it separately records challenged necessity/support-staff issues and affirms specific findings of critical support functions. Thus the statement 'all salary facts were unchallenged' would be overbroad across both orders. The findings include operating, warehouse, maintenance, mailroom and food-service workers as critical to successful implementation, A35, supported by June1993 orderA106. Their inclusion is a necessity finding, not an independent constitutional prohibition on low salaries.

The salary orders have a mixed rationale. June1992 orderA86?91 identifies inability to implement the decree, the national urban labor market, harmful rollback and the asserted desegregative-attractiveness purpose. The prior salary supplement settlement differed in funding obligations, and is not reopened here. The operative1992/1993 orders involve fifty/fifty allocation with joint-and-several responsibility within their actual terms; no reimbursement, immediate salary clawback or new tax order is inferred.

The lower court also actually found constitutional educational effects. JA23 finds lingering inferior education; JA58 finds systemwide reduced achievement; JA126 restates white flight and reduced achievement as effects. JA21 rejects experts only insofar as they entirely deny schools' influence and recognizes jobs/migration factors; JA25 finds an uncompleted dual system; JA28 records white outmigration. Petitioner's broad claim that the record contains no such finding cannot be adopted. For quality education, the November1993 appellate opinionA14?21 expressly recites the full Freeman factors, including good faith and remedial connections.
