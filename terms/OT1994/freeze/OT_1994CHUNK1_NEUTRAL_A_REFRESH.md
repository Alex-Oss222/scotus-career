## Hess / Walsh: the separate Walsh record is now available

[Walsh v. PATH, 813 F. Supp. 1095 (D.N.J. February 4, 1993)](https://static.case.law/f-supp/813/cases/1095-01.json), Civil Action No. 89-1991(WGB), supplies the previously missing Walsh district opinion. Its full judicial text was reviewed from [the preserved primary source](../tmp/reconcile_a/walsh_lower.json). This independently confirms the facts transmitted in the [Hess supplement](OT_1994CHUNK1_HESS_NEUTRAL_SUPPLEMENT.md).

Walsh filed his FELA complaint on May 9, 1989. He alleged that PATH's negligence caused a passenger-train door to strike his right wrist and arm on October 6, 1986, while he worked for PATH. These are allegations, not findings resolving negligence or causation. On those dates the filing was within FELA's three-year period and outside the one-year period invoked by PATH. The district court granted PATH's Rule 12(b)(1) motion and dismissed for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. Applying Third Circuit entity-status precedent, it treated PATH as a state agency; separately, it rejected complete waiver under Feeney and congressional abrogation through FELA. It applied the one-year condition in N.J.S.A. 32:1-163 and N.Y. Unconsol. Law §7107 and held that Walsh had not satisfied it.

Hess's different record remains separate: alleged train-window injury on June 29, 1987; complaint filed May 31, 1990; FELA and Boiler Inspection Act claims; dismissal on the conditional-consent theory. The [Hess district opinion](https://static.case.law/f-supp/809/cases/1172-01.json) quotes the one-year condition as running from accrual of the cause of action. The refresh does not substitute a universal accident-date accrual rule. No delayed-accrual finding, tolling, later filing, litigation waiver or distinct statutory exception is established in either supplied record. The lower opinions do not reproduce the complete consent legislation. A holding about an unpresented exception or every condition of consent would require the term-effective statutory text and relevant preservation source; the absence of those materials does not recreate the former gap about Walsh's actual dismissal ground.

### Distinct issues and available paths

- **Entity status:** The employees' Lake Country Estates / separate-finances argument and PATH's state-control / compact-structure argument remain live. Article XV permits no assumption that States guarantee all judgments: while operating revenues are inadequate, equal appropriations for salaries, office and other administrative expenses require the Authority's recommendation and both governors' approval, and each State is obligated only up to $100,000 in any one year. The Third Circuit's inference of potential treasury impact is its legal interpretation, not an additional statutory promise.
- **Consent and abrogation:** Feeney establishes federal-forum consent on assumed entity status; it does not itself resolve PATH's status or erase the timing conditions. If PATH qualifies for immunity, the courts' separate conclusions that the one-year consent condition was unmet and FELA supplied no abrogation must be assessed before choosing a final disposition. Acceptance of those legal grounds supports affirmance of the dismissals on this record; disagreement with a material ground may require a different disposition or bounded remand. The new Walsh source makes this a supported path without inventing Walsh-specific filing facts.
- **Coverage and remedy:** Current Hilton preserves the FELA cause of action in competent state courts and keeps federal-forum immunity distinct; Puerto Rico Aqueduct establishes immediate appealability without deciding status, waiver or abrogation. If PATH lacks arm status, reversal and remand can remove the immunity ground without deciding negligence, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits or every limitations defense. No state-court filing or adjudication is supplied.
- **Preservation:** Hess's failure to contest entity status in the district court remains a reported procedural fact. It does not establish a Supreme Court forfeiture disposition contrary to the admitted arm-status question. A terminal result resting on appellate forfeiture, rather than the admitted question and the judgments' predicates, requires the relevant appellate submissions. The Walsh opinion supplies no new forfeiture ruling.

**Bounded validation:** No source blocker remains for evaluating the admitted entity-status question and the two lower dismissals on their stated legal grounds. A new factual treasury proposition beyond the quoted legislation, a distinct exception, or a forfeiture-based terminal disposition still needs its own source. Nothing here chooses among the lawful paths.

### Reachability of the two renewed alternatives

The age-eighteen and lascivious objections may lawfully be evaluated as grounds for preserving the same favorable appellate judgment. Their rejection in the reasoning below does not itself require respondents to cross-petition: a prevailing party may defend the judgment on a ground appearing in the record even when the lower court rejected that ground, provided the party does not seek to enlarge its own rights under the judgment or lessen its opponent's rights. [United States v. American Railway Express Co., 265 U.S. 425, 435–436 (1924)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/265/425). [Dandridge v. Williams, 397 U.S. 471, 475 n.6 (1970)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/397/471), also preserves a remand option when attention was directed elsewhere or the lower court has not addressed the controlling question. These authorities support consideration; they do not command decision of every alternative or permit greater relief without the proper procedural basis.

The entering-law Rule 14 doctrine remains intact: Izumi, Caspari and Madsen distinguish stated or fairly included questions from independent requests for review, and briefing alone does not enlarge a grant. The grounds here are transmitted as defenses of respondents' existing judgment, not a request to undo a separate adverse judgment. Preserve that distinction when deciding reach and remedy. The Supreme Court briefs themselves remain unrecovered; no argument beyond the two identified renewals, no further concession, and no enlargement of requested relief is inferred.

### Claim and answer paths for each renewed alternative

**Age eighteen.** Respondents contend that extending the definition of minor from persons under sixteen to persons under eighteen creates substantial overbreadth because distributors cannot reliably assess ages near eighteen and will avoid lawful sexually explicit expression involving adults as well. The answer presented by the lower reasoning is that Ferber's substantial-overbreadth threshold is not satisfied merely by identifying this line-drawing difficulty; its approval of a statute protecting children under sixteen does not itself impose a constitutional maximum age, and cases allowing adult treatment of sixteen- or seventeen-year-olds do not require it in this setting. Both paths remain available for independent assessment under the text, Ferber and the scienter construction actually adopted. A knowledge construction may bear on the claimed chilling effect, but does not silently decide the age-line objection. No independent evidentiary finding about the extent of protected material chilled is supplied.

**Lascivious wording.** Respondents contend that replacing lewd with lascivious makes §2256's definition vague and overbroad. The answer presented below is that the terms have the same ordinary meaning in this context: Wiegand, 812 F.2d 1239, 1243 (9th Cir. 1987), treated the wording as equivalent to the lewd-exhibition terminology considered in Miller and Ferber. The enacted category supplied by the lower opinion is lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area of any person. Evaluate the asserted uncertainty and breadth of that category separately from performer age and from knowledge; do not equate any nudity with the defined conduct or invent a new multi-factor definition. Whether the alternative succeeds, fails or is expressly left for a supported later determination remains open.

**Other definition terms.** The separate lower objection addressed actual or simulated bestiality and sadistic or masochistic abuse. Its actual lower answer was that Ferber's comparable definition covered simulated conduct, bestiality is sexual in this statutory context, and sadistic or masochistic abuse does not materially differ from sado-masochistic abuse. The semicolon before abuse appeared in an unofficial code and was a typographical error; it is not operative statutory punctuation. This supplies the lower record, not an assumption that the separate objection was renewed here. A proposed present resolution of that independent alternative requires validation of its presentation and reach.

### Scienter and remedy remain distinct

The original packet's statutory-knowledge, textual-limit / constitutional-invalidity, and narrower recklessness paths remain available. The reported actual minority-knowledge finding changes the record premise against which each path's remedial consequence must be tested. In particular, Kozinski's proposed retrial cannot be imported as automatically necessary merely because the trial proceeded under a strict-liability legal theory; nor does an actual minority-knowledge finding automatically establish every element or dispose of the indictment or tape-content claim. Knowledge of the sexual character, knowledge of minority, knowledge of the handling act, the commerce or mail nexus, and knowledge of illegality remain separate. The original packet's §2252(a)(1)/(2) and independent (A)/(B) conditions remain operative.

A disposition rejecting the facial scienter ground must also account for the two renewed alternative facial grounds and the reserved appellate claims: resolve an alternative on a supported ground, or expressly preserve it within a lawful remand. Do not imply that resolving the knowledge question alone necessarily disposes of all challenges. If an independent facial ground supports the existing judgment, its scope and remedial consequence must be stated separately. A final count-specific affirmance, acquittal, retrial command or harmlessness ruling still requires the findings, indictment and preservation materials material to that conclusion. A bounded remand can preserve unresolved claims without filling those gaps.

**Bounded validation:** The supplied record is sufficient for independent modeling of the statutory and constitutional paths, the two renewed facial alternatives, and a remand preserving unresolved claims. Full findings or briefing should be retrieved only if a proposed narrower or terminal resolution turns on something the sanitized facts and primary lower opinion do not establish.
