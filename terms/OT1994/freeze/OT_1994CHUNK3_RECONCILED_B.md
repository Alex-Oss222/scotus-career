### Exact rules, qualifications and alternative routes

The narrow construction gives the term prospectus a consistent offering-related meaning across sections 2, 10 and 12: a document describing securities offered to the public by an issuer or controlling holder. The stock-purchase contract negotiated for this private acquisition is outside that class. The related oral-communication language covers oral communications related to that prospectus setting; it is not an independent means to impose section 12 liability merely by speaking the terms of an otherwise excluded private agreement. No separately established oral claim requires decision here. The result does not rest on buyer sophistication, price-adjustment success, a blanket exemption for every resale regardless of offering characteristics, or a finding that the financial statements were accurate.

The sufficient structural ground is the interlocking use of prospectus in the definition and disclosure provisions. Section 10's required content and its particular forms, modifications and qualifications supply context for the described offering documents; section 2(10)'s list is read as a class of disseminated solicitation materials rather than every private letter. The definition's broad words and confirmation clause remain the strongest objection. The four narrow-scope associates answer that the enacted context proviso permits the common statutory setting to specify their reach. Section 12's reach to exempt securities is not erased: an exempt public offering can still fall within that setting, subject to section 12's own exception for the section 3(a)(2) class. Section 10's listed omissions, alternative forms, timing rules and Commission authority are not converted into an absolute rule requiring every prospectus to reproduce every registration statement item.

The broad construction starts with the enacted definition's express inclusion of a written communication offering or confirming a sale. Section 10 imposes duties in its particular registration/disclosure setting; it does not silently replace the definition in section 12. The definition's express communication exceptions distinguish prospectuses meeting section 10 from other prospectuses. The civil provision expressly reaches specified exempt securities. Reading those features together, the four broad-scope associates reject a categorical private-contract exclusion while retaining all liability and remedy elements. Their strongest objection is the ordinary common-meaning presumption and the class of documents in section 2(10). They answer that the common presumption is rebuttable by the particular statutory functions and express cross-references; they do not declare the rest of the Act irrelevant or treat investor protection as an enacted definition.

All statutory qualifications in the validated packet survive. In particular, section 2(10)(a)'s exclusion concerns a communication sent or given after effectiveness if the qualifying section 10(a) prospectus was sent or given before or with it, with the communication described in section 10(b) excluded from that communication exception; section 2(10)(b)'s distinct limited notice exception has its own informational constraints. Section 12(2)'s section 3(a)(2) securities exclusion is not reduced to a government-securities-only shorthand. Purchaser ignorance, the seller's reasonable-care defense and the consideration-with-interest/tender remedy, as distinguished from damages when the purchaser no longer owns the security, remain separate requirements whenever section 12 applies. None is found satisfied in this appeal.

Naftalin construed section 17(a)(1), whose fraud prohibition lacks the prospectus limitation disputed here; it supplies neither an automatic broad section 12 result nor an implicit repeal of section 17's different branches. The negotiated true-up and adjustment do not create an unlegislated scope exemption or prove all statutory relief unavailable. They may matter to an otherwise reached claim, but no present award or independent contract resolution follows.

### Common six-associate rule, precedent treatment and exact remedy

The six retain Long's rule: when a state decision fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven with federal law, and the adequacy and independence of another ground are not clear from the opinion, the Court may review absent a plain statement of an independently sufficient state ground. Arizona's discussion of section 13-3925's reasonable-judgment and technical-violation categories rejects their applicability to the State's argument; it does not independently command suppression under state law. The majority's operative Leon analysis is federal. Sochor's clear preservation ground and Middlebrooks's express independently sufficient state constitutional ground remain intact. State courts remain free to afford greater protection under state law if that independent basis is actually supplied. This does not create a new universal section 1257 finality rule; the State's completed suppression appeal follows dismissal without prejudice and is the admitted review posture.

On exclusion, Leon separates the constitutional violation from the remedy. The six apply that deterrence inquiry to ordinary court employees outside the law-enforcement team. Their clerical responsibility differs from gathering evidence for prosecution; excluding evidence does not have the same corrective incentive for them as for police. The fact that maintaining the record is ministerial does not by itself make Leon inapplicable. Sheppard's protection for reasonable reliance despite a judicial clerical mistake supports extending the principle beyond a probable-cause evaluation. Calandra, Leon and Krull foreclose treating the identification of a violation as alone establishing the exclusion remedy. The arrest remains unconstitutional on the State's concession; the nonexistent warrant is not validated by the officer's reliance.

The category is bounded by **objectively reasonable reliance** and the responsibility of the nonpolice court employee. It does not rest merely on subjective innocence or employment on a nominal court payroll. It does not decide police-generated or mixed failures, failure to maintain a system where defects make reliance unreasonable, or deliberate disregard of known defects. It neither requires officers to telephone the issuing court before every warrant arrest nor declares every computer report sufficiently reliable. The record supports the ordinary character of Sargent's conduct on the information presented, but the trial judge's comments do not establish that every relevant law-enforcement actor was blameless or that the entire database was properly maintained.

The renewed model's six associates directly support this conditional categorical rule. Their factual reservations do not transform it into six votes merely for factfinding without a legal holding. Equally, the rule does not create an independent universal requirement that the State demonstrate a measured error rate, an isolated historical incident, a completed system audit, or the absence of every conceivable state corrective incentive. No such universal conditions appear in the admitted sources. O'Connor, Souter and Breyer's caution about unreliable systems illustrates the objective limit; Souter and Breyer's government-as-a-whole question is reserved rather than decided.

Whiteley's rule that invalid originating police information cannot supply a valid Fourth Amendment basis is preserved. Its treatment of a violation as carrying suppression without a separate remedial inquiry does not answer this post-Leon court-employee case. Hensley found no constitutional violation in its own circumstances and therefore did not decide the separate suppression question presented here. Leon's established limits remain: knowingly or recklessly misleading the magistrate; abandonment of the neutral judicial role; an affidavit so deficient in probable-cause indicia that reliance is unreasonable; or facial warrant deficiencies defeating reasonable reliance. Footnote 24's consideration of officers obtaining or supplying information, as well as those executing the warrant, prevents an arresting-officer-only shortcut. Krull's different legislative setting and reservations do not supply a new blanket police-negligence exception.

The six **reverse and remand**. The reversal rejects the state court's rule that even assumed court-clerk error warrants exclusion on the same basis as police error.
