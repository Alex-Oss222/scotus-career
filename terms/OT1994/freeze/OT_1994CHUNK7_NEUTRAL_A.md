## 73. Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority v. Hechinger ? June 5, 1995

### Components and two paths

A: Does the revised Board remain Congress's agent exercising legally operative federal authority? B: What relief follows, including severability? Affirmance path: the leadership-controlled mandatory nomination lists, congressional qualifications and selective delay power establish more than advisory influence. The trigger is a Board recommendation rejected by the Directors; the Board can thereby compel sixty legislative days of legal suspension for important decisions. An enacted across-the-board waiting period would differ. Congress may receive reports, advise, debate and enact legislation through both Houses and presentment; it may not appoint its agent to control execution by a selective statutory hold. CAAN, 501 U.S.252, supplies the functional structural boundary; Buckley identifies appointment accountability, Bowsher forbids congressional execution, Chadha requires the constitutional legislative process for legally operative congressional intervention.

Reversal path: the Board is appointed by the local Authority, has lost its veto, cannot stop an action permanently and provides time for lawful legislation. Strongest answer: a time-sensitive budget, bond or operating decision is legally disabled at the Board's choice; loss of the permanent veto does not make coercive delay advice. Narrow affirmance does not invalidate reporting, nonvoting attendance or advice considered separately. Severability preserves lawful airport operations insofar as the governing transfer/lease/compact permits; no automatic invalidation of the entire Authority.

## 74. Missouri v. Jenkins ? June 12, 1995

### Governing Freeman qualifications, preserved in every path

Partial withdrawal is permitted, not required, after the district proves full and satisfactory compliance and elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable. Whole-decree good faith must be demonstrated by conduct. The court must examine whether retaining control is necessary or practicable to secure compliance elsewhere, and release may not obstruct the remaining remedy. The district bears the burden of showing that imbalance for which it disclaims responsibility is not traceable to the violation. Genuinely independent demographic change does not require perpetual balancing after the required work is completed. Racial imbalance alone proves neither liability nor entitlement to release. Existing factual findings receive clear-error review; a finding on one issue does not settle an unexamined causal or remedial connection. The order must define released duties, remaining obligations and retained supervision. No categorical all-components-at-once restriction, no good-intentions substitute, no automatic demographic exemption, no general retrial of settled findings. Rufo governs consent modification within its scope and supplies no additional changed-circumstances condition for fulfilled school-decree obligations.

### Components and adversarial paths

S: instructional and noninstructional salary supplements. Q: quality-education continuation/partial release. R: breadth of review and magnet-program premise. A violation-linked salary remedy can cover support staff where their functions are necessary to implement lawful educational repair; job title alone does not decide remedial necessity. Conversely an order designed solely to draw suburban students into KCMSD cannot exceed the established intradistrict violation under Milliken I, 418 U.S.717. Milliken II, 433 U.S.267, allows educational repair of proved effects within the district. Swann supplies equitable flexibility bounded by the violation. No finding of interdistrict violation or automatic student entitlement to suburban outcomes is invented.

For Q, test scores may be evidence of remaining injury, not an automatic national-parity condition. The State must make the complete simulated Freeman showing. A legal-standard remand cannot reverse causation burdens or announce completed compliance. For R, the salaries question expressly challenges interdistrict remedial purpose; that permits examining the legal purpose supporting those orders. It does not automatically reopen every long-settled magnet order or require blanket dismantling. IA petitioner merits questions expressly challenge interdistrict salary purpose; respondent argues the four program components rest on unappealed findings and necessity. That factual dispute requires settled-findings and preservation review, not inference from slogans.

## 75. Ryder v. United States ? June 12, 1995

Two paths: reverse and give a fresh Article 66 review by properly appointed judges; or affirm under de facto validity/Buckley's remedial practice and asserted harmlessness. Buckley, 424 U.S.1, did not establish a universal rule denying relief to a timely direct challenger. Glidden, 370 U.S.530, distinguishes important constitutional judicial qualifications from technical assignment defects; Ball, 140 U.S.118, McDowell, 159 U.S.596, and Ex parte Ward, 173 U.S.452, do not erase that distinction. Freytag, 501 U.S.868, supplies the accountable appointment principle. The lower court's conceded constitutional defect is not relitigated. The Coast Guard Judge Advocate General is the Department of Transportation General Counsel, as the petition and petitioner brief explicitly state; there is no contradiction between those descriptions.

## 76. City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. ? June 12, 1995

Breyer excluded; no reason is inferred. Eight participating Justices total including Stone, seven non-Stone commitments.

Two paths: affirm the appellate rejection of denial based on mutual fault and ordinary genuine liability uncertainty; or reverse for broader equitable discretion. Ordinary prejudgment interest compensates loss of use, does not punish the defendant. Reliable Transfer, 421 U.S.397, already allocates principal by comparative fault; denying interest for that fault repeats the reduction. Genuine contest is common and does not make compensation inequitable. The Scotland, 118 U.S.507, preserves equitable judgment, but legal discretion must serve compensation. The City cannot turn general borrowing circumstances into sovereign immunity. Undue claimant-caused delay and supported distinct exceptional circumstances remain possible; respondent's proposed post-accident-only exception need not be adopted as exhaustive. Rate, period and quantum remain for calculation; the principal settlement expressly reserved interest and is untouched.
