## Actual policy and threshold

This is federal appellate review under28USC1254(1), not review of a state highest court under1257. NinthCircuit's Oregon holding rests expressly on its federal floor; it says because policy violates FourthAmendment it follows it violates ArticleI?9. Federal review is therefore available. If federal invalidation is reversed, remand must preserve the independent Oregon-law question rather than silently reinstate final dismissal. Respondents themselves request this alternative in meritspp14?15. Statutory-school-authority question was rejected below; no independent holding here reopens it or treats it as finally disposed by this federal judgment.

The actual challenged policy is interscholastic-athlete testing, not the earlier all-extracurricular version or the separate voluntary all-student policy. Initial seasonal testing and weekly random selection of about10percent of currently competing athletes use a student draw with adult witnesses and concealed selected identities; no official individualized selection discretion. Boys remain clothed, back toward monitor, genitals unobservable; monitor in room can hear and sometimes watch production. Girls produce in enclosed stall, monitor outside can hear. These are intrusions, not fictional absence of observation. Initial refusal means current-season exclusion. Athletic participation does not waive FourthAmendment protection.

JA8 actual medication provision requires prescription copy OR doctor's authorization before testing; refusal to provide verification plus positive result triggers positive-test actions. It is NOT a requirement to surrender the prescription alone or automatic exclusion for failing to verify. Respondents separately identify specimen form requiring nonprescription medicines and physician names (DefEx109); that form was not independently read. Do not hold blanket medical disclosure constitutionally valid or claim it did not happen. The narrow testing disposition leaves an independently litigated, separable medical-disclosure challenge open; no claim that confidentiality eliminates the initial invasion.

Basic actual laboratory targets amphetamines, marijuana and cocaine; no alcohol/steroid testing, occasional district-request LSD testing. Trial opinion's broader drug list cannot supersede the operation agreed in BOTH merits. Lab anonymous numbers and restricted school-result access, one-year retention, no police transmission or suspension/expulsion/general discipline. Positive screening followed by confirmatory procedure; written policy says second test MAY be administered and supplies no-action rule if negative, whereas operation testified supplies retest. After confirmed positives parents notified/hearing. First counseling plus six weekly tests OR current-and-next-season athletics suspension; written second offense current-and-next-season suspension but testimony says counseling/weekly-testing alternative available in practice; third current-and-next-TWO-season exclusion. Retest before next eligible season. Do not turn discretionary written retesting into a mandatory statutory command or erase divergence between policy and practice.

Actual increased drug use and discipline, athletes leading drug groups, observed impaired safety performance, suspected drug-related wrestling injury, and tried educational/deterrent measures support trial findings which appellate court did not set aside as clearly erroneous. No confirmed toxicology causation for the wrestling injury; no finding this child uses drugs; no claim to quantified program efficacy.

## Two paths and rules

A: reverse federal invalidation. In this concrete school-athletic setting, special protective responsibilities, reduced athletic privacy expectations, objective selection, actual drug/safety problem, limited collection and drug information, confidentiality and counseling/athletics-only consequences make this particular suspicionless urinalysis reasonable. TLO allows school warrant/probable-cause relaxation but reserves suspicionless searches; Skinner/VonRaab supply contextual reasonableness, not categorical power over all students. Strongest challenge: children retain bodily privacy; known suspected users could be individually tested and athletics is not a catastrophic-risk job. A does not require a proved catastrophic injury or exhaustion of every alternative, but actual need and safeguards remain necessary.

B: affirm federal invalidation. Reasonable individual suspicion ordinarily remains necessary even at school; this program's observation and intimate informational demands intrude seriously while its suspicionless scope extends to a concededly innocent child. The generalized school/athletic interest does not match the exceptional safety circumstances justifying suspicionless testing in Skinner/VonRaab. Strongest challenge: athlete safety, supervision and neutral selection reduce both privacy expectation and arbitrary intrusion; demanding catastrophic danger imposes a threshold the balancing cases never made universal. B does not forbid school drug testing with reasonable suspicion or decide another district's different record.
