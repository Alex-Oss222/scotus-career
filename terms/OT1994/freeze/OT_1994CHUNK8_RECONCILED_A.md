## 87. Sandin v. Conner — No.93-1911; June19

**Associates:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse on the reviewed thirty-day segregation liberty/Wolff predicate. Stevens, Ginsburg, Souter and Breyer affirm recognition of protected liberty with a process remand. Judgment directions remain; exclusive narrow-rule grounds are corrected. No Court majority or assignment is presumed.

**Formulation-specific test:** The four reversal associates support replacing regulatory-word methodology with the following: States may create prisoner liberty interests, but commands alone are insufficient; such interests ordinarily protect against atypical and significant hardship relative to ordinary prison life. Unexpected, qualitatively different restraints beyond sentence-authorized custody may receive direct due-process protection, as mental-hospital transfer/forced psychotropic medication do. Inevitably altered sentence duration raises the separate Wolff-type interest. Punitive purpose alone does not place all discipline outside lawful confinement. Eighth Amendment liability is not a substitute for these inquiries.

Strongest contrary argument: Hewitt recognized substantive-predicate interests, punishment differs from discretionary placement, and a vague hardship inquiry can use the wrong comparison population. The four answer that the verbal-formula approach incentivizes ruleless management and constitutionalizes ordinary administration; Wolff's substantial liberty and Meachum's authorized-custody rule supply a better boundary. They abandon Hewitt's methodology/anterior interest analysis, preserve its actual adequate-process disposition and do not erase every holding in Olim/Thompson. Whether that revision controls depends on assembly.
