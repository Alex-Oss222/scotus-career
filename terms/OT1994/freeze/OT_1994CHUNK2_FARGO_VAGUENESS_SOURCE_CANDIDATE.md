# Fargo — source-only earlier-authority supplement

This source extract supplies the pre-divergence authorities requested for the already identified vagueness component. It supplies no current-case outcome, commitment, statutory construction, or North Dakota fact. Fresh neutral validation must precede any material change to the modeling packet.

- **United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62, 70–72 (1971):** The Court construed the District of Columbia criminal statute to place on the prosecution the burden of pleading and proving that an abortion was not necessary to preserve life or health. Health included mental as well as physical health. The Court rejected vagueness because physicians routinely make the relevant medical judgment. These are conclusions about that enactment, not an automatic construction or burden for a different State's law. [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/402/62), paragraphs 15–17 and following.
- **Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179, 191–192 (1973):** After the lower court removed Georgia's specified restrictive reasons, the remaining best-clinical-judgment requirement was not vague under Vuitch. Professional judgment could consider physical, emotional, psychological, familial and age factors relevant to the patient's well-being. The inquiry leaves room for professional judgment; it does not make every subjective preference an emergency, import Georgia's text into North Dakota, or revive the trimester timetable displaced by current Casey. [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/410/179), paragraphs 25–28.
- **Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, 455 U.S. 489, 494–499 (1982):** Vagueness examines fair notice and restraints on arbitrary enforcement. Tolerated imprecision depends on the law's character: economic regulation ordinarily receives greater tolerance, criminal penalties less, and interference with constitutionally protected rights demands greater clarity. A scienter requirement can mitigate notice problems. The facial challenge there failed because the business conduct was sufficiently clearly covered, under even the criminal or quasi-criminal standard appropriate to that ordinance. Its commercial setting does not automatically select the test for a protected medical decision. [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/455/489), paragraphs 12–16; see also its separate preliminary facial analysis.
- **Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379, 390–401 (1979):** The Court found the viability-determination requirement ambiguous and its uncertainty aggravated by absence of fault tied to the viability determination. A homicide fault requirement about causing fetal death did not itself establish culpability regarding the disputed viability trigger. The Court also found the technique/standard-of-care requirement vague. It reserved the precise fault level required under a properly drafted statute. State-specific scienter cannot be inferred merely because some element has a mental-state requirement. [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/439/379), Parts IV–V, especially paragraphs 23, 32–35 and 44.

No additional North Dakota element-specific fault or authoritative construction source was established in this supplement. The existing packet's precise source limits remain. These paragraphs paraphrase retrieved opinion passages; no current-case merits source or post-event rule is used.
