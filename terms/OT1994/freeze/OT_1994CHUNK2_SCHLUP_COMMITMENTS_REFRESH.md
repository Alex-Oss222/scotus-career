## Governing statutory and doctrinal distinctions

Former §2244(b) is the pre-AEDPA provision verified by the neutral refresh against the official 1994 edition, current through January 4, 1995; no intervening amendment is supplied. Its complete text is:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The earlier-hearing trigger has two alternatives: an evidentiary merits hearing on a material factual issue, or a merits hearing on an issue of law, followed by federal denial of release or other remedy. The earlier federal appellate merits consideration of guilt-phase ineffective assistance supplies a concrete legal-merits proceeding for assessing the second alternative; absence of the presently requested evidentiary hearing does not defeat that alternative. It does not establish that every current claim is repetitive or that a genuinely new claim was abused.

The two unless conditions are separate and conjunctive: an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and satisfaction that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New supporting evidence does not necessarily create a new ground. Absence of deliberate withholding does not exclude negligent omission amounting to abuse under *McCleskey*. State-custody subsection (b) is not federal-custody subsection (a), and the latter's ends-of-justice language is not inserted into the former. No current-case Supreme Court adjudication is assumed under subsection (c).

Kennedy's pre-divergence *McCleskey v. Zant*, 499 U.S. 467, 487–489, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Souter among others, controls the statutory relationship: when both conditions are satisfied, the subsequent petition must be considered **if other barriers, such as nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent**. The statute does not itself define the limits on entertaining deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive grounds. Judicial habeas principles continue to define and constrain that discretion. Thus neither “need not” equals “must dismiss,” nor submission of innocence evidence equals an unconditional statutory entitlement to constitutional merits review.

The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity, identifying writ history and new claims and alleging abuse, before the burden shifts to the prisoner. Cause and prejudice ordinarily excuse an abusive omission, and the exceptional miscarriage-of-justice route remains available without cause. The supplied account says Schlup no longer presses cause and prejudice; it does not establish a different concession concerning every other gate. An appellate court must decide whether the lower court used the correct statutory and gateway rules; discretionary authority cannot insulate legal error. A claimed exercise of refusal discretion must identify the qualifying prior proceeding, the ground's repetitive or abusive character and the reason the applicable exception does not permit review. A legal construction is assessed as law, not deferred to because the ultimate decision contains discretion. The application of lawful refusal discretion remains distinct from choosing the governing legal standard.

The refreshed packet supports assessing whether an independent statutory ground **could** sustain the judgment. It does not establish a sufficiently particular, separate exercise of refusal discretion over every present claim to make the statutory permission alone an independent basis for affirmance. All eight commitments reject that shortcut. This does not remove statutory discretion or compel merits adjudication; the respective gateway and remedy positions below determine the proposed dispositions.

Current *Sawyer* specifies clear-and-convincing proof that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find a necessary death-eligibility condition; it expressly leaves crime innocence unchanged. The register's title is **Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims**, present operation effective June 22, 1992. Current *Herrera* establishes only its record-specific failure, not a controlling general freestanding burden; current *Delo v. Blair* establishes no precedent. *Carrier* supplies an exception for constitutional error probably resulting in conviction of an innocent person. *Kuhlmann*'s fair-probability and all-probative-evidence discussion is relevant plurality reasoning, not a retrospectively manufactured majority holding. *Jackson* tests the legal sufficiency of the trial record with favorable prosecution inferences; it cannot simply replace a new-evidence innocence inquiry.

The probability formulation supported below requires reliable new evidence and consideration of the probative record as a whole: the prisoner must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt in light of that record. This is a proposed specification of the guilt gateway, not a finding that Schlup passes it. The competing clear-and-convincing formulation is a proposed extension of *Sawyer*'s quantum to crime innocence. Its supporters must defend that extension on its own terms; they cannot call it *Sawyer*'s existing holding or silently replace *Carrier*'s probability language.

Current *Keeney*, May 4, 1992, retains *Townsend*'s deliberate-bypass qualification for materially undeveloped facts of a properly presented claim; ordinary negligence alone does not forfeit a necessary hearing in that setting. It does not abolish successive, abusive or default barriers. A gateway assessment may itself require factual development to evaluate materially disputed evidence, but neither filing affidavits nor passing the gateway automatically entitles the prisoner to a merits hearing. Current *Brecht*'s **Preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review**, effective April 21, 1993, concerns the Chapman merits burden for that preserved Doyle claim. Current *McFarland*'s **Capital habeas counsel before a merits petition and discretionary stays**, effective June 30, 1994, concerns counsel and individualized preparatory authority. Neither changes §2244(b), establishes innocence or supplies a new automatic stay.

## Common record and opposing remedial paths

Flowers testified to identification and Maylee's account came by deposition. The dining-hall videotape was trial evidence; it did not newly come into existence. The prosecution disputed the interval between attack and distress response. Green, Faherty and the other later declarants supply assertions about identification, prompt reporting and movement; these are not fresh findings by this Court. The appellate majority identified inconsistencies and delay, while Heaney's dissent identified reasons to test the witnesses in person. No commitment treats all affidavits as true, all prison witnesses as unreliable, or the lower court's paper discussion as a live evidentiary resolution of those disputes.

The lower court's clear-and-convincing rule can be rejected in favor of the guilt-probability inquiry, leading to vacatur and remand when its evaluation may have been shaped by the wrong rule and an undue reliance on the trial jury's original credibility task. The strongest counter is that a correct rule does not require remand when an independent, adequately established ground sustains denial or when the evidence necessarily fails even under that rule. Here no complete independent constitutional-merits basis has been established by the supplied summary: the discussion of the earlier ineffectiveness adjudication is material but does not resolve every asserted ground or new factual premise.

Alternatively the Court can affirm after expressly adopting a strict guilt-innocence quantum and concluding the submissions fail that quantum on the present record. The strongest counter is that this changes the law left intact by *Sawyer*, may demand too much from a gateway rather than a merits claim, and risks turning unresolved material reliability into a reason to avoid the factual inquiry. The commitments supporting affirmance below rely on that legal extension and documentary insufficiency; they do not claim an independent statutory command to dismiss or a new conclusive finding that witnesses lied. Any materially different complete proffer or factual-development ruling not represented in the packet would require renewed assessment.
