## 37. Director, OWCP v. Newport News Shipbuilding & Dry Dock Co. — March 21, 1995

**Components and disposition:** All eight associates affirm the Fourth Circuit's refusal to entertain the Director's independent challenge to the employee-benefit determination under §921(c). They do not affirm the entire Board order or undo the Fourth Circuit's distinct Special Fund reversal and remand. Harcum's entitlement, classification date, credit, and the merits of §8(f) remain outside this disposition.

**Common operative ground:** An administrator's disagreement with a Board benefits ruling and interest in correct application of the Act do not, without an adverse effect on a legally entrusted administrative or financial interest, establish that the administrator is a person adversely affected or aggrieved under §921(c). The administrative party-in-interest route under §921(b)(3), claimant assistance under §939, and respondent participation recognized in Perini do not confer an unrestricted right to initiate judicial review. No actual interference with an administrative function is shown here; the hypothetical employer-insolvency Fund exposure is speculative and is not reduced by the Director's requested benefits change. The ground is statutory entitlement, not a categorical constitutional incapacity of government to litigate.

**Alternative-path assessment:** The strongest reversal argument is that the Director's statutory administration and claimant-assistance responsibility make uniform legal interpretation an institutional interest, as the contrary circuit approach recognizes. It fails for these eight commitments at the requested generality because it would make the separate aggrievement condition automatic whenever the Director disagrees. Perini concerns participation as respondent, not initiation. The narrower available reversal path—an actual order altering a specific duty or impairing administration—is not established on this record. The common ground preserves that distinction and the Fund litigation rather than replacing §921(c) with an economic-injury-only rule.

## 39. Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission — March 27, 1995

**Components:** The state-law interpretation and state constitutional ruling remain the Alaska court's. The admitted federal issue concerns application of RFRA and the federal Free Exercise Clause to the housing denials. The separate notice/delegation due-process ruling is outside review. All eight accept that original RFRA governs threatened prospective enforcement, with substantial burden for the claimant to establish and government production and persuasion on compelling interest and least restrictive means. All reject a categorical commercial-activity exclusion from RFRA. No Justice supplies a retrospective damages or penalty disposition from the absent commission remedy.

**Two lawful paths:** A bounded vacatur returns the prospective-enforcement component for application-specific RFRA findings and justification, without ordering an exemption. Its strongest counterargument is that Alaska did identify the direct dignitary and transactional harm of each refusal, independently of housing scarcity, and expressly addressed least restrictive means; another remand could merely restate a completed inquiry. The affirmance path accepts that direct harm and the absence of a means that both permits these refusals and prevents that same injury. Its strongest counterargument is that describing the prohibited transaction itself as a compelling interest can make RFRA's exception inquiry circular, while commercial entry and an undifferentiated antidiscrimination purpose do not establish justification in every application. The home-sharing exception requires a comparability assessment if invoked; it does not prove either an exemption for Swanner or unlawful targeting.

## 40. Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co. — March 28, 1995

**Disposition and common rule:** All eight associates reverse the categorical color-only registrability bar and the associated cancellation direction, and remand consistently with ordinary trademark requirements. They preserve the separately affirmed §43(a) unfair-competition judgment, monetary damages, and injunction. A color that has acquired source-identifying significance and is nonfunctional may qualify as a trademark; color is not excluded merely because it is color. This does not make color inherently distinctive, dispense with confusion where required, or permit monopolization of useful or competitively necessary features.

| O'Connor | Same rule; her Two Pesos and Gardner joins support enforcing enacted requirements without a categorical policy addition. | Functionality remains independent of acquired meaning. |
| Scalia | Same rule from §§1052 and 1127; his Two Pesos and Gardner joins support rejecting an exclusion the enacted category does not contain. | No extra source of monopoly beyond the statutory mark and its scope. |
| Kennedy | Same rule; his Two Pesos join separates distinctiveness, nonfunctionality and confusion. | Competitive necessity must be considered where established; ordinary color usefulness alone does not decide this particular shade. |
| Souter | Same rule; his Two Pesos join and Gardner opinion support contextual fidelity to the broad terms and actual conditions. | Preserves the existing factual findings rather than inventing a new survey or market definition. |
| Thomas | Same rule; his textual approach in Germain and Gardner join supports the Act's nonexclusive descriptive language. | Does not premise protection on a new judicial policy of favoring appearance over competition. |
| Ginsburg | Same rule; her Gardner join and Wolens opinion support separating the source of the protected obligation and the exact statutory predicates. | Registration and §43(a) remedies remain distinct; no double recovery is ordered. |
| Breyer | Same rule; his Gardner and Asgrow joins support the enacted framework's functional application without an unwritten numerical or categorical constraint. | Shade confusion is administered through ordinary proof; finite-color competition is addressed where actual functionality/need is established. |

**Alternative-path assessment:** The categorical alternative has its strongest support in depletion of useful colors and difficulties distinguishing close shades, together with older trademark cases limiting claims to color. It does not survive the statutory language and the independently enforced functionality boundary here. The reverse path's strongest objection is that case-by-case functionality may permit anticompetitive claims before litigation corrects them; that concern justifies careful requirements, not a categorical exclusion absent from the text. Inwood supplies the essential-use/cost-or-quality criterion. The lower findings establish secondary meaning, likely confusion, and no competitive need for this particular shade; they are not a holding that color never affects quality or competition. All eight may join a bounded explanation of competitive necessity as part of preserving functional features, but not an unbriefed general extension to every aesthetic feature.

## 41. Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc. — April 3, 1995

**Shared boundary:** All eight agree the tax is internally consistent in the admitted application because the single sale occurs in only one State. The actual conflict is external apportionment and the force of Central Greyhound. A judgment applies to Oklahoma sales of travel originating there, not all tickets the statute might facially cover. No dollar amount, mileage formula, or unproved credit regime is supplied.

**Two lawful paths:** Reversal treats the completed retail sale as an independently taxable local transaction; a service's out-of-state performance does not necessarily require apportionment of its retail sale price. Its strongest objection is that price tracks the transportation service and Complete Auto rejects formal labels. Affirmance treats the full price as the interstate transportation receipts condemned in Central Greyhound. Its strongest objection is that this equation would make ordinary retail sales tax depend on where the purchased good or service is later used, despite a single local sales event. Each path must address substance, not merely whether the tax is nominally on purchaser or seller.
