## DeLoach residence correction, Abrams jurisdictional appendix 104–105; legal conclusions omitted

The Abrams Intervenors have moved the Court to 
dismiss Plaintiff George DeLoach on the ground that he 
“is not a resident of the Eleventh Congressional District 
and therefore lacks standing to complain of the configu- 
ration of such district.” Motion to Dismiss, at 1. Through 
an alleged miscommunication between Plaintiff DeLoach 
and his counsel, the original Complaint incorrectly states 


App. 105 


that DeLoach is a resident of Waynesboro, Burke County, 
Georgia (within the Eleventh Congressional District), 
when DeLoach is in fact a resident of nearby Hephzibah, 
in south Richmond County, Georgia (within the Tenth 
Congressional District). Motion to Dismiss, at 2; Motion 
to Amend, Ex. A. 




## Other plaintiffs residence and allegation description, Abrams jurisdictional appendix 110; preceding standing conclusion omitted

As residents and 
eligible voters in the Eleventh Congressional District, 
these Plaintiffs are clearly asserting their own legal right 
to participate in an electoral process free from unlawful 
racial gerrymandering. The portions of their Complaint 
stating that Georgia’s plan is harmful to both black and 
white citizens are meant, no doubt, more as a description 
of the alleged ills of the current plan than as an effort to 
directly assert the rights of black voters, much less of only 
black voters. 




## Source precision issue

The United States appellants brief uses a compressed five-resident description; the specific residence correction in the earlier district order identifies DeLoach as a Tenth-District resident. Do not silently use the compressed statement to assign all five plaintiffs to the Eleventh District. The remaining identified plaintiffs are Davida Johnson, Pam Burke, Henry Zittrouer and George Seaton. The substantive legal conclusions in the historical district standing order are not a substitute for simulated Warth/Lujan/Shaw analysis.

## Boundaries on use

The original complaint and amendment themselves have not been separately retrieved in this research. The residence correction is documented through a contemporaneous judicial recitation expressly citing Motion to Dismiss at 2 and Motion to Amend Exhibit A. The verified docket identifies a designation order and actual hearings, State counsel appearance and service. It does not reproduce a registered/certified-mail return under section 2284(b)(2). No adverse notice determination is identified. Whether those recorded participation facts sufficiently validate notice is for the independent legal validation, not a missing-source presumption either way.

The historical district order also discusses an unsuccessful candidacy theory for DeLoach. That distinct injury is not added to the reconstructed design-only allegations. The source permits assessment of personal exposure for the four Eleventh-District plaintiffs without converting their alleged racial assignment into a merits deprivation by definition. Causation and requested redistricting relief must be assessed on that precise alleged treatment.
