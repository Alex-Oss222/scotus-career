## 21. McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., No. 93-1543

**Event and reach:** Merits, January 23, 1995; argued November 2, 1994. Reach the ADEA after-acquired-evidence question on review of the Sixth Circuit's summary-judgment affirmance, 9 F.3d 539. No participation, finality or statutory-action obstacle is established.

**Provisional result:** Each of the eight non-Stone Justices reverses the categorical bar and remands for discrimination liability and appropriate relief under the distinctions below. None treats the employer's motion-specific liability assumption as an adjudicated ADEA violation.

### Components and operative scope

1. **Liability and complete remedial forfeiture.** Evidence unknown when the employer discharged the employee cannot be an actual motive for that discharge. If age actually motivated the decision and had a determinative influence under Hazen Paper, subsequently discovered misconduct does not erase that statutory violation or categorically defeat every remedy. The claimant must still prove age discrimination under the governing rules. Section 623(a)(1), Hazen Paper and St. Mary's supply the actual-motive boundary; section 626(b)'s appropriate legal and equitable relief supplies the remedial inquiry. This is not a new Title VII mixed-motive rule or a retroactive application of the 1991 amendments.
2. **Employer's remedial showing.** The employer must establish that the wrongdoing was sufficiently serious that it actually would have discharged this employee on that ground alone had it known, not merely that a discharge would have been permissible or that some policy was violated. The lower motion record includes affidavits and McKennon's acknowledgment supporting that proposition. The remand may use established record matters under ordinary procedure; this Court does not convert every motion assumption into a final trial finding. Genuine protected opposition or participation under section 623(d) cannot be transformed into a lawful discharge ground. The record concerns the reported unprotected copying and removal, not a categorical restriction on obtaining discrimination evidence.
3. **Remedial starting point.** When that would-have-discharged showing is made, reinstatement and front pay ordinarily do not fit employment the employer would lawfully terminate once the misconduct became known. Backpay ordinarily addresses the interval from the unlawful discharge to actual discovery of the independently disqualifying misconduct. Section 626(b) still requires a particular, appropriate remedy; equitable circumstances must be considered when legally relevant, without reviving automatic forfeiture of all pre-discovery relief. The Court sets no amount and does not resolve a materially different instance of protected conduct, employer tolerance, disputed seriousness, or discovery date.
4. **Unresolved determinations.** Discrimination itself remains disputed. Hazen Paper's knowledge-or-reckless-disregard willfulness rule continues to govern any statutory liquidated damages; after-acquired misconduct does not itself decide willfulness, and the Court neither awards nor categorically extinguishes that statutory remedy. No damages total, fee award or other independently unsupported remedy is supplied. No EEOC enforcement action terminating the individual section 626(c)(1) action is established.

### Adversarial paths and legal conclusions

The complete-bar path has its strongest basis in the employee's inability to retain employment once the misconduct was known and the remedial concern against placing her in a better position than lawful employment would permit. Its defect is temporal and statutory: the unknown misconduct neither caused the earlier discharge nor eliminates wages lost during the earlier period if the discharge was discriminatory. The proper response to the employer's legitimate interest is a supported remedial limit, not an invented liability defense or wholesale deletion of section 626(b)'s remedial function.

The opposite unrestricted-relief path rightly preserves the unlawful act and its deterrent consequences but would ignore an independently sufficient, genuinely proved lawful termination ground. It does not justify compulsory continuing employment or indefinite replacement pay after discovery. The selected path separates the actual unlawful decision from the employee's lawful prospective employment position. Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Price Waterhouse, 490 U.S. 228 (1989), address lawful reasons implicated in actual decisionmaking; they are distinguished at that level rather than repudiated. Neither converts a reason discovered later into a contemporaneous motivation. A narrower remand rejecting the complete bar remains compatible if the opinion preserves the employer's would-have-fired showing and does not purport to approve unlimited relief.

### Access components and supported remand instructions

1. **Governing law and the actual duty.** Apply all six Casey steps, not an obstacle-only or universal no-valid-applications shortcut. Identify each enacted command, the prospective lower-court construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and the challenged package. The statute therefore supplies no established compulsory two-trip rule. Probable gestational age still requires the attending physician's judgment; an agent may obtain history and communicate the determination. No categorical physician-only routine delivery rule is present.
2. **Interval and causation.** The telephone construction answers an alleged universal two-visit requirement; it does not itself dispose of the fixed interval's possible incremental delay, clinical interference, expense, work constraints or reduced usable provider capacity in an evidence-supported subgroup. Compare the actual system with and without the challenged duty or specified package, holding other law and circumstances constant, without double counting. Providers must establish the relevant duty, affected population, causal addition and triggering interference. Geographic distance, poverty, employment and Thursday/Friday physician availability do not automatically become burdens attributable to the State, but neither are they automatically irrelevant when the added legal interval interacts with them. 3. **Purpose, thresholds and justification.** A baseline violation, objectively established obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid; absolute impossibility is unnecessary and an established obstacle cannot be rescued by a compelling interest. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Meaningful means concrete, nontrivial additional interference, not every conceivable inconvenience. There is no new numerical threshold and no automatic State burden merely because the law concerns abortion. On remand identify the duty or application that fails and apply dated state severability law before finally severing text; do not invalidate all information, materials or certification because a timing or application defect may be established. The remand neither conclusively establishes entitlement to a final injunction nor automatically restores the dissolved preliminary injunction, commands a particular evidentiary hearing or prescribes interim protection. ## 23. Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp., No. 93-1525

**Event and reach:** Merits, February 21, 1995; argued November 7, 1994. Reach the supplied question whether Amtrak is governmental for First Amendment purposes despite the classification clause. Reverse the Second Circuit's federal-action ground and remand. The preserved federal-action claim and the actual lower ruling on corporate status support addressing the argument; no express abandonment or waiver is established. Yee permits a supporting argument on a properly presented claim but does not enlarge a question. The supplied question expressly includes this actor-status issue. If a later neutral source establishes an express waiver or materially different grant, that is a material premise requiring renewed modeling.

### Components and operative scope

1. **Governmental actor.** When the Federal Government creates a corporation by special law to accomplish governmental purposes and retains continuing governmental control over its governing direction, it cannot free that corporation from applicable First Amendment restrictions merely by giving it a separate corporate form or declaring that it is not a governmental agency. Amtrak's statutory creation, public transportation purpose and continuing governmental selection/control of its board satisfy that bounded inquiry. The holding is not that every federal charter, subsidy, regulation, preferred share or appointment alone turns a private corporation into the Government.
2. **Statutory distinctions.** Former section 541 and recodified section 24301(a) govern Amtrak's statutory classification; they do not authorize Congress conclusively to redefine the constitutional identity of an institution it creates and continues to control. This ruling grants no sovereign immunity, tort liability, universal statutory-agency status or charter jurisdiction. Red Cross supplies only its separate charter-jurisdiction rule. Hess examines a distinct immunity question; public power alone was insufficient for immunity there, and lack of immunity does not make every public institution private for the First Amendment.
3. **Application and remand.** The statutory nine-member board is not nine direct Presidential appointees: it includes the Secretary, president, specified Presidential appointees/selections and preferred-shareholder selections; the governmental preferred shareholder supplies the latter on this record, and the board chooses the corporate president. The continuing arrangement establishes institutional control without a federal official's external instruction to reject this individual advertisement. Reverse the actor-status dismissal and return the unadjudicated First Amendment merits, forum classification and appropriate relief to the lower courts. The contract claim and TDI counterclaim retain their distinct procedural questions; their dismissal premise must be reconsidered consistently with restoration of the federal claim.
4. **No compulsory-display disposition.** Actor status does not decide whether the Spectacular is a forum of the claimed kind, whether the political-advertising restriction violates the First Amendment, whether the approval/termination clause was breached, whether damages suffice, or whether mandatory display is appropriate. Do not automatically reinstate the district court's two-month-display injunction or award damages.

### Adversarial paths and legal conclusions

The private-entity/nexus path draws force from Jackson, Burton and San Francisco Arts & Athletics: financing, regulation and federal incorporation are not enough to attribute every act of an otherwise private entity to government. It also guards against mistaking ordinary corporate discretion for an external governmental direction. Its weakness here is the premise that Amtrak is merely an outside private recipient of government assistance. The continuing statutory control of its governing institution is a more direct governmental relationship. Requiring an additional external order for each act would let governmental institutional control disappear behind the chosen corporate form.

The selected structural path answers that objection through a cumulative, bounded condition: special governmental creation, governmental purposes and continuing governmental control. It does not depend solely on the political character of the rejected speech. The narrower conduct-specific path might avoid deciding other corporate activities, but the supplied record does not establish a distinct external direction of this refusal or make advertising alone an exclusive public function. Edmonson and McCollum show that public attribution can be activity-specific; they neither require treating an actually government-directed institution as private nor supply an automatic analogy between advertisements and jury selection. The clean supported ground is institutional identity, followed by a separate speech-merits remand.
