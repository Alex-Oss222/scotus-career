## Article III is ready for independent assessment

The four resident plaintiffs supply a concrete personal relationship to the challenged boundary and electoral assignment. The reconstructed claim remains that the State's purposeful racial assignment, with its asserted stereotyping and representational consequences, itself imposes unequal civic treatment on them. Whether that claimed treatment is cognizable on this record must be addressed separately from whether the governing Shaw rule ultimately grants relief. Residence alone is not a newly adopted automatic standing rule. Nor is a merits determination that predominance alone is constitutionally permissible an automatic Article III dismissal.

Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and the actual current Lujan/Northeastern Florida holdings in the entering-law slice require personal injury, causation, and redressability. The causal act alleged here is the State's assignment of these voters through the challenged map, and the requested injunction and lawful redistricting would address that asserted assignment if the constitutional theory succeeds. The model must decide whether this is personal treatment cognizable for Article III or merely an abstract objection, stating the distinction rather than presuming either outcome. The record contains no new injury independent of the challenged design. If at least one resident claimant supports the same unitary relief, a separate decision of DeLoach's individual standing is not automatically necessary; any independent disposition concerning him requires an identified basis.

Goosby v. Osser, 409 U.S. 512, 518–519 (1973), distinguishes a claim inescapably made frivolous by precedent from one merely of doubtful merit. Its test informs whether three judges were required; it is not permission to evade binding Shaw. The additional reconstructed controversy about personal political membership and the application of the preserved limits must be compared with Shaw's exact holding. A merits loss is not itself a finding of wholesale insubstantiality. Conversely, labeling a question constitutional does not remove the substantiality inquiry.

## Direct review and remaining source precision

Section 1253 permits direct appeal from an order granting or denying an interlocutory or permanent injunction, after notice and hearing, in a civil action required by an Act of Congress to be determined by three judges. Section 2284(a) covers a challenge to the constitutionality of congressional apportionment; subsection (b)(1) requires the original judge plus two designated judges, at least one a circuit judge; subsection (b)(3) reserves trial and the injunction/merits determination to the panel. The recorded designation and actual trial, read with the reconstructed merits denial of the injunction, supply the objective predicates for considering this route. Whether Article III or substantiality changes the route remains a legal determination for the model.

Section 2284(b)(2) separately requires at least five days' hearing notice by registered or certified mail to the Governor and attorney general when the State or its officer or agency is sued. The source proves actual service, advance scheduling, appearance, and hearings, but does not reproduce the specific mailing returns to both recipients. It is therefore accurate to state that advance notice and a hearing are documented and that no notice defect is established; it is inaccurate to state that a particular registered-mail return has been inspected. A court may assess the documented regular proceedings without inventing a missing defect. If a proposed disposition specifically turns on strict delivery compliance, retrieve the return or an appropriate date-eligible rule addressing the consequence of any proven defect; do not assume such a defect or announce an unsupported waiver rule.
