## Historical premises tested against entering law

The historical majority did not demand proof that the omitted hearing would actually grant parole. Its note 6, 514 U.S.510, expressly retains the claimant's ultimate burden of establishing a change in punishment while distinguishing proof of an individual's lesser sentence. Thus a supposed historical actual-release prerequisite is not a changed premise.

Historical Parts II–III distinguish altered punishment from method, reject every conceivable risk, and treat the individualized predictive requirement, narrow class, annual default, interval tailoring, possible expedited consideration, and release-date adjustment as showing only speculative and attenuated risk. Pages 512–513 treat absence of evidence precluding expedited consideration, counsel's practice representation, a possible extended administrative appeal, and Jackson's general release lag as reasons against constitutional injury. Corrected sources substantiate the predictive requirement, separate reasons, initial review, and authority to select a shorter interval; they do not substantiate an ordinary later reopening duty, a timely decision mechanism, an actual guaranteed release lag, or a maximum-deferral cohort finding. The historical opinion is evidence of how Justices assessed those submissions; it is not proof that the asserted mechanisms existed.

The dissent's opportunity analysis at pages 517–519 and 523–526 is strong evidence for Stevens and Souter. Its suggestion of wholly unreviewable deferral and its suspicion concerning shared reasons are not imported. Jackson establishes review of the initial predictive decision and permits overlap of underlying facts when postponement reasons are separately directed. Those corrections narrow an affirming rationale; they do not supply reconsideration of later developments.

The principal genuinely changed legal premise is Cavanaugh's binding holding that a removed meaningful mandatory occasion can itself make punishment more onerous without proof of release, notwithstanding unchanged substantive criteria and ultimate discretion. Historical Morales had no such controlling Supreme Court precedent; it cites the historical Roller proceedings differently. Cavanaugh nevertheless expressly reserved individualized postponements and interim-review systems. The reservation is an open question, not a holding invalidating or validating California. Both distinguishing and extending paths remain legally available. Reconciliation must identify why a particular Justice chooses between them rather than assign all Cavanaugh joiners the same vote.
