## Controlling premises and comparison boundary

The actual Brecht holding requires the State to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that a preserved Doyle violation properly before a federal habeas court did not contribute to the verdict. It rejects collateral posture alone as sufficient reason to replace Chapman for that claim. It expressly does not prescribe the standard for every constitutional collateral claim. Both the copied Holdings and the operative Standards entry, effective April 21, 1993, state that limited rule. No effective source identified in the neutral packet changes it before this event. A new standard for the present combined instructional and prosecutorial claim therefore requires an affirmative legal choice. Neither a universal Kotteakos rule nor a universal Chapman rule is already law.

Historical O'Neal, 513 U.S. 432, 435–445, treated historical Brecht's general adoption of Kotteakos as settled. Its separate allocation ruling treated virtual equipoise about substantial injurious influence, remaining after conscientious record review, as inconsistent with harmlessness. The report explicitly distinguished the standard's strictness from allocation of uncertainty, 438–439; it did not regard Brecht's actual-prejudice terminology as deciding the latter. It rejected an inference from §2254(a) or habeas's civil form that the petitioner necessarily loses that uncertainty, 440–445. That legal analysis and the participating associates' joins are strong comparison evidence where the relevant premises survive. They do not establish a present preference for Kotteakos over Chapman when the premise that Kotteakos already governs has disappeared.

Historical Thomas, joined by Scalia, treated harmful causation as part of the prisoner's collateral case and relied on finality, comity, and a direct/collateral distinction, 445–453. The statutory and institutional arguments remain available. His argument that a binding Brecht majority already allocated the burden to the prisoner does not survive this timeline. Scalia and Thomas must propose their construction in the open category and preserve the contrary existing Doyle rule; they cannot call their position an application of a general binding Brecht holding.

The neutral record is unchanged. The Sixth Circuit reversed the district court's conditional grant; it found the instructions alone constitutional, assumed error in the combined instruction-and-prosecutor claim, and found no injurious influence under a petitioner-prejudice formulation. It did not expressly find grave doubt. The instructions-only conclusion does not dispose of the combined allegation. No associate commitment below establishes the combined violation, actual equipoise, actual harmlessness, a petitioner default, a structural defect, or the exact terms of the district court's writ.

For every row below, a qualifying claim must be properly available, and ultimate relief requires an established constitutional violation rather than an assumption used to test harmlessness. The overruled objection to the prosecutor's statement is established; preservation of every instruction is not. Exhaustion, applicable default rules, and any other independent habeas gates retain their own predicates. A constitutional claim whose definition itself requires prejudice is distinct from harmlessness after an independently established trial violation. Sullivan's structural rule applies only if the governing reasonable-doubt standard for all factual findings was vitiated; the present allegation does not establish that predicate. Schlup's innocence gateway, Rule 52(b) forfeiture, former §2254(d)'s conditional factfinding burden, and errors in the habeas proceeding under §2111 are not substitutes for the question presented here.

## Justice-specific reconciliation and surviving departures

### Stevens

His published Brecht dissent supplies a direct standard preference: demanding independent Kotteakos review, the prosecution's obligation to explain harmlessness, and attention to the error's actual influence rather than mere evidentiary sufficiency. His Williamson Part III join confirms that substantial influence and grave doubt are compatible features of that method on preserved federal direct review, without deciding habeas. The historical O'Neal join confirms his allocation, not a new preference for Chapman. The strongest competing path is extending the now-controlling Chapman rule beyond Doyle to avoid differentiated collateral standards. His own prior public dissent supplies a concrete reason not to take that path in the reserved category. The lower opinion's affirmative language does not cure an allocation formulation capable of excluding relief in genuine doubt; renewed review is justified without a finding that such doubt existed. All frozen limits survive.

**Historical departure:** Stevens adopts Kotteakos only for the presented open instructional/argument category and preserves the existing Chapman rule for preserved Doyle error, whereas historical O'Neal assumed a general Kotteakos baseline. The changed premise is this Court's binding, expressly limited Brecht holding; Stevens's published Brecht dissent supports his standard preference where that holding left the question open, but does not authorize displacing it.

### O'Connor

The historical join establishes rejection of petitioner-risk equipoise under the then-settled substantive standard. It does not overcome O'Connor's own published Brecht join and concurrence in this timeline, which retain Chapman, emphasize confidence in verdict accuracy, and identify the administrative cost of multiplying harmless-error categories. The present alleged displacement of the defendant's own required intent is a constitutional verdict-protection claim; no claim-specific feature established in the neutral record explains a weaker remedial standard. Kotteakos with State risk is a serious lawful alternative because Brecht expressly reserved other categories and her concurrence allows direct and collateral procedure to differ. Her recorded grounds nevertheless favor extending Chapman. This is a new extension, not a claim that Brecht already dictated the result. She can narrow its scope to the combined claim without changing her judgment or sufficient ground. Genuine uncertainty defeats her selected standard, and remand leaves ultimate entitlement open.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor directs Chapman review rather than historical O'Neal's Kotteakos review because the historical premise that Brecht had displaced Chapman generally is absent. Her own controlling Brecht join and published reliability-and-administration concurrence make retention and extension of Chapman a supported choice for the present preserved elemental-proof claim; her historical grave-doubt join continues to support State risk, but supplies no contrary standard-choice commitment under this entering law.

### Scalia

His simulated Brecht join in Kennedy's dissent supports a collateral substantial-influence rule. His historical O'Neal join in Thomas's dissent supplies direct evidence on the independent allocation issue, rather than requiring allocation to be inferred from a general interest in finality. That dissent accepts the State's direct-review harmlessness burden but distinguishes the applicant's basis for collateral relief. Scalia's Williamson join thus presents a serious counterargument without being a contradictory habeas commitment. His proposed §2254(a) construction must rest on the custody requirement and collateral function, not an express statutory tie-breaking sentence; §2243 does not itself resolve the issue.
