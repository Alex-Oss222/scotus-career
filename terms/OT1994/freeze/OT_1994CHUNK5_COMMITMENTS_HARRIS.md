## Offense-date facts, text and penalties

The offense occurred on December 12, 1992. The borrowed 1976 Buick Electra was manufactured in Flint, Michigan and titled in Arkansas and Mississippi. The lower account, reviewing the evidence favorably to the verdict, places Harris with a 12-gauge sawed-off shotgun and Hoskins with an ax handle at the confrontation; they demanded that the occupants leave, Hoskins drove away, and Harris chased the occupants. The gang-dispute/afterthought account and Harris's claimed ignorance of the taking are defenses, not accepted findings. Each defendant was convicted of aiding and abetting carjacking and using/carrying a firearm during a crime of violence. Each received 41 months on Count One and 60 months on Count Two, consecutive.

Original section 2119 applies to a person possessing a firearm as defined by section 921 who takes or attempts to take a motor vehicle previously transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce, from another's person or presence by force and violence or intimidation. It authorizes a fine, imprisonment up to 15 years, or both; where serious bodily injury as defined in section 1365 results, the maximum is 25 years; where death results, any term up to life may be imposed, with a fine or both also authorized. No serious-injury or death-tier application is decided here. The later 1994 intent-to-cause-death-or-serious-injury replacement is not applied to the December 1992 offense. The enacted encouragement of federal/state cooperation and appropriate prosecution consistent with prosecutorial discretion is not an additional offense element or an automatic constitutional limit.

The operative section 924(c)(1) core requires a federally prosecutable crime of violence or drug-trafficking crime and firearm use or carrying during and in relation to that predicate. It expressly includes predicates with their own deadly-weapon enhancement, imposes five years in addition to the predicate punishment, and commands service consecutive to any other imprisonment, expressly including the predicate term. It bars probation and suspended sentence. The offense-date provision retained its no-parole sentence. Its additional short-barrel, machinegun/destructive-device/silencer and repeat-conviction tiers remain distinct; the later semiautomatic-assault-weapon tier and deletion of the parole sentence do not govern this 1992 conduct. The reported sawed-off description supplies neither a verified statutory barrel-length classification nor a Government sentencing challenge, and does not authorize changing the supplied 60-month judgment. The cumulative question concerns those actual sentences, not a newly selected weapon tier.

## Component One ? commerce power


Actual Lopez supplies three controlling holdings, each supported by the Chief Justice's opinion of the Court joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. First, a constitutionally identifiable connection is required before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority: the regulated class and the interstate mechanism connecting it to commerce among the States must be identified before aggregation and substantiality do constitutional work. The original stand-alone school-zone possession offense fails at that stage; the offered chain from possession through crime costs, impaired education and productivity to the national economy cannot supply the missing connection. The holding does not categorically exclude noneconomic intrastate conduct; Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope, and no universal formal-findings requirement is imposed. Second, intrastate conduct whose inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable; that route is recognized but left for later definition, and it cannot save this offense. Third, the Court will not insert an absent commerce element into original section 922(q)(1)(A). It leaves the conditional indictment alternative unresolved because the constitutional disposition is sufficient.

No holding makes every jurisdictional phrase constitutionally conclusive. The majority expressly distinguishes actual multistate commercial operations from an object's remote manufacture. The four public dissenters?Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg?would sustain school-zone possession on a rational substantial class-effects analysis, not on remote manufacture alone, and would remand unresolved sentence issues. Their broader application is noncontrolling but is relevant individual evidence for their approach to the stronger commercial-taking setting here.

Harris's original 1992 section 2119 regulates taking or attempting to take a motor vehicle from another's person or presence by force and violence or intimidation while possessing a firearm, and expressly requires that the vehicle have been transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Michigan-manufactured Buick's Arkansas and Mississippi history satisfies the reported enacted condition. That condition is already in the offense; the Court need not create a missing nexus element, add an interstate-resale intention, or supply an absent allegation. The absence of the original complete indictment in the produced sources does not establish an indictment defect.

The Court holds that Congress may protect the interstate transportation instrumentality itself against forcible seizure. Lopez recognizes authority over instrumentalities and explains that intrastate conduct may be reached when it directly attacks one. The required connection must exist before aggregate magnitude can matter; national crime costs do not establish it.

Here the vehicle’s transportation function, the direct attack and the enacted interstate element each perform a distinct task. An automobile is capable of carrying persons and property through interstate transportation channels. The prohibited taking attacks possession and control of that instrumentality itself. Section 2119 requires the Government to prove in every prosecution that the particular vehicle was transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Buick’s Michigan manufacture and Arkansas and Mississippi titling establish that condition. The Court neither supplies a missing statutory element nor finds that the Buick was traveling interstate when taken.

The holding does not make every crime in or near a vehicle federal, and past interstate movement alone creates no universal jurisdictional rule. Allied-Bruce’s reservation about remote object history remains intact because the direct attack and transportation function do constitutional work independent of that history. Wickard and Perez remain effective within their economic-class scope; the Court does not need that separate route to decide this application. Both the carjacking conviction and the dependent firearm conviction survive the commerce challenge.

### Two paths and eight final independent applications

**Sustaining path:** The Commerce Clause permits Congress to protect an instrumentality of interstate commerce against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the federal offense requires proof of the particular instrumentality’s interstate connection. Original §2119 is constitutional as applied to the forcible taking of the Buick proved here; the judgment is affirmed without adopting the Fifth Circuit’s aggregate-effects rationale.

**Strongest contrary path:** This is a local armed taking of a vehicle whose interstate movement may be remote, undertaken for a claimed noncommercial motive. A jurisdictional phrase should not become a device for federal control over every later event involving an old interstate object. If the opinion supported federal power solely by the Buick's manufacture, by all violent crime's aggregate costs, or by the assertion that all cars are instrumentalities, it would not answer actual Lopez. A broader economic-class classification must also not be defined so generally as all crime affecting the economy.

**Resolution at the governing baseline:** The defendants’ reliance on the taking’s local character does not defeat this protective authority. Unlike an offense in which an interstate object appears incidentally, this offense directly seizes control of the protected transportation instrumentality. No profit motive or intended interstate resale is necessary to that connection. The Fifth Circuit’s chain through violence, travel, insurance and the national economy does not supply the governing ground.

## Component Two ? culpability and fair notice

**Final rule and application.** Read original section 2119 to require knowing armed participation in the forcible taking, with intentional assistance in that taking for aiding-and-abetting liability. Mere presence, association or awareness of another's plan without intentional aid is insufficient. The original taking, force and firearm terms, read with ordinary criminal fault, give fair notice of this charged conduct. They do not require a profit motive, interstate resale intention, knowledge of the federal prohibition or the 1994 intent-to-kill-or-seriously-injure formulation. A particular defendant's claimed ignorance remains a factual defense under the requisite knowledge rule; it is not accepted here as an established fact contradicting the verdict. Motive for retaliation does not negate the statutory intent as a matter of law.

**Competing path tested.** Serious punishment for an unwitting or merely associated person would raise the fault concerns expressed in Staples, X-Citement and Shabani. That objection defeats an unrestricted strict-liability reading; it does not require facial invalidation once ordinary fault is retained. Ratzlaf's contextual legal-knowledge requirement is not universal. Scalia's and Thomas's X-Citement dissents distinguish application of a mens rea presumption to silence from rewriting an express allocation; the original carjacking language presents no contrary explicit allocation requiring an innocent taking to be punished. Actual Lopez's refusal to add an entirely absent commerce element does not displace ordinary criminal-fault interpretation or treat an enacted forceful-taking offense as strict liability.

**Instruction and jurisdictional-knowledge limits.** The exact original instructions, complete indictment and objection/preservation record are not supplied. Their absence does not establish an omitted-fault instruction, an indictment error, harmlessness or entitlement to a new trial. The lower account instead recognizes implied factual knowledge and intentional assistance. Reject the presented statute/fair-notice challenge under the stated construction; do not decide whether an unknown instruction conformed to it. The Court need not decide a distinct requirement of knowledge of the vehicle's interstate history to resolve the supplied claim; that question is not silently answered by a broad assertion that every statutory fact must be known. No new remand is ordered to search for a speculative trial error.

## Component Three ? cumulative convictions and consecutive punishment

**Final statutory ground.** Section 924(c)'s 'any' federally prosecutable violent predicate, nonexclusive inclusion of predicates carrying their own deadly-weapon enhancement, punishment 'in addition to' the predicate, and consecutive-service command clearly authorize the additional punishment for the original armed-carjacking offense. A firearm integral to the predicate's definition is not an implicit exclusion from that general direction. Original section 2119's later enactment and silence do not repeal or except the already enacted cumulative command. The question is resolved by the operative text; Singleton's analysis corroborates that construction without becoming controlling Supreme law or supplying its inaccurate historical Dixon statement.

**Competing statutory path tested.** Defendants' strongest reading treats the parenthetical as addressing only weapon enhancements of otherwise complete offenses and treats original section 2119 as a later specific offense with the firearm already built in. On that view its silence could leave residual uncertainty about multiplying convictions. But 'including' does not exhaust 'any,' and the addition and consecutive commands directly describe the relation of the firearm punishment to the predicate. Ordinary construction resolves this pairing before lenity is needed. Judicial disagreement alone does not establish residual ambiguity. The holding is confined to the provisions and same-trial pairing presented; it is not a general refusal to apply lenity or an implied-repeal rule without exceptions.

**Final constitutional ground and its limits.** Hunter supplies the controlling single-trial rule: clear legislative authorization permits cumulative punishment even assuming Blockburger overlap. Blockburger and Whalen provide a construction presumption absent contrary intent; they do not defeat the clear direction here. There is no need to decide that every possible original section 2119 offense necessarily meets every section 924(c) element. Smith retains actual firearm use or carrying and its purpose/effect relationship to the predicate; mere possession is not automatically all of those requirements. Deal resolves a different conviction-sequence issue within one prosecution and is not independently dispositive of this statutory pairing. Actual Dixon retains Grady for successive prosecutions; this single-trial holding does not alter that law.

**Stevens's independently tested constitutional objection.** He joined Marshall's Hunter dissent, which treated separate convictions' stigma and collateral consequences as constitutional harms beyond a permissible aggregate sentence and would constrain legislative multiplication of the same offense. That remains his own contrary constitutional view; it is not silently withdrawn. His final commitment here is nonetheless to reject this challenge by applying Hunter as governing same-trial precedent after finding the present statutory authorization clear. Neither actual Dixon nor the new April 26 cases displaces Hunter, and this source-supported narrow pairing supplies no separate reason to reopen it. He accepts application of controlling precedent, not a fresh independent endorsement of an unlimited legislative power to multiply convictions. He will not join an assertion that multiple convictions are constitutionally immaterial, a universal-overlap holding, or an implicit rejection of his earlier objection. An opinion that simply applies Hunter to the clear command, without those additional propositions, is compatible with his final disposition. If an opinion insists on the broader constitutional endorsement, his support is judgment-only with that reservation; his disposition does not change merely because that excess reasoning is proposed.

## Final dispositions, conditional limits and release

Each of the eight Associates rejects all three presented grounds and supports affirmance of the reviewed judgment as to the supplied convictions and sentences. The qualified instrumentality ground sustains the federal predicate under actual Lopez. Therefore section 924(c)'s federally prosecutable predicate requirement does not fail on the supplied commerce challenge. Had that predicate been held constitutionally unavailable, the dependent count would require corresponding treatment; that conditional remedial consequence is stated, but its triggering premise is rejected here and it yields no vacatur or remand in this decision.

The fault construction retains knowing participation and intentional aid without importing the 1994 intent amendment, profit motive or a universal knowledge-of-law rule. No particular missing-instruction defect, indictment defect, harmlessness ruling or new trial is found. The clear cumulative direction and controlling Hunter rule leave the 41-month Count One and consecutive 60-month Count Two terms intact for each defendant. Stevens's constitutional reservation limits opinion support as stated above; it does not create an unmade dissenting judgment vote. The Court's treatment of the two offenses need not decide universal Blockburger overlap.

There is no new remand for actual-Lopez reconsideration: that actual law has been applied here. Travelers does not require one, and the sources establish no preserved implementation error demanding one.
