## 33. Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton — No. 94-18

**Argument:** January 10, 1995. **Event cutoff:** immediately before March 6, 1995.

### Components and common source boundaries

- **M0 — reach:** Reach the admitted contractual/arbitral-power question. No jurisdictional or vehicle defect is established. Leave undisturbed the lower rejection of the hearing-stage waiver claim and forfeiture of the distinct Texas/Illinois public-policy theory; neither is necessary to the selected contract disposition.

- **M3 — judgment/remedy:** Specify partial-vacatur, confirmation, and alternative court-trial consequences. The paid $159,327 compensatory award stays closed. **M-A, contract permits the award:** The broad controversy clause and chosen arbitral rules give the arbitrators remedial power over the submitted controversy; the general New York-law term supplies substantive law without clearly adopting the special forum-allocation disability. Interpret the whole agreement without manufacturing either a universal express-words requirement for remedial exclusions or unlimited punitive entitlement. Volt enforces actual choice, while Allied-Bruce preserves agreement, scope, and ordinary defenses. The strongest objection is that New York law ordinarily includes Garrity and the contract selects that law without an express carveout. The answer adopted by supporters is contextual construction of this agreement and its institutional terms, not a claim that parties lack power to choose a restrictive rule.

**M-B, contract excludes the award:** The unqualified New York-law selection includes Garrity's remedial limit; broad arbitration language determines which controversies are submitted, not every available remedy. The supporter answers that general and specific terms can coexist by arbitrating all claims subject to the selected law's remedial boundary. Neither NASD award-format language nor an explanatory manual automatically supersedes the bargain.

Both paths preserve the actual clause qualifications: unenforceability under otherwise applicable federal or state law; the named institutions and election mechanism; future securities disputes excluded only to the extent compulsion is legally impermissible; and court entry of an award only by a court with jurisdiction. No newly recovered subjective negotiating history is assumed.

## 34. Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen — No. 93-1935

### Component conclusions and alternative test

**C0 — reach:** Reach §402(b)(3)'s procedural-sufficiency question; do not reopen the rejected lifetime-vesting theory or turn an unestablished disclosure claim into an award. **C1 — facial sufficiency:** A written reservation placing amendment in the Company identifies a statutory person and a method of amendment by that corporate person, whose action is determined under the applicable corporate law. Section 402(b)(3) does not additionally require the instrument to name a particular natural person where a corporate sponsor is the named amending person. **C2 — actual amendment:** That conclusion does not establish that the 1983 term was adopted by an authorized actor, or determine the authority, timing, or legal effect of ratification. **C3 — remedy:** Reverse the categorical statutory-insufficiency ground and remand for those actual-authority issues and other properly preserved questions. Do not enter a final judgment that benefits were lawfully terminated, order recoupment of payments, or convert the remand into permanent vested benefits.

The contrary statutory path distinguishes a power reservation from a real procedure and argues that employees cannot identify valid amendments without named human decisionmakers. That concern requires a real authorization inquiry and access to the instruments that identify authority. It does not justify reading “person” to exclude the corporation Congress expressly includes. The opposite overbroad company path—corporate identification proves every asserted amendment valid—fails because §402(b)(3)'s satisfaction and the exercise of the named power are different facts. Delaware §141(a)'s board-management rule retains its chapter/certificate qualification; no unexamined charter, delegation, officer act, or ratification is invented. A benefits judgment under §502(a)(1)(B) remains distinct from Mertens's §502(a)(3) remedial restriction.

## 35. Shalala v. Guernsey Memorial Hospital — No. 93-1251

**Argument:** October 31, 1994. **Event cutoff:** immediately before March 6, 1995.

### Component definitions, operative rules, and paired paths

- **G0 — scope:** Reach advance-refunding cost timing only. The separately affirmed debt-service-fund interest-offset component remains undisturbed. No post-1987 §1395hh rulemaking standard is imported into this preexisting 1983 manual dispute.
- **G1 — regulations:** Determine whether §§413.20 and 413.24 compel the claimed GAAP reimbursement timing, or prescribe a financial-reporting/accrual base while lawful allocation rules govern reimbursement.
- **G2 — manual/APA:** Determine whether the relevant §233 timing treatment interprets existing regulations without legislative force or substantively changes them and therefore requires applicable notice and comment. Section 553(b)(A)'s interpretive-rule inquiry is distinct from the unused benefits exception.
- **G3 — actual adjudication ground:** Decide whether the Administrator's actual matching/allocation rationale can sustain the determination under existing authority. No judicially invented rationale may rescue a conflicting rule, and an interpretive document cannot override regulations.
- **G4 — judgment and remedy:** Reverse or affirm only the Sixth Circuit's advance-refunding disposition, or adopt a properly grounded limited remand. Neither path fixes a new reimbursement amount or alters the separate offset issue.

**G-A, sustain allocation:** The recordkeeping and accrual directions do not make every GAAP recognition convention the rule of reimbursement. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A) directs consideration of accepted accounting methods, not their mandatory adoption as payment law. The hospital may retain GAAP books while Medicare allocates otherwise allowable costs under valid reimbursement principles. Existing regulations and the actual Administrator's matching rationale support this timing treatment; the relevant manual provision explains that interpretation without independently amending the regulations. The strongest contrary text is “are followed,” “will not be required,” and the direction to report incurred expense on accrual accounting. The answer is that those commands govern the accounting/reporting base, not an unqualified duty to reimburse every booked loss immediately.

**G-B, require lawful regulatory change:** The specific financial-data-to-cost-determination language and accrual instruction require the accepted incurred-cost treatment absent an actually applicable contrary reimbursement rule. Broad reasonable-cost authority and a general matching rationale do not themselves supply an enacted exception to that existing regulatory direction. Section 233's departure thus changes the binding method and cannot be implemented merely as interpretive guidance. The strongest contrary argument is that reporting has never made every GAAP cost reimbursable and lawful cost allocation remains the Secretary's task. The answer is confined to this recognized loss's timing, not universal payment of every accounting entry or denial of rulemaking power to select another reasonable method.

Current simulated law is neither categorical agency deference nor categorical rejection of it. **Thomas Jefferson University**, June 24, 1994, gives controlling weight to a permissible own-regulation interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent and preserves the limit of contrary text/promulgation intent. **Good Samaritan**, June 7, 1993, preserves lawful cost methods and considered interpretive change, not unsupported appellate rationalizations. **MCI**, June 17, 1994, preserves operative statutory limits without a new general anti-deference doctrine. **Lincoln**, May 24, 1993, does not turn a new binding reimbursement condition into an exempt organizational policy. **NationsBank**, January 18, 1995, preserves reasoned agency construction within the actual delegation.

Relevant Standards and Tests titles are **Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change** (present operation June 7, 1993), **Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution** (June 24, 1994), and **Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing** (June 17, 1994). Their exact operative qualifications remain those in entering-law C; none itself decides advance refunding.

## 36. Ambassador Books & Video v. City of Little Rock — No. 93-1886

**Scheduled argument:** January 10, 1995. **Event cutoff:** immediately before March 20, 1995. The expressly admitted questions include First Amendment, due process, takings, and attainder. The scope is not reduced merely because the appellate opinion discusses some theories more fully than others.

### Separable components and supported paths

**A0 — reach:** Reach those admitted constitutional questions in review of the dismissal after a bench trial and denial of injunctive relief. No new grant, waiver, valuation, compensation demand, or modern takings-ripeness rule is invented. The report of the trial's inverse-condemnation disposition does not establish the preservation or merits of every possible parcel-specific theory.

**A1 — speech classification and justification.** Path **A1-S** sustains the reported application under Renton's specific secondary-effects framework. The enacted locational scheme, cited studies, development history, and available relocation distinguish suppression of expression from regulation of its associated land-use effects; the attorney's abandoned early objective and service of enforcement notices do not establish that the enacted ordinance is censorship. The strongest contrary argument is the trial court's express adverse purpose statement and the appellate tendency to privilege declared purpose. The sustaining ground must answer that evidence and cannot say that any locational law or any benign preamble is necessarily neutral.

Path **A1-R** vacates the First Amendment disposition for renewed classification/justification under the correct legal standard. The trial court's contrary purpose assessment and the lower courts' use of the fact that businesses may relocate require a legally adequate examination of whether the law actually regulates secondary effects. Turner preserves examination of operation and justification, and an asserted benign purpose alone does not cure content selection. The strongest objection is that Renton already permits precisely this form of adult-use zoning and the appellate court addressed the actual evidence. A1-R therefore orders neither strict scrutiny automatically nor invalidation; it requires reasoned treatment of the contrary purpose assessment without treating it as conclusive merely because it was made after trial. It does not require a local-effects study or least-restrictive means.

**A2 — alternative avenues.** On the source-supported challenge, the 97 available-site finding, the precise 6.75-percent denominator, and United Arcade's obtained conditional-use permit support adequate alternative avenues. Unaffordable preferred premises or relocation expense alone do not show a First Amendment denial. The competing concern that nominal sites may be unusable is legally valid, but no parcel-specific disqualification of these 97 sites is established here. This component preserves the lower finding and rejects the asserted cost-only objection; it does not declare every parcel practically available under every unexamined constraint. A Justice supporting A1-R can agree with A2 without upholding the whole speech disposition.

**A3 — existing-business due process and transition.** Reject the categorical proposition that a locational ordinance can apply only prospectively or that established businesses have an absolute right to remain at their current sites. The reported substantial land-use relation, three-year transition, and possible reasonable extension for undue financial hardship support rejection of that claim. The extension is permissive upon the statutory showing, not automatic, and no application or denial is found. The contrary concern about a confiscatory or arbitrarily administered transition remains a different, record-dependent claim; three years is not a universal constitutional safe harbor. A3 does not substitute rational-basis review for the separate First Amendment inquiry.

**A4 — admitted takings/property question.** Reject a categorical compensation entitlement based solely on the requirement that an existing adult business relocate or cease that particular use at its present location. Neither the duration of the prior use nor a valid police-power label conclusively resolves compensation. Under actual **Lucas**, June 29, 1992, complete loss of the relevant property's economically beneficial use must be established, then the State must substantiate a background property/nuisance limit; no such complete loss follows from use relocation alone. The strongest opposing path seeks a remand because the trial court's reported recoupment-period rationale is insufficient for a developed compensation claim. The frozen position resolves the presented categorical existing-business/property theory and does **not** finally reject every parcel-specific compensation claim or fix preclusion beyond the claim actually decided.

The reported recoupment statement is an assessment that three years is a reasonable period in which the businesses can be expected to recover investment. It is **not** an accounting finding that either actually recovered every dollar, had no remaining loss, or received just compensation. Neither the 97-site count nor the conditional-use permit values a property interest. Reserving independent record-dependent compensation questions does not reopen or guarantee a later action, remove ordinary procedural defenses, determine whether a state remedy was required or used, or decide preclusion. **A5 — bill of attainder.** Reject the punishment claim on this ordinance. Nixon and the O'Brien formulation require the separate punishment inquiry. The strongest contrary evidence is the attorney's early statement and list of seven businesses, but that record does not make the ordinance a legislative penalty merely because its class is identifiable. A possible First Amendment defect does not automatically establish punishment. No immunity for all generally worded future restrictions is adopted.

Relevant current Standards are **Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions** (Turner, June 27, 1994), **Residential signs and preservation of a distinctive speech medium** (Ladue, June 13, 1994), and **Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use** (Lucas, June 29, 1992).
