## Corrections that matter to modeling

1. **Session law citation:** The Joint Appendix and Jackson identify the challenged enactment as 1981 California Statutes, chapter **1111**, §4, approved October 1 and filed October 2, 1981. The petition's chapter 111 and the released neutral packet's repetition of it are erroneous. The statutory quotation remains the same.
2. **Appendix composition:** The Joint Appendix reproduces the 1976, 1981, **1986**, 1990, and 1994 enactments. Its 1986 provision displays the separate two- and three-year alternatives. The 1982 history is supported by Jackson and the briefs; do not say a full 1982 session law is itself reproduced in this appendix.
3. **Statutory operation:** Annual review is the default. The Board **may**, after denial and for multiple life-taking offenses, schedule a next hearing no later than three years if it finds no reasonable expectation of parole at a hearing during the following years and states its bases. It need not use the maximum. A criminal-history category alone is insufficient. Jackson requires reasons separately stated and directed to postponement; the reasons may rest on facts also supporting unsuitability. The federal punishment inquiry remains independent of the state court's procedural characterization.
4. **Actual administration:** Morales's own maximum three-year deferral is established. The sources reviewed do **not** substantiate a cohort finding that maximum deferrals were nearly uniform. No assertion of that kind may be turned into a finding. Jackson's approximately 90% initial and 85% later unsuitability figures describe legislative analyses of the broader hearing population, not the maximum-deferral rate, the multiple-life-taking cohort, or Morales's probability of delayed release. Jackson acknowledges its release-lag petitions are not statistically representative.
5. **Reopening:** Reply p.3 n.1 asserts review for merit of inmate communications. Argument pp.9–14 admits no cited regulation, and respondent disputes the practice at pp.39–40. Argument pp.46–47 confirms that the 90-day appeal challenges the initial decision; it is not an appeal a year later on new circumstances. Jackson establishes initial administrative/judicial review and separately directed reasons, but calls advancing a hearing on extraordinary later circumstances conceivable. It establishes no ordinary reopening duty or timely decision mechanism. A letter, initial appeal, and collateral writ are not interchangeable substitutes for the removed annual hearing.
6. **Release timing:** Joint Appendix pp.19–20 permits justified departure from matrix base terms. Respondent's p.19 n.14 credit calculation and State reply p.4 n.2 dispute which credits affect which date and whether discretionary credits would be awarded. No actual earlier date or entitlement is proved. Argument pp.48–49 describes the State's belief that release within a year is practically zero; it is counsel's response without empirical data, not a cohort finding. The initial denial and predictive findings are supported; they do not establish mathematical impossibility of later suitability.
7. **Hearing date and later legislation:** July 15 versus July 25 remains unresolved; use the year unless the disagreement is expressly stated. The 1994 five-year enactment's review and the 1990 transition do not enter the 1981 text by implication. No actual later application, completion of a hearing, or mooting event is established.

## Binding law and competing paths

Cavanaugh protects a binding meaningful annual opportunity despite discretionary release and without proof of the omitted hearing's outcome. It expressly reserves individualized postponements and systems retaining meaningful interim review. That reservation makes a genuine distinction lawful; it does not decide its sufficiency. The neutral paths are (a) affirm the opportunity-only relief because a prediction and initial appeal do not preserve later consideration; (b) reverse because the separately reasoned, reviewable prediction under this limited statute materially distinguishes the unqualified Cavanaugh withdrawal; or (c) vacate and remand for a bounded operational inquiry where the chosen constitutional inquiry requires further facts. None supplies a release entitlement.

## Evidentiary allocation for independent testing

A claimant must identify the offense-date law, its retrospective alteration, an actually lost consideration occasion, and a meaningful connection between that occasion and a legally available route to earlier release. Merely invoking inconvenience or the chance of some procedural advantage is insufficient. Cavanaugh forecloses an additional demand to prove that the omitted hearing would actually have granted parole. If a party asserts a replacement process as an answer to that demonstrated loss, that party must substantiate the process's legal authorization or established practical operation, relevant triggers, decision responsibility, and timely capacity to provide ordinary reconsideration. This is a burden of supporting an asserted premise, not an automatic shift of the ultimate constitutional burden to the State and not a requirement that the State prove that parole was impossible. The sufficiency of California's individualized predictive decision is separately disputed; failure to establish reopening does not itself decide every individualized scheme.
