## 81. Kimberlin v. Quinlan — legal rule and limited vacatur

The official historical text, 515 U.S. 321–322, supplies only an unenumerated per curiam vacatur and remand for consideration in light of the same-day Johnson decision. The frozen neutral packet supports a separable pure-law question: whether the court of appeals may impose a categorical direct-evidence barrier to a motive-dependent constitutional claim. Mitchell permits legal interlocutory review; Elder requires independent review of contemporaneous legal authority without replacing the summary-judgment record. Harlow's objective immunity defense does not delete an element of the constitutional wrong. Siegert did not adopt the direct-evidence requirement: its majority found no constitutional injury, while Kennedy's concurrence expressly rejected direct-versus-circumstantial form as the decisive screen and required specific nonconclusory allegations. Leatherman reserves individual-immunity pleading, so it supplies only a procedural analogy here. Rules 8, 9(b), 26, and 56 and Holland do not make circumstantial form disqualifying.

The strongest contrary path is protecting officials against unsupported motive litigation and burdensome discovery, or sustaining a particular episode on an independent immunity ground. The answer is bounded: reject the categorical proof-form rule, preserve genuine legal immunity defenses, specific nonconclusory support, ordinary summary-judgment sufficiency, and controlled discovery. Neither trial nor liability follows automatically. The appellate court may sustain an independently supportable episode-specific judgment after applying the correct rule.
