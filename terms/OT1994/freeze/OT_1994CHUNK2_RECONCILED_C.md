## 21. McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., No. 93-1543

Historical authorship is recorded only for comparison and supplies no assignment in this Court.

The historical opinion, 513 U.S. 352, 356–363, rejects a complete ADEA relief bar arising from subsequently discovered wrongdoing that would independently have justified and caused discharge. An employer could not have acted on information it did not possess. Statutory compensation and deterrence preclude erasing the earlier violation, while the employee's misconduct remains relevant to an appropriate remedy. Reinstatement and front pay are generally inappropriate once the employer proves it actually would have terminated the employee for the misconduct alone. The backpay starting point runs from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, with extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party's legitimate interests available for consideration. The would-have-fired requirement demands more than a permissible discharge. The case assumes age discrimination only for the summary-judgment review; it does not finally find liability or award a sum.

The historical legal premises remain intact at the operative level used here. Entering Hazen Paper requires actual age motivation and preserves its separate knowledge-or-recklessness willfulness rule; entering St. Mary's rejects automatic liability without replacing Burdine's persuasion allocation. Neither changes the unknown-reason distinction or dictates forfeiture. Brown's causal and textual holding is a limited analogy, not an ADEA remedy holding.
