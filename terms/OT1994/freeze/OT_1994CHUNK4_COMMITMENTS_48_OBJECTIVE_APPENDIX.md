## Complete subsection (A) qualifications entering the modeled application

The ordinary rule covers the stated campaign publications and expenditures for communications designed to promote or defeat candidates or issues or influence voters. It requires conspicuous identification of the responsible person, or an organization's chairman, treasurer, or secretary, with a residence or business address. The words “paid political advertisement” alone do not satisfy that requirement. Printing or reproducing a publication in violation of the section is separately prohibited.

A regularly constituted central or executive committee organized under Chapter 3517 has a distinct alternative: the committee's name and its chairman or treasurer suffice. Personal correspondence is excepted only if not reproduced by machine for general distribution. The secretary of state may, by rule, exempt printed matter or communications whose size or nature makes identification unreasonable, with buttons, balloons, pencils and like objects given as examples; the statute does not itself automatically exempt those objects or require the secretary to grant an exemption. No implementing rule has been supplied. When a campaign committee pays for the disclaimer or identification, the additional clause requires “paid for by,” the committee's name and address, and the appropriate officer's name and title.

These are independent qualifications, not a single generalized exception. No fact establishes that Margaret's leaflets were personal correspondence, a party committee's publication, a campaign committee's paid communication, or covered by an implementing exemption rule. The absence of evidence of such a rule is not a finding that no rule existed. No decision about an actual exemption rule is required for the admitted as-applied dispute.
