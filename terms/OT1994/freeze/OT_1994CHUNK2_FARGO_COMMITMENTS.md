## Sufficient grounds and enhanced alternatives by component

### 1. Access, information, interval and facial scope

**Common construction and independent content limits.** All eight accept, at the lower court's stated prospective construction, telephone communication by the authorized physician or agent, physician responsibility for the gestational determination, and written certification/receipt before the abortion rather than twenty-four hours before arrival. No mandatory additional in-person visit is established. Accurate material medical information, concise qualified assistance notices, objective publication, chosen receipt and ordinary documentation are not inherently invalid merely because the State favors childbirth. The patient need not accept, read or endorse the materials; she receives them if she chooses; the provider may dissociate and comment within the stated statutory and lower-court construction. Benefit availability is not guaranteed payment; paternal liability is not guaranteed collection. Those propositions do not decide every compulsory application or create a separate unestablished First Amendment claim.

**Ground for the remand commitments.** The lower court's substantial-obstacle endpoint and its strict facial alternative do not complete Casey's operative inquiry. Telephone permission disposes of an assumed statutory second visit. It does not by itself resolve whether the actual mandatory interval interacts with Thursday/Friday physician availability, employment, distance, expense or usable care to add a consequential delay or other nontrivial interference. The record described is substantial, and the appellate court assumed evidentiary facts true; absence of formal district findings alone is not the error. The error is treating the remaining effects as constitutionally immaterial without the complete causal and substantive inquiry.

On remand, identify each actual duty, sanctions, lawful construction and exception; test function and objectively established purpose; compare actual access with and without the duty; define the affected population independently of success on the claim; determine causation and triggering interference. Baseline violations, obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail. Remaining severe interference and remaining meaningful lesser interference require their distinct justification inquiries. Only after a challenger establishes the trigger must the State substantiate the applicable justification and fit, including a response to a demonstrated concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative. No particular study or numerical benefit calculation is required. Clinical judgment, truthful communication, usable exceptions and dependent certification/enforcement receive their own analysis. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends without eliminating other applicable protections.

North Dakota has neither Pennsylvania's separate disclosure-health defense nor its permission to omit paternal-support information in rape cases. Remand cannot import those provisions. Their absence prevents treating the entire North Dakota scheme as already sustained by Casey's qualified content holdings, but does not establish an unconstitutional application without a preserved claim and supporting showing. Review of the information requirements remains confined to the actual challenge; no new mandatory omission rule is created. The emergency exception is not a general disclosure-health defense or a universal answer to nonemergency delay.

Step 6 governs relief. It permits relief against an indivisible mandatory condition whose ordinary operation produces demonstrated meaningful interference without adequate justification; some voluntary compliance does not alone defeat such relief. It still requires causation, scope and failed justification. Otherwise relief must follow the proved applications or groups. The packet does not establish the final statewide predicate or the state-law severance answer. Independently lawful content and documentation must be preserved, and any timing/content-dependent certification, sanctions and civil consequences must be identified rather than left to enforce an invalid prerequisite.

**Strongest competing affirmance path.** The State permits an advance call, agent communication and certification during the procedure visit. Many patients can receive information during ordinary scheduling, and the surviving travel and clinic schedule exist independently of the law. Reliable ordinary experience may support deliberation; a court cannot invent extra visits or demand substantiation before a trigger is shown. That is a serious path to eventual summary judgment under the complete rule. The five remanding associates reject treating it as conclusively established here: the packet's lower rationale does not resolve every evidence-supported incremental effect under the applicable lesser-interference threshold, and final record determinations are not available merely from the Court's rejection of an assumed second visit. They order renewed legal assessment, not a trial by default.

**Strongest competing final-invalidation path.** Casey rejected an unsupported fixed interval, including an independent meaningful-interference ground, and identified individualized counseling and time when needed or requested as practical alternatives. That does not itself establish the same added burdens, State response, indivisibility or severance in North Dakota. The five remanding associates reject automatic final invalidation for that reason. The interval may ultimately fail, but this handoff does not find that result already proved.

**Ground and barrier for the three access-affirmance commitments.** Kennedy's published Casey position retains protected choice and necessary care but rejects the general requirement that the State answer less burdensome alternatives for every demonstrated meaningful lesser burden. Scalia and Thomas joined the more fundamental constitutional objection and would return these regulatory choices to ordinary legislative judgment. At the actual one-visit, physician/agent, truthful-information scope, each regards the information and finite interval as reasonably connected to informed decisionmaking, without a proved third-party veto or a concrete necessary-care denial in the preserved access claim. The narrower text and delivery opportunities strengthen that application of their published grounds. None endorses ideological assent, misleading implementation or criminal uncertainty merely because the regulation concerns abortion.

The strongest objection to their position is decisive **as current law**: their preferred scrutiny lost in Casey, the complete replacement governs, and the lower court cannot use their disagreement to stop at absence of an obstacle. Their primary votes preserve their published dissenting rule; they do not pretend that the lower court correctly applied the majority rule. Under the operative rule, the bounded legal-error remand is a lawful alternative, and their record-based objection would instead be addressed by demanding a real causal trigger on remand. Accepting that alternative as their own judgment would require a new choice to follow Casey's residual tiers in this case. This freeze makes no such new choice and supplies no conditional remand vote. There is no proposed overruling holding, new common constitutional foundation, or framework coalition assembled from their votes.

### 2. Abortion-definition vagueness

**Sufficient ground adopted by each associate.** Affirm the rejection of the preserved facial challenge. The whole phrase defines termination of human pregnancy **with an intention** other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus. Its purpose language qualifies the pregnancy termination being defined, not every intentionally undertaken medical act that might accidentally cause a termination. Diagnostic amniocentesis followed by an unintended loss is therefore not transformed into an abortion simply because the diagnostic objective is not one of the two exclusions. This reads the enacted category; it does not borrow Title 12.1 or add a general knowledge-of-illegality element. The notice conclusion concerns the providers' specific hypothetical and this definition, not immunity from every other applicable medical offense or civil duty.

**Competing path and strongest objection.** The phrase does not separately say “intent to terminate,” and a literal separation of the result and purpose clauses could encompass an unintended loss from an intentional procedure. Criminal and punitive civil consequences warrant serious attention to that reading. Colautti prevents curing uncertainty in one trigger with fault directed to something else. A bounded remand would be appropriate if excluding the diagnostic accident depended on a missing state-law culpability rule. The associates reject that dependency here: the grammatical object of the expressed intention is the defined pregnancy termination. Treating an unrelated diagnostic aim as the purpose of an unintended termination severs that relationship. X-Citement's complete-category holding confirms the relevance of contextual definition reading, without transferring its federal knowledge rule. Scalia and Thomas reach this textual ground independently; their nonreach of X-Citement's alternative facial claims is not turned into a prior join.

**Join barrier/minimum limitation.** No associate joins a rationale that §12.1-02-02 automatically supplies willfulness to this Title 14 offense. Nor does anyone adopt blanket diagnostic immunity, a finding about a completed prosecution, or a constitutional requirement of actual knowledge for every element. The bounded textual ground supplies the full affirmative judgment without those additions. A materially different authoritative state construction or concrete enforcement remains reviewable.

### 3. Medical-emergency-definition vagueness

**Sufficient ground adopted by each associate.** Affirm rejection of the preserved facial objection to “grave,” “immediate” and “major bodily function.” Read together, the statute calls for a physician's prospective best clinical judgment whether the pregnancy requires immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay will create grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Gravity, imminence, irreversibility and the importance of the bodily function describe the prognosis to be made; they do not demand certainty, a numerical probability or waiting until the threatened loss occurs. The death alternative likewise permits necessary preventive action, not action only after death is imminent beyond prevention. A standard may be medically restrictive yet intelligible; whether it unlawfully excludes particular needed care is a different claim, not the preserved vagueness claim.

Vuitch and Doe show that a prospective clinical judgment can supply a usable professional standard. Their broad health constructions are not imported wholesale: North Dakota retains its actual death and specified twenty-four-hour peril criteria. The lower court's significant-threat protective interpretation is accepted at its stated prospective status; it is not enacted text or a binding North Dakota Supreme Court construction. The notice ground does not require treating that interpretation as permission to delete “immediate,” “irreversible,” or “major.” It rests on the comprehensible forecast the enacted words ask a physician to make. The “before the abortion if possible” explanation proviso remains conditional; certification is no permission to delay necessary treatment.

**Competing remand/invalidation path and strongest objection.** Colautti cautions that medical language and subjective judgment do not cure an uncertain trigger, and that fault about performing an abortion is not fault about the emergency assessment. The absent demonstrated Title 14 fault rule, the word “best” rather than “good faith,” and separate punitive civil exposure foreclose an assumption of bad-faith-only liability. Vuitch and Doe also afforded broader discretion than the words challenged here. Those objections would require reconsideration if the judgment depended on universal willfulness or if an actual ambiguous competing duty had been identified. They do not establish that these specified clinical severity and timing terms lack a knowable professional meaning. Unlike Colautti's conflicting viability formulations and uncertain technique priorities, the preserved wording attack identifies no incompatible triggers or rival obligation whose relative priority this definition leaves unintelligible. Possible later professional disagreement does not by itself render the prospective criterion vague.

**Join barrier/minimum limitation.** Affirmance must state that “best clinical judgment” is not an enacted synonym for a bad-faith-only safe harbor or conclusive physician self-certification. It gives no general professional immunity. Neither a later reasonable medical disagreement alone nor a cited Title 12.1 default establishes all elements of a punishable offense or civil damages. Actual culpability, burden allocation and permissible enforcement require their own applicable sources if a concrete case presents them. The associates do not adjudicate an unpressed emergency-access theory, every clinical scenario or a future contrary authoritative construction. Their facial-notice conclusion remains independent of the access remand/affirmance split.

If a later stage rejects this sufficient ground, the recorded narrower alternative is reconsideration of this vagueness component identifying the particular unclear clinical duty and legally available construction or fault rule. It is not deletion of the emergency exception while leaving consent and delay absolute. No associate presently selects that alternative.

### 4. State scienter, notice and enforcement

City of Dickinson v. Mueller, 261 N.W.2d 787, 789–790 (N.D. 1977), and State v. North Dakota Education Association, 262 N.W.2d 731, 733–734 (N.D. 1978), contradict a default applicable to all state crimes. Goetz, 312 N.W.2d 1, 11–12 (N.D. 1981), concerns an outside-title statute already saying “willfully”; it does not erase that boundary. Each associate rejects reliance on §12.1-02-02 alone to supply willfulness to §14-02.1-11. The abortion definition's own intention words and the penalty's separate expressly willful rule/regulation-infraction clause retain their respective work. The first penalty sentence and enacted civil-damages provisions do not acquire that latter clause's word automatically.

The competing ground is to remand both definitions because the appellate opinion also relied on fault. All eight reject that remedy where the independently sufficient textual and clinical grounds resolve the actual facial objections. Conversely, they do not infer strict liability, a comprehensive state constitutional answer, or automatic invalidity from the failure of one additional rationale. No independent facial relief against the general penalty is established merely by its omission of an express culpability word. No additional all-elements mental state, general knowledge-of-illegality rule, bad-faith requirement or civil-damages defense is supplied. Any later necessary fault inquiry must identify its lawful source and particular element; criminal and civil liability remain distinct.

### 5. Remedy and interim protection

The five remanding associates vacate only the access disposition specified above, affirm both preserved definition-vagueness dispositions on the independent grounds, and remand for renewed legal assessment and appropriate proceedings. They direct prompt consideration of a properly presented request for interim protection under the applicable ordinary standard and current record. They do not restore the dissolved 1991 injunction by operation of vacatur, prescribe its unidentified terms, find present irreparable harm, order a hearing or trial, finally sever provisions, or award a permanent injunction. Final relief must identify the invalid duty or application, dependent forms and sanctions, the surviving lawful requirements, and the applicable severability basis. A legal error does not establish every equity predicate.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm the access dismissal as well as the definition dispositions and deny requested restoration. Their agreement that any eventual relief must fit an established violation does not count as agreement to the remand. Their acknowledgment of ordinary lower-court authority under a mandate they oppose adds no affirmative interim direction.

The strongest competing interim path is immediate restoration to prevent continuing injury while the correct test is applied. The omitted operative decree and current equitable predicates prevent selecting that relief here. The strongest opposite path is to foreclose interim relief because no final invalidity is established. The remanding associates reject that inference: a properly supported interim application remains available before final adjudication. Transcon distinguishes remedial authority, violation and fit; its admitted violation and statutory public-enforcement setting do not create an injunction entitlement here. Casey's denial of extra materials/attendance relief on its own record is no universal bar. Benten's unusual appellate-stay/mandatory-relief setting is not imported.
