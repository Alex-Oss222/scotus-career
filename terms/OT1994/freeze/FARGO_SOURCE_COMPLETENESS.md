## Qualifications confirmed or added

No correction to the supplement's statutory transcription, stated joins, or title-limitation account was found. The following limits deserve express preservation:

- **Vuitch and Doe.** Their vagueness holdings use the particular statutory constructions described in the supplement. *Vuitch* did not adopt Justice Stewart's separate proposal of complete criminal immunity for licensed physicians; its majority retained the prosecution's burden and normal criminal safeguards. *Doe* did not eliminate professional discipline or licensure remedies. Neither supplies categorical immunity from review of clinical judgment.
- **Colautti.** The majority distinguished subjective professional judgment from culpability for error and reserved the precise fault requirement for a properly drafted statute. Its preservation discussion permits a prevailing party to support the judgment on another ground (400 n.16); that proposition does not authorize reversal on an unpresented claim. The majority's refusal to order abstention and White's contrary state-construction concerns remain distinct positions.
- **Hoffman Estates and Akron II.** The stated conditional facial-review formulations, statutory-context limits, and part-specific joins remain accurate. *Akron II* left open whether parental-notice statutes require bypass procedures, because the challenged bypass satisfied the consent-case requirements; Stevens reserved examination informed by implementation. Its bypass conclusions are not an adjudication of medical-emergency vagueness.
- **North Dakota cases.** *Mueller* reserves its voluntary-versus-coerced-conduct question. *NDEA* rejected the proposed default-fault instruction in Part IV but ultimately invalidated the election-disclosure law on speech grounds; its separate writing addressed the candidate-versus-measure speech distinction. *Goetz*, 312 N.W.2d at 12–13, additionally rejects the proposed evil-intent requirement and finds persuasive securities decisions not requiring knowledge of illegality. It preserves the distinction between defining an expressly present fault term and importing default fault into a statute containing none. Its favorable-to-defendant instruction analysis does not establish element-by-element culpability for a different statute.
- **Additional Kolender source detail.** O'Connor's majority, joined by Burger, Brennan, Marshall, Blackmun, Powell, and Stevens, expressly rejects White's universal all-applications characterization at 358–359 n.8. It cites *Colautti* for facial invalidation of a criminal statute despite conceivable valid applications and distinguishes *Hoffman Estates*' business-regulation setting. The holding concerns undefined identification demands and arbitrary enforcement. The Court reserved the other Fourth and Fifth Amendment merits at 361–362 n.10; Brennan's additional Fourth Amendment theory is a concurrence. White, joined by Rehnquist, dissented. This supplements the earlier locator entry without supplying a result for any different dispute.
