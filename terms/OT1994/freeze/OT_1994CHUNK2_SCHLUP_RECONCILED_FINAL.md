## Complete statutory terms and legal distinctions

Former 28 U.S.C. §2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The qualifying prior proceeding can be an evidentiary merits hearing on a material factual issue **or** a merits hearing on an issue of law, followed by the stated federal denial of relief to a state prisoner. The earlier appellate merits consideration of guilt-phase ineffective assistance supplies an actual legal-merits proceeding, not merely a second-petition label. Lack of the now-requested evidentiary hearing does not negate the statute's separate legal-hearing alternative.

The two qualifications are conjunctive and distinct: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence does not necessarily create a new constitutional ground. Absence of deliberate withholding does not negate other abuse. Permission not to entertain is not a command to dismiss. Subsection (a)'s separate federal-custody language cannot be inserted into subsection (b), and no current-case Supreme Court action is assumed to trigger subsection (c).

*McCleskey v. Zant*, 499 U.S. 467, 487–489, requires consideration when both statutory qualifications are met **and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent**. It explains that judicial habeas principles govern the statutory discretion concerning deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive grounds. Its abuse rule is not confined to deliberate abandonment: the State must identify the prior writ history and new claims and plead abuse with clarity and particularity, after which the burden shifts to the prisoner. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional miscarriage route remain separate. Schlup does not press cause and prejudice in this review; that fact is not an invented concession about every claim, default, exhaustion issue or statutory predicate.

The present interpretive disagreement is narrower than whether statutory permission exists. It concerns how the recognized innocence exception limits refusal, what legal error has occurred, and whether this actual refusal should stand. The conditional statutory duty for a new, nonabusive ground does not by itself decide the distinct question whether a successive or abusive ground must be entertained after an adequate innocence showing. Neither *McCleskey*'s conditional-duty passage nor a few permissive phrases in earlier opinions settle that question by themselves. *Kuhlmann*'s relevant discussion retains its actual plurality status.

For the five remand Justices, the wrong guilt-gateway rule materially shaped the decision under review. Even if refusal is discretionary, a refusal resting on that legal error warrants reconsideration here; no independently adequate reason on this record makes the error immaterial. They need not determine whether every successful gateway showing invariably requires merits consideration or choose the general appellate standard for all subsequent-petition decisions. For Scalia and Thomas, the statute preserves real refusal discretion, reviewable for abuse; they find the lower courts' refusal justified and regard the strict rule as correct. They do not claim that the word “may” suffices without reasons or that discretion makes every legal error irrelevant.

## Operative standards, evidence and remedy that assembly must preserve

### Probability path: five Associate commitments

For a prisoner invoking crime innocence to obtain review of otherwise barred independent constitutional claims, require new reliable evidence and assess it with all relevant probative evidence. The prisoner must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt on that record. This is more demanding than ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice. It is not satisfied by a mere possibility of acquittal, by one juror who might disagree, or by the reviewing judge's own reasonable doubt. A reasonable juror is assumed fairly to consider the evidence and obey the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

The inquiry is not confined by the rules of trial admissibility: relevant excluded or subsequently available evidence may matter, and illegally admitted evidence may also have probative force subject to its reliability. This is a gateway inquiry, not a new trial-admissibility decision. The original verdict remains an important starting point, but *Jackson*'s question whether credited trial evidence legally could sustain conviction does not resolve what reasonable jurors likely would do with the augmented record. The court may evaluate likely credibility, timing, delay and inconsistency; it need not accept every affidavit as true as if testing summary judgment. No categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discounts a witness solely for being a prisoner.

The trial videotape already existed. Green's prompt-call account, Faherty's description of Schlup's movement and the other identification accounts seek to change its meaning and the relationship between the attack, distress response and arrival in the dining hall. The two officers' identifications remain relevant but are not a complete legal answer to those submissions. The lower court's use of the stricter rule and its treatment of credibility as the original jury's exclusive task prevent accepting the existing refusal as a proper application of the selected probability standard. The affidavits might prove unreliable; this Court does not resolve their truth or find that the gateway has been passed.

**Remedy:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment and remand to that court with instructions to return the matter to the District Court for further proceedings under this rule. The District Court must make the legally proper gateway assessment and decide what factual development it requires. It may take testimony from the key witnesses if appropriate; a categorical mandatory-hearing direction is not part of these commitments. The remand does not order constitutional-merits review solely because remand has occurred, a writ, retrial, release or a stay. If an independently available claim later satisfies simulated *Keeney/Townsend*'s necessary-hearing conditions, their separate duty applies; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not defeat that duty in its proper setting. Those conditions are not found satisfied merely by selecting the guilt gateway.

The actual refusal is not sustained by §2244(b)'s wording alone. Nor is the lower court's discussion of earlier ineffective-assistance merits silently erased. The five conclude that no independently adequate ground established on the supplied record renders this legal error immaterial across the claims at issue. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and any genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. The Court need not hold that a successful innocence showing overrides all such barriers or makes every exercise of refusal discretion unlawful.

### Strict gateway and statutory-refusal paths: three and two Associate commitments

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would require clear and convincing evidence that, considering the probative record as a whole, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner guilty beyond a reasonable doubt. They propose an extension/specification for crime innocence, acknowledge *Carrier*'s probability language and *Sawyer*'s eligibility reservation, and regard the exceptional repeat-litigation setting as supporting the greater quantum. Their finding of insufficiency rests on the continuing identifications, uncertain timing and contradictions/delay in the new accounts. It does not find every affiant false or treat the existence of any legally sufficient trial evidence as categorically dispositive.

Scalia and Thomas additionally would sustain the actual discretionary refusal under §2244(b). The legal-merits history and accepted successive/abusive posture supply more than a bare second-petition label; the courts actually refused renewed review after considering the innocence submission, and these Justices find that refusal within lawful discretion. The statute's two qualifications and the distinct categories of a repeated ground and a new abusive ground remain intact. They do not add an adjudication that every present claim was repeated, that a particular unexamined new ground was deliberately withheld, or that all possible independent barriers were satisfied. Their analysis accepts the procedural setting presented by the gateway dispute and evaluates the refusal within it.

Their position also preserves two limits. First, an identified misconception of law can be an abuse of discretion; discretion is not an exemption from legal review. Second, *Wong Doo*'s recognition that an independent sound discretionary ground can preserve a judgment despite legal error is not an independent ruling that these affidavits necessarily fail the probability gateway. The statutory rationale and stricter-standard rationale are distinct, but their application here shares the conclusion that the lower court used a legally proper quantum. Assembly must not describe them as an unconditional affirmance even if Schlup is found to satisfy the correct probability gateway.
