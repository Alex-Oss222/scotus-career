## Facts requiring incorporation

1. The district court's opinion expressly reports that Lebron did not contend Amtrak was a governmental agency. His contention was that federal involvement in Amtrak's operations and authority made the particular challenged actions governmental. This is reported in *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 811 F. Supp. 993, 999 (S.D.N.Y. 1993), paragraph 29 in the linked transcription. It is inconsistent with treating the record as establishing no express earlier disavowal. [District opinion, paragraph 29](https://openjurist.org/811/fsupp/993/lebron-v-national-rr-passenger-corp-amtrak-2006630).
2. The identified pretrial document is Plaintiff's Pre-Trial Proposed Conclusions of Law, No. 92-CIV-9411 (S.D.N.Y.), page 12 note 1, reprinted in the appendix in Second Circuit No. 93-7127 at page 1297. The identified appellate document is Brief for Appellee in Second Circuit No. 93-7127, page 30 note 39. Both are reported as expressly disavowing the governmental-entity theory while advancing attribution based on close federal connections. The appellate brief's reported formulation distinguishes entity status in itself from treatment as a state actor for the challenged conduct. These two original documents were not independently retrieved during this correction; the document identification and appellate-brief report require the source qualification below.
3. The governmental-entity alternative was first explicitly advanced in the Supreme Court merits brief. That is a reported party act, not a conclusion about whether the argument may be considered. The parties' existing First Amendment claim, the distinction between a claim and supporting argument, express disavowal, question scope, and the actual lower-court treatment require separate examination under entering law.
4. The Second Circuit's opinion independently states in paragraph 17 that the Act created Amtrak as a private corporation and cites the nonagency provision. Paragraphs 18–32 then examine whether the challenged conduct of the private entity is attributable to government. This is the actual sequence; whether the first paragraph constitutes adjudication of the entity-status question or only an accepted premise is a legal issue, not a resolved source fact. [Second Circuit opinion](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/012/12.F3d.388.93-7127.1494.html).
5. The historical petition's reported question asks whether the court of appeals erred in holding Amtrak's political-advertising policy at Pennsylvania Station was not state action, with four listed premises: federal creation, governmental powers, voting stock and board appointments; board approval of the challenged policy; annual subsidy of losses; and federal purchase of the station and shared governmental occupancy. This description is a report of petition language, not verification of every premise listed by the petitioner. In particular, a statement in a question is not a fact finding that every director is directly appointed by the President or that the board specifically reviewed the rejection at issue. The supplied simulation question must be compared with this party document without importing a historical grant or replacing user-established scope.
