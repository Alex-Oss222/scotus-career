## B. Lower judgment and facts

**Primary lower source:** *Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund*, 6 F.3d 821 (D.C. Cir. Oct. 22, 1993), amended October 25, 1993, No. 91-5360. Silberman wrote the panel opinion. The full supplied lower opinion was read. Former Circuit Judge Ginsburg heard the argument but did not participate in that opinion; the Supreme Court participation exclusion below rests separately on admitted intake.

The challenged transaction was NRA-ILA's return to PVF of $415,744.72 that PVF previously had reimbursed for direct member-solicitation expenses. The FEC did not challenge NRA-ILA's original payment to vendors or PVF's reimbursement; it challenged the later return as an unlawful corporate contribution. After unsuccessful conciliation, it brought an enforcement action against NRA-ILA, PVF, and PVF's treasurer, Grant A. Wills. The district court held the return unlawful, rejected the defenses, imposed a civil penalty equal to investigation and prosecution costs, and enjoined similar transfers. The district decision is reported at 778 F. Supp. 62 (D.D.C. 1991); this packet verifies its posture through the appellate opinion, not a separate inspection of the district opinion.

The court of appeals reversed because it considered congressional agents' ex officio membership on an executive enforcement commission unconstitutional. It treated the ex officio provision as severable and rejected the de facto officer defense as a reason to deny these enforcement defendants relief. It did not decide the statutory contribution or First Amendment defenses.

The lower court found the enforcement defendants able to raise the commission-independence and ex officio-membership objections without proving that a differently composed agency would have decided otherwise. It rejected the separate challenge to the three-members-per-party limitation as nonjusticiable on this record and rejected the Take Care objection under the precedents it discussed. Those lower determinations must remain distinct from the ground supporting reversal and from the supplied Supreme Court question set.

## D. Law entering the threshold issues

### 1. Date-eligible statutory text

**2 U.S.C. §437d(a)(6):** the Commission has power

> to initiate (through civil actions for injunctive, declaratory, or other appropriate relief), defend (in the case of any civil action brought under section 437g(a)(8) of this title) or appeal any civil action in the name of the Commission to enforce the provisions of this Act and chapter 95 and chapter 96 of title 26, through its general counsel;

**Section 437d(e):** except as provided in §437g(a)(8), the Commission's power to initiate civil actions under (a)(6) is the exclusive civil remedy for enforcement of the Act. **Section 437c(b)(1)** directs administration, compliance efforts and policy formulation for FECA and Title 26 chapters 95 and 96, and gives the Commission exclusive civil-enforcement jurisdiction. These clauses concern enforcement; whether they also supersede the separate Supreme Court representation rule is the disputed construction, not a textual conclusion already given.

**Section 437c(f)(4)** separately permits the Commission to appear in and defend against any action instituted under the Act through its own attorneys or temporarily appointed counsel, with the appointment and compensation qualifications stated there. The statute does not use that defense clause to expressly name a petition for certiorari by the Commission seeking reversal of an adverse enforcement judgment. Its relationship to the more specific defense language of §437d(a)(6) should not be silently collapsed if it performs work in the reasoning.

**28 U.S.C. §518(a):**

> Except when the Attorney General in a particular case directs otherwise, the Attorney General and the Solicitor General shall conduct and argue suits and appeals in the Supreme Court and suits in the United States Court of Federal Claims or in the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit and in the Court of International Trade in which the United States is interested.

Section 518(b) permits the Attorney General, when he considers it in the United States' interests, personally to conduct and argue a case in a federal court or direct the Solicitor General or a Department of Justice officer to do so. Section 518(a)'s particular-case direction exception must be preserved; it is not an absolute textual ban on delegated representation.

**28 U.S.C. §516:** *Providence Journal* reproduces the general reservation of litigation involving the United States, an agency, or officer to Department of Justice officers under the Attorney General, “[e]xcept as otherwise authorized by law.” That is a distinct proviso from §518(a)'s particular-case direction language. **28 C.F.R. §0.20(a), as reproduced in the 1988 primary opinion**, delegates to the Solicitor General, in consultation with the relevant agency or official, conduct, assignment and supervision of Supreme Court cases including appeals, certiorari petitions and oppositions, briefs, argument and settlement. The packet has not separately checked the 1994 regulation; a theory depending on a regulatory change or detailed delegation beyond the reproduced provision requires that historical text.

**26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d)** each provide:

> The Commission is authorized on behalf of the United States to appeal from, and to petition the Supreme Court for certiorari to review, judgments or decrees entered with respect to actions in which it appears pursuant to the authority provided in this section.

The concluding limitation is essential. Section 9010 authorizes participation in specified presidential campaign-fund proceedings, recovery following §9007 audit, and declaratory/injunctive relief concerning that subtitle or §6096; subsection (c) separately provides three-judge treatment on application and direct Supreme Court appeal. Section 9040 authorizes specified appearances, recovery after §9038 audit, and injunctions implementing its chapter. Neither subsection (d) grants independent certiorari authority for every FECA action merely because the Commission is a party. This transaction is presented as FECA corporate-contribution enforcement, not as an action within either funding section. Their express certiorari wording remains material statutory comparison evidence; the force of that comparison is contested.

**28 U.S.C. §2101(c):**

> Any other appeal or any writ of certiorari intended to bring any judgment or decree in a civil action, suit or proceeding before the Supreme Court for review shall be taken or applied for within ninety days after the entry of such judgment or decree. A justice of the Supreme Court, for good cause shown, may extend the time for applying for a writ of certiorari for a period not exceeding sixty days.

The extension is permissive and requires good cause and action by a Justice; no extension is established by this packet. The statute governs timing but does not alone answer whether a timely filing by an allegedly unauthorized agency can be ratified after the filing period.

### 2. Primary precedents

**United States v. Providence Journal Co., 485 U.S. 693 (1988).** The Court dismissed a writ sought by a court-appointed criminal-contempt prosecutor who lacked Solicitor General authorization. Section 518(a) covered the United States' interest in vindicating judicial authority; neither the judicial origin of the prosecution nor lower-court litigating authority alone conferred Supreme Court representation authority. The majority distinguished §516's “otherwise authorized by law” proviso from §518(a), but footnote 9 expressly recognizes Congress's power to enact an exception and requires ordinary statutory construction to determine whether it did so. The case does not require Congress to employ an invented formula or decide the meaning of the FEC statutes. The Solicitor General's amicus support there did not make the unauthorized petitioner a proper representative. That situation must be distinguished from actual authorization or ratification if the latter is established here.

Blackmun authored *Providence Journal*; O'Connor and Scalia joined. Scalia separately maintained his objection to court-appointed contempt prosecutors while joining the statutory holding. Stevens dissented on historical practice, the interests of coordinate branches, and the Solicitor General's own interpretation; that dissent did not construe the FEC's express litigation provision. Kennedy did not participate. These are date-eligible public positions, not proposed FEC votes.

**Cook v. Tullis, 85 U.S. (18 Wall.) 332, 338–340 (1873).** In a property-substitution and bankruptcy dispute, ratification of an unauthorized transaction ordinarily related back, subject to intervening third-party rights. The ratifying principal had to be able to perform the act both when originally done and at ratification. The actual decision upheld ratification because the trustees had acquired no rights that defeated it, and it supplied an independent equitable-tracing ground. This supports both the usual relation-back premise and its capacity/intervening-rights qualification. Whether expiration of a statutory petition period supplies the relevant impediment is an application to be reasoned here, not an issue *Cook* itself decided.

**Dodge v. United States, 272 U.S. 530, 532–533 (1926).** The Government could adopt an unauthorized seizure and pursue forfeiture where the property was in federal custody when the libel was filed. The Court distinguished infringement of constitutional search-and-seizure rights. Its adoption principle supports a possible cure argument but does not decide whether subsequent authorization cures an appellate filing after an express statutory deadline; the property and jurisdictional facts of *Dodge* matter.

**Independent-authority path:** the FEC can argue that §437d(a)(6)'s power to “appeal any civil action” through its general counsel includes Supreme Court review, particularly alongside exclusive civil-enforcement responsibility. Section 518(a) itself uses “suits and appeals” while *Providence Journal* applies it to certiorari. **Reserved-representation path:** respondents can argue that §437d gives ordinary enforcement litigation and appellate authority while leaving Supreme Court representation with the Attorney General/Solicitor General under §518(a). The explicit certiorari grants in the funding provisions make the omitted language material, and *Providence Journal* shows that authority to prosecute below need not entail authority to petition here. This construction must answer the broad “any civil action” phrasing, contextual use of “appeals,” and the rule that asserted statutory exceptions receive ordinary construction rather than a newly imposed magic-words condition.

**Ratification path if independent authority fails:** the FEC can argue that a timely petition already existed and subsequent authorization cured a representation defect by relation back, drawing on *Cook*'s general principle and *Dodge*'s governmental adoption. Respondents can invoke *Cook*'s own requirement that the principal be able to undertake the act at ratification and §2101(c)'s elapsed period; *Dodge* involved possession and forfeiture, not an expired Supreme Court filing period. The distinction between timely form correction and unauthorized invocation must be addressed rather than assumed. The candidate supplies authorization dated May 26 but not its wording. ## F. Conditional constitutional and remedy branches

If an authorized, timely review path exists, the structural question remains contested. The lower court acknowledged the absence of formal voting or decisionmaking power comparable to *Bowsher* and *MWAA*, yet concluded that Congress impermissibly placed its agents within executive deliberations. The FEC's position is that nonvoting membership serves communication and advice and gives no legally controlling vote or veto. Respondents' position is that institutional membership and potential influence exceed legislative oversight even without a binding vote. Neither position may be converted into an established constitutional test by describing the missing vote as automatically decisive or the mere presence as already forbidden by Supreme Court law.

The challenged statutory provision is §437c(a)(1), quoted in substance above. Section 437c(b)(2) preserves Congress's investigatory, informational, oversight, supervisory and disciplinary authority with respect to federal elections. Section 437c(c) requires four affirmative Commission votes for §437d(a)(6) action and forbids delegation of a member's vote or decisionmaking duty. These clauses must remain distinct from the ex officio voting exclusion and from the reported operating limits on meeting calls and quorum.

The pertinent primary constitutional sources are *Buckley v. Valeo*, 424 U.S. 1 (1976); *Bowsher v. Synar*, 478 U.S. 714 (1986); and *Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority v. Citizens for the Abatement of Aircraft Noise*, 501 U.S. 252 (1991), with the actual limits, authors and joins of any relied-on portions. Their holdings concern appointment or exercise of governmental power; the lower opinion quotes and applies them, but those quotations alone do not independently validate every potential Supreme Court merits rationale. A substantive constitutional commitment must obtain the pertinent primary opinions and any operating rule on which that commitment materially depends.
