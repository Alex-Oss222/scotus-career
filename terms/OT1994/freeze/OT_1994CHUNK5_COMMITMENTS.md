## 49. Stone v. INS

### Components and legal alternatives

The reviewable question concerns the Sixth Circuit's dismissal of review of the July 26, 1991 original deportation order. The August 1991 motion preceded judicial review and sought reconsideration rather than reopening on new facts; the BIA denied it February 3, 1993. The March 25, 1993 judicial petition was within ninety days of that denial but outside ninety days of the original order. The lower court's separate denial of relief from the reconsideration refusal is a second judgment component, not an adjudication of original-order timeliness. Sources: 13 F.3d at 935–936, 938–939; validated packet, “Judgment, record, and scope.”

**Path preserving original-order review:** Former §1105a(a) incorporates chapter 158 review, including its established reconsideration treatment unless an INA modification displaces it. Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. at 284–285, distinguishes exhaustion from a timely motion's actual effect on finality even under language declaring an agency order final when served. Ibarra supplies the related administrable finality principle without controlling this different statute. Section (a)(6)'s consolidation command still has work where judicial review precedes the reconsideration motion or where a later motion does not suspend the original deadline. On this path, a timely pre-review motion postpones review finality; the full ninety-day period begins upon disposition, and the original-order dismissal is reversed and remanded. A late motion does not resurrect an expired review period.

The strongest objection is the amendment's explicit expectation of two judicial reviews, considered with the shortened period. This is a serious statutory argument. It does not, however, make consolidation text meaningless under the preserving construction: the provision can coordinate a previously filed original-order petition and a later petition concerning reconsideration. The objection must therefore be resolved by interpreting the INA modification, not by saying the competing construction is linguistically impossible.

**Path requiring separate deadlines:** Section (a)(6) is a specific modification of the incorporated review procedure. Its command to consolidate review of the original order with separately sought review concerning reconsideration contemplates continuing finality of the first order. The amended short deadline and the two-order structure support a petition directed to each order within its own period. On this path the original-order dismissal is affirmed; neither an optional motion nor its denial starts a new period for the original order.

The strongest objection is that the same consolidation command can operate without displacing the ordinary timely-motion rule. Locomotive Engineers is especially weighty because it concerns the very incorporated review chapter, not just criminal appeals. The separate-deadline construction must rest on the specific amendment's structural instruction; the regulations' execution and no-stay provisions, concern about delay, or a general preference for strict jurisdictional rules cannot alone answer the objection.

## 50. Kyles v. Whitley

### Operative question and alternative paths

The commitment concerns the existing Brady/Bagley suppression claim and its conviction remedy. It does not award relief on a separately unadmitted Strickland, intimidation, false-testimony, sentencing-only, or later-evidence claim.

Bagley, 473 U.S. at 674–678, 682–685, supplies reasonable-probability materiality, including impeachment. The question is whether disclosure would create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence, not whether acquittal becomes more likely than conviction and not whether remaining evidence could sustain a conviction. Individual suppressed items are evaluated for their cumulative effect without requiring each to be independently dispositive. Blackmun's discussion joined by O'Connor, pp. 682–684, specifically addresses changes to preparation and presentation; its authority at that level is not inflated beyond its stated joins. The reasonable-probability standard itself has the support identified in Bagley.

**Relief path:** The withheld identification statements, Beanie accounts, garbage memorandum, and plate list collectively permit a materially different challenge to the State's identification and physical-evidence case. Smallwood's initial account versus his detailed trial account can alter the force of one identification; Williams's original height/build description adds another distinct difficulty. Beanie's own admissions can corroborate parts of defense testimony previously vulnerable as friends' or relatives' claims, while his inconsistent chronology and knowledge of the evidence permit scrutiny of the investigation. Police examination about information used in the investigation does not necessarily require calling Beanie or offering every assertion for its truth. The apparent strength of the physical case must be reassessed alongside that evidence of provenance and investigative dependence. Sources: lower majority, 5 F.3d at 811–818; King dissent, id. at 821–827, 829–843; validated packet, “Suppressed material and its possible uses.”

The strongest objection is that Territo's identification, other identifications, ammunition, holster, pet food, and receipt remain, and that the defense already presented a framing theory. The relief path answers by assessing the different evidentiary support for that theory after disclosure, not by declaring those strands nonexistent. It does not find planting, innocence, intentional false testimony, a complete plate list, or a reason for the first jury's disagreement. It also does not adopt the dissent's suggestion that a hypothetical invocation of the privilege against self-incrimination could itself establish an inference of guilt.

**Affirmance path:** Apply the same cumulative standard but conclude that the properly grounded uses of the withheld material do not sufficiently impair confidence. Several items reinforce a defense the jury already heard; the prospect of impeaching Beanie must be kept distinct from proof that he planted evidence. The unchanged identification and tangible evidence, and the adverse credibility findings, retain force. Sources: 5 F.3d at 812–818, with the counterarguments at 831–843.

The strongest objection is the risk of treating mutually reinforcing new uses as merely cumulative and of assuming that the choice not to call Beanie proves the absence of investigative or corroborative value. The affirmance commitments below do not adopt those categorical propositions. They consider the additional uses and still find the evidentiary showing inadequate under Bagley; they cannot rest on the original jury's rejection as a conclusive answer to a different disclosure counterfactual.

**Remand without present materiality determination:** The lower majority explicitly invoked collective review; it cannot accurately be charged with expressly rejecting that rule. Its discussion nevertheless gives insufficient weight to preparation and non-testimonial uses when it ties lack of useful Beanie evidence to the separate reasonable-counsel conclusion, 5 F.3d at 815 & n.10, 818–820. A remand could correct that defect. The relief commitments find the established suppression record sufficient to decide legal materiality without finding a new witness truthful, and therefore support conditional new-trial relief rather than another open-ended materiality pass. The affirmance commitments find no outcome-affecting error requiring a second assessment once the additional lawful uses are considered.

## 51. Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co.

### Scope and competing legal paths

The judgment under review sustains the injunction against the challenged truthful numerical alcohol-content label restriction under §205(e)(2). The district judgment sustaining the advertising restriction was not appealed by Coors. The Tenth Circuit resolved material advancement and expressly did not reach the separate fit ground. Sources: 2 F.3d 355, appendix pp. 2a–5a, 9a n.6; validated packet, “Litigation path and factual dispute.”

The governing standard is actual Central Hudson/Fox law as applied in Discovery Network, Edenfield, Edge, Ibanez, and April 18 Lanphere. Assume truthful, nonmisleading information concerning lawful activity. Prevention of competitive escalation in alcohol strength is a substantial regulatory interest; the operative question is whether this suppression materially advances that interest in the demonstrated scheme. No categorical vice exception, least-restrictive-means demand, prohibition of incremental regulation, or invariable empirical-study condition enters.

**Sustaining path:** The historical experience of strength promotion and current malt-liquor advertising support a reasonable prediction that percentage comparisons will intensify competition. Current preferences may themselves reflect the long-standing restriction. Government need not wait for the predicted harm or regulate every beverage and State identically. Its strongest support comes from Fox's reasonable fit, the actual Edge allowance for effective incremental state-based regulation, and the evidence described in the lower opinion at appendix pp. 6a–8a.

The strongest objection is the missing connection between the selected truthful numerical ban and a material reduction of strength competition in the actual regulatory system. Its treatment of advertising, state-required labels, other beverage labels, and permitted low-alcohol information requires an explanation; the mere existence of an interest and some strength promotion is not that explanation. A court should not assume that the ability to compare accurate percentages means increasing the underlying strength.

**Invalidating path selected below:** The Government has not demonstrated that the challenged numerical label prohibition materially advances its strength-war interest in this scheme. The problem is not just incomplete coverage: the allowance of alcohol-content advertising under the agency's state-law construction, and the scheme's own use of alcohol information for other categories and lower-strength products, undermine the claimed practical connection without an adequate record explanation. The lower court's record discussion distinguishes promotion using strength-descriptive terms from this challenge to truthful percentages. The holding should not adopt every phrase suggesting that predictive judgment is inherently insufficient or that controlled studies are always required.

The strongest counterargument is that exceptions and distinct markets do not by themselves disprove efficacy, and label suppression may reduce a portion of the stimulus even if advertising remains. The answer is record-specific: plausible distinctions must still show material advancement under Edenfield and actual Edge. This record demonstrates the Government's concern but does not substantiate the selected means sufficiently. Alternatives such as direct strength regulation and focused promotional controls may bear on fit; the selected sufficient ground does not require adjudicating their comparative adequacy as an independently sufficient fourth-prong holding.

## 52. California Department of Corrections v. Morales, No. 93-1462

### Statutory rule, record, and entering law

The offense-date rule required: “The board shall hear each case annually thereafter.” The challenged 1981 version of California Penal Code §3041.5(b)(2) provided:

> The board shall hear each case annually thereafter, except the board may schedule the next hearing no later than three years after any hearing at which parole is denied if the prisoner has been convicted, in the same or different proceedings, of more than one offense which involves the taking of a life, and the board finds that it is not reasonable to expect that parole would be granted at a hearing during the following years and states the bases for the finding.

Annual consideration remains the statutory default. The exception permits, rather than requires, postponement. It requires both the multiple-life-taking-offense condition and an individualized prediction addressed to the intervening years, with stated bases. The next hearing must occur no later than three years after the denial; a shorter interval remains permitted. The statute does not itself establish ordinary interim reconsideration on changed circumstances. The later general two-year and later five-year regimes are not this enactment; their conditions cannot be imported into it.

Morales's 1980 offense preceded the amendment. His record includes two murder convictions, a failed parole release, the grave circumstances of the later offense, institutional-programming concerns, and psychiatric assessments. At the initial 1989 hearing the Board denied parole and separately deferred the next hearing for three years. Its postponement statement referred to the particularly cruel offense and the need for longer observation, the prior first-degree murder, a criminal history warranting longer assessment, and psychiatric evaluations requiring further observation, evaluation, or treatment. The statement is evidence of the Board's prediction, not a finding that release before the next hearing was legally impossible. His earliest eligibility date was August 2, 1990. The nineteen-year matrix figure is a guideline from which the regulations permit a justified departure, not a binding minimum foreclosing an earlier date. The claim is loss of consideration, not an established entitlement to release.

In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985), supplies authoritative state construction of the distinct postponement finding. Reasons must be separately directed to continuing unsuitability during the interval; the facts supporting them may overlap those supporting current unsuitability. Jackson invalidated the inadequate combined statement before it; it did not adjudicate Morales's separately stated reasons. Its own case involved the 1982 two-year interval. Its discussion and footnote addressing the 1981 multiple-offender provision are relevant without conflating the two enactments. Jackson's ordinary administrative/habeas review concerns the initial postponement decision. Its hypothetical discussion of changed circumstances does not establish an enforceable later reopening mechanism. Its federal ex post facto discussion must be evaluated under the subsequent controlling authorities, including Cavanaugh. Its reported aggregate denial rates do not measure this statutory class or Morales's likelihood of release. Its procedural termination after Jackson's death does not become an order for a live hearing in this case.

Actual Cavanaugh v. Roller (November 30, 1993) holds that a retrospective, unqualified replacement of mandatory annual consideration with a two-year interval increases punishment where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary interim review is established. A limited medical route conditioned on life expectancy of one year or less did not replace ordinary review. Neither unchanged initial eligibility and maximum punishment nor release discretion independently answers the loss of a binding meaningful opportunity. The prisoner need not prove an omitted hearing would have produced release. Cavanaugh reserved individualized postponement supported by findings, meaningful interim review, and other materially different mechanisms. Those reservations are not a holding that any one label automatically validates a later law, nor a command that every reserved mechanism must be present together.

Weaver protects the operation of mandatory good-conduct credits despite the absence of a vested release date; more demanding discretionary credits did not replace that lost entitlement. Miller concerns the increased effect of binding sentencing guidelines despite the same statutory maximum, including the removal of findings and review previously necessary for the longer sentence. Collins rejects a free-standing test of any procedural disadvantage, but does not immunize a procedural change that increases punishment in operation. These rules and Cavanaugh, not a bare vested-right theory or Jackson's earlier federal conclusion alone, govern.

## 53. United States v. Williams, No. 94-395

### Statutory components, record, and entering law

The lower judgment, 24 F.3d 1143 (9th Cir. 1994), reverses the district court's dismissal for lack of refund jurisdiction and remands. It is not a judgment awarding Williams a refund. Williams paid to remove federal tax liens asserted against the house she claimed as her property so a sale could close; the taxes had been assessed against her former husband, Rabin, and she was not personally liable for them. She filed an administrative refund claim within the applicable time and received a denial. The asserted coercion concerns the lien's effect on the sale and threatened consequences of failing to close. No levy is established. No substitute-fund agreement is established.

The relevant provisions must be read together and kept distinct:

- **28 U.S.C. §1346(a)(1)** grants concurrent district-court/Court of Federal Claims jurisdiction over any civil action against the United States to recover an internal-revenue tax alleged erroneously or illegally assessed or collected, a penalty allegedly collected without authority, or any sum alleged excessive or in any manner wrongfully collected under the internal-revenue laws. It is an express monetary refund waiver; the issue is its scope.
- **26 U.S.C. §7422(a)** requires a refund claim duly filed according to law and applicable regulations before suit. **Section 7422(b)** permits suit whether or not payment was under protest or duress. Duress is therefore not a universal added statutory prerequisite.
- **Section 6511(a)** prescribes the claim period: for a tax for which the taxpayer must file a return, three years from the return or two years from payment, whichever is later; if no return was filed, two years from payment. The separate stamp-tax clause provides its own three-year period. The contested question is whether “by the taxpayer” restricts claimant identity as the Government argues, not whether Williams missed a deadline.
- **Section 7701(a)(14)** defines taxpayer as “any person subject to any internal revenue tax,” within the Code's definition rule and its qualification for a distinct expression or manifest incompatibility with statutory intent. Whether that language includes a person subjected to collection through liens on claimed property, or only the legally liable taxpayer for this purpose, is disputed. A payment alone is not a complete answer.
- **Section 2410(a)(1)** permits specified quiet-title litigation over a federal lien. It does not automatically supply a postpayment refund remedy.
- **Section 7426(a)(1)** provides a wrongful-levy action for a person other than the assessed taxpayer with the required interest; this case establishes a lien and payment, not a levy.
- **Section 6325(b)(3)** permits the Secretary to discharge property when a sale agreement provides that proceeds are held as a substitute fund subject to the same lien priority. The Secretary “may” discharge under that mechanism; agreement and a fund are not established here. Sections **7426(a)(3) and (b)(4)** provide the corresponding action and monetary relief concerning an actual substitute fund. They are not a presently existing fund or a guaranteed discharge on these facts. Section **7426(c)** presumes the assessment valid for that statutory action; the same presumption cannot be silently appended to a §1346 action.

The Government's integrated reading of §§6511, 7422, and 7701 is substantial: if only the legally liable taxpayer can file the duly filed claim required for suit, §1346's broad subject-matter wording does not authorize this plaintiff. Its specialized-remedy argument also points to different limits and the conclusive-assessment provision. The Government does not identify an express exclusivity clause barring every other third-party remedy; its position is that no other waiver authorizes this claim. A court rejecting that reading must explain the connected provisions, not decide on sympathy for a remedy. Conversely, choosing that reading does not require a finding that Williams actually could obtain a substitute fund or had sufficient time for a useful quiet-title suit.

Actual Nordic Village (February 25, 1992) requires unequivocal consent for an affirmative monetary remedy. Plausible nonmonetary readings of the old Bankruptcy Code §106(c) precluded such a waiver; general jurisdiction, legislative history, or property labels did not fill the gap. It does not answer the claimant scope of this express tax-refund waiver. Actual United States v. California (April 26, 1993) declined to create a common-law refund right from federal reimbursement of state taxes and refused to revive the contractor's expired derivative claim. It reserved express congressional remedies. Neither precedent supplies a presumption that every Government interpretation, however disconnected from the statutory scheme, defeats an express waiver.

Dalm, 494 U.S. 596 (1990), requires reading §1346 with the duly filed claim and §6511 timing requirements; equitable recoupment does not independently create jurisdiction over an untimely refund suit. It did not decide this timely nonassessed-payer question. Its explanation of overpayment includes money exceeding what is properly due and does not depend on a later discovery date. Stevens's dissent supplies his own reasoning about the broad wrongfully-collected language and the pay-first structure, but does not repeal Dalm's binding claim requirement.

Stahmann, 305 U.S. 61 (1938), allowed producers who paid the tax assessed against a ginner to pursue recovery after paying to free their cotton. Its reasoning rejects treating those payments as volunteers' payments simply because assessment ran against another person. The Bankhead Act's intended incidence on producers and the collector-action posture distinguish it from this statutory waiver case. ### Question, statutory scope and remedy common to the rows

### Supported paths and operative proposition boundaries

**Sustaining path.** Congress could rationally treat school gun possession as a class producing a substantial national commercial effect through school disruption, workforce preparation and interstate travel, and select a prohibition as a means of addressing that class. Wickard and Perez permit examination of a class rather than the isolated instance; Heart of Atlanta, McClung and Hodel, as mapped in the validated packet, do not impose a universal formal-findings prerequisite. This path does not infer an unexpressed jurisdictional element. Its limiting premise is a rationally substantial relation of the identified class and a reasonable means, not that any undesirable local act is federal commerce. The strongest objection is that an education/productivity and crime-cost chain can connect almost anything to commerce, erasing an enumerated-power boundary. The sustaining rows resolve that objection through deference to the described class-wide effects under received doctrine; they do not announce that mere interstate history of every object is always enough.

**Invalidating path.** Congress's broad authority over economic activity and an interstate commercial scheme does not sustain this stand-alone possession offense on the Government's chain from guns to crime, education, productivity and national costs. The original provision supplies neither a commercial-activity limitation nor a case-specific commerce condition. On this record, that connection is too attenuated to support the charged offense. This conclusion rejects a compulsory findings rule, leaves Wickard/Perez and actual Allied-Bruce intact in their commercial settings, and does not revive traditional-governmental-function immunity. The strongest objection is that it adds an economic/non-economic boundary to precedents employing rational class-wide effects and discounts Congress's ability to address a national problem. ## Component Two ? culpability and fair notice

**Final rule and application.** Read original section 2119 to require knowing armed participation in the forcible taking, with intentional assistance in that taking for aiding-and-abetting liability. **Search path.** A targeted instrumental examination that converts otherwise unobservable heat patterns at a closed residence into information about concealed domestic activity invades the home's reasonable privacy even though the instrument receives emissions outside and does not image objects through walls. The strongest objection is that Karo ascertained an object's continuing presence inside, whereas this device measured only exterior temperatures and left officers to infer their cause. **No-search path.** The demonstrated examination reveals only comparative temperature at exterior surfaces, rather than the presence of an object or person inside; inference from that exposed physical condition is not itself acquisition of a concealed domestic fact. This ground must be confined to what this device actually disclosed. It cannot rest on a categorical right to fly, general abandonment of every emission, a supposed contraband-only capacity, or an asserted absence of all technological threats to home privacy.

The strongest objection is that separating exterior heat from its interior source can make the home's privacy depend on the mechanics of transmission—the problem Katz and Karo rejected. The no-search response is that the line rests on the content actually obtained, not simply the receiver's location: external relative warmth permits multiple causal explanations and does not itself disclose a concealed object, person, or particular intimate activity. More revealing surveillance may require a warrant. Both paths remain legally supportable; neither follows mechanically from the word “surface.”

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

### Proof, pumping evidence, and liability precision

**Path A:** Establish preponderance for enforcement because the States have already bargained over allocation and ordinary contract enforcement generally employs that standard. Its strongest support is the enforcement/modification distinction in actual Nebraska and Texas (1987)'s contract/enforcement account. Its strongest objection is that eliminating a fresh injury prerequisite does not allocate the risk of factual error about whether a sovereign breached the allocation; the United States expressly preserves that objection.

**Path B:** Require clear and convincing evidence for contested interstate violation facts because mistaken liability may invade a State's protected use. Colorado (1984) supplies a high-probability standard and the importance of allocating error risk, but its proponent sought a new diversion. The strongest objection is that requiring the downstream State to rejustify an existing entitlement can impair enforcement of the bargain and reward deficient upstream data. That objection prevents treating the new-allocation precedent as a complete answer.

**Selected narrower position for all eight:** Decide neither universal quantum. The pumping record is not a single small residual from one disputed model: Report pp. 254–255 compares approximately 395,000 acre-feet from the revised Kansas model with nearly 583,000 from Colorado's analysis for the same pumping input. These are comparisons of estimated gross Stateline depletion, not themselves findings of usable loss. Colorado's competing conclusions depend importantly on imports, ditch absorption and Plan benefits, which must be separated into actual physical offsets and an unsupported independent legal credit. The report's actual-operation pumping runs include the Plan; the record supports substantial usable loss even after actual import returns and the less generous historical usability approach. That combination supports high probability of material pumping violation without demanding an unerring forecast. It does not establish the exact amount of compensable loss, which remains for remedy proceedings. WWSP's distinct effect fails even the lesser burden for the reasons below. No evidentiary tie is being awarded to Kansas.

**Join barriers:** A draft stating that Nebraska already settled the universal persuasion quantum, that all source uncertainty is automatically resolved against Colorado, or that the identified gross model totals are usable loss would exceed every row. Minimum repair: confine the conclusion to material pumping liability under either proposed standard, identify physical offsets and usability, and reserve precise quantity and the general quantum. Stevens's objection to abandoning trial expertise does not remove the Court's independent duty; O'Connor's high-probability approach does not require certainty. Greenwich and Whitecotton reinforce distinctions among admissibility, persuasion, presumptions and defenses, but supply no APA or Vaccine Act burden here.

### Laches and pre-Compact use

**Laches Path A:** Reject the exception on absent unreasonable delay/causal prejudice. **Path B:** Protect pre-March 1985 monetary exposure because inquiry notice, missing records and reliance make delayed retrospective liability inequitable. Path B's strongest point is that the very missing evidence may reflect delayed assertion; its weakness is the missing causal link between earlier demand and different collection, preservation or development. Kansas's knowledge of wells is relevant, but cannot be equated with knowledge of the Compact injury amid the demonstrated other causes. Texas (1987) prevents good-faith uncertainty alone from becoming a complete defense to past performance. Illinois's general anti-laches statement does not resolve all equitable timing questions; actual Nebraska's acquiescence alternative confirms that specific interstate conduct can matter. All eight choose Path A and preserve remedy-stage equities rather than an absolute anti-laches rule.

**Pre-Compact Path A:** Protect established actual use and accept the reasonably supported negotiation-period 15,000 baseline. **Path B:** Preserve the full later decreed quantities or remand because the historical estimate lacks original calculation records and Colorado offers a dry-year reconstruction. The strongest Path B objection survives rejection of its legal-decree theory: publication does not validate an unreconstructable estimate. The answer on this record is comparative, not presumptive: the estimates near the relevant period, the highest negotiation-year selection, and the report's treatment of omitted well classes and climatic issues are preferable to reconstructed power coverage, assumed nonelectric usage and historic equipment efficiency. Article V-H's express ditch-right protection does not enact the same rule for later pumping. All eight accept Path A for the liability baseline while rejecting an exact annual cap or final damages figure. If a later remedy proposal depends on greater historical precision, it requires that separate evidentiary account; the exception is not converted into authorization to invent data.

**Join barriers:** No categorical laches prohibition; no presumption that missing records establish state misconduct; no assertion that five publications independently measured historical pumping; no equation of pumping with Stateline depletion. Each of those would change the adopted ground. The minimum repair is the record-specific causal/evidentiary account above.

### Plan, Trinidad, and the unexcepted approval question

**Plan Path A:** Enforce the separate bargain and deny an unagreed free-standing legal credit while measuring actual losses in the river as operated. **Path B:** Reduce liability by all practical extra water the Plan furnished because compensation should track actual injury, regardless of release intent. Path B is persuasive insofar as it identifies physical mitigation; it is not persuasive insofar as it converts benefits Kansas purchased with surrendered rights into satisfaction of a different obligation. All eight select Path A with physical mitigation expressly retained. A legal refusal of a general setoff cannot justify removing actual Plan operation or import returns from model comparisons.

**Trinidad Path A:** Treat VIII-B implementation as potentially binding within its scope, but hold that this record does not establish the asserted independent Compact obligation; reject IV-D liability measured only against perfect compliance with the Principles. **Path B:** Treat unanimous approval and mandatory wording as an independently enforceable exercise of compact administrative authority, and remand to identify breach and relief without requiring an additional depletion showing. Path B's strongest objection to A is that rules implementing a compact need not themselves be compact amendments. The answer is not a categorical amendment requirement: the shown cooperative project arrangements do not establish adoption of the claimed independent Compact command. Formal title alone is not decisive; source, adoption and scope must do the work. Texas (1983) respects action actually taken by authorized representatives within congressionally ratified powers, while denying judicial rewriting. Oklahoma (1991)'s context-sensitive majority also permits genuine interpretation; neither converts all project understandings into Compact law. All eight choose A, preserving independent contract/beneficiary theories and the unresolved Administration-duty action.

**Unexcepted prior approval:** The 1951 recommended policy and the 1944/1962 authorizations do not enact the asserted veto. The contrary route would need enacted approval language or a validly exercised delegated power with that effect; it has neither here. No second equitable ground is needed. Article IV-C flood/maintenance qualifications and IX's federal reservation/jointly approved John Martin exceptions retain their precise terms; no blanket federal immunity from Compact law is implied.

**Justice-specific construction difference:** Stevens and Souter's Oklahoma majority joins support examining genuine ambiguity and negotiating context. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy joined the Oklahoma dissent's refusal to replace clear terms with unexpressed allocation policy. Both approaches support the present bounded conclusions because Article VI expressly preserves Compact qualifications and VIII-B authorizes only consistent implementation; there is no adopted text giving the claimed decree entitlement, independent setoff or general veto. Ginsburg's actual Wolens contract opinion and Breyer's join supply an additional limited analogy: enforce a real undertaking without enlarging it through external policy. These sources do not prove a categorical rule against enforceable compact administration.

### WWSP, usability, and counterclaims

**WWSP Path A:** Reject Kansas's proof on the particular comparative models. **Path B:** Sustain Kansas's exception or obtain additional findings because paired-run error can cancel, some apparently offsetting gains were unusable flood water, and the opposing evaporation evidence has defects. Path B correctly defeats a blanket raw-error-versus-effect argument. It does not validate these particular runs: the Plan was switched off in both hypothetical scenarios despite actual operation, winter-irrigation evaporation error has no parallel in the storage run, and overpredicted storage shifts downstream releases into artificial late spills. Approximately 31,000 of the roughly 37,000 modeled accretion acre-feet cluster in May–June 1985; that does not establish that actual usable gains all occurred then. Nor does it prove their absence. The Master acknowledged flaws in the United States evaporation evidence; rejecting Kansas's proof does not adopt that competing calculation as demonstrated truth. All eight choose A. No new automatic burden shift, spoliation sanction or universal metering duty follows from asymmetrical data control. VIII-G and VIII-H retain their actual conditional data powers, mandatory investigation and discretionary reporting provisions.

**Usability Path A:** Accept Durbin with Larson's modifications for this record. **Path B:** Prefer Spronk's daily reconstruction or remand because averages understate losses in years of greatest need. Path B's concern is legally pertinent: usable availability cannot be reduced to average gross annual water. But unsupported daily precision cannot cure uncertain monthly depletion, and recharge attributable to additional Kansas pumping cannot simply become Colorado liability. All eight choose A at this stage, with the actual method's 72 percent summer, 25 percent winter and 9.9 percent pre-Compact recharge parameters, and monthly/seasonal ceilings (summer 40,000/140,000 and winter 7,500/40,000 acre-feet) preserved as features of this record rather than universal law. Spronk's Garden City reconstruction is not rejected as inherently defective; its 20 c.f.s. power-plant threshold concerns 1970–1973, with the discussed ordinary threshold 5 c.f.s. No future reliable daily method is foreclosed.

**Counterclaims:** Lake McKinney's physical pool does not itself show wrongful source, duration or retention of John Martin water; the short-throughflow function defeats an inference from pool size alone. Kansas pumping likewise does not itself demonstrate Colorado injury through extra calls, account effects or lost surplus. Their strongest contrary arguments require the missing tracing/attribution, not a different abstract proof label. ## Shared legal comparisons after the source refresh

**Precedent and special justification remain separate from first-instance construction.** Bramblett addressed §6 and the statutory history; it cannot be rejected on the factually incorrect assertion that it overlooked them. The exclusion path says its treatment was unsound and identifies what subsequent experience adds: judicial attempts to confine the broad construction, uncertain function boundaries, and the acknowledged enforcement guidance. The retention path says those developments reflect contestable lower-court exceptions, not failure of Bramblett's basic all-branch rule. The disagreement is substantive. Statutory stare decisis has force even when no private contract or property arrangement is identified, and criminal statutes are not categorically exempt from it.

**Reliance has more than one subject.** The recommendation weakens an inference that the Executive has built an indispensable, uniformly exercised enforcement program on using §1001 for litigation statements. It does not eliminate prosecutorial reliance everywhere, establish how often the advice was followed, or prove that Hubbard knew it. Lawful reliance of advocates on procedural rules differs from reliance on permission to lie; those rules themselves require honesty. Neither side gets congressional ratification from silence. The source limits prevent an empirical conclusion that practice has been uniform in either direction.

**Institutional chill is not answered simply by condemning lies.** Knowledge, falsity, and disclosure-duty limits reduce the prospect of convicting lawful advocacy. They do not make the costs of an allegation, investigation, or disputed classification disappear. Conversely, possible chilling cannot by itself defeat clear criminal coverage: perjury, obstruction, and contempt also affect litigation incentives. The legal inquiry is whether the proposed scope is supported by the enacted terms, the relevant prior construction, and their coherent operation—not whether false pleadings are socially desirable. The Government's executive-agency adjudication example must be answered on the entity definition or a defensible statutory distinction; a general claim that all adjudication must be exempt would reach beyond the present paths.

**The manual supplies no standalone defense.** All eight decline a ground that would vacate the convictions merely because charging allegedly conflicted with guidance. This record establishes no enforceable assurance to Hubbard, estoppel, selective-prosecution claim, or prejudicial breach of a procedural entitlement. The manual's descriptive significance does not transform it into law.

**Lenity cannot silently bypass Bramblett.** Those using residual lenity must first state the treatment of the precedent that otherwise supplies the broader construction, and then identify genuine uncertainty remaining after the ordinary tools. Neither competing briefs, circuit disagreement, nor a cautious prosecution policy automatically establishes ambiguity. The operative Ratzlaf and Granderson holdings supply a confined residual rule, not a universal least-punishment command. A decision rejecting one proposed judicial-function exception also does not settle whether a court is an included entity.
