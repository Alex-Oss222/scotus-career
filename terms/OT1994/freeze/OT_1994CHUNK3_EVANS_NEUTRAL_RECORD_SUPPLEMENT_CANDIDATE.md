## Balanced testimony and procedural details to validate

1. **Both halves of the chief clerk's testimony must travel together.** At Joint Appendix 37, asked about occasions when a warrant was quashed but the police were not notified during her eight years as chief clerk, she described rare occasions, estimated approximately one every three or four years in her particular court, and then said that the ensuing search of all files found three other instances on the same day, adding that the people were not all arrested. The latter answer cannot be omitted when discussing the former. The appendix's reproduction of the State's lower appellate brief, at printed 58, characterizes the check as other warrants quashed on the same day as Evans's for which the Sheriff's Office had not been notified. That characterization is a party's account, not an independent finding. The exact testimony should control over a broader inference about error frequency or the timing of each discovery.
2. **Ordinary notification procedure:** verify the chief clerk's testimony describing a telephone call to the Sheriff's Office warrant section when a warrant is quashed, removal of the computer entry by that office, and a notation in the court file identifying the caller and recipient. Verify the absence of such a notation in Evans's file. Read the surrounding testimony rather than treating the procedure as an adjudicated attribution of this error. Source leads: Appendix 27-37, especially the explanation surrounding 30.
3. **Sheriff's records:** verify the records clerk's testimony that no record of the relevant call was found. Source lead: Appendix 38-43, especially 42-43. Absence of a record does not by itself supply a trial finding assigning responsibility.
4. **Response after discovery:** verify the immediate correction of Evans's entry and the file search. Source leads: Appendix 30 and 37. Preserve the three additional missed notifications described in item 1.
5. **Trial judge's treatment:** verify the statements concerning the arresting officer's response to the computer entry, the possibility of negligence in either the Justice Court or Sheriff's Office, and the decision not to resolve which office caused the error. Source leads: Appendix 47-52. A comment that the officer was obliged to arrest is not a finding that the entire information system was reasonably maintained.
6. **Constitutional premise and litigation scope:** verify the State's stated premise concerning the invalid arrest in its merits brief, printed page 10, and the exact scope of its submitted court-employee argument. Do not infer that every category of police-generated or mixed error was presented simply because an amicus might discuss it. If the distinction matters, the separately filed United States amicus brief and its page 13 must be located and read in full; the IA item lists multiple amicus PDFs and this candidate does not identify their authors by guesswork.

## Limits and routing

This supplement does not decide how to weigh the evidence, whether the arresting officer's reliance was reasonable, who made the error, whether the record establishes isolated rather than recurring error, or what relief follows. Existing lower-court findings and the absence of an attribution finding remain separate from testimony and party submissions. A validator should read the full Joint Appendix and Petitioner's Brief, identify any material contrary or qualifying passage, and preserve it in the validated neutral supplement before renewed modeling.
