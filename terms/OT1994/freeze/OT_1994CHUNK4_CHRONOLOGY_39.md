## Material relevance and result

**No independent commitment changes.** Anderson adds no RFRA, Free Exercise, housing-discrimination, compelling-interest, or least-restrictive-means holding. Its AFDC unit-size and equity rules cannot displace RFRA's application-specific scrutiny or turn a social-welfare classification rule into a rule governing religious exemptions. Its refusal to make eligibility or available income depend on a label confirms a method already reflected in the Swanner commitments: commercial participation is not a categorical forfeiture of the statutory defense, and the actual legal requirements must be applied.

Anderson's decision of a fully presented alternative equitable-treatment ground deserves separate consideration. It shows that rejecting one lower-court rationale does not require a remand when the alternative legal ground can properly be resolved on the record. That proposition does not resolve the existing Swanner disagreement. Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer already consider the direct transactional and dignitary harm of these actual refusals and the absence of an alternative avoiding that same harm sufficient for affirmance. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas continue to regard the lower treatment of the application-specific statutory inquiry as inadequate, particularly its commercial-forfeiture formulation and least-restrictive-means transition. Their remand does not rest on a rule that every changed legal rationale compels new factfinding; it preserves a live disagreement over whether this record and explanation satisfy RFRA. None treats housing scarcity as the sole possible injury.

Anderson's bounded state discretion under Dandridge is conditioned by the governing federal welfare law. It supplies no discretion to relax RFRA's assigned burdens. Its §1983 enforceability assumption is not a decision on RFRA's enforcement channel, and its independent-construction/guidance holding provides no new deference rule for Alaska's judicial explanation. Its actual membership, income, opt-out, and proration qualifications have no direct factual application to the housing denials. The §601 family-care purpose analysis neither ranks marital-status protection nor determines the burden on Swanner's religious exercise.

The already-read Newport News result remains a statutory review entitlement holding about an agency's general administrative interest. Swanner is not the Director's attempted appeal, and the new slice establishes no change to his review route or a new RFRA standing obstacle. Ginsburg's Newport News caution about unnecessary purpose inferences is compatible with her existing insistence on concrete direct harm here. The Vaccine Table admission has no relevant application to this controversy.
