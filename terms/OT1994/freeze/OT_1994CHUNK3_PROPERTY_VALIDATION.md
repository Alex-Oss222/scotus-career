## Independent primary-source check

The entire [Ambassador Books, 20 F.3d 858](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/020/20.F3d.858.93-1509.html), paragraphs 1–61, including notes, was directly retrieved and read. No same-matter Supreme Court search or opinion was used. A local primary copy is retained at `tmp/chunk3_a_validation/ambassador.txt`.

Paragraph 19 reports an action to enjoin the ordinance alleging First and Fourteenth Amendment violations, inverse condemnation of the businesses, and a bill of attainder. Paragraph 21 reports the trial court's rejection of inverse condemnation on the ground that the three-year period was reasonable for nonconforming businesses to recoup their investment. Paragraph 17 supplies the extension qualification: a reasonable extension may be authorized when necessary to prevent undue financial hardship. Paragraphs 54–57 expressly characterize the appellate contention as the claim that such ordinances can apply only prospectively and reject an absolute right to remain at the same location under the First Amendment and substantive due process. Paragraph 61 affirms dismissal of the complaint and denial of preliminary relief.

The record thus contains an inverse-condemnation allegation and a trial rejection, followed by affirmance of the judgment, but the appellate opinion does not separately perform a Penn Central or Lucas compensation analysis. It neither says an independent compensation theory was waived nor establishes its full preservation and requested compensation remedy. It identifies no state compensation procedure, invocation of that procedure, parcel denominator, ownership/leasehold valuation, net relocation loss, compensation amount, or total loss of all beneficial use.

**Critical factual distinction:** The trial court's statement is a judgment that three years is a reasonable period in which these businesses can be expected to recoup investment. It is not a reported accounting finding that either business actually recovered every dollar, suffered no remaining economic loss, or received just compensation. The actual recoupment-related record consists of the period, its hardship-extension provision, continued operation during the period, and the reported reasonableness assessment. Do not convert those features into a universal three-year safe harbor or proof of full recoupment. Similarly, 97 relocation sites and United Arcade's conditional-use permit concern relocation opportunities; they do not value a property interest or automatically resolve a taking.

## Actual entering law

The canonical [property and economic-rights Holdings](../../../state/holdings/property-and-economic-rights.md) and [Standards and Tests](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md) were independently read for these propositions:

- **Lucas**, June 29, 1992: removal of all economically beneficial use of the relevant property requires compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the use. The owner must establish complete deprivation; the State must identify and substantiate the applicable background limitation. A newly enacted harm declaration is insufficient. The exact register title is **Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use**, present operation effective Lucas, 1992-06-29. No property denominator, valuation, or entitlement to anticipated profits is supplied by the rule. Penn Central remains for partial deprivation, and valid regulatory purpose is distinct from the compensation consequence.
- **Concrete Pipe**, June 14, 1993: economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations, and the character of governmental action are considered together. A substantial monetary burden alone does not establish a taking or a constitutional percentage ceiling. Its actual pension-withdrawal setting does not confer universal immunity on retrospective regulation.

These authorities allow evaluation of the claim that the very fact of requiring an established business to relocate necessarily creates a compensable taking. Neither already holds that every relocation requires compensation nor that three years of continued operation necessarily defeats compensation. Any selected rule must state its own trigger and preserve the distinct complete-deprivation and contextual paths. Euclid's protection against arbitrary zoning and the speech relocation analysis do not substitute for the compensation inquiry.
