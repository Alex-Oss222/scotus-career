## 81. Kimberlin v. Quinlan ? June 12, 1995

### Verified neutral addendum

The respondents' March1995 merits brief pp2?11 describes the separate November4, November7 and December22 episodes. Its pp33?35 press independent clearly-established-right, objective-reasonableness and personal-involvement defenses. The petitioner merits brief pp35?42 rejects the direct-evidence restriction, invokes ordinary Rules, but alternatively accepts a specific nonconclusory factual-allegation screen. ### Controlling entering premises

Mitchell permits interlocutory review of the separable legal immunity question. Elder's unanimous independent legal review uses all relevant contemporaneous authority but preserves the proper summary-judgment record, decides no ultimate immunity and supplies no mandatory merits-first sequencing. Leatherman rejects judge-created municipal pleading demands but explicitly reserves individual-immunity pleading; it supports an analogy only. Harlow's objective defense does not remove a motive element of the underlying claim. Siegert's majority did not establish the circuit's direct-evidence restriction: it found no constitutional injury; Kennedy's concurrence rejected the evidentiary distinction while demanding specific nonconclusory allegations. Stevens joined Marshall's dissent rejecting that distinction. The received Rules8/9(b)/26/56 and Holland, 348 U.S.121,140, do not equate circumstantial form with insufficiency. Prison press access remains governed by Pell/Turner; retaliation is distinct from an unrestricted right to a press conference.

### Two lawful paths and outcome

A: Decide the categorical legal question, vacate its use to award summary judgment, remand for ordinary Rule56 treatment, specific allegations and genuine immunity defenses. Strongest objection: this might evade immunity from burdens by requiring a factual trial merely because motive is pleaded. Answer: it orders neither trial nor unrestricted discovery; clearly-established law, personal responsibility, supported evidence and properly confined discovery remain independently required.

B: Affirm because the direct-evidence rule, or another lawful immunity ground, disposes of the First Amendment claims. Strongest objection: the evidentiary category has no connection to strength and Harlow cannot repeal a constitutional mental-state element. The categorical rule fails. A particular independent legal defense could remain sufficient on remand, but the packet and opposing episode-specific evidence do not justify this Court itself finding no personal participation or valid security purpose throughout. Dismissal of the entire appeal for factual insufficiency alone would also miss the separable pure legal rule. Thus the defensible disposition is limited vacatur and remand, not a jurisdiction-only GVR and not a liability finding.

## 82. Commissioner v. Schleier ? June 14, 1995

### Preflight and entering-law clarification

Schleier's recovered respondent merits brief pp1?3 reports settlement releasing federal ADEA, state age-discrimination and common-law wrongful-discharge claims, allocated equally between backpay and liquidated damages. This review concerns the supplied ADEA characterization; settlement wording alone does not establish separate personal-tort compensation. The Commissioner merits brief pp5?10 describes TaxCourt Downey treatment, the FifthCircuit's Purcell-based affirmance, and the competing causal ground. Its historical Burke broad majority interpretation is advocacy premised on different Court law and is not adopted as an entering holding.

The simulated Burke reversed the identified old-TitleVII payments but adopted no general remedial-range test, no physical/mental-health requirement and no discrimination-recovery exclusion. Four writers supported a restricted-remedy classification, Scalia a health-injury construction, Souter a narrow-exclusion rationale recognizing mixed tort/contract analogies; these are not logical subsets. O'Connor and Thomas opposed reversal. No judgment-only or concurrence proposition is converted into law. HazenPaper's unanimous willfulness rule requires knowledge or reckless disregard, not direct proof or additional outrageousness. McKennon distinguishes compensation and equitable employment remedies from liability but does not decide tax exclusion. Current ?104(a)(2), not the later physical-injury amendment, governs.

### Adversarial test

A: Reverse exclusion of both components. Backpay replaces earnings lost through termination, not a separate injury-specific loss; liquidated damages are triggered by willfulness and measured by backpay, not the actual personal harm. Both the underlying tort-type basis required by regulation and the statute's particular causal link must be satisfied; either one alone is insufficient. An ADEA legal injury may have dignitary consequences without these components compensating those consequences. Strongest objection: wage loss is routinely recoverable on account of a tort, and a fixed multiplier can compensate hard-to-measure harm. Answer: wage loss caused by a separately demonstrated qualifying personal injury is not excluded categorically; the ADEA provides these wage amounts for the employment deprivation itself, while Thurston's punitive/willfulness function makes the multiplier depend on the wrongdoer rather than the extent of injury. No general punitive-damages tax rule is necessary.

B: Affirm both exclusions because age discrimination invades a personal statutory right, the damages directly respond to that tort-type injury, and liquidated damages/jury trial strengthen the tort analogy absent in old TitleVII. Strongest objection: this reads ?on account of personal injuries? as satisfied merely by a discriminatory event, eliminating the independent statutory causal inquiry. Answer available to this path: lost-income damages in ordinary dignitary tort cases are not taxed merely because measured by wages; statute and regulation do not require a separate emotional-loss award. This is the substantial dissenting position; it remains strongest for O'Connor's broad-right approach and Justices with no contrary prior simulated tax commitment.

| O'Connor | Affirm both exclusions; discrimination invades a personal right and damages measured by earnings remain on account of that injury. | Burke dissent position and authored HazenPaper reject an unwarranted extra remedial condition. Refuses a separate-health-harm requirement. |

## 83. Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis ? June 14, 1995

### Neutral clarification and two paths

The respondent merits brief pp2?9 identifies competing shipboard-time estimates of 72% and about10%, not a finding selecting either. It locates the drydock work after the May1989 injury; later work may illuminate an ongoing assignment but cannot retroactively create a relationship absent at injury. Petitioner pp20?31 advances a permanent-assignment or substantial-work/exposure-to-marine-perils rule; respondent pp10?13 alternatively requests status as a matter of law or the preserved drydock-based new trial. Their wider rivalry cannot erase Rule51 preservation. Simulated Gizoni unanimously preserves a substantial employment-related vessel connection, rejects categorical occupation/benefits exclusions and leaves status factfinding open. Wilander requires mission contribution and employment-related vessel connection, not navigation duties. Senko and Butler reject mechanical immobility assumptions.

A: Affirm new trial on preserved drydock exclusion. Define statutory connection as substantial in duration and nature, sufficient to distinguish ordinary land-based/incidental work from service as a vessel's crew; temporary repair alone does not withdraw navigation status, but substantial reconstruction can. The actual record describes a $65 million conversion, removal of bottom plates and propeller, engine overhauls, rebuilt boilers and accommodation reconstruction. Whether Galileo was withdrawn must therefore be assessed on that substantial record, not as an ordinary temporary-repair case. The preserved challenge also asks whether later drydock work can evidence an ongoing relationship even if the vessel was then withdrawn; it does not automatically count every drydock hour as qualifying service. Navigation and connection are distinct questions. The mission stipulation settles only contribution. Strongest counterargument: six months of major renovation shows withdrawal and the plaintiff did not object to the broader charge. Answer: preserve that objection limit and reject only the categorical finding without conclusive factual basis; testimony and the continuing assignment leave a lawful factual question, and excluding six months could materially affect the status-only verdict.

B: Reverse and reinstate verdict because navigation withdrawal is legally conclusive or any error is harmless. Strongest counterargument: treating every drydock as withdrawal contradicts Senko/Butler and resolves disputed employment evidence. The navigation finding may be made as matter of law on conclusive facts; the actual scale, structural removal and six-month rebuilding provide a substantial lawful basis for this path, distinct from a rule about any vessel temporarily in drydock. Kearse's dissent expressly treated this conversion as unquestionably land-based and refused to find plain error in the unpreserved broader charge. The two paths remain a real judgment disagreement, not a hypothetical wholly foreclosed alternative. The Court should not itself decide that the doctor was negligent or that the seaman relationship necessarily existed. A per-voyage automatic-status theory fails to preserve the distinction between crew and a land-based worker temporarily at sea. No percentage cutoff is necessary or supported as controlling by the supplied entering law.

## 84. Witte v. United States ? June 14, 1995

### Predecision source and entering-law boundary

The UnitedStates merits brief pp1?7 confirms the 1991 attempted-marijuana-possession conviction, the sentencing court's aggregation of cocaine and marijuana conduct, Guidelines range292?365 months, substantial-assistance departure to144 months, and later cocaine-conspiracy/attempt indictment relating to1989?90. Both sides disputed the relevance aggregation at sentencing. The initial appeal was dismissed for failure to file a brief. The sentencing court's different same-course assessment is not rewritten as a party concession. The Government's brief pp10?12 relies on historical Dixon overruling; that portion is excluded from operative law. Simulated Dixon expressly retains Grady, Felix separates evidentiary use from prosecuting an offense, and KurthRanch requires careful inquiry into actual punishment rather than a regulatory label. Current Harris permits clearly legislated cumulative punishment in one prosecution only; it does not authorize all successive proceedings.

A: Affirm the FifthCircuit and permit trial. Sentencing information used to select imprisonment within the legally authorized marijuana range does not itself prosecute/convict/punish the separate cocaine offenses; it aggravates punishment for the marijuana offense of conviction. Thus neither Blockburger nor retained Grady is reached as an identity bar without an earlier prosecution of that cocaine offense. Williams v.Oklahoma,358U.S.576,584?586, and McMillan,477U.S.79, support the sentencing/legal-range distinction. Strongest objection: mandatory Guidelines aggregation caused a real and large imprisonment increase, not a discretionary incidental mention. Answer: its real sentencing effect is acknowledged, but legal authorization and the prior judgment's subject distinguish aggravating facts from separate offense punishment; no sham label or out-of-range punishment is validated. Guidelines ?5G1.3 coordination remains conditional, not an automatic constitutional cure.

B: Reverse and reinstate indictment dismissal to extent the prior cocaine-based increase was already punishment for those offenses. Strongest objection: it transforms all aggravation into prior adjudication and undermines ordinary sentencing practices without a prior cocaine conviction. Answer for this path: unlike broad character evidence, mandatory drug-quantity aggregation assigns calculable imprisonment to the identical criminal conduct, making substantive punishment analysis necessary under KurthRanch and Halper. The divergent paths turn on characterization of the prior judgment, not Dixon's historical elimination of Grady.
