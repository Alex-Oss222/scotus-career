## 54. United States v. Lopez

### Common scope and the two supported paths

The charged enactment is original 18 U.S.C. §922(q)(1)(A), not a later commerce-element statute. It prohibits knowing firearm possession at a place known, or reasonably believed, to be a school zone; the zone includes elementary/secondary school grounds and the area within 1,000 feet. The indictment alleges no commerce nexus. The neutral record's unloaded handgun, five bullets, reported proposed $40 delivery, and stipulated testimony about out-of-state manufacture do not add a statutory element or a grand-jury allegation. The Fifth Circuit's judgment directed dismissal, and the neutral record reports the ensuing dismissal.

The original possession exceptions remain part of the statute's reach: private property outside school grounds; the specified state/local license with prior law-enforcement qualification verification; an unloaded firearm in a locked container or locked vehicle rack; a school-approved program; a school contract covering the individual or employer; official law-enforcement possession; and an unloaded crossing to hunting land with school permission. The lower court found none applicable. The distinct discharge prohibition, its culpability/attempt terms, and its four exceptions—private property outside school grounds, a school-approved program, the school-contract situation, and official law enforcement—are not adjudicated. State/local school-zone enactments remain preserved by the original nonpreemption provision.

**Bounded invalidation position.** The stand-alone original possession prohibition is not sustained by the proffered chain from gun possession through crime costs or school disruption to national productivity. Neither its regulated class nor its enacted elements supplies the commercial connection that makes the aggregation precedents applicable in their settings. The absence of a case-specific nexus is relevant but not a universal requirement for every commerce statute. This position preserves Congress's broad authority over economic activity and comprehensive interstate commercial regulation, including appropriate regulation of intrastate activity essential to such a scheme; this original provision is not sustained as such an essential component. It rejects a universal formal-findings requirement and declines to overrule Wickard or Perez, revive traditional-governmental-function immunity, or decide every noncommercial federal offense. It would affirm the judgment directing dismissal of this charge.

**Bounded sustaining position.** Congress could rationally find that the class of school gun possession substantially affects national commerce through its effect on education, workforce preparation and travel, and could reasonably address that class by the original prohibition. Wickard and Perez permit assessment of class effects without treating this individual's conduct as independently substantial. The position preserves judicial review, the need for a substantial class relationship and reasonable means, and the absence of a universal formal-findings prerequisite. It does not confer commerce power merely because safe schools are desirable or because this object once crossed a state line. It would reverse the constitutional dismissal and remand for the unresolved sentencing objections, rather than validate the entire conviction and sentence without further review.

**Construction and remedy.** No associate presently adopts a new statutory commerce element. Bass and Gregory operate through an available construction; actual X-Citement interprets an existing knowledge term and does not authorize inserting any useful saving condition. If a permissible nexus construction is later proposed, the lower court's independently renewed Stirone/Russell indictment objection must be addressed: stipulated trial proof is not itself a substitute for a necessary grand-jury allegation. Under the sustaining no-element construction, that conditional objection supplies no independent defense. Under the selected constitutional-invalidation path, it need not be reached. The original §924(a)(4) authorized at most five years, a fine not exceeding $5,000, or both, prohibited concurrency with imprisonment under another provision, and deemed the offense a misdemeanor for other-law purposes except for its authorized maximum. The unresolved two-year supervised-release and special-assessment claims are therefore remand matters on the sustaining path and unnecessary on the dismissal path. No later general-fine wording is applied backward. The September 1994 findings are neither a new nexus allegation nor an automatic retroactive cure; reliance on their enactment as effective noncase law requires the operator's admission.

## 55. Travelers / Pataki / Hospital Association

### Common rule and the material scope revision

For the reviewed commercial-insurance and HMO applications, all eight associates support reversing the ERISA-preemption ruling and remanding for conforming relief. The 13% hospital-retained differential, additional 11% commercial-insurer surcharge, and conditional HMO assessment up to 9% must be addressed separately. They operate through payor/provider prices and HMO participation incentives without depending on whether the ultimate coverage was purchased by an ERISA plan or using covered-plan benefit content as a mandatory legal measure. On this record, their economic influence does not dictate a covered plan's benefits, insurer choice or uniform administration. The statute's broad connection/reference language is read in light of the plan relationships and uniform administration ERISA governs; it does not promise uniform market costs.

The no-preemption position is not confined to statutes that expressly name ERISA, and it does not establish categorical immunity for state health laws, price regulation, general laws, or indirect burdens. An indirect financial effect can be sufficiently acute to operate as a substantive coverage command or effectively restrict insurer choice; the supplied record does not establish such compulsion for the reviewed applications. No numerical safe harbor, burden-changing universal test, finding about actual premium reductions, or fact that plans switched insurers is adopted. The State's policy justification is an argument about the law's operation, not a judicial finding that every surcharge remains economically necessary.
