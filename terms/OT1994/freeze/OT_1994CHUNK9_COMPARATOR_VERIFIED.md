## Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette, No. 94-780

The official report is 515 U.S. 753 (1995), argued April 26, 1995, decided June 29, 1995. The Sixth Circuit, 30 F.3d 675, was affirmed, 7–2. Parts I–III preserve the presented Establishment Clause question, recognize protection for private religious expression and public-forum content restrictions, and apply Widmar and Lamb's Chapel to neutral access without state sponsorship. Part IV states a categorical protection for purely private religious speech in a traditional or designated public forum publicly announced and open to all on equal terms; it excludes preferential access and government manipulation. O'Connor and Souter reject a categorical public-forum exemption from the contextual endorsement inquiry. O'Connor emphasizes the informed observer, forum history and an adequate disclaimer. Souter emphasizes sufficiently clear disclaimer or a marked private-display area as alternatives to blanket denial. Stevens would allow the State to keep unattended religious monuments off the statehouse grounds and rejects the suggested observer assumptions. Ginsburg objects to the absence of a disclaimer requirement in the injunction rather than treating all identified private religious displays as impermissible.
