## 1. United States v. Shabani

### Verified lower judgment and unresolved appeal

[United States v. Shabani, 993 F.2d 1419 (9th Cir. May 17, 1993)](https://static.case.law/f2d/993/cases/1419-01.json), local `shabani_lower.json`, majority and Wallace concurrence, reviewed in full.

The indictment charged one §846 conspiracy count and omitted an overt-act allegation. Shabani unsuccessfully moved to dismiss the indictment, then requested an overt-act jury instruction; the district court refused it. The prosecution alleged he supplied cocaine transported from California to Alaska. Its cooperating witnesses included his girlfriend and her relatives. His defense disputed their credibility and the lack of direct evidence connecting him to sales. The sentence was 160 months plus 60 months' supervised release unless deported.

The Ninth Circuit held the indictment sufficient under Tavelman but considered its circuit precedent to require proof of an overt act at trial. It expressly acknowledged the textual and other-circuit arguments against that requirement; Wallace concurred while criticizing the inconsistency. The trial proof included testimony that Shabani drove a truck to accept deliveries, but the appellate court declined to find the instructional omission harmless because the single-count verdict did not necessarily resolve an overt act. Its disposition was reversal and remand. It expressly did **not** reach evidentiary sufficiency or other appellate issues. Those unresolved matters are an operative remedial fact; the source does not establish that eliminating the overt-act issue itself resolves them.

## 2. U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership

### Verified lower judgment and the limited live posture

[In re Bonner Mall Partnership, 2 F.3d 899 (9th Cir. Aug. 4, 1993)](https://static.case.law/f3d/2/cases/0899-01.json), local `bonner_lower.json`. The record/procedure, issue limits and disposition were reviewed; the extended bankruptcy interpretation and valuation discussion are not independently validated for deciding bankruptcy merits.

Bonner filed Chapter 11 on March 13, 1991, staying a trustee sale. The bankruptcy court ultimately granted Bancorp relief from the stay on the legal premise that the new-value exception did not survive the Bankruptcy Code. The district court reversed and remanded, leaving confirmation to the bankruptcy court. The Ninth Circuit decided the survival question, accepted appellate jurisdiction under §158(d), and left the separate question whether the particular plan was confirmable to the bankruptcy court. Thus the judgment sought to be erased concerns a legal bankruptcy question; it is not a lower decision on the later vacatur request.

The scoped neutral packet supplies the subsequent consensual settlement, mootness and vacatur motion. This digest adds no unverified settlement term, condition, allocation of risk or exceptional factual circumstance. The original merits cannot be resolved merely because the vacatur issue remains available.

### Authorities actually reviewed

- [United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36 (1950)](https://static.case.law/us/340/cases/0036-01.json), local `munsingwear.json`, read in full. The Government failed to request vacatur when its injunction appeal became moot and later unsuccessfully resisted preclusion in related damages actions. The opinion describes vacatur/remand/dismissal as the practice for federal civil judgments whose review becomes impossible through happenstance, cites §2106's supervisory power, and insists that the litigant use the available procedure. The actual disposition affirms application of preclusion. Its context must accompany any statement of the usual practice.
- [Karcher v. May, 484 U.S. 72 (1987)](https://static.case.law/us/484/cases/0072-01.json), local `karcher.json`, substantive opinion and White concurrence reviewed. **O'Connor** wrote; **Stevens and Scalia** joined. Departing legislative officers could not pursue the legislature's appeal personally; their successors' choice not to appeal meant the controversy ended by the losing party's choice rather than circumstances unattributable to any party. The Court declined Munsingwear vacatur and dismissed for want of jurisdiction. The successor-substitution and party-status facts are part of the holding's setting.
- [Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc., 321 U.S. 671 (1944)](https://static.case.law/us/321/cases/0671-01.json), local `walling.json`, read in full. Corporate dissolution prevented completion of review but did not establish that the district injunction had become moot against every person potentially bound. The Court described residual supervisory power to dispose of the case as justice required, vacated the appellate judgment, and restored the district judgment. That is an example of a tailored disposition, not automatic erasure of every lower judgment.
- Current **Izumi**, OT1993 chunk 1: its controlling grounds are party status and prudential question presentation. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg joined those threshold grounds, which leave settlement vacatur undecided. **Stevens's public dissent**, joined by Blackmun, would reject routine settlement vacatur and require an actual equitable assessment accounting for public and third-party interests, without categorically forbidding vacatur.
- [In re Allied-Signal Inc., 891 F.2d 974 (1st Cir. 1989)](https://static.case.law/f2d/891/cases/0974-01.json), local `allied_signal.json`, read in full. **Breyer** wrote that retrospective relief for an assumed appearance-of-partiality problem should be tailored under Liljeberg, considering investment in proceedings, impending settlements, the limited role of the affected counsel and public confidence. Settlement removed the prospective appearance issue; a mistrial was denied. This is a limited analogy concerning equitable retrospective relief, not Breyer's established answer to settlement-mootness vacatur.

## 3. Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp.

### Verified lower record and material qualifications

[Hess v. PATH, 809 F. Supp. 1172 (D.N.J. Dec. 21, 1992)](https://static.case.law/f-supp/809/cases/1172-01.json), local `hess_district.json`: Lechner's opinion; material facts, immunity/waiver reasoning, limitations reasoning and conclusion reviewed. This is the **Hess** action, not a substitute for the separate Walsh district record.

Hess alleged a train-window injury to his hand on June 29, 1987 and filed May 31, 1990. He sued under FELA and the Boiler Inspection Act. PATH invoked a one-year condition attached to the States' consent to suit. The district court treated PATH as immune under binding Third Circuit law; it expressly recorded Hess's failure to contest that status there. It rejected his separate contentions that Feeney waived immunity entirely, that FELA/Hilton removed the need for consent in federal court, and that FELA's three-year period displaced the consent condition. It dismissed for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.

The source's observation that status was not contested below is a fact for the fresh context to reconcile with the supplied granted arm-of-State question and the legal predicate of the judgment. It is **not** a conclusion here that review is forfeited or that the matter is blocked. The supplied packet and table appellate disposition establish a consolidated Hess/Walsh posture, but no separate detailed Walsh factual finding is inferred from the Hess opinion.

[Port Authority Police Benevolent Ass'n v. Port Authority, 819 F.2d 413 (3d Cir. 1987)](https://static.case.law/f2d/819/cases/0413-01.json), local `pba.json`, reviewed in full. This predecessor supplies the Third Circuit's status rationale: mixed statutory labels; state judicial characterization; financial structure; governor appointments and vetoes; state-level transportation function; legislative restrictions; tax exemption and sue-and-be-sued qualifications. It describes a self-supporting reserve fund, yet interprets Article XV as permitting a theoretical state-treasury impact from a sufficiently large adverse judgment.

**Article XV's complete material financial qualification, as quoted in PBA:** while operating revenues are inadequate for all expenditures, the legislatures must appropriate equally for salaries, office and other administrative expenses the sums recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, with each State obligated **only up to $100,000 in any one year**. PBA's broader inference of financial support is a judicial interpretation; it is not the same thing as a statutory command to pay all judgments. Do not omit the administrative-purpose, recommendation/approval, equality or annual-cap terms when using that provision.

### Prior Supreme Court and current authorities

- [Lake Country Estates v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, 440 U.S. 391 (1979)](https://static.case.law/us/440/cases/0391-01.json), local `lake_country.json`. **Stevens** wrote. The entity-status portion rejects automatic immunity merely from a shared slice of state power. It considers compact structure, state intent and congressional concurrence, local appointments/funding, nonbinding state obligations, local function and absence of state veto. It does not say every compact entity is immune or every compact entity is nonimmune. Material status and jurisdiction passages reviewed; its separate legislative-immunity issue is not supplied as a present Hess rule.
- [PATH v. Feeney, 495 U.S. 299 (1990)](https://static.case.law/us/495/cases/0299-01.json), local `feeney.json`. **O'Connor's** Part II, joined by **Scalia and Kennedy**, assumes without deciding arm status and finds sufficiently express federal-forum consent by reading the general consent provision with the venue provision referring to United States judicial districts. Its facts and majority were reviewed; concurrence's entity-status portions were reviewed. **Stevens joined Brennan's concurrence**, which independently denies PATH arm status and treats its financial independence as material. The concurrence distinguishes the arm theory, the real-party-in-interest/treasury theory and a possible congressionally supplied statutory defense. Its categorical interstate-entity reasoning is not the Court's holding.
- Current **Hilton**, OT1991 chunk 3: **Kennedy, Stevens and Souter** support retained FELA state-court coverage; **O'Connor and Scalia** dissent on clear-statement/statutory-stare-decisis grounds. The controlling holding preserves Welch's displacement of constructive federal-forum waiver and does not determine PATH's entity status.
- Current **Puerto Rico Aqueduct**, OT1992 chunk 2: **O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas** join immunity-from-suit/collateral-order reasoning, explicitly leaving arm status, waiver and abrogation unresolved. **Stevens** publicly dissents on finality/forum grounds. An immediate-appeal position alone is not a vote on PATH status.

## 4. United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.

### Verified lower record, statute and competing lower rationales

[United States v. X-Citement Video, 982 F.2d 1285 (9th Cir. Dec. 16, 1992)](https://static.case.law/f2d/982/cases/1285-01.json), local `xcitement_lower.json`. Facts, majority scienter reasoning, disposition, operative statutory quotation and Kozinski's alternative were reviewed. The complete judicial text is retained, including other facial challenges and footnotes.

Gottesman and the corporation were prosecuted after 1986-1987 undercover transactions involving tapes featuring Traci Lords. The officer said he wanted tapes made while she was under eighteen. The described transactions were 49 tapes delivered directly and eight sent to Hawaii on the officer's instructions. The judgment followed a **bench trial**, not a jury instruction. The opinion reports conviction for shipping, distributing and conspiring; Gottesman received twelve months and a $100,000 fine. After initial appeal the case was returned for reconsideration in light of Ninth Circuit Thomas, and the district court again upheld the Act.

Canby's majority considered Thomas binding on the statutory reading: knowingly modified the handling acts, not the performer's minority. It rejected §2256 vagueness/overbreadth attacks, but concluded §2252 lacked constitutionally required knowledge of minority and reversed on facial grounds. It therefore did **not** decide the as-applied contention that the tapes were not child pornography, or the indictment scienter objection. Its opinion distinguishes knowledge of a fact (minority) from knowledge of legal classification/illegality. The Government's precise oral concession is described in the lower opinion, and should be attributed to that setting rather than silently carried into Supreme Court briefing.

Kozinski agreed reversal was needed under the strict-liability trial theory but would construe a recklessness requirement and remand for retrial rather than invalidate the Act. His source is Osborne's acceptance of Ohio's independently supplied recklessness element. The lower panel thus offers two different judgment/rationale/remedy routes; merely rejecting facial invalidity does not establish an actual trial finding of the knowledge ultimately required.

Section 2252(a)(1) and (a)(2) are separately quoted in the source. Each includes an (A) production/use-of-minor-engaged-in-sexually-explicit-conduct condition and an independent (B) depiction-of-that-conduct condition. Subsection (a)(2) includes receiving/distributing with its commerce/mail nexus and separately knowingly reproducing for distribution. Keep those elements, performer minority, sexual character, handling act and interstate nexus distinct. Section 2256 defines minor as a person under eighteen and separately defines sexually explicit conduct. The source quotes the statutory version it applies; no later wording or penalty amendment is imported.

### Justice-specific authorities and current doctrine

- Current **Staples**, Holdings criminal-procedure and OT1993 chunk 5: **Thomas**, joined by **Scalia, Kennedy and Souter**, adopts the contextual factual-knowledge requirement for characteristics making the rifle a machinegun. **Ginsburg**, joined by **O'Connor**, concurs on the same narrow knowledge requirement. **Stevens** dissents. The surviving distinction is factual knowledge versus knowledge of nonregistration or illegality; the public-welfare analysis is offense-specific.
- Current **Ratzlaf**, OT1993 chunk 1: **Ginsburg**, joined by **Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter**, adopts the express-willfulness construction and independent residual-lenity alternative. **O'Connor and Thomas** join Blackmun's dissent, which still requires actual reporting knowledge and deliberate evasion rather than strict liability. This is not a universal legal-knowledge rule.
- Current **Posters 'N' Things**, OT1993 chunk 5: **Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg** join the actual factual-knowledge rule; **Scalia**, joined by **Kennedy and Thomas**, agrees with the sufficient likely-use/knowledge ground while retaining an additional seller-intent route. The difference concerns statutory coverage, not elimination of knowledge.
- [United States v. Aversa, 984 F.2d 493 (1st Cir. Jan. 13, 1993)](https://static.case.law/f2d/984/cases/0493-01.json), local `aversa.json`: **Breyer's concurrence was read in full**. He emphasizes avoiding punishment of an innocent state of mind under technical criminal laws, regards tax/currency parallels as significant, and accepts the en banc knowing-or-reckless-duty construction as close enough to that concern. This is evidence of a pre-Court interpretive position; **current Ratzlaf governs the currency offense**, and the Aversa rule cannot displace it or establish §2252's element.
- [Osborne v. Ohio, 495 U.S. 103 (1990)](https://static.case.law/us/495/cases/0103-01.json), local `osborne.json`: the scienter, narrowing and related procedural passages were reviewed. **O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy** join White; **Stevens** joins Brennan's dissent. The majority relied on a separate Ohio default-culpability statute to supply recklessness and required the other narrowed elements to govern the conviction. Its setting does not itself supply a federal default statute. The dissent includes a distinct objection to treating the scienter-instruction claim as forfeited despite the overbreadth objection. No position on §2252 is attributed to any of these Justices from Osborne alone.
