### United States v. Shabani; No. 93-981; 513 U.S. 10 — 1994-11-01

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Shabani_merits_1994-11-01.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act

**Controlling proposition:** Section 846 of Title 21 requires neither an allegation nor proof of an overt act beyond the unlawful narcotics agreement. The Government must still prove the unlawful agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt; neither mere association nor awareness of another person’s plan is enough.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this construction and its application; no fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule for every conspiracy statute is announced. The Court does not decide credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, the remaining appellate objections or the ultimate validity of the conviction. Current double-jeopardy law is unchanged.

Effective November 1, 1994, §846 conspiracy requires the unlawful agreement and knowing participation, but no further overt act in allegation or proof. This adds an offense-specific statutory construction. It does not alter the knowledge requirements of other crimes, create a universal rule about omitted statutory language, or change Felix, Dixon, Grady or Ratzlaf beyond the distinctions stated above.

Precedent treatment and its full operative scope remain in the linked Record’s public Precedent Treatment block. No prior authority is altered beyond that stated treatment.

### U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership; No. 93-714; 513 U.S. 18 — 1994-11-08

Authority: [Record](../records/US_Bancorp_Mortgage_Co_v_Bonner_Mall_Partnership_mootness_vacatur_1994-11-08.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute.

#### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Controlling proposition:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

Effective November 8, 1994, §2106 continues to authorize a proper disposition after mootness, and voluntary settlement ordinarily weighs against vacatur absent a further sufficient equitable reason. The Court adds this settlement-vacatur rule to the prior mootness framework; Izumi had left it unresolved. Exceptional relief is reserved, without a heightened proof standard. Bankruptcy new-value doctrine, settlement enforcement, collateral-order review and the merits of the judgment below remain unchanged by this disposition.

Precedent treatment and its full operative scope remain in the linked Record’s public Precedent Treatment block. No prior authority is altered beyond that stated treatment.

### Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., including Walsh; No. 93-1197; 513 U.S. 30 — 1994-11-14

Authority: [Record](../records/Hess_v_Port_Authority_Trans_Hudson_Corp_merits_1994-11-14.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Controlling proposition:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

Effective November 14, 1994, PATH is not an arm entitled to the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employee actions. Lake Country’s compact inquiry is clarified through the actual separate legal and financial structure, with treasury exposure carrying great weight but no universal sole-treasury test. The Third Circuit’s contrary PATH-status rule cannot sustain these dismissals. FELA coverage, consent of an entity that actually is immune, immunity abrogation and collateral-order appealability remain separate and unchanged.

Precedent treatment and its full operative scope remain in the linked Record’s public Precedent Treatment block. No prior authority is altered beyond that stated treatment.

### United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.; No. 93-723; 513 U.S. 64 — 1994-11-29

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_X_Citement_Video_Inc_merits_1994-11-29.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character

**Controlling proposition:** To convict under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1) or (2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knew the performer was a minor, as well as the applicable knowing handling act. It need not prove knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition; the actual statutory production, depiction and commerce-or-mail conditions remain distinct, and the Court does not decide whether knowledge of the jurisdictional nexus is required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven Justices directly adopt the factual-knowledge construction and its reasoning. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a statutory construction of §§2252(a)(1)–(2), not a universal constitutional actual-knowledge minimum, a construction of every other offense or a decision concerning §2251 producers. No new digital-image category, knowledge-of-illegality element, knowledge-of-nexus rule or automatic trial-error remedy is supplied.

#### The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Respondents may defend the existing appellate reversal on the renewed age-eighteen and lascivious-wording grounds that appear in the record, although the court of appeals rejected those grounds, because they seek no enlargement of their rights or reduction of the Government’s rights under that judgment. The Court reaches those two alternatives; the separate actual-or-simulated bestiality and sadistic-or-masochistic-abuse objection is not established as renewed and is not decided.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this reach ruling. Stone-Zsela joins only the dispositions on the two alternatives and adds no reach ground. Scalia and Thomas accept the legal availability of such defenses but do not decide either alternative because their independent ground would sustain the appellate judgment.

#### The asserted age-eighteen line does not establish substantial facial overbreadth

**Controlling proposition:** The renewed objection that §2256 defines a minor as a person under eighteen, rather than under sixteen, does not establish substantial First Amendment overbreadth in §§2252(a)(1)–(2) as construed here. Difficulty estimating age near eighteen, together with decisions permitting adult treatment of some older adolescents in other settings, does not require facial invalidation of this protective category.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part III and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general constitutional age-of-majority rule is announced, and no as-applied challenge is foreclosed merely by this facial disposition.

#### The complete lascivious-exhibition category defeats the word-substitution facial attack

**Controlling proposition:** Replacing lewd with lascivious in §2256’s category of lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area does not itself make §§2252(a)(1)–(2) facially vague or substantially overbroad. The complete category retains a sexual-conduct limit and does not cover nudity merely because a person’s body is visible; whether these particular tapes satisfy the definition remains open.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part IV and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The other objection concerning actual or simulated bestiality and sadistic or masochistic abuse is not reached. No unofficial-code punctuation is adopted and no definition of every possible application is supplied.

Effective November 29, 1994, §§2252(a)(1)–(2) require the stated actual factual knowledge of sexual character and minority, without a general knowledge-of-illegality requirement. The Court rejects the two renewed facial-definition grounds on the limited six-Justice rationales stated above. The prior offense-specific holdings of Ratzlaf, Staples and Posters remain intact. The Ninth Circuit’s facial-scienter invalidation is set aside; no acquittal, count-specific affirmance, automatic harmlessness result or mandatory retrial follows. The unresolved indictment and actual-tape claims remain open, as does the independently unrenewed definition objection.

### Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, No. 93-1062. — 1994-12-05

#### RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA applies to the threatened future implementation of Clearwater's preexisting ordinance: the Church must establish a substantial burden on its religious exercise, after which Clearwater must produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The adverse prospective determinations concerning the identifying disclosures designated below as section 100.03(1)(a), (b), (c), and (j), and the written-refund-policy requirement in section 100.05(1)(g), must therefore be vacated for that inquiry, with ordinary Article III standing and appropriate-relief limits preserved.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that RFRA governs, but does not join the opinion or its disposition of these adverse determinations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final substantial-burden or justification finding is made for the surviving identifying or written-policy requirements. The Court does not decide RFRA's constitutionality, retrospective monetary liability, a general exemption from civil fraud law, the disputed sectarian purpose, the precise Florida severability boundary, the unreviewed general anti-fraud clause, or unpreserved hardship-discretion and procedural objections. Section 7 leaves the Establishment Clause unaffected; section 6(c) supplies no authority to burden religious belief.

Effective December 5, 1994, RFRA's original burden-and-justification inquiry governs the specified prospective municipal enforcement claims despite the ordinance's earlier enactment. This adds a controlling statutory application and remand rule; it does not replace the constitutional free-exercise or establishment standards. The favorable lower relief and factual purpose/severability remand survive without adoption of the lower constitutional rationales. No final exemption or constitutional invalidity of the surviving provisions is established.

### Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund, No. 93-1151. — 1994-12-06

#### The Commission lacks independent certiorari authority for this FECA enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 437d(a)(6)'s authority to litigate and appeal civil enforcement actions through the Commission's general counsel does not displace 28 U.S.C. §518(a)'s allocation of Supreme Court representation in this FECA corporate-contribution action. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains available, and the express certiorari powers in 26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d) remain confined to actions in which the Commission appears under those respective funding sections.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule. Stone-Zsela and Stevens dissent; Ginsburg does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns this affirmative FECA enforcement petition. #### Authorization after the statutory petition period does not ratify this filing

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming that the Solicitor General's May 26, 1994 authorization fully adopted the Commission's January 18 petition, it cannot relate back to validate this unauthorized invocation after the period in which an authorized petition could be filed had expired. Section 2101(c) allows ninety days and permits a Justice, for good cause, to extend the application period by no more than sixty days; the asserted authorization falls outside even that maximum under either supplied judgment date.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six direct votes. Together with Part I, this ground requires dismissal for want of jurisdiction; it is not a constitutional merits disposition.

Effective December 6, 1994, the Commission's general FECA enforcement appeal power does not independently authorize this Supreme Court petition, and the assumed fullest later authorization cannot cure the filing after the lawful application period. Those threshold rules control; the D.C. Circuit's constitutional judgment remains in force for the parties without Supreme Court endorsement. ### Reich v. Collins, No. 93-908. — 1994-12-06

Authority: [Record](../records/Reich_v_Collins_merits_1994-12-06.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State holds out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, due process forbids withdrawing that remedy after payment for the constitutional invalidity asserted and then relying on the taxpayer's failure to pursue a different prepayment route. Georgia must afford Reich a clear and certain opportunity for the refund or constitutionally adequate equivalent relief due under federal law, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount; it may prospectively establish an adequate prepayment system with fair notice.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct votes for the bounded procedural rule and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The substantive tax invalidity is already accepted. The Court neither declares every Georgia prepayment avenue inadequate nor converts discretionary stays into guaranteed stays. It creates no general government-estoppel doctrine, exemption from clearly announced reasonable requirements, fixed refund, interest award, or reopening of final judgments. Prospective procedural changes remain permissible with adequate review and fair notice.

Effective December 6, 1994, due process bars this after-payment withdrawal of a clear apparent postpayment remedy even assuming another adequate prepayment avenue existed. The rule adds a fair-notice constraint to remedial choice; it preserves McKesson's options and Harper's separation of governing law from remedy. It changes no substantive tax-immunity rule and supplies no refund amount or final-case reopening.

### Brown v. Gardner, No. 93-1128. — 1994-12-12

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Gardner_merits_1994-12-12.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### Section 1151 requires causation, not provider fault or accident

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1151 requires an injury or aggravation resulting from covered VA hospitalization, medical or surgical treatment, chapter 31 vocational rehabilitation, or examination, producing additional disability or death and not resulting from the veteran's own willful misconduct; it does not additionally require negligence, other provider fault, or an unforeseen untoward accident. Section 3.358(c)(3)'s added fault-or-accident prerequisite is invalid, and Gardner's claim must be redetermined under the surviving statutory conditions without an automatic benefits award.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct rationale votes on the causal rule and invalidity of the additional prerequisite. Stone-Zsela also concurs separately on the rule's limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The natural-progress provision is left undisturbed. No complete rule governing consent or necessary consequences is adopted. The separate FTCA coordination sentence remains: for a qualifying judgment under 28 U.S.C. §1346(b), or settlement or compromise under §§2672 or 2677, on or after December 1, 1962, for a disability, aggravation, or death treated as service connected under §1151, benefits for months after finality are withheld until the aggregate otherwise payable equals the total recovery. That is a coordination rule, not a provider-fault requirement; no qualifying recovery is found here.

Effective December 12, 1994, the VA may not impose provider fault or accident as an additional §1151 eligibility condition. Actual causation and the other enacted conditions remain; the natural-progress provision is undisturbed. The Court adds this statutory rule without changing Chevron generally, deciding a comprehensive consent doctrine, or awarding Gardner benefits. The separate FTCA coordination remains independent of fault.

### In re Baby K; No. 93-1673. — 1994-12-12

Authority: [Record](../records/In_re_Baby_K_merits_1994-12-12.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### The acute respiratory emergency triggers stabilization or lawful transfer

**Holding and operative rule:** When a participating hospital determines that an individual has an emergency respiratory condition, §1395dd(b)(1) requires the examination and treatment within its available staff and facilities necessary to prevent likely material deterioration, within reasonable medical probability, resulting from or during transfer, or transfer complying with subsection (c). Inability to cure the patient's underlying anencephaly, an ethical or medical objection to continued life support, uniform withholding from comparable anencephalic patients, and the hospital's lack of an intention to transfer do not excuse this duty where the acute emergency and effective stabilizing capability are established.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Screening under subsection (a) is requested examination of an individual coming to the emergency department within that department's capability, including routinely available ancillary services. The diagnosed-emergency duty under subsection (b) is distinct. Transfer includes hospital-directed movement and discharge, but excludes movement after declaration of death or departure without hospital-affiliated permission. The separate labor provisions are not decided; Congress remains free to enact a defined futility exception.

#### Unstabilized transfer remains a fully conditional alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** An unstabilized transfer requires either a written request by the individual or legally responsible person after explanation of the hospital's obligations and transfer risks, or a physician-signed certification, based on then-available information, that expected medical benefits at the other facility outweigh increased transfer risks; if no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person may sign only after consulting a physician who makes that determination and later countersigns, and every certification must summarize risks and benefits. Every route also requires available treatment minimizing risk; receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance of appropriate care; transmission of all available emergency-related records, the request or certification, and the name and address of a required on-call physician who refused or failed timely to appear; qualified transportation personnel and equipment with necessary appropriate life support; and the Secretary's applicable additional health-and-safety requirements.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

#### Refusal compliance requires an actual offer and informed refusal

**Holding and operative rule:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement met when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately deems compliance when the hospital offers a transfer satisfying subsection (c), explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal; the mother's opposition alone establishes neither route here.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

#### The receiving-hospital route depends on appropriate transfer, required specialized capability and capacity

**Holding and operative rule:** If a nursing-home movement is an appropriate transfer requiring the participating hospital's specialized capabilities or facilities, §1395dd(g) prohibits the hospital from refusing acceptance when it has capacity to treat the individual. The Court does not settle every movement's classification or presume future capacity; the conditional receiving-hospital route preserves those requirements independently of the ordinary arrival-and-diagnosed-emergency route.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

#### State medical law yields only to an applicable direct conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §1395dd(f), a state or local requirement remains effective except to the extent it directly conflicts with an applicable EMTALA requirement; state law cannot authorize withholding the stabilizing treatment required here. Virginia §54.1-2990, a limitation within an article concerning adult directives and surrogates, does not itself establish an infant-care exemption, and the Court invalidates no wider field of state medical law.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

Effective December 12, 1994, EMTALA's determined-emergency duty applies to these acute respiratory episodes despite incurable anencephaly and broader futility objections. Screening, treatment, appropriate transfer, informed refusal, receiving capacity and direct-conflict preemption remain separate statutory rules. The Court adds this bounded stabilization application; it does not create a federal malpractice code, an indefinite post-stabilization intensive-care duty or a general right to preferred treatment.

### Nebraska Department of Revenue v. Loewenstein; No. 93-823. — 1994-12-12

#### The private repo return is outside the federal-obligation exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3124(a) does not exempt the return on these repurchase agreements because it is owed under a separate private financing obligation, rather than on the United States' securities; actual book-entry delivery does not change the source of the negotiated earnings. The statute continues to protect direct and indirect taxation of federal obligations and their interest, except nondiscriminatory corporate franchise or substitute corporate nonproperty taxes and estate or inheritance taxes; the State prevails because this income is outside the protected subject, not because an exception covers this individual income tax.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

An obligation that the Federal Housing Administration agreed, under a contract made before March 1, 1941, to issue at a future date retains the tax-exemption privileges provided by the authorizing law at the time of that contract. No separate construction, application or validity ruling concerning a Seller-Borrower's §77-2716(1)(e)(i) add-back is made.

**Holding and operative rule:** A ruling that expressly addresses federal-obligation repos does not, without an identified favorable policy for comparable state or local repos, establish the discriminatory taxation alleged here. The discrimination objection therefore supplies no ground to invalidate Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 on this record; the Court does not approve actual discriminatory treatment or find every Nebraska practice neutral.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Holding and operative rule:** The constitutional borrowing-power objection fails because the supplied evidence concerning the effects of classifying repos in bankruptcy does not establish the asserted tax-specific burden on federal borrowing. Rockford's exclusion of remote or conjectural impairment applies without a finding that taxation can never affect borrowing or a numerical threshold for permissible effects.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

Effective December 12, 1994, the identified private repo return is taxable under §3124(a)'s protected-subject boundary. The existing direct and indirect federal-obligation immunity and its tax exceptions remain. ### Plakas v. Drinski; No. 93-1824. — 1995-01-09

Authority: [Record](../records/Plakas_v_Drinski_merits_1995-01-09.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding and operative rule:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earlier-beating theory was not pleaded and lacks the asserted causal showing; the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. Gilmere's different provocation theory is neither approved nor disapproved. The Court does not decide a case in which the threat ended before the shot or a supported safe alternative makes the force unreasonable. The merits ground supplies no general immunity for tactical decisions.

#### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality's §1983 liability requires a cognizable federal deprivation attributable to municipal policy or training, the applicable culpability and a causal connection; officer personal immunity is not municipal immunity, and reasonable final force alone does not answer every independent municipal-created-danger theory. The county is entitled to summary judgment here because the actual equipment-and-training contention establishes neither a constitutional equipment-list duty nor a developed independent deprivation with the required causal and culpability showing; a purely derivative force theory also lacks a predicate when the force itself is reasonable.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

Effective January 9, 1995, the supported immediate-threat facts establish reasonable deadly force without a constitutional least-dangerous-tactic requirement. Complete contemporaneous circumstances and supported factual disputes remain material. The actual county equipment-and-training claim fails independently under the existing deprivation, attribution, causation and culpability requirements. No immunity rule, equipment list, categorical exclusion of earlier conduct or universal municipal exoneration is created.

### Interstate Commerce Commission v. Transcon Lines; No. 93-1318; 513 U.S. 138. — 1995-01-10

Authority: [Record](../records/Interstate_Commerce_Commission_v_Transcon_Lines_merits_1995-01-10.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

#### The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** Under the applicable pre-reform 49 U.S.C. §§10743(b)(1) and 11702(a)(4), the ICC may seek a judicial injunction against collection violating valid credit regulations, and §§10761–10762's filed-rate commands do not categorically defeat that enforcement. Section 11702(a)(4) excludes an action under a provision governing the reasonableness or discriminatory character of rates; this action enforces specified credit and billing requirements rather than asking the court to determine rate reasonableness or discrimination.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

#### The admitted collection class violates the credit conditions

**Controlling proposition:** The challenged loss-of-discount liquidated-damages collections violate 49 C.F.R. §1320.3(c) because the original bills omitted the required late-payment warning, §1320.2(g)(2)(vi) because revised billing occurred years later rather than within ninety days after authorized-credit expiration, and §1320.2(g)(2)(iii) because the demands aggregate past-shipment balances. The concessions establish those violations and the charge's liquidated-damages character for the challenged class, without establishing every account's inclusion or amount, every regulatory exception, statutory authority, remedial entitlement or a distinct contractual collection route.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All operative credit qualifications remain. Authorized credit begins the day after bill presentation; absent a published alternative the period is fifteen days including weekends and holidays, and permitted combined credit cannot exceed thirty days. Service charges within the standard period are prohibited. Published additional-period charges and published post-expiration charges are distinct; post-expiration charges neither authorize delay nor remove the carrier's good-faith reassessment obligation before another credit extension. The tariff's ninety calendar days after shipment for discount eligibility is separate from the revised-billing deadline measured from authorized-credit expiration.

Section 1320.2(g)(1) permits reasonable, certain collection-expense liquidated damages through an identified dollar or percentage charge or loss of an identified discount after a stated payment date no earlier than authorized-credit expiration. Section (g)(2) requires clear terms and conditions, prohibits unlawful prejudice or unjust discrimination, permits only original separate independent bills rather than aggregate past-shipment balance-due demands, excludes clear clerical or ministerial errors such as nonreceipt, a lost payment check or misaddressing, and prohibits duplicate collection fees already provided by the bill of lading for the same transportation. Damages arise only after authorized credit expires, with a revised bill or notice issued within ninety days after that expiration. Section (g)(3)'s separate contractual bill-of-lading collection-charge route outside the tariff is not an entitlement established here. Section 1320.3(c)'s original-bill or accompanying-notice requirements concerning penalties, time limits, service and collection charges, and discount terms remain distinct from revised billing. No unreported exception, account inclusion or amount is inferred.

#### A confined injunction is appropriate and must be implemented on remand

**Controlling proposition:** On the admitted violations and the defined loss-of-discount liquidated-damages request, an injunction preventing collection of that class is an appropriate exercise of the ICC's public enforcement authority, and the case must be remanded for its confined implementation. The lower court must specify the order's terms and may resolve genuine scope, inclusion and amount questions beyond the concessions or a properly presented distinct lawful collection basis, but may not reopen the admitted violation or charge character, restrain ordinary unpaid freight principal or other estate receivables, or treat this decision as a universal discharge.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court independently examines authority, established violation, the restraint's legal character and its fit. Review remains available for an unsupported premise, insufficient explanation, arbitrariness, excessive scope or inconsistency with the statute. No automatic Chevron deference to litigation demands, universal agency-remedy standard or broader limitation on Chevron is adopted. The prior distinct coded-rate component, individual private defenses, other statutory claims, estate distribution, exact dollar relief and unpresented reform-statute relief remain outside the disposition. A distinct contractual route requires its own presentation and proof; nothing here accepts its availability for these claims.

Effective January 10, 1995, the Court recognizes this express public credit-rule injunction as compatible with filed-rate obligations and directs its implementation for the admitted-unlawful loss-of-discount liquidated-damages class. Before this decision, Commercial Metals and Reiter distinguished public enforcement and enacted statutory remedies from a general private equitable defense without deciding this injunction. The new application resolves statutory availability, the conceded class's violation and character, and the appropriateness of this confined restraint. The actual credit periods, billing triggers, notice duties, clerical-error and duplicate-fee limits, distinct contractual route and §11702(a)(4) rate-review exception retain the terms set out in the Holdings block. Ordinary freight principal and other estate receivables remain outside the restraint; no universal debt discharge, automatic private defense, exact amount, later-reform entitlement or general agency-deference rule is created.

Precedent treatment and its full operative scope remain in the linked Record’s public Precedent Treatment block. No prior authority is altered beyond that stated treatment.

### Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., including Walsh; No. 93-1197; 513 U.S. 30 — 1994-11-14

Authority: [Record](../records/Hess_v_Port_Authority_Trans_Hudson_Corp_merits_1994-11-14.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. She would treat PATH as an arm of the States because the governors and legislatures retain substantial control over its commissioners, actions, projects and spending. Financial exposure matters, but the protection of a State from federal suit also safeguards its institutional authority. A self-supporting state enterprise need not lose that protection merely because it can pay judgments from its own revenues. Lake Country Estates permits an entity-specific finding of arm status; it does not compel treating PATH’s state controls as equivalent to the more local structure there. The dissent does not read Article XV as an unlimited state guarantee. Its equal, conditional administrative appropriations, recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, bind each State only up to $100,000 annually while operating revenues are inadequate. The claimed sovereign relationship rests on the combined legal controls, not an invented obligation to pay judgments. Having found arm status, the dissent treats consent as a separate requirement. Feeney’s federal-forum consent did not eliminate its conditions; the district courts found both actions outside the one-year period running from accrual. Hilton’s state-court FELA holding does not revive constructive waiver in federal court. The dissent therefore would affirm these dismissals without deciding liability, damages or a new exception to the consent legislation. This position does not control the Court’s status ruling or remand.

### United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.; No. 93-723; 513 U.S. 64 — 1994-11-29

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_X_Citement_Video_Inc_merits_1994-11-29.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissents. He reads the placement and structure of knowingly as applying to the handling acts, without extending it to the later conditions identifying the depicted conduct. A presumption of criminal fault helps interpret an otherwise unresolved enactment, but cannot overcome what these Justices regard as a clear allocation of the knowledge term. Their agreement with Staples’s contextual presumption does not require a different answer: the problem here is the enacted placement of an express adverb, not simply a statute’s silence about fault. Constitutional avoidance must operate through a permissible construction, not a judicial replacement of that allocation.

Under their reading, the law reaches a person who handles a depiction without awareness of its pornographic character. That content-related defect, in light of Smith and Ferber, supports preserving the appellate reversal. Their conclusion does not depend on making actual knowledge of the performer’s age an unavoidable constitutional requirement. A different enactment could treat performer age differently where the actor knows the pornographic character; the dissent does not decide that different statute. Osborne’s recklessness came from Ohio’s own law and supplies no judicially created federal default.

The dissent accepts the reported finding that respondents knew the performer was underage. It neither asserts factual innocence nor orders retrial because of an imagined missing finding. The finding does not cure the facial absence of adequate content-related fault in the statute as these Justices read it. Although the two renewed definition objections are legally available defenses of the favorable judgment, the dissent reaches neither because its existing ground is sufficient. It also leaves the indictment and actual-tape claims undecided. These positions are noncontrolling; the Court’s statutory construction and bounded remand govern.

### Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, No. 93-1062. — 1994-12-05

Authority: [Record](../records/Church_of_Scientology_Flag_Service_Organization_Inc_v_City_of_Clearwater_merits_1994-12-05.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in leaving the Church's favorable relief and the purpose-and-severability remand undisturbed, but dissents from reopening the surviving identifying and written-policy requirements. He would affirm the Court of Appeals' judgment on the grounds stated in his opinion. RFRA governs future municipal enforcement even though the ordinance predates it, and the Church's inability to invoke an unenacted statute below does not forfeit its protection. ### Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund, No. 93-1151. — 1994-12-06

Authority: [Record](../records/Federal_Election_Commission_v_NRA_Political_Victory_Fund_jurisdictional_dismissal_1994-12-06.md#public-projection); the public blocks are also copied into the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

Justice Stevens dissents in an opinion joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela. He reads section 437d(a)(6)'s express power to appeal a civil enforcement action through the Commission's general counsel as carrying that action through discretionary Supreme Court review. Section 518(a) itself uses appeal language broadly; the word therefore cannot decide the case without its statutory context. The Commission's exclusive enforcement role and the enacted grant to its own counsel support an exception under the ordinary-construction approach recognized in Providence Journal. The more explicit funding provisions do not negate that affirmative grant. He would retain the timely proceeding for consideration of the merits. He does not adopt later ratification as an additional ground and expresses no conclusion on the constitutionality of the nonvoting members or the underlying contribution dispute.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also files a separate dissent on the merits. Having accepted independent litigation authority, he would reverse the separation-of-powers ground and remand for the preserved statutory and enforcement issues the Court of Appeals did not decide. He distinguishes legally operative executive control from advice, observation, communication, or persuasion. Congressional officers cannot receive a vote, veto, quorum power, binding directive, or other legal authority over execution by being called nonvoting or ex officio. On the arrangement presented, the appointed Commissioners alone exercise the enforcement powers; the nonvoting officers have no identified authority to authorize, block, suspend, or direct Commission action. The operating account reported below also excludes chairmanship, meeting calls or adjournment, and quorum participation; those operating descriptions are not additional words of the statute.

Buckley concerns officers exercising significant governmental authority; Bowsher concerns executive authority lodged in an officer controlled by Congress; and the Airports Authority decision concerns a legally effective veto over operational decisions. In his view, potential influence without comparable legal power does not establish the constitutional defect those decisions forbid. An advisory label concealing approval, disapproval, delay, compulsory direction, decisive participation, or another legal consequence would present a different case. Because he finds no defect here, he would undertake no severability or structural-ratification inquiry. His proposed remand would not automatically revive the district court's enforcement judgment while other appellate issues remain. No other Justice joins these merits views, and they establish no rule of the Court.
