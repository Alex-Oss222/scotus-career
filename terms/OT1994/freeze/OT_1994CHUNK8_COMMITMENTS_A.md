## 85. Gutierrez de Martinez v. Lamagno — No.94-167; cutoff immediately before June 14, 1995

### Reach, components and two lawful paths

Reach the accepted certification-review question in the admitted merits posture. The action began in federal court on diversity grounds. This record therefore does not require deciding whether a removed action retains Article III jurisdiction after a certification is rejected. The lower courts' separate §904 and headquarters-claim rulings are outside the granted question, as petitioners' merits brief p.5 n.1 expressly distinguishes. An administrative remedy is neither an adjudicated payment nor an adequate-remedy finding.

**Path A — judicial review of employment scope:** reverse the certification-conclusive ruling and remand for judicial determination of scope under the applicable law. Section 2679(d)(1) directs initial substitution on certification, while (d)(2) expressly confines conclusiveness to removal. Review of substitution does not waive the foreign-country exception, create damages, or decide whether Lamagno acted within scope. The ordinary review presumption and statutory distinction support this path. Its strongest objection is the repeated mandatory substitution language and Congress's explicit employee-only petition in (d)(3); the answer distinguishes the initial litigation procedure from conclusive adjudication of the predicate for employee immunity.

**Path B — executive certification controls substitution:** affirm the challenged ruling because (d)(1)'s certification trigger and mandatory substitution, the coordinated (d)(4) procedure, and (d)(3)'s expressly authorized employee challenge imply an asymmetric statutory design. The strongest objection is that this makes the removal-only qualification in (d)(2) apparently superfluous and grants an interested executive official final control of a private tort claim. The answer is that certification and refusal arise at different statutory stages, Congress may allocate immunity predicates, and loss of a remedy under Smith does not itself require judicial rewriting. Constitutional validity is not decided more broadly than necessary to sustain this construction.

Smith establishes immunity for employment-related torts notwithstanding an FTCA exception; it does not itself decide whether the employment predicate is judicially contestable. The effective simulated Meyer decision likewise separates waiver, cognizability, cause of action and employee exclusivity. Neither supplies a direct answer to certification finality. No Justice may join a proposition that an overseas injury alone makes certification unreviewable or that review creates an FTCA recovery.

## 86. Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation — No.94-771; cutoff immediately before June 14, 1995

### Scope and component paths

The motor-fuel and member-income questions require separate positions. The inspected respondent brief pp.42–48 identifies the income question under review as the treaty claim for tribal members earning wages from their government in Indian country while residing outside Indian country. Its n.37 does not press an independent Williams v. Lee off-country infringement claim. Preserve the ordinary Sac and Fox rule protecting income both earned and resident in Indian country; do not turn this review into a new adjudication against those residents or nonmember employees.

**Fuel Path A:** affirm protection from the particular written tax. Its legal incidence rests on the tribal retailer, rather than merely passing an economic cost through it: distributors remit on a retailer's behalf and may credit uncollectible retailer taxes against subsequent liability. The Indian-country immunity rule requires congressional authorization for that tax. Yakima distinguishes property taxation from transactional incidence; Milhelm Attea permits collection of valid nonmember taxes, not manufacture of a tax on an immune tribe. The strongest contrary argument is that a commodity levy collected through distributors and reflected in consumer prices is economically a consumer tax; fair reading of the enacted remittance/credit arrangement answers that argument without requiring a magic incidence declaration.

**Fuel Path B:** reverse if fair construction places legal incidence on a nonimmune party, then assess the proper federal-preemption and collection questions. Its strongest obstacle is the statutory retailer-payment arrangement, not a general exemption for fuel or tribal commerce. On the verified old text this route fails. An amended consumer-incidence statute would require its own effective-source validation and cannot be adopted from the pending bill.

**Income Path A:** reverse the treaty-based exemption for members residing outside Indian country. Article IV's governmental guarantee operates with its protection of jurisdiction over persons and property within the Nation's western limits; it does not independently exempt a citizen's income from the resident State's nondiscriminatory tax whenever the employer is the Tribe. The strongest contrary argument is the treaty's separate prohibition of state laws for government of the Nation and descendants, favorable construction including Article XVIII, and the cost to tribal government of employee taxation. The answer is that the retained territorial and governmental protection remains meaningful without transforming an indirect payroll cost into immunity of each off-country resident.

**Income Path B:** affirm the treaty exemption because a tax on government-paid wages burdens the protected institution and descendants in their work within the protected territory. This route has genuine treaty text and history behind it; it fails for the commitments below because a law taxing an off-country resident's income does not govern the tribal institution or its internal processes simply because salary comes from that institution. Do not rest rejection on implied abrogation at statehood; construe the surviving promise. Do not adopt a general claim about all tribes' off-country self-government rights.

**Hayden-Cartwright preservation:** do not decide the newly raised merits theory. The neutral packet and respondent merits pp.15–18 establish its late introduction; no exceptional reason is established here to bypass ordinary preservation. This is not a holding that the Act does or does not authorize Indian-country fuel taxation. The generic granted tax questions do not certify this specific unpreserved ground.

## 87. Sandin v. Conner — No.93-1911; cutoff immediately before June 19, 1995

### Paths and verified limitations

Reach the protected-liberty question. Hawaii's adjustment rule is not paraphrased as a mandatory sanction: §17-201-18(b) directs a guilt finding on admission/guilty plea or substantial evidence and says disciplinary action must rest on more than mere silence; §17-201-19(a) permits sanctions commensurate with gravity and severity; §17-201-20(b) permits the administrator to review and modify decisions. A command concerning guilt and permission concerning punishment remain distinct. Petitioner's construction says the guilt command is one-way and leaves room for findings below substantial evidence; respondent reads the complete disciplinary scheme as prohibiting punishment absent a valid finding. Neither interpretation is elevated to an undisputed factual concession.

**Path A — narrow regulatory reversal:** reverse the determination that the identified substantial-evidence command alone creates an entitlement to remain outside disciplinary segregation. Hewitt and Thompson require a substantive limit giving rise to an enforceable expectation, not procedural directions alone. Read the available rule, sanction discretion and administrator review together; the committee's duty to convict when sufficient proof exists does not itself require acquittal whenever it does not. The strongest contrary argument is that categories of punishable misconduct and evidence rules necessarily confine the power to punish; the response distinguishes the asserted substantial-evidence entitlement from all possible limits on punishment. This path does not find that thirty days in segregation is harmless, decide the witnesses issue, or adopt a new universal hardship test.

**Path B — uphold the recognized liberty interest and remand:** affirm the Ninth Circuit's liberty-interest determination, retaining its remand for the disputed Wolff-process question. The scheme in context requires a finding of misconduct before punishment; discretion to choose a sanction after guilt does not eliminate an entitlement to avoid punishment without its predicate. The strongest objection is the one-way formulation and broad administrator authority; the response reads those provisions with the offense/sanction structure, rather than treating an administrator's discretion as power to convict or punish for any reason. This path does not establish an unconditional right to call every witness, immunity defeat, damages, or a trial result.

A separate nature-and-severity path is legally available for considering whether sentence-authorized custody leaves a protected interest. Hewitt's administrative-custody discussion and Thompson's expressly reserved broader rule identify the question but do not already establish a binding categorical replacement for their state-entitlement rule. The inspected briefs describe a thirty-day sanction and compare Special Holding Unit conditions with PhaseI custody, while respondent emphasizes punitive status, ordinary confinement and parole consequences. No factual shortcut makes all these comparisons interchangeable. **No associate is provisionally committed here to a new atypical-and-significant-hardship rule merely because it would yield a convenient coalition.** Any later proposal to replace Hewitt's test must receive a new formulation-specific Justice inquiry and the date-eligible record needed for its application.

## 88. United States v. Gaudin — No.94-514; cutoff immediately before June 19, 1995

### Scope, alternatives, and common ground

Reach the accepted Fifth/Sixth Amendment question. On the record presented, materiality is an element of the charged §1001 offenses. The Court need not decide whether every textual clause of the statute independently requires materiality in every prosecution. Hubbard concerns which bodies count as departments/agencies; HUD is an executive body and that entity rule does not transfer an offense element to a judge.

**Path A:** affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions and remand. The jury must determine each element beyond a reasonable doubt, applying the judge's legal definition to the record. A mixed law/fact element does not become a judicial element because the judge supplies its standard. The strongest objection relies on Sinclair's judicial treatment of pertinency and Kungys's civil review of materiality; neither defeats the constitutional jury function where materiality is assumed or established as an element of this criminal offense. Treatment of Sinclair must identify the incompatible criminal-jury allocation rather than overrule unrelated holdings or every civil review rule.

**Path B:** reverse the Ninth Circuit because materiality is a legal question traditionally reserved to judges, or sustain the convictions because evidence was overwhelming. The first route fails to distinguish instruction on law from application of the element to disputed agency functions and statements. The second cannot turn judicial materiality into an actual jury finding. The scope here does not require a general holding that all omissions of all offense elements are structural and incapable of harmless-error review in every procedural posture.

The effective simulated Sullivan rule forbids a judicial substitute for the jury's constitutionally required verdict under the stated reasonable-doubt defect; its complete error analysis must not be expanded mechanically. The en banc lower opinion separates the equity-skimming count from the §1001 counts. No acquittal, wholesale sentence calculation, or cancellation of an unrelated conviction follows from this jury-allocation ruling.

## 89. Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. v. M/V Sky Reefer — No.94-623; cutoff immediately before June 19, 1995

### Two components and two lawful paths

Exclude Breyer without assigning a reason. Reach the certified statutory issue through §1292(b). The rejected adhesion defense is outside this interlocutory review. It cannot be reopened merely because Allied-Bruce preserves ordinary contract defenses when properly presented.

**Path A — coexistence:** affirm the stay without adopting the First Circuit's priority rationale. COGSA §3(8)'s nonwaiver rule and FAA maritime arbitration can operate together: selecting a foreign arbitral forum does not itself surrender the carrier's substantive duty. Costs and inconvenience alone do not establish a contractual release; no actual foreign-law reduction is established in the admitted posture. The federal case remains stayed, not dismissed, and lawful enforcement-stage examination of a concrete statutory-waiver objection remains available. Mitsubishi's prospective-waiver reservation is not a guarantee of plenary merits review. The strongest contrary argument is that costly distant enforcement predictably reduces effective recovery and the combination of Japanese law and Tokyo arbitration may deny the nondelegable statutory duty. The answer separates actual substantive deprivation from an unrealized possibility while retaining the existing court's lawful authority.

**Path B — present prohibited lessening:** reverse the stay because a carrier-imposed foreign arbitration clause materially increases the obstacles to enforcing COGSA and so falls within §3(8)'s broad prohibition. Indussa offers a persuasive enforcement-obstacle construction but reserves arbitration and does not bind this Court. The strongest objection is that the Act guarantees substantive protection, not an American forum, and the FAA expressly covers maritime transactions. The response construes nonwaiver protection practically, without treating arbitration policy as superior to an independently applicable federal limitation. Cargo liability, choice of law and damages remain undecided.

First Options preserves antecedent assent and actual delegation, not a new appeal from an agreed arbitration's merits. Mastrobuono gives effect to actual integrated contract terms, not blanket superiority of arbitration policy. Allied-Bruce's Scalia/Thomas state-court objection does not prevent either Justice from applying the FAA in this federal maritime action. No amount is inserted into the corrupted neutral fact field.

Lamb's Chapel's viewpoint-neutral government-forum rule therefore does not transfer directly.
