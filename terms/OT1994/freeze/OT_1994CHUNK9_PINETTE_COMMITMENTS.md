## Common record and scope conclusions

The review concerns the Count One merits judgment entered after Rule 65(a)(2) consolidation and affirmed by the Sixth Circuit. Its affirmative command ran only from December 21 through December 24, 1993. The application, original denial and administrative affirmance concerned a temporary unattended cross, with a readable private-sponsorship sign offered and its content open to negotiation. The alleged shortcomings of the sign actually erected are advocacy, not resolved findings. The permit's expiration does not erase the legal dispute if the same-party repetition exception applies; it also does not authorize this Court to issue a permit for 1995 or rewrite the expired order as a permanent injunction.

The November 18 vote did not validly close unattended displays. The Statehouse's visual dominance, cross size and unattended character count in attribution analysis. The genuinely open forum, private financing and maintenance, diverse uses and absence of privileged governmental selection also count. The menorah was no longer present when the cross was erected. Neither publicity nor the Klan's identity establishes universal knowledge that the message was private. No threat, deficient bond, actual denial to some other denomination, full permit-code provision, later application, fresh damages claim or additional remedial proceeding is supplied as an independent ground.

The applicable law is the actual inherited law: Perry and Widmar; Lemon and the distinctions in Lynch, Allegheny and Mergens; and the simulated Lee, Lamb's Chapel and Kiryas Joel holdings in the entering-law copy. Lamb's Chapel preserves a contextual Lemon application and its private-access holding, not a universal exclusion of private speech from establishment analysis. Lee has independent official-sponsorship and school-pressure grounds. Kiryas Joel preserves neutral accommodation while invalidating its specific denominational allocation. Zobrest supplies no aid-merits rule. Hurley's private-public distinction is relevant but its holding does not resolve religious-display attribution. ## Threshold and scope commitments

**Common operative conclusion:** All eight reach the merits. Under Weinstein v. Bradford, 423 U.S. 147, 149 (1975), a nonclass dispute survives expiration only if its duration prevents full review and the same complaining party reasonably can be expected to encounter the challenged action again. The brief holiday period satisfies duration. The organized claimant's actual participation, continuing forum interest and the annual practice, considered with the lower court's recurrence assessment and continuing defense of the exclusion, support a reasonable expectation of another materially similar application by these respondents. This is an inference from this record, not an admitted new application. It does not depend on another organization's possible controversy.

The brief in opposition's outside-record account of a conditional intention to return reinforces the consistency of that inference but is not treated as evidence establishing a satisfied condition, a verified application or an agreed jurisdictional fact. The conclusion would remain the same without upgrading that account. No Justice demands a sworn promise as an extra categorical prerequisite. No Justice applies a rule that parties can confer jurisdiction by agreeing the exception applies.

## Enhanced merits comparison and formulation boundaries

### Stevens

**Eligible anchors.** His Allegheny separate writing, 492 U.S. 646–655, and joins identified there scrutinize the religious significance conveyed by the actual governmental setting. His simulated Lee joins support independent sponsorship analysis; his Lamb's Chapel joins protect nonsponsored private access; his Kiryas Joel concurrence distinguishes private association from the State's affirmative organization of religious attachment. His Widmar judgment concurrence, 454 U.S. 277–280, accepted access where the record disclosed no sponsorship danger. These are distinct precedents, not a personal rule forbidding all private religion on public land.

**Contextual path adopted.** Reverse. An unattended cross of the described size standing with the Statehouse as its dominant background risks communicating an official religious alignment in a way an identifiable speaker's rally or an attended film presentation does not. The legal conclusion is about governmental attribution in this placement, not offense at the message or a finding of state funding. A disclaimer naming a private owner answers title but does not necessarily answer why the seat of government accommodates this conspicuous symbolic presentation. His conclusion that this setting supplies the attribution injury makes avoiding it a compelling reason for denying the particular application.

**Categorical neutral-access path rejected and counterargument answered.** The best access argument is substantial: the forum is actually diverse, ordinary unattended uses are allowed, and no privileged placement or financial aid is shown. Lamb's Chapel and Widmar preclude simple religious disqualification. Stevens nevertheless would not make a forum designation dispositive of how the Statehouse setting communicates sponsorship. In his view, Allegheny's contextual principle extends beyond its nonforum staircase when an unattended symbol conveys the government's own religious affiliation. The counterargument exposes the reach of his extension; it does not erase his identified distinction between the attended private expression previously protected and this prominent unattended display.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** He does not rely on the allegation that the actual sign buckled as an established fact. Even the offered readable identification does not sufficiently separate this cross from the governmental setting on his analysis. No remand to measure the actual sign is needed for that sufficient ground. He can join protection of private religious speech, the ordinary forum scrutiny rule, merits reach and the expired-remedy limitations. He cannot join an application finding no establishment injury or a blanket private-forum exemption. A mere substitution of an informed-observer label does not resolve the objection; a materially different placement or presentation would be a different factual application, not a circulation edit. He announces no blanket prohibition on all unattended religious displays anywhere on public land.

### O'Connor

**Eligible anchors.** Her Allegheny concurrence, 492 U.S. 623–637, supplies contextual endorsement and sensitivity to core governmental settings. Her Mergens Part III distinguishes neutral permission and private expression, with safeguards. Her simulated Lamb's Chapel establishment join, Lee joins and Kiryas Joel concurrence preserve neutral comparable access without making one formula decide every category.

**Contextual path adopted.** Affirm. A reasonable observer considers the square's established public uses and actual neutral access, not only a cropped view of the cross and Statehouse. That observer need not know every permit history or newspaper report. Here private control, temporary ordinary use, a forum admitting secular and religious speakers, and the application offering readable private identification distinguish the State's own holiday presentation and a privileged exhibit. The access program serves a secular forum purpose; this proposed admission does not primarily advance religion as the State's message or create excessive entanglement. The conclusion preserves Lemon and the specific endorsement inquiry.

**Categorical path and counterarguments.** A rule making genuine neutral forum access dispositive would protect religious speakers from a viewer's mistake, and her own Mergens writing emphasizes that permission is not sponsorship. She rejects its categorical extension: government may itself create an apparent religious alliance despite formal private title or formally equal eligibility. Ohio's strongest contextual objection is the unattended cross's salience for viewers unfamiliar with the forum. It does not establish that every such observer's incomplete impression controls, or that a readable statement of private sponsorship is legally useless. Ohio's refusal rested on an asserted incurable location-based defect, not an adjudicated inability to clarify this proposal.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** She accepts the offered readable attribution as part of the proposal; she makes no finding that every later sign performed as offered. No constitutional rule that every religious display needs a sign follows. She may recognize a genuinely neutral attribution measure as permissible, with its actual operation still subject to speech law. She joins the narrow application and judgment, but refuses language eliminating endorsement analysis throughout public forums, treating all governmental neutrality as conclusively established by a permit, or imposing a particular unrequested sign specification. Removing those propositions is the minimum revision for a full join in a bounded contextual opinion. No factual remand is necessary to resolve the categorical 1993 denial.

### Scalia

**Eligible anchors.** His join in Kennedy's Mergens separate rationale, his simulated Lamb's Chapel concurrence joined by Thomas, his Kiryas Joel dissent and his Lee dissent distinguish neutral religious accommodation from governmental religion and reject Lemon or subjective endorsement as his chosen basis. His own current-term McIntyre dissent concerns election accountability, not this display or a general compelled-identification power.

**Neutral-access path adopted.** Affirm. When the expression is genuinely private, the forum is genuinely and equally available, and the State supplies no preferential religious treatment, denial because of the message's religious character is impermissible. The record meets those conditions; Statehouse proximity alone does not change the identity of the speaker. This is categorical only within those actual predicates. A forum label cannot immunize a state-selected religious exhibition, government financing of devotion or governmental control of a religious message.

**Contextual path and counterarguments.** A contextual Lemon application could also produce access here, but Scalia will not adopt that as his sufficient ground. Its strongest objection to his rule is that an unattended cross can look official even though title remains private. He answers that equally available speech does not become the government's speech through a viewer's misunderstanding; actual selection, favoritism or adoption would instead change the predicate. Allegheny's privileged placement does not establish those facts here, and the simulated Lamb's Chapel access result supports equal treatment without his embracing its establishment formulation.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** He does not make the permit contingent on a disclaimer and does not resolve the actual sign dispute. A voluntarily offered disclaimer can confirm private identity but is not a constitutional prerequisite in his ground. He may join a severable narrow statement that the Statehouse location does not alone turn this expression into Ohio's speech, as well as ordinary forum protection, disposition and remedy. He refuses an opinion declaring Lemon or an endorsement inquiry the governing reason for his vote. Deleting that asserted adoption, or severing those sections, permits a compatible join; no general overruling of Lemon is required or accomplished.

### Kennedy

**Eligible anchors.** His Mergens separate rationale, joined by Scalia, and simulated Lamb's Chapel separate writing support nonsponsored equal access without his endorsement-test assent. His Lee writing identifies real school-directed participation pressure. His Kiryas Joel concurrence condemns the State's own deliberate religious boundary criterion while distinguishing private association and neutral accommodation. His pre-divergence Allegheny writing preserves a limit on the State's placing its own weight behind religious proselytism.

**Neutral-access path adopted.** Affirm. There is no official religious exercise, coerced participation, state selection of a favored religious message or comparable direct establishment-level support in admitting this private applicant on ordinary public-forum terms. His sufficient ground requires genuinely private expression and actual neutral access. It does not say coercion is necessary to every conceivable establishment injury or that governmental sponsorship becomes harmless whenever attendance is voluntary.

**Contextual path and counterarguments.** The strongest competing path treats the cross and governmental backdrop as a message of affiliation. Kennedy accepts that actual state sponsorship can arise from context, but rejects mere location and anticipated misattribution as a sufficient basis for this exclusion. Allegheny's concern about a government placing its own weight behind proselytism is not established by this temporary neutral permit. A contextual opinion that independently applies Lemon can reach the same judgment, yet agreement in result does not turn his separate ground into a join in that methodology.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** Neither a disclaimer nor inquiry into the disputed later sign is necessary to his decision. He can join speech protection, actual private attribution and the bounded proposition that neutral access here is permissible. He cannot join a universal endorsement prohibition, a newly mandatory disclaimer or a claim that every government-owned location is equally available for private displays. The minimum compatible formulation identifies actual private access and excludes governmental religious adoption, leaving the broad establishment framework unsettled. He supports affirmance without a future permit command.

### Souter

**Eligible anchors.** His simulated Lamb's Chapel join accepts the contextual Lemon application; his Lee concurrence and opinion joins distinguish religious endorsement from coercion and preserve the independent sponsorship ground. His Kiryas Joel opinion requires substantive neutrality, not formal labels, while preserving private association and lawful accommodation. His current-term Hurley opinion protects private expressive authorship on public streets but expressly leaves misattribution in other settings open.

**Contextual path adopted.** Affirm the application-specific judgment. The government cannot avoid responsibility for a religious message it actually adopts merely by calling its land a forum. Here, however, an ordinary temporary permit for a private applicant in an established diverse forum, with the proposed readable private identification, does not communicate official worship. The offered clarification directly addresses the principal attribution concern without excluding the religious viewpoint. The State's assertion that no disclaimer or neutral admission practice could cure Statehouse proximity is overbroad. Even assuming an attribution concern requiring attention, categorical exclusion lacks a necessary fit where this proposal supplies a workable private-attribution alternative.

**Categorical path and counterarguments.** The broad rule offers a clear protection against religious censorship and draws strength from Widmar and Mergens. Souter rejects treating it as automatically dispositive: an unmarked unattended religious exhibit can communicate official sponsorship in circumstances where a private meeting does not. Ohio's best response to his narrower access path is that the later sign was allegedly hard to read. That allegation does not adjudicate the adequacy of the readable sign offered with the application, and this appeal does not concern a later enforcement finding that the permit holder departed from its proposal. No universal knowledge of the publicity is needed.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** His affirmative conclusion addresses the proposal with private identification. He reserves an otherwise similar proposal without effective attribution, rather than declaring that a sign must always be posted or that any sign suffices. The Constitution requires the State to avoid its own religious sponsorship; the specific means cannot be converted into a uniform judicial sign code. He joins narrow contextual findings and the expired judgment's scope. He refuses automatic forum immunity, conclusive reliance on ownership alone, a finding about the actual sign's disputed performance or a new sign injunction. An opinion preserving those distinctions needs no further evidentiary remand. A mandatory new factual inquiry merely to complete the record would expand the adjudicated dispute.

### Thomas

**Eligible anchors.** His joins in Scalia's simulated Lamb's Chapel concurrence, Kiryas Joel dissent and Lee dissent support neutral private religious access and reject Lemon as his sufficient ground. His current-term McIntyre concurrence protects the historical practice of anonymous individual political advocacy and does not adjudicate organizational attribution signs or this unattended religious expression.

**Neutral-access path adopted.** Affirm. The private party's exercise of equal expressive access is not an establishment by Ohio. The State has neither selected the cross as its own religious message nor shown that the ordinary permit would create a governmental religious institution or practice. Religious content does not diminish the private speaker's forum protection. No political-only explanation of this cross, or unsupported account of the Klan's historical purpose, is necessary.

**Contextual path and counterarguments.** The strongest contrary position treats the unattended symbol beside the Statehouse as an official message regardless of private title. Thomas rejects that attribution where actual neutral access and private control are established. A nominal forum masking favoritism would be another case. A contextual decision could reach the same access result, but his prior Lamb's Chapel and Kiryas Joel positions do not support a join in Lemon reasoning merely because it happens to reach his judgment.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** A sign is not a legal prerequisite on his ground; actual sign effectiveness therefore does not require remand. He leaves a genuinely neutral identification regulation for the case presenting its text and operation. McIntyre supplies no automatic immunity from every such rule. He joins a severable opinion protecting actual private equal access and the limited affirmance, not endorsement-based conditions or replacement factual premises. Removing reliance on such conditions is the minimum revision needed for a broader join. No affirmative view about a historically established political purpose is published without a verified record basis.

### Ginsburg

**Eligible anchors.** Her simulated Kiryas Joel joins, including Stevens's concurrence, oppose the State's affirmative religious organization while retaining the private-association and neutral-accommodation distinctions. Her current-term Hurley join protects private expression notwithstanding neutral use of public streets; it does not conclusively resolve attribution here. Her McIntyre join protects expression against an inadequately justified identification burden; it supplies no blanket rule against attribution of this display. No later display opinion is used or a prior direct vote in Lamb's Chapel or Lee attributed to her.

**Contextual path adopted.** Affirm the particular adjudicated proposal. The State's selection of a religious community as the recipient of civic power in Kiryas Joel differs from permitting an independently chosen message in a diverse existing forum. The latter distinction does actual work here: private financing and maintenance, ordinary temporary access, no religious favoritism, and an offered readable identification permit accommodation without governmental attachment to the religious claim. The Statehouse's visibility is a serious attribution consideration; it does not by itself make every private exhibit there governmental. Her judgment does not rest on an assumption that she has already adopted a general endorsement or coercion formula.

**Categorical path and counterarguments.** She refuses a categorical rule that formal equal access ends establishment scrutiny. The strongest argument for exclusion is that many observers may encounter the unattended symbol without appreciating the square's forum use or identifying its sponsor. The application addresses that problem by offering readable identification and negotiation; Ohio's response denies that any clarification could work in this location. The record does not support that categorical conclusion or show official adoption. Requiring proof that every possible viewer understands a private message would also seriously impair ordinary forum speech. The narrow resolution leaves genuinely confusing presentations and state-selected displays subject to contextual scrutiny.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** She supports the application as offered, without finding the later sign adequate or ordering a new disclaimer program. She can join a bounded contextual opinion retaining the State's responsibility for its own religious sponsorship and distinguishing actual neutral private use. She refuses an unqualified assurance that any unattended religious display in a public forum is permissible. Removing that categorical assurance and preserving the proposed-versus-actual sign distinction resolves the join barrier. No own analogous pre-event display opinion is verified; her commitment is the stated application of binding law and her limited eligible joins, not a claim of an established personal display doctrine.

### Breyer

**Eligible anchors.** His current-term Hurley join confirms that public land does not by itself convert private expression into state authorship; its attribution reservation remains. His McIntyre join requires attention to the actual speech burden and asserted justification, without deciding this sign question. He was not a participant in the simulated Lee, Lamb's Chapel or Kiryas Joel decisions. The latter holdings govern him as law, not as his own prior votes. No directly analogous personal pre-event display writing is established.

**Contextual path adopted.** Affirm. Existing binding law supplies a sufficient resolution: neutral private religious expression in an actually open forum is protected, and the proposed readable identification, private control and temporary diverse-use setting answer the State's asserted categorical attribution problem. The result neither invents a personal Breyer establishment test nor requires a comprehensive replacement of Lemon. A fact-sensitive application protects speech while preserving governmental responsibility for genuine religious sponsorship.

**Categorical path and counterarguments.** The broad neutral-access rule offers clarity and follows the real distinction between permission and sponsorship. Its weakness is the possibility of an actual governmental religious message concealed by formal eligibility; the inherited authorities leave that inquiry open. The strongest exclusion argument is the practical salience of a large unattended cross at the Statehouse to persons who do not read a sign. That supports considering attribution, not a constitutional right to censor on the assumption that all clarification is futile. Nothing in the record requires a factual remand to determine a hypothetical replacement sign or future application.

**Sign, narrower positions and joins.** He can join the narrow contextual result, speech protection and expired-remedy limits. He refuses to decide that forum status invariably defeats establishment, that every display must carry a disclaimer or that every particular future attribution measure is valid. The minimum compatible formulation stays with the proposal and leaves the later disputed sign outside the adjudication. A separate writing is not required if those limits appear in the joined opinion. This conclusion needs no additional personal doctrinal commitment; if assembly sought one, it would require eligible support beyond this packet.
