AGREED STATEMENT OF POST-CASUALTY FACTS 
Introduction 
This Agreed Statement of Post-Casualty Facts sets 
forth in a simple and direct menner the litigation history 
of the case which has been pending in the United States 
District Court for the Eastern District of Wisconsin and/ 
or the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh 
Circuit for over twelve years. The purpose of the Agreed 
Statement is to establish a basis for the parties to submit 
to the District Court the issue of prejudgment interest 
which 
is the only outstanding issue in the case that 
remains unsettled and contested. The Agreed Statement 
does not limit the facts to whic the parties may refer in 
their arguments or to which the Court may refer in its 

26 
decision, but only assists in providing focus and order to 
the briefing and decision. In order to accomplish this 
goal, the Agreed Statement will list by year the key 
events (primarily litigation events) in the case. 
In this Agreed Statement, reference to the various 
parties will be abbreviated in order to clarify and sim- 
plify the statement. The plaintiff, Cement Division, 
National Gypsum Co., will be referred to as “National 
Gypsum” or in some instances “Huron Cement.” The 
defendant, City of Milwaukee, will be referred to as the 
“City.” Third-party defendants 
in the litigation, 
i.e., 
Edward 
E. Gillen Company and Great Lakes Towing 
Company, will be referred 
to as “Gillen” and “Great 
Lakes Towing” respectively. 
1979 
On December 24, the S/S EM FORD, loaded with 
5,800 tons of bulk cement, was moored in a City-owned 
slip in Milwaukee’s outer harbor. In the course of a severe 
northeasterly storm, the FORD, over an eighteen-hour 
period, broke loose from its moorings, battered against 
the headwall of the slip, tore holes in its hull, took on 
water and sank to the bottom of the slip. The FORD was 
subsequently 
raised 
and repaired 
at 
a cost 
of 
$6,175,209.25. The value of the lost cement cargo was 
$319,000.00. The cost of repairs to the City’s dock and 
headwall was $192,432.18. 

27 
1980 
\ 
On October 29, the owner of the vessel, National 
Gypsum, brought this suit in admiralty pursuant to 28 
U.S.C. sec. 1333 against the City seeking $4.5-million in 
damages. The suit alleged, among other things, that the 
City had negligently breached its duty as a wharfinger by 
failing to warn National Gypsum of a hidden danger in 
the berthing slip and by misrepresenting conditions 
which could occur within the slip during a storm. The 
case was assigned to U.S. District Judge Robert W. War- 
ren. 
1981 
On January 15, the City denied fault and alleged ina 
counterclaim that National Gypsum was negligent in 
leaving the FORD moored in an outer harbor slip in 
winter virtually unmanned, with no means aboard of 
monitoring weather or summoning tugs or a pilot for 
assistance. The City sought to recover $250,000 for dam- 
ages caused by the vessel crashing against the City’s 
docks and headwall. Later that month, the City served a 
third-party complaint on Great Lakes Towing and Gillen, 
alleging that their negligence caused or contributed to the 
casualty. 
On April 8, National Gypsum commenced discovery 
when it served upon the City its first set of interrogato- 
ries and requests for production of documents. In addi- 
tion to the interrogatories and requests for production 
submitted and made by the various parties, the following 
depositions were taken in 1981:
