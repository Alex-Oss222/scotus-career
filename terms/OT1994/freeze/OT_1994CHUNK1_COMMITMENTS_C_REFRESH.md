## 9. Nebraska Department of Revenue v. Loewenstein — December 12, 1994

**Provisional refreshed disposition:** All eight non-Stone Justices continue to reverse the Nebraska judgment as to Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 and remand. They reject the respondent's statutory-immunity theory on the identified transactions; separately reject the discrimination and borrowing-impairment arguments as supported here; and decline to adjudicate the different Seller-Borrower's hypothetical deduction/add-back treatment. The additions strengthen and specify the existing statutory application, add an independently examined constitutional objection, and correct the characterization of the borrowing evidence. They do not expand review to Ruling 22-87-4, other repo agreements or a tax assessment not presented.

### Refreshed component commitments

**L1 — Statutory classification and actual contract.** The income taxed is the Trusts' privately negotiated return owed by the Seller-Borrower. Actual book-entry delivery and the absence of a separate note remain facts. The paired promises, return independent of Treasury yield, and additional reported terms identify the separate obligation: the Trusts must transmit the issuer's principal, interest and other sums to the Seller-Borrower; the latter maintains collateral value at 102 percent through the stated margin terms and may substitute equal-market-value federal securities; on default the Trusts may sell, keep debt and expenses, return excess and recover a deficiency. The default provisions are contractual permissions and duties, not events found to have occurred. Their legal relevance is that federal payments and ordinary market exposure remain allocated differently from the Trusts' contractual earnings. The government does not owe the negotiated repo return merely because genuine government securities have been delivered.

The duty to pass through actual issuer payments is particularly direct support for distinguishing the income streams. Margin and substitution provisions further support that distinction without supplying a universal test or proving that all repos are loans for every body of law. The statutory holding remains confined to the admitted transaction and the bounded typical-agreement description; no term is attributed to an unproduced different agreement. Section 3124(a)'s indirect-tax protection survives recodification. Its exceptions for nondiscriminatory corporate franchise or substitute corporate nonproperty taxes and estate/inheritance taxes do not describe the individual tax here. The State prevails on the covered-obligation antecedent, not an exception. Subsection (b)'s federal tax direction and qualifications do not displace subsection (a).

**L2 — Frank Lyon argument.** Independent business or regulatory reasons may explain the parties' sale-and-repurchase form and are not treated as sham or disregarded. Even accepting those reasons and actual delivery, Frank Lyon does not determine whether this privately owed return is interest on an obligation of the United States under §3124. The holding identifies the obligation generating the taxed income rather than deciding ownership or character for bankruptcy, securities, banking, commercial or other tax law. No new general substance-over-form doctrine or rejection of Frank Lyon is necessary.

**L3 — Constitutional discrimination, separately addressed.** Revenue Ruling 22-85-1's express discussion of federal-obligation repos does not itself establish favorable treatment of otherwise comparable state/local-obligation repo income. The taxpayer identifies no statute, ruling or other policy granting the asserted comparator exemption in the supplied material. The discrimination objection therefore does not support invalidating this ruling on this record. This is a failure of the asserted factual/legal comparison, not a finding that every Nebraska tax practice is nondiscriminatory and not a holding that discrimination against federal obligations or associated transactions would be lawful. The absence of §3124 statutory coverage does not by itself decide the independent constitutional claim. Memphis Bank's protection against actual discriminatory treatment of federal obligations remains intact; no issue concerning an established disparate repo policy is decided.

**L4 — Federal borrowing impairment, separately addressed.** The Sternlight affidavit and 1984 letter address consequences of repo characterization in bankruptcy and related settings. The reported Federal Reserve Bank brief distinguishes that question from the effect of taxing repo transaction income. The evidence therefore cannot be redescribed as proving that this income tax causes the liquidity, flexibility or availability losses associated with a different legal classification. The taxpayer's practical financing argument is considered, but these materials do not establish a burden sufficient to overcome Rockford's limit on remote or conjectural impairment. The Court would reject the objection on its actual support, without declaring that taxation can never affect borrowing, finding a numerical economic threshold, or denying the importance of repo markets.

**L5 — Add-back argument, presentation and remedy.** The possible §77-2716(1)(e)(i) add-back for a Seller-Borrower who subtracts federal interest under §77-2716(1)(a) concerns a different taxpayer's deductions and financing expenses. It does not convert the respondent's privately negotiated repo earnings into Treasury interest or require exempting those earnings to avoid a hypothetical separate assessment. The argument may be answered at that level without construing or upholding the add-back provision. The judgment below did not address that provision; no relevant assessment, term-effective full text, respondent-specific application or supported expanded review is supplied. All eight leave its construction, application and validity undecided. Reversal/remand removes the reviewed immunity judgment and the additional grounds advanced to sustain it; it neither determines a tax amount nor resolves a refund, add-back assessment or the unappealed ruling.

## 12. Interstate Commerce Commission v. Transcon Lines — January 10, 1995

**Provisional refreshed disposition:** All eight non-Stone Justices continue to reverse and remand, now with an affirmative conclusion that the specifically requested injunction against the conceded unlawful loss-of-discount liquidated-damages collections is an appropriate enforcement remedy on the supplied record. The lower courts should implement appropriately confined injunctive relief for that challenged class. The remand is not an invitation to reopen the conceded violation or liquidated-damages characterization merely because every invoice is absent. It remains necessary to delimit the operative order and resolve genuine questions about inclusion or amount outside what was conceded. No injunction against ordinary unpaid freight principal or every estate receivable is adopted.

This is a material refinement of the original remedial commitment. The original handoff left general regulation-specific relief dependent on proving the billing violations and stated that no express respondent concession had been supplied. The new neutral handoff establishes the two bounded concessions and identifies the three violations and the exact charge sought to be restrained. The abstract statutory holding remains; remedy suitability is now separately resolved for the admitted class. No historical result or alignment supplies that change.

### Four separate questions

**T1 — Statutory authority.** Sections 10743(b)(1) and 11702(a)(4) authorize public enforcement of valid credit/payment regulations together with, not in displacement of, §§10761–10762's filed-rate commands. Section 11702(a)(4)'s exception concerning rate reasonableness or discriminatory character does not turn this suit over compliance with specified credit rules into a judicial rate-review action. The anti-discrimination purpose of credit regulation is not itself the statutory exception's trigger. All eight retain the original limited statutory conclusion. No constitutional invalidity or independent constitutional issue is presented or decided.

**T2 — Violation and exact requested subject.** The injunction seeks to prevent additional loss-of-discount liquidated damages, not all unpaid freight principal. Expression of damages as the difference between discount and nondiscount filed rates does not alter that admitted character or expand the request. The three established defects are absent original §1320.3(c) warning, revised billing years later rather than within ninety days after authorized-credit expiration under §1320.2(g)(2)(vi), and aggregate balance-due demands on past shipments contrary to §1320.2(g)(2)(iii). No omission of the loss-of-discount term from the filed tariff is alleged. The concession of regulatory violation and the concession of liquidated-damages character govern the challenged collection; they do not concede statutory power, legal entitlement to an injunction, all account amounts, every exception or a separate contractual route.

**T3 — Appropriateness and review of remedy choice.** Statutory power to seek an injunction does not automatically require every requested injunction. Here the Commission has connected its request to stopping the very collection practice violating the credit rules. Its selection is supported rather than arbitrary: the existing liquidated-damages demands were made after the required notice/time protections were lost and through the prohibited aggregation, and permitting collection would realize the advantage the rules forbid. A prospective warning or revised bill cannot make a years-late bill timely or supply the notice required on the original bill. A cease-and-desist or compliance order forbidding these same demands would in substance accomplish the confined collection restraint; an order addressing only future carrier conduct or separate statutory sanctions does not itself prevent these claims from being collected. The mere availability of other sanctions therefore supplies no adequate reason, on the grounds presented, to refuse this precisely matched injunction. Bankruptcy does not create a special equitable exception to tariffs, but neither does it permit collection of a regulatory penalty that the carrier could not lawfully impose.

The court independently determines the statutory boundary, the established violation, the legal character of the requested restraint and the fit of the order it will issue. The Commission's reasoned selection within its enforcement mandate is entitled to respect; judicial preference for a different permissible tool is not, by itself, proof that the selected remedy exceeds that mandate or is inappropriate. Review remains available for an unsupported premise, insufficient explanation, arbitrariness, excessive scope or a remedy inconsistent with the statute. This is not automatic Chevron deference to a litigation request or a holding that any agency demand is binding on an equity court.

ABF's reasoning supplies an analogy for respecting a statutory remedial choice while checking its connection and limits. ABF reviewed a Board remedy under the NLRA; this case asks a court to issue a public-enforcement injunction. No transfer of the Board's precise remedial power, no new universal agency-remedy standard and no surrender of the court's own responsibility is adopted. The conclusion is specific: after examination of the actual demand, conceded defects and proposed alternatives, the Commission's request is a sound exercise of its enforcement discretion and the supplied reasons for denying this injunction do not sustain that denial. The filed-rate argument cannot be reintroduced as an equitable balance that makes enforcement of the credit conditions ineffective.

**T4 — Mandate and what remains below.** Reverse and remand for implementation of an injunction confined to the challenged loss-of-discount liquidated-damages collections concededly violating the stated rules. The lower court may specify the class and operative terms, determine any genuinely disputed inclusion or amount beyond the concessions, and consider a demonstrated distinct lawful collection basis if properly presented. It may not treat the admitted class's regulatory compliance or liquidated-damages character as open simply because the complete billing file was not reproduced here. All ordinary freight principal, collections beyond the identified class, the prior distinct coded-rate component, individual private defenses, other statutory claims, estate distribution and unpresented reform-statute relief remain outside this disposition. No exact dollar award or universal discharge follows.

### Operative regulatory limits retained

The narrower request does not erase any condition of the credit rules. Authorized credit begins the day after bill presentation; the default is fifteen days including weekends and holidays, and permitted combined credit cannot exceed thirty days. The tariff's ninety calendar days after shipment for discount eligibility is distinct from ninety days after authorized-credit expiration for revised billing. Service charges within the standard period are barred; published additional-period charges and published post-expiry charges remain distinct, and the latter neither authorize delay nor remove good-faith reassessment before another extension of credit.

Section 1320.2(g)(1) permits reasonable, certain collection-expense damages by specified dollar/percentage charges or loss of an identified discount after a stated payment date no earlier than authorized-credit expiration. Section (g)(2) requires clear terms, no unlawful prejudice or unjust discrimination, original separate independent bills rather than aggregated past-shipment demands, and the required timely revised bill/notice. It excludes clear clerical/ministerial errors such as nonreceipt, a lost payment check or misaddressing and prohibits duplicate collection fees already provided by the bill of lading for the same transportation. Section (g)(3)'s outside-tariff contractual collection route is distinct, and no entitlement under it is conceded. Section 1320.3(c)'s original bill/accompanying-notice requirements for penalties, time limits, service/collection charges and discount terms are distinct from revised billing. The present conceded violations need not be reproved, but they establish neither an unreported account amount nor satisfaction of an unreported alternative route or exception. The pre-reform shipment regime remains controlling.
