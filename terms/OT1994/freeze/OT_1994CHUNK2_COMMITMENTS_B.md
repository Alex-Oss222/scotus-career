## 17. American Airlines, Inc. v. Wolens — No. 93-1286

**Milestones and posture:** Argued November 1, 1994; proposed merits decision January 18, 1995. Review of the Illinois Supreme Court's interlocutory decision sustaining consumer-fraud and contract damages claims against an ADA preemption defense. The injunction branch is outside the surviving review and remains undisturbed. No breach, enforceable promise, fraud, reliance, causation, or damages has been found.

### Entering authority and the two components

The transaction-period §1305(a)(1) and §1506 govern the pleaded 1988 passenger-award changes. The 1994 recodification supplies no new merits answer. The Alaska exclusion, airport-proprietor provision, and distinct property-transportation qualifications are not factually triggered and are not generalized into airline immunity. The operative register is **Airline rates, routes and services under the express preemption clause**, present operation effective *Morales*, June 1, 1992. It supplies both connection/reference preemption and the separate fare-marketing-package effects ground, with remote-effects and unadjudicated duties preserved. The register **Cigarette-labeling preemption by statutory period and predicate duty**, effective *Cipollone*, June 24, 1992, supplies component-specific promise analysis under different statutory language, not an existing airline-contract holding.

The consumer-fraud branch concerns a state-imposed standard governing the asserted devaluation of earned flight benefits. The contract branch can proceed only to determine and enforce an undertaking the airline actually made. Both directly concern award value and the availability of airline travel; describing frequent-flyer programs as categorically peripheral cannot support either branch.

## 20. Schlup v. Delo — No. 93-7901

**Milestones and posture:** Argued October 3, 1994; proposed merits decision January 23, 1995. Review of the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal of a second federal petition without the requested evidentiary hearing. Cause and prejudice is not pressed in the current review. The questions are the crime-innocence gateway and proper treatment of the submissions, not a general freestanding innocence entitlement or adjudication of Brady/Strickland merits.

### Entering rules retained in their exact roles

*Carrier*, 477 U.S. 478, preserves exceptional access when constitutional error probably caused conviction of an innocent person. *Kuhlmann*, 477 U.S. 436, supplies plurality discussion of fair probability and the whole probative record, not a majority adoption of every phrase. *McCleskey*, 499 U.S. 467, retains an innocence exception to abuse rules and the State's particularized pleading requirement. *Jackson*, 443 U.S. 307, is a trial-record sufficiency inquiry, not automatically the test of a newly supported innocence gateway.

The operative register **Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims**, effective *Sawyer*, June 22, 1992, establishes a clear-and-convincing no-reasonable-juror rule for death eligibility and expressly leaves crime innocence unchanged. A proposed extension here is new law, not application of a previously controlling guilt rule. The simulated *Herrera*, January 25, 1993, establishes only record-specific insufficiency; no whole opinion or uniform freestanding burden controls. The *Delo v. Blair* equal division creates no precedent. *Keeney*, May 4, 1992, retains Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification for necessary factual development of properly presented, available claims; it does not itself excuse successive/abusive barriers. *Brecht*'s preserved-Doyle Chapman holding concerns merits harmlessness, not this gateway. *McFarland* supplies no automatic gateway, hearing, or stay.

**Operative probability formulation and evidence:** The six remand commitments require new reliable evidence supporting factual nonparticipation or innocence, assessed together with all relevant trial and post-trial evidence. The inquiry asks the likely effect on reasonable jurors, rather than whether any inculpatory evidence could legally sustain the original verdict. It is not an evidentiary-admissibility ruling for a new trial and does not automatically ignore relevant material merely because it is impeachment.
