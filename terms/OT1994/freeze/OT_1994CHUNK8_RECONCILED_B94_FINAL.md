## Two paths and rules

A: reverse federal invalidation. In this concrete school-athletic setting, special protective responsibilities, reduced athletic privacy expectations, objective selection, actual drug/safety problem, limited collection and drug information, confidentiality and counseling/athletics-only consequences make this particular suspicionless urinalysis reasonable. TLO allows school warrant/probable-cause relaxation but reserves suspicionless searches; Skinner/VonRaab supply contextual reasonableness, not categorical power over all students. Strongest challenge: children retain bodily privacy; known suspected users could be individually tested and athletics is not a catastrophic-risk job. A does not require a proved catastrophic injury or exhaustion of every alternative, but actual need and safeguards remain necessary.

B: affirm federal invalidation. Reasonable individual suspicion ordinarily remains necessary even at school; this program's observation and intimate informational demands intrude seriously while its suspicionless scope extends to a concededly innocent child. The generalized school/athletic interest does not match the exceptional safety circumstances justifying suspicionless testing in Skinner/VonRaab. Strongest challenge: athlete safety, supervision and neutral selection reduce both privacy expectation and arbitrary intrusion; demanding catastrophic danger imposes a threshold the balancing cases never made universal. B does not forbid school drug testing with reasonable suspicion or decide another district's different record.
