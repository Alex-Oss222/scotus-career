## Common premises that every commitment preserves

The admitted question presents the legal consequence of grave doubt concerning a constitutional trial error's substantial influence. The Sixth Circuit did not find that it was actually in grave doubt. It found the instructions alone constitutional under whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review, assumed a constitutional defect in the combination of the prosecutor's misstatement and possible instructional confusion, and found no substantial injurious influence under a rule placing actual prejudice on O'Neal. Its account of a general Brecht habeas rule is not this Court's entering law.

Brecht holds Chapman applicable to a preserved Doyle violation properly on habeas. It neither adopts Kotteakos generally nor decides that Chapman governs every constitutional collateral claim. Each present extension or refusal to extend is therefore a proposed decision of the open instructional category, not a quotation of already controlling law. Sullivan's structural rule requires a defect vitiating the governing reasonable-doubt standard for all factual findings. Neither an imputation-of-intent allegation nor the assumption below establishes that predicate. No Justice below treats the present error as established or structural.

The prosecutor's statement drew an overruled objection. The source set does not establish every instructional objection, exhaustion presentation, or other preservation predicate. None of these commitments invents a default, treats the State's magistrate-report issue as a prisoner default, or certifies the missing presentations. The harmlessness proposals apply if the constitutional trial claim is properly available; a legally applicable independent habeas barrier retains its own prerequisites. The Court can decide the legal review rule on the posture adjudicated below without finding every predicate for ultimate relief.

The full trial record and original conditional order are unavailable here. No commitment independently finds the combined constitutional violation, determines the error's actual effect, finds personal intent from the appellate summary, reinstates the district court's exact writ, supplies a retrial deadline, or directs acquittal. A new trial or release may follow only from an established violation requiring relief under the applicable rule and only for the conviction or count to which that violation and consequence attach. Murder, robbery and kidnapping are not automatically set aside together. Schlup's innocence gateway and former section 2254(d)'s conditional factual presumption do not answer the separate harmlessness question. Section 2111's review of errors in the habeas proceeding is also distinct from review of the underlying state trial.

## Enhanced Justice-specific assessment

The following records decisive conclusions, authorities, counterarguments and permissible join boundaries. It does not reproduce private deliberation or fictional dialogue. Each Justice separately addresses standard selection, uncertainty allocation and the lawful judgment consequence.

### Stevens

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Stevens would vacate because the lower court's petitioner-burden formulation does not preserve the grave-doubt consequence of the Kotteakos inquiry he would adopt for the present nonstructural instructional/argument claim. His published Brecht dissent expressly favored demanding independent Kotteakos review, the prosecution's obligation to explain harmlessness, and attention to actual influence rather than bare evidentiary sufficiency. That is the strongest direct personal authority. His current-term Schlup opinion supports correction of the legal review rule followed by a bounded remand; it does not transfer the innocence gateway into this case. His join in Williamson Part III confirms that substantial influence and grave doubt are compatible elements of genuine Kotteakos review, while its direct-review setting prevents treating it as dispositive here.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** Chapman is a lawful extension: the present allegation concerns the prosecution's elemental-proof obligation, and Brecht's controlling rationale allocates constitutional-error uncertainty to the State. Its strongest objection to Stevens is the unexplained creation of another collateral category. Stevens nevertheless retains the standard preference he publicly expressed in Brecht. He would apply the less demanding substantial-influence inquiry in this open category as a sufficient protection appropriate to collateral review, while treating the narrower Doyle holding as binding. He does not pretend that an instruction is less constitutionally important or that Brecht already adopted his view. His accepted rule is this category, not a declaration displacing Chapman everywhere.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** The competing petitioner-persuasion rule draws support from the prisoner's request for collateral relief and section 2254(a)'s custody limitation. Stevens rejects it because the State's responsibility to explain harmlessness and Kotteakos's actual-verdict inquiry cannot be reduced to an applicant label. After a constitutional violation is established or properly assumed, an unresolved substantial influence is not an affirmative assurance that the verdict survived that error. Conversely, his rule does not allow relief on mere speculation, dispense with full consideration, or prove the underlying violation. No exact probability burden is invented.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** Affirmance on the instructions-only ground does not resolve the assumed combined violation. The affirmative no-injury language below deserves attention, but does not eliminate the risk that its express petitioner-burden instruction affected the review. Stevens would require renewed Kotteakos consideration without declaring these judges actually doubtful. He joins vacatur and the no-automatic-writ, count-specific and independent-gates limits. He does not join an operative Chapman direction; agreement in vacatur is judgment agreement only at that point. A formulation he can join must require whole-record substantial-influence review with genuine grave doubt preventing harmlessness and preserve the unresolved violation and factual application. A present writ would require sources and findings not supplied.

### O'Connor

**Sufficient ground and scope.** O'Connor would vacate and remand under Chapman. Her Brecht join and separate public reliance on verdict accuracy and the costs of multiplying harmless-error categories directly favor extending the retained rule to this ordinary constitutional trial claim. The alleged permission to substitute another person's intent for O'Neal's own concerns Winship's elemental-proof requirement. The reason for retaining a State harmlessness burden for protected silence does not supply a reason to reduce protection when a charge and an overruled prosecutorial misstatement allegedly relieve the prosecution of that requirement. She accepts a rule for ordinary preserved constitutional trial errors properly on habeas, not solely an error bearing the Doyle name. This is an express extension beyond Brecht's prior holding.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** Kotteakos with its genuine grave-doubt consequence is a serious alternative: collateral review follows a completed trial and state appellate process, and Brecht deliberately left other categories open. O'Connor's earlier concurrence acknowledges that direct and collateral procedures may differ. She nevertheless finds those general institutional considerations insufficient here for a new harmlessness division. The present error, if proved, directly affects verdict accuracy; independent gates and Chapman itself already leave harmless convictions intact. She does not infer that every constitutional claim permits Chapman review or that an ineffective-assistance claimant need not prove the prejudice defining that claim.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** Under her selected Chapman rule, the State must establish noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt; grave uncertainty about substantial effect cannot satisfy it. The opposing contention that every applicant must prove harmful custody confuses proof of the violation and compliance with habeas gates with the subsequent harmlessness defense. Sections 2254(a) and 2243 do not expressly reverse that allocation. Her Greenwich opinion assigns persuasion under a particular enacted APA clause and preserves genuine defense burdens; it is not a general rule placing every issue on anyone seeking judicial relief. Williamson likewise confirms the need to distinguish preservation and review channel rather than assimilating this claim to Olano forfeiture.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** Her Schlup join supports a bounded reconsideration when the governing rule is wrong, without a present factual or ultimate entitlement finding. The contrary affirmance path relies on the lower court's unequivocal language and evidence of principal participation; its defect here is that the court used a materially less demanding legal standard, not that its evidence was necessarily weak. She would vacate for Chapman assessment of the actual combined claim, with a no-error resolution still available under the proper whole-charge rule. She will not join a finding of an established violation, equipoise, Sullivan structural error, a fixed deadline, or invalidation of all three convictions. A narrower Chapman holding confined to this preserved combined claim is acceptable; an operative Kotteakos-only remand is not.

### Scalia

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Scalia would affirm on the legal allocation issue. He would adopt substantial-and-injurious-effect review requiring the petitioner to establish that injury for this non-Doyle instructional/argument category. His join in Kennedy's Brecht dissent supplies his own published rejection of extending direct-review harmlessness to collateral review merely because the error is constitutional. His Schlup joins and separate statutory-refusal opinion show insistence on the distinct requirements of collateral relief, with statutory permissions and exceptions read at their actual scope. None supplies a default or abuse finding here. Brecht's contrary Doyle holding remains law; he would not expand its category and does not purport to overrule it in this proceeding.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** Extending Chapman has substantial force because the error concerns personal intent and Brecht rejects collateral posture alone for Doyle. Scalia's opposing conclusion rests on the still-open category and the limited remedial function of habeas, not on a denial of Winship or a claim that Brecht commands Kotteakos. He would require the petitioner to demonstrate that the assumed constitutional trial defect materially affected the conviction supporting present custody. The strongest objection is that this reasoning would also have supported the unsuccessful Brecht position. He acknowledges that tension, obeys Brecht's exact holding, and advances his published collateral rationale only where that holding left the question undecided.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** Kotteakos itself and Williamson put grave doubt against harmlessness; Scalia joined Williamson's preserved federal direct-review proposition. He does not deny those sources or transplant the APA. He would distinguish a direct appeal's statutory harmless-error inquiry from a collateral applicant's need under section 2254(a) to establish a custody-invalidating constitutional injury. In this open category, the effect terminology is borrowed while its direct-review risk allocation is not. That is a proposed collateral construction, not an express burden sentence in section 2254(a). Section 2243's law-and-justice direction must operate within the custody limitation. The strongest response is that a proved constitutional violation already supplies that custody ground and harmlessness is a State defense. He rejects that response for this ordinary collateral trial-error category because an unproved substantial effect does not establish the requisite injury; he does not impose a separate innocence burden or make all harmlessness questions factual issues governed by former subsection (d).

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** An allocation-correcting remand is unwarranted on his legal view: the Sixth Circuit articulated the rule he would adopt and affirmatively found no injurious influence. That supports affirmance within this limited review without an independent Supreme Court determination of the whole trial's effect. The mere incorrect attribution of that rule to Brecht does not require vacatur if this Court now adopts the rule in the open category. He does not rely on instructions-only validity to resolve the combined claim or announce an independently sufficient Supreme Court harmlessness finding. He cannot join Chapman, a State-risk Kotteakos instruction, or vacatur based solely on rejecting petitioner persuasion. The minimum compatible formulation retains applicant proof of substantial injury, Brecht's existing scope, and all independent structural and gate rules.

### Kennedy

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Kennedy would affirm on the governing standard and allocation. His own Brecht dissent supplies concrete finality, comity and limited-collateral-review grounds for substantial-influence review. His current-term Schlup dissent likewise insists that the type of collateral proceeding matters to the showing required; its clear-and-convincing innocence proposal does not govern this ordinary claim. He would decide the present instructional/argument category by requiring the petitioner to establish a substantial injurious effect, while leaving Brecht's preserved-Doyle rule intact. No broad ruling concerning all constitutional habeas claims, structural errors or claims whose substantive definition includes prejudice is necessary.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** Chapman would give the personal-intent protection at issue at least the remedial protection already afforded Doyle. The strongest objection to Kennedy is that his institutional rationale does not identify a uniquely instructional reduction in the value of the constitutional right. He does not claim such a reduction. He would decline to extend a narrowly controlling rule with which his earlier public dissent disagreed, applying his collateral-review rationale in the category the Court expressly reserved. Whole-record substantial-effect review still tests the actual verdict and cannot be replaced by sufficient evidence of guilt. His previous treatment of cumulative lawful impeachment was Brecht-specific and supplies no present harmlessness fact.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** Genuine doubt against the State is supported by Kotteakos's own formulation and by the argument that the beneficiary of an established constitutional violation should bear the remedial risk. Kennedy instead treats the required substantial injury as part of establishing a basis for this collateral remedy: unresolved causation does not demonstrate that the constitutional trial defect warrants disturbing final custody. Section 2254(a) supports that construction in context, but does not state it expressly. His Greenwich join supports keeping persuasion distinct from proof quantum and respecting a real statutory allocation; it does not make the APA applicable. He expressly distinguishes the legal rule from a demand for proof of innocence or a presumption that all errors are harmless.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** On his view the Sixth Circuit's legal rule is acceptable despite its mistaken attribution to the earlier Supreme Court decision. It affirmatively found no injurious influence, rather than declaring itself unable to decide. He would leave that judgment in place within the supplied legal question; he makes no fresh factual finding and does not use the instructions-only branch to erase the combined claim. A remand merely to find out whether the judges experienced unrecorded doubt is unwarranted under his allocation. He cannot join a remand directing Chapman or relief upon unresolved substantial effect. He can join a narrower affirmance recognizing that no actual grave-doubt finding is before the Court, provided it does not assert that such a finding would require relief under his rule. His Brecht reservation concerning unusually egregious deliberate error or a pattern remains an undecided possibility, not an exception created or found here.

### Souter

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Souter would vacate and remand under Chapman. His Brecht join and his join in White's broader continuity concurrence, with its explicit Powell reservation, are the most direct personal evidence. They support retaining constitutional trial-error protection after direct review where the claim is otherwise available. His proposal extends Chapman to ordinary preserved constitutional trial errors subject to harmlessness on federal habeas; it does not extend to structural defects, erase independent gates, or shift the prejudice element of a different constitutional cause of action. His disagreement with an earlier methodological criticism he did not join is not converted into a present structural-error position.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** The competing Kotteakos rule is lawful in the open category and accommodates finality with meaningful review. Brecht's narrow holding is the strongest objection to treating the present answer as predetermined. Souter therefore states an extension, not an application already required by that holding. Sections 2254(a) and 2243, the constitutional nature of the personal-intent claim, and the continuity supplied by Rose and Yates favor that extension. Rose and Yates are not called square decisions of the later standard-choice question. No legal feature of this alleged relaxation of elemental proof justifies a different harmlessness allocation from the one retained for a properly available Doyle claim.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** Under Chapman the State must demonstrate noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt; a court unable to resolve substantial influence cannot do so. A petitioner-persuasion alternative relies on the custodial remedy and the applicant's ordinary burden. Souter distinguishes the need to establish constitutional error from the State's attempt to preserve the judgment despite it. His Greenwich dissent, joined with Stevens, also shows that quantum and allocation cannot be conflated merely by giving the proceeding a general burden label, but its statutory argument does not supply habeas law. His Williamson join supports actual whole-record harmlessness and the preserved/forfeited distinction at their proper direct-review scope.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** The remand in Schlup, which Souter joined on both its gateway and bounded-refusal grounds, supports correction of a material legal framework without inventing its factual outcome. Here the instructions-only ruling leaves the combined allegation unresolved, and the lower harmlessness analysis used the wrong standard on his proposed rule. He would vacate for a lawful assessment of that combined claim and any necessary remedy for affected counts, not reinstate the conditional grant. An independent affirmance would require a genuinely sufficient, reachable basis, not merely strong evidence. A narrower opinion applying Chapman to this preserved combined personal-intent claim is within his commitment. He does not join an operative petitioner-prejudice rule or a final ruling that the error was structural, harmful or harmless. He can agree with the logical proposition that genuine grave uncertainty defeats Chapman without deciding an abstract Kotteakos question unnecessary to his chosen standard.

### Thomas

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Thomas would affirm on the legal issue. His join in Kennedy's Brecht dissent supports requiring substantial injury in collateral review, and his complete joins in Kennedy's and Scalia's Schlup writings support attention to the statutory and remedial conditions of that review. Their exact limits remain important: neither the successive-petition statute nor its refusal authority governs this record merely because the proceeding is habeas. His proposed rule covers the present non-Doyle instructional/argument trial-error category, assuming it is properly available, and requires the applicant to establish substantial injurious influence. Brecht continues to require Chapman for its preserved-Doyle category.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** Chapman protects an elemental constitutional requirement and follows the rationale of Brecht. The strongest argument for it is that treating the instructional claim differently lacks a distinctive constitutional justification. Thomas would not reduce the substantive personal-intent requirement. He would instead decline to enlarge the earlier holding and apply the limited collateral-remedy approach he already joined to the category left open. That rationale is a proposed resolution here, not current binding law. If the whole reasonable-doubt framework were actually vitiated, Sullivan would present a different structural question; that predicate is not supplied and is not assumed away.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** Kotteakos's direct-appeal method would deny harmlessness when influence remains in grave doubt. The strongest response to Thomas's rule is that borrowing substantial influence while discarding this consequence takes only part of that method, and section 2254(a) says nothing express about the allocation. Thomas accepts the need to justify the difference: collateral relief concerns unlawful present custody after completed criminal review, so an applicant in this category must establish the substantial injury that makes the ordinary trial error a ground for upsetting the judgment. Section 2243 does not create an unrestricted remedy independent of that ground. The position rests on this construction and the published collateral approach, not a false claim that the statute expressly says the petitioner loses ties, that subsection (d) governs legal harmlessness, or that Greenwich's APA sentence governs habeas.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** On that legal rule there is no allocation defect requiring renewed review. The Sixth Circuit's stated rule and its affirmative no-influence conclusion support leaving the judgment in place within the accepted question; Thomas does not independently reconstruct the trial or resolve the assumed violation. A competing vacatur could correct the lower opinion's inaccurate description of Brecht, but attribution alone does not require remand if the legal rule is now adopted for the open category. He cannot join a State-risk or Chapman remand instruction. A narrow affirmance confined to rejection of this legal challenge is acceptable; a claimed independently sufficient Supreme Court no-error or harmlessness determination would require more source support and is not supplied by this commitment.

### Ginsburg

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Ginsburg would vacate for Chapman review of the present properly available, preserved combined personal-intent claim. Her public Victor/Sandoval joins establish insistence on the actual whole charge and the prosecution's elemental-proof requirement, while stopping short of an additional explanation she did not join. Her Greenwich join distinguishes substantive proof quantum, allocation and genuine defense burdens. Her Schlup join supports a legally correct, bounded remand without an ultimate merits finding. None is a direct prior vote on habeas harmlessness. Her present commitment is a reasoned extension from Brecht's existing rule and rationale to this closely related constitutional verdict-protection claim, not an attribution of Blackmun's position to his former colleague or of an unverified circuit opinion to her.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** A Kotteakos rule could preserve meaningful protection while respecting finality and the expressly open category. Its strongest objection to Chapman is that Brecht did not decide this claim and different collateral rules may lawfully serve different functions. Ginsburg would nevertheless require a concrete reason for reducing the State's remedial burden for a claim that the jury need not find this defendant's requisite intent. The supplied record and statutory text provide no such distinction beyond collateral status, which Brecht rejected as sufficient for its category. She accepts a claim-specific extension; she does not now endorse a universal rule for every constitutional collateral claim.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** Greenwich's proponent rule might appear to put risk on the person asking for a writ, but it depended on an express APA allocation and retained genuine affirmative defenses. Section 2254(a) contains no comparable risk-allocation clause. On her selected rule, O'Neal must establish the constitutional violation and satisfy applicable gates; the State then bears Chapman's noncontribution showing. Williamson Part III, which she joined, also distinguishes the preserved error's harmlessness inquiry from Olano forfeiture. Its direct-review rule supports the analytical distinction, not an automatic habeas answer. Genuine grave uncertainty defeats beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness; neither the label applicant nor a sufficiency finding resolves that uncertainty.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** The appellate no-error holding concerned instructions alone, while the combined claim was assumed and tested under a less demanding rule. Ginsburg would remand that combined issue for the correct framework without holding that each instruction was preserved, that a constitutional violation occurred, or that actual equipoise existed. She would not require unnecessary factual proceedings if the lower court can lawfully resolve the matter on an adequate record. A narrow opinion covering the actual combined allegation, independent gates and count-specific consequences is the minimum sufficient formulation she accepts. She withholds a join from a sweeping all-habeas rule, an automatic writ, a Kotteakos-only direction, or a structural classification unsupported by the full charge. Her vacatur vote therefore supports the stated limited Chapman remand, not every instruction that other vacating Justices might favor.

### Breyer

**Sufficient ground and scope.** Breyer would vacate and remand under a limited Chapman extension for this properly available, preserved personal-intent instructional/argument claim. Anderson is his own date-eligible writing, but concerns Strickland: it insists on proof of unreasonable representation and the prejudice constituting that violation on an adequate record, rather than a categorical inference from a scant appellate presentation. It does not decide the allocation after an independently established constitutional trial error. His Schlup join likewise supports choosing the correct legal question and leaving actual evidentiary application to the receiving court. His present standard choice rests on those limited procedural commitments together with Brecht's existing constitutional-error rule, the present elemental-proof allegation and the statutory absence of a different express allocation. It is an inference, not a claimed prior Breyer holding directly resolving O'Neal.

**Alternative standard paths and strongest objections.** Adopting substantial-influence review with the Kotteakos grave-doubt consequence would recognize finality while requiring meaningful independent review. That is the strongest competing path, especially because Brecht expressly reserved other categories. Breyer would nevertheless use the already retained Chapman framework for this trial claim rather than add a second constitutional harmlessness category without a claim-specific justification. An instruction and prosecutor's remark permitting substitution of someone else's intent bear directly on the reliability of this defendant's verdict. He does not infer a complete constitutional violation from those allegations. He accepts only the current class and leaves a general all-trial-errors extension undecided.

**Alternative uncertainty paths.** The State can invoke Anderson's insistence that a habeas petitioner demonstrate prejudice, but Strickland prejudice is part of the substantive violation there. Treating it as the universal post-violation harmlessness burden would erase the distinction Anderson actually applies. Breyer would require proof of the present constitutional defect before ultimate relief, while leaving the State to establish harmlessness under the selected Chapman rule. Section 2254(a)'s custody restriction and section 2243's remedial instruction can operate together with that allocation; neither expressly supplies the opposite burden. Under Chapman genuine grave uncertainty prevents harmlessness. Mere incomplete research, a thin appellate summary or speculation about influence does not itself establish that a properly reviewing court has reached that state.

**Remedy alternatives, join barrier and minimum formulation.** Anderson strongly cautions against ordering a writ or deciding actual prejudice from this source set, while Schlup supports a remand under a corrected legal rule. The contrary affirmance path relies on the appellate court's affirmative no-injury assessment and the possibility that the same result would follow under Chapman. Breyer would not decide that possibility from a summary rather than the proper record and governing standard. He would remand for an adequate assessment of the combined claim, allowing resolution of the violation before harmlessness or an express assumption followed by correct harmlessness review. Any further factual development requires the receiving court's reasoned determination under the applicable rules; neither a hearing nor a writ follows automatically. He can join the narrow Chapman direction and the common remedial limits. A general harmlessness code, a present all-count remedy, or an asserted factual finding of equipoise exceeds this commitment; a Kotteakos-only direction is not his selected ground.

## Proposition and remedy boundaries for reconciliation

- O'Connor and Souter support the defined ordinary-preserved-constitutional-trial-error extension of Chapman. Ginsburg and Breyer support Chapman only for the present preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category. All four support that narrower proposition, but no general Court holding or vote threshold is inferred without assembly.
- Stevens supports the same vacatur at judgment level on a different standard. He does not supply a Chapman join. His grave-doubt rule under Kotteakos and the four Chapman positions share the rejection of a legal rule letting the State obtain a harmlessness finding despite genuine unresolved substantial influence in this category; that overlap does not decide which standard controls or make an actual-doubt finding.
- Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance through a proposed collateral standard and allocation for the open non-Doyle category. Their votes are not independent findings that the combined violation never occurred or was factually harmless under every possible standard. Each rejects a remand based solely on rejection of petitioner persuasion.
- No Justice supplies an assignment, a final author, a current Stone position, a new trial deadline, an actual equipoise finding, a ruling that the original conditional writ must be reinstated, or an automatic disposition of all counts. A reviewing court may resolve a properly presented no-error ground; the present legal remand votes do not command it to decide error first if a lawful assumption and correct harmlessness disposition suffice.
