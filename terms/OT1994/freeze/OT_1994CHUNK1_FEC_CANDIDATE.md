## 6. Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund


### SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

- **Case Name:** Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund
- **Citation or Docket:** 513 U.S. 88; No. 93-1151.
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits decision in the established MERITS channel.
- **Simulated Event Date:** 1994-12-06.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the FEC possessed independent statutory authority to petition for certiorari; whether Congress violated separation of powers by designating the Secretary of the Senate and Clerk of the House as nonvoting ex officio members of the FEC; and, only if the arrangement was unconstitutional, what consequences followed for the enforcement action.
- **Legal Field:** Federal courts, administrative structure, election administration, litigating authority, and separation of powers
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit reversed the district court’s enforcement judgment. It held that Congress’s placement of its agents on an independent commission exercising executive enforcement power violated separation of powers, held the ex officio provision severable, and entered judgment for respondents without deciding the underlying FECA violation.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The FEC asks the Court to exercise jurisdiction, reverse the constitutional judgment, and return the unresolved FECA dispute for adjudication. Respondents seek affirmance of the judgment ending the action.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The FEC brought a civil enforcement action concerning a transfer of corporate funds to the NRA Political Victory Fund. The Commission consisted of six presidentially appointed voting members and the Secretary of the Senate and Clerk of the House, or their designees, as ex officio members without a vote. The ex officio members could not serve as chair, call or adjourn a meeting, or count toward a quorum, and the record identifies no veto, binding directive, or other legally operative authority over Commission action. After the D.C. Circuit’s judgment, the Commission excluded the congressional officers and purported to ratify pending matters. The legal relevance of ratification depends on the threshold and merits grounds lawfully reached.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The FEC’s authority to invoke Supreme Court jurisdiction is antecedent to the separation-of-powers question. Determine the scope of § 437d(a)(6), the Solicitor General’s authority, timeliness and any attempted ratification before reaching the merits. The merits and severability paths remain conditional on review authority and an established constitutional defect.

## Other governing law

### Issue 1: Independent litigating authority

- **Other Governing Law:** 2 U.S.C. §§ 437c(b)(1), 437d(a)(6); 26 U.S.C. §§ 9010(d), 9040(d); 28 U.S.C. §§ 516, 518(a), 2101(c); 28 C.F.R. § 0.20.
- **Other Authority Used:** Federal Election Commission v. National Conservative Political Action Committee confirms the Commission’s exclusive civil-enforcement role. Section 518(a) itself uses the language of “suits and appeals” to describe Supreme Court litigation, and ordinary appellate usage informs the meaning of “appeal any civil action.”
- **Open Point:** The statute does not separately define “appeal” or expressly name a petition for certiorari in § 437d(a)(6).

### Issue 2: Nonvoting congressional officers on an executive commission

- **Other Governing Law:** U.S. Const. arts. I and II; Buckley v. Valeo; Bowsher v. Synar; Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority v. Citizens for the Abatement of Aircraft Noise, Inc.
- **Other Authority Used:** Buckley, Bowsher, and the prior Airports Authority decision forbid Congress or its agents from exercising legally operative executive authority outside the constitutionally prescribed structure. They do not establish a constitutional prohibition on communication, observation, consultation, or persuasion as such.
- **Open Point:** Whether the ex officio officers possessed any legally operative authority beyond attendance and advice.
