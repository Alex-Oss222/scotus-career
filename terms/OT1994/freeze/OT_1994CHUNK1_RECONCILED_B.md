## 5. Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, No. 93-1062

**Event and cutoff:** Established merits proceeding; argument October 11, 1994; decision December 5, 1994. No Supreme Court merits comparator or historical Justice vote exists to import. The source anchor is the September 30, 1993 Eleventh Circuit judgment, before RFRA's November 16, 1993 enactment.

### Reconciled disposition and scope

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur in part and remand to the Eleventh Circuit for RFRA adjudication of the Church's rejected prospective-relief claims: the identifying disclosures designated in the lower holding as §100.03(1)(a), (b), (c) and (j), and the contemporaneous written-refund-policy requirement, §100.05(1)(g). Preserve the existing purpose remand and relief already favorable to the Church; no established City cross-petition authorizes enlarging the City's rights. Necessary district-court proceedings must determine actual statutory burden and justification. This is a reasoned statutory decision with a bounded remand, not an unexplained GVR or new grant.

RFRA applies to threatened future municipal implementation, including implementation of a law enacted before RFRA. The Church must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The City must then produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. General anti-fraud interests do not establish that application by themselves. Ordinary Article III standing and appropriate relief remain required. Section 7 leaves the Establishment Clause unaffected. No Justice is committed to RFRA's constitutionality, retrospective monetary liability, a general religious exemption, or a final application outcome.

The express temporal text and future-enforcement setting satisfy both the controlling Landgraf framework and the narrower activity-regulated approach preserved by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. A common opinion may state this agreement without giving those three a new join in Stevens's entire Landgraf methodology. Current Lukumi and Kiryas Joel do not eliminate the substantial-burden inquiry or establish this City's disputed predominant purpose.

### Common dismissal ground, preserved in six commitments

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support dismissal for failure of an authorized petitioner timely to invoke Supreme Court review. Section 437d(a)(6)'s enforcement-litigation power does not supersede §518(a)'s specific Supreme Court representation allocation. Enforcement exclusivity and §437c(f)(4)'s defense authority do not resolve this affirmative petition's authority.

This is ordinary contextual statutory construction, not a mandatory verbal formula for exceptions or a dictionary rule that every use of appeal excludes certiorari. Providence Journal separates authority to litigate below from representation here while recognizing Congress's ability to create exceptions. ## 7. Reich v. Collins, No. 93-908

**Event and cutoff:** Argument October 11, 1994; decision December 6, 1994. The official comparator confirms unanimous reversal and remand and O'Connor's historical authorship. It confirms the narrow distinction between a State's choice of a clear remedial system and withdrawing an apparently available postpayment remedy after payment. The historical authorship is not imported.

### Reconciled rule and mandate

All eight participating non-Stone Justices support reversing the denial of constitutionally adequate backward-looking relief and remanding. A State may provide meaningful prepayment review, adequate postpayment relief or both, and may clearly reconfigure those routes prospectively. Where the State held out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, due process forbids withdrawing that remedy after payment for the very constitutional invalidity asserted and then invoking failure to use a different prepayment route.

Section 48-2-35(a)'s broad refund promise and its subsequent restrictive construction supply the setting. The state court controls state-law meaning; the federal defect is the unfair change in remedial availability, not a federal rewrite of the statute. The substantive tax invalidity is already accepted. McKesson and actual Harper require meaningful lawful correction while preserving lawful refunds, actual nondiscriminatory retroactive assessments or their constitutionally adequate combination. Neither an automatic refund amount nor reopening of final cases follows.

The adequacy of each disputed prepayment mechanism is unnecessary to this ground. Even assuming adequate prepayment review, its existence does not make the represented postpayment route disappear. The historical opinion expressly adopts that assumption. Assembly must not turn the packet's bond, administrative competence and stay disputes into separate adjudicated defects. Equally, no permissive stay is a mandatory stay and no state-court description of remedies as ample is an enacted guarantee.

## 8. Brown v. Gardner, No. 93-1128

**Event and cutoff:** Argument October 31, 1994; decision December 12, 1994. The official comparator confirms unanimous affirmance and Souter's historical authorship; neither authorship nor unexamined ancillary reasoning is assigned by that fact.

### Reconciled rule and mandate

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support affirming invalidation of §3.358(c)(3)'s added fault-or-accident condition, with the lower remand for administrative eligibility redetermination continuing. Section 1151 requires injury or aggravation resulting from the covered VA services and consequent additional disability or death, excluding the veteran's own willful misconduct. Causal connection does not authorize a further demand for negligent or otherwise faulty care, or an unforeseen untoward accident. Actual causation, covered services, additional disability/death and claimant willful misconduct remain distinct conditions. The FTCA coordination sentence concerns withholding benefits against a qualifying recovery; it is not a provider-fault element, and no such recovery is established here.

Text and context, supported by the actual King rule and the limits on agency delegation in actual MCI, reject the additional condition. Administrative duration, costs, ambiguous hearing examples, reenactment and legislative silence do not overcome that reading. Chevron is applied at the statutory-meaning stage, not displaced generally. A merely dictionary-possible meaning does not create statutory ambiguity, and a veterans-protective canon does not replace the enacted conditions.

### Historical qualifications checked

The historical footnote at 513 U.S. 119 n.3 expressly leaves the natural-progress regulation undisturbed and explains that VA action is not the cause of disability in the described natural-progression setting. It also gives the necessary consequence of a consented amputation as an example against boundless compensation. The frozen commitments already preserve actual causation, reject payment for every outcome following treatment and withhold adjudication of a complete consent/necessary-consequences doctrine. At that scope there is no contrary holding: the existing natural-progress provision is undisturbed, not invalidated or affirmatively placed in doubt. The opinion must not suggest that consented necessary consequences are automatically compensable. It likewise must not invent a comprehensive consent rule, medical finding or benefits award that neither the neutral record nor the committed disposition requires. A broader operative rule on those matters needs fresh neutral verification of the pertinent historical CFR and record.

The historical opinion at 121 n.4 leaves disputed whether the VA used a fault rule at the time of 1934 reenactment. The lower opinion separately identifies the definitive restrictive administrative stance in August 1934, after March reenactment. The frozen reference to that definitive stance can retain its precise source attribution; it must not become a new finding that no earlier practice ever embodied fault. The common sufficient ground is the statute's incompatibility with the added condition. No independent Supreme Court adjudication of every historical administrative practice is needed.
