## Qualification checks against the preserved Record

1. **Evidence reached different courts at different times.** Petitioner’s brief, printed pages 10 and 18, identifies Faherty’s affidavit as submitted and considered on appeal rather than before the District Court. Respondent confirms the first appellate submission at printed page 38, note 19. Green’s affidavit accompanied the Rule 59(e) reconsideration request; respondent’s note 8 and printed pages 36–37 likewise describe piecemeal presentation. The Record describes contested later submissions and orders an assessment on remand; it does not say every affidavit was before the District Court at its initial dismissal or create a new finding about admission or preservation.

2. **The two timing accounts remain contested.** Petitioner’s appended Faherty affidavit, App. 2–6, estimates the duration of the encounter and radio call and supplies his reasons for excluding Schlup’s participation. Respondent’s printed pages 31–40 contests those estimates through prior testimony, inmate accounts, the sequence of movements, and the two identifications. Respondent’s printed page 32 and note 14 emphasize prior familiarity with Schlup; page 33 and note 15 discuss the alleged scratches and the explanation for O’Neal’s bleeding. These are adversarial submissions, not new credibility determinations. They support the Record’s whole-record assessment and its refusal to treat an affidavit, videotape or prior identification as mechanically decisive.

3. **Later investigation is not automatically part of this judicial record.** Petitioner’s printed pages 14 and 19, including notes 18 and 23–24, describe later investigation, a polygraph, clemency proceedings and contemplated Rule 60(b) submissions. Respondent’s printed page 34, note 16, disputes the piecemeal presentation. Exhibit A itself marks some witness statements as outside the lower-court record and others as not yet located. The preserved Record does not adopt those items as adjudicated facts, presume their proper admission, or create a judicial stay from the clemency proceeding.

4. **The State’s alternative merits contention was not absent from the complete brief.** Respondent’s printed pages 11–15 argues that the courts below rejected underlying constitutional claims in addition to applying procedural barriers. This matches the source qualification already carried through the refreshed packet, final reconciliation and Record: the extra merits discussion is acknowledged but is not converted into an independently established final resolution of every current claim. Petitioner and respondent describe aspects of the first District Court ruling differently; the Record rests on the established earlier appellate legal-merits consideration and does not resolve that party dispute by invention. No new claim-by-claim classification or sufficient independent merits ground is established by this assembly review.

5. **Distinct legal questions remain distinct.** Respondent’s printed pages 29–30 expressly disclaims a trial-record-only Jackson sufficiency test and argues instead for credible, noncumulative and admissible evidence under its reading of Sawyer. That argument does not eliminate the Record’s distinction between trial sufficiency and the augmented whole-record gateway. Petitioner’s invocation of subsection (a)’s ends-of-justice language does not insert that wording into subsection (b); the Record uses the verified former subsection (b), including both hearing alternatives and both conjunctive unless clauses. The briefs also confirm that cause and prejudice is not the ground now pressed. Neither party’s requested outcome supplies an adjudicated innocence finding or an automatic entitlement to a hearing, writ, retrial or release.
