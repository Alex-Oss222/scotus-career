**Certification routes and limits verified in competing submissions:** The SCC itself (JA24) accepts SBA or state highway certification; another governmental agency, county or city certification may be accepted individually only if the contracting officer finds an acceptable and viable DBE certification program. This qualification cannot become automatic acceptance. The contract requires acceptable certification evidence and a certified copy of the executed subcontract when compensation is requested. Respondent brief pp.13?16, particularly n.16, distinguishes SBA's rebuttable social-disadvantage presumption from the DOT state-certification presumption of both social and economic disadvantage. SBA's separate economic inquiry considers personal finances, business finances and access to capital/credit; neither minority membership alone nor a small-business label establishes economic disadvantage under that route. DOT's presumption of both disadvantages remains rebuttable; any third party may challenge actual social or economic disadvantage. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Petitioner brief pp.22?24 argues that initial presumptions in practice avoid investigations and create unequal certification burdens. That is an adversarial contention; it does not prove irrebuttability or convert the SBA social presumption into an economic one. Respondent brief pp.33?34 n.25 expressly observes that petitioner did not identify the actual certification process and argues a facial-challenge consequence. That sourced party assertion supports preserving route uncertainty without asserting that no source anywhere identifies it. The JA declarations establish economic inducement and lost bid, not the particular certification procedure used for Gonzales. No route-specific invalidation or finding Gonzales lacked genuine disadvantage follows from this review.

**Incentive versus command:** The appellate court distinguishes SCC's optional monetary inducement for this small-business prime contractor from a mandatory state-recipient set-aside or subcontracting-plan duty. Proffit's JA30?31 declaration says the prime felt economically compelled and would otherwise have accepted Adarand's low bid. Economic motivation is record evidence, not a legal contractual obligation. The SCC JA25?26 conditions compensation on a DBE subcontract total exceeding 10% of the original prime contract; the amount is 10% of final approved DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE or 2% for two or more. Payment covers the assistance and documentation duties stated in the contract. The clause permits adjustment for work sent to non-DBE second-tier subcontractors, confines compensation to DBE-performed work, and permits full compensation when only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. The present ruling about a racial certification preference does not hold these assistance duties imaginary or say every compensation dollar is an unearned bonus. The existence of a rebuttable preference remains sufficient to present scrutiny despite those limits.
