## 10. In re Baby K — December 12, 1994

### Reconciled common result and grounds

All eight frozen non-Stone commitments are accepted without change: affirm the prospective EMTALA emergency-treatment obligation at the presented scope. Every condition in the frozen commitment remains operative. Stabilization uses reasonable medical probability of no material deterioration resulting from or during transfer. An unstabilized transfer requires informed written request or benefits-over-risks certification; the qualified-medical-person alternative requires consultation and later physician countersignature when no physician is physically present. Appropriate transfer also requires available risk-minimizing care, receiving capacity and acceptance, required available records/request/certification and on-call identification, qualified transport with needed life support, and applicable Secretary requirements. The record's lack of a certification and accepting facility are distinct obstacles; a future compliant transfer remains permissible. Refusal provisions require the offered care or compliant transfer, information, refusal, and reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal; opposition alone does not satisfy them. The conditional receiving-hospital branch retains both specialized capability and capacity, with an appropriate transfer requiring those capabilities.

Virginia's quoted article concerns adult directives/surrogates and does not itself establish an infant-care exemption. Section 1395dd(f) displaces a state requirement only if it applies and directly conflicts.
