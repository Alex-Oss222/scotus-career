## Shared disposition and controlling proposition proposed for circulation

All eight associates reach the authorized commerce question. Each would reverse the Ninth Circuit's reversal of Count Six on its enterprise-commerce ground and remand for the unresolved RICO appellate grounds and any properly pending RICO sentencing issue. None votes to declare Robertson finally guilty on all RICO grounds, impose a Guidelines sentence, revive acquitted or dismissed counts, or undo the separate vacatur of sentences on affirmed Counts One–Four. The remedial distinction is part of each commitment.

**P1 — Direct enterprise commerce:** A mining enterprise is engaged in interstate commerce under §1962(a) when its own establishment and operation include obtaining goods or workers across state lines and interstate travel undertaken for the mine's work. Those actual interstate enterprise transactions do not cease to be commerce because the mine is small, most equipment is obtained locally, or its ordinary gold sales are to local refiners; an individual substantial-effects showing is not a prerequisite to the direct-engagement alternative.

**P2 — Application:** On the verified record, the mine's interstate procurement, worker recruitment and operation-related movement suffice to defeat the panel's classification of all interstate contacts as legally incidental. The independent, adequate worker/operation ground does not depend on contested continued Cadillac use, airplane attribution, an assumed out-of-state sale, an exact procurement amount, downstream refining, or an export fraction. The approximately $30,000 of output carried from Alaska corroborates actual movement of mine-produced goods, but no additional holding is made that every proprietor's removal of personally retained enterprise output necessarily constitutes enterprise commerce. That attribution issue is unnecessary to the sufficient procurement-and-work ground.

**P3 — Limited relation to constitutional and statutory alternatives:** American Building Maintenance, 422 U.S. 271, 283–286 (1975), supplies the distinction between direct participation in interstate goods/services transactions and local purchases after interstate flow has ended. Its different statutory setting is acknowledged. Actual goods and labor obtained across state lines here satisfy the narrower direct-engagement route; broad construction of the separate affecting-commerce alternative is unnecessary. Lopez's stand-alone gun-possession/general social-cost holding supplies no immunity for actual interstate enterprise activity. Harris preserves economic-class regulation but does not replace the proof of this mine's enacted commerce condition. RICO predicates alone cannot satisfy the enterprise's separate condition. No new management, profit-intent, or individual substantial-effect element is inserted into subsection (a).

**P4 — Remedy:** Reverse only the challenged commerce-based Count Six disposition and remand. The six unresolved RICO grounds remain for the Court of Appeals: special verdict, venue, severance, Agent Devetko testimony, CJA funds concerning the Cadillac owner, and vagueness. The unresolved RICO Guidelines-straddle question remains unresolved. Counts One–Four stay affirmed as convictions and subject to their independent Rule 32(c)(3)(D) actual-resentencing mandate. Counts Five/Eight acquittals and Count Seven dismissal remain unaffected.

P1–P4 have eight provisional direct joins at their stated generality, not an inferred composite of distinct grounds. The controlling opinion need not take a position on full economic-class aggregation, all RICO applications, the complete constitutional ceiling, the independent vagueness claim, or the nugget-attribution issue to maintain those joins.

## Why the competing insufficiency path fails on this packet

The mine is not classified as engaged in commerce because Alaska is geographically separated, because gold has a national price, because drugs crossed state lines, or because any useful machine once came from elsewhere. The evidence instead describes this enterprise obtaining workers and supplies across boundaries and work-related interstate movement. A distinction between establishment and operation cannot erase those transactions: subsection (a) expressly addresses establishment, and operation-related movement is independently verified. Majority-local equipment and local sales do not make the remaining direct transactions fictitious. American Building Maintenance's concern that local purchasers buy after interstate flow has ended does not control actual out-of-state procurement and labor movements. The narrower objection about retained output's private attribution remains viable but cannot defeat the independently sufficient work/procurement ground. No Justice's reversal depends on resolving that objection against respondent.
