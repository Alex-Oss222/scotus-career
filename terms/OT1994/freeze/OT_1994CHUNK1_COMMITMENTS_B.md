## 5. Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, No. 93-1062

### Committed disposition and sufficient statutory ground

Each of the eight participating non-Stone Justices supports **vacatur in part and remand for RFRA adjudication of the Church's rejected prospective-relief claims**, leaving undisturbed the existing remand on disputed purpose and the portions of the judgment favorable to the Church that no established cross-petition places before this Court for enlargement of the City's rights. Specifically, the adverse determinations concerning the identifying disclosures designated below as §100.03(1)(a), (b), (c), and (j), and the written-refund-policy provision, §100.05(1)(g), must be reconsidered under RFRA. The mandate is to the Eleventh Circuit for such further district-court proceedings as the statutory burden and justification inquiries require. This is a reasoned statutory merits/remedial disposition, not an unexplained GVR and not a new grant of review.

RFRA §§3, 5 and 6 govern threatened future municipal implementation after November 16, 1993, including implementation of an older ordinance. The Church must establish a substantial burden on its religious exercise. If it does, the City bears both production and persuasion that this application to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The availability of ordinary fraud prevention is relevant but does not discharge those burdens abstractly. Section 3(c)'s ordinary standing and appropriate-relief limits remain. Section 7 preserves the separate Establishment Clause. This case does not decide RFRA's constitutionality, retrospective monetary liability, a later amendment, or a post-1994 limitation.

The prospective application follows the statute's express implementation language and the existing Landgraf distinction. The relevant act being restrained is future enforcement, not the 1984 enactment as a completed transaction. Neither the Court's general Landgraf framework nor Scalia's distinct activity-regulated approach bars that application. The lower court had decided before RFRA existed. The supplied record does not establish the substantial-burden and application-specific justification findings for every remaining provision. Reconsideration therefore does not predetermine entitlement to an exemption.

The mandate **does not affirm the lower court's constitutional reasoning** merely by leaving favorable unchallenged relief undisturbed. It does not authorize renewed enforcement of provisions set aside below; direct repayment, damages, or a whole-ordinance injunction; turn the continuing purpose remand into a purpose finding; or set the unresolved Florida severability boundary. The unreviewed general anti-fraud clause, hardship-discretion challenge and unpreserved procedural claims receive no new adjudication. An independently established cross-petition or expanded claim would require a fresh neutral scope handoff before a different mandate could enlarge the City's relief. Its absence is not a blocker to this bounded disposition.

### Independent authority: two-path comparison

The six dismissing Justices adopt this sufficient construction: §437d(a)(6)'s authority to initiate, defend in the specified §437g(a)(8) setting, or appeal a civil enforcement action in the Commission's own name through its general counsel gives the FEC its enforcement-litigation role. It does not displace §518(a)'s specific allocation of Supreme Court representation. Sections 9010(d) and 9040(d), by contrast, expressly authorize appeals and certiorari in the actions to which those funding sections apply. This enforcement case concerns FECA corporate contributions and is not such a funding action. Section 437c(f)(4)'s defense authority and the Commission's exclusive enforcement jurisdiction do not convert this affirmative certiorari petition into an authorized Supreme Court appearance.

**Strongest agency argument:** “appeal any civil action” can naturally include discretionary Supreme Court review; §518 itself uses suits and appeals although Providence Journal applies it to certiorari. Congress need not use prescribed words to create an exception, and the independent Commission's exclusive enforcement responsibility makes review of an adverse judgment integral to its task. **Answer at the adopted scope:** the result does not rest on a universal dictionary rule that appeal excludes certiorari or a magic-words requirement. Read as a scheme, general enforcement authority can coexist with the specific Supreme Court representation arrangement, while the express funding provisions have their own confined work. Providence Journal establishes the distinction between authority to litigate below and authority to represent the United States here; its footnote 9 preserves ordinary statutory construction of an actual exception. Neither the nominal Commission party nor enforcement exclusivity eliminates the United States' enforcement interest. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains real and available; it is not rewritten as an absolute prohibition.

Stevens need not resolve the full independent-authority question against the agency to sustain his preferred procedural result. His committed narrower route assumes that §518 required approval and holds that the later fullest authorization can cure the timely filing. He does not join the six-Justice construction merely because that assumption permits the alternative inquiry. He remains willing to consider the agency's broad textual reading, but makes no independent certiorari-authority holding unnecessary to his chosen route.

### Later authorization: two-path comparison

**Common factual premise, not new finding:** the FEC filed January 18, 1994; the Solicitor General authorization is dated May 26. The Court can assume for this legal test that the later authorization adopted the petition to the fullest extent the FEC claims. No quotation, earlier permission, scope limitation, concession, new filing, extension, rehearing or period reset is invented. This assumption avoids deciding an unnecessary question about the unproduced letter's fine wording.

**Six-Justice ground:** §2101(c) requires a civil certiorari application within ninety days, with a Justice permitted for good cause to extend that period by at most sixty days. A proper principal must have capacity to perform the act both at the initial act and at ratification under Cook v. Tullis. By May 26 the Solicitor General could not initiate review within either the ordinary period or even the outer hypothetical statutory extension. An unauthorized application cannot acquire timely governmental authorization after that point by calling the action ratification. The relation-back principle does not remove its own capacity limitation. Dodge allows governmental adoption of an unauthorized seizure where the relevant custody and forfeiture premises existed; it does not authorize circumvention of an expired appellate period. Smith v. Barry allows a timely authorized litigant's document to supply mandatory notice information regardless of title, not a later decision by the lawful government representative to invoke review after time has expired.

On October 22 as the judgment date, January 18 is day 88, January 20 day 90, and May 26 day 216. Even an additional sixty days would end March 21. Taking October 25 as a hypothetical alternative starting event would yield January 23 and March 24, also before May 26. No determination about amendment restart, weekend rules or an actually granted extension is needed because none of these alternatives approaches May 26. The omission of an original extension paper is not affirmative proof of misconduct; the bounded legal holding assumes the strongest extension consistent with §2101 and still rejects this cure.

**Strongest contrary route, adopted by Stevens:** §2101 requires the application itself to be timely, and it was. It does not expressly command the Solicitor General's approval within that period. Authorization now adopts the same petition, parties and judgment; it neither gives the respondent a later case to defend nor makes the Court entertain an application first lodged after the deadline. Cook's relation-back principle and Dodge's governmental adoption support treating representation as curable, and Smith v. Barry warns against adding an unnecessary jurisdictional condition beyond the enacted and rule-based requirements. Under this view the relevant act the Solicitor General performs at ratification is adoption of an already timely pending application, which remains within his authority; Cook's capacity qualification does not equate that act with filing a new late petition. Providence Journal involved absence of authorization, not actual subsequent approval of the filing.

**Strongest objection to Stevens's route:** this construction permits an agency to occupy the Court's docket without authorized executive approval and makes the representative's later act evade the deadline. **His limiting answer:** only the same timely petition for the same government interest can be adopted, and actual approval is necessary; neither an amicus expressing agreement nor the agency's own wish to litigate suffices. The precise petition, timing and fullest-authorization premise matter. The rule does not extend the deadline for a new application, presume authorization, or validate the Commission's original enforcement conduct. This remains a distinct narrower procedural position, not a majority holding.

## 7. Reich v. Collins, No. 93-908

### Committed disposition, rule and competing path

All eight participating non-Stone Justices support **reversing the denial of a constitutionally adequate backward-looking remedy and remanding**. Georgia may ordinarily choose adequate prepayment review instead of a refund system. But where its law holds out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including taxes paid voluntarily, it may not after payment withdraw that route by construing it to exclude the very constitutional invalidity asserted and rely instead on the taxpayer's failure to pursue a different prepayment route. Due process requires a clear and certain remedial system at the time a taxpayer must choose how to contest payment. This is not a holding that taxpayers may ignore announced reasonable procedural requirements.

The quoted §48-2-35(a) promises refunds of any and all erroneously or illegally assessed and collected taxes, whether paid voluntarily or involuntarily. The later state-court exclusion of a tax levied under an unconstitutional law and demand-at-payment-or-return rule are constructions, not words of that promise. Georgia's authority to interpret its own law is accepted; the federal question is whether the resulting withdrawal of an apparently available remedy satisfies due process. McKesson's clear-and-certain requirement and Harper's separation of governing law from actual relief answer that question. The State did not appeal the substantive tax-invalidity determination, and the eligibility years supplied are 1985–1988. No new substantive tax holding is needed.

**Strongest State path:** McKesson permits meaningful prepayment review, and a litigant's failure to use an adequate existing remedy can defeat a later refund. A state court controls the construction of its own refund law. **Why this path does not decide this case:** a choice between honestly presented routes differs from retroactively withdrawing the route the State offered. The State cannot cure that defect merely by labeling other remedies ample. The Court need not adjudicate whether every APA, declaratory or appeal route was independently adequate. The bond/security restriction, administrative inability to decide constitutionality, discretionary rather than automatic postponement, and lack of an established collection stay prevent treating the majority's characterization as an undisputed factual answer in any event.

**Strongest objection to reversal:** fiscal planning and predictable procedural limits would be impaired if every disappointed refund claimant could invoke reliance. **Limit answering that objection:** this rule concerns after-payment removal of the represented remedy for unconstitutional taxation. It does not create equitable estoppel against all government statements, repeal preexisting clear limitations/protest rules, guarantee a refund amount, reopen final judgments, or make every change in tax law unconstitutional. McKesson permits prospectively specified reasonable procedures; Harper preserves actual lawful nondiscriminatory retroactive assessment, refund or combination where constitutionally adequate. Georgia must supply an actual lawful correction, not a hypothetical alternative tax.

## 8. Brown v. Gardner, No. 93-1128

### Committed disposition and sufficient statutory ground

All eight participating non-Stone Justices support **affirming the Federal Circuit's invalidation of the fault-or-accident condition and the remand for administrative eligibility redetermination**. Section 1151 requires injury or aggravation resulting from the covered VA service and consequent additional disability or death, except the veteran's own willful misconduct. A causal requirement does not authorize an additional requirement that the provider was careless, negligent, unskilled, mistaken or similarly at fault, or that an unforeseen untoward accident occurred. The challenged §3.358(c)(3) adds that condition and cannot govern Gardner's claim.

The causation words have real work without importing negligence. Congress expressly excludes claimant misconduct yet supplies no corresponding provider-fault limitation. The statute separately coordinates qualifying FTCA judgments, settlements or compromises by withholding monthly benefits until the aggregate otherwise payable equals the qualifying recovery; that coordination does not transform the veterans benefit into a tort remedy. No such recovery is established for Gardner.

**Strongest Secretary path:** the word injury can be informed by a long-standing administrative understanding; the causal language is not a comprehensive definition; legislation and reenactment in the shadow of the practice may justify deference. **Why it fails here:** fault is a different condition from causation, the statute explicitly identifies a misconduct limit of a different kind, and the definitive fault construction postdates the asserted 1934 reenactment. The lack of broadly available judicial review before 1988 also weakens inference from long judicial silence. Individual hearing references to negligence or accidents do not rewrite the operative coverage. Costs alone cannot add the condition.

**Strongest objection to the veteran's path:** rejecting fault might mean paying for every medical outcome after care, including intended consequences and natural progression. **Limit answering it:** the decision removes the fault-or-accident prerequisite only. Actual causal connection, additional disability/death, statutory covered services and claimant willful misconduct remain distinct. Mere sequence is not causation. The natural-progress provision and every possible consent/necessary-consequences issue are not adjudicated by this holding; their exact meaning and validity require a presented issue and source. The Court does not find that this surgery caused Gardner's alleged injury or award benefits.
