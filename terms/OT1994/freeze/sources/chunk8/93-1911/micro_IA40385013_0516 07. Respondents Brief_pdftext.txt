protections when he is imprisoned for crime."

constitutional due process protections in a wide range of

Treating prisoners in a lawless fashion cannot promote                           DeMont R.D. Conner
their allegiance to the rule of law. The entire rehabilitative
ethos, even in maximum security facilities like HCF, as
well as the need for security and order in the facility,
depend upon an environment that gives prisoners reason-
able expectations that they will not be punished arbi-
trarily. To command obedience, the law itself must be
credible and fair. For prisoners, once convicted and sen-
tenced, their primary experience of the law is the prison
disciplinary system. To hold that states can place this
system wholly outside the bounds of the Due Process
Clause, the Constitution's central guarantee of official
lawfulness, would author a lesson for which all society
ultimately may pay.
