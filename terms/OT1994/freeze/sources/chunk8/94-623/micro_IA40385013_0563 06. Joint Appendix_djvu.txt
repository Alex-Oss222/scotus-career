COMPLAINT 


Plaintiffs Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. (“Vimar”) and 
Bacchus Associates (“Bacchus”), for their complaint against 
the M/V SKY REEFER, her engines, etc., in rem, and M. H. 
Maritima, S.A. (“Maritima”), in personam, in a cause of action 
both civil and maritime, aver as follows: 


1. 


Jurisdiction is based on 28 U.S.C. § 1333 and venue in this 
district is proper under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2). This is an admi- 
ralty and maritime claim within the meaning of Rule 9(h) of the 
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure. 


J.A. 12 
2. 


Vimar is a corporation organized and existing under the laws 
of Spain with its principal place of business in Madrid. At all 
pertinent times it insured that certain cargo of Moroccan oranges 
and lemons laden on board the SKY REEFER (“the cargo”) 
and transported from Agadir, Morocco to New Bedford, 
Massachusetts under that certain Agadir/New Bedford bill of 
lading No. | dated February 8, 1991. 


3. 


Bacchus is a general partnership existing under the laws of 
the State of New York with its principal place of business in 
New York, N.Y. At all pertinent times it was the owner of the 
cargo, the holder in due course of the aforesaid bill of lading 


and the insured under Vimar’s policy of insurance on the 
cargo. 


4. 


The SKY REEFER is a four hatch refrigerated cargo vessel 
of 5,132 gross tons, bearing Official Number 18163-89 under 
the Panamanian flag. During the pendency of process herein 
it will be within this district and the jurisdiction of this Court. 


5. 


Maritima is a corporation organized under the laws of 
Panama and was at all material times the owner and/or oper- 
ator of the SKY REEFER, doing business in this district and 
within the jurisdiction of this Court. 


6. 


On or about February 8, 1991, in Agadir, Morocco, the 
SKY REEFER lifted the cargo, consisting of 2,052 pallets of 


J.A. 13 


Moroccan oranges and 6 pallets of Moroccan lemons, in 
good order and condition, for carriage to New Bedford, 
Massachusetts. 


7. 


Thereafter, the vessel sailed to New Bedford, where the 
cargo was discharged and delivered in a severely damaged 
condition, due to the negligence of Maritima or those for 
whom it is responsible and/or the unseaworthiness of the SKY 
REEFER. 


On account of the damage to the cargo, plaintiffs Vimar and 
Bacchus have sustained losses totalling approximately 
$1,033,442.90. 


9. 


On account of the damage to the cargo, Vimar, on or about 
October 7, 1991, paid Bacchus an insured loss in the sum of 
$733,442.90, pursuant to the terms and conditions of the 
applicable policy of insurance. Accordingly, Vimar is sub- 
rogated pro tanto to Bacchus’s rights against Maritima and 
the SKY REEFER. 


10. 


On account of the damage to the cargo, Vimar has incurred 
additional surveying fees and costs in an amount now esti- 
mated to be $75,000.00. 


a

11. 


All and singular, the matters alleged are true and correct. 


Plaintiffs reserve the right to amend and supplement this com- 
plaint as further facts become known.

1, Maritima be served with a copy of this complaint, 


together with a summons to appear and answer under oath all 
and singular the matters aforesaid; 


2. The Court order, adjudge and decree that Maritima pay 
to plaintiffs the losses sustained by them, together with inter- 
est thereon and all costs and disbursements; 


3. The SKY REEFER be arrested and sold to satisfy plain- 
tiffs’ claims as aforesaid; and 


4. Plaintiffs have such other and further relief in the 
premises as in law and justice they may be entitled to receive.

FIRST AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


This Court lacks personal jurisdiction over Maritima. 


SECOND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


Plaintiffs have made insufficient service of process upon 
Maritima. 


THIRD AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


The process utilized by the plaintiffs is insufficient. 


FOURTH AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


Venue in this Court is improper. 


FIFTH AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


The Complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief can 
be granted. 


SIXTH AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


The plaintiffs claim must be arbitrated and pursuant to the 
provisions of the Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C., §§ 1-15, 
this action must be stayed pending arbitration. 


SEVENTH AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


If the plaintiffs sustained any damages, which is denied, 
any such damages were the result of negligent or improper 
stowage for which Maritima is not liable. 


J.A. 22 
EIGHTH AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE 


Plaintiffs did not have title to sue at the relevant time, and 
therefore, are not the real parties in interest.

Affidavit of Edward T. McArdle 


Edward T. McArdle, being duly sworn, deposes and says: 


1. I am the principal of McArdle Associates, P.O. Box 
1319, Summit, New Jersey 07901, and regularly act as an 
independent consultant to the marine shipping industry, spe- 
cializing in refrigerated fruit cargoes. 


2. I was hired by John Mangia, the principal of Bacchus 
Associates (“Bacchus”), to oversee the disposition of the 
cargo of damaged fruit discharged ex the M/V SKY REEFER 
in New Bedford, Massachusetts in February 1991. I am famil- 
iar with the claim that Bacchus submitted to its insurers, 


J.A. 39 


Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. (“Vimar”), and the claims 
that Bacchus and Vimar have filed in this action. 


3. I submit this affidavit in opposition to the defendants’ 
motion to stay this action pending arbitration in Tokyo. The 
exhibits I refer to in this affidavit are the exhibits that are 
appended to plaintiffs’ opposition memorandum. 


4. Bacchus, a wholesaler, imports and sells perishable fruit 
throughout the Northeastern United States. Its practice is to 
sell the imported fruit to retailers on FOB terms at the entry 
ports in the Northeastern United States, including New Bed- 
ford, Massachusetts and New York City. Much of the fruit 
sold to New England retailers is imported into New Bedford. 


5. As the bill of lading, Exh. 1, recites, the cargo on board 
the Sky Reefer consisted of 2052 pallets of oranges and 6 pal- 
lets of lemons, each pallet consisting of 63 boxes (stacked 
3x3x7 on each pallet). The cargo occupied the entire reach of 
the vessel’s four cargo holds. Bacchus purchased the fruit, 
which was of Moroccan origin, from Galaxie Negoce S.A., 
the Moroccan company listed as the shipper in the bill of 
lading. 


6. The vessel arrived in New Bedford listing to starboard, 
with much of the palletized cargo crushed and broken. The 
apparent cause of the damage was the crew’s failure to secure 
the stow adequately for a trans-Atlantic crossing in mid-win- 
ter. The cargo was thereupon discharged in New Bedford into 
the custody and control of Marine Terminal, Inc. (“Marine 
Terminal”), a refrigerated storage facility able to handle large 
perishable fruit cargoes. 


7. The damaged fruit was inspected in New Bedford on one 
or more occasions by a number of surveyors, including, in 
Bacchus’s behalf, by John M. Hughes, of Southampton, Penn- 
sylvania; in Vimar’s behalf, by Alan Harley, of Associated 
Independent Marine Surveyors, Inc. in New York; in the ves- 
sel’s behalf, by Capt. John H. Wilson III of Boston; and in 
Marine Terminal’s behalf, by Capt. Michael Taylor, the ter- 
minal manager in New Bedford. 


J.A. 40 


8. Marine Terminal thereupon released the undamaged fruit 
to Bacchus’s retail customers but was unwilling to recondition 
the large volume of damaged fruit from the Sky Reefer. 


9. Accordingly, Bacchus, to mitigate its losses, trucked the 
damaged fruit to reconditioning facilities in the New York 
area who specialize in reconditioning damaged fruit. Such 
reconditioning consists, for the most part, in (a) examining 
the fruit; (b) repackaging the fruit that remains marketable; 
and (c) destroying the fruit that is not marketable. The facil- 
ities to whom Bacchus shipped the damaged fruit ex the Sky 
Reefer are: (a) Donato Bros., Inc., Buena, New Jersey; 
(b) Timberline Cold Storage Inc., Pitman, New Jersey; 
(c) Pig-Tainer Express Corp., Elizabeth, New Jersey; and 
(d) Lucca’s Freezer & Cold Storage Inc., Hammonton, New 
Jersey. 


10. Bacchus’s out-of-pocket losses exceed $1 million. The 
losses consist of (a) the extra trucking costs from New 
Bedford to the reconditioning facilities; (b) the recondition- 
ing expenses; (c) the value of the lost fruit; (d) the diminution 
in value of the fruit that, following reconditioning, was sold 
to retail customers at discounts; and (e) the market value loss 
on the fruit that could not be sold to customers on the antic- 
ipated FOB New Bedford terms. Vimar, Bacchus’s insurer, 
has paid Bacchus $733,442.90 in respect of its losses on the 
Sky Reefer cargo and is subrogated to Bacchus’s rights in this 
action to the extent of its payment to Bacchus. 


11. I was responsible for submitting Bacchus’s insurance 
claim to Transatiantic Marine Claims Agency (“TMCA”), 
which acted as adjusters in Vimar’s behalf. I reviewed with 
TMCA, in order for it to process Bacchus’s claim, all the 
third-party and resale invoices supporting Bacchus’s losses. 


12. The claim review undertaken by TMCA was laborious 
and extensive, due to the voluminous records detailing the 
movement of the damaged fruit from Marine Terminal in New 
Bedford to the various reconditioning facilities in New Jersey 
and eventually to Bacchus’s retail customers in the New 
Jersey/New York area. Vimar paid Bacchus’s claim based on 


J.A. 4] 


the extensive claim documentation that I furnished to and 
reviewed with TMCA. 


13. The proposed arbitration in Tokyo would be extremely 
prejudicial to Bacchus. The surveyors who examined the dam- 
aged fruit in New Bedford and who thus are in the best posi- 
tion to testify concerning the cause and extent of the damage 
to the cargo, including the vessel’s P&I surveyor, are all 
located in the Northeastern United States. So, too, are the 
Bacchus representatives who made the decisions concerning 
the reconditioning and resale of the damaged fruit and the 
personnel of the reconditioning facilities who supervised the 
reprocessing and repackaging of the fruit. 


14. Likewise, the records documenting the reconditioning 
and resale of the damaged cargo were all prepared in the 
Northeastern United States, where the insurance claim was 
also submitted and adjusted. It would be extremely burden- 
some for Bacchus to be required to adduce all this evidence 
and testimony in Tokyo, in the Japanese language, simply 
because the bill of lading, which Bacchus had no role in 
preparing, contains a Tokyo arbitration clause. 


15. Bacchus did not negotiate and never agreed to the 
Japanese choice-of-law clause and the Tokyo arbitration 
clause in the Nichiro Corporation form bill of lading which 
the shipper of the cargo, Galaxie Negoce S.A., transferred to 
Bacchus. Nichiro’s agents issued the Nichiro-form bill of lad- 
ing to Galaxie in Agadir, Morocco, and thereafter Galaxie 
negotiated the bill of lading to Bacchus in connection with the 
sale of the fruit. It is obvious that Nichiro, a Japanese com- 
pany, includes the Japanese choice-of-law and Tokyo arbi- 
tration clause in the bill of lading solely-for its convenience 
and the convenience of its Japanese insurers. Given a choice, 
Bacchus would never willingly agree to arbitrate with a 
Japanese carrier in Tokyo (a) a bill-of-lading claim that would 
be governed by the United States Carriage of Goods by Sea 
Act, where (b) virtually all the material witnesses would be 
located in the United States. 


J.A.42 


16. Bacchus threatened to arrest the Sky Reefer at the time 
of discharge in order to enforce its maritime lien on the ves-

vent the arrest of the vessel, the vessel’s P&I club furnished

for the vessel. Exh. 5. 


17. In the letter of undertaking the vessel's P&I club

undertaking and thereby (b) enforce Bacchus’s maritime lien 
over the vessel. 


18. Accordingly, Bacchus respectfully requests that this

arbitration in Tokyo.

1. Definitions 


“Carrier” means the party whose name appears in the head 
of this Bill of Lading, including the Vessel and or her 
owners. 

“Merchant” includes the shipper, consignor, consignee, 
owner and receiver of the Goods and the holder of this 
Bill of Lading. 

“Goods” means the cargo described on the face of this Bill 
of Lading. 


. Acceptance 


In accepting this Bill of Lading, the Merchant expressly 
accepts and agrees to all its terms, conditions, stipulations 
and exceptions whether written, typed, stamped or printed 
as fully as if signed by him. 


- Governing Law and Arbitration 


(1) The contract evidenced by or contained in this Bill of 
Lading shall be governed by the Japanese law. 


(2) Any dispute arising from this Bill of Lading shall be 
referred to arbitration in Tokyo by the Tokyo Maritime 
Arbitration Commission (TOMAC) of The Japan Shipping 
Exchange, Inc., in accordance with the Rules of TOMAC 
and any amendment thereto, and the award given by the 
arbitrators shall be final and binding on both parties. 


. Description of Goods 


(1) This Bill of Lading shall be prima facie evidence of 
the receipt by the Carrier of the total number of packages 
or units enumerated overleaf. 


(2) No representation is made by the Carrier as to the 
weight, measure, quantity, contents, quality, description, 
condition, marks, numbers or value of the Goods and the 
Carrier shall not be responsible for the accuracy thereof. 


(3) The shipper warrants to the Carrier that the particulars 
relating to the Goods as set out overleaf have been 
check by the shipper on receipt of this Bill of Lading

and that such particulars and any other particulars fur- 
nished by or on behalf of the shipper are correct. 


(4) The shipper shall indemnify the Carrier against all 
loss, damage, delay or expenses arising or resulting from 
inaccuracies in or inadequacy of such particulars. 


. Exemptions 


The Carrier shall not be responsible for loss or damage 
arising or resulting from: (1) Act, neglect, or default of 
the master, mariner, pilot, or the servants of the Carrier in 
the navigation or in the management of the Vessel; (2) 
Fire, unless caused by the actual fault or privity of the 
Carrier; (3) Perils, dangers, and accidents of the sea or 
other navigable waters; (4) Act of God; (5) Act of war; (6) 
Act of public enemies; (7) Arrest or restraint of princes, 
rulers or people, or seizure under legal process; (8) Quar- 
antine restrictions; (9) Act or omission of the Merchant, 
his agent or representative; (10) Strikes or lockouts or 
stoppage or restraint of labor from whatever cause, 
whether partial or general; (11) Riots and civil commo- 
tions; (12) Saving or attempting to save life or property at 
sea; (13) Inherent defect, quality, or vice of the Goods; 
(14) Insufficiency of packing; (15) Insufficiency or inad- 
equacy of marks; (16) Latent defects not discoverable by 
due diligence; (17) Any other cause arising without the 
actual fault or privity of the Carrier, or without the actual 
fault or neglect of the agents or servants of the Carrier. 


. Deck Cargo 


The Carrier shall not be liable in any capacity whatsoever 
for any delay or loss of or damage to the Goods which are 
carried on deck and specially stated herein to be so car- 
ried, whether or not caused by the Carrier’s negligence or 
the Vessel’s unseaworthiness.

denced by this Bill of Lading is or is likely to be affected

place which the Carrier deems safe and convenient, 
whereupon the responsibility of the Carrier in respect of 
such Goods shall cease: or 


(2) to deliver the Goods at the place designated for deliv- 
ery. 


In any event the Carrier shall be entitled to full freight 
and charges on the Goods received for transportation, and 
the Merchant shall pay any additional cost of carriage to 
and delivery and storage at such place as abovementioned. 


. Reefer Clause 


(1) Seaworthiness 


If before the loading of the Goods into any refrigerated 
holds the Carrier obtains the certificate of the Classifi- 
cation Society’s Surveyor or other surveyor appointed 
either by the Merchant or the Carrier or representative of 
the Merchant (including the master of the other vessel 
from which the Vessel has received the Goods on the sea, 
hereinafter referred to “the Representative of the Mer- 
chant”) that such refrigerated holds and the refrigerating 
machinery are in the opinion of the Surveyor or the other 
surveyor or the Representative of the Merchant fit and 
safe for the carriage of the Goods, the aforesaid certificate 
shall be conclusive evidence against the Merchant that the 
Vessel’s refrigerated holds and refrigerating machinery 
were before and at the beginning of the voyage fit and 


safe for the reception, carriage and preservation of the 
Goods.

(2) Temperature Instruction 


(i) If a written instruction is given by the Merchant 
regarding the carrying temperature of the Goods during 
the voyage, such instruction temperature shall apply to the

after the closing of hatches thereof on to the time of the 
opening of the same hatches. 


(ii) The carrying temperature of the Goods shall, unless 
otherwise instructed in writing by the Merchant, be mea- 
sured at such place where the Vessel customarily carriers 
out the measurement thereof. 


(iii) The reading of carrying temperature taken pursuant 
to the preceding paragraph (ii) hereof shall be used in 
deciding whether the instructed temperature has been 
maintained or not. 


(3) Fluctuation of Temperature during Cargo Operation 


The Carrier shall not be liable for loss of or damage to the 
Goods resulting from the fluctuation of the temperature in 
the refrigerated holds during loading, discharging or tran- 
shipping operation of the Goods.

tort.

independent contractor of the Carrier, such person shall be

Limitation of Liability 


The Carrier shall in no event be liable for any loss of or 
damage to or in connection with the Goods in an amount 
exceeding One Hundred Thousand Yen in Japanese Cur-

by the shipper before shipment and the nature and the 
value thereof inserted in this Bill of Lading and extra 
freight is paid as agreed upon. In such case if the actual 
value of the Goods per package or unit exceeds such 
declared value, the value shall nevertheless be deemed to 
be declared-value. The Carrier’s liability shall not exceed 
such declared-value and any partial loss or damage shall 
be adjusted pro rata on the basis of such value.

cating the general nature of such loss or damage shall be 
given in writing to the Carrier or his agent at the port of

delivery thereof under this Bill of Lading or, if the loss or 
damage is not apparent, within three days of the delivery 
of the Goods, such removal shall be prima facie evidence 
of the delivery by the Carrier of the Goods as described in 
this Bill of Lading.

liability in respect of the Goods unless arbitration is filed 
pursuant to Clause 3 within one year after delivery of the 
Goods or the date when the Goods should have been

GENERAL STRIKE CLAUSE 


Neither Charterers nor Owners shall be responsible for 
the consequences of any strikes or lock-outs preventing 
or delaying the fulfillment of any obligations under this 
contract. 

If there is a strike or lock-out affecting the loading of the 
cargo, or any part of it, when vessel is ready to proceed 
from her last port or at any time during the voyage to the 
port or ports of loading or after her arrival there, Captain 
or Owners may ask Charterers to declare, that they agree 
to reckon the laydays as if there were no strike or lock- 
out. Unless Charterers have given such declaration in 
writing (by telegram, if necessary) within 24 hours Own- 
ers shall have the option of cancelling this contract, if 
part cargo has already been loaded. Owners must proceed 
with same, (freight payable on loaded quantity only) hav- 
ing liberty to complete with other cargo on the way for 
their own account. 

If there is a strike or lock-out affecting the discharge of 
the cargo on or after vessel's arrival at or off port of dis- 
charge and same has not been settled within 48 hours. 
Receivers shall have the option of keeping vessel waiting 
until such strike or lock-out is at an end against paying 
half demurrage after expiration of the time provided for 
discharging, or of ordering the vessel to a safe port where 
she can safely discharge without risk of being detained by 
strike or lock-out. Such orders to be given within 48 
hours after Captain or Owners have given notice to Char- 
terers of the strike or lock-out affecting the discharge. On 
delivery of the cargo at such port, all conditions of this 
Charterparty and of the Bill of Lading shall apply and

ARTICLE | 


In this convention the following words are employed, 
with the meanings set out below: 


(a) “Carrier” includes the owner or the charterer who 
enters into a contract of carriage with a shipper. 


(b) “Contract of Carriage” applies only to contracts 
of carriage covered by a bill of lading or any sim- 
ilar document of title, in so far as such document 
relates to the carriage of goods by sea, including 
any bill of lading or any similar document as 
aforesaid issued under or pursuant to a charter- 
party from the moment at which such a bill of

(c) “Goods” includes goods, wares, merchandise, 
and articles of every kind whatsoever except live 
animals and cargo which by the contract of car- 
riage is stated as being carried on deck and is so 
carried. 


(d) “Ships” means any vessel used for the carriage of 
goods by sea. 


(e) “Carriage of goods” covers the period from the 
time when the goods are loaded on to the time 
they are discharged from the ship.

APPENDIX 5D 


The International Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 
(Kokusai Kaijé Buppin Unso Ho) 
(Law No. 172, 1957) 


Article 1. (Application) 


This law shall apply to the carriage of goods by ship when 
either the port of loading or the port of discharge is outside 
this country. 


Article 2. (Definitions) 


1. In this law the term “ship” has the meaning provided 
in the Commercial Code Article 684, paragraph 1, but 
excludes those boats and small vessels described in Article 
684, paragraph 2. 


2. In this law the term “carrier” means a shipowner, a 
lessee of a ship, or a charterer carrying goods according to the 
previous Article. 


3. In this law “consignor” means a consignor or charterer 
who commissions the carriage of goods according to the pre- 
vious Article. 


Article 3. (Duty of care regarding the goods carried) 


1. The carrier is liable to pay damages when he or the per- 
sons employed by him for the receipt, loading, stowage, car- 
riage, storage, unloading and delivery of the goods fail to take 
due care, thus causing loss, damage or delay to the goods 
carried. 


2. The previous paragraph does not apply to damage 
caused by the acts of the master, crew, pilot, or other persons 
employed by the carrier in the navigation or management of 
the ship, or by fire on board the ship (unless due to negli- 
gence or intention of the carrier). 


JA. 113 


Article 4. (Duty of care regarding the goods carried) 


1. Unless the carrier can prove that he took due care 
according to the previous Article, cannot avoid liability under 
that article. 


2. Notwithstanding the previous paragraph, the carrier is 
exempt from liability under the preceding article if he proves 
that the damage to the goods carried arose from any of the 
following facts. However, if it is proved that the damage 
could have been avoided by the exercise of due care accord- 
ing to Article 3, and that such due care was not exercised, 
then this paragraph shall not apply. 


1. Peril peculiar to the sea or other navigable waters. 
Natural calamity or disaster. 


2 

3. War, riot, or civil war. 

4. Piracy or other similar act. 
5 


Judicial attachment, quarantine restriction, or other 
disposal measure taken under public authority. 


6. Act of the consignor or of the owner of the goods car- 
ried or of a person employed by one of them. 


7. Strike, slow-down, lock-out or other similar dispute. 


8. Attempt to save life or property at sea, or any devia- 
tion for that purpose, or any other deviation for fair 
and appropriate reason. 


9. The particular nature of the goods carried or their 
latent defect. 


10. Imperfect packaging or marking of the goods carried. 
11. Latent defect in a derrick or similar equipment. 


3. The previous paragraph does not preclude the applica- 
tion of Article 9. 


JA. 114 


Article 5. (Dull of care regarding fitness for the voyage) 


|. The carrier is liable to pay damages when he or the per- 
sons employed by him fail to exercise due care as to the fol- 
lowing at the time of departure, thus causing delay, loss, or 
damage to the goods carried: 


1. to make the ship seaworthy; 
2. to man and equip the ship and replenish supplies; or 


3. to make the holds, refrigeration chambers and any 
other area used for the storage of the goods carried 
suitable for the reception and preservation of those 
goods. 


2. Unless the carrier can prove that he took due care 
according to the preceding paragraph, he cannot avoid the lia- 
bility provided for under that paragraph. 


Article 6. (Duty to deliver a bill of lading) 


1. The carrier, master or carrier's agent, at the request of 
the consignor, shall issue, without delay after the goods have 
been loaded, one or more bills of lading recording that the 
goods have been shipped (“hereafter referred to as a shipped 
bill of lading”). Also, as to goods delivered, the carrier after 
their receipt but before loading, shall at the request of the 
consignor issue one or more bills of lading, recording that the 
goods have been received (hereafter referred to as a “received 
bill of lading”). 


2. Where receipt bills of lading have been issued, the 
delivery of a shipped bill of lading cannot be demanded, 
except in exchange for all the issued received bills of lading. 


Article 7. (Issuing a bill of lading) 


1. The bill of lading must state the following particulars (in 
the case of a receipt bill of lading items 7 and 8 shall be 
excluded) and the carrier, master or the carrier's agent shall 
sign it or inscribe and affix his seal to it. 


J.A. 115 


|. The kind of goods. 


2. The weight or volume of the goods, or the number of 
packages or individual pieces and the marks of the 
goods. 


The apparent condition of the goods. 
The consignor's full name or trade name. 
The consignee's full name or trade name. 
The carrier's full name or trade name. 
The name of the ship and its nationality. 
The date and port of loading. 


7? FF PF PF 


The port of discharge. 
10, The freight. 


11. When several bills of lading are issued, the number of 
bills issued. 


12. The place and date of issuance. 


2. Where the delivery of a shipped bill of lading has been 
demanded in exchange for the received bill of lading, that 
received bill of lading containing a statement that the goods 
have been loaded and which has been signed or inscribed and 
sealed may be substituted for a shipped bill of lading. In this 
case, the previous particulars described in numbers 7 and 8 
shall be stated thereon. 


Article 8. (Consignor's Notice) 


1. The particulars described in paragraph |, items | and 2 
of the previous article must, in cases where a consignor has 
given written notice of those required particulars, be entered 
by the carrier, master or carrier's agent according to that 
notice. 


2. The preceding paragraph shall not apply where the car- 
rier with reasonable ground believes that the notice described 


J.A. 116 


in that paragraph is not correct, or when there is no suitable 
method of confirming whether the notice is correct. That 
paragraph also shall not apply in cases where the goods car- 
ried or their cases or packages have no marks, or where the 
marks will not withstand the voyage. 


3. The consignor shall guarantee to the carrier that the 
notice under paragraph | of this article is correct. 


Article 9. (False entry on a bill of lading) 


When an entry on the bill of lading is contrary to the actual 
facts, the carrier cannot set up the difference against a good 
faith holder of the bill of lading unless the carrier proves that 
he took due care when making that entry. 


Article 10. (Provisions applied mutatis mutandis) 


Articles 573 to 575, Article 584 and Articles 770 to 775 of 
the Commercial Code shall apply mutatis mutandis to a bill of 
lading under this law. 


Article 11, (Disposal of dangerous cargo) 


1. When the carrier, master, or carrier's agent does not 
know at the time of loading that the goods are inflammable, 
explosive, or of a similar dangerous nature, then those goods 
may at any time be unloaded, destroyed or rendered harmless. 


2. The provisions of the preceding paragraph shall not 
preclude the carrier from demanding compensatory damages 
from the consignor. 


3. When the carrier, the master or the carrier's agent knew 
at the time of loading that the goods were inflammable, 
explosive, or of a similar dangerous nature, those goods may 
be unloaded, destroyed or rendered harmless if they endanger 
the ship or other cargo. 


4. The carrier having disposed of the goods according to 


paragraph one or the preceding paragraph is not liable to pay 
damages for the loss caused by such disposal. 


J.A.117 


Article 12. (Duty of the consignee, etc., to give notice) 


1. When there is any partial loss of or partial damage to the 
goods carried, the consignee or the holder of the bill of lad- 
ing must at the time of receipt give the carrier a written report 
of the general nature of the loss or damage. However, where 
the loss or damage is not immediately discoverable, it shall be 
sufficient to give the report within three days from the day of 
receipt. 


2. When a report, as specified in the preceding paragraph, 
has not been given, it shall be presumed that the goods carried 
were delivered without loss or damage. 


3. The preceding two paragraphs do not apply to cases 
where the actual condition of the goods carried is confirmed 
at the time of delivery in the presence of the parties 
concerned. 


4, When there are doubts about the loss of or damage to the 
goods carried, the carrier, the consignor and the holder of the 
bill of lading must grant each other the necessary facilities for 
inspection of the goods. 


Article 13. (Limitation of liability) 


1, Concerning the goods carried, the carrier's liability per 
package or unit is limited to 100,000 yen. 


2. The previous paragraph does not apply when notice as to 
the kind and value of the goods carried is given by the con- 
Signor at the time of entrusting them for shipment and the 
same is recorded on the bill of lading. 


3. In the case provided for in the previous paragraph, when 
the consignor has intentionally reported a value significantly 
in excess of the real value of the goods carried, the carrier is 


not liable to pay any compensatory damages for those goods. 


4. In the case provided for in paragraph 2, if the consignor 
has intentionally reported a value significantly below the real 


JA. 118 


value, the reported value shall be presumed to be the value of 
the goods carried with regard to damage to the goods carried. 


5. The preceding two paragraphs shall not apply in cases 
where the carrier has acted in bad faith. 


Article 14, (Limited period of liability) 


The carrier's liability for the goods carried shall cease if 
court proceedings are not commenced within one year from 
the day the goods were delivered (where all the goods have 
been lost, then from the day when they should have been 
delivered). However, this shall not apply when the carrier has 
acted in bad faith. 


Article 15. (Prohibition of special agreements) 


1. Any special agreement unfavorable to the consignor, 
consignee or to the holder of the bill of lading and contrary to 
the provisions of Articles 3 to 5, Articles 8 and 9, and Arti- 
cles 12 to 14, is void. The same shall apply to any contract 
purporting to transfer to the carrier rights arising from the 
insurance contracts for the goods carried and other similar 
contracts. 


2. The provisions of the preceding paragraph shall not pre- 
vent the making of special agreements disadvantageous to the 
carrier. In such a case the consignor may require that the spe- 
cial agreement be entered on the bill of lading. 


3. The provisions of paragraph | of this article shall not 
apply to damage which occurred before the goods carried 
were loaded or after they were discharged. 


4. Where a special agreement as provided for in paragraph 
| has been made with regard to those damages described in 
the preceding paragraph, unless that special agreement has 
been entered on the bill of lading, the carrier cannot set it up 
against the holder of the bill of lading. 


JA. 119 


Article 16. (Exception to the prohibition of special 
agreements) 


The provisions of paragraph one of the preceding article 
shall not apply where a contract of carriage is made having 
for its subject all or part of a ship. However, this shall not 
affect the relationship between the carrier and the holder of 
the bill of lading. 


Article 17. (Ibid.) 


The provisions of the preceding article shall apply mutatis 
mutandis to the carriage when the special nature or character 
of the goods carried or the special conditions under which the 
carriage is to be performed make it appropriate to exempt the 
carrier from liability or to reduce that liability. 


Article 18. (Ibid.) 


1. The provisions of Article 15, paragraph |, shall not 
apply to the carriage of live animals or to the carriage of 
goods on deck. 


2. When a special agreement as provided for in Article 15, 
paragraph |, has been made as to the carriage referred to in 
the preceding paragraph, and when that special agreement has 
not been recorded on the bill of lading, the carrier cannot set 
up that special agreement against the holder of the bill of lad- 
ing. The same shall apply to goods carried on deck when no 
statement to that effect is recorded on the bill of lading. 


Article 19. (Preferential rights over the ship) 


1. When a contract of carriage has for its subject all or part 
of a ship, and the charterer then makes a contract of carriage 
with a third person, a person having the right to claim com- 
pensation for damage to the goods carried occurring within 
the limits of the master's responsibility has a preferential 
right over the ship and its equipment. 


J.A. 120 


2. The preferential right in the preceding paragraph shall 
be next to a preferential right under Article 842, item 8 of the 
Commercial Code. 


3. The provisions of Article 844, paragraphs 2 and 3, Arti- 
cles 845, 846, 847, paragraph 1 and Article 849 of the Com- 
mercial Code apply mutatis mutandis to the preferential right 
provided for in paragraph 1. 


Article 20. (Application of the Commercial Code) 


1. The Commercial Code, with the exception of Articles 
738, 739, 759, and 766 to 776 inclusive, shall apply to car- 
riage according to Article 1 of this law. 


2. The provisions of Article 576 and 579 to 583, inclusive, 
of the Commercial Code shall apply mutatis mutandis to a 
carriage according to Article 1 of this law. 


Article 21. (Exclusion of the carriage of mail) 


This law shall not apply to the carriage of mail.
