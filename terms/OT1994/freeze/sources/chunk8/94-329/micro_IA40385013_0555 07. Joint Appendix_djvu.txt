INJUNCTIVE RELIEF AND FOR DAMAGES

Plaintiffs allege and verify as follows: 
NATURE AND SUMMARY OF THE ACTION 


1. This is a civil action for declaratory and injunctive 
relief and for damages and attorneys’ fees arising from 
defendants’ violation of plaintiffs’ constitutiona?- and civil 
rights and liberties under the United States and Virginia 
constitutions and under federal and Virginia law. 


2. Plaintiffs; RONALD w. ROSENBERGER, as a 
member of WIDE AWAKE PRODUCTIONS, an unin- 
corporated Contracted Independent Organization (“CIO”) 
of the University of Virginia, and as the Editor-in-Chief 
of WIDE AWAKE MAGAZINE, and ROBERT J. 
PRINCE and GREGORY W. MOURAD, and other past 
and present students of the University of Virginia simi- 
larly situated (“Plaintiffs”), seek: 


A. a declaration that the conduct of the various 
named defendants in establishing and applying a policy 
for the University of Virginia, the Rector and Visitors of 
the University of Virginia, the Student Council of the 
University of Virginia, and the Appropriations Committee 
of the Student Council, which denies to plaintiffs the same 
benefits (including, but not limited to, eligibility to re- 
ceive Student Activities Fund (“SAF”) monies) granted 
other similarly situated students and student organiza- 
tions, and which denies plaintiffs Student Activities Fund 
benefits solely and expressly because of the content or 
viewpoint of plaintiffs’ speech, violates plaintiffs’ rights to 
freedom of speech, freedom of association, freedom of the 
press, free exercise of religion, and to equal protection 
under the law, as guaranteed by the First and Fourteenth 
Amendments to the United States Constitution; by Article 
I, Sections 11, 12 and 16 of the Virginia Constitution; 
and by the Virginia Act for Religious Freedom, Code of 
Virginia Title 57, Chapter 1, Sections 57-1 and 57-2; 


5 


B. a declaration that the above-described conduct of 
the various named defendants denies plaintiffs their fed- 
eral civil rights under color of state law in violation of 
Chapter 22, Section 1 of the Civil Rights Act of 1871, 
as amended, 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (1988); 


C. a permanent order enjoining defendants from con- 
tinuing and applying a policy of discriminating against 
plaintiffs in the appropriation of Student Activities Fund 
benefits or in any other manner on the basis of the con- 
tent or viewpoint of plaintiffs’ speech or on the basis of 
tle content or viewpoint of the speech of the Contracted 
Independent Organization of which plaintiffs are mem- 
bers or of the journals, magazines, newsletters or other 
things that plaintiffs publish or may publish in the future; 


D. such compensatory damages for violation of plain- 
tiffs’ federal and state constitutional and statutory rights 
as the Court may find just and proper, including but not 
limited to $5,862.00, the sum of the SAF funds unlaw- 
fully denied plaintiffs, plus interest thereon from the date 
of such denial; 


E. an order awarding plaintiffs reasonable costs and 
attorneys’ fees pursuant to Section 2 of the Civil Rights 
Attorney’s Fees Award Act of 1976, as amended, 42 
U.S.C. § 1988 (1988); and 


F. such other relief as the Court deems just and 


proper. 
JURISDICTION 


3. This action arises under the First and Fourteenth 
Amendments to the United States Constitution. Jurisdic- 
tion over the claims is conferred on this Court by 28 
U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1343 and by 42 U.S.C. §§ 1983 
and 1988. Declaratory relief is authorized by 28 U.S.C. 
§§ 2201 and 2202. This Court has pendent jurisdiction 
over the state constitutional and statutory claims.

Constitution 
ArticleI: Name. C. S. Lewis Society 


Article II: Purpose. To promote interest in and dis- 
cussion of, various literary, moral, and philosophical 
topics, with a particular emphasis on the work of 
the “Oxford Christians.” 


Article III. Membership. The C. S. Lewis Society does 
not discriminate on the basis of race, color, creed, 
national origin, affectional orientation, or sex. It is 
open to students, faculty members, and townspeople. 


Article IV: Officers. John Stinneford, President. As 

' this is a new organization, other positions and/or a 
managing committee will be set up at an organiza- 
tional meeting in the fall. 


Article V: Elections. Positions within the organization 
will be determined by a vote of the general membev- 
ship. 


Article VI: Meetings. The group will tentatively meet 
weekly on Sunday evenings. 


Article VII: Finances. Members of the group may be 
expected to pay a nominal membership fee, and will 
be expected to help in any Fundraising activities. 
Authority for expenditure of the Funds shall reside 
in a majority vote of the organization officers. 


Article VIII: Amendments. The Constitution may be 
amended by vote of the club membership.
