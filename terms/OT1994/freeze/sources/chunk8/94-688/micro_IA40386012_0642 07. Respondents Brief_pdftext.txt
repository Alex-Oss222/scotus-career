              SUMMARY OF THE ARGUMENT
    No action, claim or remedy is or was necessary under 42
U.S.C. § 1983 to vindicate Petitioners' rights under the

Commerce Clause in this case, or to award them the tax
refunds they sought. All of their remedies, which were
adequate and complete, were and are provided by state
law. In such a case, Congress' intent and policy, as

manifested by 28 U.S.C. § 1341, is one of nonintrusion and

noninterference. Petitioners' argument, therefore, that the
state court's refusal to grant additional remedies under
' Jeral law is in conflict with the policies and purposes of
 he federal statutes, is simply untenable. The Court's
`ecisions have well established that where state laws
                                       6

provide taxpayers a complete and adequate remedy, one
that is "plain, speedy and efficient," the intent and policy
of Congress is that those taxpayers should be remitted
solely to those state remedies. That is what was done in
this case. The decision below is not in conflict with federal
policy; rather, it preserves and follows it. The decision
should be affirmed.

It is no answer that the State has a law. which

state tax systems is no more determinative--or destruc-
tive--of the adequacy and completeness of those remedies
than the failure to pay interest.

    In sum, Congress and this Court have recognized that,
"in the unique area of state taxation," the principles of

comity and federalism dictate a policy of federal noninter-

ference in state tax matters, and Congress has expressed

that policy by passing the Tax Injunction Act. The only
condition for such noninterference is that the States
provide complete and adequate remedies and procedures
for challenging those state taxes and, as required by due
process, provide "meaningful relief." Oklahoma has done
                                      16

so, and the policies and dictates of Congress have been
satisfied.

                                       II

        NEITHER THE SUPREMACY CLAUSE NOR THIS
        COURT'S DECISIONS REQUIRE THE STATE TO
       GRANT ADDITIONAL REMEDIES UNDER § 1988 IN
        STATE TAX CHALLENGES.

    Petitioners argue that it is "clear" that their request for
injunctive-and declaratory relief against the challenged
state taxes can be maintained under § 1983, relying on the
footnote in Will v. Michigan Dep't of State Police, 491 U.S.
58, 71, n. 10 (1989). See Pet. Brf. at 20, n. 11. Thus, say
Petitioners, by appending a request for such relief to their
state-provided action to recover taxes paid under protest,
they became entitled to engraft upon their state remedies
a "right" to attorneys' fees under § 1988. However, the
availability of injunctive and declaratory relief under
§ 1983 is far from "clear" in this case, and Petitioners'
asserted "right" to attorneys' fees by virtue of federal law
is directly contrary to Congress' expressed policy regarding
state tax challenges.

   First, the relied-upon footnote in Will says only that
state officials in their official capacity, when sued for
injunctive relief, are "persons" under § 1983 because
official-capacity actions for prospective relief are not
treated as actions against the state. Jbid., citing Kentucky
v. Graham, 473 U.S. 159, 167, n. 14 (1985); and Ex Parte
Young, 209 U.S. 123, 159-160 (1908). Graham, of course,
relied upon Young for this proposition. g.v. And, contrary
to Petitioners' assumption, whether injunctive or declarato-
ry relief may be sought against state officials in their
                                       17

 official capacity in state tax matters is another question
 altogether.

     As the Court has noted, after it was decided in Ex Parte
  Young that federal courts may enjoin state officers from
 enforcing an unconstitutional state law, Congress in 1937
 enacted § 1341 and specifically prohibited such injunctive
 federal intrusions in state tax challenges where state law
 provided a plain, speedy and efficient remedy. Fair
 Assessment in Real Estate Ass'n v. McNary, 454 U.S. at
 102-103. For the same reasons, declaratory judgments
 against the validity of state taxes are also prohibited.
 California v. Grace Brethren Church, 457 U.S. at 411. As
 these cases hold, injunctive and declaratory relief would
 not be available in federal court under § 1983 or other-
 wise. Nor, as independent claims for relief, would they be
available in state court in this case. See, eg., Oklahoma
 Tax Comm'n v. Harris, supra (taxpayer may not maintain
action to enjoin collection of a tax); and Fair Assessment,
454 U.S. at 115 (a declaration in this case as to the state
statutes' validity is merely incident to Petitioners' claim
for refund.) See n. 7, supra, at p. 6. Petitioners' claim
against the State for refunds, of course, is not cognizable
under § 1983 under any circumstance. See Will, 491 U.S.
at 68-69.

    Secondly, the simple answer to Petitioners' Supremacy
Clause argument is that this case does not present a
situation where federal remedies have been denied because
they were thought to be contrary to the policy of the State.
Just the opposite: the policy of the State is to provide
taxpayers a plain, speedy and efficient method for chal-
lenging its tax laws, and to provide meaningful relief from
those tax laws found to be unconstitutional--in this case,
refunds. That, itself, is the very essence of the federal
policy as reflected in § 1341 and the decisions of this Court.
                                      18

    Finally, Petitioners' claim of conflict with the purpose
and policy of federal statutes is based upon their assump-
tion that they have a "right" to attorneys' fees under §
1988. However, § 1988 simply does not create a fee
liability where there is no liability under § 1983. See, e.g.,
Kentucky v. Graham, 473 U.S. 159, 168 (1985). It would be
anomalous indeed to acknowledge Congress' intent "to
limit drastically" federal-court interference with state tax
systems, and at the same time urge that Congress intend-
ed to interfere with those systems directly by mandating
the award of attorneys fees in state court actions regarding
those systems, irrespective of state law.

                            CONCLUSION

   The remedies here provided to Petitioners are plain,
speedy and efficient. Consistent with Congress' announced
policy, those state remedies are all the remedies to which
Petitioners are entitled. The decision of the Oklahoma
Supreme Court should be affirmed.
