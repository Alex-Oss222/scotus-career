SUPPLEMENTAL BRIEF OF RESPONDENT 


In an appendix to its brief, see Resp. Br. 1a-23a, 
Respondent Chickasaw Nation reproduced the text of 
legislation then pending before the Oklahoma legislature! 
which would have placed the legal incidence of the 
state’s motor fuel tax on consumers. Noting that effect, 
Resp. Br. 11, and maintaining that in consequence, adop- 
tion of that legislation would moot the first two questions 
presented by Petitioner Tax Commission (with the excep- 
tion of any issue concerning Indian-country sales by the 
Tribe to tribal members), id. at 12 & n.12, the Chickasaw 
Nation suggested that should that legislation be adopted, 
dismissal of the Writ as improvidently granted might be 
an appropriate course of conduct, id. at 10-13, 49. 


As of the date set for filing of the Chickasaw Nation’s 
brief (March 27, 1995), the legislation discussed above 
had been adopted by the Oklahoma House of Representa- 
tives, and was pending before the Oklahoma Senate. But 
on March 29, 1995, that legislation was defeated before 
the Senate Rules Committee. Thus, it would be literally 
accurate to represent to this Court that that legislation has 
now been finally defeated and will not be enacted during 
the Oklahoma legislature’s 1995 legislative session. But 
such a representation would incompletely describe the 
current situation in material respect. 


As this Court may be aware, Oklahoma’s legislative 
process, like the processes of some other states, permits

the introduction of “blank” or “shell bills,” whose pur- 
pose is to allow the “opening up” of a particular title of 
Oklahoma Statutes for possible last-minute amendment. 
Such a bill currently exists with regard to Title 68 of 
Oklahoma Statutes, which is the title containing the state’s 
fuel-tax provisions. S.B. 574, 45th Okla. Leg., 1st Sess. 
(1995). Thus, as of April 17, 1995, Title 68 remains “open” 
to possible late amendment, including amendment of the 
type proposed in the legislation reproduced in the appen- 
dix to the Chickasaw Nation’s brief-in-chief. 


So as to be able to present this Court with the most 
up-to-date information concerning relevant develop- 
ments before the Oklahoma legislature, Respondent 
Chickasaw Nation, having monitored such developments 
during the nineteen days following the defeat of H.B. 
1522 before the Oklahoma Senate, has deferred to filing of 
this supplemental brief until one week prior to oral argu- 
ment.2 The Chickasaw Nation will continue to monitor 
legislative developments during the week of April 17-24, 
and in the event that Petitioner Tax Commission does not 
address the matter during its oral argument, will, with 
this Court’s permission, report on such developments 
during the Chickasaw Nation’s allotted oral-argument 
time. 


It should be noted that by rule, the 1995 Oklahoma 
legislature has established May 3, 1995, as the deadline 
date for the filing of conference committee reports, and
