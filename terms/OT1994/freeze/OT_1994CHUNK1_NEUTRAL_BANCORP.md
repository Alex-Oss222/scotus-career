## D. Law entering the event

### 1. Statutory authority and Article III

28 U.S.C. §2106, enacted June 25, 1948, 62 Stat. 963, provides:

> The Supreme Court or any other court of appellate jurisdiction may affirm, modify, vacate, set aside or reverse any judgment, decree, or order of a court lawfully brought before it for review, and may remand the cause and direct the entry of such appropriate judgment, decree, or order, or require such further proceedings to be had as may be just under the circumstances.

The text authorizes specified dispositions of a judgment **lawfully brought before** the reviewing court and permits appropriate further proceedings **as may be just under the circumstances**. It says “may,” not “must.” Whether and how that power operates after the admitted consensual mootness must be analyzed with Article III and the precedents; the statute's text does not itself state a settlement-specific test, burden of proof, exception, or automatic-vacatur command. The admitted mootness prevents adjudication of the underlying bankruptcy merits. The separate question is the Court's power and proper use of authority to dispose of the pending proceeding and existing judgment.

The statutory wording and 1948 enactment citation were checked in [Cornell LII's §2106 reproduction](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2106); attempts to retrieve the historical official section online did not succeed. The text check supplies no later decisional interpretation. *Munsingwear* independently identifies §2106 as the source of broad supervisory power over lower federal judgments.

### 2. Surviving pre-divergence authority

**United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36, 39–41 (1950).** The Government's appeal from denial of an injunction became moot after the commodity was decontrolled. It did not request vacatur and acquiesced in dismissal; the unmodified judgment later precluded related damages litigation. The Court affirmed that preclusive effect. In explaining the available procedure the Government failed to use, the opinion described vacatur or reversal and remand with directions to dismiss as the established practice for a federal civil judgment whose review becomes impossible through happenstance, preserving future litigation rights and preventing an unreviewable judgment from producing legal consequences. Both the description of the practice and the actual refusal to relieve the Government from its procedural omission matter. The opinion does not adjudicate an express request arising from the consensual settlement now admitted here.

**Karcher v. May, 484 U.S. 72, 82–83 (1987).** Former legislative officers could not pursue the legislature's appeal in personal or other capacities in which they had not become parties. Their successors had authority to pursue the litigation but chose not to do so. The Court distinguished *Munsingwear*: review had not become unavailable through circumstances unattributable to the parties; the controversy ended when the losing legislature declined to pursue review. It dismissed for want of jurisdiction without vacating the lower judgments. The successor-substitution and party-status setting must accompany the voluntary-abandonment reasoning. Whether consensual settlement warrants the same treatment is the present question, not a holding already supplied by *Karcher*. O'Connor authored the opinion; Stevens and Scalia joined it. White's separate concurrence emphasized that no proper appeal had reached the Court and did not erase the lower judgments.

**Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc., 321 U.S. 671, 676–678 (1944).** Corporate dissolution prevented completion of appellate merits review but did not establish that the district injunction was moot against everyone potentially bound by it. The Court described supervisory authority to make an appropriate disposition even when it could not proceed on the merits. It vacated the appellate reversal and restored the district injunction, leaving enforcement against persons potentially bound for proper further proceedings. This supports a distinction between merits power and dispositional authority, while its actual remedy shows that disposition can preserve one judgment and vacate another. It is not a holding that every lower judgment must disappear whenever review ends.

These authorities support examining the source of mootness, the availability and use of review, the effect on the parties' adjudicated rights, and the appropriate treatment of particular judgments. They do not supply a mechanically controlling settlement-specific checklist. The generality, weight, and application of those considerations remain for adjudication.

### 3. Current controlling law and its limits

The authorized entering-law slice supplies the following law. Links in it are authority navigation; no raw earlier Record was opened for this packet.

- **Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp., November 30, 1993.** An unsuccessful intervention applicant may seek review of the intervention denial, but an undisturbed denial does not provide party status to challenge the underlying judgment. Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding an independent question neither stated nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. An antecedent relationship and briefing alone did not justify reviewing the separate intervention denial there. The dismissal leaves settlement vacatur, collateral estoppel, and a new construction of §2106 undecided. The current case's admitted party and review posture must not be replaced with *Izumi*'s distinct defect.
- **Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, May 16, 1994.** After a stipulated dismissal neither incorporating settlement obligations nor retaining enforcement jurisdiction, the separate contract-enforcement controversy requires an independent jurisdictional basis. A lawful order can incorporate settlement obligations or retain enforcement authority; under Rule 41(a)(1)(ii), the parties must agree and the court must timely embody the provision in its order. Rule 41(a)(2) separately permits the court to impose lawful terms in its discretion. Awareness, approval, or private consent alone does not supply jurisdiction. Rule 60(b) reopening remains undecided. This concerns settlement enforcement, not disposition of a judgment already under appellate review.
- **Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., June 6, 1994.** Rejection of an ordinary private settlement defense does not permit immediate collateral-order appeal merely because it defeats a claimed right to avoid trial. The category lacks the required public importance; independently, final-judgment review adequately protects ordinary settlement rights despite intervening litigation expense. Other authorized interlocutory routes retain their own requirements. This does not decide settlement vacatur or the validity of a particular settlement.

## E. Competing positions and the questions they must answer

The candidate supplies the parties' requested outcomes and attributes the arguments below. Because the Supreme Court briefs and motion papers were not recovered, these are **supplied litigating positions and supported analytical alternatives**, not verified quotations, exhaustive brief summaries, or concessions. The Ninth Circuit's earlier bankruptcy opinion cannot verify what either party later argued about vacatur.

**For vacatur:** Bancorp can invoke *Munsingwear*'s protection against legal consequences of an unreviewable judgment, §2106's discretion, and the value of settling litigation. On this view, resolving the parties' dispute need not leave the losing party bound by an adverse judgment it was pursuing through review. The argument must address why the voluntary resolution is materially different from the abandonment of review in *Karcher*, what force the judgment's public and third-party consequences should have, and why its requested treatment of the identified appellate judgment is appropriate. A rule favoring settlement need not authorize advisory resolution of the original merits.

**Against vacatur:** Bonner can distinguish unavoidable loss of review from a litigant's voluntary decision to settle. *Karcher* supports attention to that difference, while the actual outcome in *Munsingwear* shows that inability to complete review does not invariably erase the existing judgment. Public and third-party interests in adjudication can be offered as reasons not to treat judgment erasure as a private settlement entitlement. That is an argument to evaluate, not an already adopted settlement-specific holding. This position must address *Munsingwear*'s broad description of practice, the settlement interest, and whether equitable discretion should remain available in circumstances beyond the admitted facts.

**Residual-power objection:** The candidate attributes to Bonner an additional objection that Article III removes power to grant vacatur after mootness. Treat that attribution as supplied and unverified by the missing motion papers. The legal question can nevertheless be tested on the admitted posture. It must confront §2106, *Munsingwear*, and especially *Walling*'s distinction between inability to decide merits and authority to make a proper disposition. A court's power to act and the propriety of exercising that power are separate issues.

No express waiver, concession beyond admitted mootness, Solicitor General position, or material amicus position is established. The packet supplies no particular evidentiary burden or heightened proof standard for settlement vacatur.
