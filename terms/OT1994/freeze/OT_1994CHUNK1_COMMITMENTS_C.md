## 9. Nebraska Department of Revenue v. Loewenstein — December 12, 1994

**Frozen result:** Each of the eight non-Stone Justices votes to reverse the Nebraska Supreme Court's judgment as to Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 and remand. On the stipulated transactions the negotiated repo return is income from the private counterparty's repayment obligation, not interest on an obligation of the United States within §3124(a). All eight can join that bounded statutory ground and application. No separate writing is needed to preserve a different committed proposition.

### Operative ground, record and limits

Actual Treasury securities were delivered through the Federal Reserve book-entry system. There is no separate promissory note. Neither fact is disregarded. The simultaneous agreement to resell and repurchase at principal plus a privately negotiated return, the separation of that return from Treasury yield, and the allocation of market risk identify the source of the income taxed. Legal ownership labels alone do not answer whether the United States owes these earnings. The privately promised return remains private when federal securities secure performance.

Section 3124(a) protects federal obligations and taxes requiring those obligations or their interest to be considered in computing the tax; the recodification did not remove indirect-tax protection. Its exceptions for nondiscriminatory corporate franchise or substitute corporate nonproperty taxes and estate/inheritance taxes do not cover the individual income tax here. The State prevails because the taxed income fails the protected-obligation antecedent, not because an exception applies. Subsection (b)'s federal income-tax direction, pre-March 1, 1941 FHA contractual protection, and territorial/District exclusions do not erase subsection (a).

Rockford, Stevens's unanimous 1987 opinion joined by O'Connor and Scalia, identifies the federal obligation rather than extending immunity to every private instrument supporting federal finance. Its subject was privately issued GNMA certificates backed by a contingent guarantee; the present securities really are federal debt. The relevant analogy is nevertheless the distinction between the government's own payment obligation and the separate private income-producing obligation. Smith v. Davis supplies that distinction. Memphis Bank, American Bank, and First National Bank of Atlanta retain protection where computation actually taxes protected federal obligations or their income. Nothing here narrows their indirect-tax rule.

The borrowing-power objection also fails on this record: the asserted financing-market benefit does not transform the private promise or establish the direct constitutional burden absent in Rockford. No general test invalidating every indirect market effect is announced. No amount, refund, recomputation, differently structured repo, or unappealed ruling 22-87-4 is adjudicated.

## 10. In re Baby K — December 12, 1994

**Frozen result:** Each of the eight non-Stone Justices votes to affirm on EMTALA the obligation to stabilize the identified acute respiratory emergencies within available staff and facilities, subject to the statute's compliant-transfer and informed-refusal alternatives. All eight can join the precise emergency-duty construction and conditional direct-conflict analysis below. None commits to deciding the independent disability statutes, general end-of-life care, parental authority, or a damages claim. No separate writing is required if the Court's formulation carries these limits.

### Components and common adopted scope

1. **Determined acute emergency and stabilization.** When this participating hospital determines that Baby K presents an emergency respiratory condition, §1395dd(b)(1) requires the examination and treatment within its available staff and facilities necessary to stabilize that condition, or a transfer satisfying subsection (c). Acute symptoms must satisfy subsection (e)(1)(A): without immediate attention, serious jeopardy to health, serious impairment of bodily functions, or serious dysfunction of an organ or part could reasonably be expected. The hospital concedes this respiratory emergency and that ventilation prevents its deterioration. Its inability to cure anencephaly does not negate the admitted ability to stabilize respiratory distress. The standard of stabilization is no likely material deterioration within reasonable medical probability resulting from or during transfer. Transfer includes discharge, with the statutory exclusions for departure after declaration of death or without hospital-affiliated permission. The separate labor provisions are not decided.
2. **Medical judgment, screening and intent to transfer.** Uniform screening under subsection (a) and its emergency-department capability limitation do not replace the triggered subsection (b) duty. The statute does not become a general malpractice standard or a right to any preferred treatment. Once the hospital identifies the acute emergency and concedes necessary stabilizing care, a uniform decision to withhold it from similarly situated anencephalic infants is not statutory compliance. The transfer-centered definition measures stabilization; it does not make a hospital's voluntary intent to transfer a prerequisite that permits withholding all stabilizing care while keeping the patient. A medical/ethical objection to ventilation is not, by itself, factual unavailability of otherwise available hospital staff and facilities. The actual statutory capacity limitation remains intact.
3. **Transfer and refusal alternatives.** An unstabilized transfer requires an informed written request by the patient or legally responsible person, or a physician-signed certification that reasonably anticipated medical benefits outweigh the risks, with a summary of those risks and benefits. If a physician is not physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person may sign after consulting a physician who determines the balance and later countersigns. Every route requires an appropriate transfer: available treatment minimizing risk; a receiving facility with available space, qualified staff and agreement to accept; available emergency-related records, the request/certification and required identification of an on-call physician who refused or failed to appear; qualified transport personnel and equipment, including needed life support; and applicable additional Secretary requirements. Section (b)(2) deems treatment compliance after an offered examination/treatment, risks-and-benefits information, patient/representative refusal, and reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Section (b)(3) similarly requires a transfer offered in accordance with (c), information, refusal, and reasonable steps to secure written informed refusal. The mother's objection alone satisfies neither provision. Here absence of certification and of an accepting facility are separate obstacles; a future compliant transfer is not foreclosed.
4. **Receiving-hospital branch.** The appellate record does not finally settle whether each nursing-home movement is discharge/new presentation or a transfer. For an appropriate transfer requiring this hospital's specialized capabilities or facilities, subsection (g) forbids refusal if it has capacity. The supplied pediatric intensive-care and ventilation capabilities support this conditional branch; capacity and appropriateness are not omitted or presumed for every future movement. The judgment can affirm the stated emergency duty without settling that classification universally.
5. **State law.** The reported Virginia §54.1-2990 clause says its article does not require treatment a physician considers medically or ethically inappropriate. The reported article concerns adult directives/surrogates; it does not establish an applicable infant-treatment authorization here. If a state requirement applies and directly conflicts with the federal duty established above, §1395dd(f) displaces it only to that extent. No wholesale invalidation of Virginia medical law follows, and state law cannot by characterization eliminate otherwise available federal statutory capability.
6. **Remedy and unreached grounds.** Affirmance rests on the identified EMTALA emergencies, with all express alternatives and conditions retained. No damages or unlimited ongoing treatment is ordered. Physician administrative penalties under (d)(1)(B), the distinct on-call protection under (d)(1)(C), and hospital-only civil liability under (d)(2)(A) are not merged. The on-call exception requires notification and failure to appear plus the treating physician's benefits-over-risks determination; it does not exempt the hospital or the nonappearing physician. The private action requires personal harm directly resulting from a violation, allows the specified state-law personal-injury damages and appropriate equitable relief, and carries the two-year limitation. This hospital's prospective declaration does not adjudicate such a claim. The ADA/Rehabilitation Act grounds remain unreviewed, as do CAPTA/state-law questions and parental/guardian authority. Reversal of EMTALA alone would not dispose of them or authorize the hospital's full proposed declaration.

## 11. Plakas v. Drinski — January 9, 1995

**Frozen result:** Each of the eight non-Stone Justices votes to affirm summary judgment on the presented Fourth Amendment claim and to affirm the county's judgment on the separately examined equipment/training contention. The common ground is narrow: the supported record supplies an immediate serious threat despite critical examination, and the Constitution does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic or an undeveloped equipment list. No Justice is committed to a categorical exclusion of all earlier police conduct, an immunity disposition, or municipal immunity merely because an officer is protected.

### Officer component and alternative paths

Graham governs the complete contemporaneous reasonableness inquiry, including the severity of the events, immediate threat, resistance and flight, from a reasonable officer's on-scene perspective rather than hindsight or subjective malice. Garner supplies the serious-threat requirement and its necessity/warning qualifications. The supported record describes prior assault with a poker, threats that one of them would die, prolonged surrender efforts, an advance with the poker raised from approximately twelve to fifteen feet, retreat blocked involuntarily, and a shot at close range. This is an immediate-threat case, not shooting a nondangerous suspect merely to prevent flight.

The estate disputes the charge, but the lower report supplies no supported physical or testimonial contradiction creating a material trial issue. Police-only eyewitnesses require critical review, not either automatic credit or automatic rejection. Perras was not a defendant or county employee. The photograph was not itself in the appellate record, and the body was moved before the paramedic's observation. No new reconstruction or credibility finding can be invented. The judgment rests on the legally sufficient supported record, not trial factfinding.

The rejected alternatives are not abstractly irrelevant. Their actual availability matters: Perras had CS repellant and identified distance and danger to other officers; no chemical on Drinski's belt is established. Canine services had been offered about ten minutes earlier; no dog beside Drinski is established. Additional retreat was blocked when the charge culminated. The prior conduct described does not establish a separate constitutional wrong causally producing the confrontation. The beating theory was not pleaded, and the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. The decision therefore does not adopt or reject Gilmere's rule for a developed claim of unjustified police violence provoking retaliatory danger.

### County component, separate from personal liability

The county is not entitled to the officer's personal immunity. Monell and Canton require identification of an underlying federal deprivation, the municipal policy/training ground, appropriate culpability, and causal connection. Collins makes attribution and deprivation separate; it does not make all municipal creation of danger lawful. Heller defeats a theory deriving municipal liability solely from a nonexistent violation in the particular force used, but does not alone answer every independently pleaded municipal wrong.

The actual county theory contends that inadequate equipment or training created circumstances in which Drinski's final choice was reasonable. Its strongest lawful path is a proved municipal policy causing a cognizable deprivation with the required deliberate indifference, even where an officer's personal liability fails. Its strongest obstacle is that this record offers an undeveloped constitutional equipment list rather than evidence establishing that distinct wrong, causation and culpability. The Constitution does not require instruction in every device simply because it exists. On the supplied case the independent theory fails on its own footing, not by transferring personal immunity to the county.

## 12. Interstate Commerce Commission v. Transcon Lines — January 10, 1995

**Frozen result:** Each of the eight non-Stone Justices votes to reverse the categorical legal bar to the ICC's public credit-rule injunction and remand for regulation-specific relief supported by the billing record. All eight can join the common statutory ground below. Individual debts are not adjudicated or discharged. The distinct previously resolved coded-rate component is not reopened.

### Statutory ground and remedial boundaries

The filed-rate requirements in §§10761–10762 remain in force, but are read together with Congress's express §10743(b)(1) delegation to govern credit/payment and §11702(a)(4)'s civil-enforcement authority. An injunction enforcing valid credit regulations is not a judicially selected rate or an implied shipper defense. The exception in §11702(a)(4) for actions under provisions governing rate reasonableness or discrimination does not bar this action to enforce distinct billing/credit conditions; the action does not ask the court to judge a rate's reasonableness or discriminatory character. The credit statute's purpose of preventing discrimination does not convert every enforcement of its payment rules into an excluded rate-review action.

Maislin prohibits defeating a filed rate merely because a lower negotiated rate seems fair. It does not erase the separate credit delegation. Commercial Metals rejects an implied private consignor defense from a credit violation; it separately recognizes Commission enforcement tools, including injunctions. Reiter's express statutory counterclaims and remedial allocation reinforce the distinction without themselves deciding this action. Kmart concerns a published cancellation leaving no effective essential tariff component; the present collection restriction has a different express statutory source. MCI requires a valid delegation, which must be demonstrated rather than assumed from an agency label. ABF's review of actual delegated remedies provides an analogy, not transportation authority.

The Commission must establish the violated requirement and the entitlement/scope of the requested injunction. Summary-judgment statements that original notices were absent and revised individual bills not supplied are not a determination of every account's amount or status. The remand permits the lower courts to apply the valid rules to proved claims and to enter properly confined relief. It does not rewrite tariffs, discharge all ordinary freight principal, create an automatic private defense, or award estate distributions.

### Regulatory qualifications binding the common ground

- Authorized credit begins on the day after bill presentation. The default is fifteen days including weekends and holidays; a published alternative and combined standard/additional authorized credit cannot exceed thirty days. The tariff's ninety-calendar-day period after shipment for discount eligibility is different from the regulation's ninety days after expiration of authorized credit for revised billing.
- The provisions for service charges during an additional authorized period and charges after credit expires retain their distinct publication and other conditions. No service charge may be imposed within the standard period. Post-expiration charges neither authorize delay nor relieve the carrier of good-faith credit reassessment before extending credit again. They do not replace the collection-expense rules.
- Section 1320.2(g)(1) permits reasonable and certain collection-expense liquidated damages through an identified dollar/percentage charge or loss of an identified discount after a stated payment date no earlier than authorized-credit expiration.
- Under (g)(2), tariff damages and conditions must be clear and operate without unlawful prejudice or unjust discrimination. They apply only to original, separate, independent bills, not aggregated balance-due demands on past shipments by a bankruptcy trustee or other collector. They do not apply to clear clerical or ministerial errors, including nonreceipt of a bill, a lost payment check, or misaddressing. They cannot duplicate collection fees separately imposed under the bill of lading for the same transportation. Liability arises only after authorized credit expires and requires a revised bill or notice within ninety days after that expiration.
- Section (g)(3)'s outside-tariff contractual bill-of-lading collection-charge route remains separate. Its existence does not establish that any particular demand qualifies.
- Section 1320.3(c) requires the original bill or accompanying notice to state the late-payment penalties, time limits, collection/service charges, discount terms and warning that untimely payment may incur tariff penalties. The revised-bill condition does not replace this original notice requirement.
- Each exception must be applied on its actual facts. The earlier-shipment statutory regime governs. New relief under the Negotiated Rates Act or Trucking Industry Regulatory Reform Act is not decided without the presented claim and applicable transition text.
