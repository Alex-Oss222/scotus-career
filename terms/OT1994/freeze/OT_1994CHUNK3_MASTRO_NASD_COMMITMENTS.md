## Common source premise and precise issue

Rule 21(f)(5), quoted on printed page 10 of the filing and visually verified in the PDF, makes subparagraphs (f)(1)–(4) applicable only to new agreements signed by an existing or new customer **after September 7, 1989**. The 1985 agreement is excluded; no later new agreement has been established. The timing of the arbitration and the promise to use institutional rules then in effect do not silently remove the rule's express agreement-date qualification.

All eight associates commit that **Rule 21(f)(4) is unavailable as an operative regulatory prohibition controlling this agreement or independently overriding its terms**. They also reject a supposed incorporation of the prohibition stripped of its temporal condition. They do not infer that the customers conceded any position: the filing is the United States and SEC's amicus advocacy. The Court's own legal assessment is not an unreported arbitration finding.

The loss of that ground does **not alone** answer whether the complete contract authorizes punitive relief. The broad submission of controversies, general New York law provision, chosen institutional arbitration, absence of an express punitive clause, and FAA review remain. NASD Code §41(e)'s reference to damages and other relief describes award contents, not an express authorization of punitive damages in every dispute. The explanatory Manual can be assessed for its limited interpretive significance; it is not automatically incorporated governing law or an independent override of an actual restriction. Each associate accepts these source distinctions for the bounded renewed analysis.

## Counterpath, minimum revisions, and effect on the existing freeze

The strongest attempt to retain Rule 21 would emphasize the promise to use institutional rules then in effect when arbitration occurred. All eight reject treating that language alone as an instruction to discard those rules' own transition terms. A separately proved post-cutoff new agreement could change the application, but no such record event is established. It cannot be invented to preserve an earlier rationale.

All eight also reject the opposite shortcut: absence of this later prohibition is not affirmative proof that the 1985 contract incorporates Garrity, that the institutional provisions have no interpretive significance, or that the punitive award necessarily exceeded contractual authority. Regulatory unavailability and contract interpretation answer different questions. A legally sufficient whole-contract route may remain; a narrower §10(a)(4) route may remain if supported within the admitted question and actual law. This file does not newly adopt the amicus's complete analogy to labor-arbitration deference, any universal delegation rule, a constitutional waiver, an unpreserved public-policy ground, or a due-process holding about the punitive amount.

**Mandatory minimum revision to any operative rationale:** remove Rule 21(f)(4) as law governing the 1985 bargain or as a prohibition compelling punitive availability; state the (f)(5) agreement-date limitation if the rule is discussed; keep §41(e), Manual guidance, and genuine contract interpretation separate. Historical context may be described as such, but cannot perform the work of an applicable legal command.
