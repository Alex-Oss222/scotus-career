## Independent enforcement-guidance assessment

*Kolender v. Lawson*, 461 U.S. 352, 355–361 & n.8 (1983), requires minimum enforcement guidelines independently of notice. Its majority rejects a universal demand that facial vagueness affect every possible application, with particular attention to criminal consequences and protected conduct. Each of the eight associates' definition commitments remains independent of such a categorical facial bar. Neither the existence of an obvious elective-abortion application nor a possibility of a valid prosecution supplies the reason for rejecting the actual claims here. Likewise, a culpability term cannot automatically answer an otherwise unguided enforcement demand.

**Abortion definition.** The purposeful-termination reading supplies an enforcement boundary as well as physician notice: an enforcer must identify a termination undertaken with the purpose described by the actual definition, not merely an intentionally performed diagnostic act followed by an unintended loss. The two excluded purposes remain operative. This is a defined medical act and purpose category, not an officer's authority to prescribe additional compliance requirements case by case. The providers' amniocentesis hypothetical falls outside that category for the same objective statutory reason regardless of an official's preference. The strongest contrary contention remains that this reading impermissibly relocates the purpose language. The associates reject that grammatical severance for the reasons frozen in the main handoff; they do not rely on a default fault provision or every-application rule. A contrary authoritative construction would require its own review.

**Emergency definition.** Enforcement is bounded by the identified pregnancy complication, the two specified outcomes, the twenty-four-hour delay forecast in the second alternative, and the physician's prospective best clinical judgment. An official cannot treat personal disapproval of abortion as a substitute for those criteria, invent a new emergency condition, or establish all elements merely by pointing to a different later prognosis. Professional assessment of gravity, urgency, irreversibility and bodily function can be examined against clinical evidence and the statutory criteria; it is neither unconstrained official satisfaction nor unreviewable physician assertion. The legislature has specified what the professional judgment must concern. That distinguishes Kolender's undefined identification demand while leaving Colautti's separate concern about an unclear clinical trigger fully applicable where such ambiguity is shown.

The strongest objection is that those qualitative criteria, combined with punitive consequences and unresolved fault, could encourage defensive delay or inconsistent enforcement. That calls for careful examination and preservation of concrete challenges. It does not, on the specified facial wording objection, establish that the law delegates the virtually complete discretion condemned in Kolender. This conclusion does not turn “best” into “good faith,” delete any severity or immediacy word, guarantee immunity, or resolve a distinct claim that the emergency's substantive scope excludes constitutionally necessary care. The lower court's protective construction retains only the status and limits stated in the main handoff.
