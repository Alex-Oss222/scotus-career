## Threshold decisions and review channel

All eight reach the merits. Davida Johnson, Pam Burke, Henry Zittrouer and George Seaton are established residents and eligible voters in the challenged Eleventh District. Their asserted injury concerns their own current electoral assignment through the plan whose racial predominance is admitted; they do not rely only on disapproval of racial awareness elsewhere. The challenged State action directly governs that assignment. Relief against the plan would redress the asserted personal treatment if their constitutional theory succeeds. Residence is evidence of that relationship, not an automatic standing rule. The allegation of racial treatment does not establish its unconstitutionality. No individual racial identity, denied ballot, lost election or denied constituent service is supplied by inference.

Warth requires a plaintiff personally affected by the challenged conduct while keeping entitlement to prevail distinct from standing. The admitted present assignment, unlike a hypothetical future move or third-party decision, supplies the personal connection here. The operative Lujan rule still requires evidence appropriate to the litigation stage: established residence and the admitted predominance finding supply the factual predicates; a bare allegation is not used to replace a missing disputed fact. Northeastern Florida and current Adarand show that a personal equality claim need not prove an ultimate electoral or economic victory. They do not establish that every race-conscious map denies equal opportunity. That remains the merits dispute. [Warth, 422 U.S. 490, 498–502](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/422/490).

The strongest threshold objection is that, because Shaw rejects design alone, the alleged treatment is only an abstract objection with no legally cognizable injury. The model rejects converting that substantive answer into a finding that these personally governed voters cannot litigate their claimed constitutional status. A generalized assertion about other citizens would be different. At least these four seek the same unitary district relief, so DeLoach's separate standing need not be decided. His corrected Tenth-District residence is retained; no campaign injury or automatic district-residence presumption is used to bring him within the other four's position.

Goosby distinguishes a constitutional controversy foreclosed as frivolous from one with doubtful merits. The serious question here is whether asserted official racial assignment, stereotyping and representational status constitute the personal deprivation Shaw preserves, despite the absence of conventional exclusion or dilution. Shaw strongly governs the ultimate answer but expressly preserves noneconomic equality injuries and does not establish a universal downstream-loss requirement. Applying that boundary, together with the State-permission and statutory-justification distinctions, is a substantial constitutional controversy. The fact that the model ultimately rejects the claimed distinction for four Justices does not make it fictitious. Neither disagreement with precedent alone nor the inventory's admission of review suffices for this conclusion. ## Common merits premises and question definitions

The reconstructed lower court found actual racial predominance and subordination of traditional districting considerations. This model accepts that finding. It does not demote it to an allegation, reconstruct witness credibility, or invent neutral mapmaking reasons. The challenger theories are racial design, sorting, stereotyping and representational consequences themselves. No independent exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior legal civic status or separately pleaded statutory violation is present. Whether the design itself creates unequal personal treatment remains a disputed legal conclusion, not a factual stipulation against the challengers.

The current rule is simulated Shaw's substantive rejection of territorial racial design alone as the asserted deprivation. Its exact reservations preserve real noneconomic wrongs and ordinary pleading; they do not create a general requirement to prove lost elections or downstream monetary injury. Croson remains state/local classification law at its own scope. Adarand preserves the federal remedial rule rather than imposing uniform strict scrutiny; Jenkins concerns repair of an independently established segregation violation. Neither changes Shaw or makes every benevolent objective valid.

Q1 asks whether predominance alone supplies the constitutional violation/trigger. Q2 tests whether the asserted sorting, stereotypes and representation establish the independently cognizable personal inequality. Q3 distinguishes permissible voluntary inclusion from federal compulsion. Q4 assigns evidentiary roles to shape, demographics, direct purpose and departures from ordinary principles. Q5 accepts or rejects the admitted finding and states its consequence. Q6 separately answers strict scrutiny's trigger and the expressly required conditional question about the asserted VRA/DOJ justification.

The common conditional answer to Q6 is narrow. Assuming strict scrutiny applies, the asserted statutory-compliance justification fails on the reconstructed record. Section 5 did not require this particular district, and reliance on DOJ demands did not establish that it was reasonably necessary to meet the substantive obligation. The second premise is essential: lack of an exact command for one map is not by itself proof that every alternative compliance measure lacks a sufficient fit. Beer supplies nonretrogression; Pleasant Grove preserves discriminatory-purpose inquiry. Gingles, Holder and De Grandy retain distinct §2 proof and totality requirements, without a maximization duty. Compliance may be important without an administrator's demand being conclusive evidence of necessity. No independently established alternative justification is supplied. The model assumes for this bounded answer that lawful compliance can be a sufficiently important interest; it need not decide its compelling status in every possible application because the asserted fit fails here.

For Justices who apply Shaw, that answer is conditional explanation and is not an independently sufficient ground for the judgment, an actual strict-scrutiny holding, or a judgment on a separate §5 action. For Justices who would replace Shaw and trigger strict scrutiny, the same record failure becomes operative in their proposed liability conclusion. An eight-Justice agreement on the conditional result does not create eight votes to apply strict scrutiny or invalidate the district.
