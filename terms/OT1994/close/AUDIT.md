## Unresolved findings

### F01 — Medium — Rambo: the Standards candidate attaches the one-year limit to the wrong event

**Location:** `close/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md:4082`, “Economic-capacity modification of nonscheduled disability awards.”

The current-rule sentence says that changed conditions or a factual mistake “must fall within one year after the last compensation payment” or claim rejection. That turns the period for seeking/reviewing modification into a period in which the change or mistake must occur. A factual mistake may predate the final compensation payment, and the rule does not require the qualifying condition to arise only after payment ends. The distinction matters to the availability of modification.

The current [Rambo Record](../records/Metropolitan_Stevedore_Co_v_Rambo_merits_1995-06-12.md), including its first controlling proposition (line 20) and Public Projection (line 137), places the limitation on modification; its Render Input (chunk 7, line 396), render (chunk 7, line 341), and Holdings entry (line 15584) preserve that construction. The official opinion quotes §22 and describes the period in which a party may seek modification, rather than an occurrence window for the ground. See [515 U.S. 291, 295, 298–299](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-515/pdf/USREPORTS-515-291.pdf). The discrepancy is confined to the Standards formulation; the eight-Justice construction, burden of proving a qualifying capacity change, §8(h) limits, and reversal/remand otherwise agree.

**Unresolved work:** Correct the candidate's actor/timing relationship from its canonical source in a separate task; do not alter the adjudication.

### F03 — Medium — Witte: current workspace copies omit §5G1.3(a)'s priority and mandatory-consecutive trigger

**Locations:** `workspace/continuity.md:4247,4251`; `workspace/neutral-projection.md:4213,4217`.

These current-law handoffs retain the earlier subsection (b)/(c) account. They do not state subsection (a)'s priority: consecutive sentencing is required when the instant offense was committed while serving the undischarged term, including work release, furlough or escape, or after sentencing for that term but before service began. Subsection (b)'s concurrency rule applies only if (a) does not. Omitting that order can prescribe concurrency for a case controlled by (a).

The corrected [Witte Record](../records/Witte_v_United_States_merits_1995-06-14.md), Public Projection, Render Input, render, and both candidates now carry the complete (a), then (b), then (c) sequence. They also explain why (a) does not govern Witte's cocaine conduct, which preceded the earlier sentencing and imprisonment. No future sentence is imposed here. The missing qualification is thus a defect in the reusable workspace rule, not a change to Witte's judgment or the distinct constitutional and coordination coalitions.

**Unresolved work:** Replace the two stale workspace rule/explanation copies with the complete current source, preserving all existing qualifications and coalition distinctions.
