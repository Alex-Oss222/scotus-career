## Event

United States v. Shabani, 513 U.S. 10, No. 93-981. Merits decision, November 1, 1994. Argued October 3, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The question is whether 21 U.S.C. §846 requires proof of an overt act in furtherance of a narcotics conspiracy. Shabani was convicted after the district court refused his requested overt-act instruction. The Ninth Circuit, 993 F.2d 1419, reversed under its circuit precedent and expressly left sufficiency and other appellate claims undecided. The indictment omitted an overt-act allegation; its sufficiency on that ground follows from the same statutory construction.

Render form: compact. Basis: 9–0 judgment, a single common statutory ground and a bounded appellate remand.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand for the reserved appellate issues, 9–0. Supporting Justices: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice opposes.

The appellate court must apply the rule that §846 has no additional overt-act element and address the remaining preserved issues. No new factfinding is required on the overt-act question. The Court does not order acquittal, a new trial or unconditional reinstatement of every aspect of the conviction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act

**Controlling proposition:** Section 846 of Title 21 requires neither an allegation nor proof of an overt act beyond the unlawful narcotics agreement. The Government must still prove the unlawful agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt; neither mere association nor awareness of another person’s plan is enough.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this construction and its application; no fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress made the unlawful agreement the completed conspiracy offense. Section 846 contains no additional overt-act language, while 18 U.S.C. §371 expressly requires an act to effect the conspiracy’s object. Nash supplies the common-law understanding of conspiracy, and Singer shows why Congress’s omission of an extra act requirement matters when it uses that settled term. This reading does not punish a thought: the agreement itself is conduct, and the Government must prove knowing participation in it. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary element cannot rest on circuit precedent when the governing statute supplies none. Felix’s treatment of successive prosecutions did not decide this statutory question and cannot add the omitted element. Nor does lenity supply it; ordinary construction resolves the question without the residual ambiguity addressed in Ratzlaf. The refused overt-act instruction therefore supplies no ground for reversal of the conviction. The Court leaves the appeal’s reserved sufficiency and other claims to the court of appeals.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Nash v. United States*, 229 U.S. 373 (1913), applies the common-law meaning under which the agreement completes conspiracy without a further overt act.
- *Singer v. United States*, 323 U.S. 338 (1945), applies the significance of Congress’s omission of an additional overt-act requirement from a statutory conspiracy offense.
- *United States v. Felix*, 503 U.S. 378 (1992), remains a double-jeopardy decision distinguishing agreement from substantive conduct; it did not decide §846’s overt-act element. Its narrower separate position likewise supplies no contrary statutory element.
- *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), preserves lenity for genuine ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction; that condition is absent here. Its distinct willfulness rule is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule for every conspiracy statute is announced. The Court does not decide credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, the remaining appellate objections or the ultimate validity of the conviction. Current double-jeopardy law is unchanged.

This adds an offense-specific statutory construction. It does not alter the knowledge requirements of other crimes, create a universal rule about omitted statutory language, or change Felix, Dixon, Grady or Ratzlaf beyond the distinctions stated above.

## Event

U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18, No. 93-714. Decision on mootness and vacatur, November 8, 1994. Argued October 4, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The Ninth Circuit, 2 F.3d 899, affirmed the district court’s reversal of a bankruptcy ruling and remanded for further bankruptcy proceedings. Its decision recognized the availability of the new-value doctrine; it did not find a particular plan confirmable. A consensual settlement while review was pending mooted the underlying controversy. ## Holdings

### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ending a controversy and disposing of the pending review are distinct judicial acts. Section 2106 authorizes a reviewing court to affirm, modify, vacate, set aside or reverse a judgment lawfully before it, and to direct further proceedings as justice requires. Walling recognizes the authority to make an appropriate disposition even when merits review cannot continue. Munsingwear likewise rests on power to address the consequences of a judgment that has become unreviewable. A categorical rule stripping all dispositional authority after mootness would contradict that practice. Article III still forbids an advisory ruling on the bankruptcy dispute; the Court therefore does not decide whether the new-value doctrine survives or whether a former proposed plan could be confirmed. Kokkonen concerns jurisdiction over a separate contract-enforcement controversy after dismissal, which no party obtains here. The existing review supplies the occasion for considering vacatur; the parties’ consent does not create jurisdiction or dictate the equitable result.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute.

### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Controlling proposition:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a losing party’s voluntary surrender of review ordinarily leaves the existing judgment in place. Munsingwear protects against an unreviewable judgment when review fails through happenstance; Karcher shows why a party’s own decision to end review receives different treatment. Bancorp accepted a consensual resolution while review remained available and has identified no further equitable reason to erase the appellate judgment. Settlement is lawful and valuable, but that value does not turn a completed judicial act into a term the litigants can remove by agreement. The judgment also has a public significance beyond their immediate bargain. Exceptional circumstances may justify relief in another settlement case; a clause requesting vacatur alone merely repeats the private preference and cannot supply the missing equity. This is no heightened evidentiary test or finding of settlement misconduct. The Court denies the requested alteration and dismisses this proceeding as moot, without affirming the Ninth Circuit’s bankruptcy reasoning or determining the judgment’s effect on every outsider.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

## Event

Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., including Walsh’s employee action, 513 U.S. 30, No. 93-1197. Merits decision, November 14, 1994. Argued October 3, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.

The question is whether PATH, the Port Authority’s bistate railway subsidiary, is an arm of New York and New Jersey entitled to Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employees’ federal suits. Hess and Walsh alleged separate workplace injuries and brought separate FELA actions; Hess also invoked the Boiler Inspection Act. The district courts dismissed under the Third Circuit’s arm-of-State precedent and the unmet one-year condition attached to the States’ consent. The reviewed appellate disposition left those dismissals in place. The two actions remain distinct; this decision resolves their common immunity premise.

## Holdings

### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Controlling proposition:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that PATH’s actual relationship to New York and New Jersey does not make these suits actions against the States. Lake Country Estates requires examining the compact structure, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes separate public entities from the State itself. Gubernatorial appointment and veto, legislative restrictions and public transportation responsibilities favor PATH’s position, but do not overcome this enterprise’s separate identity, revenues, assets and obligations. Article XV requires equal state appropriations only while operating revenues are inadequate, only for salaries, office and other administrative expenses, in amounts recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, with each State obligated only up to $100,000 annually. It is no promise to pay judgments. Financial independence therefore carries great weight in this mixed setting, without making treasury liability immunity’s sole condition. Feeney assumed status when deciding consent; it did not resolve the antecedent question now presented. Because PATH is not an immune arm, its conditional consent is unnecessary to overcome an Eleventh Amendment bar. The Court leaves ordinary defenses and liability for the alleged injuries to proceedings on remand.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 14, 1994, PATH is not an arm entitled to the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employee actions. Lake Country’s compact inquiry is clarified through the actual separate legal and financial structure, with treasury exposure carrying great weight but no universal sole-treasury test. The Third Circuit’s contrary PATH-status rule cannot sustain these dismissals. FELA coverage, consent of an entity that actually is immune, immunity abrogation and collateral-order appealability remain separate and unchanged.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. She would treat PATH as an arm of the States because the governors and legislatures retain substantial control over its commissioners, actions, projects and spending. Financial exposure matters, but the protection of a State from federal suit also safeguards its institutional authority. A self-supporting state enterprise need not lose that protection merely because it can pay judgments from its own revenues. Lake Country Estates permits an entity-specific finding of arm status; it does not compel treating PATH’s state controls as equivalent to the more local structure there. Puerto Rico Aqueduct recognizes protection from suit for qualifying arms while leaving the qualification question separate.

The dissent does not read Article XV as an unlimited state guarantee. Its equal, conditional administrative appropriations, recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, bind each State only up to $100,000 annually while operating revenues are inadequate. The claimed sovereign relationship rests on the combined legal controls, not an invented obligation to pay judgments. Having found arm status, the dissent treats consent as a separate requirement. Feeney’s federal-forum consent did not eliminate its conditions; the district courts found both actions outside the one-year period running from accrual. Hilton’s state-court FELA holding does not revive constructive waiver in federal court. The dissent therefore would affirm these dismissals without deciding liability, damages or a new exception to the consent legislation. This position does not control the Court’s status ruling or remand.

## Holdings

### The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character

**Controlling proposition:** To convict under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1) or (2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knew the performer was a minor, as well as the applicable knowing handling act. It need not prove knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition; the actual statutory production, depiction and commerce-or-mail conditions remain distinct, and the Court does not decide whether knowledge of the jurisdictional nexus is required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven Justices directly adopt the factual-knowledge construction and its reasoning. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that knowingly reaches the factual features that make these depictions criminal. Morissette supplies the presumption of culpability, applied to the whole statutory setting rather than confined by the distance between the adverb and the later conditions. Staples supports requiring awareness of the characteristics that distinguish a prohibited object from an otherwise familiar one, while Posters preserves the difference between factual knowledge and legal classification. Neither decision mechanically resolves this statute. Ratzlaf’s distinct willfulness language required knowledge of unlawfulness there; no such general requirement follows here. **Controlling proposition:** The renewed objection that §2256 defines a minor as a person under eighteen, rather than under sixteen, does not establish substantial First Amendment overbreadth in §§2252(a)(1)–(2) as construed here. **Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part III and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the asserted difficulty of distinguishing older adolescents from adults does not establish the substantial overbreadth needed to invalidate this statute on its face. Its particular statute protected children under sixteen, but the decision did not constitutionalize that age as an upper boundary. Permission to treat some sixteen- or seventeen-year-olds as adults in other settings likewise does not require adult treatment for sexual exploitation. ## Holdings

### The Commission lacks independent certiorari authority for this FECA enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 437d(a)(6)'s authority to litigate and appeal civil enforcement actions through the Commission's general counsel does not displace 28 U.S.C. §518(a)'s allocation of Supreme Court representation in this FECA corporate-contribution action. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains available, and the express certiorari powers in 26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d) remain confined to actions in which the Commission appears under those respective funding sections.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule. Stone-Zsela and Stevens dissent; Ginsburg does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress did not authorize the Commission to petition independently in this enforcement action. Providence Journal establishes that authority to conduct litigation below does not necessarily include authority to represent the Government here. Congress may create an exception, and ordinary construction determines whether it has done so. Section 437d(a)(6) gives the Commission substantial enforcement autonomy, but its general appeal language must be read with section 518(a)'s specific allocation and the express certiorari grants in the presidential-funding statutes. Those grants apply only to actions brought within their respective sections; this corporate-contribution action is not one of them. Enforcement exclusivity and the separate power to defend actions do not settle who may affirmatively invoke this Court's review. Germain permits the grants to coexist with their distinct conditions. The Court adopts neither a rule that appeal invariably excludes certiorari nor a required formula for statutory exceptions. #### The acute respiratory emergency triggers stabilization or lawful transfer

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the immediate respiratory emergency is the condition to be stabilized. Uniform screening under subsection (a) therefore cannot replace the separate stabilization obligation after an emergency is found. #### Refusal compliance requires an actual offer and informed refusal

**Holding and operative rule:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement met when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. **Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that refusal provisions are conditional compliance routes, not exceptions triggered by disagreement alone. A treatment offer and a transfer offer perform different statutory work. Here the record supplies neither a compliant transfer offer nor a finding that the mother refused the stabilizing treatment the hospital must offer. Her opposition to the requested declaration therefore does not release the hospital from its emergency obligation. King supports reading the alternatives within the coordinated enactment, and Suter cautions against confusing a defined duty with an assumed remedial result. ## Holdings

#### The private repo return is outside the federal-obligation exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3124(a) does not exempt the return on these repurchase agreements because it is owed under a separate private financing obligation, rather than on the United States' securities; actual book-entry delivery does not change the source of the negotiated earnings. The statute continues to protect direct and indirect taxation of federal obligations and their interest, except nondiscriminatory corporate franchise or substitute corporate nonproperty taxes and estate or inheritance taxes; the State prevails because this income is outside the protected subject, not because an exception covers this individual income tax.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the obligation producing the taxed return controls. Rockford distinguishes the Government's payment obligation from a private obligation supported by federal credit, and Smith v. Davis supplies the same direct-obligation boundary. Here the Trusts advance cash for a privately negotiated return unrelated to Treasury yield. The Trusts transmit the issuer's payments to the Seller-Borrower; the Seller-Borrower maintains collateral under the margin provisions; and the Trusts retain recourse for a deficiency after default liquidation. Those arrangements identify secured financing for this exemption even though securities actually passed through the Federal Reserve system and no separate note was signed. Frank Lyon respects genuine business arrangements; it does not determine whether the Government owes these particular earnings. The State therefore may tax the private return. The removal of surplus language during recodification did not abolish indirect-tax protection. The Court decides neither repo ownership under other bodies of law nor a transaction conveying genuine beneficial ownership and federal coupon income.

**Holding and operative rule:** A ruling that expressly addresses federal-obligation repos does not, without an identified favorable policy for comparable state or local repos, establish the discriminatory taxation alleged here. #### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

The District Court granted summary judgment for respondents, and the court of appeals sustained denial of the credit-rule collection injunction, reasoning that the filed-rate obligation barred it and that alternative enforcement measures remained. The question is whether the ICC's express credit and enforcement provisions authorize this injunction and whether it is appropriate for the challenged loss-of-discount liquidated damages. The separate earlier coded-rate component is outside review.

**Render form: compact.** The unanimous decision has a margin greater than one vote; no material change in judgment, controlling propositions, non-Chief alignment, remedy, mandate, reserved issues or precedent treatment requires the full form.

## Participation

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision. No Justice is disqualified or otherwise nonparticipating.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded, 9-0, for implementation of an injunction confined to the admitted-unlawful loss-of-discount liquidated-damages class, with genuine scope, inclusion and amount questions beyond the concessions preserved. Ordinary unpaid freight principal and other estate receivables are excluded from the restraint.

## Holdings

### The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** Under the applicable pre-reform 49 U.S.C. §§10743(b)(1) and 11702(a)(4), the ICC may seek a judicial injunction against collection violating valid credit regulations, and §§10761–10762's filed-rate commands do not categorically defeat that enforcement. Section 11702(a)(4) excludes an action under a provision governing the reasonableness or discriminatory character of rates; this action enforces specified credit and billing requirements rather than asking the court to determine rate reasonableness or discrimination.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's credit delegation and its filed-rate commands must operate together. Section 10743 permits credit under Commission regulations governing payment and preventing discrimination; §11702 supplies the public enforcement channel. The anti-discrimination purpose of credit regulation does not itself turn this suit into the rate-review action excluded from that channel. Commercial Metals rejected an implied private consignor defense while recognizing the Commission's express enforcement tools. Maislin rejected a negotiated-rate policy inconsistent with the filed-rate scheme, not every independently authorized statutory remedy. Reiter likewise preserves claims and defenses supplied by the Act without creating a general equitable exception. MCI requires an actual delegation, which these credit and enforcement provisions provide; Gardner's insistence on statutory meaning before deference does not eliminate that delegation. The trustee therefore cannot defeat this action merely by expressing the challenged damages as a difference between filed rates. Statutory authority still does not establish every violation or entitle the Commission to every requested injunction. The Court decides no general private defense, constitutional question or entitlement under later transportation reforms.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Southern Pacific Transportation Co. v. Commercial Metals Co., 456 U.S. 336 (1982): applies its distinction between an implied private credit defense and the ICC's express public enforcement tools; the private-defense holding remains intact.
- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990): distinguishes its invalid negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy from enforcement under the separate credit delegation; no general equitable departure from filed rates is permitted.
- Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993): applies its preservation of specifically enacted statutory routes alongside filed-rate duties; counterclaims, recoupment, primary jurisdiction and separate-judgment discretion retain their own conditions.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218 (1994): applies the requirement of actual delegated authority; its limit on replacing mandatory tariff filing does not erase Congress's distinct credit and enforcement provisions.
- Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115 (1994): applies statutory construction before deference while preserving actual delegated discretion; its veterans-benefit causation and eligibility rules remain confined to that statute.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): preserves deference within a lawful statutory delegation; neither a litigation demand nor general agency expertise supplies power Congress withheld.

### The admitted collection class violates the credit conditions

**Controlling proposition:** The challenged loss-of-discount liquidated-damages collections violate 49 C.F.R. §1320.3(c) because the original bills omitted the required late-payment warning, §1320.2(g)(2)(vi) because revised billing occurred years later rather than within ninety days after authorized-credit expiration, and §1320.2(g)(2)(iii) because the demands aggregate past-shipment balances. The concessions establish those violations and the charge's liquidated-damages character for the challenged class, without establishing every account's inclusion or amount, every regulatory exception, statutory authority, remedial entitlement or a distinct contractual collection route.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the admitted charge and violations supply the factual predicates for this confined enforcement action. Commercial Metals requires attention to the difference between lawful freight charges and the consequences of a particular credit violation; it does not convert every charge into unconditional freight principal. Here the request concerns additional collection damages measured by the loss of a discount. The original bills omitted the warning, the revised billing came years after the regulatory deadline, and the trustee demanded aggregate past-shipment balances. The concession of noncompliance does not rest merely on bankruptcy or on the existence of an undercharge demand. Nor does the tariff's ninety-day payment condition measured from shipment replace the regulation's period measured from expiration of authorized credit. The missing complete account file leaves genuine applications beyond the concessions open; it does not reopen the conceded class's violation or character. No omission of the loss-of-discount term from the filed tariff is alleged, and no independent contractual entitlement is established.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Commercial Metals, 456 U.S. 336: preserves its private-defense rule and distinction between lawful freight principal and enforcement of particular credit obligations; it does not immunize the admitted liquidated-damages class.
- Security Services, Inc. v. Kmart Corp., 511 U.S. 431 (1994): distinguishes an effectively canceled essential tariff component from this violation of credit conditions; no absent tariff component or wholesale tariff cancellation is found here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All operative credit qualifications remain. Authorized credit begins the day after bill presentation; absent a published alternative the period is fifteen days including weekends and holidays, and permitted combined credit cannot exceed thirty days. Service charges within the standard period are prohibited. Published additional-period charges and published post-expiration charges are distinct; post-expiration charges neither authorize delay nor remove the carrier's good-faith reassessment obligation before another credit extension. The tariff's ninety calendar days after shipment for discount eligibility is separate from the revised-billing deadline measured from authorized-credit expiration.

Section 1320.2(g)(1) permits reasonable, certain collection-expense liquidated damages through an identified dollar or percentage charge or loss of an identified discount after a stated payment date no earlier than authorized-credit expiration. Section (g)(2) requires clear terms and conditions, prohibits unlawful prejudice or unjust discrimination, permits only original separate independent bills rather than aggregate past-shipment balance-due demands, excludes clear clerical or ministerial errors such as nonreceipt, a lost payment check or misaddressing, and prohibits duplicate collection fees already provided by the bill of lading for the same transportation. Damages arise only after authorized credit expires, with a revised bill or notice issued within ninety days after that expiration. Section (g)(3)'s separate contractual bill-of-lading collection-charge route outside the tariff is not an entitlement established here. Section 1320.3(c)'s original-bill or accompanying-notice requirements concerning penalties, time limits, service and collection charges, and discount terms remain distinct from revised billing. No unreported exception, account inclusion or amount is inferred.

### A confined injunction is appropriate and must be implemented on remand

**Controlling proposition:** On the admitted violations and the defined loss-of-discount liquidated-damages request, an injunction preventing collection of that class is an appropriate exercise of the ICC's public enforcement authority, and the case must be remanded for its confined implementation. The lower court must specify the order's terms and may resolve genuine scope, inclusion and amount questions beyond the concessions or a properly presented distinct lawful collection basis, but may not reopen the admitted violation or charge character, restrain ordinary unpaid freight principal or other estate receivables, or treat this decision as a universal discharge.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this injunction fits the violation and the protections Congress authorized. Commercial Metals recognizes public enforcement, and ABF Freight supports respect for a reasoned remedial choice within its statutory limits, subject to judicial review. The aggregation restraint directly prevents prohibited demands. The notice and billing protections guard shippers against collection penalties imposed without timely warning; permitting the admitted demands would realize the advantage those requirements forbid. A future warning cannot supply notice on the original bill, and a new bill cannot make years-late billing timely. An order forbidding these same demands would accomplish the confined restraint; other sanctions or an order addressing only future carrier conduct would not themselves stop collection. Judicial preference for another permissible tool therefore does not justify denial on the grounds presented. The Commission's selection is reasoned and appropriate, not binding merely because the agency requests it. The court retains responsibility for statutory boundaries and its order's fit. ABF's Board-order setting supplies no transfer of remedial power or universal deference formula. Genuine account questions remain, while bankruptcy supplies neither tariff immunity nor permission to collect these unlawful penalties.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Commercial Metals, 456 U.S. 336: applies its recognition of public enforcement to a confined injunction protecting the intended beneficiaries of the violated credit rules; no new automatic private defense follows.
- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, 510 U.S. 317 (1994): applies by analogy its respect for explained statutory remedial discretion and meaningful review; its review of a Board remedy neither transfers that power to the ICC nor binds a court to every injunction request.
- Maislin, 497 U.S. 116: preserves the ban on a general nonstatutory negotiated-rate exception; the filed-rate objection cannot be recast as an equitable balance that defeats these expressly authorized credit conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court independently examines authority, established violation, the restraint's legal character and its fit. Review remains available for an unsupported premise, insufficient explanation, arbitrariness, excessive scope or inconsistency with the statute. No automatic Chevron deference to litigation demands, universal agency-remedy standard or broader limitation on Chevron is adopted. The prior distinct coded-rate component, individual private defenses, other statutory claims, estate distribution, exact dollar relief and unpresented reform-statute relief remain outside the disposition. A distinct contractual route requires its own presentation and proof; nothing here accepts its availability for these claims.

## Precedent Treatment

Commercial Metals preserves the private-defense/public-enforcement distinction; Maislin preserves the filed-rate rule against a general negotiated-rate policy; Reiter preserves separately enacted claims and judicial procedures. MCI and Gardner require actual statutory authority while leaving valid delegated discretion intact. Chevron is neither displaced nor converted into deference to a request for litigation relief. Kmart's effectively canceled tariff component remains a distinct ground, unused here. ABF Freight supplies the bounded remedial-discretion analogy described in the Holdings block; neither its Board remedy nor its broader public-remedy rationale is imported as a universal rule. No precedent is overruled, and no withdrawn portion of Delta Traffic supplies authority.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 10, 1995, the Court recognizes this express public credit-rule injunction as compatible with filed-rate obligations and directs its implementation for the admitted-unlawful loss-of-discount liquidated-damages class. Before this decision, Commercial Metals and Reiter distinguished public enforcement and enacted statutory remedies from a general private equitable defense without deciding this injunction. The new application resolves statutory availability, the conceded class's violation and character, and the appropriateness of this confined restraint. The actual credit periods, billing triggers, notice duties, clerical-error and duplicate-fee limits, distinct contractual route and §11702(a)(4) rate-review exception retain the terms set out in the Holdings block. Ordinary freight principal and other estate receivables remain outside the restraint; no universal debt discharge, automatic private defense, exact amount, later-reform entitlement or general agency-deference rule is created.
