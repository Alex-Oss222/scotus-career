### *United States v. Ibarra*, No. 90-1713

Summary disposition, October 15, 1991

*United States v. Ibarra*, 502 U.S. 1 (1991), came to the Court on a petition for certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 920 F.2d 702. Without argument, the Court considered whether a timely Government motion to reconsider suppression postponed appellate finality even though the motion renewed an abandoned ground. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. The Marshall line was vacant, and Thomas had not taken the oath.

The Court granted certiorari, vacated the judgment, and remanded by an 8-0 vote. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter supported the disposition. A per curiam opinion was joined by every participating Justice; there was no separate writing.

The Court holds that a single timely motion asking a district court to alter a suppression ruling renders that ruling nonfinal for appeal purposes until the motion is decided, even when the motion renews a ground the Government earlier abandoned. The full appeal period begins upon disposition. The unanimous per curiam opinion supplies the controlling authority.

The Court applies *United States v. Healy*, which supplies the rule that a timely criminal rehearing request suspends finality, and *United States v. Dieter*, which rejects classifications based on the kind or strength of asserted error. The Government filed one motion within thirty days and appealed within thirty days after denial. Earlier abandonment may matter to the merits, but the motion still sought alteration of adjudicated rights. Making time depend on assessing strength, preservation, or likely success would leave litigants guessing about the jurisdictional date. *Department of Banking v. Pink* is distinct because its filing did not seek substantive alteration.

*Healy* and *Dieter* are applied, and *Pink* is distinguished. No appeal period or preservation rule changes. The timely-reconsideration rule enters law on October 15, 1991. The decision addresses only finality: it leaves bad faith, successive motions, waiver, the renewed ground's possible waiver on the merits, suppression, consent, and the search undecided.

The Tenth Circuit must consider the appeal. The Supreme Court proceeding is closed, and the case returns to the Tenth Circuit for further appellate proceedings.

Source note: Official United States Reports, 502 U.S. 1–8, read in full. No quotation is required.

**End of entry: *United States v. Ibarra*, summary disposition, October 15, 1991.**

### Darby v. Cisneros, No. 91-2045

Merits disposition, 1993-06-21

Argued 1993-03-22; decided 1993-06-21. On writ of certiorari to Fourth Circuit, 957 F.2d 145: optional intra-agency review required as a judicial exhaustion condition; dismissal directed.

The question presented is whether §704 permits courts to require an optional administrative appeal before APA review of otherwise final agency action.

An administrative law judge ordered an 18-month debarment. The parties did not take the available appeal to the Secretary within 15 days. The agency’s scheme did not make that appeal both mandatory and a condition that rendered the action inoperative pending appeal. The district court addressed the debarment, but the court of appeals required further administrative exhaustion.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

On optional agency appeal, Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas supported the judgment.

Blackmun delivered the Opinion of the Court on the statutory rule and reversal, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Scalia and Thomas did not join the legislative-history support.

In an APA action, an otherwise final agency decision is reviewable without an optional superior-agency appeal unless a statute expressly requires exhaustion or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the challenged action inoperative pending it. Courts cannot add their own exhaustion requirement beyond those alternatives; remaining APA finality, reviewability and adequate-remedy conditions still apply.

Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, supported on this holding by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, controls by a majority of 9 Justices.

The Court holds that Congress has specified the effect of additional administrative review on finality. Section704 makes otherwise final action final despite reconsideration or an available appeal, subject to an express statutory requirement or an agency rule combining mandatory appeal with inoperativeness. HUD’s optional review provision does not satisfy that rule-based exception. McKart explains institutional reasons for exhaustion but cannot authorize courts to rewrite a governing statute. McCarthy preserves congressional control while addressing judicially imposed exhaustion in a different setting, and McNeil enforces an express FTCA prerequisite rather than a free-standing exhaustion policy. The Government’s expertise and efficiency arguments do not supply the missing statutory condition. The Court’s procedural holding does not establish that the debarment was substantively wrong or that every agency action is reviewable. Lincoln’s distinct reviewability limits remain. The judgment is reversed so the remaining issues can be addressed under the correct APA framework, without requiring this optional appeal.

5 U.S.C.§704 — statutory requirement OR mandatory-and-inoperative agency rule; alternatives and conjunction preserved. McKart, 395 U.S.185 — exhaustion policy subordinate to enacted conditions. McCarthy, March 4, 1992 — express statutory primacy retained; different damages-only setting. McNeil, May 17, 1993 — express FTCA exhaustion distinguished. Lincoln, May 24, 1993 — distinct reviewability limits preserved.

The decision adds a binding APA limit on extra judicial exhaustion. No repeal of express statutory exhaustion, review of nonfinal action or universal common-law exhaustion holding.

Scalia and Thomas join the statutory construction and judgment but not the legislative-history discussion. No separate opinion issues; text and structure independently support the Court’s holding.

These sources support identity, argument and decision dates, lower proceedings, factual posture and cited historical texts. The reporter citation identifies the source location. No automatic restoration of a particular district-court merits result or exoneration from debarment. ### Edenfield v. Fane, No. 91-1594

Merits decision, 1993-04-26

Edenfield v. Fane; No. 91-1594; October Term 1992. Argued: 1992-12-07; decided 1993-04-26. Source locator: 507 U.S. 761 (1993). Eleventh Circuit, 945 F.2d 1514 (1991). On writ of certiorari to that court.

Question: Whether Florida may categorically prohibit direct personal solicitation of business clients by certified public accountants on the justifications and evidence presented.

Fane proposed contacting business executives to offer CPA services. The Board invoked prevention of fraud, protection of privacy and professional independence. It supplied neither studies nor anecdotal evidence showing the alleged harms from CPA solicitation; the Dooner affidavit made generalized assertions. Contrary professional evidence and the controlled business setting distinguished lawyer solicitation of vulnerable accident victims. The injunction reaches the challenged business-solicitation prohibition, not fraud or coercion.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participate at argument (1992-12-07) and decision (1993-04-26). No recusal or other nonparticipation is established.

The Court affirms, 8–1. The prohibition on direct, uninvited business solicitation by CPAs cannot stand on this record. Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support the judgment; O’Connor dissents. Justice Kennedy delivers the Court’s commercial-speech opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Blackmun also concurs separately; O’Connor files a dissent. Neither separate writing has another joiner.

When truthful, nonmisleading solicitation concerns lawful CPA services, the State must demonstrate that the harms supporting its restriction are real and that the restriction alleviates them to a material degree. Florida’s unsupported predictions about direct solicitation of business clients do not satisfy that burden; the ruling neither requires empirical studies in every case nor immunizes deceptive or coercive solicitation.

The controlling authority is Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). This proposition commands a majority of the Court.

The Court holds that the State must connect its legitimate concerns to the speech it suppresses. Central Hudson requires more than a substantial interest stated in the abstract; Fox preserves a reasonable fit rather than demanding the least restrictive imaginable rule. Florida’s interests in preventing fraud, protecting privacy and maintaining independence are substantial, but the record does not establish the asserted threat from CPA business solicitation. The Board’s generalized affidavit does not show that such contacts undermine professional judgment, and the professional evidence points the other way. Ohralik permitted prophylaxis against personal solicitation by lawyers in circumstances involving vulnerable clients and pressure for immediate commitment. Business executives considering accounting services can control the time and setting and need not decide immediately. That difference defeats the proposed analogy, not every professional prophylactic rule. Nor can labeling the prohibition a time, place and manner restriction cure its failure materially to advance the asserted interests, even assuming that alternative framework applies. Fraud, coercion and materially different settings remain open.

Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the real-advancement burden.

Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: retains reasonable fit without least restrictive means.

Ohralik v. Ohio State Bar Assn., 436 U.S. 447: distinguished by professional setting, vulnerability and pressure.

Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: alternative time/place/manner characterization cannot cure deficient advancement.

Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: consistent application; no expansion to strict scrutiny.

Before: Discovery Network, Central Hudson and Fox require real advancement and reasonable fit. After: those requirements invalidate this unsupported CPA business-solicitation prohibition; no strict scrutiny, universal studies requirement or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted. Fraud, coercion and materially different professional settings remain regulable.

Blackmun joins the opinion and judgment but maintains that truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech deserves more protection than the intermediate framework affords; that broader view is not adopted. O’Connor dissents. Her Shapero and Peel positions support attention to the culture and responsibilities of a profession, not simply the sophistication of a particular prospective client. She would allow Florida to protect independence and prevent pressure through a prophylactic restriction without waiting for proven abuse. In her view the Court understates experience-based risks and permits an injunction extending to business solicitation generally on a record centered on Fane’s proposed conduct. She would reverse; no Justice joins her writing.
