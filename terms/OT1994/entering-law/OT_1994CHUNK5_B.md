## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory state regulation

### Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations

**Governing question:** An exercise of ordinary federal commerce power making a State choose between administering federal instructions for private actors and assuming their property and liabilities.

**Current rule:** Congress may not require an unwilling State to choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting ownership and possession of their waste with associated liability. Two unauthorized commands do not create a lawful option. State officials' advocacy for the federal statute neither enlarges congressional powers nor estops a noncompacting State from challenging the command. This differs from authorized monetary or access inducements leaving the State free to choose whether and how to govern, where consequences of refusal fall on private generators rather than transferring their responsibilities to the State.

**Authority by component:**
- Compulsory-ownership alternative and officials' assent: [New York v. United States, “5. Compulsory state ownership or regulation”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/New_York_v_United_States_merits_1992-06-19.md), June 19, 1992; O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five), extending Hodel's legislative-compulsion boundary.
- Distinct actual monetary and access inducements: New York, “3. Monetary incentives” and “4. Access incentives,” June 19, 1992; O'Connor Parts III-A and III-B, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author, applying the received spending and commerce doctrines without creating a numerical coercion threshold.

**Controlling construction:** The 1985 Act's general state-responsibility language states an objective implemented through the specified incentives; it supplies no independently enforceable command to regulate. This is the five-Justice holding in New York, “2. No independent command in the responsibility clause,” not a generic avoidance rule.

**Present operation effective:** New York v. United States, June 19, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Generally applicable regulation of state activity under Garcia and Baker remains distinct and undisturbed. Actual compact obligations, state market conduct, state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law are reserved. The decision establishes no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule or power to direct other States to accept waste.

**Holdings navigation:** [New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992) — Federal incentives and compulsory state administration](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#new-york-v-united-states-505-us-144-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Mandatory replacement benefits

### Mandatory replacement benefits measured by an ERISA-covered plan

**Governing question:** What instructions govern mandatory replacement benefits measured by an ERISA-covered plan?

**Current rule:** ERISA §514(a) preempts a replacement-coverage obligation insofar as it uses a covered health plan's benefit content as the mandatory legal measure, even if replacement benefits may be administered separately through exempt workers' compensation arrangements. Requirements measured solely by exempt governmental or church plans are not preempted by that holding. Section 514(b) savings questions and ordinary indirect economic effects remain unresolved; the obligation's 52-week cap supplies no federal safe harbor.

**Authority by component:**

- Covered-plan content as a mandatory legal measure: [District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade — Covered-plan content as a mandatory legal measure](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/District_of_Columbia_v_Greater_Washington_Board_of_Trade_merits_1992-12-14.md), 1992-12-14. Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt the same operative rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1992-12-14; District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade, for the components identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal sovereign immunity from affirmative

### Federal sovereign immunity from affirmative bankruptcy money judgments

**Governing question:** Whether original §106(c), estate-property jurisdiction, or property and trust characterizations authorize an affirmative monetary recovery from the United States under §550.

**Current rule:** Original §106(c) does not unequivocally waive immunity from an affirmative personal money judgment recovering an unauthorized transfer under §550. A plausible reading under which its application-and-binding language governs nonmonetary or claim determinations defeats clear consent to that remedy; legislative history cannot supply missing enacted clarity. Sections 106(a) and (b) retain their own claim-linked and offset operations. General estate-property jurisdiction under §1334(d), an asserted trust interest, and the money's origin in estate assets do not supply the missing consent. An order requiring payment of a sum, rather than adjudicating identified property under the court's control, cannot be preserved by calling it a property proceeding or defensive equitable recoupment.

**Authority by component:**

- Original §106(c) monetary-waiver construction: [United States v. Nordic Village — Original §106(c) does not clearly authorize this monetary recovery](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Nordic_Village_merits_1992-02-25.md), February 25, 1992.
- Distinction between jurisdiction and consent; inadequacy of property, trust, and recoupment labels: [Nordic Village — Jurisdictional and property labels do not sustain this money judgment](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Nordic_Village_merits_1992-02-25.md), February 25, 1992. Scalia's opinion joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas controls both.

**Present operation effective:** *Nordic Village*, February 25, 1992.

**Open question:** Genuinely distinct specific-property proceedings require their actual facts and independent authority; their validity is not decided. State immunity and a different express statutory waiver remain outside the decision.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992) — Federal sovereign immunity](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-nordic-village-inc-503-us-30-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Reimbursed contractor taxes

### Reimbursed contractor taxes and derivative federal recovery

**Governing question:** What instructions govern reimbursed contractor taxes and derivative federal recovery?

**Current rule:** Federal reimbursement or advance funding of an independent contractor's state taxes alone creates no federal common-law money-had-and-received action for alleged state-law assessment errors. Economic Treasury cost does not make the contractor's tax a direct federal tax. Subrogation takes a derivative right subject to pre-existing infirmities; where governing state law requires active assertion and the contractor's claim lapses before that assertion, later federal suit does not revive it. No universal choice between state and federal limitations for federal plaintiffs is decided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Additional firearm convictions

### Additional firearm convictions and consecutive enhancement

**Governing question:** What instructions govern additional firearm convictions and consecutive enhancement?

**Current rule:** Under the applicable §924(c)(1), conviction means the finding of guilt. A second or subsequent conviction on a distinct firearm count in the same prosecution can trigger the enhanced tier; the later offense need not have followed a previous conviction. Enhanced terms remain subject to the statutory consecutive-service command. No general appellate-finality or proportionality rule follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Firearms offered as consideration

### Firearms offered as consideration in a drug transaction

**Governing question:** What instructions govern firearms offered as consideration in a drug transaction?

**Current rule:** Offering a firearm as consideration in a drug exchange is use under the applicable §924(c)(1) despite use as merchandise rather than as a weapon. The separate during-and-in-relation-to condition requires a purpose or effect respecting trafficking, not accident or coincidence: at minimum facilitation or potential facilitation. Offering the gun as consideration satisfies that relation. Mere possession is not held sufficient, and the outer boundary of sufficient facilitation remains open.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing unlawfulness

### Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

**Governing question:** What §5322(a)’s willfulness requires for the charged §5324(3) structuring offense.

**Current rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowledge of the financial institution’s reporting duty and a purpose to evade it. Reasonable circumstantial inferences may establish knowledge; knowledge of a section number or having read the Code is unnecessary. If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity on that additional knowledge requirement, lenity independently requires this narrower criminal construction; conflicting history or general purpose cannot enlarge the crime beyond sufficiently clear enacted language.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge elements and proof: [Ratzlaf v. United States — Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md#public-projection), 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.
- Independent residual-lenity ground: [Ratzlaf v. United States — Residual ambiguity independently requires the narrower criminal construction](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md#public-projection), 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Present operation effective:** Ratzlaf v. United States, 1994-01-11.

**Exceptions and limits:** The §5322(a) exception for §5315 and its regulations is separate from §5324(3). This is no universal ignorance-of-law defense, legal-proceeds requirement or civil-forfeiture rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowledge of machinegun

### Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

**Governing question:** What must be proved when a conventional-looking rifle falls within the whole-weapon machinegun definition.

**Current rule:** For §5861(d) possession of an unregistered rifle prosecuted as a whole-weapon machinegun under §5845(b), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics making it a machinegun: it shoots, is designed to shoot, or can readily be restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. Knowledge that the weapon is unregistered or that possession is illegal is not required. Ordinary possession of a rifle is not enough to presume knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge of the regulated weapon characteristics: [Staples v. United States — Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Staples_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Present operation effective:** Staples v. United States, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** The holding does not decide every separately defined machinegun part or combination, every other regulated weapon, or the sufficiency of proof in every conversion setting.

**Holdings navigation:** [Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#knowledge-of-the-characteristics-making-this-rifle-a-machinegun).

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA guaranteed-benefit

### ERISA guaranteed-benefit exclusions and insurer fiduciary functions

**Governing question:** Which insurer assets are excluded from plan-asset status by ERISA §401(b)(2).

**Current rule:** A guaranteed-benefit policy itself is a plan asset; the insurer’s underlying assets are not deemed plan assets solely because it issued the policy only to the extent the policy genuinely guarantees the amount of benefits. A nonguaranteed component does not gain the exclusion from general-account placement. Plan-asset status subjects the insurer to §3(21)(A) fiduciary duties to the extent it exercises the specified authority or control over management or disposition; breach, causation, loss and remedy remain separate. McCarran-Ferguson does not displace ERISA’s express insurance-related duties. Compatible state insurance regulation survives the saving clause, but a concrete conflicting obligation cannot control over ERISA itself. An agency interpretation cannot erase the exclusion’s component limitation.

**Authority by component:**

- Component-specific exclusion and asset-and-function inquiry: [John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank — The guaranteed-benefit exclusion operates by component](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/John_Hancock_Mutual_Life_Insurance_Co_v_Harris_Trust_Savings_Bank_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection), 1993-12-13. Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. Six Justices adopt the component-specific construction and its application to the disputed free funds.
- Federal duties and compatible state insurance regulation: [John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank — Insurance regulation does not displace ERISA's express direction](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/John_Hancock_Mutual_Life_Insurance_Co_v_Harris_Trust_Savings_Bank_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection), 1993-12-13. Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. This six-Justice ground rejects the asserted state-insurance obstacle to affirmance.
- Statutory limit on the categorical agency exclusion: [John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank — An agency interpretation cannot erase the qualified exclusion](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/John_Hancock_Mutual_Life_Insurance_Co_v_Harris_Trust_Savings_Bank_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection), 1993-12-13. Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. These six adopt the statutory-limit ground; the dissenters separately reject the categorical agency position without adopting the Court's entire investment-risk analysis.

**Present operation effective:** John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 1993-12-13.

**Open question:** No universal guarantee formula, illiquidity-only rule, categorical rejection of changed agency views or general rule for agency litigation positions is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** [An agency interpretation cannot erase the qualified exclusion](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#an-agency-interpretation-cannot-erase-the-qualified-exclusion); [The guaranteed-benefit exclusion operates by component](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-guaranteed-benefit-exclusion-operates-by-component); [Insurance regulation does not displace ERISA's express direction](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#insurance-regulation-does-not-displace-erisas-express-direction).

## Standards and Tests selection: Successive prosecutions

### Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt

**Governing question:** When evidentiary overlap, conspiracy or completed criminal contempt bars a successive prosecution.

**Current rule:** Evidentiary use of an uncharged crime does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. A conspiracy and its substantive objects remain distinct offenses; prosecuting the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because acts help prove both. Grady's essential-conduct rule remains operative and does not displace the established agreement/substantive-offense distinction or become a same-evidence or same-transaction rule. Ordinary evidentiary restrictions retain their separate force. Beyond Blockburger, Grady bars a later prosecution when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense already prosecuted; evidence reuse or a common episode alone does not suffice. Completed nonsummary criminal contempt places the accused in criminal jeopardy despite private initiation or a protective purpose. When contempt necessarily prosecutes a substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same offense again. Coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns remain outside Dixon. Foster II-V may proceed under its case-specific judgment, but no controlling rationale governs those counts: the Blockburger-only/overruling and special judicial-order rationales are incompatible, not logical subsets. Grady survives without an invented general contempt exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Evidentiary use is not prior prosecution: [United States v. Felix](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Felix_merits_1992-03-25.md), March 25, 1992, holding 1, White's unanimous Part I.
- Grady's relation to the distinct-agreement rule: the same record, holding 2, “Agreement remains a distinct offense,” White's Part II for Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. The two narrower judgment votes do not limit the seven-Justice rationale.

- Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection: [United States v. Dixon — Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/United_States_v_Dixon_merits_1993-06-28.md), June 28, 1993. White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again: [United States v. Dixon — Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/United_States_v_Dixon_merits_1993-06-28.md), June 28, 1993. White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law: [United States v. Dixon — Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/United_States_v_Dixon_merits_1993-06-28.md), June 28, 1993. ## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: United States v. Nordic Village


#### United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1629.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.

##### Whether original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) waives immunity from this affirmative personal money judgment

**Holding:** Original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) does not unequivocally waive the United States’ immunity from an affirmative personal money judgment recovering an unauthorized transfer under §550. A plausible construction permitting binding determinations of governmental rights and estate liabilities without an affirmative payment order defeats the required clear consent to this monetary remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices control the statutory threshold.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for original §106(c) and the personal monetary remedy adjudicated.

**Material application:** The trustee avoided an unauthorized transfer under §549 and sought $20,000 from the United States under §550 after corporate money paid an officer’s individual taxes. Ordinary recovery authority under §550 does not answer the separate sovereign-consent question; no claim-linked waiver under §106(a) or (b) supplies this judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not validate the diversion, strip §106(c) of effect, decide state constitutional immunity, or construe a different or later waiver. It does not alter §106(a) or (b); neither subsection supplies consent to the actual judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and the $20,000 personal money judgment set aside for want of sovereign consent. No substitute award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89; *United States v. Mitchell*, 445 U.S. 535; and *United States v. King*, 395 U.S. 1, supply the requirement of unequivocal consent to the particular monetary remedy. *Hoffman v. Connecticut Department of Income Maintenance*, 492 U.S. 96, supplies plurality statutory reasoning adopted for this federal question; its fractured state judgment is not a controlling federal-waiver holding. *Ruckelshaus v. Sierra Club*, 463 U.S. 680, and *McMahon v. United States*, 342 U.S. 25, prevent enlarging consent beyond Congress’s language. *United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. 528, supports giving both paragraphs of §106(c) operative work. *Dellmuth v. Muth*, 491 U.S. 223, supplies textual clear-statement reasoning without a new ruling about States.

##### Whether bankruptcy jurisdiction, property and trust characterizations, or equitable recoupment sustain this money judgment

**Holding:** A general grant of jurisdiction over bankruptcy-estate property in §1334(d), an asserted trust interest, or the origin of transferred money in estate assets does not supply federal consent to this personal money judgment. Because the trustee seeks a sum rather than identified property under the court’s control, this order cannot be sustained as a genuine property adjudication or as equitable recoupment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; the same seven Justices reject each alternative offered to sustain the monetary judgment.

**Material application:** The order imposes a general obligation to pay $20,000. No identified property or fund supports it, and the relief is an affirmative recovery rather than defensive recoupment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine specific-property proceeding requires its own facts and authority and is not adjudicated here. No traceable fund is assumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative grounds do not preserve the personal money judgment. Genuinely distinct, properly supported remedies remain for ordinary proceedings; none is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Blatchford v. Native Village of Noatak*, 501 U.S. 775, distinguishes subject-matter jurisdiction from immunity. *United States v. Whiting Pools, Inc.*, 462 U.S. 198, is distinguished because tangible debtor-property turnover does not authorize this Treasury payment. *Begier v. IRS*, 496 U.S. 53, does not supply an identified fund here. *United States v. Shaw*, 309 U.S. 495, is preserved against inferring monetary consent from a property characterization. *Bull v. United States*, 295 U.S. 247, and *United States v. Dalm*, 494 U.S. 596, preserve recoupment’s limited defensive operation, which does not sustain this affirmative award.


## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: United States v. California


#### United States v. California, 507 U.S. 746 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2003.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Reimbursement does not create the asserted federal refund right

**Holding:** Federal reimbursement or advance funding of an independent contractor’s state taxes does not alone create a federal common-law action for money had and received to recover alleged state-law assessment errors. The contractor’s legal tax liability does not become a direct tax on the United States merely because the Treasury bears its economic cost.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. New Mexico, 455 U.S. 720: applies legal incidence/economic burden distinction.
- Brady v. Roosevelt S.S. Co., 317 U.S. 575: applies limits on altering third-party rights by indemnity.
- Bayne v. United States, 93 U.S. 642, and Gaines v. Miller, 111 U.S. 395: distinguished; unlawful diversion and recipient responsibility are absent.

##### Later subrogation does not revive the expired claim

**Holding:** Although indemnification gave the Government a right to seek subrogation, applicable California law required active assertion before substitution occurred; by the May 1988 federal suit, WBEC’s remaining refund claim had lapsed. The United States takes that derivative claim subject to its pre-existing infirmity; the Court does not decide generally whether state or federal limitations govern state-law claims brought by the United States.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Direct federal taxation, congressionally created remedies and the correctness of California’s tax classification are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for California on both asserted theories; the completed $3 million partial refund remains unaffected. No further refund or interest is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Munsey Trust Co., 332 U.S. 234: applies derivative nature of subrogation.
- Offer v. Superior Court, 194 Cal.114: applies the state-law active-assertion requirement.
- Guaranty Trust Co. v. United States, 304 U.S. 126: applies pre-acquisition infirmity rule.
- United States v. Summerlin, 310 U.S. 414: distinguished and preserved for live federal claims against subsequent state limits.


## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: New York v. United States


#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 91-543, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-558, County of Allegany, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-563, County of Cortland, New York v. United States et al. The same component-specific relief applies in all three dockets.

For the ripe-review holding in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563, see Federal Courts — Ripeness of future statutory obligations.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.

- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.

- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to the distinction between an authorized conditional arrangement and compulsory state regulation; it supplies no blanket Guarantee Clause justiciability rule.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: United States v. Dixon


#### United States v. Dixon, 509 U.S. 688 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1231.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection

**Holding:** A completed nonsummary criminal-contempt prosecution places the accused in criminal jeopardy; private initiation and an order’s protective purpose do not categorically remove that protection. Whether a later prosecution is for the same offense remains a separate inquiry; coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S.194: criminal character of punitive contempt applied.
- Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range Co., 221 U.S.418: punitive/coercive distinction applied.
- Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton, 481 U.S.787: independent judicial enforcement authority retained, not a categorical exception from criminal protection.

##### Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again

**Holding:** When a criminal-contempt adjudication necessarily prosecutes the substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same substantive offense again. Dixon’s drug offense and Foster I’s November assault were necessarily prosecuted in the respective contempts and are barred; this shared proposition does not supply a common analysis of Foster II–V.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: successive greater/included-offense protection applied.
- Harris v. Oklahoma, 433 U.S.682: necessary substantive offense cannot be erased by an abstract description of the first charge.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S.299: elements inquiry retained; disagreement over its broader application separately recorded.

##### Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law

**Holding:** Grady is not overruled: beyond Blockburger’s elements protection, a later prosecution is barred when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense for which the defendant has already been prosecuted. Mere reuse of evidence or a common episode does not suffice, and Felix’s independently punishable conspiracy holding remains; no five-Justice rationale determines Grady’s application to Foster II–V in this fractured decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady survives; evidentiary overlap and a common episode do not alone establish a bar. Summary contempt, coercive civil sanctions and separate sovereigns are not decided. Foster II–V have no controlling rationale, as stated separately below.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the bar for Dixon’s drug charge and Foster I (6–3), leaving the contempt judgments intact. Reverse the bar for Foster II–V and remand (5–4); threat charges remain subject to a properly established Ashe bar from actual acquittals and other defenses. Foster V may proceed without authorizing a barred simple-assault conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S.508: expressly retained; no overruling by this decision.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: conspiracy and evidentiary-overlap distinctions preserved, not recast as an overruling.
- Illinois v. Vitale, 447 U.S.410: successive-prosecution concern beyond formal elements supports retention.
- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: finality and greater/included-offense protection retained.
- Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S.436: independent issue-preclusion rule preserved for the remanded threat charges.

##### Whether Foster II–V may proceed under a common double-jeopardy rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse the bars to Foster II–V and remand, 5–4. No controlling rationale exists under Marks: the four-Justice elements-only rationale would overrule Grady, while Blackmun retains Grady but treats judicial-order enforcement differently. Those incompatible grounds are not logical subsets. The judgment permits these counts without creating a general contempt exception or displacing the separately majority-supported retention of Grady.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foster II–IV remain subject to any actual-acquittal issue preclusion established under Ashe and other preserved defenses. ### United States v. Shabani; No. 93-981; 513 U.S. 10 — 1994-11-01

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Shabani_merits_1994-11-01.md#public-projection); public substance is copied in the [generated chunk handoff](../render-inputs/OT_1994CHUNK1.md).

### Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act

**Controlling proposition:** Section 846 of Title 21 requires neither an allegation nor proof of an overt act beyond the unlawful narcotics agreement. The Government must still prove the unlawful agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt; neither mere association nor awareness of another person’s plan is enough.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this construction and its application; no fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress made the unlawful agreement the completed conspiracy offense. Section 846 contains no additional overt-act language, while 18 U.S.C. §371 expressly requires an act to effect the conspiracy’s object. Nash supplies the common-law understanding of conspiracy, and Singer shows why Congress’s omission of an extra act requirement matters when it uses that settled term. This reading does not punish a thought: the agreement itself is conduct, and the Government must prove knowing participation in it. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary element cannot rest on circuit precedent when the governing statute supplies none. Felix’s treatment of successive prosecutions did not decide this statutory question and cannot add the omitted element. Nor does lenity supply it; ordinary construction resolves the question without the residual ambiguity addressed in Ratzlaf. The refused overt-act instruction therefore supplies no ground for reversal of the conviction. The Court leaves the appeal’s reserved sufficiency and other claims to the court of appeals.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Nash v. United States*, 229 U.S. 373 (1913), applies the common-law meaning under which the agreement completes conspiracy without a further overt act.
- *Singer v. United States*, 323 U.S. 338 (1945), applies the significance of Congress’s omission of an additional overt-act requirement from a statutory conspiracy offense.
- *United States v. Felix*, 503 U.S. 378 (1992), remains a double-jeopardy decision distinguishing agreement from substantive conduct; it did not decide §846’s overt-act element. Its narrower separate position likewise supplies no contrary statutory element.
- *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), preserves lenity for genuine ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction; that condition is absent here. Its distinct willfulness rule is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule for every conspiracy statute is announced. The Court does not decide credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, the remaining appellate objections or the ultimate validity of the conviction. Current double-jeopardy law is unchanged.

This adds an offense-specific statutory construction.
