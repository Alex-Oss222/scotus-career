## Tome_v_United_States_merits_1995-01-10.md — 1995-01-10

Source: terms/OT1994/records/Tome_v_United_States_merits_1995-01-10.md, bounded Public Projection only.

### Event

Tome v. United States, No. 93-6892. Argued October 5, 1994; decided January 10, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, reviewing 3 F.3d 342 (1993), which affirmed an aggravated-sexual-abuse conviction. The question is whether original Rule 801(d)(1)(B) permits substantive admission of prior consistent statements made after the alleged motive to fabricate arose. A.T.'s accounts to six witnesses followed the asserted custody motive; motive and abuse remain allegations rather than fresh factual findings. Render form: full. Basis: the writings distinguish the controlling temporal rule, interpretive limits, and alternative-admission remand.

### Holdings

### Substantive prior consistent statements require the premotive relationship

**Controlling proposition:** Under original Federal Rule of Evidence 801(d)(1)(B), a prior statement offered substantively to rebut an express or implied charge of recent fabrication or improper influence or motive must have been made before the particular charged fabrication, influence, or motive arose. The declarant must testify and be subject to cross-examination concerning the statement, and the statement must be consistent with that testimony and offered for that specified rebuttal purpose; general relevance or repetition alone does not satisfy this nonhearsay rule.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the same rule and explanation; no aggregation of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Rule carries forward the premotive requirement associated with its specific rebuttal terms. Salerno requires satisfaction of the chosen evidentiary provision's actual conditions; general fairness cannot replace them. Daubert displaces common-law requirements incompatible with enacted rules but permits compatible inherited meaning to illuminate their terms. Here the words describing rebuttal of recent fabrication or improper motive invoke the distinction between answering the source of an accusation and merely repeating an account. The defense asserted that A.T. accused her father to remain with her mother. The challenged accounts followed that asserted custody motive and therefore do not qualify under this subparagraph. A later statement may sometimes possess indirect probative force, as the Government argues, but relevance alone does not establish this particular condition for substantive admission. The Court decides neither that A.T. fabricated her account nor that the statements are inadmissible for every purpose. Other exceptions and properly limited nonhearsay use retain their separate requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): applies its requirement that the selected hearsay provision's conditions be satisfied; its similar-motive and forfeiture holdings remain distinct.
- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993): applies its distinction between incompatible common-law additions and compatible interpretive guidance; no expert-evidence factor is transferred to this Rule.

### Alternative admissibility and ordinary error review remain for remand

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal of admission under Rule 801(d)(1)(B) leaves the Court of Appeals to decide other preserved grounds of admission and, if the statements are not admissible on such a ground, the consequences under ordinary harmless-error rules. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness, and neither immediate retrial nor acquittal follows solely from the Rule-construction error.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt these remedial limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying the wrong admission rule does not complete appellate review of the conviction. Williamson separates an exception's proper construction from the admissibility of particular evidence and from the relief an established error requires. The Court of Appeals left the medical-diagnosis and residual-exception grounds undecided; those grounds remain available only within their own terms and ordinary preservation limits. The reviewing court may not assume their satisfaction from the present holding. If no lawful ground sustains admission, Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos require evaluation of the whole record under the Government's harmlessness burden. The rejection of the temporal limitation below did not supply that evaluation. Chapman would apply only if a constitutional trial error were actually reached and found; this decision finds none. The Court also does not revive an unpreserved aggregate Rule 403 objection or reopen every separate appellate issue. Remand permits the necessary bounded inquiry while leaving both guilt and the ultimate need for a new trial undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594 (1994): applies its separation of admissibility, preservation, and judgment consequences.
- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record substantial-influence standard to preserved nonconstitutional error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves its beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard only for constitutional trial error actually reached and found.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): distinguishes forfeited error from this preserved construction question; no new preservation finding is made.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 803(4) requires statements for medical diagnosis or treatment describing history, symptoms, pain, sensations, or their inception or general cause insofar as reasonably pertinent to diagnosis or treatment. Then-Rule 803(24) applies to a statement not specifically covered by any of the foregoing exceptions and requires equivalent circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness, a material fact, greater probative value on the offered point than other evidence obtainable through reasonable efforts, service of the Rules' general purposes and the interests of justice, and advance notice affording a fair opportunity to prepare, including the intended statement's particulars and the declarant's name and address. The Court finds no such condition satisfied or unsatisfied. Rehabilitation-only use, other properly limited nonhearsay purposes, and factbound questions outside the admitted temporal issue remain undecided.

### Precedent Treatment

Salerno and Daubert supply the enacted-rule and compatible-background principles. Williamson and Kotteakos govern the separate remedial inquiry; Chapman and Olano retain their distinct constitutional-error and forfeiture scopes. No earlier decision is overruled.

### Law After Decision

Effective January 10, 1995, original Rule 801(d)(1)(B) has the stated premotive condition for this substantive use. The Court adds no general exclusion of postmotive statements and changes no other hearsay exception, rehabilitation rule, preservation rule, or harmless-error burden.

### Separate Writings

Justice Scalia joins the Court's text-and-context construction and explains that Advisory Committee Notes can persuade as expert commentary without obtaining special authority from their authors' role as draftsmen. He does not adopt a rule that committee expectations themselves establish an omitted condition. The Court's explanation rests on enacted language and compatible inherited usage, so his concurrence does not subtract from the six votes for either holding.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Thomas, would affirm. The Rule requires genuine rebuttal, but its words do not expressly make chronology conclusive in every case. A statement's circumstances can weaken an accusation of fabrication even when an asserted motive existed earlier. The Tenth Circuit relied on the initial disclosure's spontaneity and its assessment of the alleged custody motive, rather than on consistency alone. Breyer's approach retains the declarant's testimony and cross-examination, a true rebuttal purpose, and Rules 402 and 403. His method of examining the actual purpose and inferential force of a statement, reflected in United States v. Moore, does not equate every repeated account with admissible evidence. He regards compatible common-law guidance as useful but insufficient to impose an invariant condition the enacted text does not expressly state. The dissent finds no separate exception satisfied and offers no independent harmlessness determination; its disagreement is the construction supporting the judgment below.

### Procedure After Action

The mandate reverses the Tenth Circuit judgment and returns the case to that court to determine whether another preserved ground admits the statements and, if not, to apply ordinary harmless-error rules. Review here is complete; appellate proceedings continue. A new trial follows only if error requiring that relief is established.

## Young_v_Northern_Illinois_Conference_merits_1995-01-17.md — 1995-01-17

Source: terms/OT1994/records/Young_v_Northern_Illinois_Conference_merits_1995-01-17.md, bounded Public Projection only.

### Event

Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church, No. 93-1917. Argued November 29, 1994; decided January 17, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, reviewing 21 F.3d 184 (1994), lower-court No. 93-2157. The questions are whether the Religion Clauses bar a probationary minister's Title VII race, sex, and retaliation claims challenging advancement and termination, and whether that protection is jurisdictional or substantive. Young sought reinstatement, reconsideration for full clergy membership, back pay and benefits, compensatory and punitive damages, fees, and costs after the March 4, 1992 decision. No discrimination is found. Render form: full. Basis: distinct constitutional and jurisdictional holdings and a separate formal disposition.

### Holdings

### Ministerial selection claims encounter a constitutional defense

**Controlling proposition:** The Religion Clauses bar the application of Title VII to Young's race, sex, and retaliation claims challenging her advancement and retention as a probationary minister, because deciding those claims or imposing the requested relief would subject the church's choice of its religious leadership to civil review. The defense covers reinstatement, compelled reconsideration, and financial liability resting on that same selection decision; it does not exempt every religious employee or independently actionable secular conduct from civil law.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this claim-specific rule; their formal descriptions of the judgment are distinguished below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these claims would place a civil court in control of a ministerial selection. Kedroff protects church government and the selection of ecclesiastical authorities from state control. Milivojevich forbids civil reconsideration of ecclesiastical decisions through an inquiry into whether the church followed its own internal rules. Young seeks review of denied advancement and termination of her ministerial service, including alleged departures from church procedure. Reinstatement or compelled reconsideration would directly control that office; damages for the same decision do not make the underlying adjudication independent. Race and sex discrimination and retaliation are serious civil wrongs, but calling their asserted motives secular does not remove this inquiry's control of religious leadership. Section 702's religious-preference permission is not enlarged to include those categories. Jones v. Wolf permits neutral adjudication of genuine property obligations; this record establishes no independently pleaded secular claim of that kind. The Court does not find discrimination, religious motivation, or procedural compliance, and leaves disputed ministerial status and unrelated civil obligations for appropriate cases.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kedroff v. St. Nicholas Cathedral, 344 U.S. 94 (1952): applies its protection of church government and selection of ecclesiastical authorities to this ministerial-employment claim.
- Serbian Eastern Orthodox Diocese v. Milivojevich, 426 U.S. 696 (1976): applies its bar on reviewing internal ecclesiastical decisions for arbitrariness or compliance with church procedures; its unresolved fraud-or-collusion possibility remains unresolved.
- Jones v. Wolf, 443 U.S. 595 (1979): distinguishes neutral property adjudication from review of ministerial selection and preserves the former within its own limits.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990): distinguishes regulation of ordinary conduct by neutral general law from civil control of the choice of religious leaders; its rule is not replaced.

### The defense defeats the claims on their merits, not statutory jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** A federal court with jurisdiction under Title VII §706(f)(3) and 28 U.S.C. §1331 over a substantial Title VII claim exercises that jurisdiction when it decides that the Religion Clauses defeat the claim; it must enter a substantive dismissal rather than characterize this defense as a withdrawal of subject-matter jurisdiction. Young's pleaded claims are substantial federal claims, and the remand is for entry of a merits dismissal of the stated ministerial advancement-and-retention claims, not a discrimination trial or an award of relief.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the jurisdiction-and-merits distinction; eight vacate the jurisdictional judgment as a whole, while the Chief would vacate the jurisdictional characterization and affirm the dismissal on the merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to decide a federal claim does not depend on the claimant ultimately overcoming a constitutional defense. Bell v. Hood separates a substantial federal question from the legal sufficiency of the asserted right to relief. Congress supplied jurisdiction over Title VII claims, and Young's discrimination allegations invoke that statutory subject. The church's successful defense therefore supplies the reason for a merits judgment, not a reason to deny that the court possessed adjudicatory authority. Ecclesiastical autonomy limits the permissible application of civil law; deciding that limit is itself an exercise of jurisdiction. Bell's exceptions for immaterial claims made solely to obtain jurisdiction and wholly insubstantial and frivolous claims are not presented here. The Court does not decide their treatment. The correction changes the legal basis and preclusive character of the dismissal without requiring a trial about church motives. No reinstatement, promotion, back pay, or damages follows from recognition that the federal court had power to decide the defense.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678 (1946): applies the distinction between a substantial federal claim's jurisdictional basis and its legal defeat; the immaterial-jurisdiction-device and wholly insubstantial-or-frivolous exceptions are not reached.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375 (1994): preserves the need for a statutory or otherwise lawful jurisdictional basis; Title VII and §1331 supply that basis here, unlike the separate enforcement claim in Kokkonen.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional defense rests on Young's undisputed reported ministerial role and her selection-dependent claims, not a comprehensive definition of minister. No fraud or collusion is alleged; the Court neither adopts nor rejects an exception for it. The relevant church decision is that of the highest body to which this dispute was actually carried, without a new exhaustion requirement reaching the denomination's ultimate hierarchy. RFRA's invocation, preservation, and application to this suit remain undecided. A genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim remains outside this adjudication only if independently pleaded and resolvable without deciding doctrine, ministerial fitness, or the selection of religious leaders; no such claim is found to exist here.

### Precedent Treatment

Kedroff and Milivojevich supply the claim-specific selection defense. Jones preserves neutral independent property adjudication. Bell supplies statutory jurisdiction despite a defeating defense. Smith, Lukumi, Kiryas Joel, Lamb's Chapel, and Clearwater remain within their different subjects; no RFRA application or new §702 race/sex exemption is adopted. Kokkonen's requirement of a jurisdictional source is satisfied, not displaced.

### Law After Decision

Effective January 17, 1995, these selection-dependent Title VII claims encounter a substantive Religion Clause defense, and substantial federal jurisdiction survives to decide that defense. The ruling creates no general immunity from civil law for religious institutions, does not enlarge Title VII §702, and leaves RFRA applicability and independently pleaded secular claims outside its holding.

### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's ministerial-selection defense and jurisdictional distinction. He would vacate the jurisdictional characterization, affirm dismissal of the ministerial-employment claims on the merits, and direct dismissal with prejudice insofar as those claims challenge ordination, ministerial promotion, discipline, assignment, or continued ministerial employment. The Court's mandate also requires substantive dismissal, but he would expressly affirm that disposition while correcting its basis.

In his view ministerial autonomy follows genuine function rather than title: who preaches, leads worship, teaches doctrine, and represents a faith belongs to the religious body. Even substantial evidence that sex entered a selection decision cannot authorize judicial review of whether doctrinal or ministerial reasons were valid or pretextual. That limit does not excuse discrimination in ordinary secular employment and supplies no general church immunity. An independently pleaded and genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim may proceed only if adjudication avoids religious doctrine, ministerial fitness, and the selection of religious leaders; he identifies no such claim in Young's complaint as reported. Public money, tax benefits, licensing, or other governmental contact alone does not waive ministerial autonomy. Government may impose lawful, clearly stated conditions on secular uses of public funds and recover diverted funds, but cannot purchase control of clergy selection. Those additional public-funding observations are his own; the Court does not decide a funding controversy in this case.

### Procedure After Action

The Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is vacated and the case remanded for entry of a substantive dismissal of the stated ministerial advancement-and-retention claims. No new discrimination trial, ordination, reinstatement, promotion, reconsideration, money, or costs is ordered. The Chief would additionally affirm dismissal on the merits and expressly direct dismissal with prejudice. Review here is complete; entry of the corrected judgment remains below.

## Allied_Bruce_Terminix_Cos_v_Dobson_merits_1995-01-18.md — 1995-01-18

Source: terms/OT1994/records/Allied_Bruce_Terminix_Cos_v_Dobson_merits_1995-01-18.md, bounded Public Projection only.

### Event

Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, No. 93-1001. Argued October 4, 1994; merits decision January 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Alabama, reviewing 628 So. 2d 354, which affirmed denial of a stay and motion to compel arbitration in a dispute concerning a transferred termite-service bond. The questions concern FAA §2's state-court application, the breadth of involving commerce and the requirement of actual rather than contemplated interstate activity. Render form: full; the state-court and commerce holdings are distinct, and the dissenters do not reach the commerce alternatives.

### Holdings

#### Section 2 continues to govern covered agreements in state court

**Controlling proposition:** FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court, making them valid, irrevocable and enforceable save upon grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. The Court retains Southland's state-court holding; Alabama may apply generally applicable contract defenses but may not deny enforcement solely because its law singles out predispute arbitration agreements for a categorical disability.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the established state-court application of §2 remains governing law. Southland supplies that substantive construction, and Perry confirms that a State cannot withhold enforcement by assigning a covered dispute to a judicial forum while preserving generally applicable contract defenses. Respondents and supporting States ask the Court to reject that interpretation in light of the Act's federal procedural structure. The statutory-construction objection deserves consideration, but repetition of that original disagreement does not establish a sufficient reason to overturn the settled rule and the reliance it permits across forums. Congress remains able to alter the statute. Alabama's categorical prohibition is directed at arbitration itself, not a defense to contracts generally. Volt does not save that prohibition: it enforces an actual contractual choice of state rules, and no such choice is established by the forum's location alone. Retaining §2's substantive command does not decide whether every direction in §§3 and 4 must operate identically in state courts or settle the assent and scope questions of every claim here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1: retains the substantive state-court application of §2 against the express request to overrule it; its commerce and general-contract-defense conditions remain.
- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483: applies its rejection of a state judicial-forum exception directed at arbitration while preserving generally applicable contract defenses.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Junior University, 489 U.S. 468: preserves enforcement of an actual contractual choice of state arbitration rules; a forum-wide hostility to arbitration is not itself that choice.

#### Coverage turns on actual commerce within Congress's power

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2's phrase involving commerce reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full commerce authority Congress constitutionally possesses, and coverage turns on the transaction's actual interstate commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity when contracting. The multistate service operations and interstate procurement of treatment and repair materials establish the necessary commerce here, requiring reversal of Alabama's coverage ruling; this statutory construction does not define the outer constitutional boundary or make remote interstate history alone conclusive.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §2 describes the transaction actually made and carried out. Its broad commerce language does not confine coverage to goods moving in an interstate stream, and its reference to a contract evidencing a transaction adds no private expectation or foresight requirement. Bernhardt preserves the need to show a transaction involving commerce; its failure-of-proof setting does not supply Alabama's contemplation test. Here a commercial termite-service arrangement involved firms operating across state lines and actual materials obtained outside Alabama. Local work on the house does not erase those commercial connections. The competing reading would make enforceability turn on what parties predicted about commerce instead of what their transaction involved, although Congress supplied no such mental-state condition. These facts establish genuine interstate commercial operation, rather than a talisman based on the remote history of one object. The ruling resolves statutory coverage under the commerce authority Congress possesses. It neither fixes that authority's furthest constitutional reach nor determines who agreed to arbitrate every pleaded claim or which ordinary contract defenses may succeed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bernhardt v. Polygraphic Co. of America, Inc., 350 U.S. 198: distinguishes a record failing to establish the requisite commerce; its transaction predicate survives, without a newly attributed contemplation requirement.
- Southland, 465 U.S. 1: applies its substantive enforceability rule after identifying the actual covered transaction; general hostility to arbitration does not substitute for the statutory commerce inquiry.

**Remedy and limits:** The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded under the actual-commerce rule. Formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other generally applicable contract defenses remain for ordinary adjudication to the extent properly presented and preserved below, together with party assent, clause scope and appropriate implementing procedure. Coverage is not a final order compelling all parties to arbitrate every claim. Section 1's separate employment exclusion is not presented by this service bond. The Court does not decide the tort merits, damages, a particular fraud-in-the-inducement defense, a contractual choice of state rules not established here, or wholesale state-court incorporation of §§3–4.

### Precedent Treatment

Southland's substantive application in state court is retained, and Perry continues to reject arbitration-specific state barriers while preserving ordinary contract defenses. Volt protects an actual choice of state rules, not an assumed adoption of the forum's antiarbitration policy. Bernhardt retains the commerce predicate but supplies no contemplation requirement. No earlier decision is overruled, and §§3–4's entire state-procedure reach is not decided.

### Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, §2 reaches actual transactions within Congress's constitutional commerce power without subjective contemplation or a stream-only limitation. State courts remain bound by its substantive enforceability rule and full general-contract-defense qualification. The genuine multistate operations and interstate materials suffice here; the outer constitutional ceiling, every party's assent, clause scope and particular contract defenses remain for their proper settings. The decision adds no special immunity from ordinary contract law.

### Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor joins the Court's opinion and concurs separately. She continues to believe that Southland originally misconstrued the Act's application in state courts, for the reasons set out in her Southland and Perry dissents. Statutory stare decisis nevertheless weighs strongly against reopening an interpretation that has governed for a decade without a demonstrated inability to administer it. Once that interpretation is retained, a broad and uniform actual-commerce rule is appropriate; narrowing commerce coverage is not a substitute for overruling Southland. Her original-construction reservation does not subtract from her join in the Court's present rule or mandate.

Justice Thomas dissents, joined by Justice Scalia. He reads the Act's structure and federal-court judicial directions as failing to create the substantive command to state courts that Southland announced. He would overrule Southland to that extent and affirm the denial of arbitration on the ground that the Act does not govern this state proceeding. He does not decide the alternative scope of commerce coverage or the factual success of a general contract defense. The dissent presents no additional independently sufficient specific-performance holding under §§3–4.

Justice Scalia also dissents separately. His earlier acceptance of Southland when its overruling was not requested does not resolve the express request presented here. In his view, reliance on a statutory interpretation cannot by itself preserve a state-court command the statute does not establish. He joins Thomas's state-court ground and would affirm without deciding the Court's commerce construction. These proposed changes to Southland do not control; the Court retains that precedent.

### Procedure After Action

The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded. The state courts must apply the actual-commerce rule and address formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other ordinary contract issues insofar as properly presented and preserved, as well as clause scope and implementing procedure. No waived defense is revived, final damages ordered or universal party-by-party arbitration order entered. Supreme Court merits review ends; the arbitration request and underlying litigation continue below within these limits.

## American_Airlines_Inc_v_Wolens_merits_1995-01-18.md — 1995-01-18

Source: terms/OT1994/records/American_Airlines_Inc_v_Wolens_merits_1995-01-18.md, bounded Public Projection only.

### Event

American Airlines, Inc. v. Wolens, No. 93-1286. Merits decision, January 18, 1995; argued November 1, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois, reviewing its interlocutory ruling sustaining damages claims under the Illinois Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act and the airline's frequent-flyer agreement against federal preemption. The claims concern alleged devaluation of previously earned travel credits by 1988 redemption restrictions. The questions concern the two sources of obligation separately. Render form: full; the judgment has distinct component coalitions and partial opinion joins.

### Holdings

#### State consumer-fraud standards governing these airline benefits are preempted

**Controlling proposition:** The Airline Deregulation Act's transaction-period §1305(a)(1) preempts application of the Illinois Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act to the pleaded devaluation of earned airline-travel awards through changed redemption restrictions, because that claim enforces a state-imposed substantive standard directly related to airline rates and services. General applicability, a damages remedy and the general §1506 savings clause do not avoid that express prohibition; materially different duties with only tenuous, remote or peripheral connections remain outside this ruling.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, consumer-fraud portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Five of the eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule and explanation. Justices O'Connor and Thomas also vote to preempt this claim on their separate broader ground; Justice Stevens dissents on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged award restrictions concern the price and availability of airline transportation. Capacity controls govern how many seats passengers may obtain at an award level; the alleged restrictions on use of earned credits govern what travel those credits purchase. Morales supplies the rule that a generally applicable law can regulate airline rates and services when its application has the required connection. The Illinois court's description of frequent-flyer benefits as peripheral does not answer that direct relation. A damages action may enforce the same substantive command as prospective regulation, so the form of relief does not remove the conflict. Section 1506 preserves remedies outside the specific prohibition rather than undoing it. Cipollone's distinct cigarette provisions require attention to the duty and wording at issue, not a universal exception for claims called fraud. The Court decides this pleaded challenge without resolving the disputed retroactive application of particular blackout dates or declaring every airline deception claim preempted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., 504 U.S. 374: applies the controlling connection-or-reference rule and the specific-preemption/general-savings distinction to these award restrictions; its separate fare-marketing effects holding remains confined to that package, and its remote-effects reservation remains.
- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504: distinguishes its clause-specific preservation of general intentional falsehood; that cigarette holding supplies no universal airline-fraud exemption and retains its own scope.

#### An airline's actual voluntary bargain remains enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1305(a)(1) does not preempt ordinary court enforcement of an airline's self-imposed contractual obligations, including ordinary contract remedies, when the claim is confined to the undertaking the parties actually made. Courts may use ordinary contract law to identify and enforce that undertaking, but may not enlarge or reform it through a state policy external to the agreement; the pleaded contract claims survive dismissal only within that boundary.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, contract portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six of eight participants directly adopt this rationale; no synthesis of narrower separate opinions is required. Justices O'Connor and Thomas dissent on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that enforcing the parties' own bargain differs from prescribing its substantive terms. The airline chooses the obligation; ordinary adjudication determines what it promised and supplies the usual remedy for a proved breach. The Act's prohibition on state enactment or enforcement of governing legal standards, read with its savings clause, leaves room for that private ordering. Cipollone supplies a useful distinction between a voluntarily undertaken promise and a duty imposed by the State, although the airline clause requires its own construction. The contrary position correctly observes that contracts depend on law for enforcement. That observation does not make every privately chosen term a state-prescribed airline policy. The limitation is therefore substantive: a contract label cannot preserve an external fairness requirement. American's reservation of authority to alter its program may defeat the claimed promise, but its reach must be determined below. No binding promise protecting earned credits, breach, reliance, causation or damages is found here; the Court orders only the confined contract proceeding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Cipollone, 505 U.S. 504: extends the voluntary-source distinction by analogy to this independently construed airline clause; its narrower warranty ground does not become a blanket common-law exemption.
- Morales, 504 U.S. 374: preserves preemption of state-prescribed airline terms while resolving the contract-enforcement question that Morales left open; neither that case's injunction nor its fare-marketing effects holding decides the actual bargain here.
- Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. American Train Dispatchers Association, 499 U.S. 117: distinguishes the dependence of a contract on law for enforceability from the source of the substantive obligation under this statute; its construction of the distinct railroad immunity provision is undisturbed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The lower courts must determine the actual agreement, including whether American reserved authority to alter already earned credits, applicable ordinary defenses and any proved remedy. Good-faith and implied-term tools may identify the agreement but may not reintroduce external state rate policy; no particular such theory, punitive award or nonflight-benefit claim is adjudicated. The earlier rejection of injunctive relief remains undisturbed. No final fraud or contract liability, amount or precise retroactive blackout application is decided.

### Precedent Treatment

Morales supplies the controlling airline-preemption rule while preserving remote effects and unresolved duties. Cipollone supplies the voluntary-source analogy without transferring its differently worded cigarette-law holdings wholesale. Norfolk & Western retains its railroad-law force; the fact that contracts require legal enforcement does not alone determine this airline clause's reach. No earlier precedent is overruled.

### Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, the airline-preemption rule distinguishes these state-prescribed consumer-fraud obligations from enforcement confined to an airline's voluntary bargain. The contract question previously left open is now resolved within that limit. Ordinary formation, interpretation, defenses and remedies remain available to identify and enforce the agreement; external state policy cannot enlarge it. The May 1988 claims are governed by the transaction-period provisions. The 1994 recodification creates no additional holding, and no Alaska, airport-proprietor or property-transportation exception is invoked. The Court establishes no general immunity from state law and no replacement federal contract regime.

### Separate Writings

Justice Stevens concurs in the contract judgment and joins the Court's contract reasoning, but dissents from preemption of the consumer-fraud claim. He distinguishes ordinary private redress for deception from the binding fare-marketing requirements addressed in Morales, which supplies the governing airline precedent but did not decide every application of a general fraud law. His approach is consistent with Cipollone's preservation of intentional material falsehood and voluntary promises under the provisions there construed. He would permit both damages branches to proceed, without adopting a finding that American deceived its customers or that the asserted contract existed. His fraud position does not control.

Justice O'Connor concurs in the judgment preempting consumer-fraud damages and dissents from preservation of contract damages. Justice Thomas joins her statutory-enforcement analysis and proposed reversal of both branches, but does not join her separate discussion of adherence to statutory precedent. They read the Act's prohibition on state enforcement as reaching legal enforcement of contract obligations directly related to airline rates and services. ### Event

United States v. Mezzanatto, No. 93-1340. Argued November 2, 1994; decided January 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 998 F.2d 1452 (1993), which reversed a methamphetamine conviction and ordered a new trial because Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) categorically barred enforcement of the advance impeachment waiver. The question is whether the represented defendant could knowingly and voluntarily permit use of plea-discussion statements to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. ### Holdings

### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Their carefully limited exceptions do not themselves permit ordinary impeachment, but that does not answer the separate question of voluntary relinquishment. Congress's interest in candid negotiation deserves respect; the record does not establish that this counseled, limited agreement necessarily defeats it. Mezzanatto consulted counsel, accepted the impeachment condition, and presented no individualized claim that the acceptance was unknowing or involuntary. Predictions of universal prosecutorial abuse do not invalidate that agreement categorically. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for the employer, 9 F.3d 539. The question is whether wrongdoing discovered after discharge bars every remedy for an age-discriminatory discharge and, if not, how it bears on available relief. Render form: compact. Basis: unanimous judgment and a common statutory liability-and-remedy rationale.

### Holdings

### Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation

**Controlling proposition:** Misconduct unknown to the employer when it discharged an employee cannot defeat liability for an age-discriminatory discharge or categorically bar every remedy. The employee must still establish an ADEA violation under the governing liability rules; an employer’s assumption of discrimination for summary judgment is not a final liability finding.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this proposition and explanation; it is a direct majority holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The ADEA forbids discharge because of age and gives courts authority to grant relief that serves the statute’s purposes. Hazen Paper requires age actually to motivate the challenged decision and have a determinative influence on it; a reason the employer did not know cannot have motivated it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse address lawful and unlawful reasons operating in the actual decision, so their same-decision inquiry does not transform later discovery into an original cause. The Banner assumed discrimination only to obtain summary judgment and relied on documents discovered after McKennon’s dismissal. That discovery cannot erase an otherwise proved violation. The contrary rule would remove compensation for an unlawful period and weaken enforcement whenever later investigation uncovered a sufficient ground for termination. The employee’s misconduct nevertheless matters to remedy, which the next holding addresses. The Court does not find age discrimination, change the employee’s ultimate burden, or decide whether the violation was willful.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper’s actual-motive requirement remains controlling; Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse are distinguished because they concern reasons involved in the challenged decision. St. Mary’s Honor Center retains its existing proof framework and does not make rejection of an employer’s explanation an automatic liability judgment.

### Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief

**Controlling proposition:** To limit relief through after-acquired evidence, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone had it known, rather than merely showing that discharge was permissible. Once that showing is made, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate, and backpay’s starting measure is the period from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the proof requirement and bounded remedial rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 626(b) authorizes appropriate legal and equitable relief to effectuate the ADEA, which requires attention to both discrimination and the employment consequences of serious misconduct. An order requiring continued employment or its prospective monetary substitute ordinarily makes no sense when proved wrongdoing would have ended that employment lawfully. But the employer must establish what it actually would have done; an available pretext, minor infraction, or merely possible discharge does not establish that limit. Backpay ordinarily compensates the interval before actual discovery, when the later ground had not supplied a lawful termination basis. The court may account for extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests, consistently with the statutory purposes. The Banner’s affidavits and McKennon’s acknowledgment supported its motion-specific would-discharge showing, but the erroneous complete bar prevented proper adjudication of liability and relief. The Court awards no amount, imposes no reinstatement decree, and leaves statutory willfulness and other distinct remedial predicates for their proper determination.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper’s knowledge-or-recklessness standard continues to govern any claim of willfulness; this remedial ruling does not lower it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse supply no complete forfeiture rule for a reason discovered after the challenged discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine protected opposition or participation cannot supply a lawful discharge ground; this case announces no general ban on obtaining evidence of discrimination. No final discrimination, willfulness, compensation, liquidated-damages, fee, or reinstatement finding is made. Section 626(c)(1)’s termination of the individual right to bring an action when the EEOC commences an action to enforce that employee’s right remains distinct; no such agency action is established here. The 1991 Title VII amendments supply no new rule for this ADEA discharge.

### Precedent Treatment

- Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins preserves actual age motivation and determinative influence as the liability inquiry and knowledge or reckless disregard as the willfulness inquiry. After-acquired evidence changes neither requirement.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle addresses actual mixed reasons for the challenged action; it does not make an unknown later reason a complete defense.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins likewise concerns contemporaneous decisionmaking and supplies no after-acquired-evidence forfeiture rule.
- St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks preserves the governing burden of persuasion and rejects automatic liability from disbelief of an employer’s explanation; this decision does not alter that allocation.

### Law After Decision

Effective January 23, 1995, later-discovered misconduct supplies a possible limitation on ADEA remedies, not erasure of an otherwise proved discriminatory discharge. The employer’s actual-would-discharge burden, ordinary limits on reinstatement and front pay, and discovery-based backpay measure with extraordinary-equity consideration govern the remand. Actual age motivation, the employee’s ultimate persuasion obligation, and the separate willfulness rule remain intact.

### Separate Writings

No separate opinion was filed.

### Procedure After Action

The Sixth Circuit must return the case for proceedings consistent with the liability/remedy distinction. Liability remains to be adjudicated; any relief must rest on its statutory and factual predicates. This Court enters no amount, no final willfulness finding, and no order of reinstatement or front pay.

## Schlup_v_Delo_merits_1995-01-23.md — 1995-01-23

Source: terms/OT1994/records/Schlup_v_Delo_merits_1995-01-23.md, bounded Public Projection only.

### Event

Schlup v. Delo, No. 93-7901. Argued October 3, 1994; merits decision January 23, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, reviewing 11 F.3d 738, which sustained denial of a second federal habeas application without the requested evidentiary hearing. Schlup offers crime-innocence evidence as a gateway to otherwise barred ineffective-assistance and suppression claims arising from his conviction for participation in a prison murder. The questions concern the gateway quantum, the augmented evidentiary inquiry, statutory refusal authority and the proper remand. Render form: full; judgment, gateway rationale and statutory-refusal rationale have distinct coalitions and partial joins.

### Holdings

### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the probability inquiry concerns innocence of the crime, not whether a concededly guilty person qualified for a death sentence. Carrier supplies the exceptional route where constitutional error probably convicted an innocent person; Sawyer preserves its different eligibility inquiry without deciding this crime question. McCleskey retains the innocence exception while enforcing finality through barriers to repetitive and abusive claims. The prisoner's burden remains demanding because the question concerns reasonable jurors considering the augmented evidentiary record, not the habeas judge's personal doubt. Kuhlmann's plurality discussion supports examination of all probative evidence, but its formulations are not converted into a separate majority holding. Jackson's trial-record sufficiency question cannot replace that task: evidence sufficient when credited at trial may have a different effect when reliable new evidence is considered with it. The State's request for one uniformly higher burden disregards the different question and the gateway's limited function. Passing this threshold permits further consideration within the governing claim rules; it does not award a writ or adjudicate innocence.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Operative evidentiary qualifications:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Controlling proposition:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that discretion to refuse renewed review must be exercised under the proper governing law. McCleskey explains both the statutory permission and its qualifications; neither the label second petition nor the filing of new affidavits decides the result. The earlier federal consideration of guilt-phase ineffective assistance supplies a real legal-merits proceeding, and the lower court's additional discussion of that claim remains relevant. It does not establish an independently sufficient ground disposing of every claim despite today's legal correction. The new accounts concern the meaning of the existing videotape, identification and the interval between attack and response. The lower analysis used a stricter burden and treated credibility too categorically as the original jury's task. Those features prevent accepting it as the required probability assessment. Even assuming the ultimate refusal is discretionary, reconsideration is required here; the Court need not settle the broader discretion question. Keeney and Townsend preserve necessary factual development of properly available claims, but their predicates are not established merely by this remand. The District Court must decide what further development this gateway assessment needs.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Governing statutory terms:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

**Application, reservations and remedy:** The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

### Precedent Treatment

Carrier supplies the crime-innocence route, while Sawyer retains its distinct clear-and-convincing eligibility rule. McCleskey's statutory qualifications, abuse pleading, cause/prejudice framework and innocence exception remain. Kuhlmann's relevant discussion retains plurality force, and Jackson's trial-record sufficiency rule remains separate. Keeney and Townsend govern necessary factual development within their actual available-claim conditions. Herrera retains only its record-specific insufficiency holding, Delo v. Blair supplies no precedent from equal division, Brecht remains a preserved-error merits decision, and McFarland supplies no automatic gateway or stay. No prior controlling holding is overruled.

### Law After Decision

Effective January 23, 1995, reliable new evidence of crime innocence is assessed with the whole probative record under the more-likely-than-not/no-reasonable-juror gateway. The prisoner retains that demanding burden and must separately establish any underlying constitutional claim. Section 2244(b)'s qualified permission, lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain; the Court decides neither a universal mandatory-review rule after a successful showing nor a general appellate standard for refusal. The judgment corrects the legal inquiry and creates no freestanding innocence remedy.

### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's probability gateway and all-evidence rule and concurs in vacatur and remand. Finality is weighty, but the procedural route remains available where reliable new evidence indicates that constitutional error may have convicted an innocent person. Sawyer's death-eligibility threshold answers a different question. The lower courts must evaluate the videotape, timing and eyewitness accounts together with the trial record; passage through the gateway authorizes consideration of otherwise barred constitutional claims within their governing rules, not an innocence judgment or automatic release.

He adds guidance for the reliability inquiry. The habeas court should explain the new evidence's reliability, any independent corroboration, why the evidence was unavailable at trial, whether delay impairs credibility, whether state misconduct or ineffective counsel explains its absence, and exactly how it changes the evidentiary case the jury heard. These are nonexclusive considerations relevant to reliability and probability, not additional elements, a checklist or a new diligence requirement. He does not decide the witnesses' truthfulness or shift the underlying claims' ultimate burden. He does not join the Court's separate statutory-refusal analysis or announce a general rule governing residual refusal discretion.

Justice Kennedy dissents. Justice Thomas joins his opinion in full; Justice Scalia joins its stricter-gateway rule and application, but not its alternative discussion of the probability formulation. Kennedy would require clear and convincing evidence, on the probative record as a whole, that no reasonable juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. He proposes extending Sawyer's quantum to crime innocence; he does not claim that Sawyer already decided that question. McCleskey's treatment of abusive litigation and the exceptional nature of renewed review support the more rigorous showing in his view. The continuing identifications, uncertain timing and delayed or inconsistent accounts leave the submitted evidence insufficient under that quantum. This is not a finding that every new witness lied or that any inculpatory trial evidence invariably defeats an innocence claim.

Kennedy and Thomas additionally question the Court's predictive formulation if a probability rule is used. They would adapt Jackson's reasonable-juror approach to the enlarged evidentiary record, with limited factual inquiry where the submissions indicate that the necessary innocence showing may be made. They do not substitute the unchanged trial-record-only sufficiency test, order a hearing automatically, or establish an independent failure under a correctly applied Carrier rule. Their proposed stricter rule and application would sustain the judgment; their alternative discussion does not control.

Justice Scalia also dissents in an opinion joined by Justice Thomas. Section 2244(b), in their reading, preserves real authority to decline successive or abusive review, subject to its two qualifications and review for abuse. The earlier federal legal-merits adjudication satisfies a hearing alternative despite the lack of the now-requested factual hearing. They rely on the accepted repeat/abuse posture, the actual consideration and refusal below, and the evidentiary submission to find no abuse; they also regard the stricter gateway as correct. They do not convert permission into mandatory dismissal, classify every present claim as previously adjudicated, or find an unexamined ground deliberately withheld.

Salinger and Sanders support consideration of prior litigation within their respective settings, while McCleskey preserves the statute's conditions and judicial limits on discretion. Wong Doo shows that an adequate independent discretionary ground can sometimes preserve a judgment despite a legal error. Scalia and Thomas do not use that proposition to declare these affidavits necessarily insufficient under a correctly applied probability rule. Their statutory rationale is additional to the stricter-standard objection, but its application here shares their conclusion that the lower court used a legally proper quantum. They recognize that misconception of law or arbitrary reasons can constitute abuse and announce no unconditional affirmance should the proper gateway later be satisfied. These views do not control the remand.

### Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated. That court must remand to the District Court for the proper gateway assessment, including a reasoned decision about factual development. The existing videotape and new timing and identification accounts must be considered together; the Court predetermines no credibility finding, gateway success or hearing requirement. Otherwise barred constitutional claims may proceed only within the gateway and other governing conditions, and passing the gateway proves none of their merits. Supreme Court review ends; the habeas proceeding continues below without an automatic writ, retrial, release or stay.
