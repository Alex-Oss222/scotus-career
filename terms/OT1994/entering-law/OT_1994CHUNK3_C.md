### ERISA employee classification

#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

### Military leave and reemployment

#### King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-889.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Whether 38 U.S.C. §2024(d) contains an implied duration limit on covered military service

**Holding:** Section 2024(d) contains no implicit reasonableness limit on the duration of otherwise covered military service. Neither a categorical three-year exclusion nor a free-ranging durational-reasonableness balance may be added to the provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion; Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support the proposition unanimously among the eight participants.

**Material application:** The Army required the three-year tour associated with King's position. Neighboring service categories contain express duration limits, while §2024(d) ties protected leave to the period required for covered duty without adding such a cap.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns only an implied duration restriction. It does not excuse failure to satisfy the provision's enacted coverage, notice, service, or return requirements, decide damages, or determine obligations after a failure properly to return.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded with the implied and categorical duration limits rejected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morash supplies the requirement to read provisions within the whole enactment. Fishgold supports protective construction of reemployment rights, while text and structure control this decision. Circuit precedent adding a reasonableness condition is rejected. Wolas remains statute-specific.

### Nonemployee organizing access

#### Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, 502 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-970.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

##### When nonemployee organizers may obtain access despite nondiscriminatory exclusion from private property

**Holding:** For a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy, nonemployee organizers must show that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before the Board may balance a need for entry against the employer's property rights; greater convenience or organizing success does not satisfy that threshold. This workforce's community residence, public approaches and substantial direct contacts defeat the asserted exception, while discriminatory exclusion, employees' own solicitation and genuinely inaccessible workforces remain governed by their distinct rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices control both the statutory rule and denial of enforcement.

**Material application:** Employees lived in the surrounding community; organizers used public approaches and obtained direct home-contact information for roughly one fifth of the workforce. Few authorization cards did not establish an inability to communicate. Board expertise remains relevant to actual access obstacles, within the judicially established statutory rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither organizational success nor exhaustion of every imaginable communication technique. Employees' own solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, lawful public-property advocacy, and genuinely inaccessible workforces retain their separate treatment. No new general deference doctrine or constitutional right of entry is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit judgment is reversed and enforcement denied. The Board's order is set aside insofar as it requires nonemployee-organizer access on these facts; no remand for a new balancing exercise is ordered, and the judgment permits no interference with employees' distinct organizing rights or lawful public activity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *NLRB v. Babcock & Wilcox Co.*, 351 U.S. 105 — applied and reaffirmed: employee and nonemployee activity differ, and demonstrated inaccessibility precedes balancing under a nondiscriminatory rule.
- *Republic Aviation Corp. v. NLRB*, 324 U.S. 793 — employees' own organizing rights remain distinct and are not narrowed here.
- *Central Hardware Co. v. NLRB*, 407 U.S. 539 — applied for the prior showing of a need for nonemployee access.
- *Hudgens v. NLRB*, 424 U.S. 507 — accommodation language is preserved within Babcock's nonemployee threshold, not treated as authorization for universal balancing.
- *Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Carpenters*, 436 U.S. 180 — applied for the heavy burden concerning alternative access and the separate discrimination route.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, and *Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc.*, 497 U.S. 116 — general deference remains; a later agency approach must respect a governing judicial statutory construction.
- *Jean Country*, 291 N.L.R.B. 11 — rejected insofar as its universal balancing replaces the antecedent inaccessibility inquiry for nondiscriminatory exclusion of nonemployee organizers; other contexts are not adjudicated.

### Union-constitution enforcement

#### Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, 502 U.S. 93 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-967.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether an individual member-beneficiary may enforce a qualifying interunion constitution under section 301

**Holding:** Section 301 permits an individual union member who is a beneficiary of a qualifying contract between labor organizations to sue for its alleged violation. An international constitution binding the international and its local retains its interunion-contract character when a member invokes it.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** Section 301 classifies covered contracts rather than limiting plaintiffs to signatory organizations. Uniform federal interpretation applies when a beneficiary enforces the qualifying interunion contract.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary contract predicates remain necessary. The Court does not classify standalone local bylaws, federalize every internal union dispute, or decide breach, defenses, or damages.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The dismissal of the union-constitution claim is reversed and the claim restored for §301 proceedings; no liability or damages are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Plumbers v. Local 334 supplies the international constitution's interunion-contract status. Smith v. Evening News Association permits individual beneficiaries to enforce covered labor agreements.

For the Seventh Amendment jury holding in Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, decided December 4, 1991, No. 90-967, see Federal Courts — Civil jury right.

### Age motivation and willful discrimination

#### Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1600.

**Decided:** April 20, 1993.

##### Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation

**Holding:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; pension interference based only on years of service does not by itself establish that requirement. Liability remains possible when pension status is deliberately used as an age proxy or age and pension status both motivate the decision; age-triggered vesting and disparate impact are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, and Burdine, 450 U.S. 248 — applied: actual motivation may be proved circumstantially; no automatic inference from any unlawful motive.
- Ingersoll-Rand, 498 U.S. 133 — distinguished: ERISA pension interference and age discrimination remain distinct.
- McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. 792 — preserved; its legitimate-reason terminology does not make every improper motive an ADEA violation.

##### Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies

**Holding:** An ADEA disparate-treatment violation is willful when the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether its conduct violated the Act, whether the decision was individual or a formal policy. Once that showing is made, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive; a good-faith, nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willfulness.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thurston, 469 U.S. 111 — reaffirmed and applied to individual disparate-treatment decisions.
- Richland Shoe, 486 U.S. 128 — applied: negligence and mere awareness of statutory relevance are not willfulness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Pension status is no safe harbor when used as an age proxy. Age-based vesting and disparate impact are not decided. A nonreckless good-faith mistake about a statutory defense or exemption can defeat willfulness without defeating ordinary liability. No direct-evidence, outrageousness or predominant-age requirement is added.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for reconsideration of ADEA sufficiency and, if needed, willfulness. The younger replacement, confidentiality demands and comments are not held sufficient or insufficient here. Independent ERISA and state-law judgments retain their own effect; no liquidated-damages award is made.

### ERISA preemption of benefit mandates

#### District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade, 506 U.S. 125 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1326.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### Covered-plan content as a mandatory legal measure

**Holding:** ERISA §514(a) preempts this continuation obligation insofar as it incorporates an ERISA-covered health plan’s benefit content as the legal measure of mandatory replacement coverage, even when an employer may administer the replacement benefits separately through an exempt workers’ compensation arrangement. No §514(b) savings claim is decided, and this ruling does not preempt requirements measured solely by an ERISA-exempt governmental or church plan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt the same operative rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw v. Delta Air Lines, Inc., 463 U.S. 85 — applied; its separate-unit permission concerns independently fixed exempt benefits and does not eliminate operative reference to a different covered plan.
- Mackey v. Lanier Collection Agency & Service, Inc., 486 U.S. 825 — applied; specific legal reference is distinguished from ordinary general garnishment.
- Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon, 498 U.S. 133 — applied; dependence on a covered plan can trigger preemption despite the nominal subject of the state rule.
- Alessi v. Raybestos-Manhattan, Inc., 451 U.S. 504 — applied; regulation through workers’ compensation does not avoid ERISA’s covered-plan restriction.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1 — distinguished; a one-time payment without ongoing administration differs from this health plan.
- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts, 471 U.S. 724 — preserved; its insurance-saving holding supplies no additional §4(b) escape from a covered-plan reference.
- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, June 1, 1992 — consistent similar-language analogy only; its aviation holding does not replace ERISA’s own coverage and saving provisions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The obligation's 52-week cap is not a federal safe harbor. A separately maintained workers' compensation, unemployment-compensation or disability-insurance arrangement may remain exempt under §4(b)(3), but that does not exempt the different covered plan used as the mandatory benchmark. Exempt-only governmental or church-plan measures, §514(b) savings claims and ordinary indirect economic effects are outside the holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm preemption in the covered-plan-dependent application; the continuation mandate cannot be enforced to that extent.

### Proprietary public construction contracts

#### Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), 507 U.S. 218 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-261; No. 91-274.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Massachusetts Water Resources Authority v. Associated Builders & Contractors, No. 91-274; consolidated with No. 91-261. The same unanimous holding and reversal/remand govern both dockets.

##### Genuine proprietary project purchases are not state labor regulation

**Holding:** Garmon and Machinists preemption do not prohibit a State's project-specific contracting condition when the State pursues its own proprietary interests as owner and purchaser, the analogous private conduct is lawful, and Congress has not expressly or impliedly displaced that proprietary conduct. MWRA's Bid Specification 13.1 meets those conditions; it governs performance of the Boston Harbor project rather than imposing general labor policy on unrelated conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices: unanimous controlling authority in both dockets.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon, 359 U.S. 236 (1959): preserved as protecting the NLRA/NLRB field against state regulation; genuine proprietary purchasing does not trigger that rule.
- Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Commission, 427 U.S. 132 (1976): preserved as protecting Congress's intended free play of economic forces; a lawful project purchase is not regulatory displacement of those forces.
- Wisconsin Department of Industry v. Gould Inc., 475 U.S. 282 (1986): distinguished; debarment to enforce labor law against unrelated conduct was regulatory, while this contract concerns the State's purchased performance.
- Golden State Transit Corp. v. Los Angeles, 475 U.S. 608 (1986): distinguished; conditioning a sovereign operating license on labor settlement is regulation, not a purchase of the licensee's services.
- Jim McNeff, Inc. v. Todd, 461 U.S. 260 (1983): applied for construction prehire agreements under § 8(f); no general immunity from the Act follows.
- Woelke & Romero Framing, Inc. v. NLRB, 456 U.S. 645 (1982): applied for the construction-industry subcontracting proviso under § 8(e).
- NLRB v. Iron Workers, 434 U.S. 335 (1978): preserved concerning the § 8(f) proviso permitting employees to use elections under §§ 9(c), (e) to reject representation or union-security arrangements.
- Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218 (1947), and Maryland v. Louisiana, 451 U.S. 725 (1981): applied as caution against inferring congressional displacement not supplied by the federal scheme.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public ownership and procurement labels alone are insufficient. The lawful private analogy is undisputed here; §8(e)’s construction proviso and §8(f)’s prehire permission remain distinct, including employee elections under §§9(c), (e). The decision does not make MWRA an NLRA employer or adjudicate §8(d), ERISA, equal protection, due process, Sherman Act or state-law alternatives.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the First Circuit in both dockets and remand. The preliminary injunction cannot rest on the rejected Garmon or Machinists preemption theory; properly remaining independent grounds are not resolved.

### Scheduled occupational hearing-loss compensation

#### Bath Iron Works Corp. v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs, 506 U.S. 153 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-871.

**Decided:** January 12, 1993.

##### Compensation branch and injury date

**Holding:** Noise-induced occupational hearing loss sustained during workplace exposure is a scheduled, presumptively disabling injury governed by §908(c)(13), whether claimed before or after retirement; the injury is complete at last exposure, which supplies the injury date for the wage basis. Later awareness or filing does not make it an occupational disease that does not immediately result in disability under §910(i), and §908(c)(23)’s special retiree formula therefore does not apply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; nine votes for the rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director, OWCP, 449 U.S. 268 (1980), is applied: scheduled compensation is mandatory within its statutory branch and does not require actual earning loss. Todd Shipyards Corp. v. Black, 717 F.2d 1280 (9th Cir. 1983), is distinguished as a latent-disease case; its rationale does not move the completed hearing injury to the awareness date. The contrary retiree classifications in Ingalls Shipbuilding and Alabama Dry Dock do not survive this statutory construction.

##### The distinct filing protection

**Holding:** Section 908(c)(13)(D) postpones the running of the §912 notice period and §913 claim period for hearing loss until the employee receives an audiogram and its accompanying report indicating hearing loss; it does not postpone the substantive time of injury to awareness. The 1984 amendments accordingly provide a hearing-specific filing protection, independently defeating the contention that congressional concern about late hearing claims necessarily places them in the latent-disease compensation system.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; nine votes for the rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Potomac Electric remains the source of the schedule’s fixed statutory operation. Redick v. Bethlehem Steel Corp., 16 BRBS 155 (1984), cannot justify denying a scheduled claim solely because hearing loss was discovered after voluntary retirement; reference to it in debate does not change either statutory trigger.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The schedule does not require reduced actual earnings and uses two-thirds of the applicable average weekly wage for the prescribed weeks (200 for total bilateral loss, proportionately reduced for partial loss). The special delayed-disease retiree wage formula cannot be combined with the schedule. Retirement audiograms may identify compensable loss but are not mandatory testing duties; no new aging-apportionment dispute is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Brown’s award. Its 82.4 percent impairment finding is unchallenged; use of the 1985 national wage remains because the recalculation objection was waived, not because that wage is the governing scheduled-loss rule.

### Public employee compensatory time

#### Moreau v. Klevenhagen, 508 U.S. 22 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1.
**Decided:** May 3, 1993.

##### The earlier-hire component: the transition rule supplies the agreement

**Holding:** For employees described in § 207(o)(2)(A)(ii) who were hired before April 15, 1986, the regular compensatory-time practice in effect on that date constitutes the agreement or understanding the statute otherwise requires before the work is performed. This transition rule operates independently of, and does not require, a further individual or collective agreement for that cohort.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine support this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No prior Supreme Court holding addressed this transition provision; the Court applies its text without displacing any earlier authority.

##### The later-hire component: individual agreements where no representative could bind

**Holding:** Where employees have selected a representative organization that state law does not authorize to enter a binding § 207(o)(2)(A)(i) agreement, the employer may rely on individual § 207(o)(2)(A)(ii) agreements or understandings with the affected employees hired on or after April 15, 1986, rather than lose the compensatory-time option entirely for want of a legally capable collective representative.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine support this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general collective-bargaining entitlement, overtime exemption or presumption of individual consent is created. The Court does not decide a practice adopted after April 15, 1986 or employees with a representative legally capable of making the collective agreement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm on both cohorts: the regular April 15, 1986 practice supplies the earlier-hire agreement; actual individual agreements remain available for later hires lacking a legally capable representative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No Supreme Court precedent is overruled, limited, or extended beyond its own terms; the Court decides only the two routes actually presented on this record.

### ERISA equitable relief against nonfiduciaries

#### Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1671.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief

**Holding:** Section502(a)(3)’s appropriate equitable relief encompasses traditionally equitable categories of relief, not compensatory damages merely because a court of equity could award them in trust litigation. The requested monetary compensation from this admitted nonfiduciary for the plan’s losses is therefore unavailable under that provision; the Court does not bar every monetary equitable remedy or decide the antecedent, expressly disclaimed cause-of-action question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The expressly disclaimed antecedent cause of action is reserved, not rejected as jurisdictional. Other equitable monetary remedies are not categorically barred; restitution, unappealed claims and the separate malpractice disposition remain untouched.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the reviewed compensatory claim under §502(a)(3); no liability finding or award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Curtis v. Loether, 415 U.S.189: traditional legal compensation distinction applied.
- Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers v. Terry, 494 U.S.558: remedy classification applied; trust forum alone does not decide the statutory category.
- Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch, 489 U.S.101: trust-law guidance preserved subject to express statutory limits.
- Clews v. Jamieson, 182 U.S.461: equity’s monetary powers acknowledged but distinguished from the statutory category.
- Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell, 473 U.S.134: enforcement-structure caution applied; its reserved question is now resolved only within this holding.
- United States v. Burke, May26,1992: its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general remedial-range test.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February26,1992: appropriate-remedy presumption distinguished because Congress expressly limited this provision to equitable relief.

### Federal supervision of employee benefit trusts

#### Local 144 Nursing Home Pension Fund v. Demisay, 508 U.S. 581 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-610.
**Decided:** June 14, 1993.

##### Section 302 restrains prohibited transactions

**Holding:** Section302(e) authorizes restraint of violations of the payment, demand, receipt and related prohibitions in §302(a) and (b); §302(c)(5) identifies qualifying trusts rather than a general supervisory remedy for later administration. Retaining these reserves supplies no transfer remedy without an actual prohibited transaction; payment to a nonqualifying trust remains subject to the statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, Section 302 restrains prohibited transactions portion; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The three-Justice pooled-fund rationale is noncontrolling. Separate ERISA asset-transfer and fiduciary claims remain unresolved; lawful receipt does not immunize later fiduciary misconduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the §302 transfer direction and remand; the court of appeals may consider the unreviewed ERISA claims previously rejected below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arroyo, 359 U.S.419 — lawful receipt distinguished from later diversion; contrary supervisory language is dictum.
- Amax, 453 U.S.322 — fiduciary independence retained; supervisory language does not establish jurisdiction.
- Robinson, 455 U.S.562 — reasonableness-review rejection retained; reserved supervisory scope resolved.
- Mertens, June 1, 1993 — unchanged; its remedy limit does not resolve these unreviewed ERISA claims.

### Employment discrimination and proof of pretext

#### St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-602.
**Decided:** June 25, 1993.

##### Pretext alone does not command judgment, and circumstantial evidence need not be supplemented by a special category of proof

**Holding:** After an employer produces admissible evidence of nondiscriminatory reasons, a proven prima-facie case and rejection of the offered explanations do not automatically command judgment for the employee. Those circumstances may support a finding of intentional discrimination without a mandatory additional category of direct or independent proof; the Eighth Circuit’s automatic-liability mandate is vacated, while no majority here adopts a replacement persuasion allocation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Burdine’s persuasion allocation is not replaced. No universal Title VII/§1983 equivalence, clear-error decision, resolution of retaliation, remaining defenses, relief or applicability of the 1991 Act is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the appellate automatic-liability mandate under Title VII and the related §1983 claim against Long and remand. No district-court vacatur, new burden allocation or judgment for either side is ordered. Existing district findings remain subject to proper review; retaliation remains for consideration and the unappealed §1981 dismissal is unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green,411 U.S.792: indirect framework retained; no automatic liability holding adopted.
- Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine,450 U.S.248: production stage retained; no five-Justice replacement of its persuasion rule.
- Postal Service Board of Governors v. Aikens,460 U.S.711: actual discrimination and circumstantial evidence remain central.
- Hazen, April20,1993: prohibited motive remains distinct from general unfairness; no added evidence category follows.

### Hostile work environments

#### [Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Harris_v_Forklift_Systems_Inc_merits_1993-11-09.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

### Insured employee-benefit assets

#### [John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 510 U.S. 86](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/John_Hancock_Mutual_Life_Insurance_Co_v_Harris_Trust_Savings_Bank_merits_1993-12-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1074.

**Decided:** 1993-12-13.

For the other questions in John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 1993-12-13, No. 92-1074, see Administrative Law — An agency interpretation cannot erase the qualified exclusion.

##### The guaranteed-benefit exclusion operates by component

**Holding:** Under ERISA §401(b)(2), a guaranteed-benefit policy itself is a plan asset, while the insurer's underlying assets are not deemed plan assets solely because it issued the policy to the extent the policy genuinely guarantees the amount of benefits. The disputed nonguaranteed free-funds component is not excluded: its plan-asset character subjects Hancock to fiduciary obligations to the extent it exercises authority or control over management or disposition of those assets under §3(21)(A), without deciding breach, causation, loss, or remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. Six Justices adopt the component-specific construction and its application to the disputed free funds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- SEC v. United Benefit Life Insurance Co.: used for the distinction between a genuine guarantee and retained investment risk; the ERISA text supplies the governing exclusion.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: kept separate; its treatment of available equitable categories does not decide whether these funds are plan assets or whether Hancock committed a breach.

##### Insurance regulation does not displace ERISA's express direction

**Holding:** McCarran-Ferguson does not bar application of ERISA's express insurance-related fiduciary and guaranteed-benefit provisions to this general-account contract. ERISA's insurance-saving clause preserves compatible state insurance regulation, but does not make a conflicting state obligation controlling over ERISA itself; a concrete conflict must be assessed as such rather than treated as a categorical exemption for all insurer assets.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. This six-Justice ground rejects the asserted state-insurance obstacle to affirmance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts: applied to the coexistence of ERISA and state insurance regulation; no blanket withdrawal of federal obligations follows.
- FMC Corp. v. Holliday: applied to the coexistence of ERISA and state insurance regulation; no blanket withdrawal of federal obligations follows.
- Silkwood v. Kerr-McGee Corp.: applied for the distinction between compatible state regulation and a conflicting obstacle to federal law.
- United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe: distinguished because its federal-priority and policyholder-protection issue is different; its holding remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The contract contains guarantees and a residual floor; those do not guarantee the disputed free-funds benefits. A guaranteed reasonable return and fixed conversion protection are indicators, not an exhaustive mandatory formula. No breach, loss or remedy follows from status alone, and no categorical anti-deference rule for inconsistency or litigation positions is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit's relevant judgment is affirmed, 6–3. Asset status and applicable fiduciary functions remain distinct from breach, causation, loss and relief, which must be addressed in further proceedings; no monetary or equitable award is made.

### Labor remedies and hearing misconduct

#### [ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, 510 U.S. 317](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/ABF_Freight_System_v_NLRB_merits_1994-01-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1550.

**Decided:** 1994-01-24.

##### Hearing falsehood and the statutory remedy

**Holding:** Under NLRA section 10(c), an employee's deliberate false testimony at the remedial hearing does not automatically forfeit reinstatement or backpay for an established unfair labor practice; the Board may withhold relief for supported misconduct but is not required to apply a categorical forfeiture rule. The separate statutory prohibition applies when the employee was actually suspended or discharged for cause; later hearing testimony does not itself establish that earlier discharge ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor agree with the lawfulness of the result in a separate judgment concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB: applied to the Board's primary responsibility for connecting relief with the Act's policies.
- Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB: applied to the judicially enforceable boundary against remedies pursuing unrelated ends.
- Precision Instrument Manufacturing Co. v. Automotive Maintenance Machinery Co.: distinguished; equitable misconduct principles do not impose an extra categorical restriction on section 10(c).
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB: preserved; its binding statutory construction limits Board authority but does not decide remedies for this established unlawful discharge.

##### Review of this remedial choice

**Holding:** A Board remedy remains reviewable for arbitrariness, an inadequate explanation or departure from section 10(c)'s statutory purpose; the Board must recognize material misconduct and give reasons sufficient to connect its selected remedy to the Act, but need not adopt a new sanctions formula or expressly discuss a separate forfeiture sanction in every case. On this record, the Board's recognition of the false account, its accepted finding of antiunion discharge and its stated restorative labor rationale support enforcement without a further explanatory remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor find the action lawful on their narrower statutory-discretion ground, without endorsing the Court's public-remedy assessment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB: applied to review the remedy's statutory connection without replacing agency discretion with judicial policy.
- Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB: applied to preserve meaningful review of the actual remedial explanation and statutory purpose.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB: distinguished on the issue decided; enforcing a statutory limit and reviewing an expressly delegated remedy are different inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not impose automatic employee entitlement, excuse falsehood, presume a separate Board perjury-sanction inquiry, or require express forfeiture discussion in every case. Independently authorized sanctions remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit enforcement judgment is affirmed. Reinstatement and backpay remain enforceable; no explanatory remand, new sanction or recalculation is ordered. The established unfair-labor-practice finding is outside this limited review.

### Skilled employees and supervisory status

#### [NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, 511 U.S. 571](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/NLRB_v_Health_Care_Retirement_Corp_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1964.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority

**Holding:** The Board permissibly interprets NLRA §2(11), in the context of the Act's protection of skilled and professional employees, to distinguish direction incidental to skilled patient care from genuine supervisory authority exercised for the employer. On this record, its functional construction and supported determination that the four licensed practical nurses are employees must be sustained; no occupational exemption or independent judicial substitute for the agency's rationale is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. All five directly adopt the permissible-construction ground; Stone-Zsela's proposed mandatory construction appears only in his concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fall River Dyeing & Finishing Corp. v. NLRB, 482 U.S. 27 (1987): applies review for rational interpretation consistent with the Act and substantial evidentiary support.
- NLRB v. Yeshiva University, 444 U.S. 672 (1980): uses its distinction between ordinary professional duties and management, while preserving professional/institutional overlap and the separate managerial question.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves binding statutory boundaries; it does not settle this classification tension.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943), and National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: permit reliance on the agency's discernible actual rationale, not an invented substitute.
- Packard Motor Car Co. v. NLRB, 330 U.S. 485 (1947): distinguishes its pre-amendment employer-interest discussion from the later statutory definition.
- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, January 24, 1994: preserves Board judgment within an enacted delegation, without authorizing disregard of statutory text.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2(11)'s alternative authorities are hiring, transfer, suspension, layoff, recall, promotion, discharge, assignment, reward, discipline, responsible direction, grievance adjustment and effective recommendation of such action. Any one may suffice, even if rarely exercised, but exercise must serve the employer and require independent judgment beyond routine or clerical action. Hiring or firing is not indispensable. Professional judgment can be independent judgment exercised through genuine supervisory authority; it does not itself establish that authority. The sustained construction is permissible, not compulsory for all future Boards.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit's supervisory-status ruling is reversed and remanded, 5–4. Give effect to the supported Board employee-status determination and decide independently preserved remaining issues. No backpay award or finding of every unfair-labor-practice element is supplied.

### Independent state rights and railway-labor preemption

#### [Hawaiian Airlines, Inc. v. Norris, 512 U.S. 246](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Hawaiian_Airlines_v_Norris_merits_1994-06-20.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-2058, including the related Finazzo judgment under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The related Finazzo judgment is included under Rule 12.2; the airline and officers actions retain their distinct surviving counts.

##### Independent state rights remain enforceable without interpreting the labor agreement

**Holding:** The Railway Labor Act's exclusive machinery for minor disputes covers claims founded on a collective-bargaining agreement or requiring interpretation of its disputed terms; it does not displace independent state-law claims resolvable through factual questions about conduct or motive without that interpretation. Norris's public-policy and whistleblower theories meet that independence test on the presented preemption question, so both reviewed Hawaii judgments are affirmed, leaving the surviving claims, their ordinary elements and defenses, and any relief for state proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion for a unanimous Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the complete rule, application, precedent treatment, and bounded affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A statutory or nonnegotiable label does not by itself establish independence. Courts examine the claim's actual elements; a state claim using disputed contractual just cause as its substantive standard differs from the factual retaliatory-motive inquiry here. Overlapping facts, parallel remedies, unfinished grievance steps, or an asserted contract defense do not alone defeat an independent right. The separate federal dismissal of the CBA claim remains intact. State elements, defenses, ordinary limits against duplicate recovery, and genuine arbitration duties remain applicable. Affirmance establishes no unsafe-aircraft finding, wrongful discharge, damages amount, reinstatement, or new FAA cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lingle v. Norge Division of Magic Chef, Inc., 486 U.S. 399 (1988): extends its independent-right and necessary-interpretation distinction from the Labor Management Relations Act context to RLA preemption, without equating the two statutes in every respect.
- Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. Buell, 480 U.S. 557 (1987): applies its refusal to substitute RLA grievance machinery for an independent substantive protection; no new federal injury or whistleblower action follows.
- Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Railway Labor Executives' Assn., 491 U.S. 299 (1989): preserves its arguable-contractual-justification test within the major/minor distinction for contractual disputes; it does not answer the antecedent source-of-right question.
- Andrews v. Louisville & Nashville Railroad Co., 406 U.S. 320 (1972): distinguishes its agreement-founded entitlement while preserving compulsory adjustment for that category.
- Elgin, Joliet & Eastern Railway Co. v. Burley, 325 U.S. 711 (1945): disavows its broader suggestion that an omitted contractual case necessarily includes an independent state-law claim within minor disputes; that dictum does not control. Its contractual and representation holdings are not overruled.
- Livadas v. Bradshaw, June 13, 1994: applies the distinction between factual consultation and necessary interpretation, preserving genuinely contract-dependent claims; its separate bargaining-policy and §1983 holdings are not extended here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both reviewed Hawaii judgments are affirmed: the airline action's public-policy preemption disposition and the officers action's public-policy and whistleblower dispositions. Their existing remands remain effective for adjudication of surviving state claims, defenses and relief, subject to ordinary limits on duplicate recovery. The separate federal dismissal of the airline contract claim remains intact. No extra airline-count judgment, liability, damages, reinstatement or renewed grievance is ordered.

### Independent wage rights and collective bargaining

#### [Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Livadas_v_Bradshaw_merits_1994-06-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1920.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

For the other questions in Livadas v. Bradshaw, 1994-06-13, No. 92-1920, see Civil Rights — Section 1983 supplies a channel for this identified federal labor right.

##### An independent wage-penalty claim is not preempted by §301

**Holding:** Section 301 does not displace a nonnegotiable state wage-payment claim whose resolution requires no interpretation of disputed collective-bargaining terms. Consulting an undisputed contractual wage rate to calculate the possible statutory penalty supplies arithmetic, not the contract interpretation needed for §301 preemption; genuinely contract-dependent claims remain different.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this antecedent holding.

**Material application:** Section 201 requires earned unpaid wages immediately on discharge; §203 addresses willful delay, continuing wages at the same rate until payment or commencement of an action for no more than thirty days. Section 219 bars private agreements setting aside those protections but permits more frequent, greater or earlier payment. Section 229 permits wage-collection suits despite arbitration, with a separate exception for disputes over interpretation or application of a bargaining agreement containing arbitration. The broader administrative exclusion is the Commissioner's policy, not §229's command. Contract §18.8 permitted this direct claim through arbitration or an authorized tribunal or agency without contract interpretation. No penalty is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lingle v. Norge Division of Magic Chef, Inc., 486 U.S. 399: applies independence from contract interpretation; use of undisputed wage information does not change the claim's source.
- Allis-Chalmers Corp. v. Lueck, 471 U.S. 202: preserves preemption of genuinely contract-dependent claims; it does not preempt this arithmetic use.
- Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, December 4, 1991: preserves the distinct §301 route for a beneficiary enforcing a qualifying interunion contract, not every union-related claim.

##### California may not penalize completion of protected collective bargaining

**Holding:** The NLRA forbids California's policy withholding otherwise available enforcement of an independent wage right solely because an employee is covered by a collective-bargaining agreement containing arbitration. The employee's protected freedom to bargain includes carrying bargaining through to such an agreement; this rule neither compels employer assent nor generally requires the State to create an enforcement service.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt this statutory labor-right holding.

**Material application:** The holding does not guarantee identical access through every enforcement route. Proceedings under §§98 and 98.1 differ from representation under §98.3(a), which depends on the Commissioner's assessment of financial inability to employ counsel and a valid, enforceable claim. The employee's own §218 suit and §218.5 fee provision remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nash v. Florida Industrial Commission, 389 U.S. 235: applies the prohibition on state benefit conditions burdening federally protected labor activity.
- Golden State Transit Corp. v. Los Angeles, 493 U.S. 103: applies protection of the federally secured bargaining process against governmental interference.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1: preserves independent minimum labor standards and does not authorize this penalty for protected bargaining.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for source-authorized relief against the official-capacity policy. Monetary-relief and Eleventh Amendment constraints remain distinct. No employer wage penalty, personal damages or automatic reinstatement of a money award is ordered; willfulness, the effect of backpay and other penalty defenses remain unresolved.

### Railroad emotional-injury liability

#### [Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall, 512 U.S. 532](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Consolidated_Rail_Corp_v_Gottshall_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1956, including Carlisle under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Carlisle is included under Rule 12.2. The two reviewed judgments receive different mandatory remand instructions.

##### Negligent emotional injury is actionable within the employee's zone of danger

**Holding:** FELA permits recovery for negligently inflicted emotional injury when the employee sustains physical impact from the employer's negligence or is placed in immediate risk of physical harm by that negligence; on the threatened-impact branch, the emotional injury must result from fear of physical harm to the employee himself. The employee must still establish negligence, legally cognizable injury and FELA causation; seriousness, genuineness and foreseeability alone do not substitute for the physical-danger boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the same operative rule; no combination of separate rationales supplies its force.

**Material application:** Whether every otherwise qualifying emotional injury needs physical manifestations, intentional infliction, and the full category of fear-of-future-disease claims remain undecided. FELA negligence and causation are still required independently of the physical-danger boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. Buell, 480 U.S. 557 (1987): answers its reserved negligent-emotional-injury question; its protection of independently cognizable FELA claims from RLA displacement remains intact.
- Urie v. Thompson, 337 U.S. 163 (1949): preserves occupational-disease coverage; distinguishes that coverage from the boundary for negligent emotional harm.
- Monessen Southwestern Railway Co. v. Morgan, 486 U.S. 330 (1988): applies the importance of common-law background without making every tort rule immutable.
- Ellis v. Union Pacific Railroad Co., 329 U.S. 649 (1947): applies the requirement of negligence and rejection of insurer liability.
- Rogers v. Missouri Pacific Railroad Co., 352 U.S. 500 (1957): preserves FELA's causal-contribution rule without extending it to eliminate duty, breach or the compensable-injury boundary.

##### Gottshall's exposure and causal application remain for the court below

**Holding:** The Third Circuit's Gottshall judgment is reversed because it did not apply the zone-of-danger rule, and the case is remanded for that court to apply the rule in the first instance. The Court determines neither whether Gottshall was placed in immediate risk of physical harm nor whether his emotional injury resulted from fear of harm to himself; witnessing and attempting to prevent his coworker's death alone do not establish those predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this application and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Buell, 480 U.S. 557: its reservation requires resolution and application of the governing injury boundary, rather than treating emotional injury as already fully defined.
- Urie, 337 U.S. 163: occupational-injury coverage remains effective but supplies no finding about Gottshall's immediate danger or the cause of his distress.

##### Carlisle's work-stress claim is excluded and requires judgment for Conrail

**Holding:** A FELA negligent-emotional-injury claim arising from the prolonged work stress presented in Carlisle, without physical impact or immediate risk of physical harm within the zone-of-danger rule, is not compensable. The Third Circuit's judgment sustaining the $386,500 verdict is reversed, and the case is remanded with instructions to enter judgment for Conrail, not to retry the same excluded claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five Justices adopt both the legal exclusion and mandatory defendant-judgment remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ellis, 329 U.S. 649: applies the distinction between fault-based liability and workplace insurance without discounting the evidence actually offered.
- Monessen, 486 U.S. 330: applies common-law guidance to the compensable-injury boundary, not to reweigh a jury's supported factual findings.
- Rogers, 352 U.S. 500: preserves relaxed causal contribution but does not let causation evidence replace the required injury category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Third Circuit judgments are reversed, 5–4. Gottshall is remanded for first-instance application of the zone-of-danger rule, without automatic reinstatement of summary judgment, a trial entitlement or a damages award. Carlisle is remanded with instructions to enter judgment for Conrail, with no new trial of the excluded work-stress claim.

##### Whether an INA deportation hearing is an adjudication under section 554 for administrative EAJA fees

**Holding:** An administrative deportation hearing governed by the INA's exclusive procedure rather than by 5 U.S.C. §554 is not an adjudication under section 554 and therefore falls outside EAJA §504's administrative fee authorization, even if the hearing resembles formal APA adjudication.

### Bank regulatory review

#### Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc., 502 U.S. 32 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-913 and 90-914.  
**Decided:** December 3, 1991.

##### Whether bankruptcy jurisdiction or Leedom v. Kyne permits an injunction against ongoing Board proceedings despite section 1818(i)(1)

**Holding:** Section 1818(i)(1) bars a district court from enjoining an ongoing Board notice-or-order proceeding except through channels that §1818 itself authorizes. Neither general bankruptcy jurisdiction nor Leedom v. Kyne supplies an alternative intervention channel where the proceeding falls within the governmental regulatory exception and meaningful review of a final order remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The adversary action fits none of §1818's authorized channels for temporary-order challenges, final-order review, or Board enforcement. Section 362(b)(4) permits governmental regulatory proceedings to continue; §§362(a)(3) and (a)(6) do not halt these nonfinal proceedings before seizure of estate property or recovery of a prepetition claim. Section 1334 supplies no competing jurisdiction because the Board is not another court and continuation does not displace control of estate property.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The regulation's validity and any actual conflict at enforcement are reserved. The decision changes no agency-deference rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No. 90-913 is reversed, removing the source-of-strength injunction; No. 90-914 is affirmed, leaving the refusal to enjoin the §23A proceeding intact. Both proceedings may continue through the statutory channels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Leedom v. Kyne is confined to circumstances without meaningful later review; §1818(h)(2) supplies that review here and §1818(i)(1) expressly precludes intervention. Boire's narrow understanding of Kyne is applied.

### Census determinations and judicial review

#### Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1502.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the separate standing and constitutional-counting holdings in Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992, No. 91-1502, see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury and Election Law — Interstate congressional apportionment.

##### The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable

**Holding:** The Secretary's completed census determination supplies final agency action under APA §704 even though the President later transmits the resulting apportionment statement. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit the counting decision entirely to unreviewable discretion: the duty to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice to the Secretary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule while agreeing with reversal on their own reviewability ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presidential transmission under §2a does not confer an independent presidential choice of the population counted under §141. The President's own APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief remain undecided; Waterman's independent foreign-affairs decision structure is different.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): applied for reviewability and practical completion of an agency determination with concrete consequences; the statutory transmittal here does not postpone finality.
- Chicago & Southern Air Lines, Inc. v. Waterman Steamship Corp., 333 U.S. 103 (1948): distinguished because Congress there required an independent presidential foreign-affairs judgment; its rule for that decision structure is preserved.
- Japan Whaling Assn. v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): applied for review against statutory limits despite broad administrative judgment; methodological latitude is not absence of law to apply.

##### The statutory and administrative objections fail on this record

**Holding:** Section 141 permits the supported inclusion of temporarily overseas personnel with enduring state ties, and the Secretary's comparative choice of supplemented home-of-record information is not arbitrary or capricious on this record. The agency must supply a contemporaneous, reasonably discernible explanation connecting its available information and residence objective to its choice; imperfection alone does not require rejection of every available dataset.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. The other four Justices do not join the statutory or APA merits determination.

**Material application:** The contemporaneous record supplies supplementary personnel information and continuing residence reasons despite cancellation of the proposed survey. Home of record may identify enduring ties; tax residence and last duty station have different defects. The holding relies on actual agency reasons, not stricken later declarations or counsel's substitute explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No replacement allocation, judicially selected dataset, injunction against the President, or general APA status for the President is decided. No claim that another residence method necessarily restores a seat is found proved. The statutory and administrative holdings are record-specific applications; no earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): applied for consideration of relevant factors, reasoned explanation and review on the agency's actual rationale; no exemption from review accompanies census discretion.
- Bowman Transportation, Inc. v. Arkansas-Best Freight System, Inc., 419 U.S. 281 (1974): applied for a reasonably discernible decisional path despite imperfect exposition; a court may not substitute a new rationale.

### Coastal permits and Administrative Procedure Act review

#### United States v. Alaska, 503 U.S. 569 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 118, Original.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Alaska*, April 21, 1992, No. 118, Original, see Federal Courts — Standing and original jurisdiction (live title controversy), Constitutional Structure — Federal and state submerged-land boundaries (§10 condition), and Administrative Law — Coastal permits and Administrative Procedure Act review (regulatory and APA objections).

##### Whether the governing regulation permits shelf-rights review and the Corps unlawfully delegated its decision

**Holding:** The public-interest and coastline provisions of 33 C.F.R. §320.4(a) and (f) permit consideration of the Nome project's prospective effect on federal shelf rights; subsection (g)(6)'s exclusion of disputes about an applicant's construction-site property interest does not forbid that inquiry. The Corps' documented adoption of the boundary concern and its own permit decision reject Alaska's claim of unlawful delegation on this record, without authorizing Interior or the Attorney General to exercise an independent permitting veto.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part III, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices support both propositions. Stone agrees with the disposition of this component only.

**Material application:** Section 320.4(a) and (f) authorize shelf-effects review; subsection (g)(6) concerns the applicant’s construction-site property interest. The Corps adopted Interior’s stated concern and made its own permit determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent Interior or Attorney General veto is authorized. Compliance with unpresented internal-routing requirements and automatic disclaimer rules for other permits are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, February 26, 1992 — applied at its source-specific limit: a valid regulation guides the agency's own permit determination; it does not transfer responsibility to a different agency.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992 — applied: the agency's own discernible ground supports review; agreement with an adviser is not a substitute for finding that ground in the agency record.

##### Whether publication or actual and timely notice defeats the §552 challenge to the Nome condition

**Holding:** Section 552(a)(1) does not invalidate this condition as adverse application of a secret review criterion: the governing public-interest and coastline regulations published consideration of the project's effects on federal outer continental rights. The absence of an express catalogue of every possible curative disclaimer does not make that published criterion an undisclosed rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part IV-A, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the §552 challenge. Stone agrees with its disposition only.

**Material application:** The published coastline regulation expressly identified baseline shifts and effects on federal outer continental rights. A catalogue of every possible curative disclaimer was unnecessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This applied published criterion does not validate an undisclosed binding rule for every coastal project or dispense with separate §553 duties.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S. 199 (1974) — distinguished on the applied criterion and preserved: undisclosed eligibility restrictions and disregard of required agency procedures remain impermissible; this case applies a published shelf-effects criterion.

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently, §552(a)(1)'s bar on adverse application of unpublished matter does not protect a person who had actual and timely notice of its terms. Alaska had that notice of the Nome condition and its stated ground before executing the disclaimer, so any failure separately to publish those particular terms supplies no basis for invalidating the disclaimer as applied to Alaska.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** White's opinion, Part IV-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding. Stone agrees with its disposition only.

**Material application of alternative holding:** The July 1, 1983 communication supplied the condition and ground, followed by approximately ten months for consideration and negotiation before Alaska executed the disclaimer on May 9, 1984.

**Limits of alternative holding:** Notice of this condition is not notice of every general policy and does not itself cure §553 or another mandatory-procedure violation.

**Treatment of earlier authority for alternative holding:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S. 199 (1974) — applied with the statutory actual-notice qualification intact; its protection against undisclosed restrictions is not converted into invalidation regardless of timely knowledge.

##### Whether §553 required an additional legislative rule for the individual Nome permit condition

**Holding:** The underlying public-interest and coastline provisions were adopted through notice and comment, and §553 did not require the Corps to promulgate an additional legislative rule before specifying this curative condition in Nome's individual licensing proceeding. This holding depends on application of those existing criteria and the absence of an established, separately binding Corps rule requiring every coastal permit to contain a disclaimer.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part V, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices support the rule and application. Stone agrees with the disposition of the §553 component only.

**Material application:** The 1973 proposal and 1974 final rule underwent notice and comment. The individual condition applied those criteria in licensing, which §551 includes within adjudication.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No public-property exception or exemption for legislative rules is adopted. The record establishes no separately binding Corps policy requiring all coastal permits to carry disclaimers.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. ### Filed rates, counterclaims and primary jurisdiction

#### Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1496.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### The statutory claim may be asserted before payment

**Holding:** A shipper may assert its claim for unreasonable-rate damages under 49 U.S.C.§ 11705(b)(3) as a counterclaim in the carrier's action for undercharges on the same shipments; Rule 8(c) allows correction of its designation as a defense when justice requires. Under§ 11706(g), the claim accrues upon delivery or tender of delivery, so payment of the disputed tariff charge is not a prerequisite; a negotiated rate alone supplies no common-law defense to the filed tariff.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this statutory rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116: applied; its rejection of the negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy remains, alongside the statutory unreasonable-rate remedy.
- United States ex rel. Louisville Cement Co. v. ICC, 246 U.S. 638: its payment-based accrual construction was superseded by Congress's delivery-accrual provision; it cannot establish the proposed prerequisite.
- Southern Construction Co. v. Pickard, 371 U.S. 57: applied for assertion of related counterclaims under ordinary federal procedure.
- Texas & Pacific Railway v. Mugg, 202 U.S. 242; Louisville & Nashville Railroad v. Maxwell, 237 U.S. 94; Pittsburgh, Cincinnati, Chicago & St.Louis Railway v. Fink, 250 U.S. 577: their bars on common-law avoidance of filed tariffs remain; statutory reparations are distinct.
- T.I.M.E. Inc. v. United States, 359 U.S. 464: its rejection of a free-standing unreasonable-rate defense does not eliminate the later express statutory cause of action.

##### Same-transaction recoupment survives the affirmative limitation

**Holding:** The two-year period in§ 11706(c)(2) for an affirmative unreasonable-rate damages action does not bar a shipper from reducing liability in a timely carrier action through recoupment arising from the same shipments. This defensive recovery extends only to the asserted liability; the decision creates no unlimited extension for unrelated claims or affirmative recovery beyond it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support this independent limitation ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bull v. United States, 295 U.S. 247: applied; same-transaction recoupment survives while the principal action is timely.
- United States v. Western Pacific Railroad, 352 U.S. 59: applied for the analogous statutory limitation rationale; the Government's distinct setoff power is not made a condition of private recoupment.

##### Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction

**Holding:** A court hearing the statutory unreasonable-rate claim must allow a reasonable opportunity for the parties to obtain the ICC's determination of rate reasonableness, but that primary-jurisdiction procedure does not deprive the court of jurisdiction. The court may retain the action, ordinarily staying proceedings for that purpose, or dismiss without prejudice only if the parties would not be unfairly disadvantaged; neither prior payment nor a completed ICC ruling is required to file the civil claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this allocation and its qualifications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Western Pacific Railroad, 352 U.S. 59: applied to allocate the expert rate issue while preserving the judicial claim.
- Mitchell Coal & Coke Co. v. Pennsylvania Railroad, 230 U.S. 247: applied for a stay affording a reasonable opportunity to request an agency ruling.
- Carnation Co. v. Pacific Westbound Conference, 383 U.S. 213: applied to retention or nondisadvantaging dismissal without prejudice.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, 467 U.S. 837: applied to the ICC's longstanding reasonable construction of its reparations authority, without new deference doctrine.
- Myers v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp., 303 U.S. 41, and Heckler v. Ringer, 466 U.S. 602: distinguished; their exhaustion setting does not supply the missing statutory prerequisite here.

##### Separate judgment requires the ordinary discretionary determination

**Holding:** A separate final judgment on the carrier's claim requires Rule 54(b)'s express determination that there is no just reason for delay, considering judicial administration and the actual equities. Prompt suit by a solvent carrier ordinarily favors separate judgment, while actual or threatened insolvency of the party seeking judgment weighs against it without creating an absolute bar; appropriate security and an enforcement stay under Rule 62(h) remain available where other equities justify them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support the rule and remand rather than deciding discretion initially.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Mackey, 351 U.S. 427, and Cold Metal Process Co. v. United Engineering & Foundry Co., 351 U.S. 445: applied for Rule 54(b)'s separate-judgment authority and safeguards.
- Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. General Electric Co., 446 U.S. 1: applied; actual or threatened insolvency is relevant, not an absolute bar, and protective conditions may address competing risks.
- Crancer v. Lowden, 315 U.S. 631: distinguished; it sustained discretionary collection without an available judicial reparations counterclaim after the shippers chose the separate rail-agency route, rather than requiring collection in every case.
- Keogh v. Chicago & Northwestern Railway, 260 U.S. 156; Arizona Grocery Co. v. Atchison,Topeka & Santa Fe Railway, 284 U.S. 370; Lowden v. Simonds-Shields-Lonsdale Grain Co., 306 U.S. 516: applied for the continuing force of filed rates not disapproved by the ICC.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Referral means allowing a party time to seek an ICC determination, not commanding the agency to answer. Negotiated rates do not supplant filed tariffs. Same-transaction recoupment fixes the estate’s proper claim rather than paying an unrelated preference. No rate, affirmative excess damages or separate-judgment outcome is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the statutory counterclaims, an appropriate opportunity to obtain the ICC rate ruling and an express Rule 54(b) determination. No automatic carrier priority or insolvency bar applies.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The hospitals concede no applicable regulatory exemption or exception. Independent challenges to unlawful reimbursement methods remain available outside this review; no automatic actual-cost escape or replacement of Chevron is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm; no unconditional individualized reimbursement above otherwise valid ceilings or particular additional payment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FEC v. Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, 454 U.S.27: reasonable agency interpretation within the statutory delegation applied.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S.421: judicial interpretation and the significance of inconsistent agency positions preserved.
- Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S.204: unsupported litigation rationalizations receive no automatic controlling weight; change itself is a different issue.

### Exhaustion of superior-agency review

#### Darby v. Cisneros, 509 U.S. 137 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2045.
**Decided:** June 21, 1993.

##### Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review

**Holding:** In an APA action, an otherwise final agency decision is reviewable without an optional superior-agency appeal unless a statute expressly requires exhaustion or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the challenged action inoperative pending it. Courts cannot add their own exhaustion requirement beyond those alternatives; remaining APA finality, reviewability and adequate-remedy conditions still apply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Otherwise final action remains subject to reviewability and adequate-remedy requirements; express statutory exhaustion and lawful mandatory-and-inoperative agency appeals survive. No universal common-law exhaustion rule is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand without requiring HUD’s optional appeal; resolve remaining review questions, without automatic restoration of a district-court merits result or exoneration from debarment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- 5 U.S.C.§704 — statutory requirement OR mandatory-and-inoperative agency rule; alternatives and conjunction preserved.
- McKart, 395 U.S.185 — exhaustion policy subordinate to enacted conditions.
- McCarthy, March 4, 1992 — express statutory primacy retained; different damages-only setting.
- McNeil, May 17, 1993 — express FTCA exhaustion distinguished.

### Filed tariffs and cancellation

#### [Security Services, Inc. v. Kmart Corp., 511 U.S. 431](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Security_Services_v_Kmart_merits_1994-05-16.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-284.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### Published cancellation of essential mileage participation defeats these undercharges

**Holding:** A carrier whose filed monetary tariff incorporates another filed tariff for essential mileage information cannot recover undercharges for shipments after its participation has been effectively canceled through the required public tariff filing, absent an effective replacement filing: the asserted rate then lacks a legally operative essential component under 49 CFR §§1312.4(d), 1312.27(e), and 1312.30. Private revocation or nonpayment alone is insufficient—§1312.10(a) leaves existing rates applicable until lawfully revised—and this rule neither erases charges for earlier shipments nor creates a negotiated-rate or bankruptcy exception to an otherwise effective filed rate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy; seven Justices adopt the rule and affirmance. Thomas and Ginsburg separately dissent from the statutory-authority and remedy conclusions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990): preserved for applicable filed rates; distinguished because the asserted rate lacks an effective incorporated component.
- ICC v. American Trucking Assns., Inc., 467 U.S. 354 (1984): distinguished; its limits on rejection of effective tariffs do not displace this prospective operation of preexisting filing and participation rules.
- Berwind-White Coal Mining Co. v. Chicago & Erie R.R., 235 U.S. 371 (1914): distinguished; technical defects in an otherwise effective tariff differ from the legally missing mileage component.
- Davis v. Portland Seed Co., 264 U.S. 403 (1924): distinguished; its remedy for an effective tariff's violation does not create a complete rate here.
- Kansas City Southern Ry. v. Carl, 227 U.S. 639 (1913): applied; constructive notice includes the relevant tariff filings and cancellation, not only selected rate pages.
- Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993): preserved for statutory counterclaims, recoupment, and appropriate agency referral; it supplies no holding that this asserted tariff was effective.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Physical ascertainability of the monetary and mileage numbers does not replace legally effective incorporation. No general tariff-nullification power, negotiated-rate exception or special bankruptcy exception is adopted; contract carriage, rate reasonableness and unpresented statutory relief remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit and Kmart's summary judgment are affirmed, 7–2. No tariff replacement, undercharge award, calculation, agency referral or bankruptcy distribution is ordered. Unpresented defenses and Negotiated Rates Act relief are not adjudicated; waived or unpreserved theories are not revived.

### Freedom of information and employee privacy

#### [Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Department_of_Defense_v_FLRA_merits_1994-02-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1223.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration

**Holding:** Section 7114(b)(4)'s duty to supply qualifying bargaining data operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law; when the asserted exception to the Privacy Act is §552a(b)(2), the question is whether FOIA itself requires disclosure. Retained Reporters Committee precedent governs the ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry, so collective-bargaining utility alone does not enlarge the incorporated FOIA balance; the Court does not decide that this precedent's restriction was inevitably compelled by FOIA's original text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labor duty concerns requested data normally maintained by the agency in its regular course of business, reasonably available and necessary for full and proper discussion, understanding and negotiation of subjects within collective bargaining, and not guidance, advice, counsel or training provided for management officials or supervisors relating to collective bargaining. These conditions are not independently disputed or newly adjudicated here. For a record contained in a system of records, §552a(b) bars agency disclosure absent a written request by, or prior written consent of, the individual to whom it pertains, or an applicable enumerated exception. A union request is not the subject individual's request or consent. Routine use under §552a(b)(3) and other exceptions are distinct and undecided; their applicability is not guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: retained and applied as statutory precedent for ordinary third-party public-interest analysis and requester neutrality; its original-text justification is not newly endorsed as inevitable, and requester identity can still matter when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground and the requester is the person protected by that privilege, a situation absent here.
- Department of the Treasury, IRS v. FLRA: applied for respecting enacted limits on labor-agency authority; the uncontested bargaining-necessity determination is not reconsidered.
- Department of State v. Ray: preserved; the incorporated Exemption 6 inquiry remains a concrete balance, not a rule that another statute's useful purpose automatically establishes FOIA-required disclosure.

##### The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record

**Holding:** On these requests, official disclosure of employee home addresses would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy under Exemption 6 because the real interest in controlling dissemination of home locations outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated addition to public understanding after names and work stations were supplied. Exemption 6 permits withholding and FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the resulting prohibition against compelling release through the invoked §552a(b)(2) route, without deciding a different demonstrated transparency use, routine use, subject consent or another exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exemption 6 applies to personnel, medical and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy; the requested employee-address records fall within that category. Exemption 7(C) instead concerns information compiled for law-enforcement purposes and disclosures that could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; that different standard is not used here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of State v. Ray: applied for concrete privacy injury and demonstrated incremental public understanding; its confidentiality assurance is not a prerequisite or automatic exemption, and no general bar to derivative benefits or prior-wrongdoing requirement is added.
- Department of the Air Force v. Rose: applied; disclosure remains the general rule, with the enacted Exemption 6 privacy balance preserved.
- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: applied to the relevant public interest and continued control over officially disseminated personal information despite scattered public availability; its rap-sheet sensitivity and Exemption 7(C) threshold do not replace the different record or Exemption 6 standard here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Enforcement of both the Gulfport and Lowry disclosure orders is reversed on the asserted §552a(b)(2) ground. No new necessity determination, constitutional privacy remedy or approval of a routine use is ordered; other disclosure routes must meet their own requirements.

### Resource-recovery ash and statutory exclusions

#### [City of Chicago v. Environmental Defense Fund, 511 U.S. 328](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/City_of_Chicago_v_Environmental_Defense_Fund_merits_1994-05-02.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1639.

**Decided:** 1994-05-02.

For the other questions in City of Chicago v. Environmental Defense Fund, 1994-05-02, No. 92-1639, see Environmental Law — The facility exclusion does not exempt generated hazardous ash.

##### Agency reconsideration does not authorize an interpretation beyond the statute

**Holding:** Under Chevron, an agency may choose a permissible construction within a genuine statutory ambiguity, including through a lawful change of view, but cannot extend an exclusion beyond Congress’s resolved statutory limit. **Proposition-level authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion: Scalia, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stevens and O’Connor disagree about the statutory range, not the existence of Congress’s limiting authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applied; Congress’s resolved limit precedes the permissible-agency-construction inquiry.
- Pauley v. BethEnergy Mines, Inc., 501 U.S. 680 (1991): preserved for lawful agency choices in technical statutory context, not read to eliminate statutory boundaries.
- John Hancock: applied consistently with its reservation of general deference questions and rejection of a categorical exemption beyond the enacted terms.

The pre-effective-date validity of the household-only regulation and household waste outside resource-recovery facilities remain open. Neither agency reconsideration nor use of a memorandum categorically defeats deference.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit judgment is affirmed, 7–2. Exemption-based summary judgment remains unavailable; coverage and any relief require their own predicates. No finding that all ash is hazardous, universal permit duty, disposal technology, injunction, penalty or cleanup allocation is imposed.

### Statutory review channels

#### [Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Thunder_Basin_Coal_Co_v_Reich_merits_1994-01-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-896.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applied to discern congressional review allocation from the statutory scheme.
- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): distinguished because that scheme preserved supplemental judicial review; the presumption favoring review does not require every forum.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): distinguished because its collateral systemic challenge lacked meaningful review through individual proceedings; that protection remains.
- Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Inc., December 3, 1991: its scheme-specific channel rule supports the analysis, without extending its express bar to the Mine Act by assumption.
- Darby v. Cisneros, June 21, 1993: preserved; limits on judicially added APA exhaustion do not displace Congress's allocation of original review.

##### The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908): distinguished on practical access; its protection against a penalty structure effectively foreclosing judicial review remains intact.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): its requirement of meaningful practical review is preserved and satisfied on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed; the district-court injunction remains unavailable. The representation/posting challenge must use the authorized Commission and appellate process. No labor-law merits determination, penalty assessment, guaranteed stay or new administrative outcome is ordered.

#### [Dalton v. Specter, 511 U.S. 462](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Dalton_v_Specter_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-289.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The approved closure selection cannot be reopened through this implementation challenge

**Holding:** The 1990 Base Closure Act precludes the present APA challenge seeking to stop implementation of an approved indivisible closure selection because of alleged antecedent selection-process defects. Its combined package-approval structure, strict deadlines, temporary Commission, mandatory implementation, congressional review and expressly confined implementation review overcome the presumption of review for this relief; they do not erase every earlier procedural duty or decide distinct constitutional or unauthorized-action claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. The statutory-preclusion ground alone commands the Court; a four-Justice concurrence on recommendation finality is not controlling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians, 476 U.S. 667 (1986): applies the strong review presumption, overcome only for this remedy by the combined statutory design.
- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applies structural evidence of preclusion.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992: preserves review of the completed census determination and its reservation of presidential APA status; prescribed transmission does not automatically insulate subordinate action.
- Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, January 19, 1994: uses scheme-specific channel analysis, distinguishing its meaningful later-review route.
- Lincoln v. Vigil, May 24, 1993: preserves the limits of its particular discretionary allocation; it creates no general military or executive immunity.
- Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer, 343 U.S. 579 (1952): preserves actual constitutional limits without converting every alleged statutory mistake into a constitutional cause of action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presidential APA status, the four-Justice recommendation-finality theory, genuine constitutional claims, distinct timely hearing or publication claims and unauthorized additions remain undecided. Section 2905(c) excludes NEPA from presidential and Commission actions and Defense Department actions except property disposal and relocation after selection of the receiving installation but before functions move. In covered processes, the Secretaries may omit the need for selected closure or realignment, the need to transfer to the selected installation, and alternative military installations; omission is permitted, not compelled. Review of a covered NEPA act or failure must be sought within 60 days of that act or failure, not presidential approval or congressional receipt. No such claim is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Third Circuit and remand for dismissal of the claim seeking to stop implementation because of alleged selection defects. No finding that all procedures were followed, advisory injunction or remedy for reserved claims is entered.

### Administrative burdens of persuasion

#### [Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 512 U.S. 267](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Director_Office_of_Workers_Compensation_Programs_v_Greenwich_Collieries_merits_1994-06-20.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Maher Terminals is included under Rule 12.2, covering lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims; Greenwich concerns black-lung benefits.

##### The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order

**Holding:** When APA §556(d) governs and no applicable statutory exception provides otherwise, the proponent of a rule or order bears the burden of persuasion, not merely the obligation to produce evidence. With the preponderance standard governing these benefits proceedings, a claimant cannot prevail on a necessary element left in genuine evidentiary equipoise after complete weighing and application of operative statutory presumptions and lawful defense burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves its preponderance standard and uses its explanation of the APA framework; its proof-quantum holding is not recast as an earlier decision squarely settling allocation.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393, 404 n.7 (1983): disapproves the production-only construction of APA §7(c), now §556(d), to the extent inconsistent with the persuasion allocation; preserves the Wright Line holding requiring the General Counsel to prove unlawful motivation and permitting an employer same-decision affirmative defense.

##### The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation

**Holding:** Longshore Act §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), requires APA adjudication notwithstanding other provisions of the chapter, and §23(a), 33 U.S.C. §923(a), does not exempt these claims from §556(d)'s persuasion allocation by relaxing evidentiary and technical procedural rules. Section 923(a) separately requires reception of a deceased employee's declarations concerning the injury under investigation and makes them sufficient to establish the injury when corroborated by other evidence; that preserved rule does not establish Santoro's disputed aggravation and hastened death on the claim presented here, and the vacatur and remand for a lawful preponderance determination are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

**Material application:** Section 923(a) frees the deputy commissioner or Board from common-law or statutory evidence rules and technical or formal procedure except as provided by the chapter; it permits, rather than commands, a chosen investigative or hearing method best suited to ascertain the parties' rights. Its deceased-declaration reception and corroborated-sufficiency commands are separate. The §20(a) presumption that a claim comes within the Act operates in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; the ALJ found it rebutted here, and it is no longer evidence supporting ultimate persuasion. The causal questions concern aggravation and hastened death, not an established finding that the workplace incident originally caused the cancer. Incomplete weighing remains a question for administrative reconsideration and ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): applies the preponderance quantum in APA adjudication while retaining the distinction between agency factfinding and appellate review.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393 (1983): retains genuine affirmative-defense burdens; it does not authorize transfer of claimant persuasion merely because a benefits scheme is remedial.

##### The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA

**Holding:** The Black Lung Benefits Act's §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore adjudicatory provisions subject to its statutory and regulatory qualifications; even assuming it authorizes an appropriate regulatory departure from APA §556(d), §718.3(c)'s statement of a congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish that departure. The APA persuasion rule therefore governs the disputed pneumoconiosis and total-disability determinations here, and the Greenwich vacatur and remand are affirmed without deciding the full scope of §932(a)'s regulatory power or adopting an independent ground under §718.403 or Mullins.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Material application:** Section 932(a) applies after December 31, 1973 during periods when the State's compensation law is not on the Secretary's §931(b) list, to coal-mine operators respecting death or total disability due to pneumoconiosis arising from mine employment, or entitlements under §921(c)(5). It excludes Longshore Act §§1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 37, 38, 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 and 51. Incorporation is except as otherwise provided in §932(a) or by the Secretary's regulations, and references to the employer refer to fund trustees as the Secretary considers appropriate consistently with 26 U.S.C. §9501(d). Additional provisions the Secretary prescribes in the Federal Register to provide payment to entitled persons may not be inconsistent with the provisions specifically excluded. The cross-reference to §28(h)(1) of the 1984 Longshore amendments excludes that Act's §7 medical-services amendments from black-lung claims. None of those excluded-provision, fund-substitution or medical-services matters is adjudicated here.

Section 718.3(c) expresses the policy of giving claimants the benefit of all reasonable doubt about total or partial disability or death due to pneumoconiosis; it does not expressly prescribe an ultimate equipoise award. Section 718.403 places proof of an alleged fact on the party alleging it except as provided in the subchapter, but its independent effect is not decided. Ondecko's more than ten years of coal-mine employment bears on whether established pneumoconiosis arose from that employment; it does not supply the separate disease and total-disability findings disputed here. An ALJ must not stop weighing at an apparent tie or rename a tie a slight preponderance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no final entitlement, medical fact, benefit amount or award of relief to either employer. It preserves statutory exceptions and presumptions according to their own triggers and effects, does not settle the full regulatory-exception power under §932(a), and does not decide the independent §718.403/Mullins ground. There is no separate alternative holding and no fractured rationale requiring a Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mullins Coal Co. v. Director, OWCP, 484 U.S. 135 (1987): preserves its requirement of actual weighing under its interim-presumption regulation and its reservation of the APA question; the Court does not adopt the Third Circuit's separate use of Mullins and §718.403 as an independent bar.
- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves the preponderance standard while the incorporated APA allocation determines the consequence of genuine equipoise.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Third Circuit judgments are affirmed: the Greenwich award and Maher lifetime-disability and death-benefit orders remain vacated for administrative reconsideration. The ALJs must complete weighing under the persuasion rule, operative presumptions and lawful defense burdens. No final medical finding, unconditional denial or restoration of benefits, employer entitlement or dollar amount is ordered.

### Medicare educational-cost redistribution

#### [Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, 512 U.S. 504](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Thomas_Jefferson_University_v_Shalala_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-120.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The redistribution provision reasonably limits newly shifted educational costs

**Holding:** Under the governing rule giving an administering agency's interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with that regulation, the Secretary reasonably construes 42 C.F.R. §413.85(c) to bar increased reimbursement resulting from transferring previously medical-college-borne educational costs to the hospital's patient-care account, even when the underlying clinical education is generally eligible. That construction sustains the challenged denial for the reporting year ending June 30, 1985; it does not establish a universal historic-dollar ceiling, make every newly measured expense ineligible, or decide the separate community-support ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. Five participating Justices directly adopt this regulatory construction, its application and its limits.

**Material application:** Section 413.85(a)(1) provides that allowable cost may include the net cost of approved educational activities under paragraph (g), apportioned for Medicare utilization under §413.50. Permission to include those costs is not an unconditional payment command. Paragraph (g) deducts tuition revenue and includes trainee stipends, teacher compensation and other direct and indirect educational-activity costs under Medicare cost-finding principles. The recodified paragraph (a)(2) directs payment for approved residency programs in medicine, osteopathy, dentistry and podiatry to §413.86 for cost reporting periods beginning on or after July 1, 1985. That later-period exception does not itself disallow the reporting year ending June 30, 1985, and the Court fixes no later-period payment or base-year amount.

Section 413.17(a), subject to the separate exception in paragraph (d), includes the cost of services, facilities and supplies furnished by organizations related through common ownership or control at the related organization's cost, capped at the price of comparable items that could be purchased elsewhere. It does not independently make an otherwise excluded educational cost allowable. No party's entitlement under paragraph (d) is decided, and its separate exception is not a premise of this holding. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)'s reasonable-cost system takes direct and indirect costs into account within authorized methods and included-item rules, excludes unnecessary costs in efficient delivery, and bars shifting Medicare costs to non-Medicare patients or the reverse. It does not guarantee payment of every asserted actual expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co., 325 U.S. 410 (1945): applies controlling weight to a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation; the plainly-erroneous-or-inconsistent limit remains operative.
- Udall v. Tallman, 380 U.S. 1 (1965): applies the same own-regulation standard; an alternative reasonable reading does not by itself displace the Secretary's permissible construction.
- Gardebring v. Jenkins, 485 U.S. 415 (1988): preserves the limit where an alternative reading is compelled by the regulation's plain language or other indications of the Secretary's intent at promulgation; no such compulsion is established here.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144 (1991): applies its account of reasonable own-regulation interpretation and administrative expertise; its allocation of interpretive authority under the Occupational Safety and Health Act remains specific to that statutory structure.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves reimbursement within lawful cost methods and limitations, including authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods; actual expenditure alone does not establish entitlement here.

Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: preserves the distinction between delegated authority and its actual exercise; its treatment of a general reasonable-doubt policy does not render every statement of regulatory intent nonoperative or create a Medicare proof burden.

Secretary, Department of Health and Human Services, 996 F.2d 122 (6th Cir. 1993): rejects its contrary construction insofar as it excludes this transfer of otherwise eligible clinical-education costs from the redistribution provision; its separate community-support analysis is not resolved.

##### The actual agency ground survives the asserted inconsistency

**Holding:** The Administrator's expressed redistribution ground and the established history of college-borne costs support this denial; a memorandum's silence about redistribution and an intermediary's prior allowance do not, on this record, establish an authoritative contrary interpretation that defeats it. A considered, otherwise permissible interpretive change is not automatically invalid, but contrary text, genuine inconsistency and the required agency explanation must be examined, and a reviewing court may not substitute a new ground or missing finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this answer to the consistency and actual-ground objections; it supports application of the redistribution holding rather than furnishing an alternative reimbursement bar.

**Material application:** Letter 78-7 requires that faculty-service costs would be allowable if incurred directly, be allocated consistently among recipient components, comply with related-organization and comparable-price limits, and satisfy the applicable patient-care relation, documentation, reasonable-allocation and reasonable-amount requirements. Its identified additional nonfaculty costs are limited to direct and space costs. It does not permit duplicative additions where the hospital maintains its own corresponding library, administrative structure, office or clerical support. Those conditions survive; the Court does not read silence about redistribution as an unconditional promise of all overhead. The record does not explain the failure to claim reimbursement before 1974, and the Court does not turn that silence into a finding of community support or an estoppel rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court affirms on redistribution alone. It neither endorses nor rejects the separate construction treating all non-Medicare funding as community support; decides no merits of the Administrator's additional university general-and-administrative-cost ruling under §§413.9, 413.85 and Letter 78-7; and orders no reimbursement, penalty, recoupment, reopening of the settled faculty-compensation amounts, or relief on the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim. The original $2,861,247 disputed difference is not adjudicated as an amount remaining after settlement. Materially different retrospective applications, later §413.86 calculations and entitlement under §413.17(d) remain unresolved. No independently sufficient alternative holding is adopted, and no fractured-rationale synthesis is needed.

- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): preserves the rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; this decision rests on the Administrator's stated rationale and decides no new retroactive-rulemaking question.

The separate community-support construction and general-and-administrative-cost ruling are neither independently sustained nor rejected; settled faculty amounts and the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim remain untouched.

### Tariff-filing modification authority

#### [MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/MCI_Telecommunications_Corp_v_AT_and_T_merits_1994-06-17.md)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

### Abortion information and compelled speech

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Independent speech challenge to medical consent information

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not forbid requiring the accurate, material medical facts in section 3205(a)(1) as part of informed consent to this treatment. This is a bounded consent rule, not a holding that paid medical speech is categorically commercial or that any truthful statement can be compelled.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Required content is the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks, not an ideological script. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. Stone concurs in the judgment only and adds no rationale vote. The separately invalidated timing and exclusive-speaker conditions do not acquire approval through this speech holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny the independent facial medical-content speech claim at this bounded scope; materially false, irrelevant or ideological implementation is not approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781 (1988): payment alone does not remove speech protection.
- Danforth: supplies the legitimate informed-consent connection.
- Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705 (1977): distinguished because factual treatment information does not require ideological affirmation.

##### Independent speech challenge to the three ancillary notices

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not facially invalidate the concise truthful notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal liability at the scopes separately sustained. None requires ideological assent or endorsement of a listed agency; the notice of materials remains distinct from chosen receipt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 7 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Availability means a right to review and obtain free materials if chosen, without acceptance, reading or endorsement. Assistance may be available for prenatal, childbirth and neonatal care, without eligibility or payment guarantees. Paternal liability remains despite an offer to pay for abortion and is not guaranteed collection; rape-related omission is permitted without a police report, including spousal rape. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. Clinicians may correct or criticize information. Stone concurs in the judgments only; Blackmun does not reach these independent speech grounds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny facial relief on each of the three independent speech claims at its bounded factual scope. Concrete falsehood, coercive distribution or materially different enforcement remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Riley: forecloses the compensation-based shortcut; each command is examined independently.
- Wooley: protects against compelled ideological assent, absent from the sustained commands.
- Danforth: supplies the informed-choice connection.

##### Independent speech challenge to chosen materials and lawful receipt certification

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not invalidate state publication and genuinely chosen receipt of objective materials, or a certificate confined to receipt of lawful information rather than ideological assent. This principle does not supply a vote sustaining a contested underlying notice, timing or delivery obligation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Receipt of a copy occurs only if the patient chooses to review it; no reading or ideological assent is compelled. A written certificate documents actual lawful receipt, not personal agreement. Stone concurs in judgment only. The independent speech ruling does not add votes to any disputed prerequisite or certification dependency.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny the independent speech challenge to chosen materials and basic receipt certification. False distribution, forced reading or a certificate demanding ideological agreement remains challengeable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wooley: distinguished at voluntary receipt and factual certification scope; ideological assent remains protected.
- Danforth: applied to consent documentation.
- Riley: paid status supplies no categorical exception to speech protection.

### Association evidence at capital sentencing

#### Dawson v. Delaware, 503 U.S. 159 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6704.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### When protected association may aggravate a capital sentence

**Holding:** The State may not use protected association to aggravate a capital sentence when the evidence establishes only abstract beliefs and supplies no supported connection to the crime, future danger, another legitimate sentencing issue, or the mitigation actually offered. The stipulated association evidence here lacks that connection and its admission violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of White’s opinion; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter join White at this level of generality.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal bar excludes association evidence tied to witness bias, demonstrated racial motive, future danger, another legitimate sentencing issue, or actual mitigation. A white victim does not categorically exclude racial motive in another case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the death sentence is vacated and remanded, 8–1. Delaware may consider its harmless-error contention consistently with the constitutional relevance ruling; the Court neither finds harmlessness nor adopts a mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- NAACP v. Alabama, 357 U.S. 449 — applied: freedom of association protects unpopular affiliations.
- Bates v. Little Rock, 361 U.S. 516 — applied: protected association cannot itself justify adverse governmental treatment.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 — distinguished on the facts: association established witness bias there; relevant association remains admissible.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: demonstrated racial motivation connected the association to the crime.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — applied: protected beliefs cannot themselves supply an aggravating circumstance.
- Payne v. Tennessee, 501 U.S. 808 — applied with its relevance limit: rebuttal of mitigation does not make unrelated belief probative.
- Schware v. Board of Bar Examiners, 353 U.S. 232 — applied by analogy: an affiliation without a rational connection to the relevant character inquiry is insufficient.

##### Whether harmless-error review is available and satisfied for this association-evidence error

**Holding:** The state judgment sustaining this sentence cannot stand on the constitutional reasoning it adopted. On remand the state court may consider Delaware’s harmless-error contention; this Court decides neither whether harmless-error analysis is available for this violation nor whether the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of White’s opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remand’s reserved scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither availability nor satisfaction of harmless-error review and does not command new sentencing automatically. The convictions remain intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the death sentence is vacated and remanded, 8–1. Delaware may consider its harmless-error contention consistently with the constitutional relevance ruling; the Court neither finds harmlessness nor adopts a mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — preserved: its constitutional harmless-error burden is not relaxed; applicability and satisfaction in this case remain undecided.

### Compulsory bar dues

#### Gibson v. Florida Bar, 502 U.S. 104 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1102.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### When a continuing general objector's disputed compulsory bar dues must be protected

**Holding:** Once a compulsory-bar member makes a continuing objection to financing nongermane ideological activity, the bar may not require repeated objections to each announced position or leave the reasonably disputed share available for use until each new objection arrives. The bar must provide adequate advance explanation and protect the disputed share before use through a reasonable reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow; it retains the burden to justify chargeability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight of nine participants.

**Material application:** Annual collection preceded twice-monthly lobbying announcements, and protection began only after position-specific objections. Budget estimates and protection of a reasonably sufficient amount can address uncertainty without exact advance prediction; repeated 45-day waivers cannot shift the monitoring burden back to a known dissenter.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compulsory support for activities germane to professional regulation and improvement of legal services remains permissible. No universal opt-in requirement is adopted. Accounting details and injunction terms are left for remand; an unpreserved retroactive-refund request is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The timing and repeated-objection components are reversed and remanded for prospective reduction or escrow protection. The correction requiring interest from dues collection remains intact; no retroactive damages are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Keller applies compelled-dues safeguards to integrated bars. Hudson requires explanation, prompt review, and protection of disputed funds. Ellis rejects later repayment with interest as a cure for temporary ideological use. Abood permits requiring an objection, but after opposition is known places the chargeability burden on the organization.

##### Whether Bar membership alone makes the prescribed balanced arbitration panel constitutionally biased

**Holding:** Membership in the compulsory bar alone does not establish constitutional bias in every arbitrator selected through a procedure giving the bar and objector one selection each and requiring a mutually selected or judicially appointed chair. Actual bias, a direct stake, or obstructive delay remains subject to review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion joined on this proposition by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices.

**Material application:** Neither party controls the chair, and judicial appointment is available if agreement fails. The shared indirect incidence of bar dues does not itself establish a direct stake in a particular award.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling does not approve an actually biased panel, indefinite delay in constituting it, or a procedure ineffective in operation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The rejection of the facial panel-membership challenge is affirmed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hudson's demand for reasonably prompt impartial review is applied; Bar membership alone does not defeat that requirement under this selection arrangement.

### Content-based financial burdens

#### Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, 502 U.S. 105 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1059.  
**Decided:** December 10, 1991.

##### Whether New York's crime-related-expression escrow survives scrutiny of a content-based financial burden

**Holding:** A law selecting crime-related expression for a special financial escrow is content based and must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest. Section 632-a fails because it burdens an entire covered contract, including incidental discussion and unprosecuted admissions, while leaving other assets outside its special reach; it therefore may not be enforced against this publishing contract.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter; six of eight participants. Blackmun's and Kennedy's judgment-only concurrences are not counted as joins in this rationale.

**Material application:** The payment restraint depends on the work's content and affects access to protected expression without requiring a purpose to suppress ideas. Excluding victimless crimes and releasing limited production or legal expenses do not cure the statute's breadth.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides this statute, leaving materially different laws open. The decision does not protect stolen property, discharge victim or creditor claims, or immunize publishing income from neutral restitution, forfeiture, attachment, judgments, and collection rules.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit is reversed, and the case returns for appropriate declaratory and injunctive relief against applying §632-a to the contract. No payment to Hill is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arkansas Writers' Project supplies scrutiny of content-based differential financial burdens. Leathers distinguishes general financial regulation from selective burdens keyed to expression. Caplin & Drysdale is distinguished because criminal proceeds differ from lawful compensation for discussing crime. Gibson's compelled-bar-financing rule does not replace this scrutiny.

### Demonstration permits and fees

#### Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, 505 U.S. 123 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-538.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the County's demonstration-fee mechanism is facially invalid for unbounded discretion or audience-hostility pricing

**Holding:** A licensing rule directed at protected demonstrations is facially invalid when neither its text, authoritative construction, nor established administration supplies narrow, objective, definite standards controlling whether and how much officials charge. A challenger need not prove discriminatory administration of its own application, and a maximum fee does not supply the missing standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela separately concurs in the judgment on compatible grounds and does not formally join that opinion.

**Material application:** The administrator may select whether to charge, which time and support costs to include and whether to discount them without a binding method or explanation requirement. A $1,000 cap does not constrain those choices, and the finding that the particular $100 assessment reflected administrative work does not cure the facial defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Different fees alone do not prove invalidity. Authoritative narrowing construction and established administration may supply constraints; presumed benign discretion does not. Concrete indigency, fixed neutral fees, particular content-independent costs and municipal funding duties remain undecided; separate City and School Board claims are outside this review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm facial relief against enforcement of the County fee mechanism. The County may enact lawful neutral coordination and sufficiently bounded cost rules. No damages, refund amount, fee schedule or mandatory municipal spending formula is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained and distinguished; its authoritative nondiscrimination construction supports flexible lawful fees, not unbounded discretion.
- Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147 (1969): applied; expressive licensing requires narrow, objective, definite standards.
- Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268 (1951): applied; officials licensing protected expression must act under definite standards.
- City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co., 486 U.S. 750 (1988): applied; the risk inherent in unbounded expressive licensing supports facial review despite one unobjectionable application.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied as to authoritative constructions and actual administration; a binding narrowing construction matters, a presumed benign discretion does not.

**Independent alternative holding:** A permit fee that varies with anticipated hostile reaction to a demonstration is content based and violates the First Amendment when justified by recovering the costs of protecting speakers from that reaction. Neither calling those expenses a secondary effect nor imposing a small numerical ceiling makes the charge constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: an independently sufficient five-Justice holding. Stone-Zsela's separate concurrence agrees with the rule without a formal join.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Audience-hostility pricing independently sustains the same facial protection against enforcement. It does not find the actual $100 assessment retaliatory, fix police expenditures or forbid genuinely content-independent administrative charges. The following precedent treatment applies to this alternative ground:

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied; the justification must be independent of the message.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988): applied at its supported content-classification level; listeners' hostile responses are not neutral secondary effects.
- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): distinguished and retained; coordination costs do not include a message-dependent hostility premium.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991 simulated decision: consistent application of the prohibition on unjustified content-based financial burdens; its separate remedial limits are unchanged.
- Terminiello v. Chicago, 337 U.S. 1 (1949): applied in its protection against suppressing speech because of hostile response; no new police-protection entitlement is inferred.

##### Whether the First Amendment imposes a universal nominal-fee ceiling or makes a small fee necessarily valid

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not make a demonstration fee invalid merely because it exceeds a nominal amount, or valid merely because it is small. Cox's allowance for lawful, nondiscriminatory regulatory cost recovery survives; a fee's lawful purpose, content neutrality, and bounded administration govern the defects presented here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, independently controls both rejections of numerical shortcuts. White's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, and Stone's separate concurrence also reject a universal nominal ceiling; no judgment-only position is converted into a full-opinion join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permissible dollar amount or comprehensive schedule of recoverable costs is fixed. A fee exceeding a nominal amount must still have a lawful purpose, neutral criteria and bounded administration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court rejects the court of appeals' amount-only reasoning while affirming the facial relief on the independently sufficient discretion and hostility grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained; flexible, nondiscriminatory regulatory fees are not limited to nominal amounts.
- Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943): distinguished; its nominal-fee reference does not establish an amount-only rule or displace Cox.

### Electioneering on public sidewalks

#### Burson v. Freeman, 504 U.S. 191 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1056.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### What scrutiny governs the campaign-specific restriction on ordinary streets and sidewalks within the polling-place boundary

**Holding:** Tennessee's campaign-specific prohibition is a content-based restriction on political speech, and ordinary public sidewalks and streets within the 100-foot boundary remain public forums. The State must show that this restriction is necessary to serve a compelling interest and narrowly drawn to achieve that end; viewpoint neutrality and the availability of speech beyond the boundary do not by themselves lower that scrutiny.

**Proposition-level authority:** Seven participating Justices expressly adopt this governing proposition at the same level of generality: Blackmun's plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, and Stevens's dissent, joined by O'Connor and Souter. It is an independently majority-supported proposition, not a seven-Justice judgment coalition or an Opinion of the Court. Scalia rejects the forum classification and applies a different standard.

**Material application:** Viewpoint neutrality and opportunities to speak beyond the boundary do not make campaign-specific content discrimination neutral. The Justices applying strict scrutiny disagree about necessity and tailoring in this facial challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The majority-supported scrutiny rule supplies no shared validity rationale or numerical safe harbor. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated political speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment invalidating the 100-foot restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3, by Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy. The governing scrutiny proposition has a different, seven-Justice coalition; Thomas takes no part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Ass'n v. Perry Local Educators' Ass'n, 460 U.S. 37 (1983) — applies its traditional-public-forum and content-based scrutiny framework to the ordinary streets and sidewalks reached here.
- United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171 (1983) — applies its protection of ordinary public sidewalks; government-building proximity does not alone remove that status.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988) — applies strict scrutiny to political subject discrimination; a claim of regulatory purpose does not automatically make this campaign restriction content neutral.
- Mills v. Alabama, 384 U.S. 214 (1966) — preserves its protection of general election-day advocacy and its reservation concerning conduct in and around polls; it does not automatically settle this boundary's validity.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, simulated decision — preserves exacting review of content-based burdens; this case's divided application creates no general exception to that rule.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989) — distinguishes its content-neutral time, place and manner standard; it does not replace strict scrutiny here.

##### Whether this 100-foot restriction survives the facial challenge

**Authority of the judgment:** The judgment invalidating the restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3: Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy support reversal; Stevens, O’Connor and Souter oppose it. Thomas takes no part. No controlling validity rationale exists under *Marks*: Blackmun’s plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, treats the place as a public forum and finds strict scrutiny satisfied; Scalia treats the polling environs during voting as a nonpublic forum subject to reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality. The different forum predicates are not established logical subsets, and Scalia does not adopt the plurality’s necessity or tailoring conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds this facial challenge to the 100-foot boundary during voting. Neither the four-Justice application nor Scalia’s nonpublic-forum theory becomes a controlling exception. The separately majority-supported scrutiny rule appears above. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

### Official school prayer

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

### Substantial overbreadth and fighting words

#### R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul, 505 U.S. 377 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-7675.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### Whether the authoritative state construction cures the ordinance's substantial protected coverage

**Holding:** A criminal prohibition is facially invalid when, even under its authoritative state-law construction, it reaches substantial protected expression relative to its legitimate sweep and cannot be cured by permissible limiting construction or partial invalidation. St. Paul's injury-and-resentment coverage extends beyond personally abusive provocation of an immediate breach of the peace and is therefore substantially overbroad.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this rule and application. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support the same judgment on a distinct content-discrimination ground. Those paths are not combined into a broader nine-Justice rule, and no Marks synthesis enlarges the actual majority holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated; the decision creates no general rule governing distinctions wholly within unprotected speech categories.

**Material application:** Religious criticism and hostile debate about race or gender can cause resentment without personally provoking immediate violence. The authoritative state construction binds on state law, but its repeated injury branch still reaches protected speech; calling it fighting words, or adding an incitement branch, does not cure that coverage. The serious cross-burning allegation does not validate the ordinance's ordinary unconstitutional applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The broader content-discrimination question within genuinely unprotected categories, vagueness, guilt on count one, threat mental state, sentencing enhancements and antidiscrimination laws not before the Court remain undecided. Independently punishable conduct is not immunized. The separate writings' proposed content-selection rule and exceptions remain noncontrolling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Minnesota Supreme Court and remand for dismissal of count two under the facially invalid ordinance. Count one under the separate racially motivated assault provision remains unadjudicated; independently valid charges remain available. The remand creates no new offense through construction or severance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chaplinsky v. New Hampshire, 315 U.S. 568, retains its narrow fighting-words scope rather than a general emotional-injury category. Cohen v. California, 403 U.S. 15, distinguishes offensive ideas from personally directed fighting words. Gooding v. Wilson, 405 U.S. 518, and Lewis v. City of New Orleans, 415 U.S. 130, require an authoritative construction actually to remove protected coverage. Broadrick v. Oklahoma, 413 U.S. 601, supplies real and substantial overbreadth and prior consideration of permissible narrowing. Brandenburg v. Ohio, 395 U.S. 444, retains advocacy directed and likely to produce imminent lawless action as the incitement boundary; that branch does not cure the separate injury coverage. New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S. 747, and Brockett v. Spokane Arcades, Inc., 472 U.S. 491, supply narrowing and partial-invalidation principles without authorizing judicial rewriting here. Simon & Schuster, December 10, 1991, Forsyth County, June 19, 1992, and Burson, May 26, 1992, retain their respective protected-speech, licensing and fractured electioneering holdings without resolving the reserved within-category question.

### Commercial speech and selective newsrack restrictions

#### Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1200.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### The selected commercial category lacks the required fit

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading commercial publications concerning lawful activity and newspapers cause the same asserted physical harms through their newsracks, the City must justify why its selected commercial category reasonably fits its safety and aesthetic interests. Cincinnati has not done so: removing respondents' sixty-two racks while retaining approximately 1,500–2,000 physically similar newspaper racks violates the First Amendment; a small reduction is not invalid merely for being small, and no least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven, throughout.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S.557 (1980): applies the existing commercial-speech test to the accepted classification.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S.469 (1989): applies reasonable proportional fit and government burden; preserves rejection of least restrictive means.
- Metromedia, Inc. v. San Diego, 453 U.S.490 (1981): distinguishes the billboard treatment; does not permit an unrelated content distinction without the required fit.

##### The selective prohibition is not content neutral

**Holding:** A newsrack prohibition determined by whether its publication is a commercial handbill is content based when the asserted safety and aesthetic harms do not justify that distinction without reference to the publication's content. Cincinnati cannot sustain this selective ban as a content-neutral time, place and manner restriction merely because its objective is benign; otherwise lawful neutral regulation of newsracks remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S.781 (1989): applies justification without reference to content; a benign objective alone does not validate the selective line.
- Clark v. Community for Creative Non-Violence, 468 U.S.288 (1984): preserves valid content-neutral time, place and manner regulation.
- Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc., 475 U.S.41 (1986): distinguishes different secondary effects; no such difference justifies this rack classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Central Hudson and Fox remain governing. Incremental regulation remains possible; alternatives inform fit but do not impose least restrictive means. No strict-scrutiny rule for all truthful commercial speech, all-sources-at-once duty or commercial/noncommercial equality rule is adopted. Other lawful neutral rack controls remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit and the injunction against the selective commercial-handbill newsrack prohibition.

### Access to preliminary criminal hearings

#### El Vocero de Puerto Rico v. Puerto Rico, 508 U.S. 147 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-949.
**Decided:** May 17, 1993.

##### Trial-like preliminary hearings require qualified public access

**Holding:** The First Amendment’s qualified right of access applies to these adversarial preliminary hearings; the history inquiry concerns this type of proceeding, not only Puerto Rico’s own practice. Closure requires specific findings supporting an overriding interest and a narrowly tailored order after reasonable alternatives are considered; where fair-trial prejudice is invoked, there must be a substantial probability of prejudice that closure would prevent and no adequate reasonable alternative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richmond Newspapers, Inc. v. Virginia, 448 U.S.555: access’s public oversight function applied.
- Globe Newspaper Co. v. Superior Court, 457 U.S.596: categorical closure rejected in favor of a particularized justification.

### Religious viewpoints in a school forum

#### Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, 508 U.S. 384 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2024.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### A religious perspective cannot be excluded from an admitted subject

**Holding:** When a public school makes an after-hours forum available for private discussion of family life and child-rearing, it may not reject that otherwise eligible discussion solely because the viewpoint is religious. This prohibition applies even assuming a nonpublic forum; it does not require an unlimited public forum or invalidate neutral restrictions on subjects and activities outside the actual forum.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s speech portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

- Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund, 473 U.S.788: permissible forum limits distinguished from forbidden viewpoint exclusion.
- Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S.263: religious perspective does not by itself remove otherwise eligible private speech from equal-access protection.

##### Equal private access does not establish religion here

**Holding:** Allowing this voluntary, publicly accessible, after-hours film presentation without school sponsorship does not violate the Establishment Clause. Equal access serves a secular forum purpose and neither primarily advances religion as governmental activity nor creates excessive entanglement; this holding authorizes access to private speech, not an official devotional program.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s establishment portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate Sunday-worship application and the facial validity of every religious-use restriction are outside review. No unlimited public forum, school sponsorship, compulsory worship or public devotional financing is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for nondiscriminatory treatment and declaratory or injunctive relief as necessary; genuinely neutral scheduling, safety and subject limits remain available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Widmar, 454 U.S.263: neutral access and private attribution applied.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S.602: purpose, primary effect and entanglement applied, not replaced.
- Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992: the official-worship and pressure holdings distinguished and preserved.

### Religiously selective animal-control laws

#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fowler v. Rhode Island, 345 U.S.67: discrimination in the treatment of religious exercise prohibited.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S.872: targeting falls outside the neutral-law rule; that rule is not reconsidered here.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S.618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S.693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

##### Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. Each challenged ordinance fails that minimum requirement here, including 87-72’s express state-law exemption for small-number hog or cattle operations; the Court does not define every boundary of general applicability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Smith, 494 U.S.872: general-applicability requirement applied, without reopening its neutral-law holding.
- Fowler, 345 U.S.67: unequal treatment of religious exercise applied.
- Hobbie v. Unemployment Appeals Commission, 480 U.S.136: protection against unequal treatment of religious observers preserved.

##### Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance

**Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. These ordinances fail narrow tailoring independently of whether the asserted ends could be compelling because comparable harms are left unregulated and neutral, narrower controls on disposal, care, killing methods and location could address the city’s concerns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S.205: rigorous justification of restrictions on religious exercise applied.
- McDaniel, 435 U.S.618: demanding scrutiny of religious disabilities applied.
- Arkansas Writers’ Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S.221: lack of narrow tailoring independently suffices under strict scrutiny.
- Schneider v. State, 308 U.S.147: collateral public harms cannot justify unnecessary suppression when direct regulation is available.

##### The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S.524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S.205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

### Bias-motivated crime and expression evidence

#### Wisconsin v. Mitchell, 508 U.S. 476 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-515.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Federal constitutional effect is not settled by a state court’s characterization

**Holding:** The Court accepts the state court’s authoritative construction of statutory terms, but independently determines the federal constitutional effect of the law so construed. Wisconsin’s characterization of the intentional-selection enhancement as punishment of thought therefore does not resolve the First Amendment question or remove the statutory requirement of an independently committed crime.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Parts I and II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S.747: authoritative state construction accepted.
- Terminiello v. Chicago, 337 U.S.1: binding construction of operative state-law terms distinguished from the federal characterization at issue here.

##### Intentional racial selection can aggravate an independently criminal battery

**Holding:** The First Amendment permits an enhanced penalty for an independently criminal battery when the defendant intentionally selects the victim because of race under the applicable statutory predicate. The State may prove a relevant motive through lawful evidence, but may not punish abstract racist belief, expression or association without its legally relevant connection to the offense or another legitimate issue.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Parts I and II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dawson v. Delaware, March 9, 1992: connection-to-legitimate-issue limit applied; abstract belief remains protected.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S.939: racial motivation connected to the criminal act distinguished from abstract association evidence.
- R.A.V. v. City of St. Paul, June 22, 1992: substantial-overbreadth holding distinguished; noncontrolling content-selection reasoning is not imported.
- Hishon v. King & Spalding, 467 U.S.69, and Roberts v. United States Jaycees, 468 U.S.609: regulation of discriminatory conduct distinguished from suppression of a protected message.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S.160: protection for belief does not confer a right to engage in unlawful discrimination.

##### Speculative future evidentiary use does not establish substantial overbreadth

**Holding:** The possibility that a person might suppress protected views for fear that those views could later help prove intentional selection in a future crime does not establish substantial overbreadth of this enhancement. The First Amendment permits relevant statements to prove criminal intent, subject to evidentiary safeguards and Dawson’s prohibition on punishment for abstract belief.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Part III; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State-law meanings remain binding while federal constitutional effect is independently determined. Abstract belief cannot be punished, relevance and other evidentiary safeguards remain, and no later jury-allocation rule or blanket admissibility rule is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the First Amendment invalidation of the intentional-selection enhancement and reject the speculative-chill facial challenge; punishment may be enhanced on the proved statutory selection predicate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Haupt v. United States, 330 U.S.631: relevant earlier statements may establish criminal intent subject to careful evidentiary scrutiny.
- Dawson, March 9, 1992: the limit on irrelevant abstract-belief evidence preserved.
- R.A.V., June 22, 1992: direct substantial reach into protected expression distinguished from speculative evidentiary chilling.

##### Meaningful reduction and reasonable fit in a border broadcaster’s application

### Postconviction forfeiture of expressive enterprises

#### Alexander v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1526.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the distinct Excessive Fines question in *Alexander v. United States*, June 28, 1993, No. 91-1526, see Criminal Procedure — Excessive fines and criminal RICO forfeiture.

##### Whether this postconviction RICO forfeiture constitutes a traditional prior restraint

**Holding:** A postconviction RICO forfeiture of identified assets linked to adjudicated racketeering does not constitute a traditional prior restraint merely because it impairs the owner's future ability to publish, where it neither forbids future publication nor requires permission to publish. That classification does not dispose of an independent First Amendment challenge to removal of protected works.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt this proposition. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy do not join the classification. The independent First Amendment question has a different coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The classification neither licenses a future-publication ban nor permits seizure of protected stock on probable cause alone. It does not resolve the independent challenge to removal of lawful works or immunize publishing assets from neutral sanctions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The traditional-prior-restraint claim is rejected, 6–3; that rejection does not sustain the entire forfeiture. Approval of the challenged forfeiture is vacated for the independent First and Eighth Amendment examinations described in the other question blocks. The convictions remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Near v. Minnesota, 283 U.S.697, and Vance v. Universal Amusement Co., 445 U.S.308: distinguished as restraints on future expression; their protection remains in force.
- Fort Wayne Books, Inc. v. Indiana, 489 U.S.46: its pretrial protection is preserved; the present adjudicated RICO case differs, and its reserved postconviction question is not treated as settled.
- Arcara v. Cloud Books, Inc., 478 U.S.697: applied to distinguish an ordinary sanction's incidental consequences from regulation implicating expression; no universal asset exemption follows.

##### Whether valid convictions and the absence of a traditional prior restraint suffice to sustain forfeiture of lawful expressive inventory

**Holding:** Where obscenity predicates produce forfeiture of an expressive enterprise and removal of lawful inventory, the judgment sustaining that forfeiture cannot rest solely on the existence of valid convictions and the absence of a traditional prior restraint. The judgment must be vacated for an independent First Amendment examination of the challenged expressive burden, distinguishing the judicial forfeiture from subsequent administrative destruction and confining correction to a reviewable injury and available relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy; five Justices expressly adopt this limited rule and remand. Their separate positions do not establish a majority rule either categorically immunizing expressive inventory or adopting a comprehensive scrutiny framework for every postconviction forfeiture.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves the ultimate constitutional treatment of the distinct assets unresolved. Neither categorical immunity for expressive inventory nor a comprehensive scrutiny framework commands a majority. Judicial forfeiture, subsequent administrative destruction, statutory nexus, causation, reviewable injury and available relief remain distinct. The Court neither adopts a content-selection rationale from R.A.V. nor enlarges its entered overbreadth rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment insofar as it sustains the challenged forfeiture without independent First Amendment examination, and remand. The lower court must address statutory nexus and reviewable injury attributable to the judgment, distinguishing subsequent administrative destruction. Relief remains confined to what this appeal can supply: no return of destroyed stock, new damages cause of action or mandatory business restoration is ordered. The convictions remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arcara, 478 U.S.697: its nonexpressive-conduct rationale does not dispense with examination of this expression-triggered sanction; ordinary neutral sanctions remain enforceable.
- Fort Wayne Books, 489 U.S.46: the reservation concerning postconviction forfeiture is respected; its pretrial safeguard remains unchanged.
- Marcus v. Search Warrant, 367 U.S.717, and A Quantity of Copies of Books v. Kansas, 378 U.S.205: their protection against indiscriminate removal of expressive works supplies the need for distinct examination, without mechanically converting their pretrial remedies into this mandate.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, simulated decision: content-selective financial burdens remain subject to its rule; lawful publishing does not immunize assets from neutral forfeiture.
- Wisconsin v. Mitchell, June 11, 1993: its allowance for punishment of independently criminal conduct and relevant motive is preserved; the battery enhancement does not settle removal of protected inventory.

### Public-employee speech and factual error

#### [Waters v. Churchill, 511 U.S. 661](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Waters_v_Churchill_merits_1994-05-31.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1450.

**Decided:** 1994-05-31.

##### A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor

**Authority of the judgment:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated and the case remanded, 7–2; defendants' summary judgment remains set aside. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support vacatur. No complete controlling rationale exists under Marks; the express majority propositions below have only their stated scope.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** A public employer does not violate the employee's substantive speech right by disciplining her because of a genuinely held, objectively reasonable account of speech that the Pickering–Connick balance permits it to discipline, even if that account is later shown mistaken. The protection requires actual reliance on the permissible ground; it does not protect a reasonable investigation used to conceal retaliation for different protected speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968): applies the public-employee speech balance, adding this limited factual-error permission.
- Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138 (1983): applies contextual public-concern analysis and reasonable prospective assessment of institutional disruption; personal embarrassment alone is insufficient.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): preserves private speech protection and the relevance of manner, time and place.
- Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): preserves actual causation; a potentially permissible ground is not proof of the ground actually used.

##### An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Discipline because of an unreasonable and incorrect report of speech that was actually protected violates the substantive speech right when the protected speech bears the required causal relation to the action, subject to distinct causation defenses and remedial immunity. This agreement does not establish liability for every investigative imperfection, for an unreasonable report of actually unprotected speech, or without proof of the underlying protected conduct and causal action.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering, Connick and Givhan: preserve the substantive protection of the actual public-concern speech within the employment balance.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): distinguishes a property-based hearing entitlement from this substantive speech claim.
- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): retains the distinct causation and same-decision requirements; unreasonableness alone is not an automatic recovery rule.

##### The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated, but defendants' summary judgment remains set aside because genuine reliance and retaliation for other protected criticism are materially disputed. On remand the employee must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred without it, and individual qualified immunity remains available and, if necessary, must be assessed as a distinct defense under the law relevant to the 1987 conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Givhan, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): apply the employee's causal showing and the employer's preponderance same-decision defense.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982): preserves individual qualified immunity as distinct from the substantive speech rule.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: requires considering relevant authority on the preserved immunity issue, without expanding the claim or supplying notice from later law.
- Rankin v. McPherson, June 24, 1987, and Anderson v. Creighton, June 25, 1987: may inform later doctrine but cannot themselves establish notice for the January and February 1987 actions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete factual-investigation theory commands five votes. The seven-Justice reasonable-and-genuine safe harbor and six-Justice unreasonable-false-actually-protected proposition are express agreements, not a unified Marks framework; the latter includes two judgment dissenters and is not selected as a judgment-supporting narrowest rationale. No universal freestanding investigation duty or rule for unreasonable investigation of actually unprotected speech is established. Personal immunity remains tied to law giving notice at the 1987 conduct, not the 1994 decision itself.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, without restoring defendants' summary judgment. Determine genuine reliance, actual motivation and the protection of causally relevant other speech under ordinary summary-judgment and Mt. Healthy rules. The completed investigation's conditional objective adequacy and the reported account's conditional permissibility are not reopened as wholly unresolved issues. Personal immunity is considered if necessary; no damages, reinstatement, municipal-liability finding or revived association claim is ordered.

### Cable carriage and editorial expression

#### [Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Turner_Broadcasting_System_Inc_v_FCC_merits_1994-06-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-44.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Cable editorial expression receives ordinary First Amendment protection

**Holding:** Cable operators' editorial selection and cable programmers' expression are protected speech, and compulsory carriage burdens those interests. Cable's physical transmission capacity and market power do not justify importing broadcasting's spectrum-scarcity standard or reducing review of this expressive burden to rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-A and II-B as relevant to this proposition, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens recognizes protected cable expression but does not join these complete portions and is not counted for their formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock, 499 U.S. 439: applies its recognition of cable speech and press; its approval of a broad content-neutral tax does not resolve compulsory carriage.
- City of Los Angeles v. Preferred Communications, Inc., 476 U.S. 488: applies its protection of editorial selection and attention to cable's characteristics; it had left the precise standard open.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC, 395 U.S. 367: confines its scarcity rationale to the broadcasting setting; it supplies no diminished cable standard here.

##### Content classification depends on what the law selects and why

**Holding:** A law burdening protected expression because of its content ordinarily must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest; a burden justified without reference to content is subject to the appropriate intermediate review. A benign purpose or absence of hostility does not neutralize a content-based operation, while a distinction among speakers or media does not by itself establish content discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens does not join that portion; his agreement with the case-specific classification below is distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991: applies its actual content-based escrow holding; neutral collection remedies remain outside that holding.
- Arkansas Writers' Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: applies its scrutiny of differential burdens dependent on a publication's content.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content as the classification inquiry.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: applies its rejection of benign-purpose neutrality for an unexplained content distinction; its commercial-speech fit holding remains bounded.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its distinction between permissible neutral differential treatment and censorial or content-based burdens.

##### The general full-power carriage requirements are content neutral

**Holding:** The general commercial and noncommercial full-power must-carry obligations regulate cable's distribution bottleneck and preservation of independently available broadcast service without selecting the stations' individual messages; intermediate scrutiny therefore governs those obligations. This classification does not decide the distinct low-power local-programming criteria, the market-adjustment provision, or a materially different content-based administration, and creates no general right to compel private editorial publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-C and II-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this application.

**Material application:** The classification addresses the general full-power core of §§4–5, whose commercial and noncommercial eligibility, capacity, duplication, signal-quality and channel-position provisions remain distinct. It does not adjudicate the separately reserved low-power programming criteria or market adjustments, authorize an editorial-balance mandate for another medium, or confer both must-carry and retransmission-consent entitlement at once.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241: distinguishes compelled reply and interference with newspaper editorial judgment; its protection is not displaced.
- Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. Public Utilities Commission, 475 U.S. 1: distinguishes its fractured compelled-access reasoning concerning speech-responsive access and attribution; no unanimous categorical rule is attributed to it.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its allowance for justified neutral distinctions between media; the cable-specific burden still requires constitutional scrutiny.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC: supplies no basis for the classification; broadcast scarcity is not extended.

##### Intermediate scrutiny requires advancement and a proportionate speech burden

**Holding:** A qualifying content-neutral carriage requirement must further an important or substantial governmental interest unrelated to suppressing expression and must not burden substantially more speech than necessary to further that interest. The Government need not establish that the law is the least restrictive conceivable alternative, but the actual expressive burden and relevant alternatives bear on whether its fit is sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. This common standard does not establish common agreement about whether the existing record satisfies it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367: applies the important-interest, nonsuppression and incidental-burden requirements to this content-neutral carriage burden.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism: applies its requirement against substantially excessive burdens while preserving its rejection of least-restrictive-means review.

##### Preserving broadcast availability, diverse sources and fair competition are substantial interests

**Holding:** Preserving free local broadcast service, maintaining diverse sources of information and promoting fair competition in television distribution are important content-neutral interests supporting consideration of the general full-power carriage scheme under intermediate scrutiny. Their importance does not itself prove threatened harm, advancement, adequate fit or the scheme's final constitutionality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. The separate opinions disputing the neutrality of the Act's localism and programming preferences do not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock: applies the distinction between a medium's economic characteristics and selection of protected ideas.
- United States v. O'Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism: apply the requirement of a substantial nonsuppressive interest without treating that element as proof of advancement or tailoring.

##### Predictive judgments receive substantial deference and remain subject to review

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** In reviewing Congress's predictive judgments relevant to the carriage scheme, a court must give substantial deference while independently assuring that Congress drew reasonable inferences from substantial evidence. Congress need not compile the formal evidentiary record required of a court or agency; this general principle neither decides whether the evidence here suffices nor joins the four-Justice application requiring additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, supported by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Souter, together with Stevens's express agreement in his concurrence at this limited level of generality: five Justices. Stevens does not join Part III-B, its real-harm formulation as a complete passage, or its record-insufficiency application. This is direct agreement on the stated proposition across writings, not a Marks inference or a fifth join in the plurality portion.

**Current force:** Controlling at this express five-Justice level of agreement only; no majority adopts Part III-B's present-record insufficiency application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landmark Communications, Inc. v. Virginia, 435 U.S. 829: applies the judiciary's continuing responsibility to review an expressive burden despite legislative findings.
- Sable Communications of California, Inc. v. FCC, 492 U.S. 115: applies the refusal to make congressional findings conclusive; its strict-scrutiny setting does not impose that standard here.

##### Authority of the remand on evidentiary sufficiency

**Authority of the judgment:** The summary judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter. No controlling rationale exists under Marks for the present-record insufficiency conclusion: only Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter adopt it; Stevens agrees in remand without adopting it. The separate grounds do not establish a logical subset. The six controlling propositions above retain only their separately stated support and scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final constitutionality of the general scheme remains open. The district court must consider the separate low-power criteria in the first instance and may address the market-adjustment provision; neither is independently upheld or judicially severed. The initial home-shopping transition clause does not establish current FCC treatment. The remand orders no replacement quota, damages, blanket injunction against the Act or particular interim relief. Any interim relief requires its own properly presented legal basis. The legal standards and the judgment govern; the four-Justice explanation of why further proof is needed remains identified as such.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The summary judgment sustaining the challenged carriage scheme is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, under the controlling intermediate framework. Final constitutionality remains open. The district court must consider low-power eligibility in the first instance and may consider market adjustments; any interim relief needs its own properly presented basis. No new channel quota, damages or blanket injunction against the Act is ordered.

### Professional credentials and disclosures

#### [Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, Board of Accountancy, 512 U.S. 136](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ibanez_v_Florida_Board_of_Accountancy_merits_1994-06-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-639.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### An active CPA credential is not false because its holder disputed regulation

**Holding:** The Board may not reprimand Ibanez for truthfully identifying her active Florida CPA credential on these law-practice communications on the unsupported theory that the title falsely implies submission to regulation. Her earlier legal challenge to the Board's authority does not falsify the credential or prove misleading advertising; the CPA ground for the reprimand must be set aside, with otherwise lawful professional regulation preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the CPA ground and its limited remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies protection for truthful, nonmisleading commercial information and preserves regulation of actual deception.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: applies the distinction between factual professional information and misleading advertising; no immunity from appropriately justified regulation follows.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies the requirement of justification in the relevant professional setting, without imposing an invariable empirical-study requirement.

##### The private CFP credential is not inherently deceptive on this record

**Holding:** The Board may not sustain this reprimand merely because Ibanez's authorized private CFP credential, displayed beside CPA without naming the private certifier, could be mistaken for government recognition. On this record the Board has not established actual or inherent deception or a constitutionally adequate justification for suppressing that truthful certification information; the CFP prohibition ground must be set aside, without validating every private credential or every form of its advertisement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices directly adopt this as-applied holding; O'Connor dissents from it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the distinction between protected factual information and actually or inherently misleading speech.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: preserves regulation of deception without treating possible confusion as automatically justifying suppression.
- Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91: applies its protection against the certification ban within the distinct plurality and concurrence rationales; no unanimous rule against certification regulation is attributed to it.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies its real-harm and material-advancement inquiry to the actual asserted professional deception.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: preserves reasonable fit between the selected restriction and its asserted harm without requiring regulation of every source at once.
- United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co., June 25, 1993: preserves supported incremental regulation and reasonable fit; it supplies no presumption that this credential is misleading.

##### The actual immediate disclosure demand does not justify this reprimand

**Holding:** Paragraph (j)'s demand for an immediately adjacent disclaimer that the certifier is neither affiliated with nor sanctioned by state or federal government, together with the certifier's recognition requirements including but not limited to education, experience and testing, cannot sustain this reprimand without adequate justification for that actual burden. The Board has not justified it on this record; the holding preserves supported, proportionate factual clarification and neither prescribes a replacement disclaimer nor creates an exception to paragraph (i)'s separate prohibition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices reject this disclosure justification as applied; O'Connor would independently sustain it if the advertisement is merely potentially misleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Paragraph (g) addresses stated or implied recognition as a public-accountancy specialist unless the recognizing agency is Board-approved; paragraph (i) independently prohibits recognition by another entity using certified; paragraph (j) separately imposes the actual disclosure just analyzed. Compliance with (j) is not an exception to (i). The Court resolves the sanction on this record, not all applications of those provisions, the withdrawn unlicensed-firm charge, future advertising, licensing jurisdiction, or the validity of a differently justified shorter disclosure. An expired credential, fictitious certifier or express false claim of state endorsement presents a different case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, 471 U.S. 626: applies the distinction between reasonably related factual clarification and unjustified or unduly burdensome disclosure.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: preserves the possibility of adequately justified clarification of potentially misleading professional information.
- Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91: preserves the separate concurrence's limited-disclosure rationale without converting its permission into a duty to supply exhaustive standards.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies record-sensitive justification without requiring empirical studies in every professional-regulation case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The CPA basis for the reprimand is reversed unanimously, and the CFP basis and actual disclosure justification are rejected by eight Justices. The case is remanded to set aside the reprimand on both challenged grounds. Licensing jurisdiction remains intact; no new disclaimer, damages, credential revocation or finding on the withdrawn unlicensed-firm charge is ordered.

### Religious neutrality in public school government

#### [Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Board_of_Education_of_Kiryas_Joel_Village_School_District_v_Grumet_merits_1994-06-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-517, 93-527 and 93-539.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### The State may not select a denomination as the recipient of general governmental authority

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause forbids a State deliberately to allocate general civil authority to a community selected as a religious community, even when the immediate recipients are elected public lay officials rather than clergy. Chapter 748 violates that rule because its design and context use denominational membership as the organizing basis of the special school district; religious homogeneity or coincidence with an existing village line alone would not establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II introduction and II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg: five Justices. This is an independently sufficient ground. O'Connor does not join that portion, and Kennedy's separate judgment rationale is not counted as a join.

**Material application:** Chapter 748 grants all powers of a union-free school district through a board of five to nine members elected by village voters for terms not exceeding five years. It contains no special-education-only limitation, sunset, religious voter or office qualification, or rabbinical appointment. The secular public program and professional staffing do not narrow that general statutory grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Larkin v. Grendel's Den, Inc., 459 U.S. 116: applies its civil-authority boundary by structural analogy to deliberate denominational allocation through a lay public board; it does not treat this board as a church or clergy veto.
- Larson v. Valente, 456 U.S. 228: applies denominational neutrality to the State's choice of recipients of general public authority; no general disability on religious communities follows.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: preserves equal political citizenship and distinguishes religious officeholders from governmental allocation by denomination.

**Independent alternative holding:** Chapter 748 independently violates religious neutrality by conferring an anomalously particular benefit of general school-government power on this selected denominational community without neutral terms or dependable assurance of equal treatment for comparable communities. The defect follows from this enactment's design and context, not a rule that every special act is invalid, every general statute is neutral, or an unproved finding that another religious community has already been refused.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This is an independently sufficient alternative ground; Kennedy expressly declines it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Larson v. Valente: applies the prohibition on denominational preference to the particular governmental benefit and its allocation.
- Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos, 483 U.S. 327: distinguishes its general nonprofit religious-employer accommodation from a special denominational franchise; its permission to accommodate remains effective.

##### Accommodation preserves educational access but does not cure this allocation

**Holding:** A State may accommodate religiously grounded needs beyond what free exercise independently requires, but accommodation does not authorize this denominational allocation of general governmental power. Affirmance leaves the children's statutory special-education rights and lawful neutral local public services intact, without commanding a particular placement, validating the adequacy of earlier services or prescribing a replacement district.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II-C and the implementing limits in Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This holding rejects the accommodation defense and defines the remedy's limits; Kennedy reaches the judgment on his separate ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos: preserves permissible accommodation beyond free-exercise compulsion, with its denominational-neutrality and application limits.
- Zorach v. Clauson, 343 U.S. 306: preserves lawful accommodation of private religious needs without authorizing this governmental franchise.
- Wolman v. Walter, 433 U.S. 229: preserves the possibility of properly administered neutral-site services; no particular replacement is approved.
- Board of Education v. Wieder, 72 N.Y.2d 174, 527 N.E.2d 767: respects the state court's recognition of local educational discretion without turning permission into a compulsory separate school.
- Aguilar v. Felton, 473 U.S. 402, and School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball, 473 U.S. 373: neither is overruled, modified or used to order restoration of the terminated annex program.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602: remains governing law; this bounded neutrality decision neither replaces its framework nor imposes a new universal analytical sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling rationale is synthesized under Marks from Kennedy's different religious-boundary theory or other separate writings. The Court does not replace Lemon, overrule Aguilar or Ball, create an aid-merits holding in Zobrest, revise Shaw, decide the state constitutional issue, or find religious voter qualifications, religious instruction, mandatory assimilation, a denied request by another faith, permanent religious demographics or a free-exercise entitlement to a separate district. It neither selects individual placements nor requires elimination of culturally responsive secular instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal invalidation of Chapter 748 is affirmed, 7–2, in all three dockets. Implementation must preserve statutory educational duties and lawful neutral services. The Court prescribes no replacement district, individual placement, transfer date, damages or repayment, and does not invalidate the village, close private schools, restore the discontinued annex program or resolve the reserved state constitutional question.

### Speech injunctions and remedial scope

#### [Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Madsen_v_Womens_Health_Center_Inc_merits_1994-06-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-880.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

For the other questions in Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 1994-06-30, No. 93-880, see Federal Courts — The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review; Federal Courts — An injunction binds responsible parties and actual concert, not a viewpoint; Federal Courts — The independent conduct-language claims remain undecided; Federal Courts — Relief removes invalidated predicates; Federal Courts — Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone.

##### The decree as confined is not categorically content based

**Holding:** As authoritatively confined to named responsible parties and actual concert with notice, this injunction addresses established interference with access, traffic, medical care and residential privacy rather than abortion advocacy as such; defendant-specific selection does not make it categorically content or viewpoint based. Each restraint still requires independent constitutional scrutiny, and a materially different viewpoint-based application receives no approval.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A(2), joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this application while accepting the separate actual-concert principle.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content; defendant-specific relief still requires scrutiny.
- *Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC*, June 27, 1994: applies its operation-and-justification distinction without importing its cable-specific result or plurality evidentiary application.
- *Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, Inc.*, March 24, 1993: preserves the rule that a benign objective alone does not cure a speech-selective burden.

##### A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary

**Holding:** A content-neutral injunction burdening protected expression must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve the corresponding significant governmental interest. Its precise terms require a closer fit than ordinary review of generally applicable neutral time, place and manner rules; neither an injunction label nor past misconduct permits suppression of unrelated lawful advocacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: distinguishes generally applicable neutral regulation from this direct injunction; its ordinary rule remains intact.
- *Carroll v. President and Commissioners of Princess Anne*, 393 U.S. 175: applies its demand for precision in speech-burdening injunctive relief.
- *NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.*: applies the relation between unlawful conduct, responsible persons and permissible relief.
- *Califano v. Yamasaki*, 442 U.S. 682: applies the limitation of equitable relief to what the established violation requires.

##### The qualified public access arrangement survives

**Holding:** The public portion of the 36-foot clinic clearance, with its retained south shoulder, east shoulder subject to the five-foot setback and no-embankment rule, and prompt-crossing exception, burdens no more speech than necessary on this record to secure driveway access and traffic after narrower obstruction commands failed. It remains enforceable at that qualified scope, without creating a generally permissible clinic-buffer distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five on geographic fit. Stevens adds a vote to leave the arrangement intact only against the categorical challenge; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the expressive exclusion.

**Material application:** The southern boundary is the south edge of Dixie Way's pavement, leaving the unpaved south shoulder available subject to lawful restrictions. On the east, the U.S. Highway One shoulder remains usable, but defendants may not ascend the embankment and must remain five feet from the clinic's east line. Pending painted lines, prompt crossing uses an eight-foot strip directly from Dixie Way's south to north edge, with its western line coextensive with Highway One's western right-of-way line; once marked, the crosswalk governs. Within the buffer no other pavement entry is allowed. Crossing permits no stopping, hesitation, picketing, congregation, demonstration or traffic impediment. This is an existing crossing dimension, not a personal-separation order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Carroll*: applies precision to the access boundary and actual crossing and shoulder permissions.
- *Claiborne Hardware*: confines the expressive burden to supported access relief.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*, June 13, 1994: applies attention to usable alternatives without creating an entitlement to every preferred location.

##### The private-property extension lacks the necessary remedial connection

**Holding:** The north/west extension of the 36-foot exclusion cannot suppress otherwise lawful expression on adjoining private land without a demonstrated connection between that full exclusion and the access or operational harm to be remedied. Florida's approval of that extension is reversed; removing the government's expressive restriction neither authorizes trespass nor adjudicates owners' property rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on their stricter grounds; Stevens declines distinct geographic review.

**Material application:** The north/west owners and invitees were excepted from the entry prohibition, while the other expressive restrictions still applied when they acted in concert. Removing the governmental expressive exclusion adjudicates neither permission to enter nor private property boundaries.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies the requirement that relief correspond to the established wrong.
- *Carroll*: applies precision to the unsupported geographic extension.
- *Ladue*: preserves practical channel analysis; it supplies no right to use another person's property.

##### The qualified medical-noise restriction survives

**Holding:** Paragraph (4)'s noise restriction is valid in its medical-operation context from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery periods, as to the listed and other sounds within earshot of patients inside the clinic. Its affirmance rests on the treatment interference established here, not disagreement with a message, and adds no decibel, intent or newly drafted volume threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on strict direct-restraint and adequate-unlawful-predicate grounds, despite accepting fit under the Court's assumed standard; Stevens declines merits.

**Material application:** The covered sounds are singing, chanting, whistling, shouting, yelling, bullhorns, auto horns, amplification equipment and other sounds within the earshot of patients inside the clinic, in the lower court's medical-operation context. Both the 7:30 a.m.–noon Monday–Saturday window and the surgery-or-recovery condition apply; neither is replaced by a newly invented sound threshold.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: applies the distinction between controlling sound's physical effects and suppressing its content; the heightened injunction rule governs this remedy.
- *Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement*, June 19, 1992: preserves the distinction between a neutral harm and burdens resting on hostility to a message.
- *Carroll*: applies precision through the time, treatment and patient-location limits.

##### The prohibition on observable images is too broad

**Holding:** The prohibition on all images observable to patients inside the clinic, even within the stated morning surgery-and-recovery periods, burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be removed. Patient vulnerability or distress at a message does not establish a general power to prevent visible advocacy; independently lawful threat or intrusion relief remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens declines merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville*, 422 U.S. 205: applies protection against broad suppression merely because images are visible to unwilling viewers.
- *Boos v. Barry*, 485 U.S. 312: applies the distinction between communicative emotional effects and neutral physical harms.
- *Carroll*: requires precision beyond a categorical visual prohibition unsupported by the demonstrated need.

##### Peaceful initial approach cannot depend on advance invitation throughout 300 feet

**Holding:** Paragraph (5)'s requirement of an indication of interest before any physical approach to a person seeking clinic services within 300 feet burdens more speech than necessary and must be removed. This holding protects peaceful initial advocacy, not a right to accompany, encircle, surround, threaten, obstruct or force communication upon a person who declines it, and approves no universal application of disputed harassment or abuse terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-E, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens dissents from reversal of this physical-approach condition.

**Material application:** The clause regulates physical approach, not every word or sign within 300 feet. Invited nonthreatening conversation and literature were permitted when a service-seeker approached or inquired, but never inside the 36-foot zone. Protections against accompanying, encircling, surrounding, harassment, threats and physical or verbal abuse of a declining person retain only their independently lawful scope; reversal neither invalidates every conduct protection nor approves every disputed use of those labels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies protection of persuasion and the connection between misconduct and relief.
- *Boos*: preserves political approach while allowing properly grounded protection against threats and coercion.
- *Carroll*: requires the restriction to distinguish protected initiation from the conduct justifying restraint.

##### Residential privacy does not justify this territorial exclusion

**Holding:** The 300-foot residential prohibition on approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstrating and amplification sweeps beyond the supported protection of a targeted home and must be removed. Its advance-invitation qualification does not restore general neighborhood expression; distinct lawful obstruction, trespass, threat and properly focused residential protections are preserved without selecting a replacement distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-F, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six on this bounded ground. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal under their stricter direct-restraint account; the judgment is unanimous.

**Material application:** Paragraph (6)'s separate bans on blocking entrances, exits or driveways and impeding a street providing sole access to the residences' street are distinct from the territorial ban. The separate invited-contact provision allows willing contact outside the clinic buffer, with residential invitation communicated from a distance before contact, not obtained by an uninvited knock or doorbell. Invitation may be revoked, requiring immediate termination and departure. Retained language must conform to reversal and cannot recreate the invalid territorial exclusion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Frisby v. Schultz*, 487 U.S. 474: applies its distinction between focused home picketing and general neighborhood expression; its narrow residential protection remains.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*: applies practical attention to lost channels while preserving the distinction between one's own home and intrusion on another's.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Florida judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded for a conforming decree. The qualified public-access arrangement and medical-noise restriction remain; the private extension, images prohibition, initial-invitation requirement and residential territorial ban must be removed, including dependent use under paragraph (9). The predecessor and independently lawful unreviewed terms are not automatically dissolved. The court may consider precisely fitted lawful conduct relief, but no replacement injunction, personal distance, new sound threshold, damages or contempt judgment is commanded.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medicare corrective adjustments

### Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

**Governing question:** What instructions govern medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change?

**Current rule:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) commands suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when aggregate reimbursement for a provider and period proves inadequate or excessive. The Secretary may construe that command as reconciling interim payments to sums payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and ceilings, without an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed the ceiling. Authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain. Interpretive change can reduce weight without automatically invalidating a considered permissible construction; text, delegation and reasons must be examined. Unsupported litigation positions neither replace required agency explanation nor gain automatic controlling weight.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medicare educational costs

### Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution

**Governing question:** How the Medicare anti-redistribution regulation limits otherwise eligible education costs.

**Current rule:** The Secretary may reasonably apply §413.85(c)’s prohibition on redistribution of costs from educational institutions to hospitals to deny newly shifted costs formerly borne by a related college. Eligibility as an educational activity does not create an unconditional reimbursement right. Section 413.17’s cost-related-organization rule does not override the separate redistribution limitation. The validity of the actual agency ground must be assessed under the regulation and the relevant agency practice; neither an invented historical-cost ceiling nor a different rationale can replace that ground.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited modification authority

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded modification and distinct notice authority: [MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co — The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/MCI_Telecommunications_Corp_v_AT_and_T_merits_1994-06-17.md#public-projection), 1994-06-17. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co, 1994-06-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Limited deferral or waiver is not categorically foreclosed, but no particular waiver is approved. Failure of the special-circumstances-or-conditions predicate is a four-Justice alternative, not a separate controlling holding. No universal major-questions doctrine is created; the statute’s permissible flexibility remains available within the actual delegation.
