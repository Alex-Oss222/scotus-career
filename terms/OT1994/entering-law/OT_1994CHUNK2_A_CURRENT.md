# Church_of_Scientology_Flag_Service_Organization_Inc_v_City_of_Clearwater_merits_1994-12-05

Effective 1994-12-05; authority location ../records/Church_of_Scientology_Flag_Service_Organization_Inc_v_City_of_Clearwater_merits_1994-12-05.md (neutral contexts read only this public copy).

## Holdings

### RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA applies to the threatened future implementation of Clearwater's preexisting ordinance: the Church must establish a substantial burden on its religious exercise, after which Clearwater must produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The adverse prospective determinations concerning the identifying disclosures designated below as section 100.03(1)(a), (b), (c), and (j), and the written-refund-policy requirement in section 100.05(1)(g), must therefore be vacated for that inquiry, with ordinary Article III standing and appropriate-relief limits preserved.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that RFRA governs, but does not join the opinion or its disposition of these adverse determinations.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's protection of religious exercise governs the proposed enforcement. Section 6 expressly covers implementation of laws adopted before RFRA, and the act to be restrained is future enforcement, not the completed enactment of the ordinance. Landgraf supplies the distinction between that prospective operation and new liability for completed conduct. The Court of Appeals decided before RFRA became law; its constitutional disposition cannot substitute for the statutory inquiry. Limited identifying information and disclosure of a voluntarily offered refund policy may serve neutral fraud prevention, but that description does not establish either the absence of a substantial burden or the least restrictive means of addressing a demonstrated burden. The Church must prove the predicate burden; the City must then justify the application to this claimant. Further proceedings must undertake those distinct inquiries. The existing favorable relief and purpose remand remain intact. RFRA leaves the Establishment Clause untouched, so statutory reconsideration neither resolves the disputed purpose nor approves the lower court's constitutional reasoning.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies the distinction between future enforcement and new legal consequences for completed conduct, consistently with RFRA's express temporal direction; neither Landgraf's general framework nor its distinct relevant-activity rationale is enlarged.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993): preserves its objective religious-targeting, substantial secular-underinclusion, and strict-scrutiny holdings; the statutory remand neither supplies a purpose finding nor converts the enactment-history discussion into a controlling test.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687 (1994): preserves the distinct denominational-allocation and unequal particular-grant rules; accommodation does not cure an establishment violation, and RFRA does not decide that constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final substantial-burden or justification finding is made for the surviving identifying or written-policy requirements. The Court does not decide RFRA's constitutionality, retrospective monetary liability, a general exemption from civil fraud law, the disputed sectarian purpose, the precise Florida severability boundary, the unreviewed general anti-fraud clause, or unpreserved hardship-discretion and procedural objections. Section 7 leaves the Establishment Clause unaffected; section 6(c) supplies no authority to burden religious belief.

## Law After Decision

Effective December 5, 1994, RFRA's original burden-and-justification inquiry governs the specified prospective municipal enforcement claims despite the ordinance's earlier enactment. This adds a controlling statutory application and remand rule; it does not replace the constitutional free-exercise or establishment standards. The favorable lower relief and factual purpose/severability remand survive without adoption of the lower constitutional rationales. No final exemption or constitutional invalidity of the surviving provisions is established.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in leaving the Church's favorable relief and the purpose-and-severability remand undisturbed, but dissents from reopening the surviving identifying and written-policy requirements. He would affirm the Court of Appeals' judgment on the grounds stated in his opinion. RFRA governs future municipal enforcement even though the ordinance predates it, and the Church's inability to invoke an unenacted statute below does not forfeit its protection. The Church must establish a substantial burden; the City then bears production and persuasion on a compelling interest and the least restrictive means.

He regards the existing record as sufficient to invalidate the broad internal financial, operational, organizational, and recordkeeping demands under RFRA. The private-statement option still requires annual sworn information and supporting records, and criminal sanctions reinforce the burden. The sixty-day refund requirement, triggered by a voluntary refund promise, a written request, and willful nonpayment, also displaces the Church's religiously connected refund process. Fraud prevention is compelling, but targeted fraud law, limited identification, transaction-specific investigation, and accurate statements of promised refund terms address the shown secular harms more narrowly. He adds no constitutional holding against those provisions because RFRA suffices.

In his view, the limited identifying requirements establish no substantial religious burden on this record and perform ordinary civil identification without theological licensing or interruption of solicitation pending judicial review. The contemporaneous written-policy requirement, including its good-faith proviso, survives even assuming a substantial burden: concrete evidence of misleading refund representations supports requiring accurate disclosure of the policy voluntarily chosen, without dictating that policy or deciding spiritual entitlement. He also rejects the independent First Amendment challenges to those bounded surviving requirements, subject to purpose and severability.

He would leave the disputed sectarian-purpose issue for the district court because evidence of official hostility or denominational targeting precludes summary judgment for the City without establishing the final factual answer. Florida severability follows that determination. Materially different future applications require their own RFRA assessment. His position supplies no immunity from ordinary fraud, contract, restitution, tax, reporting, or criminal law, no bar to a targeted subpoena or audit grounded in a concrete secular enforcement need and lawful process, and no authority to decide religious truth. Private donations do not become public financing through truthful-solicitation regulation. These application and constitutional positions are his alone; the Court does not adopt them.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit judgment is vacated only as to its rejection of prospective relief against the identifying disclosures designated as section 100.03(1)(a), (b), (c), and (j), and section 100.05(1)(g). The matter returns to that court for RFRA adjudication and any district-court findings required. Existing Church-favorable relief and the purpose/severability remand remain undisturbed. Ordinary review here ends; proceedings continue below. No damages, refund payment, whole-ordinance injunction, new calendar deadline, or restored enforcement of invalidated provisions is ordered.

# United_States_v_X_Citement_Video_Inc_merits_1994-11-29

Effective 1994-11-29; authority location ../records/United_States_v_X_Citement_Video_Inc_merits_1994-11-29.md (neutral contexts read only this public copy).

## Holdings

### The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character

**Controlling proposition:** To convict under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1) or (2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knew the performer was a minor, as well as the applicable knowing handling act. It need not prove knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition; the actual statutory production, depiction and commerce-or-mail conditions remain distinct, and the Court does not decide whether knowledge of the jurisdictional nexus is required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven Justices directly adopt the factual-knowledge construction and its reasoning. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that knowingly reaches the factual features that make these depictions criminal. Morissette supplies the presumption of culpability, applied to the whole statutory setting rather than confined by the distance between the adverb and the later conditions. Staples supports requiring awareness of the characteristics that distinguish a prohibited object from an otherwise familiar one, while Posters preserves the difference between factual knowledge and legal classification. Neither decision mechanically resolves this statute. Ratzlaf’s distinct willfulness language required knowledge of unlawfulness there; no such general requirement follows here. Reading §2252 to punish a distributor who lacks awareness of the material’s incriminating character would also raise the speech concerns identified in Smith and Ferber. The fair contextual reading avoids that unnecessary constitutional ruling without rewriting a federal default of recklessness from Osborne’s Ohio statute. The reported finding that respondents knew the performer was underage remains relevant, but does not resolve every count, the indictment objection or whether these tapes contain the conduct the statute defines.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Morissette v. United States*, 342 U.S. 246 (1952), applies the presumption of culpability to the statutory setting rather than treating grammatical distance as conclusive.
- *Staples v. United States*, 511 U.S. 600 (1994), applies its distinction between knowledge of facts making an object regulated and knowledge of legal duties; its weapon-specific holding does not automatically construe §2252.
- *Posters ’N’ Things, Ltd. v. United States*, 511 U.S. 513 (1994), preserves actual factual knowledge as distinct from legal classification and from a desire to cause the forbidden use; its different coverage dispute remains unchanged.
- *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), is distinguished as a text-specific willfulness requirement concerning unlawful structuring; it creates no general requirement to know that conduct is illegal.
- *Smith v. California*, 361 U.S. 147 (1959), and *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), inform the constitutional concern about penalizing dissemination without adequate awareness of incriminating content; the Court does not decide an unavoidable constitutional requirement of actual knowledge of minority in every enactment.
- *Osborne v. Ohio*, 495 U.S. 103 (1990), remains distinct: its Ohio default-culpability statute supplied recklessness and supplies no federal default here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a statutory construction of §§2252(a)(1)–(2), not a universal constitutional actual-knowledge minimum, a construction of every other offense or a decision concerning §2251 producers. No new digital-image category, knowledge-of-illegality element, knowledge-of-nexus rule or automatic trial-error remedy is supplied.

### The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Respondents may defend the existing appellate reversal on the renewed age-eighteen and lascivious-wording grounds that appear in the record, although the court of appeals rejected those grounds, because they seek no enlargement of their rights or reduction of the Government’s rights under that judgment. The Court reaches those two alternatives; the separate actual-or-simulated bestiality and sadistic-or-masochistic-abuse objection is not established as renewed and is not decided.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this reach ruling. Stone-Zsela joins only the dispositions on the two alternatives and adds no reach ground. Scalia and Thomas accept the legal availability of such defenses but do not decide either alternative because their independent ground would sustain the appellate judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that respondents may offer these two grounds to preserve the judgment they already obtained. American Railway Express distinguishes defending a judgment from asking the Court to enlarge it or overturn a separate adverse judgment. Respondents seek the same appellate reversal, not greater relief, and the two renewed objections were considered in the record below. Izumi therefore does not require treating each rejected reason as a separate adverse judgment that respondents must overturn. The limited question rules preserved in Caspari and Madsen remain intact; briefing alone still does not expand a request for different relief. Availability is not compulsion: Dandridge leaves room for a proper remand where the issues warrant one. Here the Court resolves the two renewed facial grounds on their stated limited terms while reserving the unresolved indictment and tape-content claims. The other definition challenge is not established as renewed, so its treatment below does not become a new holding of this Court.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *United States v. American Railway Express Co.*, 265 U.S. 425 (1924), applies the prevailing party’s ability to defend the existing judgment on a ground appearing in the record, even one rejected below, without seeking greater relief.
- *Dandridge v. Williams*, 397 U.S. 471 (1970), preserves a lawful remand option rather than imposing a duty to decide every available alternative.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains applicable to an attempt to overturn a distinct adverse judgment; defending this already favorable judgment does not undo its presented-question rule.
- *Caspari v. Bohlen*, 510 U.S. 383 (1994), and *Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc.*, 512 U.S. 753 (1994), retain their limited subsidiary-question and exceptional-review boundaries; the Court grants no general enlargement of review based on briefing alone.

### The asserted age-eighteen line does not establish substantial facial overbreadth

**Controlling proposition:** The renewed objection that §2256 defines a minor as a person under eighteen, rather than under sixteen, does not establish substantial First Amendment overbreadth in §§2252(a)(1)–(2) as construed here. Difficulty estimating age near eighteen, together with decisions permitting adult treatment of some older adolescents in other settings, does not require facial invalidation of this protective category.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part III and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the asserted difficulty of distinguishing older adolescents from adults does not establish the substantial overbreadth needed to invalidate this statute on its face. Ferber supplies that demanding facial standard and recognizes the governmental interest in preventing the sexual exploitation of children. Its particular statute protected children under sixteen, but the decision did not constitutionalize that age as an upper boundary. Permission to treat some sixteen- or seventeen-year-olds as adults in other settings likewise does not require adult treatment for sexual exploitation. The concern that distributors may avoid protected adult expression deserves consideration; the Court does not equate it with proof of a substantial unconstitutional sweep. The required actual knowledge of minority further narrows that concern, although the age-line ruling does not rest on scienter alone. No empirical finding about the amount of chilled material is made. The Court rejects this facial ground without deciding every application or the disputed content of respondents’ tapes.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), applies the requirement of substantial overbreadth for facial invalidation and its protection of children from sexual exploitation. Its approval of a statute protecting children under sixteen does not establish sixteen as the highest permissible protective age.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general constitutional age-of-majority rule is announced, and no as-applied challenge is foreclosed merely by this facial disposition.

### The complete lascivious-exhibition category defeats the word-substitution facial attack

**Controlling proposition:** Replacing lewd with lascivious in §2256’s category of lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area does not itself make §§2252(a)(1)–(2) facially vague or substantially overbroad. The complete category retains a sexual-conduct limit and does not cover nudity merely because a person’s body is visible; whether these particular tapes satisfy the definition remains open.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part IV and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged word must be read with the conduct it describes. The provision concerns lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area, not nudity as such. Ferber’s treatment of lewd exhibition supplies a meaningful comparison, and the ordinary terminology considered in Miller does not become constitutionally indeterminate merely through the substitution of lascivious. Wiegand persuasively recognizes the words’ equivalent meaning in this context. Respondents therefore have not shown that the substitution itself creates the uncertainty or substantial unconstitutional breadth they assert. This conclusion keeps content classification distinct from culpability: knowledge does not automatically make every nude depiction covered, and the Court does not hold that scienter cures every vague definition. The inquiry must still concern the actual statutory conduct. The Court adopts no new checklist of factors and makes no final classification of the tapes here. Their reserved contention that the tapes are not child pornography remains for appellate consideration.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), applies the meaningful limit supplied by lewd exhibition of the genitals and the substantial-overbreadth standard; it does not equate ordinary nudity with the prohibited sexual conduct.
- *Miller v. California*, 413 U.S. 15 (1973), supplies the ordinary lewd-exhibition terminology considered in this comparison; the Court does not add Miller’s complete obscenity test as a requirement for the child-pornography category.
- *United States v. Wiegand*, 812 F.2d 1239 (9th Cir. 1987), provides persuasive contextual support for the equivalence of lascivious and lewd exhibition. It supplies no new mandatory multifactor definition of the federal category.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The other objection concerning actual or simulated bestiality and sadistic or masochistic abuse is not reached. No unofficial-code punctuation is adopted and no definition of every possible application is supplied.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 29, 1994, §§2252(a)(1)–(2) require the stated actual factual knowledge of sexual character and minority, without a general knowledge-of-illegality requirement. The Court rejects the two renewed facial-definition grounds on the limited six-Justice rationales stated above. The prior offense-specific holdings of Ratzlaf, Staples and Posters remain intact. The Ninth Circuit’s facial-scienter invalidation is set aside; no acquittal, count-specific affirmance, automatic harmlessness result or mandatory retrial follows. The unresolved indictment and actual-tape claims remain open, as does the independently unrenewed definition objection.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissents. He reads the placement and structure of knowingly as applying to the handling acts, without extending it to the later conditions identifying the depicted conduct. A presumption of criminal fault helps interpret an otherwise unresolved enactment, but cannot overcome what these Justices regard as a clear allocation of the knowledge term. Their agreement with Staples’s contextual presumption does not require a different answer: the problem here is the enacted placement of an express adverb, not simply a statute’s silence about fault. Constitutional avoidance must operate through a permissible construction, not a judicial replacement of that allocation.

Under their reading, the law reaches a person who handles a depiction without awareness of its pornographic character. That content-related defect, in light of Smith and Ferber, supports preserving the appellate reversal. Their conclusion does not depend on making actual knowledge of the performer’s age an unavoidable constitutional requirement. A different enactment could treat performer age differently where the actor knows the pornographic character; the dissent does not decide that different statute. Osborne’s recklessness came from Ohio’s own law and supplies no judicially created federal default.

The dissent accepts the reported finding that respondents knew the performer was underage. It neither asserts factual innocence nor orders retrial because of an imagined missing finding. The finding does not cure the facial absence of adequate content-related fault in the statute as these Justices read it. Although the two renewed definition objections are legally available defenses of the favorable judgment, the dissent reaches neither because its existing ground is sufficient. It also leaves the indictment and actual-tape claims undecided. These positions are noncontrolling; the Court’s statutory construction and bounded remand govern.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must apply the statutory construction, account for the existing minority-knowledge finding, and address the reserved indictment-scienter and actual-tape claims and properly presented implementation issues within its lawful review. The two renewed facial-definition grounds are rejected on the limited grounds stated. No acquittal, count-specific final affirmance, harmlessness conclusion or mandatory retrial is ordered.

# Brown_v_Gardner_merits_1994-12-12

Effective 1994-12-12; authority location ../records/Brown_v_Gardner_merits_1994-12-12.md (neutral contexts read only this public copy).

## Holdings

### Section 1151 requires causation, not provider fault or accident

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1151 requires an injury or aggravation resulting from covered VA hospitalization, medical or surgical treatment, chapter 31 vocational rehabilitation, or examination, producing additional disability or death and not resulting from the veteran's own willful misconduct; it does not additionally require negligence, other provider fault, or an unforeseen untoward accident. Section 3.358(c)(3)'s added fault-or-accident prerequisite is invalid, and Gardner's claim must be redetermined under the surviving statutory conditions without an automatic benefits award.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct rationale votes on the causal rule and invalidity of the additional prerequisite. Stone-Zsela also concurs separately on the rule's limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that causal connection and fault are different requirements. Section 1151 speaks in terms of injury or aggravation resulting from the listed services and expressly excludes the veteran's own willful misconduct. Congress did not impose the regulation's additional demand for deficient care or an unforeseen accident. King supplies contextual interpretation that respects enacted limits, with protective construction as confirmation. Chevron requires the agency first to respect Congress's answer, and MCI confirms that administrative expertise cannot enlarge a statutory delegation. The Secretary's reliance on duration of the regulation, costs, hearing examples, reenactment, and legislative silence does not overcome that text. Removing fault does not make every later disability compensable: actual causation and the other statutory conditions remain. The existing natural-progress provision is undisturbed, and a necessary consequence of consented treatment is not automatically compensable. The Court neither finds that surgery caused Gardner's claimed disability nor resolves a comprehensive consent or necessary-consequences doctrine. The existing administrative redetermination must proceed under the lawful standard.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the statutory-meaning inquiry before deference; the clear causal standard leaves no authority to add provider fault, without abolishing deference in genuine interpretive gaps.
- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): applies contextual reading of protective veterans legislation and respect for express rather than invented limits; the veterans-protective canon confirms the text instead of replacing it.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218 (1994): applies the limit that administrative expertise cannot supply statutory power Congress did not grant; the distinct tariff-modification rule is not extended into a general rejection of agency discretion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The natural-progress provision is left undisturbed. No complete rule governing consent or necessary consequences is adopted. The separate FTCA coordination sentence remains: for a qualifying judgment under 28 U.S.C. §1346(b), or settlement or compromise under §§2672 or 2677, on or after December 1, 1962, for a disability, aggravation, or death treated as service connected under §1151, benefits for months after finality are withheld until the aggregate otherwise payable equals the total recovery. That is a coordination rule, not a provider-fault requirement; no qualifying recovery is found here.

## Law After Decision

Effective December 12, 1994, the VA may not impose provider fault or accident as an additional §1151 eligibility condition. Actual causation and the other enacted conditions remain; the natural-progress provision is undisturbed. The Court adds this statutory rule without changing Chevron generally, deciding a comprehensive consent doctrine, or awarding Gardner benefits. The separate FTCA coordination remains independent of fault.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the causal holding and affirmance and adds a concurrence preserving its boundary. He independently reads section 1151 to omit negligence, fault, and accident as eligibility requirements; the veteran-protective interpretive rule confirms the enacted coverage. The agency may exclude natural progression of the underlying disease and consequences certain or intended from proper treatment. Removing the fault prerequisite does not make the VA an insurer of the underlying disease or of consequences not caused by treatment. His further statement about certain or intended consequences is a separate position, not a comprehensive rule adopted by the Court or a medical finding about Gardner.

## Procedure After Action

The Federal Circuit judgment is affirmed. The existing remand for administrative redetermination of Gardner's eligibility continues without the invalid fault-or-accident prerequisite. No automatic compensation, medical causal finding, refund, deadline, or broader regulatory rewrite is ordered. Ordinary Supreme Court review ends; the administrative claim remains for decision under the lawful conditions.
