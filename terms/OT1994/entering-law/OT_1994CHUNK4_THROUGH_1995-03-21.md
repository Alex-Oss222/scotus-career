### Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Newport News Shipbuilding & Dry Dock Co., No. 93-1783. — 1995-03-21

Authority: [Record](../records/Director_Office_of_Workers_Compensation_Programs_v_Newport_News_Shipbuilding_and_Dry_Dock_Co_merits_1995-03-21.md#public-projection).

#### Holdings

### The Director's general administrative interest does not confer independent review

**Holding and operative rule:** The Director's general interest in correct administration of the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act does not by itself make the Director adversely affected or aggrieved under 33 U.S.C. §921(c) by this employee's unappealed benefits award. The refusal to entertain that challenge is affirmed; no rule of categorical governmental incapacity is adopted, and the separate Special Fund disposition remains undisturbed.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Justice Ginsburg concurs in the judgment and agrees with the case-specific statutory result, but does not join this explanation. No fractured-opinion synthesis is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that administrative responsibility does not itself satisfy the condition Congress placed on judicial review. Section 921(c) requires a person adversely affected or aggrieved; §921(b)(3)'s administrative party-in-interest route and §939's claimant-assistance duties do not transfer Harcum's appellate entitlement to the Director. Germain requires respect for the particular statutory review route, and Gardner requires fidelity to enacted conditions rather than an administrator's preferred policy. The Director identifies no impairment of a specific entrusted function here. Harcum did not appeal, and disagreement with his award does not establish an independent adverse interest. Possible employer insolvency presents speculative Fund exposure that the requested benefits change would not reduce. Perini's treatment of respondent participation does not decide who may initiate review. Greenwich Collieries governs persuasion in covered adjudications, not this appellate entitlement. Uniform administration is important, but accepting that interest alone would erase the statutory condition. The Court reserves express governmental review authority, genuine financial or independently statutory injury, and the Director's respondent role; it decides neither compensation nor Fund liability.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: applies respect for the requirements of the particular statutory route; its bankruptcy appellate holding remains unchanged.
- Brown v. Gardner, December 12, 1994: applies fidelity to enacted conditions rather than administrative additions; its benefits-causation holding is not expanded.
- Director, OWCP v. Perini North River Associates, 459 U.S. 297: distinguishes respondent participation from a Director-initiated appeal; the respondent issue remains reserved.
- Director, OWCP v. Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: preserves its persuasion-burden rules and treats them as distinct from statutory appellate entitlement.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992: leaves its Article III holdings unchanged; the statutory disposition makes a new constitutional rule unnecessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

The Court applies Germain's respect for statutory review conditions and Gardner's fidelity to enacted limits. Perini does not resolve a right to initiate appellate review. Greenwich Collieries remains the rule for persuasion in covered adjudication, and Lujan's Article III holdings remain unchanged. No precedent is overruled, and no general restriction on Congress's capacity to authorize governmental litigation is announced.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 21, 1995, a general interest in correct administration does not alone satisfy the Director's §921(c) aggrievement requirement for this claimant's unappealed award. This adds the bounded statutory application; it does not decide all governmental aggrievement, respondent status, disability merits, or Fund liability. Congress's ability to confer an express review right and claims based on genuine financial or independently statutory injury remain open.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Ginsburg concurs in the judgment. She agrees that the existing review provision does not authorize this independent challenge on the general administrative interest asserted. She does not infer that Congress deliberately intended every disparity produced by that provision. Congress may supply governmental review authority; statutory agreement here does not require endorsement of a broader account of legislative purpose. She does not decide Harcum's benefits, the Special Fund merits, or the Director's respondent role.

#### Procedure After Action

The reviewed employee-award challenge ends without review of compensation merits. Harcum's unappealed award is undisturbed by this decision, as is the Fourth Circuit's separate Special Fund reversal and remand. No mandate revises §908(f) liability or awards new compensation.
