## Event

United States v. Shabani, 513 U.S. 10, No. 93-981. Merits decision, November 1, 1994. Argued October 3, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The question is whether 21 U.S.C. §846 requires proof of an overt act in furtherance of a narcotics conspiracy. Shabani was convicted after the district court refused his requested overt-act instruction. The Ninth Circuit, 993 F.2d 1419, reversed under its circuit precedent and expressly left sufficiency and other appellate claims undecided. The indictment omitted an overt-act allegation; its sufficiency on that ground follows from the same statutory construction.

Render form: compact. Basis: 9–0 judgment, a single common statutory ground and a bounded appellate remand.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand for the reserved appellate issues, 9–0. Supporting Justices: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice opposes.

The appellate court must apply the rule that §846 has no additional overt-act element and address the remaining preserved issues. No new factfinding is required on the overt-act question. The Court does not order acquittal, a new trial or unconditional reinstatement of every aspect of the conviction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act

**Controlling proposition:** Section 846 of Title 21 requires neither an allegation nor proof of an overt act beyond the unlawful narcotics agreement. The Government must still prove the unlawful agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt; neither mere association nor awareness of another person’s plan is enough.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this construction and its application; no fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress made the unlawful agreement the completed conspiracy offense. Section 846 contains no additional overt-act language, while 18 U.S.C. §371 expressly requires an act to effect the conspiracy’s object. Nash supplies the common-law understanding of conspiracy, and Singer shows why Congress’s omission of an extra act requirement matters when it uses that settled term. This reading does not punish a thought: the agreement itself is conduct, and the Government must prove knowing participation in it. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary element cannot rest on circuit precedent when the governing statute supplies none. Felix’s treatment of successive prosecutions did not decide this statutory question and cannot add the omitted element. Nor does lenity supply it; ordinary construction resolves the question without the residual ambiguity addressed in Ratzlaf. The refused overt-act instruction therefore supplies no ground for reversal of the conviction. The Court leaves the appeal’s reserved sufficiency and other claims to the court of appeals.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Nash v. United States*, 229 U.S. 373 (1913), applies the common-law meaning under which the agreement completes conspiracy without a further overt act.
- *Singer v. United States*, 323 U.S. 338 (1945), applies the significance of Congress’s omission of an additional overt-act requirement from a statutory conspiracy offense.
- *United States v. Felix*, 503 U.S. 378 (1992), remains a double-jeopardy decision distinguishing agreement from substantive conduct; it did not decide §846’s overt-act element. Its narrower separate position likewise supplies no contrary statutory element.
- *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), preserves lenity for genuine ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction; that condition is absent here. Its distinct willfulness rule is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule for every conspiracy statute is announced. The Court does not decide credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, the remaining appellate objections or the ultimate validity of the conviction. Current double-jeopardy law is unchanged.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 1, 1994, §846 conspiracy requires the unlawful agreement and knowing participation, but no further overt act in allegation or proof. This adds an offense-specific statutory construction. It does not alter the knowledge requirements of other crimes, create a universal rule about omitted statutory language, or change Felix, Dixon, Grady or Ratzlaf beyond the distinctions stated above.

## Event

U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18, No. 93-714. Decision on mootness and vacatur, November 8, 1994. Argued October 4, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The Ninth Circuit, 2 F.3d 899, affirmed the district court’s reversal of a bankruptcy ruling and remanded for further bankruptcy proceedings. Its decision recognized the availability of the new-value doctrine; it did not find a particular plan confirmable. A consensual settlement while review was pending mooted the underlying controversy. Bancorp requests vacatur of the appellate judgment; Bonner opposes it. The live questions are the Court’s continuing authority to dispose of the judgment and whether the voluntary settlement justifies vacatur. The bankruptcy merits are not reached.

Render form: compact. Basis: unanimous disposition on two directly supported grounds, with the appellate judgment preserved and a single uncomplicated Court opinion.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court denies the motion to vacate and dismisses the case as moot.

## Judgment & Remedy

The motion to vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment is denied and the Supreme Court proceeding is dismissed as moot, 9–0 on both components. Supporting Justices: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice opposes either component.

The appellate judgment remains undisturbed, without endorsement of its bankruptcy merits. The Court neither dismisses the underlying Chapter 11 case nor vacates any other order. No settlement-enforcement order, plan-confirmation ruling or Rule 60(b) remand is entered.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ending a controversy and disposing of the pending review are distinct judicial acts. Section 2106 authorizes a reviewing court to affirm, modify, vacate, set aside or reverse a judgment lawfully before it, and to direct further proceedings as justice requires. Walling recognizes the authority to make an appropriate disposition even when merits review cannot continue. Munsingwear likewise rests on power to address the consequences of a judgment that has become unreviewable. A categorical rule stripping all dispositional authority after mootness would contradict that practice. Article III still forbids an advisory ruling on the bankruptcy dispute; the Court therefore does not decide whether the new-value doctrine survives or whether a former proposed plan could be confirmed. Kokkonen concerns jurisdiction over a separate contract-enforcement controversy after dismissal, which no party obtains here. The existing review supplies the occasion for considering vacatur; the parties’ consent does not create jurisdiction or dictate the equitable result.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute.

### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Controlling proposition:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a losing party’s voluntary surrender of review ordinarily leaves the existing judgment in place. Munsingwear protects against an unreviewable judgment when review fails through happenstance; Karcher shows why a party’s own decision to end review receives different treatment. Bancorp accepted a consensual resolution while review remained available and has identified no further equitable reason to erase the appellate judgment. Settlement is lawful and valuable, but that value does not turn a completed judicial act into a term the litigants can remove by agreement. The judgment also has a public significance beyond their immediate bargain. Exceptional circumstances may justify relief in another settlement case; a clause requesting vacatur alone merely repeats the private preference and cannot supply the missing equity. This is no heightened evidentiary test or finding of settlement misconduct. The Court denies the requested alteration and dismisses this proceeding as moot, without affirming the Ninth Circuit’s bankruptcy reasoning or determining the judgment’s effect on every outsider.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

Bankruptcy new-value doctrine, settlement enforcement, collateral-order review and the merits of the judgment below remain unchanged by this disposition.

## Event

Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., including Walsh’s employee action, 513 U.S. 30, No. 93-1197. Merits decision, November 14, 1994. Argued October 3, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.

The question is whether PATH, the Port Authority’s bistate railway subsidiary, is an arm of New York and New Jersey entitled to Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employees’ federal suits. Hess and Walsh alleged separate workplace injuries and brought separate FELA actions; Hess also invoked the Boiler Inspection Act. The district courts dismissed under the Third Circuit’s arm-of-State precedent and the unmet one-year condition attached to the States’ consent. The reviewed appellate disposition left those dismissals in place. The two actions remain distinct; this decision resolves their common immunity premise.

Render form: full. Basis: a divided judgment with a material disagreement about the entity-status rule and the operation of conditional consent; the component judgment and opinion tables preserve both actions and the dissent’s separate ground.

## Holdings

### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Controlling proposition:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that PATH’s actual relationship to New York and New Jersey does not make these suits actions against the States. Lake Country Estates requires examining the compact structure, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes separate public entities from the State itself. Gubernatorial appointment and veto, legislative restrictions and public transportation responsibilities favor PATH’s position, but do not overcome this enterprise’s separate identity, revenues, assets and obligations. Article XV requires equal state appropriations only while operating revenues are inadequate, only for salaries, office and other administrative expenses, in amounts recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, with each State obligated only up to $100,000 annually. It is no promise to pay judgments. Financial independence therefore carries great weight in this mixed setting, without making treasury liability immunity’s sole condition. Feeney assumed status when deciding consent; it did not resolve the antecedent question now presented. Because PATH is not an immune arm, its conditional consent is unnecessary to overcome an Eleventh Amendment bar. The Court leaves ordinary defenses and liability for the alleged injuries to proceedings on remand.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 14, 1994, PATH is not an arm entitled to the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employee actions. Lake Country’s compact inquiry is clarified through the actual separate legal and financial structure, with treasury exposure carrying great weight but no universal sole-treasury test. The Third Circuit’s contrary PATH-status rule cannot sustain these dismissals. FELA coverage, consent of an entity that actually is immune, immunity abrogation and collateral-order appealability remain separate and unchanged.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. She would treat PATH as an arm of the States because the governors and legislatures retain substantial control over its commissioners, actions, projects and spending. Financial exposure matters, but the protection of a State from federal suit also safeguards its institutional authority. A self-supporting state enterprise need not lose that protection merely because it can pay judgments from its own revenues. Lake Country Estates permits an entity-specific finding of arm status; it does not compel treating PATH’s state controls as equivalent to the more local structure there. Puerto Rico Aqueduct recognizes protection from suit for qualifying arms while leaving the qualification question separate.

The dissent does not read Article XV as an unlimited state guarantee. Its equal, conditional administrative appropriations, recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, bind each State only up to $100,000 annually while operating revenues are inadequate. The claimed sovereign relationship rests on the combined legal controls, not an invented obligation to pay judgments. Having found arm status, the dissent treats consent as a separate requirement. Feeney’s federal-forum consent did not eliminate its conditions; the district courts found both actions outside the one-year period running from accrual. Hilton’s state-court FELA holding does not revive constructive waiver in federal court. The dissent therefore would affirm these dismissals without deciding liability, damages or a new exception to the consent legislation. This position does not control the Court’s status ruling or remand.

The Court awards no damages and decides no negligence, Boiler Inspection Act merits or independent nonimmunity limitations question.

## Event

## Holdings

### The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character

**Controlling proposition:** To convict under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1) or (2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knew the performer was a minor, as well as the applicable knowing handling act. It need not prove knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition; the actual statutory production, depiction and commerce-or-mail conditions remain distinct, and the Court does not decide whether knowledge of the jurisdictional nexus is required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven Justices directly adopt the factual-knowledge construction and its reasoning. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that knowingly reaches the factual features that make these depictions criminal. Morissette supplies the presumption of culpability, applied to the whole statutory setting rather than confined by the distance between the adverb and the later conditions. Staples supports requiring awareness of the characteristics that distinguish a prohibited object from an otherwise familiar one, while Posters preserves the difference between factual knowledge and legal classification. Neither decision mechanically resolves this statute. Ratzlaf’s distinct willfulness language required knowledge of unlawfulness there; no such general requirement follows here. Reading §2252 to punish a distributor who lacks awareness of the material’s incriminating character would also raise the speech concerns identified in Smith and Ferber. The fair contextual reading avoids that unnecessary constitutional ruling without rewriting a federal default of recklessness from Osborne’s Ohio statute. The reported finding that respondents knew the performer was underage remains relevant, but does not resolve every count, the indictment objection or whether these tapes contain the conduct the statute defines.

### The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Respondents may defend the existing appellate reversal on the renewed age-eighteen and lascivious-wording grounds that appear in the record, although the court of appeals rejected those grounds, because they seek no enlargement of their rights or reduction of the Government’s rights under that judgment. The Court reaches those two alternatives; the separate actual-or-simulated bestiality and sadistic-or-masochistic-abuse objection is not established as renewed and is not decided.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this reach ruling. Stone-Zsela joins only the dispositions on the two alternatives and adds no reach ground. Scalia and Thomas accept the legal availability of such defenses but do not decide either alternative because their independent ground would sustain the appellate judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that respondents may offer these two grounds to preserve the judgment they already obtained. American Railway Express distinguishes defending a judgment from asking the Court to enlarge it or overturn a separate adverse judgment. Respondents seek the same appellate reversal, not greater relief, and the two renewed objections were considered in the record below. Izumi therefore does not require treating each rejected reason as a separate adverse judgment that respondents must overturn. ### The asserted age-eighteen line does not establish substantial facial overbreadth

**Controlling proposition:** The renewed objection that §2256 defines a minor as a person under eighteen, rather than under sixteen, does not establish substantial First Amendment overbreadth in §§2252(a)(1)–(2) as construed here. Difficulty estimating age near eighteen, together with decisions permitting adult treatment of some older adolescents in other settings, does not require facial invalidation of this protective category.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part III and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the asserted difficulty of distinguishing older adolescents from adults does not establish the substantial overbreadth needed to invalidate this statute on its face. Ferber supplies that demanding facial standard and recognizes the governmental interest in preventing the sexual exploitation of children. Its particular statute protected children under sixteen, but the decision did not constitutionalize that age as an upper boundary. Permission to treat some sixteen- or seventeen-year-olds as adults in other settings likewise does not require adult treatment for sexual exploitation. The concern that distributors may avoid protected adult expression deserves consideration; the Court does not equate it with proof of a substantial unconstitutional sweep. The required actual knowledge of minority further narrows that concern, although the age-line ruling does not rest on scienter alone. No empirical finding about the amount of chilled material is made. The Court rejects this facial ground without deciding every application or the disputed content of respondents’ tapes.

### The complete lascivious-exhibition category defeats the word-substitution facial attack

**Controlling proposition:** Replacing lewd with lascivious in §2256’s category of lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area does not itself make §§2252(a)(1)–(2) facially vague or substantially overbroad. The complete category retains a sexual-conduct limit and does not cover nudity merely because a person’s body is visible; whether these particular tapes satisfy the definition remains open.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part IV and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

The provision concerns lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area, not nudity as such. Ferber’s treatment of lewd exhibition supplies a meaningful comparison, and the ordinary terminology considered in Miller does not become constitutionally indeterminate merely through the substitution of lascivious. ## Holdings

### RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA applies to the threatened future implementation of Clearwater's preexisting ordinance: the Church must establish a substantial burden on its religious exercise, after which Clearwater must produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. **Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's protection of religious exercise governs the proposed enforcement. Section 6 expressly covers implementation of laws adopted before RFRA, and the act to be restrained is future enforcement, not the completed enactment of the ordinance. Landgraf supplies the distinction between that prospective operation and new liability for completed conduct. The Court of Appeals decided before RFRA became law; its constitutional disposition cannot substitute for the statutory inquiry. Limited identifying information and disclosure of a voluntarily offered refund policy may serve neutral fraud prevention, but that description does not establish either the absence of a substantial burden or the least restrictive means of addressing a demonstrated burden. ## Holdings

### The Commission lacks independent certiorari authority for this FECA enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 437d(a)(6)'s authority to litigate and appeal civil enforcement actions through the Commission's general counsel does not displace 28 U.S.C. §518(a)'s allocation of Supreme Court representation in this FECA corporate-contribution action. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains available, and the express certiorari powers in 26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d) remain confined to actions in which the Commission appears under those respective funding sections.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule. Stone-Zsela and Stevens dissent; Ginsburg does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress did not authorize the Commission to petition independently in this enforcement action. Providence Journal establishes that authority to conduct litigation below does not necessarily include authority to represent the Government here. Congress may create an exception, and ordinary construction determines whether it has done so. Section 437d(a)(6) gives the Commission substantial enforcement autonomy, but its general appeal language must be read with section 518(a)'s specific allocation and the express certiorari grants in the presidential-funding statutes. Those grants apply only to actions brought within their respective sections; this corporate-contribution action is not one of them. Enforcement exclusivity and the separate power to defend actions do not settle who may affirmatively invoke this Court's review. Germain permits the grants to coexist with their distinct conditions. ## Holdings

#### The acute respiratory emergency triggers stabilization or lawful transfer

**Holding and operative rule:** When a participating hospital determines that an individual has an emergency respiratory condition, §1395dd(b)(1) requires the examination and treatment within its available staff and facilities necessary to prevent likely material deterioration, within reasonable medical probability, resulting from or during transfer, or transfer complying with subsection (c). Inability to cure the patient's underlying anencephaly, an ethical or medical objection to continued life support, uniform withholding from comparable anencephalic patients, and the hospital's lack of an intention to transfer do not excuse this duty where the acute emergency and effective stabilizing capability are established.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the immediate respiratory emergency is the condition to be stabilized. Section 1395dd(e)(1)(A) addresses acute symptoms sufficiently severe that absence of immediate attention could reasonably cause serious jeopardy to health, serious impairment of bodily functions, or serious dysfunction of an organ or part. The hospital concedes that Baby K's episodes meet that standard and that ventilation prevents deterioration. Incurable anencephaly does not negate those concessions. King requires reading the whole statute without adding an unexpressed restriction; Suter keeps the enacted duty distinct from an inferred remedy. Uniform screening under subsection (a) therefore cannot replace the separate stabilization obligation after an emergency is found. Nor may the hospital erase that obligation by retaining the patient without intending transfer. Ethical objection does not establish that otherwise available staff or facilities are unavailable. The holding concerns these acute presentations, not all inpatient, nonemergency or end-of-life care, and creates no entitlement to every preferred treatment.

- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): applies contextual reading and respect for expressed statutory coverage and limits; its military-leave rule does not decide a medical question.

#### Unstabilized transfer remains a fully conditional alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** An unstabilized transfer requires either a written request by the individual or legally responsible person after explanation of the hospital's obligations and transfer risks, or a physician-signed certification, based on then-available information, that expected medical benefits at the other facility outweigh increased transfer risks; if no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person may sign only after consulting a physician who makes that determination and later countersigns, and every certification must summarize risks and benefits. Every route also requires available treatment minimizing risk; receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance of appropriate care; transmission of all available emergency-related records, the request or certification, and the name and address of a required on-call physician who refused or failed timely to appear; qualified transportation personnel and equipment with necessary appropriate life support; and the Secretary's applicable additional health-and-safety requirements.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress provided a lawful transfer route and specified what makes it lawful. The treatment-or-transfer structure cannot be restated as treatment at this hospital under all future circumstances. #### Refusal compliance requires an actual offer and informed refusal

**Holding and operative rule:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement met when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately deems compliance when the hospital offers a transfer satisfying subsection (c), explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal; the mother's opposition alone establishes neither route here.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that refusal provisions are conditional compliance routes, not exceptions triggered by disagreement alone. A treatment offer and a transfer offer perform different statutory work. ## Holdings

#### The private repo return is outside the federal-obligation exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3124(a) does not exempt the return on these repurchase agreements because it is owed under a separate private financing obligation, rather than on the United States' securities; actual book-entry delivery does not change the source of the negotiated earnings. The statute continues to protect direct and indirect taxation of federal obligations and their interest, except nondiscriminatory corporate franchise or substitute corporate nonproperty taxes and estate or inheritance taxes; the State prevails because this income is outside the protected subject, not because an exception covers this individual income tax.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the obligation producing the taxed return controls. Rockford distinguishes the Government's payment obligation from a private obligation supported by federal credit, and Smith v. Davis supplies the same direct-obligation boundary. Here the Trusts advance cash for a privately negotiated return unrelated to Treasury yield. The Trusts transmit the issuer's payments to the Seller-Borrower; the Seller-Borrower maintains collateral under the margin provisions; and the Trusts retain recourse for a deficiency after default liquidation. Those arrangements identify secured financing for this exemption even though securities actually passed through the Federal Reserve system and no separate note was signed. Frank Lyon respects genuine business arrangements; it does not determine whether the Government owes these particular earnings. The State therefore may tax the private return. The removal of surplus language during recodification did not abolish indirect-tax protection. The Court decides neither repo ownership under other bodies of law nor a transaction conveying genuine beneficial ownership and federal coupon income.

- Rockford Life Insurance Co. v. Illinois Department of Revenue, 482 U.S. 182 (1987): applies its distinction between an actual federal obligation and separate private earnings; its GNMA facts are not substituted for these delivered federal securities.
- Smith v. Davis, 323 U.S. 111 (1944): applies the boundary between the Government's own direct obligation and a separate private obligation supported by federal assets or credit.
- Frank Lyon Co. v. United States, 435 U.S. 561 (1978): distinguishes respect for a genuine transaction's form from identifying the obligation producing income under §3124; no sham or universal form-disregard rule is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b) sends the tax status of interest on Government or agency obligations, income from evidences of ownership, and disposition gain or loss to the Internal Revenue Code. **Holding and operative rule:** A ruling that expressly addresses federal-obligation repos does not, without an identified favorable policy for comparable state or local repos, establish the discriminatory taxation alleged here. The discrimination objection therefore supplies no ground to invalidate Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 on this record; the Court does not approve actual discriminatory treatment or find every Nebraska practice neutral.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the taxpayer's comparison must concern an actual difference in treatment. Memphis Bank forbids favoring state obligations over protected federal obligations; that protection remains intact. But the taxpayer identifies no statute, ruling or other Nebraska policy exempting the return on otherwise comparable state-obligation repurchase agreements. Revenue Ruling 22-85-1 names federal transactions because those transactions are its subject. That choice of subject does not itself establish the asserted favored comparator. Nor does the private character of the repo return alone answer the separate constitutional contention: the Court examines the alleged discrimination independently. The strongest objection is that singling out federal instruments might conceal unequal treatment. The present materials do not establish the other side of that comparison, and the Court cannot supply an exemption that has not been identified. **Holding and operative rule:** The constitutional borrowing-power objection fails because the supplied evidence concerning the effects of classifying repos in bankruptcy does not establish the asserted tax-specific burden on federal borrowing. ## Holdings

#### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding and operative rule:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that reasonableness requires the actual encounter, not a choice among tactics imagined afterward. Graham supplies the on-scene, objective inquiry into immediate danger, resistance, flight and the surrounding circumstances, while Garner preserves the serious-threat boundary and its necessity and warning conditions. #### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

## Holdings

### The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** Under the applicable pre-reform 49 U.S.C. §§10743(b)(1) and 11702(a)(4), the ICC may seek a judicial injunction against collection violating valid credit regulations, and §§10761–10762's filed-rate commands do not categorically defeat that enforcement. Section 11702(a)(4) excludes an action under a provision governing the reasonableness or discriminatory character of rates; this action enforces specified credit and billing requirements rather than asking the court to determine rate reasonableness or discrimination.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's credit delegation and its filed-rate commands must operate together. Section 10743 permits credit under Commission regulations governing payment and preventing discrimination; §11702 supplies the public enforcement channel. The anti-discrimination purpose of credit regulation does not itself turn this suit into the rate-review action excluded from that channel. Commercial Metals rejected an implied private consignor defense while recognizing the Commission's express enforcement tools. Maislin rejected a negotiated-rate policy inconsistent with the filed-rate scheme, not every independently authorized statutory remedy. Reiter likewise preserves claims and defenses supplied by the Act without creating a general equitable exception. MCI requires an actual delegation, which these credit and enforcement provisions provide; Gardner's insistence on statutory meaning before deference does not eliminate that delegation. The trustee therefore cannot defeat this action merely by expressing the challenged damages as a difference between filed rates. Statutory authority still does not establish every violation or entitle the Commission to every requested injunction. The Court decides no general private defense, constitutional question or entitlement under later transportation reforms.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Southern Pacific Transportation Co. v. Commercial Metals Co., 456 U.S. 336 (1982): applies its distinction between an implied private credit defense and the ICC's express public enforcement tools; the private-defense holding remains intact.
- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990): distinguishes its invalid negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy from enforcement under the separate credit delegation; no general equitable departure from filed rates is permitted.
- Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993): applies its preservation of specifically enacted statutory routes alongside filed-rate duties; counterclaims, recoupment, primary jurisdiction and separate-judgment discretion retain their own conditions.

## Holdings

### Substantive prior consistent statements require the premotive relationship

**Controlling proposition:** Under original Federal Rule of Evidence 801(d)(1)(B), a prior statement offered substantively to rebut an express or implied charge of recent fabrication or improper influence or motive must have been made before the particular charged fabrication, influence, or motive arose. The declarant must testify and be subject to cross-examination concerning the statement, and the statement must be consistent with that testimony and offered for that specified rebuttal purpose; general relevance or repetition alone does not satisfy this nonhearsay rule.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the same rule and explanation; no aggregation of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Rule carries forward the premotive requirement associated with its specific rebuttal terms. Salerno requires satisfaction of the chosen evidentiary provision's actual conditions; general fairness cannot replace them. Daubert displaces common-law requirements incompatible with enacted rules but permits compatible inherited meaning to illuminate their terms. Here the words describing rebuttal of recent fabrication or improper motive invoke the distinction between answering the source of an accusation and merely repeating an account. The defense asserted that A.T. accused her father to remain with her mother. The challenged accounts followed that asserted custody motive and therefore do not qualify under this subparagraph. A later statement may sometimes possess indirect probative force, as the Government argues, but relevance alone does not establish this particular condition for substantive admission. The Court decides neither that A.T. fabricated her account nor that the statements are inadmissible for every purpose. Other exceptions and properly limited nonhearsay use retain their separate requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): applies its requirement that the selected hearsay provision's conditions be satisfied; its similar-motive and forfeiture holdings remain distinct.
- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993): applies its distinction between incompatible common-law additions and compatible interpretive guidance; no expert-evidence factor is transferred to this Rule.

### Alternative admissibility and ordinary error review remain for remand

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal of admission under Rule 801(d)(1)(B) leaves the Court of Appeals to decide other preserved grounds of admission and, if the statements are not admissible on such a ground, the consequences under ordinary harmless-error rules. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness, and neither immediate retrial nor acquittal follows solely from the Rule-construction error.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt these remedial limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying the wrong admission rule does not complete appellate review of the conviction. Williamson separates an exception's proper construction from the admissibility of particular evidence and from the relief an established error requires. The Court of Appeals left the medical-diagnosis and residual-exception grounds undecided; those grounds remain available only within their own terms and ordinary preservation limits. The reviewing court may not assume their satisfaction from the present holding. If no lawful ground sustains admission, Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos require evaluation of the whole record under the Government's harmlessness burden. The rejection of the temporal limitation below did not supply that evaluation. Chapman would apply only if a constitutional trial error were actually reached and found; this decision finds none. The Court also does not revive an unpreserved aggregate Rule 403 objection or reopen every separate appellate issue. Remand permits the necessary bounded inquiry while leaving both guilt and the ultimate need for a new trial undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594 (1994): applies its separation of admissibility, preservation, and judgment consequences.
- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record substantial-influence standard to preserved nonconstitutional error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves its beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard only for constitutional trial error actually reached and found.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): distinguishes forfeited error from this preserved construction question; no new preservation finding is made.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 803(4) requires statements for medical diagnosis or treatment describing history, symptoms, pain, sensations, or their inception or general cause insofar as reasonably pertinent to diagnosis or treatment. Then-Rule 803(24) applies to a statement not specifically covered by any of the foregoing exceptions and requires equivalent circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness, a material fact, greater probative value on the offered point than other evidence obtainable through reasonable efforts, service of the Rules' general purposes and the interests of justice, and advance notice affording a fair opportunity to prepare, including the intended statement's particulars and the declarant's name and address. The Court finds no such condition satisfied or unsatisfied. Rehabilitation-only use, other properly limited nonhearsay purposes, and factbound questions outside the admitted temporal issue remain undecided.

## Holdings

### Ministerial selection claims encounter a constitutional defense

**Controlling proposition:** The Religion Clauses bar the application of Title VII to Young's race, sex, and retaliation claims challenging her advancement and retention as a probationary minister, because deciding those claims or imposing the requested relief would subject the church's choice of its religious leadership to civil review. The defense covers reinstatement, compelled reconsideration, and financial liability resting on that same selection decision; it does not exempt every religious employee or independently actionable secular conduct from civil law.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this claim-specific rule; their formal descriptions of the judgment are distinguished below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these claims would place a civil court in control of a ministerial selection. Kedroff protects church government and the selection of ecclesiastical authorities from state control. Milivojevich forbids civil reconsideration of ecclesiastical decisions through an inquiry into whether the church followed its own internal rules. Young seeks review of denied advancement and termination of her ministerial service, including alleged departures from church procedure. Reinstatement or compelled reconsideration would directly control that office; damages for the same decision do not make the underlying adjudication independent. Race and sex discrimination and retaliation are serious civil wrongs, but calling their asserted motives secular does not remove this inquiry's control of religious leadership. Section 702's religious-preference permission is not enlarged to include those categories. Jones v. Wolf permits neutral adjudication of genuine property obligations; this record establishes no independently pleaded secular claim of that kind. The Court does not find discrimination, religious motivation, or procedural compliance, and leaves disputed ministerial status and unrelated civil obligations for appropriate cases.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kedroff v. St. Nicholas Cathedral, 344 U.S. 94 (1952): applies its protection of church government and selection of ecclesiastical authorities to this ministerial-employment claim.
- Serbian Eastern Orthodox Diocese v. Milivojevich, 426 U.S. 696 (1976): applies its bar on reviewing internal ecclesiastical decisions for arbitrariness or compliance with church procedures; its unresolved fraud-or-collusion possibility remains unresolved.
- Jones v. Wolf, 443 U.S. 595 (1979): distinguishes neutral property adjudication from review of ministerial selection and preserves the former within its own limits.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990): distinguishes regulation of ordinary conduct by neutral general law from civil control of the choice of religious leaders; its rule is not replaced.

### The defense defeats the claims on their merits, not statutory jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** A federal court with jurisdiction under Title VII §706(f)(3) and 28 U.S.C. §1331 over a substantial Title VII claim exercises that jurisdiction when it decides that the Religion Clauses defeat the claim; it must enter a substantive dismissal rather than characterize this defense as a withdrawal of subject-matter jurisdiction. Young's pleaded claims are substantial federal claims, and the remand is for entry of a merits dismissal of the stated ministerial advancement-and-retention claims, not a discrimination trial or an award of relief.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the jurisdiction-and-merits distinction; eight vacate the jurisdictional judgment as a whole, while the Chief would vacate the jurisdictional characterization and affirm the dismissal on the merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to decide a federal claim does not depend on the claimant ultimately overcoming a constitutional defense. Bell v. Hood separates a substantial federal question from the legal sufficiency of the asserted right to relief. Congress supplied jurisdiction over Title VII claims, and Young's discrimination allegations invoke that statutory subject. The church's successful defense therefore supplies the reason for a merits judgment, not a reason to deny that the court possessed adjudicatory authority. Ecclesiastical autonomy limits the permissible application of civil law; deciding that limit is itself an exercise of jurisdiction. Bell's exceptions for immaterial claims made solely to obtain jurisdiction and wholly insubstantial and frivolous claims are not presented here. The Court does not decide their treatment. The correction changes the legal basis and preclusive character of the dismissal without requiring a trial about church motives. No reinstatement, promotion, back pay, or damages follows from recognition that the federal court had power to decide the defense.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678 (1946): applies the distinction between a substantial federal claim's jurisdictional basis and its legal defeat; the immaterial-jurisdiction-device and wholly insubstantial-or-frivolous exceptions are not reached.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375 (1994): preserves the need for a statutory or otherwise lawful jurisdictional basis; Title VII and §1331 supply that basis here, unlike the separate enforcement claim in Kokkonen.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional defense rests on Young's undisputed reported ministerial role and her selection-dependent claims, not a comprehensive definition of minister. No fraud or collusion is alleged; the Court neither adopts nor rejects an exception for it. The relevant church decision is that of the highest body to which this dispute was actually carried, without a new exhaustion requirement reaching the denomination's ultimate hierarchy. RFRA's invocation, preservation, and application to this suit remain undecided. A genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim remains outside this adjudication only if independently pleaded and resolvable without deciding doctrine, ministerial fitness, or the selection of religious leaders; no such claim is found to exist here.

## Precedent Treatment

Kedroff and Milivojevich supply the claim-specific selection defense. Jones preserves neutral independent property adjudication. Bell supplies statutory jurisdiction despite a defeating defense. Smith, Lukumi, Kiryas Joel, Lamb's Chapel, and Clearwater remain within their different subjects; no RFRA application or new §702 race/sex exemption is adopted. Kokkonen's requirement of a jurisdictional source is satisfied, not displaced.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 17, 1995, these selection-dependent Title VII claims encounter a substantive Religion Clause defense, and substantial federal jurisdiction survives to decide that defense. The ruling creates no general immunity from civil law for religious institutions, does not enlarge Title VII §702, and leaves RFRA applicability and independently pleaded secular claims outside its holding.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's ministerial-selection defense and jurisdictional distinction. He would vacate the jurisdictional characterization, affirm dismissal of the ministerial-employment claims on the merits, and direct dismissal with prejudice insofar as those claims challenge ordination, ministerial promotion, discipline, assignment, or continued ministerial employment. The Court's mandate also requires substantive dismissal, but he would expressly affirm that disposition while correcting its basis.

In his view ministerial autonomy follows genuine function rather than title: who preaches, leads worship, teaches doctrine, and represents a faith belongs to the religious body. Even substantial evidence that sex entered a selection decision cannot authorize judicial review of whether doctrinal or ministerial reasons were valid or pretextual. That limit does not excuse discrimination in ordinary secular employment and supplies no general church immunity. An independently pleaded and genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim may proceed only if adjudication avoids religious doctrine, ministerial fitness, and the selection of religious leaders; he identifies no such claim in Young's complaint as reported. Public money, tax benefits, licensing, or other governmental contact alone does not waive ministerial autonomy. Government may impose lawful, clearly stated conditions on secular uses of public funds and recover diverted funds, but cannot purchase control of clergy selection. Those additional public-funding observations are his own; the Court does not decide a funding controversy in this case.

## Procedure After Action

The Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is vacated and the case remanded for entry of a substantive dismissal of the stated ministerial advancement-and-retention claims. No new discrimination trial, ordination, reinstatement, promotion, reconsideration, money, or costs is ordered. The Chief would additionally affirm dismissal on the merits and expressly direct dismissal with prejudice. Review here is complete; entry of the corrected judgment remains below.

The Court reverses and remands. Section 2 continues to apply in state court, and this transaction falls within its commerce coverage without a requirement that the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity.

## Holdings

#### Section 2 continues to govern covered agreements in state court

**Controlling proposition:** FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court, making them valid, irrevocable and enforceable save upon grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. The Court retains Southland's state-court holding; Alabama may apply generally applicable contract defenses but may not deny enforcement solely because its law singles out predispute arbitration agreements for a categorical disability.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the established state-court application of §2 remains governing law. Southland supplies that substantive construction, and Perry confirms that a State cannot withhold enforcement by assigning a covered dispute to a judicial forum while preserving generally applicable contract defenses. Respondents and supporting States ask the Court to reject that interpretation in light of the Act's federal procedural structure. The statutory-construction objection deserves consideration, but repetition of that original disagreement does not establish a sufficient reason to overturn the settled rule and the reliance it permits across forums. Congress remains able to alter the statute. Alabama's categorical prohibition is directed at arbitration itself, not a defense to contracts generally. Volt does not save that prohibition: it enforces an actual contractual choice of state rules, and no such choice is established by the forum's location alone. Retaining §2's substantive command does not decide whether every direction in §§3 and 4 must operate identically in state courts or settle the assent and scope questions of every claim here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1: retains the substantive state-court application of §2 against the express request to overrule it; its commerce and general-contract-defense conditions remain.
- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483: applies its rejection of a state judicial-forum exception directed at arbitration while preserving generally applicable contract defenses.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Junior University, 489 U.S. 468: preserves enforcement of an actual contractual choice of state arbitration rules; a forum-wide hostility to arbitration is not itself that choice.

#### Coverage turns on actual commerce within Congress's power

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2's phrase involving commerce reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full commerce authority Congress constitutionally possesses, and coverage turns on the transaction's actual interstate commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity when contracting. The multistate service operations and interstate procurement of treatment and repair materials establish the necessary commerce here, requiring reversal of Alabama's coverage ruling; this statutory construction does not define the outer constitutional boundary or make remote interstate history alone conclusive.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §2 describes the transaction actually made and carried out. Its broad commerce language does not confine coverage to goods moving in an interstate stream, and its reference to a contract evidencing a transaction adds no private expectation or foresight requirement. Bernhardt preserves the need to show a transaction involving commerce; its failure-of-proof setting does not supply Alabama's contemplation test. Here a commercial termite-service arrangement involved firms operating across state lines and actual materials obtained outside Alabama. Local work on the house does not erase those commercial connections. The competing reading would make enforceability turn on what parties predicted about commerce instead of what their transaction involved, although Congress supplied no such mental-state condition. These facts establish genuine interstate commercial operation, rather than a talisman based on the remote history of one object. The ruling resolves statutory coverage under the commerce authority Congress possesses. It neither fixes that authority's furthest constitutional reach nor determines who agreed to arbitrate every pleaded claim or which ordinary contract defenses may succeed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bernhardt v. Polygraphic Co. of America, Inc., 350 U.S. 198: distinguishes a record failing to establish the requisite commerce; its transaction predicate survives, without a newly attributed contemplation requirement.
- Southland, 465 U.S. 1: applies its substantive enforceability rule after identifying the actual covered transaction; general hostility to arbitration does not substitute for the statutory commerce inquiry.

**Remedy and limits:** The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded under the actual-commerce rule. Formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other generally applicable contract defenses remain for ordinary adjudication to the extent properly presented and preserved below, together with party assent, clause scope and appropriate implementing procedure. Coverage is not a final order compelling all parties to arbitrate every claim. Section 1's separate employment exclusion is not presented by this service bond. The Court does not decide the tort merits, damages, a particular fraud-in-the-inducement defense, a contractual choice of state rules not established here, or wholesale state-court incorporation of §§3–4.

## Precedent Treatment

Southland's substantive application in state court is retained, and Perry continues to reject arbitration-specific state barriers while preserving ordinary contract defenses. Volt protects an actual choice of state rules, not an assumed adoption of the forum's antiarbitration policy. Bernhardt retains the commerce predicate but supplies no contemplation requirement. No earlier decision is overruled, and §§3–4's entire state-procedure reach is not decided.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, §2 reaches actual transactions within Congress's constitutional commerce power without subjective contemplation or a stream-only limitation. State courts remain bound by its substantive enforceability rule and full general-contract-defense qualification. The genuine multistate operations and interstate materials suffice here; the outer constitutional ceiling, every party's assent, clause scope and particular contract defenses remain for their proper settings. The decision adds no special immunity from ordinary contract law.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor joins the Court's opinion and concurs separately. She continues to believe that Southland originally misconstrued the Act's application in state courts, for the reasons set out in her Southland and Perry dissents. Statutory stare decisis nevertheless weighs strongly against reopening an interpretation that has governed for a decade without a demonstrated inability to administer it. Once that interpretation is retained, a broad and uniform actual-commerce rule is appropriate; narrowing commerce coverage is not a substitute for overruling Southland. Her original-construction reservation does not subtract from her join in the Court's present rule or mandate.

Justice Thomas dissents, joined by Justice Scalia. He reads the Act's structure and federal-court judicial directions as failing to create the substantive command to state courts that Southland announced. He would overrule Southland to that extent and affirm the denial of arbitration on the ground that the Act does not govern this state proceeding. He does not decide the alternative scope of commerce coverage or the factual success of a general contract defense. The dissent presents no additional independently sufficient specific-performance holding under §§3–4.

Justice Scalia also dissents separately. His earlier acceptance of Southland when its overruling was not requested does not resolve the express request presented here. In his view, reliance on a statutory interpretation cannot by itself preserve a state-court command the statute does not establish. He joins Thomas's state-court ground and would affirm without deciding the Court's commerce construction. These proposed changes to Southland do not control; the Court retains that precedent.

## Procedure After Action

The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded. The state courts must apply the actual-commerce rule and address formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other ordinary contract issues insofar as properly presented and preserved, as well as clause scope and implementing procedure. No waived defense is revived, final damages ordered or universal party-by-party arbitration order entered. Supreme Court merits review ends; the arbitration request and underlying litigation continue below within these limits.

## Holdings

#### State consumer-fraud standards governing these airline benefits are preempted

**Controlling proposition:** The Airline Deregulation Act's transaction-period §1305(a)(1) preempts application of the Illinois Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act to the pleaded devaluation of earned airline-travel awards through changed redemption restrictions, because that claim enforces a state-imposed substantive standard directly related to airline rates and services. General applicability, a damages remedy and the general §1506 savings clause do not avoid that express prohibition; materially different duties with only tenuous, remote or peripheral connections remain outside this ruling.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, consumer-fraud portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Five of the eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule and explanation. Justices O'Connor and Thomas also vote to preempt this claim on their separate broader ground; Justice Stevens dissents on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged award restrictions concern the price and availability of airline transportation. Capacity controls govern how many seats passengers may obtain at an award level; the alleged restrictions on use of earned credits govern what travel those credits purchase. Morales supplies the rule that a generally applicable law can regulate airline rates and services when its application has the required connection. The Illinois court's description of frequent-flyer benefits as peripheral does not answer that direct relation. A damages action may enforce the same substantive command as prospective regulation, so the form of relief does not remove the conflict. Section 1506 preserves remedies outside the specific prohibition rather than undoing it. Cipollone's distinct cigarette provisions require attention to the duty and wording at issue, not a universal exception for claims called fraud. The Court decides this pleaded challenge without resolving the disputed retroactive application of particular blackout dates or declaring every airline deception claim preempted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., 504 U.S. 374: applies the controlling connection-or-reference rule and the specific-preemption/general-savings distinction to these award restrictions; its separate fare-marketing effects holding remains confined to that package, and its remote-effects reservation remains.
- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504: distinguishes its clause-specific preservation of general intentional falsehood; that cigarette holding supplies no universal airline-fraud exemption and retains its own scope.

#### An airline's actual voluntary bargain remains enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1305(a)(1) does not preempt ordinary court enforcement of an airline's self-imposed contractual obligations, including ordinary contract remedies, when the claim is confined to the undertaking the parties actually made. Courts may use ordinary contract law to identify and enforce that undertaking, but may not enlarge or reform it through a state policy external to the agreement; the pleaded contract claims survive dismissal only within that boundary.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, contract portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six of eight participants directly adopt this rationale; no synthesis of narrower separate opinions is required. Justices O'Connor and Thomas dissent on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that enforcing the parties' own bargain differs from prescribing its substantive terms. The airline chooses the obligation; ordinary adjudication determines what it promised and supplies the usual remedy for a proved breach. The Act's prohibition on state enactment or enforcement of governing legal standards, read with its savings clause, leaves room for that private ordering. Cipollone supplies a useful distinction between a voluntarily undertaken promise and a duty imposed by the State, although the airline clause requires its own construction. The contrary position correctly observes that contracts depend on law for enforcement. That observation does not make every privately chosen term a state-prescribed airline policy. The limitation is therefore substantive: a contract label cannot preserve an external fairness requirement. American's reservation of authority to alter its program may defeat the claimed promise, but its reach must be determined below. No binding promise protecting earned credits, breach, reliance, causation or damages is found here; the Court orders only the confined contract proceeding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Cipollone, 505 U.S. 504: extends the voluntary-source distinction by analogy to this independently construed airline clause; its narrower warranty ground does not become a blanket common-law exemption.

- Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. American Train Dispatchers Association, 499 U.S. 117: distinguishes the dependence of a contract on law for enforceability from the source of the substantive obligation under this statute; its construction of the distinct railroad immunity provision is undisturbed.

## Holdings

### Saved seed means seed retained for the farmer's own planting

**Controlling proposition:** Under former 7 U.S.C. §2543, a farmer satisfying the statutory conditions may sell for reproductive purposes only seed genuinely saved for replanting the farmer's own acreage; intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing does not become exempt merely because the seller primarily grows food or feed crops. Qualifying later disposition of genuinely retained seed remains possible when planting plans change, without a fixed percentage or mechanical ceiling based on the previous year's acreage.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt the construction, explanation, and application that these sales fall outside the exemption. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela independently accepts the stated construction but does not join the Court's final application; his separate remand position is not counted as an additional application vote.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the farmer-sales proviso protects a limited category of seed, rather than every crop produced by a person whose principal occupation is farming. The opening sentence reserves infringement through multiplication for reproductive marketing, while the proviso permits sale of such saved seed. Those provisions operate together when the farmer initially retains seed for planting and later changes plans. The proviso's direction to disregard §2541(3) prevents the qualifying later sale from retroactively converting lawful production into infringement; it does not erase the opening reservation for production intended for the planting market. The contrary reading gives the sales proviso work only by depriving saved seed and the reservation of their limiting function. Reasonably anticipated acreage expansion and crop-appropriate retention remain possible, so this construction creates no rigid prior-acreage quota. These reproductive-production sales, greatly exceeding the Winterboers' own planting needs, fail the necessary saved-seed condition. Their asserted food-and-feed business proportion cannot cure that failure, and no finding about disputed state-law compliance is needed to establish it.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: follows its respect for the enacted condition in statutory context, without importing the veterans statute's protective construction or inventing a numerical PVPA condition.

**Operative qualifications:** Saved seed must be produced by the person from seed obtained, or descended from seed obtained, with the owner's authority for seeding purposes. Seller and purchaser must each have as their primary farming occupation growing crops for sale for nonreproductive purposes; the sale must comply with applicable state seed-sale laws. The retained §2541(4) restriction on using the variety to produce, rather than develop, a hybrid or different variety remains separate. Bona fide nonreproductive sales in customary nonreproductive channels retain their independent statutory protection; a purchaser who diverts that seed to planting is deemed to have notice under §2567 that the diversion infringes. That diversion provision is distinct from §2541(6)'s notice claim. No general ruling on a buyer's later authorized-source lineage is adopted.

### Marketing does not require an advertising or distribution apparatus

**Controlling proposition:** Former §2541(3)'s multiplication as a step in marketing for growing purposes includes multiplication directed toward holding protected seed forth for sale and the preparatory activities of selling; extensive advertising, intermediaries, or coordinated merchandising are not required. The proviso's primary-farming-occupation condition concerns the farmer's business as a whole, not a judicial rule allowing reproductive sale of less than half of each protected variety's crop, and that occupation condition does not dispense with the distinct saved-seed limitation.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt these related constructions; Justice Stevens rejects the marketing premise. The Chief's separate opinion rejects an advertising prerequisite and a judicial fifty-percent quota without joining the final application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ordinary marketing encompasses offering seed for reproductive sale without the elaborate commercial machinery demanded by the Court of Appeals. The statute regulates multiplication as a step in marketing, so its protection reaches production directed toward the later sale as well as the completed transfer separately described in §2541(1). Using different words for the transfer and its preparatory reproductive activity does not require limiting the latter to wholesalers, cooperatives, or advertising campaigns. The enacted context, rather than a preference for plant breeders, supplies that distinction. The lower court's variety-by-variety percentage rule likewise has no textual foundation. Congress described a person's primary farming occupation, not the percentage of each variety marketed for planting. Reading occupation at the whole-business level does not authorize all reproductive sales by that farmer: the seed itself must still satisfy the own-planting condition. Stevens's narrower commercial-distribution reading would preserve the lower judgment, but it would remove ordinary reproductive-sale production from the reserved protection without a statutory basis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: applies contextual attention to the enacted terms rather than an additional condition; its separate VA-benefits rule remains unchanged.

### The former Act governs these transactions

**Controlling proposition:** The 1994 Plant Variety Protection Act amendments do not govern these transactions or supply the meaning of the former saved-seed provision: Pub. L. 103-349 §15 makes the amendments effective April 4, 1995, after this decision, and §14 preserves former-law treatment for specified older certificates and pending applications subject to its express exceptions. The later deletion of the farmer-sales proviso therefore creates no retrospective liability here and does not establish that every later sale involving an older protected variety loses former-law treatment.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's temporal-text portion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: seven direct opinion votes. The Chief and Justice Stevens also apply former law in their separate writings, without joining every broader temporal rationale of one another or of Landgraf.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Congress's actual temporal directions before considering a general presumption about retrospective consequences. Landgraf and Rivers preserve that starting point, although their broader formulations received different joins. The amendment became law on October 6, 1994 and specified effectiveness 180 days later. The statute therefore has not yet displaced the former law for this January decision, much less retroactively changed the consequences of completed sales. Congress also addressed older certificates and pending applications separately, which forecloses a universal inference that the new farmer-sales restriction immediately reaches all protected varieties. The pendency of this appeal does not change the relevant statutory dates or turn later legislation into conclusive evidence of the prior text's meaning. The Court neither approves nor condemns the new policy. It construes the former Act on its own terms and leaves any future transaction to the provisions that actually govern it, including the transition conditions Congress expressly enacted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies Congress's express temporal direction as the starting point; does not convert Scalia, Kennedy, or Thomas into joiners of its broader general framework.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298 (1994): preserves the separation of later legislative change from the law governing completed conduct; the new amendment is not a conclusive interpretation of old text.

**Transition qualifications:** Section 14(a), except as otherwise provided in §14, preserves prior law for varieties with certificates issued before April 4, 1995 and applications pending then. Section 14(b) permits refiling a pending application on or after that date under amended eligibility and protection terms, while §42 uses the original filing date. Section 14(c) requires a notice claiming amended protection to identify that protection and applies §128 sanctions to false or misleading claims or labels. No relevant refiling or transition exception is found.

**Notice and remedial limits:** The seven-Justice Court opinion does not decide whether a sale actually authorized by §2543 must nevertheless satisfy §2541(6), because these sales fail the saved-seed condition. It makes no separate notice-infringement finding or damages award. Section 2541 concerns unauthorized acts in the United States or regulated or affected commerce, before protection expires but after certificate issuance or distribution with §2567 notice, subject to the subchapter's exceptions. Section 2541(6) concerns dispensing propagable protected material without the protected-variety notice under which it was received; failure of the saved-seed defense alone does not establish those elements. Former §2567 permits owners to give public notice by associating a label with seed or affixing it to its container or the variety, stating that unauthorized propagation or seed multiplication is prohibited and, after the certificate issues, adding a protected-variety designation. When an owner-authorized distribution reaches the infringer without that marking, the owner cannot recover damages unless the infringer has actual awareness that propagation is prohibited or the variety is protected; damages then extend only to infringement after such notice. For both damages and an injunction, a court may be lenient concerning disposal of material acquired in good faith through acts before such notice. These are reserved remedial conditions, not findings of marking, actual awareness, good faith, or entitlement to leniency in this case. Actual relief and precise lawful injunction implementation remain for the receiving courts. The adjudicated own-planting failure is not reopened by that limited return.

## Precedent Treatment

Brown supplies contextual respect for enacted conditions without extending its veterans-specific rule. Landgraf and Rivers support obedience to the express temporal directions, with their actual coalition boundaries preserved. No precedent is overruled, no statutory quota is invented, and the hypothetical notice status of a truly exempt sale remains unresolved by the Court.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, the former Act protects qualifying later disposition of seed genuinely saved for the farmer's own planting, while reproductive-market production and these sales fall outside that condition. Ordinary marketing needs no advertising apparatus. The farmer's primary occupation concerns the business as a whole, without a crop-specific half-crop safe harbor. The Court establishes no final §2541(6) liability or damages and no rule resolving notice for a truly §2543-exempt sale. The 1994 amendments retain their actual future effective date and qualified grandfather provisions.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that the Federal Circuit's broad occupation-based exemption must be reversed and that former law controls. Seed genuinely saved to replant the farmer's own acreage may later be sold to qualified farmers when plans change and the statutory conditions are met. Intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing is outside that protected core, but neither high yield alone nor an invented fifty-percent rule establishes infringement. Marketing does not require advertising. The scale and reproductive destination of these sales support applying the multiplication restriction; he would remand for application of the narrower construction and resolution of unresolved infringement and remedial questions, without joining the Court's conclusive application to every challenged sale.

He separately reaches the legal relationship of the exemption to the notice claim. Former §2543 expressly retains §§2541(3) and (4) but does not separately except §2541(6) from its protection. In his view, a sale actually satisfying the correctly construed exemption remains exempt from notice-infringement liability; if it is not exempt, the receiving court must determine §2541(6)'s ordinary elements and lawful relief. Rejection of the broad exemption does not establish absence of notice, satisfaction of every element, or damages. His remand includes that pleaded claim and requires reconsideration of its exemption premise under the narrower rule. He makes no final notice finding, imposes no new notice form, and imports no later amendment backward. This conditional notice proposition is his alone; the Court expressly leaves it undecided.

Justice Stevens would affirm because Congress used marketing in §2541(3) and selling in §2541(1). He reads marketing more narrowly to address commercial distribution through cooperatives, wholesalers, retailers, or comparable extensive coordinated activity, rather than every ordinary farmer sale. Unclear restraints on alienation should not be enlarged by treating those distinct terms as equivalent. He accepts that the Court's own-planting exposition follows if its broad marketing premise is adopted, but rejects that premise. His position is therefore not simply a preference for a different acreage quota or an exemption from every statutory condition. He applies former law and would preserve the Federal Circuit's judgment and eligibility remand; he makes no independent final notice-liability or damages finding.

## Procedure After Action

The Federal Circuit judgment is reversed and the case returns for proceedings consistent with the Court's construction and seven-Justice determination that these sales fail the saved-seed condition. The receiving courts address genuinely unresolved notice elements, §2567 damages limits, and lawful relief; they do not reopen that controlling failure or automatically adopt every term of the earlier injunction. No damages amount or separate notice-infringement finding is entered. The Chief would remand for application and his stated conditional notice treatment; Stevens would affirm.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Fifth Circuit in both consolidated dockets, sustaining the Comptroller's conditioned approval against the statutory objections presented.

## Holdings

#### Reasoned approval of annuity brokerage falls within the banking power

**Controlling proposition:** The business of banking under 12 U.S.C. §24 Seventh is not exhausted by its enumerated activities, and the Comptroller's reasonable construction may include financial-intermediary agency sales of the fixed, variable and combined annuity products covered by this approval. The construction must remain within banking and its incidental powers and comply with enacted restrictions; this conditioned brokerage approval authorizes no underwriting, issuance or guarantee of the insurers' obligations and no unrelated commercial enterprise.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight of nine participating Justices directly adopt this rule and its agency-review rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments on an independent statutory ground and does not join this opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Act leaves room for a reasoned administrative judgment about financial services within banking. Section 24 Seventh identifies core activities but also grants the incidental powers necessary to carry on the business of banking. Clarke supplies specific support for substantial respect for the Comptroller's reasonable construction of that banking authority in the brokerage setting. Chevron requires courts first to enforce clear statutory boundaries; MCI and Gardner preserve that obligation rather than eliminating lawful interpretive discretion. Here the bank acts as agent between customers and insurers, offering financial instruments used for saving and investment. It does not issue the products or bear the insurer's actuarial or interest-rate risk. Camp requires genuine expert judgment, not a rationale invented by counsel, and the approval gives the relevant product and agency reasons. The contrary reading treats the enumerated activities as the entire banking business, overlooking the enacted broader language. The holding sustains this approval within its disclosure conditions, without authorizing every profitable financial activity or a bank's own-account underwriting.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Clarke v. Securities Industry Association, 479 U.S. 388: applies its substantial respect for reasonable Comptroller banking-law construction and brokerage analysis to this agency activity.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies deference within the ambiguity left after the enacted banking boundaries are determined; clear prohibitions still govern.
- Investment Company Institute v. Camp, 401 U.S. 617: preserves substantive banking restrictions and the need for an actual reasoned agency judgment, which this approval supplies.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218, and Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115: preserve text-first review and actual statutory limits; neither displaces permissible construction within a genuine delegation.

#### Section 92 does not defeat this annuity classification

**Controlling proposition:** The Comptroller reasonably classifies the annuity agency products covered by this approval as investment instruments outside the insurance category of 12 U.S.C. §92 for this statutory purpose, so §92 does not invalidate the approval. The Court does not decide whether §92 exclusively limits genuine insurance-agency activity to banks in qualifying small places, and it neither repeals that provision's conditions nor determines classification under a distinct state insurance law.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight of nine participating Justices directly adopt this rule and its agency-review rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments on an independent statutory ground and does not join this opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a product's label under other regulatory laws does not compel its classification under this banking provision. National Bank of Oregon establishes that §92 remains in force, but left its reach and the Comptroller's interpretation undecided. Deferred annuities can accumulate funds and permit lump-sum distribution or systematic withdrawal; variable products tie returns to selected investments. A lifetime payout option carries mortality risk, but it does not describe every use or feature of these instruments. The Comptroller therefore reasonably treated these agency sales as investment intermediation. Chevron and Clarke permit that bounded construction where Congress has not required the contrary classification. The Fifth Circuit inferred an exclusive geographic insurance prohibition from §92's specific small-place authorization and treated annuities categorically as insurance. The first inference need not be resolved because the second premise is not compelled. Section 92 begins by adding to existing powers; the Court leaves its application to true insurance sales intact. This judgment neither authorizes bank guarantees nor displaces any otherwise preserved state regulation.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America, Inc., 508 U.S. 439: applies the statutory-survival holding and respects its reservation of scope and agency-construction questions; no repeal issue is reopened.
- Chevron, 467 U.S. 837, and Clarke, 479 U.S. 388: apply permissible, reasoned construction to this statutory classification after examining the text and product features.

**Conditions and limits:** The Comptroller's approval separately requires advertising, promotional materials and customer descriptions to disclose that the annuities are not bank products and are not FDIC insured, together with a signed purchaser acknowledgment of agency status and lack of federal insurance. Brokerage does not issue, underwrite or guarantee an insurer's undertaking. Applicable banking, securities and insurance requirements otherwise remain.

When §92 applies, it requires the national bank to be located and doing business in a place of no more than 5,000 inhabitants under the last preceding decennial census; the insurer must be authorized by the State where the bank is located; and the agency must operate under Comptroller rules. It permits solicitation and sale of insurance and collection of premiums for agreed fees or commissions, but prohibits the bank from assuming or guaranteeing premium payment and separately prohibits guaranteeing the truth of the insured applicant's statements. Those statutory terms are not the same as this approval's disclosure conditions. Section 24's distinction between without-recourse, customer-order/customer-account securities transactions and own-account activity remains; no own-account investment exception is adjudicated. No individual customer's contract, universal annuity classification or separate state-law preemption dispute is resolved.

## Precedent Treatment

Clarke and Chevron govern reasonable banking-law construction within an actual delegation; Camp preserves enacted restrictions and genuine administrative reasoning. National Bank of Oregon settles §92's survival and leaves the present scope question open for decision. MCI and Gardner continue to require obedience to statutory boundaries; no general retreat from their text-first rule or from Chevron is adopted.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, the conditioned annuity agency business is permissible under the reasonable banking and classification constructions adopted by the Court. The listed banking activities do not exhaust the business of banking, but real banking bounds and express restrictions remain. Section 92's survival, small-place conditions and two prohibitions remain intact whenever that provision applies. The Court leaves broader insurance-agency geography, underwriting and separate state-law preemption unresolved. Stone-Zsela's independent statutory reading does not replace the controlling agency-review rationale.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments in both dockets. He independently reads §24 Seventh to permit brokerage of financial investment products closely connected with banking. These annuities serve retirement saving and asset management, while the bank's agency role avoids underwriting the issuer's risk. The Comptroller's expertise and reasoned explanation are persuasive confirmation of the statutory fit; deference cannot supply a power the statute withholds. He does not join the Court's agency-deference rationale.

In his reading, §92's limited insurance authorization neither compels treating these annuities as insurance nor prohibits this incidental banking activity. He would reinstate the conditioned approval subject to applicable banking, securities and insurance rules. The approval gives no general power to underwrite insurance and displaces no state regulation preserved by federal law. His independent construction supplies his judgment votes, not a replacement for the eight-Justice controlling rationale.

## Event

United States v. Mezzanatto, No. 93-1340. Argued November 2, 1994; decided January 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 998 F.2d 1452 (1993), which reversed a methamphetamine conviction and ordered a new trial because Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) categorically barred enforcement of the advance impeachment waiver. The question is whether the represented defendant could knowingly and voluntarily permit use of plea-discussion statements to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. He and counsel requested the October 17, 1991 discussion; after counsel consultation he accepted the impeachment condition. He later gave inconsistent testimony and preserved an objection to the Government's impeachment use. Render form: full. Basis: distinct waiver-proof rationales and explicit broader-use reservations.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No Justice is noted as not participating.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed, 7–2. Six Justices enforce the limited impeachment waiver under the Court's affirmative-challenge rule; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal and remand under his separate Government-proof requirement.

## Holdings

### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule forbidding admission without consent does not necessarily forbid a defendant from giving informed consent. Ricketts enforces the actual terms of a lawful agreement, while Rumery rejects categorical invalidity without dispensing with review of voluntariness and overreaching. The evidentiary Rules contain no express antiwaiver command. Their carefully limited exceptions do not themselves permit ordinary impeachment, but that does not answer the separate question of voluntary relinquishment. Congress's interest in candid negotiation deserves respect; the record does not establish that this counseled, limited agreement necessarily defeats it. Mezzanatto consulted counsel, accepted the impeachment condition, and presented no individualized claim that the acceptance was unknowing or involuntary. Predictions of universal prosecutorial abuse do not invalidate that agreement categorically. Godinez keeps competence distinct from actual informed assent, and Olano keeps intentional relinquishment distinct from failure to object. Neither supplies a universal waiver rule. The Court enforces only the agreement governing this defendant's inconsistent testimony, leaving supported validity challenges and broader uses to their own facts and legal requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Application and remedy:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver rule and the new-trial order resting on it are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the limited waiver decision; no new mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction, or sentencing ruling is ordered. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supports reversal and remand under his separately stated Government-proof requirement. Stevens and Souter would affirm the new-trial judgment. A preserved nonconstitutional error actually found remains subject to Rule 52(a), Williamson, and Kotteakos: the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt precludes harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and the ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error under Olano is not substituted for that rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

## Precedent Treatment

The Court uses Ricketts, Rumery, Brady, and Peretz within their actual consent settings, distinguishes Brooklyn Savings's particular public protection, and preserves Salerno's enacted conditions. Godinez and Olano retain separate capacity, actual-assent, and forfeiture inquiries. Williamson and Kotteakos continue to govern any preserved nonconstitutional error actually found. No universal waiver rule, ordinary nonconsensual impeachment exception, or broader-use rule is adopted.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, a knowing and voluntary advance agreement can waive Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) for impeachment of the defendant's own inconsistent testimony. Six Justices adopt enforcement absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, subject to supported individual challenges. The Chief's distinct Government-proof requirement remains noncontrolling. The Rules' express exceptions and independent evidentiary safeguards remain in force; other uses and fundamental nonwaivable protections are reserved.

## Separate Writings

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justices O'Connor and Breyer, joins the Court and emphasizes the limited issue before it. Impeachment follows the defendant's own decision to give inconsistent trial testimony. The Government did not seek to use the proffer as proof in its case in chief, a use that could affect the incentive for candid negotiations differently. Agreement with the limited waiver therefore supplies no answer about that different agreement or other broader uses. The concurrence preserves a boundary of the Court's holding, without imposing a separate public-interest burden or requiring another hearing on an unraised individual validity issue.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal and would remand under a distinct allocation of proof. A represented defendant may knowingly and voluntarily agree before the proffer that the Government may use his statements solely to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. The Government invokes that waiver and controls the proposed condition, so it must prove knowing and voluntary acceptance. Representation and competence do not settle whether the actual term was understood and freely accepted; clarity, prosecutorial threats or deception, and the circumstances of the demand matter to that inquiry. He does not presume actual coercion from bargaining or require a new hearing when the existing record permits the necessary determination. Enforcement remains subject to ordinary constitutional and evidentiary safeguards.

His ground does not approve case-in-chief or sentencing use, or a waiver effectively eliminating the right to trial. Defense counsel's cross-examination, independent defense evidence, and argument of another theory do not ordinarily trigger this personal-testimony waiver. Any broader trigger would require a separate explicit agreement and a distinct validity analysis, neither of which is decided. Existing rule-based perjury, false-statement, and comparable-abuse exceptions retain their own prerequisites. He therefore joins the judgment rejecting categorical nonwaivability while declining the Court's affirmative-challenge presumption. His Government-proof requirement is not the Court's controlling allocation.

Justice Souter, joined by Justice Stevens, would affirm the Ninth Circuit's new-trial judgment. General waiver doctrine does not resolve whether Congress protected plea discussions partly for the judicial system's benefit. Brooklyn Savings demonstrates that private consent cannot always defeat a public statutory policy, and these Rules deliberately encourage candid bargaining through enumerated, limited exceptions. Allowing an advance waiver to become the price of entering discussions can remove the protection before its function begins. Peretz's consent to allocation of a trial function and ordinary evidentiary waiver do not decide that distinct statutory design. The dissent neither denies all criminal waivers nor finds Mezzanatto personally coerced. It would sustain the preserved objection, and the lower court already found the admission nonharmless; its result is a new trial rather than acquittal or a rule of automatic reversal for every violation.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment and the retrial direction based on categorical nonwaivability are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the bounded impeachment decision. The Court orders no additional validity hearing, automatic retrial, acquittal, or sentencing change. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand with enforcement conditioned on Government proof of knowing and voluntary acceptance; Stevens and Souter would affirm the existing new-trial judgment.

## Event

McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., No. 93-1543. Argued November 2, 1994; decided January 23, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for the employer, 9 F.3d 539. The question is whether wrongdoing discovered after discharge bars every remedy for an age-discriminatory discharge and, if not, how it bears on available relief. Render form: compact. Basis: unanimous judgment and a common statutory liability-and-remedy rationale.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision. The Court has nine participating members.

## Public Action

Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

The Sixth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and the case remanded, 9–0. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer support both rejection of the complete bar and the stated remedial rule. No Justice opposes either component. The district court must adjudicate liability without treating unknown misconduct as a cause of the original discharge and determine relief under the employer’s actual-would-discharge showing and the statutory limits. No damages amount or final liability judgment is entered.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Every participating Justice joins each holding, its stated limits, and the judgment. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation

**Controlling proposition:** Misconduct unknown to the employer when it discharged an employee cannot defeat liability for an age-discriminatory discharge or categorically bar every remedy. The employee must still establish an ADEA violation under the governing liability rules; an employer’s assumption of discrimination for summary judgment is not a final liability finding.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this proposition and explanation; it is a direct majority holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The ADEA forbids discharge because of age and gives courts authority to grant relief that serves the statute’s purposes. Hazen Paper requires age actually to motivate the challenged decision and have a determinative influence on it; a reason the employer did not know cannot have motivated it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse address lawful and unlawful reasons operating in the actual decision, so their same-decision inquiry does not transform later discovery into an original cause. The Banner assumed discrimination only to obtain summary judgment and relied on documents discovered after McKennon’s dismissal. That discovery cannot erase an otherwise proved violation. The contrary rule would remove compensation for an unlawful period and weaken enforcement whenever later investigation uncovered a sufficient ground for termination. The employee’s misconduct nevertheless matters to remedy, which the next holding addresses. The Court does not find age discrimination, change the employee’s ultimate burden, or decide whether the violation was willful.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper’s actual-motive requirement remains controlling; Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse are distinguished because they concern reasons involved in the challenged decision. St. Mary’s Honor Center retains its existing proof framework and does not make rejection of an employer’s explanation an automatic liability judgment.

### Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief

**Controlling proposition:** To limit relief through after-acquired evidence, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone had it known, rather than merely showing that discharge was permissible. Once that showing is made, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate, and backpay’s starting measure is the period from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the proof requirement and bounded remedial rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 626(b) authorizes appropriate legal and equitable relief to effectuate the ADEA, which requires attention to both discrimination and the employment consequences of serious misconduct. An order requiring continued employment or its prospective monetary substitute ordinarily makes no sense when proved wrongdoing would have ended that employment lawfully. But the employer must establish what it actually would have done; an available pretext, minor infraction, or merely possible discharge does not establish that limit. Backpay ordinarily compensates the interval before actual discovery, when the later ground had not supplied a lawful termination basis. The court may account for extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests, consistently with the statutory purposes. The Banner’s affidavits and McKennon’s acknowledgment supported its motion-specific would-discharge showing, but the erroneous complete bar prevented proper adjudication of liability and relief. The Court awards no amount, imposes no reinstatement decree, and leaves statutory willfulness and other distinct remedial predicates for their proper determination.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper’s knowledge-or-recklessness standard continues to govern any claim of willfulness; this remedial ruling does not lower it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse supply no complete forfeiture rule for a reason discovered after the challenged discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine protected opposition or participation cannot supply a lawful discharge ground; this case announces no general ban on obtaining evidence of discrimination. No final discrimination, willfulness, compensation, liquidated-damages, fee, or reinstatement finding is made. Section 626(c)(1)’s termination of the individual right to bring an action when the EEOC commences an action to enforce that employee’s right remains distinct; no such agency action is established here. The 1991 Title VII amendments supply no new rule for this ADEA discharge.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins preserves actual age motivation and determinative influence as the liability inquiry and knowledge or reckless disregard as the willfulness inquiry. After-acquired evidence changes neither requirement.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle addresses actual mixed reasons for the challenged action; it does not make an unknown later reason a complete defense.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins likewise concerns contemporaneous decisionmaking and supplies no after-acquired-evidence forfeiture rule.
- St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks preserves the governing burden of persuasion and rejects automatic liability from disbelief of an employer’s explanation; this decision does not alter that allocation.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 23, 1995, later-discovered misconduct supplies a possible limitation on ADEA remedies, not erasure of an otherwise proved discriminatory discharge. The employer’s actual-would-discharge burden, ordinary limits on reinstatement and front pay, and discovery-based backpay measure with extraordinary-equity consideration govern the remand. Actual age motivation, the employee’s ultimate persuasion obligation, and the separate willfulness rule remain intact.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion was filed.

## Procedure After Action

The Sixth Circuit must return the case for proceedings consistent with the liability/remedy distinction. Liability remains to be adjudicated; any relief must rest on its statutory and factual predicates. This Court enters no amount, no final willfulness finding, and no order of reinstatement or front pay.

## Source Notes

The record and lower-court grounds appear in [9 F.3d 539](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/009/9.F3d.539.92-5917.html), reviewing 797 F. Supp. 604. The remedial authority and its qualifications appear in [29 U.S.C. §626, 1994 edition](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-1994-title29-section626&num=0&edition=1994). The motion-specific assumption of age discrimination is not a trial finding; the opinion decides the legal effect of later-discovered misconduct and leaves unresolved liability and relief to the lower courts.

## Event

Schlup v. Delo, No. 93-7901. Argued October 3, 1994; merits decision January 23, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, reviewing 11 F.3d 738, which sustained denial of a second federal habeas application without the requested evidentiary hearing. Schlup offers crime-innocence evidence as a gateway to otherwise barred ineffective-assistance and suppression claims arising from his conviction for participation in a prison murder. The questions concern the gateway quantum, the augmented evidentiary inquiry, statutory refusal authority and the proper remand. Render form: full; judgment, gateway rationale and statutory-refusal rationale have distinct coalitions and partial joins.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Eighth Circuit judgment and remands with instructions to return the matter to the District Court for proceedings under the probability crime-innocence gateway. It does not find Schlup innocent or find the gateway satisfied.

## Judgment & Remedy

The three dissenters would affirm under the stricter crime-innocence showing; Scalia and Thomas also support a bounded statutory-discretion ground. The mandate orders no automatic hearing, constitutional-merits adjudication, retrial, writ, release or stay.

## Holdings

### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the probability inquiry concerns innocence of the crime, not whether a concededly guilty person qualified for a death sentence. Carrier supplies the exceptional route where constitutional error probably convicted an innocent person; Sawyer preserves its different eligibility inquiry without deciding this crime question. McCleskey retains the innocence exception while enforcing finality through barriers to repetitive and abusive claims. The prisoner's burden remains demanding because the question concerns reasonable jurors considering the augmented evidentiary record, not the habeas judge's personal doubt. Kuhlmann's plurality discussion supports examination of all probative evidence, but its formulations are not converted into a separate majority holding. Jackson's trial-record sufficiency question cannot replace that task: evidence sufficient when credited at trial may have a different effect when reliable new evidence is considered with it. The State's request for one uniformly higher burden disregards the different question and the gateway's limited function. Passing this threshold permits further consideration within the governing claim rules; it does not award a writ or adjudicate innocence.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Operative evidentiary qualifications:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Controlling proposition:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that discretion to refuse renewed review must be exercised under the proper governing law. McCleskey explains both the statutory permission and its qualifications; neither the label second petition nor the filing of new affidavits decides the result. The earlier federal consideration of guilt-phase ineffective assistance supplies a real legal-merits proceeding, and the lower court's additional discussion of that claim remains relevant. It does not establish an independently sufficient ground disposing of every claim despite today's legal correction. The new accounts concern the meaning of the existing videotape, identification and the interval between attack and response. The lower analysis used a stricter burden and treated credibility too categorically as the original jury's task. Those features prevent accepting it as the required probability assessment. Even assuming the ultimate refusal is discretionary, reconsideration is required here; the Court need not settle the broader discretion question. Keeney and Townsend preserve necessary factual development of properly available claims, but their predicates are not established merely by this remand. The District Court must decide what further development this gateway assessment needs.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Governing statutory terms:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

**Application, reservations and remedy:** The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

Carrier supplies the crime-innocence route, while Sawyer retains its distinct clear-and-convincing eligibility rule. McCleskey's statutory qualifications, abuse pleading, cause/prejudice framework and innocence exception remain. Kuhlmann's relevant discussion retains plurality force, and Jackson's trial-record sufficiency rule remains separate. Keeney and Townsend govern necessary factual development within their actual available-claim conditions. Herrera retains only its record-specific insufficiency holding, Delo v. Blair supplies no precedent from equal division, Brecht remains a preserved-error merits decision, and McFarland supplies no automatic gateway or stay. No prior controlling holding is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 23, 1995, reliable new evidence of crime innocence is assessed with the whole probative record under the more-likely-than-not/no-reasonable-juror gateway. The prisoner retains that demanding burden and must separately establish any underlying constitutional claim. Section 2244(b)'s qualified permission, lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain; the Court decides neither a universal mandatory-review rule after a successful showing nor a general appellate standard for refusal. The judgment corrects the legal inquiry and creates no freestanding innocence remedy.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's probability gateway and all-evidence rule and concurs in vacatur and remand. Finality is weighty, but the procedural route remains available where reliable new evidence indicates that constitutional error may have convicted an innocent person. Sawyer's death-eligibility threshold answers a different question. The lower courts must evaluate the videotape, timing and eyewitness accounts together with the trial record; passage through the gateway authorizes consideration of otherwise barred constitutional claims within their governing rules, not an innocence judgment or automatic release.

He adds guidance for the reliability inquiry. The habeas court should explain the new evidence's reliability, any independent corroboration, why the evidence was unavailable at trial, whether delay impairs credibility, whether state misconduct or ineffective counsel explains its absence, and exactly how it changes the evidentiary case the jury heard. These are nonexclusive considerations relevant to reliability and probability, not additional elements, a checklist or a new diligence requirement. He does not decide the witnesses' truthfulness or shift the underlying claims' ultimate burden. He does not join the Court's separate statutory-refusal analysis or announce a general rule governing residual refusal discretion.

Justice Kennedy dissents. Justice Thomas joins his opinion in full; Justice Scalia joins its stricter-gateway rule and application, but not its alternative discussion of the probability formulation. Kennedy would require clear and convincing evidence, on the probative record as a whole, that no reasonable juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. He proposes extending Sawyer's quantum to crime innocence; he does not claim that Sawyer already decided that question. McCleskey's treatment of abusive litigation and the exceptional nature of renewed review support the more rigorous showing in his view. The continuing identifications, uncertain timing and delayed or inconsistent accounts leave the submitted evidence insufficient under that quantum. This is not a finding that every new witness lied or that any inculpatory trial evidence invariably defeats an innocence claim.

Kennedy and Thomas additionally question the Court's predictive formulation if a probability rule is used. They would adapt Jackson's reasonable-juror approach to the enlarged evidentiary record, with limited factual inquiry where the submissions indicate that the necessary innocence showing may be made. They do not substitute the unchanged trial-record-only sufficiency test, order a hearing automatically, or establish an independent failure under a correctly applied Carrier rule. Their proposed stricter rule and application would sustain the judgment; their alternative discussion does not control.

Justice Scalia also dissents in an opinion joined by Justice Thomas. Section 2244(b), in their reading, preserves real authority to decline successive or abusive review, subject to its two qualifications and review for abuse. The earlier federal legal-merits adjudication satisfies a hearing alternative despite the lack of the now-requested factual hearing. They rely on the accepted repeat/abuse posture, the actual consideration and refusal below, and the evidentiary submission to find no abuse; they also regard the stricter gateway as correct. Salinger and Sanders support consideration of prior litigation within their respective settings, while McCleskey preserves the statute's conditions and judicial limits on discretion. Wong Doo shows that an adequate independent discretionary ground can sometimes preserve a judgment despite a legal error. Scalia and Thomas do not use that proposition to declare these affidavits necessarily insufficient under a correctly applied probability rule. Their statutory rationale is additional to the stricter-standard objection, but its application here shares their conclusion that the lower court used a legally proper quantum. They recognize that misconception of law or arbitrary reasons can constitute abuse and announce no unconditional affirmance should the proper gateway later be satisfied. ## Holdings

### The complete Casey standard governs the access and facial challenge

**Controlling proposition:** Casey's complete six-step standard governs the challenged previability access duties, without Salerno's independent no-valid-applications barrier or an independent numerical facial-review threshold. Courts must identify the duty and protected baseline, function and purpose, attributable interference and the affected class, the applicable substantive protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and relief corresponding to the proved violation; finding no substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the same application of Casey; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Operative scope applied without change:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judgment used an incomplete constitutional inquiry. Casey supplies the entire sequence, including review of meaningful interference that falls short of a substantial obstacle. The Eighth Circuit's telephone construction removes an assumed statutory second visit, but does not establish that the compulsory interval adds no consequential delay or other concrete burden to the actual availability of physicians, travel, employment and appointment conditions. Those circumstances are not themselves state violations; their relevance is the additional interference caused by the duty. The substantial affidavits and depositions must be assessed under the correct rule, with evidentiary facts distinguished from unsupported conclusions. The absence of district findings alone does not require a trial. Salerno cannot make constitutional applications outside the properly identified class a conclusive answer. Nor does Casey's Pennsylvania result establish North Dakota's ultimate invalidity. The Court leaves the evidence-supported trigger, the State's responsive justification, material factual disputes and the breadth of appropriate relief for renewed assessment.

**Application and qualifications:** Telephone communication by the authorized physician or agent remains relevant; no statutory additional personal visit is found. The physician retains responsibility for the gestational determination, while an agent may obtain history and convey that determination. The patient's written certification and its receipt by the physician or agent precede the abortion, not necessarily the visit by twenty-four hours. Notice that materials are available does not require accepting, reading or endorsing them; copies must be furnished if chosen. Assistance may be available, but payment is not guaranteed. A support obligation is not a promise of collection. Statutory freedom to comment or dissociate concerns the printed materials; the lower court additionally permits comment on assistance and paternal-support statements.

Casey's qualified information rulings cannot be imported without their limits. Pennsylvania separately protects omission of information upon proof by a preponderance of a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect physical or mental health; the feared injury need not occur, and that defense does not waive the waiting period. Pennsylvania also permits, but does not require, omission of paternal-support information in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition. North Dakota's supplied text contains neither provision. Their absence requires attention to supported and preserved compulsory-information applications; it neither inserts these exceptions into North Dakota law nor establishes automatic invalidity. No independent new speech claim or parental-involvement challenge is adjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): applies the complete six-step rule, its proof allocation, broad necessary-care safeguards and tailored-relief qualification unchanged; Pennsylvania's particular interval result is not an automatic North Dakota disposition.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): does not supply an independent all-applications barrier overriding Casey in this abortion-access challenge; its operation outside this setting is not decided.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 497 U.S. 502 (1990): its rejection of speculative additional duties remains relevant, while its older facial formulation remains subject to Casey's actual qualification; a hypothetical second visit is not treated as enacted law.
### The abortion definition excludes an unintended diagnostic termination

**Controlling proposition:** Section 14-02.1-02(1), defining abortion as termination of human pregnancy with an intention other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus, identifies a purposeful pregnancy termination. Diagnostic amniocentesis followed by an unintended pregnancy loss is not brought within that definition merely because the diagnostic purpose is neither of the two stated exclusions; this reading defeats the preserved facial notice and enforcement objection without a borrowed culpability default.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part II, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight Justices directly adopt this textual ground; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the purpose language must be read with the pregnancy termination it describes. The competing reading separates that language from its object and treats every intentionally performed medical procedure as an intentional termination if pregnancy loss happens to follow. The statute does not define that category. Its two excluded purposes remain meaningful when the act being classified is a purposeful termination. X-Citement Video requires attention to the complete statutory category rather than a displaced phrase; its distinct federal knowledge rule is not transferred here. Kolender requires enforcement guidance as well as notice. The definition supplies both: an official must identify the defined termination and purpose, rather than infer them from an unwanted diagnostic result or personal disapproval. The Court does not rest on the existence of some clear applications or a requirement that every conceivable application be uncertain. It decides the stated facial objection, leaving materially different authoritative constructions and concrete enforcement challenges open.

**Limits:** This is not immunity for diagnostic treatment, negligence or other misconduct; it does not decide a completed prosecution, civil award, general knowledge-of-illegality requirement or actual-knowledge minimum for every statutory element. It does not resolve the emergency definition or every sanction attached to consent duties.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): applies its contextual definition analysis without transplanting its federal scienter construction or treating fault as a universal cure.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies the separate notice and minimum-enforcement-guidance requirements; distinguishes an undefined official compliance demand from this bounded medical act and purpose.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves its requirement to examine the particular trigger and fault separately; the present ground rests on the category's own words rather than fault addressed to a different element.
### The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion

**Controlling proposition:** The preserved facial vagueness challenge to section 14-02.1-02(7) fails because its specified outcomes and timing, assessed through the physician's prospective best clinical judgment, give notice and enforcement guidance: the pregnancy must require immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay must create grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Gravity, immediacy and major bodily function call for a professional forecast, not certainty, a numerical prognosis or waiting until the threatened harm occurs; this holding does not adjudicate the distinct claim that the exception's substantive breadth fails to protect particular necessary care.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight Justices directly adopt the bounded clarity and enforcement ground; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the identified medical consequences and time relationship constrain the judgment demanded of the physician and the officials enforcing it. Vuitch and Doe recognize that intelligible professional judgments can guide conduct without a catalogue of every emergency. Their broader health constructions do not replace North Dakota's enacted criteria. Colautti requires separate examination of the trigger and culpability; it does not establish that these qualitative terms are unintelligible merely because doctors may disagree. Unlike the conflicting formulations and uncertain priorities there, this wording identifies the forecast to be made. Kolender likewise requires enforcement boundaries. An official must address the pregnancy complication, specified consequences and prospective clinical assessment, rather than invent a condition or substitute opposition to abortion. The Court neither transforms best clinical judgment into bad-faith-only liability nor treats a physician's assertion as conclusive. Whether particular necessary care falls outside the exception, or a materially different construction or enforcement violates the Constitution, remains a separate question.

**Limits and retained safeguards:** Clarity is not a ruling that this restricted emergency category satisfies every Casey life-and-health requirement. Casey's broader necessary-care protection, including individualized physical and mental health and care before catastrophic deterioration, remains law. The emergency undue-burden claim was not pressed below and is not decided here. The federal appellate significant-threat construction is not enacted text or an authoritative North Dakota Supreme Court construction; it does not license deleting immediate, irreversible or major from the statute. The present notice ground stands independently of that broader construction and of any presumed default fault rule.

Section 14-02.1-03(1)'s emergency exception excuses that subsection's ordinary requirements, but requires an explanation of the medical indications before the abortion if possible and written certification of those indications. The conditional explanation is no advance-permission requirement, and certification supplies no authority to delay necessary treatment. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish all elements of a criminal offense or civil damages; disagreement may remain relevant under actual applicable law. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct are not immunized.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971): applies the intelligibility of professional judgment at the stated scope; its D.C. statute's life-or-health construction and prosecution burden do not become North Dakota law.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973): applies its recognition of clinical judgment without importing Georgia's broader statutory construction or creating professional immunity.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): distinguishes its conflicting viability and treatment duties, while retaining independent scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability for error.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies minimum enforcement guidelines and distinguishes undefined official satisfaction from the stated clinical criteria; no universal all-applications bar is adopted.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): preserves broad necessary-care safeguards and conditional emergency explanation; its Pennsylvania construction was not a separate emergency-vagueness holding and does not determine this distinct claim.
### A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself

**Controlling proposition:** Section 12.1-02-02 alone does not establish default willfulness for the silent misdemeanor clause of section 14-02.1-11, and neither that default nor the latter section's separately expressed willful rule-or-regulation infraction supplies the criminal or civil elements of every chapter violation. Rejection of that additional rationale does not defeat the independently sufficient definition holdings or establish facial invalidity of the general penalty merely because its first sentence lacks an express culpability term; actual element-specific fault and liability remain undecided.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part IV, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight Justices adopt this limited rejection of the penalty theory and preserve the stated element-specific questions. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would remand the affected definitions and enforcement questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the additional culpability rationale must be separated from the sufficient grounds for rejecting the two wording objections. City of Dickinson v. Mueller and North Dakota Education Association limit the default criminal-code provision to Title 12.1. Goetz concerns an outside-title law already using a willfulness term and does not abolish that limitation. The Eighth Circuit therefore could not establish every relevant Title 14 element merely by citing the default. The abortion definition nevertheless contains its own purpose language, and the emergency definition supplies the bounded clinical criterion already discussed. Hoffman Estates recognizes that scienter may mitigate uncertainty, especially notice; it does not make a missing express fault word invariably fatal or cure an otherwise undefined enforcement demand. Nor does a criminal term automatically become a civil-damages defense. The Court supplies no comprehensive state culpability rule, strict-liability conclusion, bad-faith requirement or burden of proof. Any actual liability must be supported under the law governing its particular elements.

**Limits:** The misdemeanor clause covers a chapter violation for which no other penalty is specifically prescribed. The distinct infraction clause concerns a willful violation of a promulgated rule or regulation. The latter's express term does not amend the former. The independent civil remedy provides $10,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an abortion without the required informed consent, and $5,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an attempted abortion without compliance. The Court enters no liability determination or award under either provision and creates no civil scienter defense. No separate facial judgment on an unpresented civil-damages theory is entered.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, 455 U.S. 489 (1982): applies contextual notice, enforcement and scienter principles, with greater clarity required for criminal sanctions and protected conduct; its commercial all-applications formulation does not govern without its premise.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves the separation of an intelligible clinical standard from fault concerning an erroneous assessment; no particular universal fault level is supplied.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): preserves its context-specific distinction between definition and fault; no universal knowledge element or scienter cure is inferred.
- City of Dickinson v. Mueller, 261 N.W.2d 787 (N.D. 1977), and State v. North Dakota Education Association, 262 N.W.2d 731 (N.D. 1978): apply their title limitation when assessing the asserted default; no abortion-specific state construction is attributed to either case.
- State v. Goetz, 312 N.W.2d 1 (N.D. 1981): distinguishes definition of an expressly enacted willfulness term from importing default fault; its securities-law instruction and knowledge-of-illegality discussion do not settle these elements.
### The access claims return for a supported assessment and prompt interim consideration

**Controlling proposition:** The access portion of the Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated, and that court must remand for the District Court to apply Casey to the challenged access duties on a properly supported record. The District Court must promptly consider the providers' request for interim protection under the applicable ordinary standard and current record; this Court neither reinstates the dissolved injunction automatically nor orders a final injunction, hearing or trial.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part V, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer as to the access remand and interim-consideration direction. The Chief would additionally vacate the definition and associated penalty dispositions; the affirmance of those components rests on Parts II–IV's eight-Justice coalition, not his join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that correcting the governing legal inquiry requires renewed assessment rather than immediate final relief. The lower courts must identify what the record establishes under the proper causal and substantive standards and what genuine disputes require further proceedings. Casey requires a remedy corresponding to the duty or application that fails, while Transcon distinguishes remedial authority from a demonstrated violation and the fit of an injunction. Neither decision supplies the absent factual and equitable predicates here. The pre-enforcement penal setting warrants prompt attention to interim protection, but the prior injunction's dissolution is not undone by vacatur alone. The District Court must evaluate present grounds and the scope of any necessary restraint. Its final remedy must preserve independently lawful duties under applicable severability law and address certifications and sanctions dependent on an invalid prerequisite. The Court leaves that work below without prejudging ultimate validity, the need for testimony or the precise terms of relief.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): applies Step 6's matched relief and preserves its distinction between an actual duty and assumed administration; its case-specific denial of additional relief is no universal bar.
- Interstate Commerce Commission v. Transcon Lines, decided January 10, 1995: applies the distinction among remedial authority, violation and scope by analogy, without transferring its admitted public-enforcement violation or statutory injunction predicates.

**Questions not reached:** Final statewide invalidation, the ultimate State justification, disputed factual burdens, a definitive state severance, actual criminal or civil culpability, damages, a compelled evidentiary hearing, and a new parental, speech or emergency-access claim are not decided. Affirmance of the specified vagueness grounds does not approve a materially different state construction or concrete enforcement.

## Precedent Treatment

Casey's complete standard and broader life-and-health safeguards remain unchanged. The access decision applies its affected-population, causal-comparison, residual-interference and relief requirements; Salerno supplies no additional all-applications veto in this setting. The definition decisions apply Vuitch, Doe, Hoffman Estates, Colautti and Kolender at their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes, with X-Citement's contextual approach. No earlier Supreme Court decision is overruled. The North Dakota decisions establish the limitation of the asserted default source, not a comprehensive abortion-specific fault rule. Each precise treatment and limitation appears with its holding.

## Law After Decision

Effective February 13, 1995, North Dakota's challenged access duties require renewed assessment under the full six-step Casey standard, without an independent numerical facial-review threshold or automatic approval based on telephone delivery or similarity to another State's law. The Court separately rejects the stated abortion-definition and emergency-definition vagueness claims on the bounded grounds above and rejects automatic facial invalidity of the general penalty from the absence of an express fault word. Those holdings do not establish a general default culpability rule, immunize clinicians, validate every emergency restriction or decide civil damages. The remand creates no final injunction or categorical ruling on every twenty-four-hour period.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** The Chief agrees that Casey governs the access challenge and that the actual affected class, the additional burden against existing access conditions, and interacting requirements must be assessed under the complete six-step inquiry. He joins the access remand and prompt consideration of interim protection. He would, however, vacate the entire Eighth Circuit judgment and return each challenged provision for application and legally supported construction, rather than affirm the definition and associated penalty dispositions now.

For the Chief, a practically usable route must permit prompt action in a physician's good-faith clinical judgment when compliance creates a material risk of death, serious physical injury, substantial irreversible impairment or another grave medically established danger to health. Those examples do not narrow Casey's broader necessary-care protection, including relevant physical and mental health. Treatment cannot await approach to death or delay incompatible with the emergency. A protective construction must be fairly supported by the enacted words and controlling state law; the courts must establish the actual culpability requirements rather than presume them. If the text cannot bear the necessary construction, the defective requirement may not be enforced. He would obtain that assessment on remand. He creates neither immunity for negligent practice nor an emergency based on unsupported preference, and would not impose a categorical injunction without the necessary findings. His additional remand position does not control the affirmed components.

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Kennedy joins the independent definition and limited penalty holdings. He distinguishes the intelligibility of professional criteria from a ruling that every possible restriction on necessary care is constitutional. He would also affirm the access judgment under the reasonable-regulation position he stated in Casey, retaining protected choice and meaningful clinical safeguards but rejecting the general residual lesser-burden substantiation rule. Telephone disclosure, authorized agents, actual medical judgment and certification during the procedure visit distinguish the enacted duties from a mandatory second trip. On his approach, truthful information and a finite interval bear a reasonable relationship to informed decisionmaking, without an established third-party veto or concrete denial of necessary care on the preserved access claim. His view supplies no alternative rule for lower courts to substitute for the Court's controlling framework. He would deny restoration of the injunction and does not join the remand merely because lower courts retain ordinary remedial authority under the Court's mandate.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the definition and limited penalty portions because their grounds arise from the actual purposeful-termination words and specified professional criterion, independently of a judicially supplied fault term. They do not adopt a universal scienter cure, a bad-faith-only safe harbor or a rule that statutory silence invariably creates strict liability. They would affirm the access dismissal on their continuing constitutional objection to Casey's heightened abortion-access framework and the ordinary reasonable-regulation ground they supported there. At the actual telephone-and-agent scope, they regard consent information and the interval as permissible legislative regulation rather than a basis for the Court's residual scrutiny. This remains a dissent from governing law, not a finding that the Eighth Circuit performed the six-step inquiry. Their votes neither endorse misleading information or ideological assent nor permit invented criminal elements. They oppose the access remand and requested restoration of interim relief.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit must preserve the affirmed rejection of the specified definition-vagueness and limited penalty grounds and return the access claims to the District Court. The District Court must apply the complete Casey standard to each challenged access duty on a properly supported record, identify its actual burdened class, assess attributable additional interference against real conditions, and consider interacting requirements together without double counting. It must promptly consider the providers' interim-relief request under the ordinary standard and current record, given the pre-enforcement penal posture. No existing injunction is automatically reinstated, and no hearing, trial, final severance, permanent injunction or damages award is ordered. Supreme Court review ends; the access proceeding and lawful remedial work continue below. The affirmed grounds do not foreclose a materially different authoritative construction or concrete enforcement challenge.

## Holdings

### The Court may consider this included legal argument despite its earlier disavowal

**Controlling proposition:** The Court elects to consider Amtrak's governmental-entity status despite Lebron's earlier express disavowal of that argument because it supplies a legal basis for the same preserved First Amendment claim, falls within the governmental-actor question presented, and can be resolved on the established institutional record without a new factual theory or additional findings. This bounded exercise of review creates no obligation to consider every abandoned argument and does not dispense with limits on distinct claims, questions, requested relief, factual stipulations, or missing material facts.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's reachability portion, joined by Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt the stated discretionary-review rationale; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela reaches the question but does not join this portion, and Justice O'Connor declines the argument as a prudential matter.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the earlier litigation choice deserves consideration but does not require refusal of this legal argument. Yee separates a new supporting argument from a distinct claim and independently enforces the question presented. Lebron's injury, constitutional claim, rejected advertisement, and requested relief remain the same. The question before the Court expressly concerns governmental status despite the statutory disclaimer, and the established corporate structure permits its resolution without new factual findings. Bray withheld a separate statutory claim outside the granted dispute and lacking a necessary finding; those defects are absent here. Deliberate disavowal is more substantial than mere omission, but the Court exercises its authority to decide this included legal basis on the present record. The appellate reliance on Amtrak's private status makes that premise relevant without establishing a rule that any passing discussion preserves every argument. No actual adversarial prejudice or broader concession is found, and the Court imposes no burden on the respondent to prove prejudice.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido, 503 U.S. 519 (1992): applies its distinction between a supporting argument and a new claim, together with its independent question-presented limit; the present decision separately addresses deliberate disavowal in this setting.
- Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic, 506 U.S. 263 (1993): distinguishes the unadjudicated separate hindrance theory, outside the granted questions and lacking a necessary finding; its scope limits remain operative.
- Bankers Life & Casualty Co. v. Crenshaw, 486 U.S. 71 (1988): applies, through Yee, the allowance of supporting arguments for a preserved federal claim, without erasing this Court's discretion over deliberately excluded grounds.
- Kamen v. Kemper Financial Services, Inc., 500 U.S. 90 (1991): preserves the distinction between legal arguments and the claim to which they relate; no unrelated claim is introduced.
- United States v. Mezzanatto, decided January 18, 1995: distinguishes the actual, limited evidentiary agreement there from this decision whether to consider an included argument; its waiver rule does not make every litigation choice an irrevocable surrender of an entire constitutional claim.

### Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental

**Controlling proposition:** When the Federal Government creates a corporation by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains authority to appoint a majority of its governing directors, the corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes notwithstanding a statutory declaration that it is not a governmental agency. Amtrak satisfies those combined conditions; ordinary operating discretion in its corporate officers does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight Justices directly support the same permanent-majority-appointment rule and its application; Justice O'Connor's conditional view of a question she declines to reach is not a join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress cannot settle constitutional responsibility simply by choosing a corporate label. Amtrak was specially created to pursue national transportation objectives, and the statutory arrangement permanently retains governmental authority over selection of its governing majority. The Secretary's position, the five other direct Presidential selections, and the preferred shareholder's governmental selections must not be mistaken for nine direct Presidential appointments. The different routes nevertheless establish the required enduring majority authority; the corporate president's selection within the institution does not defeat it. Congress's nonagency classification keeps its statutory operation, but cannot place an institution created and governed in this manner beyond the Constitution. San Francisco Arts & Athletics distinguishes a federally chartered and assisted private corporation; charter, subsidy, or regulation alone is not the ground here. Nor does delegation of everyday decisions to corporate officers make this institution private. An external federal order to reject Lebron's work is therefore unnecessary. The Court decides governmental status, leaving the advertising policy's constitutionality and the suitable remedy for later adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:**

- San Francisco Arts & Athletics, Inc. v. United States Olympic Committee, 483 U.S. 522 (1987): distinguishes federal charter and assistance to a private institution from special creation coupled with permanent governmental majority-appointment authority.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 (1991), and Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992): preserve attention to actual governmental legal machinery in their jury-selection settings; neither makes billboard advertising an exclusively governmental function or supplies a general rule for all publicly benefited corporations.
- Jackson v. Metropolitan Edison Co., 419 U.S. 345 (1974), and Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991 (1982): distinguish attribution of a private entity's particular act from identifying an institution that is itself governmental under the combined rule.
- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., decided November 14, 1994: keeps the distinct inquiry into an entity's state sovereign immunity separate; absence of immunity does not imply constitutional privacy.
- American National Red Cross v. S. G., 505 U.S. 247 (1992): preserves its express charter-jurisdiction rule, which supplies no constitutional actor classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal charter, subsidy, regulation, governmental property, public benefit, a single appointment, temporary investment, or merely the current ownership of shares is not independently sufficient under this rule. Permanent authority to appoint the governing majority, governmental objectives, and special governmental creation must be present together. The Court decides no universal agency status, sovereign immunity, Treasury obligation, federal tort-liability rule, or charter jurisdiction. The 1994 recodification from former 45 U.S.C. §541 to 49 U.S.C. §24301(a) does not itself adjudicate constitutional status. The statutory board includes the Secretary of Transportation; Amtrak's president; three Presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed directors representing the statutory labor, state-executive, and business categories; two Presidential selections from commuter-authority nominees under the statute; and two preferred-shareholder selections. The governmental preferred shareholder supplies those last two selections on this record. Neither the entire board's consideration of this particular refusal nor a new direct federal order to reject it is found.

### Governmental status requires a remand, not an immediate display order

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal of the Second Circuit's governmental-action ground restores the First Amendment claim for adjudication with Amtrak treated as governmental, but does not establish that this rejection was unconstitutional or that Lebron is entitled to mandatory display. The lower courts must address the unreviewed speech and forum questions, lawful relief, and the related disposition of the contract claim and TDI counterclaim under their own governing rules; the district display injunction is not automatically reinstated.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's remand portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight Justices adopt the same threshold reversal and limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that governmental responsibility identifies the institution's constitutional duties without deciding whether the challenged restriction violates them. The Court of Appeals stopped at attribution and did not adjudicate the First Amendment merits. The district court's contrary decision and mandatory-display order therefore cannot be restored merely by correcting that threshold. The character of the advertising forum, the grounds and operation of the speech restriction, and the conditions for equitable relief remain separate questions. Young preserves the distinction between the existence of substantial federal jurisdiction and the legal defeat of a federal claim; restoration of the claim accordingly requires reconsideration of associated state-law dispositions without prescribing their answers. Tome illustrates only the limited principle that correcting one ground does not automatically establish ultimate relief; its hearsay and criminal-error rules do not govern this civil remand. No damages amount, contract entitlement, forum classification, or validity of a substitute advertising policy is determined here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church, decided January 17, 1995: applies the distinction between statutory jurisdiction and legal defeat on the federal merits without extending its ministerial-selection defense.
- Tome v. United States, decided January 10, 1995: uses only its separation of threshold legal error from automatic ultimate relief by analogy; no evidentiary or criminal harmless-error standard is transferred.

**Remedial limits:** The Court awards no display, damages, final contractual relief, or adjudication of TDI's requested declaration permitting termination. The Second Circuit's dismissal directions must be reconsidered consistently with the restored federal claim and ordinary law governing the separate state-law issues. The lower courts may not treat this actor-status ruling as a merits judgment approving the advertisement or invalidating every political-advertising policy.

## Precedent Treatment

Yee and Bray preserve the distinction between a legal supporting argument, a separate claim, and the actual question presented; the Court exercises bounded discretion over this deliberately disavowed argument. San Francisco Arts & Athletics, Jackson, and Blum retain their private-actor scope. Edmonson and McCollum concern governmental jury machinery. ## Public Action

## Judgment & Remedy

## Holdings

### The assumed payment date fixes the computational starting point

**Controlling proposition:** Under 29 U.S.C. §1399(c)(1)(A)(i), the withdrawal-liability installment calculation uses the first day of the plan year after the withdrawal year as the assumed first-payment and computational-interest starting point; it does not capitalize interest for the preceding withdrawal year. Actual payment commencement, overdue-payment interest, default acceleration, prepayment, and mass-withdrawal rules retain their separate statutory triggers and qualifications.

**Authority:** Breyer’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1399 expressly separates an assumed payment schedule from the actual commencement of payments. Its calculation directs the first assumed payment to the opening of the plan year after withdrawal and each later payment to the opening of a successive year. Amortization includes interest, but that word alone does not answer when this statutory obligation is treated as outstanding for computation. The Plan’s earlier valuation date measures the withdrawal amount; it does not authorize an additional year of interest while the employer was still contributing. Brown v. Gardner requires attention to enacted conditions before adding a condition the text does not supply. Concrete Pipe’s separate treatment of facts, actuarial assumptions, and legal rules likewise leaves this statutory date for judicial construction. The Plan’s appeal to complete actuarial equivalence cannot displace Congress’s explicit assumptions. The Court resolves the demanded additional year, without recalculating the agreed amount, reopening the allocation method, or deciding a distinct default or collection-delay controversy.

**Precedent treatment:** Concrete Pipe and Products of California, Inc. v. Construction Laborers Pension Trust retains its independent arbitration, factual-review, and actuarial-review rules; this legal date question changes none of their burdens. Brown v. Gardner’s attention to statutory text and context is applied without creating a broader agency-deference rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** When the calculated payment period exceeds twenty years, ordinary liability is limited to the first twenty annual payments. That cap does not apply when the plan terminates through withdrawal of every employer or withdrawal of substantially all employers pursuant to an agreement or arrangement to withdraw. Actual payments begin no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal; the assumed date permits no delay. Payments are quarterly unless the plan specifies other intervals, and overdue-payment interest runs from the due date to payment. Prepayment of outstanding annual payments and accrued interest is allowed without penalty, subject to redetermination if the withdrawal later qualifies under the mass-withdrawal rule. After statutory default, acceleration may include interest from the first untimely payment’s due date; nonpayment must remain uncured sixty days after written notice, while other events defined in adopted plan rules must indicate a substantial likelihood that the employer will be unable to pay its withdrawal liability. The prevailing-market-rate rule is distinct from the amortization assumptions. No mass-withdrawal, cap, default, independent partial-year-demand, or agency-litigation-deference question is decided here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Concrete Pipe and Products of California, Inc. v. Construction Laborers Pension Trust retains its distinction between legal interpretation, factual rebuttal, and the statutory routes for challenging actuarial assumptions. No actuarial presumption answers the legal starting-date question.
- Brown v. Gardner supplies attention to statutory text and context before adding a requirement; its holding creates no new pension amount or categorical rule about agency litigation positions.
- John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank leaves the general treatment of agency litigation positions unresolved. This decision does not resolve that reserved issue.

The prior-year valuation date supplies no authority to add a year of interest to the withdrawal amount. Statutory actual-payment, delinquency, prepayment, and default provisions continue to operate independently; Concrete Pipe’s review rules remain unchanged.

## Holdings

### Present ripeness after loss of required federal approval

**Holding and operative rule:** A constitutional challenge to this approval-dependent benefit program is not ripe when the necessary federal authorization has been removed and no replacement authorization is established. The mere possibility of a new approval does not permit adjudication of the constitutionality of a program whose operative terms remain contingent.

**Authority:** The Court's per curiam opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; all nine adopt this threshold rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the present controversy cannot support a constitutional judgment. California law makes the necessary federal approval a condition of this benefit provision's operation. Beno removed that approval, and no replacement has been established. Regional Rail Reorganization Act Cases requires assessment of ripeness when the Court acts; Hall v. Beals prevents a decision based on conjectural future events. California's request for administrative reconsideration does not establish what program could lawfully operate or what terms a new approval might impose. Church of Scientology preserves a controversy when meaningful partial relief remains available, but this record identifies no comparable continuing effect that a constitutional ruling could remedy. The presence of a statute and a class including future recipients cannot substitute for the missing implementation prerequisite. **Precedent treatment:** Regional Rail Reorganization Act Cases is applied to present ripeness. Hall v. Beals is applied to conjectural future controversies. ## Holdings

### The Constitution does not require numerical weight for advisory jury sentencing advice

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eighth Amendment does not require a State to prescribe a numerical or mathematical weight for a jury's advisory sentencing recommendation. That proposition does not resolve whether judicial selection remains adequately guided and reviewable, whether relevant mitigation was considered, or whether a distinct substantive constraint on overriding life advice is constitutionally required.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Part I is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this limited proposition; the two sentence-relief votes do not convert it into a holding that this sentence is valid.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional sentencing safeguards cannot be reduced to a required numerical allocation for advisory votes. Tuilaepa and Proctor distinguish relevant individualized selection from a mathematical formula for moral judgment. Lockett and Eddings require that qualifying mitigation remain available for consideration, but they do not assign a numerical value to each reason for mercy. Alabama requires its judge to consider the jury's recommendation and leaves the recommendation nonbinding; those provisions must be read together. The absence of a specified number therefore does not establish arbitrariness by itself. The contrary concern has force in a different form: a State cannot avoid examination of an independently alleged selection error simply by calling its jury advisory. Whether this record sufficiently explains and channels the choice, and whether a substantive override constraint is necessary, remain distinct questions addressed by the separate positions below. No Justice's agreement on arithmetic is counted as agreement that every advisory-jury scheme or this death sentence is adequate.

**Precedent treatment:** Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, June 30, 1994, are applied for relevant individualized selection without fixed numerical weights, preserving their limits on forbidden considerations. Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, retain effective consideration of relevant mitigation; neither is converted into a numerical scheme.

### Alabama’s consideration rule and this reviewed sentencing record satisfy the admitted challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Where Alabama requires consideration of an unwaived advisory recommendation but assigns final sentencing to the judge, the Constitution does not additionally require Tedder's particular substantive override standard. On Harris's actual individualized findings and appellate review, the absence of a prescribed weight or separate comparative formula for rejecting the life recommendation establishes no constitutional error; this ruling does not excuse disregard of the recommendation, mitigation or a proved independent sentencing defect.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Part II, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, has seven votes for the rule, application and affirmance. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela disputes this record's legal adequacy; Justice Stevens requires the distinct Tedder protection. Their agreement with Part I does not join Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this sentence does not fail the admitted constitutional challenge. Spaziano permits a State to place final capital selection with a judge notwithstanding advisory life advice; its discussion of Florida's safeguards did not impose Tedder on every State. Section 13A-5-47(e) commands consideration of the unwaived advisory recommendation presented here while making that recommendation nonbinding; the conditions for waiver are not decided. The judge described the recommendation, the pecuniary-gain aggravator, the absence of significant criminal history, other mitigation and Harris's role in arranging the killing. The lower courts examined the mitigation wording, independently weighed the circumstances and reviewed proportionality and arbitrary influence. The Court rejects the argument that the absence of a separate prescribed amount of deference makes those actual findings constitutionally meaningless. Espinosa concerns defective advice with a legally consequential role, while Romano concerns a different distortion of sentencing responsibility; neither defect is established here. This is a no-error decision, not harmlessness based merely on the judge's title or the existence of a valid aggravator.

**Precedent treatment:** Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447, is applied to judicial final sentencing; Tedder v. State, 322 So. 2d 908, is not made a federal constitutional command. Espinosa v. Florida, June 29, 1992, retains its rule concerning invalid-factor advice carrying required great weight, without deciding every Alabama advice error. Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994, retains its contextual protection against an irrelevant separate death decision distorting responsibility. Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, continues to require a lawful cure for an invalid aggravator; no such error is found on this question. Tuilaepa/Proctor preserves independent selection-error review.

## Precedent Treatment

Spaziano continues to permit a judicial final capital sentencer; the Court declines to impose Florida's Tedder standard on Alabama. Tuilaepa/Proctor supplies rejection of mathematical weighting without erasing review of independent constitutional errors. Lockett and Eddings preserve meaningful consideration of mitigation. Espinosa remains applicable to its invalid-factor and legally consequential advice setting; advisory status is not an automatic cure. Romano retains its rule concerning distorted sentencing responsibility from an irrelevant separate death decision. Stringer retains its lawful-cure rule when invalid aggravators infect weighing. No precedent is overruled, and no harmless-error holding excuses a defect in this case.

## Law After Decision

Effective February 22, 1995, a State need not assign numerical weight to advisory sentencing advice. Alabama's required-consideration, nonbinding-recommendation arrangement does not additionally require Tedder, and Harris's actual findings and review survive this admitted challenge. The rule does not authorize ignoring advice, excluding mitigation, using an invalid aggravator or insulating a demonstrated independent selection error. The seven-Justice sufficiency holding, not the two distinct proposed protections, governs. No broader immunity for post-eligibility discretion is adopted.

## Holdings

### Vessel-caused land injury satisfies admiralty locality through ordinary proximate causation

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 28 U.S.C. §1333(1) and the Admiralty Extension Act, damage consummated on land satisfies admiralty's location requirement when it is proximately caused by a vessel on navigable waters. The alleged damage caused by barge-based piling equipment to the underwater tunnel meets that requirement at this jurisdictional stage; neither a fixed distance nor a fixed interval between the vessel operation and land flooding replaces the ordinary causal inquiry.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, has five votes, more than the four required on this seven-member Court. Justices Thomas and Scalia agree that vessel causation and locality support jurisdiction but join the judgment on their separate locality rule, not this Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that landfall of the injury does not defeat admiralty when the statutory vessel connection exists. The Admiralty Extension Act reaches injury caused by a vessel on navigable water even though the damage is completed on land. Gutierrez gives effect to that extension rather than preserving the former water's-edge limitation through a new verbal formula. Here the transport-capable barge and its attached piling equipment supply the alleged vessel instrumentality; the complaint connects the operation to damage to an underwater tunnel and the resulting flood. The city basements where losses accumulated do not erase that causal allegation. The objection based on elapsed time or physical distance is properly tested through ordinary proximate causation, not an invented numerical cutoff. This is a jurisdictional determination on the presented record, not a final finding that Great Lakes negligently caused any particular loss. Ordinary procedures for resolving disputed jurisdictional facts remain available. Nor does the conclusion classify every floating object as a vessel or every land injury near a waterway as maritime.

**Precedent treatment:** Gutierrez v. Waterman Steamship Corp., 373 U.S. 206, supplies the Extension Act's reach to vessel-caused land harm. Executive Jet Aviation v. Cleveland, 409 U.S. 249, and Sisson v. Ruby, 497 U.S. 358, preserve a separate maritime-connection inquiry; satisfying locality alone does not displace that inquiry in the Court's framework.

### This underwater-structure incident and vessel-based repair activity satisfy Sisson

**Holding and operative rule:** The maritime-connection requirement examines the potential effect on maritime commerce of the general type of incident and the substantial relationship between the activity giving rise to it and traditional maritime activity. Damage by a vessel on navigable water to an underwater structure has the required potential, and repair or maintenance of waterway structures performed from a vessel has the required relationship; the actual closure of navigation corroborates this application but is not a universal prerequisite.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part II is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. These five directly adopt both connection inquiries and their application. Justices Thomas and Scalia reject imposing the additional inquiries as necessary conditions in this vessel setting.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the incident and activity must be described at a level that preserves their relevant maritime features. Sisson asks about the potential disruption associated with the general type of incident, not only the final place of loss or the precise sequence of one accident. Damage by a vessel to an underwater structure may interfere with navigation; the resulting interruption of river traffic here confirms that the description is neither fanciful nor disconnected from the event. Sisson and Foremost also require a substantial traditional maritime relationship. Vessel-based repair or maintenance of structures in a navigable waterway supplies that relationship without requiring that every purpose of every piling be exclusively maritime. Describing the occurrence solely as flooded city basements suppresses the alleged vessel casualty, while calling the activity merely vessel activity would make the second inquiry empty. The Court rejects both extremes and an open-ended policy-factor substitute. It reserves final negligence and causation, and it does not make all bridge work or every urban flood a maritime tort.

**Precedent treatment:** Sisson v. Ruby, 497 U.S. 358, is applied to general incident characterization and substantial maritime activity. Foremost Insurance Co. v. Richardson, 457 U.S. 668, preserves admiralty's concern beyond collisions between commercial vessels. Executive Jet's rejection of locality alone remains part of the Court's framework; the separate writings' simpler rule does not replace it.

### An alleged maritime proximate cause is not erased by a contributing nonmaritime actor

**Holding and operative rule:** For the activity branch of Sisson, the substantial maritime relationship is satisfied when at least one alleged tortfeasor's relevant maritime activity is an alleged proximate cause of the incident; a contributing actor's nonmaritime conduct does not erase that connection. This rule establishes jurisdiction over the connected maritime claim, not automatic jurisdiction over every joined party or independent claim, and it decides neither liability nor entitlement to statutory limitation.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Part III, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, supplies five direct votes. The two locality-route Justices concur in the jurisdictional judgment without joining this application of Sisson's activity inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that adding a nonmaritime contributor does not remove an otherwise sufficient maritime cause of the incident. Sisson left mixed instrumentalities for another case; the Court now answers the relevant activity question where an alleged vessel operation proximately contributes to the casualty. Great Lakes' piling activity supplies the maritime connection even though Chicago's alleged disclosure, maintenance or repair failures may also have contributed. Requiring each actor independently to perform maritime activity would make jurisdiction over the vessel claim disappear upon identifying a land-based cause of the same incident. Conversely, merely joining a vessel owner cannot create jurisdiction over unrelated land conduct. Ordinary supplemental-jurisdiction and impleader requirements remain applicable to claims that need them. American Dredging preserves the distinction between admiralty competence and an exclusively federal forum. The Seventh Circuit's reversal permits the limitation proceeding to continue, but jurisdiction is not exoneration, a limitation cap or a finding about corporate privity, knowledge or a personal contractual promise. Those issues remain for their proper proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Sisson's express mixed-activity reservation is resolved only for the causally connected incident described here. American Dredging Co. v. Miller, February 23, 1994, preserves saving-to-suitors and competent-state-forum distinctions without altering admiralty jurisdiction. McDermott, Inc. v. AmClyde and Boca Grande Club v. Florida Power & Light, April 20, 1994, retain their defined settlement/contribution rules, not a general jurisdictional or limitation entitlement.

## Precedent Treatment

The Admiralty Extension Act is applied through ordinary proximate causation, consistent with Gutierrez. Sisson remains the governing location-and-connection framework; its mixed-activity reservation is resolved only for an alleged maritime proximate cause of the connected incident. Foremost's treatment of maritime activity is not confined to commercial-vessel collisions. Executive Jet's objection to locality alone remains in the Court's framework. American Dredging preserves saving-to-suitors and state-forum distinctions; Gizoni, McDermott and Boca Grande retain their distinct status, benefits and settlement rules. No precedent is overruled, and the locality concurrence does not replace the five-Justice test.

## Law After Decision

Effective February 22, 1995, alleged proximate vessel causation can satisfy locality for land damage without fixed temporal or distance limits. The described underwater-structure incident and vessel-based repair activity meet Sisson. A causally contributing maritime actor suffices for the activity branch despite another actor's nonmaritime contribution; mere joinder does not create jurisdiction over unrelated claims. The five-Justice rationale directly controls on the seven-member Court. Admiralty jurisdiction remains distinct from negligence, liability, limitation, exclusive federal adjudication and any independent jurisdictional basis for other claims.

## Separate Writings

A conclusion that one alleged maritime activity proximately contributed to the incident does not automatically bring every other cause of action or party within admiralty. Other claims may require a proper basis in supplemental jurisdiction, impleader or another source, with those sources' own limits. The Court's refusal to let a nonmaritime contributing actor erase the vessel claim is not the converse proposition that adding a vessel owner makes all connected litigation maritime. They regard those inquiries' changing levels of generality as creating avoidable uncertainty and litigation about descriptions rather than providing a stable jurisdictional boundary. ## Holdings

### A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden

**Holding and operative rule:** When the Government broadly restricts future compensated expression by its employees acting as citizens, Pickering requires a justification commensurate with the restriction's actual reach and burden on employees and listeners. Section 501(b)'s honoraria ban is unconstitutional as applied to the represented lower-level employees' protected expression unrelated to their employment because the record does not adequately connect that burden to actual or apparent misuse of office; narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and explanation. Justice O'Connor agrees with invalidity for unrelated expression on her separate, case-specific Pickering ground; her vote is not counted for this prospective-burden formulation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Government must justify the speech burden it has imposed. Pickering balances the employee's interest as a citizen against the Government's interests as employer; Connick does not confine public concern to workplace controversy. A nationwide restriction on compensation discourages expression before any particular disruption and affects the audiences who would receive it. Waters addresses an employer's account of particular speech and does not establish the validity of this prospective prohibition. Congress may prevent corruption and its appearance without awaiting a completed exchange, and its predictive judgments deserve substantial consideration. But the record must connect the prohibition's breadth to those interests. Here many covered subjects and payors have no employment connection. Expense reimbursement and qualifying charitable payments do not compensate expressive labor. Simon & Schuster confirms that a financial restriction may burden expression, while Turner requires meaningful examination of the asserted fit. The Court leaves senior officials, genuinely connected payments, nonpublic information and independently valid conflict rules outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563, supplies the employment balance, applied to the breadth of a prospective restriction. Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138, supplies public-concern principles without limiting protected subjects to politics or workplace criticism. Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994, retains its distinct particular-speech and reasonable-account rules. Simon & Schuster v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, supplies the relevance of financial burdens. Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC, June 27, 1994, preserves substantial but reviewable legislative predictive judgments; it does not decide this employment case.

### The existing ban is unenforceable throughout the certified class

**Holding and operative rule:** Relief against the existing §501(b) prohibition extends throughout the certified class of affected Executive Branch employees below GS–16. The Court declines to create a general employment-nexus qualification by moving the series-specific parenthesis in §505(3) into the entire honorarium definition; this class remedy does not establish that every class payment is unrelated or that a law separately and validly restricting connected payments would fail.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Part II is joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly support the operative class remedy and statutory-remedial explanation. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justice O'Connor would protect unrelated expression only; Justices Scalia and Thomas would sustain the ban. The five-Justice rationale controls without a fractured-opinion synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an injunction against the existing prohibition may protect the represented class without drafting a replacement prohibition. Congress expressly used a duties-or-status nexus for a series of appearances, speeches or articles. It did not place that qualification in the general prohibition on individual honoraria. Extending the parenthesis by construction would choose a new coverage boundary rather than interpret a plausible meaning of the enacted arrangement. The Government's alternative request for payment-specific relief deserves consideration, but it does not require the judiciary to administer a substitute ethics law that Congress has not enacted. The class remedy responds to the demonstrated breadth of this prohibition and the represented claims. It does not find every employee's activity unrelated to federal work or grant immunity from separate valid restrictions. Congressional and judicial coverage, the distinct high-level outside-income ceiling, and claims of unrepresented senior employees remain outside this class disposition. The lower decree must be narrowed as to its beneficiaries, while retaining this class protection.

**Precedent treatment:** Pickering remains the merits framework rather than authority to rewrite statutory coverage. Simon & Schuster and Turner retain their stated financial-burden and evidentiary principles; neither compels a judicially created nexus statute. The remedy preserves the independent force of valid ethics laws and decides no new constitutional rule for congressional or judicial employees.

### The injunction does not adjudicate the claims of unrepresented senior employees

**Holding and operative rule:** The injunction must be confined to the represented class and the individually represented claim retained here; it may not extend this adjudication to unrepresented Executive employees at GS–16 or above. Removing that nonparty protection does not hold their claims meritless or decide materially different applications of the honoraria ban.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Part III-A is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven votes for this beneficiary limitation. Justices Scalia and Thomas also would remove nonparty relief, but because they would vacate the entire constitutional injunction; their judgment agreement does not add to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the relief must respect the controversy actually represented. The certified class stops below GS–16, while the judgment below enjoined enforcement throughout the Executive Branch. The positions and responsibilities of senior employees may present a different balance, and this record does not adjudicate those employees' claims merely because the same statute governs them. The Government's alternative submission seeks relief confined to respondents and class members. That beneficiary distinction can be given effect without accepting every proposed limitation on payments. Madsen keeps the persons bound or protected by a decree distinct from an unlimited claim concerning everyone who might share an interest. The existing individually represented claim must likewise be distinguished from the class boundary. Narrowing the beneficiaries therefore does not require a merits ruling against all senior employees, nor does it reopen every represented claim. On remand the lower court must remove the decree's extension to unrepresented Executive employees while preserving the relief this judgment sustains.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen v. Women's Health Center, June 30, 1994, supplies attention to actual persons, claims and the lawful scope of remedial adjustment; it is applied without importing its different clinic facts or an automatic bar on all broader relief in other cases.

### Crane retains his individual relief without a grade-only remand

**Holding and operative rule:** Peter G. Crane's existing individual relief is retained; his GS–16 status and resulting exclusion from the certified class do not alone require its removal or a separate remand. This disposition concerns a named respondent under the Government's alternative respondents-plus-class request, not a general constitutional exemption for senior employees or a substantive concession of Crane's entire merits case.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Part III-B is joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes for the stated procedural ground. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote to retain Crane's existing individual relief but adds no ground on that component. Justice O'Connor would retain only unrelated-payment protection; Justices Scalia and Thomas would vacate the relief under their merits position.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Crane's named-party status cannot be replaced by the limits of a class to which he does not belong. The Government preserves its primary defense of the statute, but its alternative remedial submission expressly includes respondents as well as class members. Crane is one of those respondents. A statement that senior employees may present different merits cannot silently erase that particular inclusion. The Court has rejected the primary defense to the extent supporting the operative relief and independently settled the payment-scope question for the represented controversy. Nothing about removing relief for strangers to that controversy requires reopening Crane's individual protection solely because of his grade. Madsen's distinction between a necessary conforming correction and independently justified further relief supports that limited treatment. The Government has not substantively conceded every possible payment to Crane, and the Court announces no general waiver doctrine. It leaves materially different senior-employee claims and separately valid ethics restrictions for their own adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's bounded remedial treatment is applied to distinguish the necessary removal of nonparty protection from an unsupported grade-only reopening of an individual judgment. No new universal appellate-waiver rule or general senior-employee First Amendment exemption is adopted.

## Precedent Treatment

Pickering and Connick continue to govern protected citizen speech in public employment; the Court applies that balance with justification responsive to a broad prospective financial restraint. Waters retains its distinct rules concerning particular speech, reasonable factual accounts and causation. Simon & Schuster establishes that financial restrictions can burden expression; Turner preserves legislative deference while requiring review of the connection between asserted harms and actual coverage. Madsen supplies attention to represented persons and bounded remedial changes, without importing unrelated factual holdings. No precedent is overruled, and Thomas's 1991 interim ruling is not treated as a merits adjudication.

## Law After Decision

Effective February 22, 1995, the six-Justice prospective-burden rule governs the stated public-employee speech setting. The five-Justice class remedy prevents enforcement of this existing ban throughout the represented below-GS–16 class; it does not constitutionalize every payment. The injunction is restricted to the class and retained individual claim, excluding unrepresented senior employees. The distinct holding and remedy coalitions directly command majorities; no Marks synthesis is needed.

Section 501(b) prohibits receipt of an honorarium by a Member, officer or employee; §505(2) excludes special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202. Section 505(3) covers money or anything of value for an appearance, speech or article; a series falls within the definition if its subject directly relates to official duties or payment is because of governmental status. That parenthesis does not already restrict every single honorarium. Actual and necessary travel expenses for the individual and one relative are excluded to the extent another person pays or reimburses them, and otherwise deducted to the extent they are not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses mean transportation, lodging and meals away from residence or principal employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received, subject to a $2,000 ceiling on each payment and no financial benefit to the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child or dependent relative. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and a civil penalty up to the greater of $10,000 or compensation received. Under §504(b), a written opinion from an entity identified by §503 protects its recipient and another covered person whose facts are materially indistinguishable from subsection (a)'s sanction only for conduct after issuance, in good faith and in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings. These are qualified provisions, not general exemptions. The separate §501(a) high-level outside-income ceiling is not adjudicated.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part. He joins the Court's explanation that the nationwide prospective ban requires justification for its actual operation and its rule against extending relief to unrepresented senior officials. The Government's anticorruption interests can sustain narrower restrictions, disclosure and conflict rules, but this record does not connect the broad burden on unrelated expression to actual or apparent misuse of office. His disagreement concerns the class remedy. He would keep the injunction for represented lower-level employees' compensated expression unrelated to their duties and would reserve connected payments, misuse of nonpublic information, genuine conflicts and materially different applications. He does not join the five-Justice refusal to narrow the payment scope of class relief. He joins the disposition retaining Crane's existing individual relief without adding a separate ground; that individual result does not extend his class rule to senior employees generally.

Justice O'Connor concurs in part and dissents in part. She finds the actual burden on unrelated expression unjustified under Pickering, but does not treat the existence of an advance workplace rule, by itself, as automatically increasing the constitutional standard for every employment policy. The breadth and operation of this restriction matter. Congress's preventive and administrative interests are legitimate; the record does not substantiate their application to the unrelated expression at issue. She would protect payments lacking the relevant employment connection while leaving connected applications for their own analysis. That narrower application remedy is distinct from rewriting the definition's series parenthesis as enacted general language. She joins the removal of nonparty senior relief and retains Crane as a named beneficiary at her narrower payment scope. Neither the Government's alternative request nor Crane's grade concedes the First Amendment merits of all his possible payments. She declines the broad class remedy and the broader prospective-rule rationale.

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Thomas. They would uphold the compensation restriction under the public-employment balance and vacate the constitutional injunction. They recognize that loss of compensation can discourage expression, but consider the retained ability to speak, expense treatment, charitable-payment route, Congress's predictive judgment and administrable preventive protection against corruption and its appearance sufficient. Their position does not treat public employment as surrender of every speech right. They reject demanding proof of completed corrupt transactions before Congress acts and regard the Court's treatment of the law's breadth as giving insufficient weight to the employer's preventive interests. Thomas's earlier interim opinion reserved the merits and is not a prior merits disposition. If invalidity were otherwise established, these Justices would prefer a narrower unrelated-expression remedy and would not remove Crane merely because he is outside the class; those conditional positions do not add votes to any injunction they oppose.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts for a decree preserving class-wide nonenforcement of the existing §501(b) ban and Crane's existing individual relief, while removing the extension to unrepresented Executive employees. No grade-only reconsideration of Crane is required. The Court decides no penalty or damages claim, supplies no replacement ethics code, and leaves separately valid regulation and materially different unrepresented claims for their proper proceedings.
