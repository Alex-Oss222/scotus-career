## Standards and Tests selection: Direct relation between a civil RICO

### Direct relation between a civil RICO violation and injury

**Governing question:** Causation for damages under 18 U.S.C. §1964(c).

**Current rule:** A plaintiff must establish factual causation and proximate cause, including some direct relation between the injury asserted and the alleged §1962 violation. Directness is a contextual requirement informed by attribution difficulties, overlapping recovery and available directly injured plaintiffs; those considerations explain the inherited requirement and are not separate newly required elements. Factual connection, foreseeable loss and an obligation to advance money alone are insufficient. Subrogation changes the claimant's identity without making the underlying injury more direct. The construction imports no antitrust-injury requirement, special racketeering injury or prior-conviction requirement, and creates no exceptionless bar to every third-party injury.

**Authority by component:**
- Factual and proximate cause, contextual directness and preserved limits: [Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Holmes_v_Securities_Investor_Protection_Corp_merits_1992-03-24.md), March 24, 1992, “Civil RICO requires a sufficiently direct causal relation,” Part II.
- Subrogation cannot improve the directness of the asserted injury: the same record, “The nonpurchasing customers' derivative losses are too remote,” Part III-A. Souter for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas controls both propositions; Scalia's judgment-only rationale is distinct.

**Present operation effective:** Holmes, March 24, 1992.

**Open question:** Whether securities-fraud RICO plaintiffs must be purchasers or sellers remains undecided; separate opinions rejecting that restriction do not create a Court holding.

**Holdings navigation:** [Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp., 503 U.S. 258 (1992) — Civil RICO causation and SIPA remedies](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#holmes-v-securities-investor-protection-corp-503-us-258-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Direction of enterprise affairs

### Direction of enterprise affairs under §1962(c)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern direction of enterprise affairs under §1962(c)?

**Current rule:** Section 1962(c) requires some role in operating or managing the enterprise itself through the prohibited racketeering pattern, not merely one's own affairs or service provision. Primary responsibility and formal executive status are unnecessary. An outsider who actually directs enterprise affairs can qualify, as can lower participants operating under upper management; the exact lower-rung boundary remains open. No professional immunity, general aiding-and-abetting rule, conspiracy construction or lenity alternative is adopted. Holmes's civil direct-causation requirement remains independent.

**Authority by component:**

- Some role in directing the enterprise is necessary, without an executive-status requirement: [Reves v. Ernst & Young — Some role in directing the enterprise is necessary, without an executive-status requirement](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/reves-v-ernst-young-merits-1993-03-03.md), 1993-03-03. The statutory-construction portion of Blackmun's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: seven Justices adopt the stated rule at the same level of generality. Souter and White reject the directional requirement.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-03-03; Reves v. Ernst & Young, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** [Reves v. Ernst & Young, 507 U.S. 170 (1993)](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#reves-v-ernst--young-507-us-170-1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: No economic-motive element

### No economic-motive element in §1962(c)

**Governing question:** Whether a noneconomic enterprise or purpose falls categorically outside §1962(c).

**Current rule:** Section 1962(c) adds no economic or profit-seeking motive requirement for the enterprise or predicate acts. Enterprise, commerce, direction, predicate offenses, pattern, causation, individual responsibility and constitutional limits remain independently necessary; a dependent §1962(d) claim cannot be rejected on that invented motive requirement. Obstruction, coercion and extortion remain distinct, including the Hobbs Act’s obtaining requirement when invoked.

**Authority by component:**

- Absence of economic-motive element and unchanged predicates: [National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler — Section 1962(c) has no added economic-motive element](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/National_Organization_for_Women_Inc_v_Scheidler_merits_1994-01-24.md#public-projection), 1994-01-24. Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same statutory construction and its limited remedial consequence.

**Present operation effective:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 1994-01-24.

**Related current rules:** “Direction of enterprise affairs under §1962(c)” and “Direct relation between a civil RICO violation and injury” retain their distinct requirements.

**Open question:** Private RICO injunctive relief, actual First Amendment defenses and §1962(a)’s income requirement are not decided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Possessory seizures

### Possessory seizures and the claimant's own Fourth Amendment interest

**Governing question:** What instructions govern possessory seizures and the claimant's own Fourth Amendment interest?

**Current rule:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a Fourth Amendment seizure without a search, privacy or liberty invasion, or criminal investigative purpose. A competing due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim; coverage does not establish unreasonableness or §1983 liability. A suppression claimant must show interference with his or her own protected property interest in a seizure or reasonable expectation of privacy in a search. Conspiracy membership neither creates an exception to that personal-interest requirement nor destroys an actual protected interest. Attribution, immunity and the remaining merits must be separately established.

## Standards and Tests selection: Contraband recognized

### Contraband recognized through a lawful protective patdown

**Governing question:** What instructions govern contraband recognized through a lawful protective patdown?

**Current rule:** During a lawful protective patdown, authorized touching that itself supplies probable cause of incriminating character permits seizure of contraband to which the officer has lawful access. Immediate recognition requires probable cause, not certainty. Manipulating or exploring a nonthreatening object beyond the protective frisk to obtain that cause is an additional unauthorized search; identification resulting only from such manipulation cannot justify the seizure. No exploratory-touch exception follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Newly clarified appellate error, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved.

**Authority by component:**

- Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority: [United States v. Olano — Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/olano_merits_1993-04-26.md), 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Correction remains discretionary: [United States v. Olano — Correction remains discretionary](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/olano_merits_1993-04-26.md), 1993-04-26. ## Standards and Tests selection: Enforcement and modification of interstate water

### Enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees

**Governing question:** What instructions govern enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees?

**Current rule:** Enforcement of an established water-decree right requires no new injury showing; the decree, original opinion, report, record and relevant course of conduct may identify the right. New allocation or modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury. Rule 17.2 uses Rule 56 and its precedents as guides: no genuine material factual dispute and entitlement as law are required. A nonmovant with the ultimate burden must sufficiently support essential elements under the applicable enforcement or clear-and-convincing modification standard. This is distinct from Rufo's institutional-consent-decree framework and creates no general transfer of water rights through silence.

#### Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp., 503 U.S. 258 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-727.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### What causal relation civil RICO requires between a violation and business or property injury

**Holding:** A plaintiff seeking damages under 18 U.S.C. §1964(c) must establish both factual causation and proximate cause, including some direct relation between the injury asserted and the alleged §1962 violation. Injury that merely follows harm to another person is insufficient on the causal chain presented here; directness is a contextual requirement informed by attribution, overlapping recovery and the availability of directly injured plaintiffs, not a mechanical rule resolving every third-party injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this rule. Scalia agrees that proximate cause is required but joins no part of the Court's opinion; his distinct reasoning is not added to the opinion's support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchaser-seller requirement is decided: Souter, Stone, Blackmun, Kennedy and Thomas join Part IV’s reservation. No special racketeering injury, antitrust-injury requirement, universal third-party-injury bar, or general zone-of-interests rule is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Associated General Contractors of California, Inc. v. Carpenters, 459 U.S. 519 (1983) — its account of common-law causal limits in the borrowed Clayton Act language is extended to §1964(c); its antitrust-specific injury requirement is not imported.
- Agency Holding Corp. v. Malley-Duff & Associates, Inc., 483 U.S. 143 (1987) — relied on for the close statutory relationship between civil RICO and the Clayton Act; its limitations holding remains unchanged.
- Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., 473 U.S. 479 (1985) — preserved: no special racketeering injury or prior conviction is required; the enacted causal limitation still must be satisfied.
- Cannon v. University of Chicago, 441 U.S. 677 (1979) — applied for Congress's presumed awareness of the settled interpretation of borrowed language; no new implied action is recognized.

##### Whether SIPC may recover for the nonpurchasing customers’ derivative losses

**Holding:** Even assuming SIPC may be subrogated to the nonpurchasing customers' rights against Holmes, it cannot recover under §1964(c) on the asserted stock-manipulation theory because their unpaid claims depend on the broker-dealers' prior losses and intervening insolvency. The trustees' distinct claims remain undisturbed, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery according to the priority SIPA lawfully gives its claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III-A, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and its limits. The validity and source of the asserted common-law subrogation are assumed, not established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Customer subrogation and responsibility for coconspirators’ conduct are assumed without decision. Purchasing-customer claims, separately grounded parking claims, directly aimed injury to SIPC, fraud merits, actual causation and damages remain unadjudicated; the unappealed Rule 10b-5 judgment is not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Associated General Contractors, 459 U.S. 519 — applied to the derivative creditor-loss chain, attribution difficulties, overlapping recovery and available directly injured plaintiffs.
- Blue Shield of Virginia v. McCready, 457 U.S. 465 (1982) — relied on for causal limits and avoidance of overlapping damages; its recognition of a directly injured claimant's action is not displaced.
- Hawaii v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 405 U.S. 251 (1972) — applied for the concern about duplicate recovery; no state parens patriae issue is decided here.
- Southern Pacific Co. v. Darnell-Taenzer Lumber Co., 245 U.S. 531 (1918) — applied as a general direct-injury principle, without converting its first-step formulation into an exceptionless rule.

##### Whether §78eee(d) authorizes SIPC’s own administration-advance damages claim

**Holding:** Section 78eee(d) of SIPA gives SIPC party-in-interest status, a right to be heard and deemed intervention in liquidation matters; it does not itself create an independent right to recover administration advances as damages from Holmes. Neither that participation provision alone nor its combination with §1964(c) supplies a missing substantive recovery right or dispenses with RICO's own conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III-B, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient statutory ground for rejecting SIPC's own §78eee(d) claim. This is distinct from the customer-subrogation causation holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide every other-law claim or the unbriefed theory of subrogation to trustees for expense advances. The statute’s participation rights and administration-advance duties remain; a separate RICO-causation ground for SIPC’s own advances is not a controlling alternative holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No earlier decision is independently construed, extended or overruled by this provision-specific holding. Its participation/recovery distinction is consistent with the existing action/remedy distinction; it does not announce a general rule foreclosing implied or subrogated actions under other law.

#### Reves v. Ernst & Young, 507 U.S. 170 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-886.

**Decided:** March 3, 1993.

##### Some role in directing the enterprise is necessary, without an executive-status requirement

**Holding:** Liability under § 1962(c) requires participation in the operation or management of the enterprise itself: some part in directing its affairs through the prohibited racketeering pattern, not merely conducting the defendant's own affairs or rendering services to the enterprise. Primary responsibility or a formal executive position is unnecessary; an associated outsider can qualify by actually directing enterprise affairs, and lower-level participants operating under upper management can qualify, while the exact downward reach is reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory-construction portion of Blackmun's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: seven Justices adopt the stated rule at the same level of generality. Souter and White reject the directional requirement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 (1981): applied for text-based interpretation of distinct RICO elements; its inclusion of criminal enterprises remains unchanged.
- Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury, 475 U.S. 851 (1986): applied for consistent treatment of repeated statutory language.
- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16 (1983): preserved as recognizing broad participation language; breadth operates within the enacted conduct requirement.
- Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., 473 U.S. 479 (1985): preserved; broad construction and rejection of special racketeering injury do not eliminate § 1962(c)'s conduct element.
- Bennett v. Berg, 710 F.2d 1361 (8th Cir.1983): its operation-or-management formulation is adopted with the stated limits.
- Yellow Bus Lines v. Drivers Local 639, 913 F.2d 948 (D.C.Cir.1990): operation/management approach accepted, but a requirement of significant control is rejected; some role in direction suffices.
- Bank of America National Trust & Savings Assn. v. Touche Ross & Co., 782 F.2d 966 (11th Cir.1986): its rejection of an operation-or-management requirement is disapproved.

##### This auditing and reporting record does not establish the required direction

**Holding:** The accounting firm's preparation and explanation of the cooperative's audit reports, using the financial records and disclosing an investment-based plant valuation, do not establish participation in directing the cooperative merely because the firm failed to require or communicate a fair-market valuation. On the RICO component reviewed here, that failure is insufficient under § 1962(c), and the firm's summary judgment is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** The application portion of Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, controls by six votes. Stone, White and Souter reject this application; their judgments cannot be counted as joins.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett v. Berg, 710 F.2d 1361 (8th Cir.1983): applied as the lower courts' statutory-role formulation, subject to the stated qualifications of the operation-or-management requirement.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): remains the governing favorable-evidence and reasonable-inference rule at summary judgment; no alteration of its standard is adopted.
- Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp., March 24, 1992: its factual/proximate causation requirement is preserved, not decided or replaced by the failure to show statutory direction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exact lowest operational level, general aiding-and-abetting or conspiracy liability, predicate acts, injury and causation remain unresolved. No professional immunity, significant-control requirement, lenity alternative holding or displacement of Holmes causation follows. Sections 1962(a), (b) and (d) remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the accountant on the reviewed §1962(c) component. The seven-Justice construction and six-Justice application are distinct; separate securities and other lower-court rulings are not adopted.


## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: National Organization for Women

#### [National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/National_Organization_for_Women_Inc_v_Scheidler_merits_1994-01-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-780.

**Decided:** 1994-01-24.

##### The clinics' own injuries suffice at the pleading stage

**Holding:** Delaware Women's Health Organization and Summit Women's Health Organization have standing at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage on allegations that respondents' threatened and actual force against staff and patients caused injury to the clinics' own business or property. Under the governing pleading standard, the allegations suffice where relief could be granted on facts consistent with them; this neither supplies standing to NOW on an unasserted RICO count nor establishes liability, a certified class or final proof of causation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this threshold ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization: applied; named plaintiffs need their own standing despite requested class treatment.
- Allen v. Wright: applied; the allegations identify injury traceable to the challenged conduct.
- Hishon v. King & Spalding: applied at Rule 12(b)(6); later proof is not demanded to sustain consistent allegations.
- Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.: preserved; actual and proximate causation, including contextual directness, remain independent requirements.

##### Section 1962(c) has no added economic-motive element

**Holding:** Section 1962(c) requires no economic or profit-seeking motive in either the enterprise or its predicate racketeering acts; a noneconomic purpose does not exclude conduct satisfying the statute's enacted elements. The dismissal of the clinics' §1962(c) claim and the dependent §1962(d) conspiracy claim therefore cannot stand on the omitted motive requirement, while enterprise, commerce, direction, predicate offenses, pattern, causation, individual responsibility and applicable constitutional limits remain to be established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same statutory construction and its limited remedial consequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette: applied to the breadth of the enterprise definition and the absence of an ambiguity supporting an invented motive limitation.
- Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co.: applied; statutory breadth does not justify extra-textual restrictions or itself invoke lenity.
- Reves v. Ernst & Young: preserved; some role in directing the enterprise itself is required, rather than merely rendering services or conducting one's own affairs. Primary responsibility or formal executive status is unnecessary; an associated outsider who directs enterprise affairs and lower participants operating under upper management can qualify, while the exact lower-rung boundary remains open.
- Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.: preserved; no motive ruling relaxes factual/proximate causation or contextual directness.
- Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic: distinguished; its §1985(3) requirements do not supply a RICO immunity or an economic-motive element.
- NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.: its protection of lawful advocacy and limits on association-based liability remain available; no particular First Amendment defense is adjudicated here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The standing ruling concerns only the two named clinics' own RICO injuries; NOW asserted no RICO count and no class was certified. Reves's directing-enterprise requirement, Holmes's factual and proximate causation, statutory predicates, individual responsibility and particular First Amendment defenses remain independent. No finding that respondents obtained property under the Hobbs Act is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed as to the two clinics' §1962(c) and dependent §1962(d) dismissals. Further proceedings must apply the enacted elements without an economic-motive requirement. No liability, pattern, predicate, obtained property, causation, damages, fees or injunction is found or awarded; the antitrust and §1962(a) rulings are not revived.

#### Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6516.

**Decided:** December 8, 1992.

##### A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy

**Holding:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a seizure under the Fourth Amendment even without a search, invasion of privacy or liberty, or criminal investigation. The family’s alleged forcible dispossession meets that threshold, but coverage alone does not establish unreasonableness or every element of §1983 liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 — applied; possession and privacy remain independently protected interests.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 — applied; seizure scrutiny does not require an accompanying search.

### Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson; No. 93-1001. — 1995-01-18

#### Section 2 continues to govern covered agreements in state court

**Controlling proposition:** FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court, making them valid, irrevocable and enforceable save upon grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. The Court retains Southland's state-court holding; Alabama may apply generally applicable contract defenses but may not deny enforcement solely because its law singles out predispute arbitration agreements for a categorical disability.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the established state-court application of §2 remains governing law. Southland supplies that substantive construction, and Perry confirms that a State cannot withhold enforcement by assigning a covered dispute to a judicial forum while preserving generally applicable contract defenses. Respondents and supporting States ask the Court to reject that interpretation in light of the Act's federal procedural structure. The statutory-construction objection deserves consideration, but repetition of that original disagreement does not establish a sufficient reason to overturn the settled rule and the reliance it permits across forums. Congress remains able to alter the statute. Alabama's categorical prohibition is directed at arbitration itself, not a defense to contracts generally. Volt does not save that prohibition: it enforces an actual contractual choice of state rules, and no such choice is established by the forum's location alone. Retaining §2's substantive command does not decide whether every direction in §§3 and 4 must operate identically in state courts or settle the assent and scope questions of every claim here.

- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483: applies its rejection of a state judicial-forum exception directed at arbitration while preserving generally applicable contract defenses.

#### Coverage turns on actual commerce within Congress's power

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2's phrase involving commerce reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full commerce authority Congress constitutionally possesses, and coverage turns on the transaction's actual interstate commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity when contracting. **Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. **Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §2 describes the transaction actually made and carried out. Here a commercial termite-service arrangement involved firms operating across state lines and actual materials obtained outside Alabama. Local work on the house does not erase those commercial connections. The competing reading would make enforceability turn on what parties predicted about commerce instead of what their transaction involved, although Congress supplied no such mental-state condition. These facts establish genuine interstate commercial operation, rather than a talisman based on the remote history of one object. The ruling resolves statutory coverage under the commerce authority Congress possesses. Volt protects an actual choice of state rules, not an assumed adoption of the forum's antiarbitration policy. Bernhardt retains the commerce predicate but supplies no contemplation requirement. No earlier decision is overruled, and §§3–4's entire state-procedure reach is not decided.

State courts remain bound by its substantive enforceability rule and full general-contract-defense qualification. ### Federal review of the suppression judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily to rest on federal law or to be interwoven with it, and the adequacy and independence of another ground are not clear, Long permits federal review absent a plain statement of an independently sufficient state ground. Arizona's substantially federal exclusion analysis supplies no such independent ground here, so the Court exercises section 1257 review without creating a new general exception to that section's final-judgment requirement.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: seven direct joins. Stevens and Ginsburg would replace Long's presumption and dismiss.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the federal question is reviewable. Michigan v. Long supplies a workable presumption when the opinion's federal basis appears and another ground's independence is unclear; a State remains free to rest its judgment expressly on sufficient state law. Arizona distinguished Leon in applying the federal exclusionary rule. Its discussion of section 13-3925 rejected the asserted reasonable-judgment and technical-violation categories; failure of an exception does not itself create an independent state command to suppress. Sochor respects a clear state preservation ground despite alternative federal analysis, and Middlebrooks respects an express, independently sufficient state constitutional ground confirmed by clarification. Neither description fits this opinion. The contrary jurisdictional approach would presume state independence without the statement Long requires. The Court retains that requirement while preserving Arizona's power to articulate a genuine state basis in an appropriate case. Review follows the completed state suppression appeal after dismissal without prejudice; no broader rule governing all interlocutory suppression orders is announced.

**Precedent treatment:** Long is applied and retained. Sochor and Middlebrooks are distinguished on their demonstrated independent grounds; neither is narrowed. State authority to afford greater constitutional protection remains intact.

### A qualifying court-employee error and objectively reasonable reliance

**Holding and operative rule:** The federal exclusionary rule does not require suppression for a clerical warrant-record error attributable to a non-law-enforcement court employee when the arresting officer's reliance on the apparently current warrant information is objectively reasonable. The rule does not validate the arrest, rest on subjective innocence alone, resolve police-generated or mixed errors, or excuse reliance made unreasonable by known or sufficiently evident defects in the information system.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six direct joins to the rule and these limits. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents on the remedy. Stevens objects conditionally on the merits; Ginsburg leaves the federal merits undecided.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ministerial recordkeeping is not categorically outside Leon's remedial analysis. Leon separates an unconstitutional seizure from the justification for exclusion; its inquiry considers the conduct exclusion can deter and the objective reasonableness of reliance. Ordinary court employees outside the law-enforcement team do not gather evidence for prosecution, so exclusion does not provide the same corrective incentive for their clerical errors as for police misconduct. Sheppard confirms that reasonable reliance can remain protected despite a judicial clerical mistake. The contrary argument correctly identifies a governmental duty to maintain accurate arrest records, but that duty does not make every responsible actor's relationship to exclusion identical. The rule remains objective and does not insulate a defective system merely because the last officer was innocent. Leon's protections do not extend to unreasonable reliance, and its attention to information-supplying officers defeats an arresting-officer-only shortcut. The Court leaves police and mixed failures unresolved and does not require a confirming call to the issuing court before every arrest.

**Precedent treatment:** Leon is extended to the bounded court-employee setting while retaining its objective limits; Sheppard supports reasonable reliance despite judicial clerical error. Calandra and Krull preserve the separate remedial inquiry. Whiteley's rule concerning the invalidity of originating information remains; its treatment of violation and suppression as synonymous does not determine this post-Leon remedy. Hensley found no constitutional violation and did not settle the distinct suppression question here.

### The rule requires a limited remand, not an admission order

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the trial court expressly left the source of the warrant-record error unresolved, reversal of Arizona's categorical federal exclusion rule requires remand for proper application of the court-employee/objective-reliance rule. The rejected intermediate attribution inference, the street officer's ordinary response to the reported warrant, and the clerk's frequency estimate do not establish sole court fault, a reliably maintained system or entitlement to final admission.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six direct joins. This is the case-specific application and mandate, not an additional exception to exclusion or a new evidentiary burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reverses the categorical rule and remands without directing that the marijuana be admitted. The trial judge regarded court and Sheriff's Office negligence as legally equivalent and therefore made no attribution finding. Neither witness could exclude a completed but unrecorded call, and the intermediate court's inference was rejected by Arizona's highest court. The record also contains both the chief clerk's estimate of an occurrence every three or four years in her particular court and three other irregularities found in the associated review. Those facts cannot be reduced to either an isolated-error finding or a statewide reliability conclusion. The I-Bureau confirmation followed the arrest, and the later call and fax do not establish the precise computer-clearance time. The state court may use its proper procedures to determine responsibility and apply the objective rule. No universal audit, numerical error threshold or new proof burden is imposed. The conceded unconstitutional arrest is not validated, and there is no conviction to reinstate.

**Precedent treatment:** Leon's distinction between rule and application governs. Dickerson preserves the importance of supported findings under the governing search rule; it does not supply a computer-error exception or authorize an appellate finding on a question the trial judge left unresolved.

### Qualifications and nonreach

Leon retains its exclusions for an affidavit knowingly or recklessly misleading the magistrate, abandonment of the neutral judicial role, an affidavit so lacking in probable-cause indicia that reliance is entirely unreasonable, and a facially deficient warrant on which an officer cannot reasonably rely. Its rule assumes proper execution within the warrant's scope and considers relevant officers who obtain or supply information as well as the executing officer. The present court-employee rule adds no immunity for police recordkeeping, mixed responsibility, deliberate disregard of defects or objectively unreasonable system reliance. The Court decides neither who caused this entry to remain nor any universal error frequency, statewide maintenance standard, guilt, civil damages, renewed state-law ground or general database-safeguards test. Full Arizona statutory text is not construed beyond the categories quoted and treated in the lower opinion.

Long remains the jurisdictional rule; Sochor and Middlebrooks retain their independent-ground holdings. Leon is extended only to the stated court-employee setting, with Sheppard supporting clerical-error reliance and Calandra/Krull preserving the separate remedial inquiry. Whiteley's constitutional rule remains; its automatic-remedy inference does not settle this post-Leon question. Hensley did not decide this remedy, and Dickerson does not permit invented findings.

Effective March 1, 1995, qualifying nonpolice court-employee record error does not require federal exclusion when reliance is objectively reasonable. Police-generated and mixed errors, objectively unreasonable system reliance and the further government-wide deterrence question remain unresolved. The Court imposes no universal proof of isolation or system audit. Jurisdiction follows Long, and greater protection on an actual independent state ground remains possible under its own law.

### Heintz v. Jenkins, No. 94-367. — 1995-04-18

Authority: [Record](../records/Heintz_v_Jenkins_merits_1995-04-18.md#public-projection).

#### Holdings

### Regular attorney debt collectors remain covered when they use litigation

**Holding and operative rule:** The FDCPA applies to an attorney who regularly collects or attempts to collect covered consumer debts owed or asserted to be owed another, including through litigation, subject to the statute's actual definition, exceptions, elements and defenses. There is no categorical attorney-litigation exemption; the decision does not cover lawyers merely because they practice law or establish that this settlement letter violated the Act.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct rationale and judgment votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that collection litigation is a means of collecting a debt within the enacted definition. The statute covers regular direct or indirect collection for another, and Congress deleted the former attorney exemption in 1986. Gardner supports enforcing those retained conditions without restoring an exclusion Congress removed. The strongest contrary argument is that rules regulating collection threats and communications can interact awkwardly with ordinary judicial proceedings. That concern does not justify excluding every litigating attorney from the definition. Congress retained an activity-specific exclusion for serving or attempting to serve legal process in connection with judicial debt enforcement, not a general occupational exclusion. Questions about a particular threat, communication or defense must be decided under the provision actually presented. An unsuccessful collection action is not automatically unlawful, and coverage does not prove the alleged insurance demand false or unauthorized. The complaint therefore returns for the ordinary inquiries into regularity, contractual authorization, alleged misrepresentation and defenses, without a liability or damages finding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, December 12, 1994: applies respect for enacted conditions and rejection of an added category restriction; it does not create a new FDCPA proof rule or resolve conduct liability.
- Green v. Hocking, 9 F.3d 18: rejects the lower-court categorical litigation-exemption approach as inconsistent with the enacted definition and attorney-exemption repeal; it supplies no continuing exemption in this Court's law.

**Operative statutory terms and questions not reached:** A covered debt is an obligation or alleged obligation of a consumer to pay money arising from a transaction whose money, property, insurance or services are primarily for personal, family or household purposes, whether or not reduced to judgment. The retained §1692a(6)(D) exception concerns a person while serving or attempting to serve legal process on another in connection with judicial enforcement of a debt; it does not create a whole-attorney exemption. No factual statutory exception is established here.

Section 1692e prohibits false, deceptive or misleading representations in debt collection; §1692f(1) addresses collection of an amount not expressly authorized by the agreement creating the debt or permitted by law. The alleged $4,173 charge remains to be tested under those provisions. Section 1692e(5) concerns threatened action that cannot legally be taken or is not intended; failure to prevail alone does not necessarily satisfy it. No authoritative resolution of those conduct applications is made.

The communication and defense provisions invoked in the objection retain their exact conditions. Under §1692c(c), a consumer's written refusal to pay or written request to cease communication bars further communications except to advise that collection efforts are terminated, to notify that the collector or creditor may invoke specified remedies ordinarily invoked by that collector or creditor, or, where applicable, to notify that the collector or creditor intends to invoke a specified remedy. A mailed notice is complete on receipt. These are alternative permissions, not mandatory notices. Under §1692k(c), the collector must prove by a preponderance that a violation was unintentional and resulted from bona fide error despite procedures reasonably adapted to avoid it; no general immunity for legal error is decided. Under §1692k(e), good-faith conformity with an FTC advisory opinion protects an act or omission even if the opinion is later amended, rescinded or held invalid; staff commentary is not automatically such an opinion. Section 814(d) denies the FTC and the other agencies assigned enforcement responsibility under §814(b) authority to promulgate trade-regulation rules or other regulations concerning collection of debts by debt collectors as defined in the Act. No defense is established on these allegations, and no comprehensive litigation exception or fee entitlement is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

Gardner supports applying the statutory conditions without a judicial category exemption. Green v. Hocking's contrary lower-court exemption is rejected. The decision settles regular-attorney/litigation coverage, not authoritative interpretations of every conduct-regulating provision or defense.

#### Law After Decision

Effective April 18, 1995, an attorney's regular collection of covered consumer debts for another is not exempt because it occurs through litigation. Coverage remains distinct from the ordinary elements and defenses of a particular claim. Neither a failed lawsuit nor the presence of a settlement letter automatically creates liability, privilege, good faith, damages or fees. The actual statutory exceptions operate on their stated terms, and no comprehensive litigation exemption is created.

### Arizona v. Evans, No. 93-1660; October Term 1994. — 1995-03-01

Authority: [Record](../records/Arizona_v_Evans_merits_1995-03-01.md#public-projection).

O'Connor, joined by Souter and Breyer, joins the Court in full. Its ruling concerns objectively reasonable reliance on a qualifying court-employee error, not trust in every computer entry. A police agency cannot obtain protection simply by keeping its own officers ignorant of a system's inadequacies; reliance may be unreasonable where accuracy mechanisms are lacking or defects are sufficiently evident. The paired clerk testimony does not establish either perfect reliability or pervasive breakdown. This caution applies the Court's objective limit and leaves its actual application to proper proceedings. It does not impose a mandatory statewide audit, a numerical safe harbor, an isolation burden in every case or an obligation to call the court before every warrant arrest.

Souter, joined by Breyer, joins both the Court and O'Connor. He emphasizes that the present decision does not resolve whether deterring government as a whole from maintaining a system that produces unacceptable false arrests could justify exclusion in another properly developed setting. That question remains open; it is not a present general-government exclusion rule. His full join does not depend on proof of a universally prescribed error rate or on a finding that the current system has already been adjudicated reliable. The distinction between the error source, reasonable reliance and future institutional deterrence must remain visible.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins Part I and dissents from reversal. He finds the substantially federal state judgment reviewable but concludes that Leon should not be extended on this record. The withdrawn warrant remained actionable in the governmental information system for seventeen days, and the State did not establish a genuinely isolated, nonrecurring mistake within an otherwise reliable process. The source's title does not alone determine the deterrent value of suppressing evidence obtained through erroneous arrest records. Both the rare-error estimate and three additional irregularities bear on whether the proposed assurance has been established; he does not find a statewide failure or invent attribution. He would keep the marijuana suppressed and allow any prosecution only on evidence lawfully available independently of the invalid arrest. He reserves a proved isolated clerical mistake in a demonstrably reliable system and proposes no multipart database-safeguards test. His proposed State showing is not a condition adopted by the six-Justice Court rule.

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, would dismiss the writ. She would replace Long's presumption with one requiring affirmative establishment of the Court's authority where a state judgment may rest on an independently sufficient state basis. State courts should remain able to explore protections in a changing technological setting without automatic federal intervention. The distinction between records maintained by courts and police may be artificial in an integrated information chain, and the three additional irregularities prevent treating the rare-error estimate as a complete account. Those concerns support restraint, but she expressly leaves unresolved what the federal exclusion precedents require. She does not find that Arizona expressly adopted an independent constitutional ground, does not affirm on a federal merits theory, and does not join Stevens's additional merits conclusion. Appropriate exceptional clarification proceedings remain possible under her approach, but she selects dismissal here.

Stevens joins Ginsburg's jurisdictional dissent and adds a conditional merits objection. If the Court reaches the question, he would sustain suppression because failure to implement a valid quashing order differs from reasonable reliance on a magistrate's probable-cause judgment. The ministerial record chain can respond to an institutional incentive even when the final arresting officer is blameless. Both halves of Crossman's testimony must be considered; rarity would limit suppression's cost, not necessarily remove its value. He preserves Leon's distinct judicial setting while rejecting the proposed court-employee extension. His position does not require the State-showing rule proposed by the Chief and does not simply join that dissent. It is conditional merits reasoning accompanying his dismissal vote, not a second vote or a federal merits position shared by Ginsburg.

These are the published positions in Evans on March 1, 1995. O'Connor's three-Justice and Souter's two-Justice elaborations do not add a compulsory system audit or isolated-error burden to the Court's rule. The Chief's proposed State showing is noncontrolling. The two-Justice jurisdictional dissent does not overrule Long, and Stevens's conditional merits ground does not acquire Ginsburg's join or a second disposition vote.
