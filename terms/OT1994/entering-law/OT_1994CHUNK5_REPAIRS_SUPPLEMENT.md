#### United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America, Inc., 508 U.S. 439 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 92-484 and 92-507.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America, Inc., June 7, 1993, Nos. 92-484 and 92-507, see Federal Courts — Antecedent questions of governing law.

##### The enacted statutory structure establishes that the insurance authorization survived

**Holding:** The 1916 insurance-agency provision amended §13 of the Federal Reserve Act rather than Revised Statutes §5202, notwithstanding the misplaced quotation marks in the printed text. The 1918 amendment of §5202 therefore did not repeal it; omission from a nonpositive-law Code edition cannot overcome the governing Statutes at Large.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general power to rewrite statutes or duty to raise every unargued question is created. Geographic reach of the insurance activity and the Comptroller’s construction remain undecided; no deference holding is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the repeal holding and remand both consolidated petitions for the actual scope dispute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hammock v. Loan & Trust Co., 105 U.S.77: punctuation read in the whole enacted context where that context establishes the legal meaning.
- Marshall Field & Co. v. Clark, 143 U.S.649: enrolled-bill authenticity distinguished from interpretation of an agreed authentic text.
- Posadas v. National City Bank, 296 U.S.497: a description of §5202 amendments does not decide this repeal question.
- Commissioner v. First Security Bank of Utah, 405 U.S.394: prior assumed validity is not treated as a holding resolving statutory survival.
- Germain, March 9, 1992: fidelity to operative statutory language and coexistence preserved.

#### [Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1196.

**Decided:** 1994-01-11.

##### Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful

**Holding:** For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943): applied for contextual interpretation of willfulness and permissible circumstantial proof.
- Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991): its recognition of a statutory known-duty requirement informs this distinct scheme; its tax rule is not made universal.
- Liparota v. United States, 471 U.S. 419 (1985): applied for context-sensitive knowledge requirements where apparently lawful conduct may otherwise be swept into a crime.
- Potter v. United States, 155 U.S. 438 (1894): the criminal statute's separate willfulness term receives operative effect rather than disappearing into other elements.

**Independent alternative holding:** If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hughey v. United States, 495 U.S. 411 (1990): applied to resolve residual criminal statutory ambiguity in favor of the accused.
- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152 (1990): applied to prevent policy or legislative materials from enlarging a crime beyond sufficiently clear text.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and Congress's responsibility to define criminal liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank's reporting duty and the customer's structuring prohibition are distinct. Section 5322(a)'s exception for §5315 and its regulations remains an exception to the penalty provision, not to §5324(3). No universal ignorance-of-law defense, requirement that proceeds be lawful or civil-forfeiture rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment for both Waldemar and Loretta Ratzlaf is reversed and remanded. Waldemar's conspiracy, four structuring and interstate-travel counts and Loretta's conspiracy and interstate-travel counts require count-specific implementation of the erroneous instruction; no count is declared automatically invalid. Hunt's convictions are outside review. No acquittal or unconditional discharge is ordered.

#### [Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Caspari_v_Bohlen_merits_1994-02-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1500.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido: applied to distinguish subsidiary legal arguments from distinct questions; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate requirements.
- Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.: preserved; an omitted independent question does not become reviewable merely through notice, logical priority or ordinary economy. This confined same-rule habeas limitation is distinguished from its separate intervention determination.
- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

#### [United States v. Granderson, 511 U.S. 39](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/United_States_v_Granderson_merits_1994-03-22.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1662.

**Decided:** 1994-03-22.

##### The former proviso requires two to six months’ imprisonment on this record

**Holding:** When controlled-substance possession triggers former §3565(a)'s mandatory probation-revocation proviso on an ordinary record with an initially available zero-to-six-month Guidelines imprisonment range and no established lawful departure, the replacement imprisonment term must be at least one-third of the range's six-month maximum and no more than that maximum: two to six months here. The sixty months of probation actually imposed do not become sixty equivalent prison months; materially different original ranges and downward-departure situations are not resolved by this calculation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; six Justices support this complete construction, independently of Scalia's and Kennedy's judgment concurrences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. R. L. C., 503 U.S. 291 (1992): its recognition of a lawful Guidelines ceiling informs the distinct revocation context; its juvenile minimum and interpretive divisions are not transplanted.
- Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992): preserved for actual lawful departure grounds; no departure is invented to sustain twenty months.
- Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (1993): preserved; commentary cannot override a federal statute or resolve the proviso by contrary text.

**Independent alternative holding:** After examination of the former proviso's text, sentencing structure and companion enactment, any remaining genuine uncertainty about the Government's proposed conversion of sixty probation months into a twenty-month prison minimum must be resolved against that greater punishment. This residual-lenity ground independently rejects the Government's construction; it neither makes every competing reading ambiguous nor supplies a universal lowest-number rule for different sentencing records.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same six Justices adopt this independently sufficient reason to reject the Government's twenty-month construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and clear legislative authorization of punishment.
- Bifulco v. United States, 447 U.S. 381 (1980): applied to genuine uncertainty about the authorized penal consequence.
- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): preserved; interpretive disagreement alone does not activate lenity.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: its residual-lenity principle is applied to this distinct statutory ambiguity, without importing its offense-specific knowledge requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materially different original ranges and probation following a substantial downward departure remain unresolved, including the denominator and any distinct lenity question. An actual lawful departure is necessary; no later statutory amendment or probation-to-prison exchange rate is applied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit is affirmed, 8–1. Its vacatur of twenty months' imprisonment and release order remain operative, and revocation remains intact. No new custodial sentencing, renewed probation, substitute fine or separate supervised-release change is ordered; the unrevoked fine's payment status is undecided.

#### Yee v. City of Escondido, 503 U.S. 519 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1947.
**Decided:** April 1, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Yee v. City of Escondido*, April 1, 1992, No. 90-1947: whether restrictions on voluntarily created pad tenancies compel permanent physical occupation (Property and Economic Rights — Physical takings and voluntary tenancies).

##### Whether the unpreserved substantive-due-process claim is reviewable

**Holding:** The Court declines a substantive due-process claim neither properly raised nor decided in the state courts; presentation only in a denied request for discretionary review does not supply the missing state-court decision. The Court need not decide here whether this review limitation is jurisdictional or prudential.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; six Justices adopt this procedural proposition. Stone does not join this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Due-process merits are not rejected. Whether the review limitation is jurisdictional or prudential is expressly undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213 — applied: an unpresented state-court claim is not decided on review; the jurisdictional/prudential classification remains open.
- People v. Triggs, 8 Cal.3d 884 — applied only to the effect of denied California discretionary review, which expresses no merits determination.

##### Whether a new taking argument and this facial theory satisfy preservation and ripeness

**Holding:** A party that properly presents a federal taking claim below may offer a different supporting argument on review, subject to the separate limitation imposed by the question presented. Under the then-governing standards, this facial allegation that the ordinance never substantially advances a legitimate state interest is ripe without a rent-increase request because it does not depend on the particular property’s economic loss or compensation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B–C of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; six Justices adopt this procedural proposition. Stone does not join this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding uses the then-governing facial substantial-advancement theory and does not decide regulatory-taking merits or override the separate question-presented limit.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bankers Life & Casualty Co. v. Crenshaw, 486 U.S. 71 — applied: new arguments may support a preserved federal claim.
- Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213 — applied: argument and claim preservation are distinct.
- Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank, 473 U.S. 172 — distinguished: the asserted facial theory does not require a property-specific rent determination.
- Agins v. Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 — applied only to the then-current facial-ripeness proposition; no merits determination follows.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Assn. v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 — applied to distinguish facial allegations from property-specific application.

##### Whether the physical-taking question fairly includes regulatory-taking merits

**Holding:** Under Rule 14.1(a), a question asking whether the ordinance causes a physical taking does not fairly include the related but independent question whether it causes a regulatory taking. No exceptional urgency or economy justifies departing from that prudential limit here, so the regulatory merits remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor, II-D, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: seven votes; this section adopts no regulatory merits or ripeness conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preserving a general taking claim does not enlarge the grant. Physical and regulatory taking questions are related but not subsidiary to one another, and no exceptional urgency or economy warrants enlargement here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Stone v. Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — applied: departure from the question-presented rule remains exceptional.
- Lytle v. Household Manufacturing, Inc., 494 U.S. 545 — applied: developed arguments and lower-court consideration support disciplined review.
- American National Bank & Trust Co. v. Haroco, Inc., 473 U.S. 606 — applied by analogy: related questions are not necessarily fairly included within one another.

#### Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission, 502 U.S. 197 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-848.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Whether FELA liability of a state-owned interstate railroad remains enforceable in state court

**Holding:** The Federal Employers' Liability Act's established inclusion of state-owned interstate railroads remains enforceable in a competent state court. This rule does not revive constructive waiver of Eleventh Amendment immunity in federal court or establish a general rule for every Article I damages action against a State.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, and Souter; these five Justices supply a majority of the eight participants. Blackmun concurs in the judgment on a different ground and does not supply the controlling rationale.

**Material application:** The retained statutory construction governs the specialized railroad relationship. Congress retained the relevant statutory language, and South Carolina excluded FELA-covered employees from workers' compensation against that established background.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not reopen Hilton's dismissed federal suit, decide negligence or damages, or hold that entering commerce generally waives immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The state-court judgment is reversed and the case remanded so the FELA claim may be adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Parden's FELA coverage holding is reaffirmed as independent of its constructive federal-forum waiver rationale, which remains displaced by Welch. Welch expressly reserved the statutory coverage question. Will, construing a different statutory term, is distinguished. Howlett is applied: a competent state court may not use state immunity to defeat liability under valid federal law.

#### Rowland v. California Men’s Colony, Unit II Men’s Advisory Council, 506 U.S. 194 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1188.

**Decided:** January 12, 1993.

##### Associations may apply

**Holding:** An association is not categorically excluded from then-effective §1915: the Dictionary Act’s inclusion of associations within person applies because §1915’s affidavit, financial-inability and counsel provisions can operate through ordinary agency and representation rules. The context exception remains operative, but these features do not establish an incompatible statutory setting or authorize a natural-person-only restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five votes for this construction and its operative limits. Kennedy’s separate account of permissible workability analysis adds no condition to the Court’s holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. A & P Trucking Co., 358 U.S. 121 (1958): applied for giving statutory entity language effective operation through ordinary legal mechanisms; criminal-liability rules are not imported.
- Wilson v. Omaha Indian Tribe, 442 U.S. 653 (1979): applied for the Dictionary Act’s inclusive default subject to actual context; no requirement of an express contrary definition is created.
- Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319 (1989): applied to preserve statutory construction against a policy substitution based on administrative convenience.
- Mallard v. United States District Court, 490 U.S. 296 (1989): preserved; §1915(d) permits a request for counsel and does not compel service.
- Osborn v. Bank of the United States, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 738 (1824): preserved; an entity’s lawful counsel requirement is separate from financial eligibility.

##### Eligibility does not establish the showing

**Holding:** An eligible association must submit a properly authorized affidavit establishing its actual inability to pay costs or give security and its asserted entitlement to redress; the court may examine legally available resources and sham concealment without automatically attributing every member’s assets to it. Inability to hold an entity bank account or a balance-sheet deficit alone does not compel fee relief, so the Council’s application returns for the actual financial and representation determinations; §1915 neither licenses lay entity advocacy nor guarantees appointed counsel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five votes for this construction and its operative limits. Kennedy’s separate account of permissible workability analysis adds no condition to the Court’s holding. The dissenters would require denial because of entity status and do not join the Court’s application process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Adkins v. E. I. DuPont de Nemours & Co., 335 U.S. 331 (1948): applied to actual financial inability; its individual-applicant facts do not categorically exclude associations or prescribe a corporate insolvency test.
- Osborn and Mallard: preserved as distinct counsel and representation limits rather than grounds for automatic financial disqualification.
- Neitzke and Denton v. Hernandez (May 4, 1992): preserved for their respective legal and factual screening rules, with Denton’s abuse-of-discretion review and independent correction of legal error unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Relief remains discretionary after the statutory showing. The Council’s restriction on holding funds is relevant but does not preclude examination of resources lawfully available through direct payment. No automatic aggregation of members’ assets, universal organizational insolvency formula, lay entity advocacy, compelled counsel, subsidized-litigation constitutional right or underlying tobacco-policy merits ruling is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of categorical association ineligibility, vacate the automatic fee grant and remand for an authorized affidavit, actual resources, lawful representation and statutory screening.

#### [Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp., 510 U.S. 27](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Izumi_Seimitsu_Kogyo_Kabushiki_Kaisha_v_US_Philips_Corp_DIG_1993-11-30.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1123.

**Decided:** 1993-11-30.

##### Review of a denied intervention application is distinct from review as a party

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), an unsuccessful applicant for intervention may seek review of the denial, but cannot obtain review of the parties' underlying judgment on the strength of an unreviewed intervention denial or a financial interest alone. Because Izumi's denial of intervention remains undisturbed, Izumi lacks the party status necessary to press the settlement-vacatur question in this petition; the Court does not decide whether intervention should have been allowed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion of the Court supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg, seven Justices; Blackmun and Stevens dissent. This stated threshold ground supports the dismissal and carries procedural authority, without a settlement-vacatur holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Automobile Workers v. Scofield*, 382 U.S. 205 (1965): applies its route for an applicant denied intervention to seek review of that denial; no automatic party status or right to review the underlying judgment follows from applying to intervene.

##### The intervention question was not fairly included and no exception is warranted

**Holding:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding a question not stated in the certiorari petition or fairly included in a stated question, subject to exceptional circumstances; a separate intervention denial is not fairly included merely because it must be overturned before the Court can reach a settlement-vacatur question. Neither discussion in the petition's body and later merits briefing nor the antecedent relationship establishes exceptional urgency or economy on this record, so the Court declines review of intervention and dismisses the writ as improvidently granted, leaving vacatur undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion of the Court supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg, seven Justices; Blackmun and Stevens dissent. The reasoned dismissal establishes this question-presentation ruling, not the merits of the judgment below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Yee v. City of Escondido*, 503 U.S. 519 (1992), question-presented portion: applies the distinction between related independent questions and subsidiary questions, with the narrow prudential exception retained; it does not convert the limit into a jurisdictional prohibition.
- *Automobile Workers v. Scofield*, 382 U.S. 205 (1965): its review route remains available, but does not dispense with presenting the intervention question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory party status, Article III interest and prudential question presentation are separate. The Court does not decide intervention entitlement, settlement vacatur, collateral estoppel, or a new construction of §2106. Munsingwear, Karcher and Cardinal Chemical receive no controlling settlement-vacatur treatment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The writ is dismissed as improvidently granted, 7–2. The Federal Circuit's intervention denial and vacatur orders remain undisturbed; the Court neither affirms their merits nor reinstates a district-court judgment or orders a new remand.

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate.

### Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

**Governing question:** What §5322(a)’s willfulness requires for the charged §5324(3) structuring offense.

**Current rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowledge of the financial institution’s reporting duty and a purpose to evade it. Reasonable circumstantial inferences may establish knowledge; knowledge of a section number or having read the Code is unnecessary. If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity on that additional knowledge requirement, lenity independently requires this narrower criminal construction; conflicting history or general purpose cannot enlarge the crime beyond sufficiently clear enacted language.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge elements and proof: [Ratzlaf v. United States — Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md#public-projection), 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

United States — Residual ambiguity independently requires the narrower criminal construction](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md#public-projection), 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Present operation effective:** Ratzlaf v. United States, 1994-01-11.

**Exceptions and limits:** The §5322(a) exception for §5315 and its regulations is separate from §5324(3). This is no universal ignorance-of-law defense, legal-proceeds requirement or civil-forfeiture rule.

### Antecedent governing law and nonpositive-law Code omissions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern antecedent governing law and nonpositive-law Code omissions?

**Current rule:** In a live case a court may address whether the asserted authorizing statute remains in force after giving the parties fair opportunity to brief that necessary antecedent question. This does not require raising every unargued issue or bypassing adversarial fairness. Governing Statutes at Large control over an omission from a nonpositive-law Code edition; the enacted structure establishes that the 1916 insurance-agency provision amended Federal Reserve Act §13 rather than Revised Statutes §5202 and survived the 1918 amendment of the latter. This is no general license for policy rewriting. Geographic reach and the Comptroller's construction remain unresolved.
