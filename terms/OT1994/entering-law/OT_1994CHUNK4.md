## Standards and Tests selection: APA persuasion burdens

### APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

**Governing question:** Who loses when evidence is genuinely balanced in an adjudication governed by APA §556(d).

**Current rule:** Unless a controlling statute otherwise provides, §556(d)’s burden of proof assigns the burden of persuasion to the proponent of the order; in the proceedings governed here, the proponent must establish the claim by a preponderance and loses at genuine equipoise. Burdens of producing evidence, lawful presumptions and separately allocated defenses remain distinct. The Longshore Act’s incorporation of APA adjudication is not displaced by §23’s evidentiary flexibility or its treatment of corroborated declarations of deceased employees. In the black-lung setting, even assuming statutory authority for an otherwise valid regulatory departure, the invoked remedial-policy provision does not establish an ultimate tie-breaking rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Persuasion allocation: [Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Director_Office_of_Workers_Compensation_Programs_v_Greenwich_Collieries_merits_1994-06-20.md#public-projection), 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.
- Longshore incorporation despite evidentiary flexibility: [Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

Greenwich Collieries — The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Director_Office_of_Workers_Compensation_Programs_v_Greenwich_Collieries_merits_1994-06-20.md#public-projection), 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Present operation effective:** Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 1994-06-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Transportation Management’s contrary description of the APA burden is disapproved; its distinct mixed-motive defense allocation is not overruled. The full scope of power to create a black-lung exception is left open.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious targeting

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test.

## Standards and Tests selection: Inherently distinctive

### Inherently distinctive nonfunctional restaurant trade dress

**Governing question:** Whether acquired secondary meaning is additionally necessary under pre-1988 Lanham Act §43(a).

**Current rule:** Nonfunctional restaurant trade dress that is inherently distinctive may be protected without acquired secondary meaning if the remaining infringement requirements, including likelihood of confusion, are met. Protection does not expire after an unspecified period merely because acquired meaning has not developed. Functionality, distinctiveness and confusion remain independent requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Inherent-distinctiveness alternative and rejection of an unwritten expiration period: [Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., “Inherent distinctiveness suffices without acquired secondary meaning”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Two_Pesos_v_Taco_Cabana_merits_1992-06-26.md), June 26, 1992; White, Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter.

**Present operation effective:** *Two Pesos*, June 26, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general ownership of cuisine, decorative style, useful designs or generic matter, later product-design rule, new proof burden or resolution of the excluded functionality challenge follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Separate due-process

### Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection

**Governing question:** A State's imposition of sales-or-use-tax collection duties on a remote seller.

**Current rule:** Purposeful and continuous solicitation of a State's consumers may supply due process's minimum connection without physical presence when the collection obligation is rationally related to benefits received through the market. Bellas Hess's indispensable physical-presence requirement for due process no longer governs this collection duty. The separate Commerce Clause rule remains: a seller whose customer connections are limited to mail or common-carrier commerce without the requisite local presence cannot be required to collect sales or use tax merely because purposeful market access satisfies due process. Complete Auto's substantial-nexus requirement remains distinct from its other requirements. Congress may alter dormant-Commerce constraints through valid legislation but cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Authority by component:**
- Revised due-process connection: [Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, “1. Purposeful contacts can satisfy due process without physical presence”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Quill_Corp_v_North_Dakota_merits_1992-05-26.md), May 26, 1992; Stevens Parts I–III, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author; Stone does not reach it.
- Surviving specific Commerce collection boundary: Quill, “2. The specific Commerce collection precedent remains controlling,” May 26, 1992; express common narrower ground of Stevens, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Stone (eight), with the record's stated common-ground authority rather than the four-Justice Part IV policy discussion.

**Present operation effective:** Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, May 26, 1992, separating the revised due-process rule from the surviving specific Commerce collection rule.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not govern every state tax, immunize consumers from use tax, set numerical sales or property thresholds, or establish a general software-nexus rule. The particular software application does not supply an additional reusable property rule. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant.

**Holdings navigation:** [Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, 504 U.S. 298 (1992) — State-tax collection and interstate commerce](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#quill-corp-v-north-dakota-504-us-298-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Container importation

### Container importation exemptions and local lease taxation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern container importation exemptions and local lease taxation?

**Current rule:** The 1956 and 1972 Container Conventions prohibit direct or indirect taxes by reason of or in connection with importation of qualifying temporarily admitted containers, not every container transaction. The 1972 definition excludes fees and charges limited to approximate service cost. A nondiscriminatory local transfer-of-possession lease tax is not importation taxation; the conventions, §1322 and regulations neither occupy all container taxation nor preempt a tax that does not obstruct temporary admission. Complete Auto's nexus, apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair-service-relation requirements remain necessary. Foreign-commerce taxation additionally must avoid substantial risk of international multiple taxation and interference with national one-voice authority; same-transaction credits and actual federal arrangements matter, while Executive support informs but does not control. Michelin's uniformity, federal-revenue and interstate-harmony concerns remain distinct. Whether Michelin changed Richfield for direct taxation of goods in transit remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory taxes

### Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

**Governing question:** When an otherwise discriminatory tax can compensate for a tax already borne by the corresponding intrastate activity.

**Current rule:** The State must identify the actual intrastate burden for which it compensates, show that the interstate charge roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and establish that the taxed events are substantially equivalent, reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies for one another. General income taxation and waste disposal are not such equivalent events. For a use tax said to equalize local sales taxes, the comparison must use the sales tax actually imposed in the affected locality. Statewide averages and burdens elsewhere cannot cure an excess there. Equal or lower use-tax burdens in other localities are not invalidated merely because an excess exists elsewhere; relief addresses the established inequality through lawful remedial procedures.

**Authority by component:**

- Three compensatory-tax predicates: [Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality — General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Oregon_Waste_Systems_Inc_v_Department_of_Environmental_Quality_merits_1994-04-04.md#public-projection), 1994-04-04. Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.
- Local comparison, bounded invalidity and relief: [Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman — The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality; Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Associated_Industries_of_Missouri_v_Lohman_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Blackmun agrees with the judgment without joining this full framework.

**Present operation effective:** Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman, 1994-05-23.

**Holdings navigation:** [General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#general-income-taxes-do-not-establish-a-compensatory-disposal-tax); [The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-compensating-burden-must-exist-in-the-affected-locality); [Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#relief-must-address-the-established-inequality-through-lawful-state-procedures).

## Standards and Tests selection: Congressional reopening

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

**Governing question:** Whether §27A(b) may compel reopening of an already final, unappealed private securities judgment.

**Current rule:** Article III forbids compelling a federal court to reopen a completed, unappealed judgment in a private securities action through §27A(b)’s retroactive reinstatement command. That command addresses a private §10(b) action commenced on or before June 19, 1991, dismissed as time-barred after that date, timely under the jurisdiction’s law as it stood on that date, and the subject of a reinstatement motion within sixty days after enactment. Meeting those statutory conditions does not overcome the constitutional finality of the completed private judgment. Pending litigation, prospective relief, government waiver of its own protection and established Rule 60 mechanisms are distinct questions; the holding does not decide them by analogy alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Final private judgment and the limited reinstatement command: [Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co — Congress may not command reopening of this completed private federal judgment](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Morgan_Stanley_v_Pacific_Mutual_merits_1994-05-23.md#public-projection), 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five participating Justices directly join this as-applied Article III rule and the limited remedy; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co, 1994-05-23.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: [Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Landgraf_v_USI_Film_Products_merits_1994-04-26.md#public-projection), 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: [Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Rivers_v_Roadway_Express_merits_1994-04-26.md#public-projection), 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** [Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#determine-statutory-temporal-reach-before-applying-the-presumption-against-new-retrospective-consequences); [Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#corrective-legislative-purpose-does-not-itself-give-an-amendment-retrospective-reach).

## Standards and Tests selection: Cigarette-labeling

### Cigarette-labeling preemption by statutory period and predicate duty

**Governing question:** Whether the 1965 or amended 1969 cigarette-labeling provisions displace common-law damages obligations.

**Current rule:** Original §5 of the 1965 Act bars positive enactments requiring particular cautionary statements on cigarette packages or in advertising; it does not displace the presented common-law damages claims. Federal warning compliance does not itself confer immunity.

For federally compliant packages, amended §5(b) reaches common-law duties imposing additional or clearer smoking-health warnings in advertising or promotion, including liability premised on advertising's neutralization of the mandated warning. Identify the duty necessary for liability rather than its pleading label. The clause preserves: voluntarily undertaken express warranties, because the manufacturer supplies their substantive promise; claims resting solely on testing, research or other nonpromotional conduct or an independently actionable nonpromotional disclosure duty; and general intentional material falsehood, including advertising falsehood, when liability does not prescribe additional warning content or punish warning neutralization. A conspiracy claim survives only insofar as its predicate deceptive duty survives. Advertising as evidence does not necessarily make the underlying duty promotional, but assigning consumer warnings to an alternative channel does not by itself establish an independent duty.

**Authority by component:**

- Original-Act construction: [Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., “The 1965 Act does not displace these common-law damages claims”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Cipollone_v_Liggett_Group_Inc_merits_1992-06-24.md), June 24, 1992; Stevens, Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- Covered warning and neutralization duties under the successor clause: *Cipollone*, “The successor clause reaches covered warning and warning-neutralization duties”; Stevens, Stone, White and O'Connor plus Scalia and Thomas expressly support that operative proposition.
- Warranty, independent nonpromotional duties, intentional falsehood and surviving conspiracy: *Cipollone*, “Voluntarily undertaken express warranties survive,” “Independently actionable nonpromotional conduct remains outside the clause,” and “General intentional falsehood and surviving concealment conspiracies are not barred.” The record identifies Stevens's four-member narrower grounds as controlling under Marks, with Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter's broader no-common-law-preemption grounds; each surviving category is a logical subset producing the same result, with seven judgment supporters. No whole amended-Act opinion or blanket common-law exemption is adopted.

**Present operation effective:** *Cipollone*, June 24, 1992, construing each Act for its own statutory period.

**Exceptions and limits:** Preserved claims still require an actual state-law duty and all applicable elements. No agency-disclosure duty, design-defect ruling, later-amendment construction, cigarette field immunity or generic bar on independently established actual-conflict preemption is supplied. Gade's negative overlap rule and Morales's separate airline language retain their own scopes.

**Holdings navigation:** [Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504 (1992) — Federal preemption](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#cipollone-v-liggett-group-inc-505-us-504-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Occupational-safety supplementation

### Occupational-safety supplementation without an approved state plan

**Governing question:** Whether OSH Act §18 automatically preempts a supplementary state occupational-safety standard on an issue covered by a federal standard when the State lacks an approved plan.

**Current rule:** Occupational overlap and lack of an approved state plan do not alone establish preemption. The provisions require examination for an actual basis of displacement; neither overlap nor a dual worker-and-public-safety purpose is conclusive. This is a limited negative holding: it supplies no complete replacement test for conflict, no universal permission whenever simultaneous compliance is physically possible and no approval of every compatible state license. Surviving pre-divergence conflict principles, including Hines and Florida Lime, remain operative on their own terms.

**Authority by component:**
- Rejection of automatic displacement and provision-specific inquiry: [Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, “1. Section 18 does not automatically displace compatible supplementation”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Gade_v_National_Solid_Wastes_Management_Association_merits_1992-06-18.md), June 18, 1992; expressly shared proposition of Souter, Blackmun, Stevens, Thomas and Stone-Zsela (five). Souter's complete opinion has only four joins; no broader Marks rule is extracted.

**Present operation effective:** Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, June 18, 1992.

**Unresolved interaction:** Morales construes a different express airline clause and supplies no general anti-duplication objective for §18. Gade does not replace the independent force of an actual statutory ceiling, exclusive arrangement, savings provision or proved incompatibility; the competing writings do not establish a unified new positive conflict test.

**Holdings navigation:** [Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, 505 U.S. 88 (1992) — Federal preemption](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#gade-v-national-solid-wastes-management-association-505-us-88-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal railroad coverage

### Federal railroad coverage and separate warning and speed duties

**Governing question:** What instructions govern federal railroad coverage and separate warning and speed duties?

**Current rule:** FRSA §434 reaches common-law railroad duties only when operative federal rules substantially subsume the relevant subject, rather than merely touch it. Additional or more stringent covered requirements survive only when necessary to reduce an essentially local safety hazard, compatible with federal requirements and not an undue interstate-commerce burden. General crossing planning and MUTCD compliance alone do not preempt warning negligence. Sections 646.214(b)(3)-(4) displace independent device-selection duties when their federally participating installation predicates apply; abandoned gates, redirected funds or nonwarning circuitry do not alone establish them. Under (b)(3)(i), gates with flashing lights are required for multiple main-line tracks; nearby tracks allowing one train to obscure another; high speed with limited sight distance or moderately high highway and rail traffic; high vehicular or train volume, substantial schoolbus or hazardous-material traffic, unusually restricted sight distance, continuing accidents or combinations; or diagnostic-team recommendation. Under (b)(3)(ii), a diagnostic-team justification that gates are inappropriate permits FHWA to find those requirements inapplicable, without commanding omission. Otherwise (b)(4) requires FHWA approval of device choice. The separate (b)(2) highway-project route is unadjudicated. Track-class speed rules cover ordinary time-and-place excessive-speed negligence despite compliance with the federal maximum; local facts alone do not invoke the saving clause. A duty to slow or stop for a specific individual hazard remains unresolved.

Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

#### [Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 512 U.S. 267](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Director_Office_of_Workers_Compensation_Programs_v_Greenwich_Collieries_merits_1994-06-20.md)

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Maher Terminals is included under Rule 12.2, covering lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims; Greenwich concerns black-lung benefits.

##### The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order

**Holding:** When APA §556(d) governs and no applicable statutory exception provides otherwise, the proponent of a rule or order bears the burden of persuasion, not merely the obligation to produce evidence. With the preponderance standard governing these benefits proceedings, a claimant cannot prevail on a necessary element left in genuine evidentiary equipoise after complete weighing and application of operative statutory presumptions and lawful defense burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves its preponderance standard and uses its explanation of the APA framework; its proof-quantum holding is not recast as an earlier decision squarely settling allocation.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393, 404 n.7 (1983): disapproves the production-only construction of APA §7(c), now §556(d), to the extent inconsistent with the persuasion allocation; preserves the Wright Line holding requiring the General Counsel to prove unlawful motivation and permitting an employer same-decision affirmative defense.

##### The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation

**Holding:** Longshore Act §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), requires APA adjudication notwithstanding other provisions of the chapter, and §23(a), 33 U.S.C. §923(a), does not exempt these claims from §556(d)'s persuasion allocation by relaxing evidentiary and technical procedural rules. Section 923(a) separately requires reception of a deceased employee's declarations concerning the injury under investigation and makes them sufficient to establish the injury when corroborated by other evidence; that preserved rule does not establish Santoro's disputed aggravation and hastened death on the claim presented here, and the vacatur and remand for a lawful preponderance determination are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

**Material application:** Section 923(a) frees the deputy commissioner or Board from common-law or statutory evidence rules and technical or formal procedure except as provided by the chapter; it permits, rather than commands, a chosen investigative or hearing method best suited to ascertain the parties' rights. Its deceased-declaration reception and corroborated-sufficiency commands are separate. The §20(a) presumption that a claim comes within the Act operates in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; the ALJ found it rebutted here, and it is no longer evidence supporting ultimate persuasion. The causal questions concern aggravation and hastened death, not an established finding that the workplace incident originally caused the cancer. Incomplete weighing remains a question for administrative reconsideration and ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): applies the preponderance quantum in APA adjudication while retaining the distinction between agency factfinding and appellate review.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393 (1983): retains genuine affirmative-defense burdens; it does not authorize transfer of claimant persuasion merely because a benefits scheme is remedial.

##### The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA

**Holding:** The Black Lung Benefits Act's §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore adjudicatory provisions subject to its statutory and regulatory qualifications; even assuming it authorizes an appropriate regulatory departure from APA §556(d), §718.3(c)'s statement of a congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish that departure. The APA persuasion rule therefore governs the disputed pneumoconiosis and total-disability determinations here, and the Greenwich vacatur and remand are affirmed without deciding the full scope of §932(a)'s regulatory power or adopting an independent ground under §718.403 or Mullins.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Material application:** Section 932(a) applies after December 31, 1973 during periods when the State's compensation law is not on the Secretary's §931(b) list, to coal-mine operators respecting death or total disability due to pneumoconiosis arising from mine employment, or entitlements under §921(c)(5). It excludes Longshore Act §§1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 37, 38, 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 and 51. Incorporation is except as otherwise provided in §932(a) or by the Secretary's regulations, and references to the employer refer to fund trustees as the Secretary considers appropriate consistently with 26 U.S.C. §9501(d). Additional provisions the Secretary prescribes in the Federal Register to provide payment to entitled persons may not be inconsistent with the provisions specifically excluded. The cross-reference to §28(h)(1) of the 1984 Longshore amendments excludes that Act's §7 medical-services amendments from black-lung claims. None of those excluded-provision, fund-substitution or medical-services matters is adjudicated here.

Section 718.3(c) expresses the policy of giving claimants the benefit of all reasonable doubt about total or partial disability or death due to pneumoconiosis; it does not expressly prescribe an ultimate equipoise award. Section 718.403 places proof of an alleged fact on the party alleging it except as provided in the subchapter, but its independent effect is not decided. Ondecko's more than ten years of coal-mine employment bears on whether established pneumoconiosis arose from that employment; it does not supply the separate disease and total-disability findings disputed here. An ALJ must not stop weighing at an apparent tie or rename a tie a slight preponderance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no final entitlement, medical fact, benefit amount or award of relief to either employer. It preserves statutory exceptions and presumptions according to their own triggers and effects, does not settle the full regulatory-exception power under §932(a), and does not decide the independent §718.403/Mullins ground. No final medical finding, unconditional denial or restoration of benefits, employer entitlement or dollar amount is ordered.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. #### Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, 505 U.S. 88 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1676.

##### Whether occupational overlap and absence of an approved state plan automatically preempt supplementary state standards under OSH Act §18

**Holding:** Section 18 does not automatically preempt a supplementary state occupational-safety standard merely because a federal standard covers the same issue and the State lacks an approved plan. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s plurality, joined by Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas, and Stone-Zsela’s concurrence in the judgment independently and expressly support this limited negative proposition and its remand consequence: five of nine Justices. No complete opinion commands five joins.

**Material application:** Section 18(a) preserves authority where no federal standard exists. Section 18(b), including its enacted heading, and the approval and withdrawal provisions can govern a State assuming the federal regulatory role without establishing a universal prohibition on compatible supplementation. Federal minimum training and additional state training do not become incompatible solely through overlap.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete opinion commands a majority. Souter, Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas alone would permit concurrent standards whenever simultaneous compliance is physically possible; Stone alone preserves a separate account of demonstrated conflict, a clear ceiling or exclusivity. Neither positive account is adopted as a new Court-wide test, and no broader rule is extracted under Marks. The shared negative proposition does not endorse every compatible license, find any challenged provision valid, eliminate existing conflict principles, or decide the remaining Commerce Clause and severability questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit judgment is reversed only insofar as it establishes automatic occupational-overlap/no-plan displacement and remanded for provision-specific review. Its 4,000-hour ruling cannot rest solely on that rationale. The unappealed invalidation of training within Illinois and independently supported rulings remain undisturbed. The Court authorizes neither wholesale enforcement nor blanket invalidation.

**Material judgment:** Reversed in part and remanded, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Thomas support reversal; White, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy oppose it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218 — applied at majority level for requiring a clear basis before displacement of traditional police regulation.
- English v. General Electric Co., 496 U.S. 72 — applied for identifying the actual statutory basis and scope of preemption; no universal prohibition of dual regulation follows.
- Florida Lime & Avocado Growers, Inc. v. Paul, 373 U.S. 132 — preserved; impossibility is an established conflict ground, but four Justices’ proposed exclusive use of that ground is not adopted by five.

- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc. (June 1, 1992) — distinguished; its express airline wording does not decide §18.

#### Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1038.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

##### Whether the 1965 cigarette Act displaces the presented common-law damages claims

**Holding:** Section 5 of the 1965 Federal Cigarette Labeling and Advertising Act bars positive enactments requiring particular cautionary statements on cigarette packages or in advertising; it does not preempt the common-law damages claims presented here. Compliance with the required federal package warning does not itself immunize manufacturers from those claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion on the 1965 Act, joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, and by Stone as to this statutory construction and its application; seven Justices support the controlling construction. Scalia and Thomas also support nonpreemption of liability for voluntary warranty and fraudulent statements, without joining the seven-Justice construction concerning warning duties.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the 1965 Act and the claims presented.

**Material application:** Damages can influence conduct without falling within this Act's prohibition of compulsory cautionary statements. Presented consumer-warning claims, including concealment and conspiracy insofar as they require smoking-health statements to consumers, remain available. Voluntarily made warranty, fraudulent and warning-neutralization statements, and conspiracy predicated on those statements, also escape this Act's statement requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This construction does not create universal immunity from independently established actual conflict, decide design-defect liability, or construe later amendments. Duties genuinely unrelated to advertising are reserved rather than assigned an opposing vote. State-law liability and the existing appellate questions concerning comparative fault and warranty instructions are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the 1965 Act preemption barriers and remand for application of the appropriate statutory periods and remaining state-law proceedings. The 1965 Act took effect January 1, 1966; the successor statute was enacted in 1970 despite its 1969 title. No verdict is reinstated, damages awarded, or fraud, causation or breach found.

**Material judgment:** The warning-duty barrier is reversed 7–2 by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, with Scalia and Thomas opposing. The voluntary-statement barrier is reversed 9–0; that unanimity does not enlarge the narrower proposition-level agreement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218, supplies the presumption preserving traditional state authority absent clear displacement. English v. General Electric Co., 496 U.S. 72, requires attention to the actual statutory operation and rejects displacement from additional tort consequences alone. San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon, 359 U.S. 236, establishes that damages can regulate but does not determine this cigarette provision's scope. Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, June 18, 1992, supplies only its shared rule against automatic preemption from regulatory overlap; its broader separate theories are not adopted.

##### Whether the successor Act preempts advertising warning and warning-neutralization duties

**Holding:** For cigarettes whose packages comply with federal labeling requirements, amended §5(b) reaches common-law duties and preempts liability requiring additional or clearer smoking-health warnings in advertising or promotion, including a duty forbidding advertising because it neutralizes the mandated warning. The duty necessary to liability controls; pleading a warning claim as fraud does not avoid preemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's amended-Act analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, and Scalia's separate express-preemption analysis, joined by Thomas; six Justices expressly support this operative inclusion and displacement. It controls directly across the writings, without treating Scalia's broader theory as a join to Stevens's entire opinion or invoking Marks.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for covered promotional warning duties and federally compliant cigarette packages.

**Material application:** A demand for additional health-warning content and a prohibition on advertising because it neutralizes that warning impose the covered obligation from opposite directions. The amended words concerning state-law requirements or prohibitions in advertising or promotion reach obligations enforced through damages.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No entire amended-Act opinion commands five formal joins. This holding creates no cigarette-wide immunity, constitutional advertising rule or general displacement of independent nonpromotional obligations; it does not decide liability, causation, damages or the effect of later amendments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm preemption of the covered warning and warning-neutralization claims for the successor Act's applicable period, preserving its federal-labeling-compliance condition. Remand for claim-specific application together with the surviving claims described below.

**Material judgment:** Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas sustain the bar, 6–3; Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter oppose it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Garmon, 359 U.S. 236, supplies the treatment of common-law duties as legal requirements without importing labor-law exclusivity. Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., June 1, 1992, is distinguished by its airline language; enforcing that express clause's enacted reach does not establish cigarette-wide immunity here.

##### Whether amended §5(b) preempts voluntarily undertaken express warranties

**Holding:** Amended §5(b) does not preempt an otherwise valid claim enforcing a manufacturer's voluntarily undertaken express warranty because the manufacturer supplies the substantive promise, rather than the State imposing a smoking-health command. Printing the promise in an advertisement does not change that result.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, supplies the controlling narrow ground under Marks v. United States. Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter support restoration on the broader ground that the amended Act preempts no common-law damages claims. Voluntary-warranty claims are a true subset of that broader rule and receive the same result whenever the narrower rule applies; the two judgment-supporting groups total seven. The broader common-law exemption does not control.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for enforcement of voluntarily undertaken substantive promises, at the stated claim-specific Marks scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State law still determines whether a binding warranty exists and whether reliance, breach, causation and damages are proved. This decision makes none of those findings and does not immunize disguised state warning commands.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the express-warranty preemption barrier and remand for the remaining state-law proceedings; no particular verdict or award is reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. American Train Dispatchers Association, 499 U.S. 117, is distinguished: the dependence of contracts on law for enforceability does not turn a manufacturer's voluntary substantive promise into the state-imposed requirement covered by this differently worded clause. Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, supplies only the described component-specific narrower ground.

##### Whether amended §5(b) reaches independently actionable nonpromotional conduct and disclosure

**Holding:** Amended §5(b) does not preempt claims resting solely on testing, research or other conduct unrelated to advertising or promotion, or concealment claims resting on an independently actionable duty to disclose through nonpromotional channels. This boundary neither preserves a disguised demand for additional advertising warnings nor establishes that an asserted state-law duty exists.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, supplies the narrow ground under Marks together with Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter's broader rule exempting common-law damages claims. Claims independently outside the activity boundary form a logical subset of that broader rule, producing the same nonpreemption result whenever the narrower rule applies; the groups total seven. Scalia and Thomas oppose restoration of the presented consumer-warning theories through alternative communication channels but reserve genuinely unrelated nonpromotional duties.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated activity boundary and claim-specific Marks scope; it establishes no particular nonpromotional disclosure obligation.

**Material application:** The necessary liability duty, rather than the pleading label or the use of advertising as evidence of another wrong, determines whether the claim is promotional. Practical compulsion matters when identifying a disguised advertising requirement, but cannot erase genuinely independent nonpromotional obligations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular obligation to report to an agency or breach of such an obligation is found. Genuinely unrelated duties, including the hypothetical agency-disclosure duty, receive no adjudicated opposing vote or liability determination. The lower courts must identify viable allegations and the relevant state-law elements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the presented concealment barrier to permit claim-specific examination under this boundary, without adjudicating independent hypothetical duties or damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** English, 496 U.S. 72, supplies examination of the particular duty and its relation to federally regulated conduct. Morales, June 1, 1992, does not replace this statute's activity limitation with the airline clause's relation language or fare-effect analysis. Marks, 430 U.S. 188, supplies only the narrow surviving category, not a complete nonpreemption theory.

##### Whether intentional material falsehood and concealment conspiracies survive amended §5(b)

**Holding:** Amended §5(b) does not preempt liability for intentional material falsehood under the general duty not to deceive, including a false statement in advertising, when liability does not depend on prescribing additional warning content or punishing warning neutralization. A conspiracy to misrepresent or conceal smoking-health facts survives only insofar as its predicate deceptive duty survives; its label creates no separate exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's claim-specific analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, supplies the controlling narrow Marks ground together with Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter's broader exemption for common-law damages claims. The protected falsehood and surviving-concealment categories are logical subsets yielding the same restoration whenever they apply; the groups total seven. Scalia and Thomas oppose restoration of the presented amended-Act claims. Blackmun's general exemption remains noncontrolling.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the stated intentional-falsehood and surviving-predicate categories, subject to the warning and warning-neutralization exclusions.

**Material application:** Liability for knowingly making a material false representation need not ask a jury to prescribe warning content. A conspiracy based on independently actionable deception or concealment remains available, but one based on a preempted warning duty does not.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Every state-law element remains to be established; this ruling finds no fraud, causation or damages and supplies no general exemption for claims labeled fraud or conspiracy. Independently established actual-impossibility preemption, industry-wide field immunity and later amendments are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse these preemption barriers, 7–2 with Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter supporting and Scalia and Thomas opposing, and remand for claim-specific proceedings. No existing verdict is reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Garmon, 359 U.S. 236, preserves the regulatory effect of damages without making every damages claim a covered smoking-health requirement. Marks, 430 U.S. 188, supplies the separate narrower grounds for these surviving categories.

##### Whether purposeful solicitation can satisfy due process for use-tax collection without physical presence

**Holding:** A mail-order seller that purposefully and continuously solicits a State’s consumers may have the minimum connection required for a use-tax collection obligation even without physical presence, when the obligation is rationally related to benefits received through that market. **Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices. Stone expressly does not reach due process and supplies no join.

**Material application:** Sustained solicitation and about $1 million in annual North Dakota sales establish the purposeful connection; the obligation relates rationally to benefits from that market. The distinct Commerce inquiry remains necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

#### Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston, 507 U.S. 60 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-321.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### The Container Conventions exempt importation charges, not every container transaction

**Holding:** The 1956 and 1972 Container Conventions prohibit direct or indirect taxes imposed by reason of, or in connection with, importation of qualifying temporarily admitted containers, rather than every tax involving those containers. Tennessee's nondiscriminatory tax on the transfer of possession under a local lease is not such an importation charge; the 1972 definition also excludes fees and charges limited to the approximate cost of services rendered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's treaty portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: eight Justices adopt this construction and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S.392 (1985): applied for treaty interpretation beginning with text while considering relevant context.
- Factor v. Laubenheimer, 290 U.S.276 (1933): applied for consideration of treaty parties' practical construction; the practice asserted here does not displace the operative importation limitation.

##### Federal container regulation does not preempt this local lease tax

**Holding:** The Container Conventions, 19 U.S.C.1322 and their implementing regulations do not occupy all container taxation or preempt a nondiscriminatory local lease tax that neither taxes importation nor obstructs the federal temporary-admission policy. Tennessee's tax therefore survives both the asserted field-preemption and obstacle-preemption grounds; the distinct federal rules governing bonded warehouses do not confer the same exemption on these lease transactions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's regulatory-preemption portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: eight Justices adopt the stated rejection of both grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S.52 (1941): applied for conflict with federal purposes; no such obstacle established here.
- McGoldrick v. Gulf Oil Corp., 309 U.S.414 (1940): distinguished because the bonded-warehouse benefit and supervision differ from container leasing.
- Xerox Corp. v. County of Harris, 459 U.S.145 (1982): distinguished on the same specific federal warehouse purpose; its protection remains.
- R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Co. v. Durham County, 479 U.S.130 (1986): applied against inferring complete field occupation from detailed customs regulation.

##### The local lease tax satisfies the additional foreign-commerce requirements

**Holding:** In addition to Complete Auto's substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair relation to state services, a tax affecting foreign commerce must avoid a substantial risk of international multiple taxation and interference with the Nation's ability to speak with one voice. With Complete Auto compliance conceded here, Tennessee's discrete local transaction and credit for tax properly paid to another domestic or foreign jurisdiction on that same transaction, together with the demonstrated federal arrangement, satisfy those additional requirements; Executive support informs but does not decide their application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's foreign-commerce portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas: seven Justices adopt this rule and application. Scalia agrees only in the judgment on this issue; Blackmun dissents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, 430 U.S.274 (1977): its four requirements remain mandatory; their satisfaction is conceded in this case.
- Japan Line, Ltd. v. County of Los Angeles, 441 U.S.434 (1979): retained and distinguished; foreign-owned property taxed fully abroad presented a different multiple-taxation and uniformity problem.
- Container Corp. of America v. Franchise Tax Board, 463 U.S.159 (1983): applied to distinguish inevitable duplication from potential foreign taxation and to assess foreign-policy evidence.
- Wardair Canada Inc. v. Florida Department of Revenue, 477 U.S.1 (1986): applied for the significance of a discrete local transaction and the actual federal arrangement.

##### A local lease transaction is not the import or export taxed in Richfield

**Holding:** Under Michelin, this nondiscriminatory local lease tax does not offend national commercial uniformity, divert federal import revenue or disrupt interstate harmony, and therefore does not violate the Import-Export Clause. The tax attaches to temporary possession transferred in Tennessee, rather than to the containers or their cargo as imports or exports in transit; the Court leaves unresolved whether Michelin otherwise altered Richfield's rule for direct taxation of goods in transit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Import-Export portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas: seven Justices adopt this rule and application. Scalia concurs only in this component's judgment on a distinct export-status ground; Blackmun does not supply a separate Import-Export disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Michelin Tire Corp. v. Wages, 423 U.S.276 (1976): applied through its three constitutional concerns.
- Department of Revenue v. Association of Washington Stevedoring Cos., 435 U.S.734 (1978): applied for distinct local services and the interstate-harmony inquiry.
- Richfield Oil Corp. v. State Board of Equalization, 329 U.S.69 (1946): distinguished; its direct-export rule is not overruled or finally reconciled with every implication of Michelin.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The temporary-admission regime requires reexportation within three months unless customs authorities extend it. Domestic ownership does not remove Japan Line review; Executive support is informative, not dispositive. Complete Auto compliance is conceded rather than independently adjudicated. No general apportionment exemption, field occupation, express-conflict-only rule or overruling of Richfield follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the treaty, preemption and constitutional challenges to this nondiscriminatory Tennessee lease tax. The constitutional analyses retain the distinct proposition coalitions stated above.

#### [Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman, 511 U.S. 641](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Associated_Industries_of_Missouri_v_Lohman_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-397.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality

**Holding:** Missouri's additional 1.5% use tax discriminates against interstate commerce to the extent it exceeds the corresponding local sales-tax burden on an equivalent purchase where the goods are used; greater sales taxes on other transactions elsewhere in the State cannot compensate that excess. The ordinary matched 4.225% statewide taxes remain outside this challenge, and equal or lower interstate burdens are not invalid merely because other localities impose discriminatory excesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Blackmun agrees with the judgment without joining this full framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Henneford v. Silas Mason Co., 300 U.S. 577: preserves genuinely complementary sales and use taxation.
- Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality, April 4, 1994: applies the actual three-part compensatory-tax requirements; its distinct income/disposal mismatch is not imported here.
- Halliburton Oil Well Cementing Co. v. Reily, 373 U.S. 64, and Armco Inc. v. Hardesty, 467 U.S. 638: apply comparison of the actual competing burdens.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992, and C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown, May 16, 1994: preserve nondiscrimination despite local governmental boundaries and arrangements.
- General American Tank Car Corp. v. Day, 270 U.S. 367: distinguished; it does not authorize aggregate offsets that leave the compared interstate purchase more heavily taxed.
- Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, May 26, 1992: its separate collection-nexus rules remain unchanged and supply no discriminatory-rate defense.

##### Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures

**Holding:** Reversal of the statewide-averaging justification requires remand for locality-specific application and constitutionally adequate relief, not automatic invalidation of every application or a predetermined refund. If Missouri compelled payment before a meaningful opportunity to contest the discriminatory tax, McKesson requires adequate backward-looking correction; the availability of predeprivation procedures, affected payments, preserved claims and lawful remedial choices must be determined rather than assumed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Blackmun agrees with the judgment without joining this full framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco, 496 U.S. 18: applies its conditional backward-looking-relief requirement while preserving lawful state remedial choices and procedural predicates.
- Henneford v. Silas Mason Co., 300 U.S. 577: preserves equal complementary applications rather than compelling statewide invalidation.
- Chemical Waste Management, Inc. v. Hunt, June 1, 1992: preserves the distinction between invalidating a discriminatory exaction and determining the applicable state remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sale and use are substantially equivalent here; the defect is excess over the local counterpart, unlike Oregon Waste's different-events defect. No aggregate offset, compulsory increase in lower interstate rates, statewide invalidation or automatic refund follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Missouri Supreme Court is reversed and remanded for locality-specific application and lawful relief. Affected payments, preservation, meaningful predeprivation review and permissible correction must be established. #### [Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., 511 U.S. 658](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Morgan_Stanley_v_Pacific_Mutual_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-609.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Congress may not command reopening of this completed private federal judgment

**Holding:** Article III prevents application of §27A(b) to require reinstatement of this private securities action after its dismissal with prejudice became final, without appeal, before the statute was enacted. Congress may change governing law, but that power does not authorize this command to reopen a completed federal judgment; pending litigation, prospective injunctions and independently lawful judicial reopening remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five participating Justices directly join this as-applied Article III rule and the limited remedy; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: preserves legislative change of law in pending litigation; resolves its reserved final-judgment question only for this completed private dismissal.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102 (1898): relies on the distinction between changing law and disturbing a completed adjudication.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128 (1872): applies protection of judicial adjudication, without denying Congress's power to amend governing law.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421 (1856): distinguishes changes to the continuing prospective operation of an injunction.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304 (1945): leaves its due-process revival rule intact; it does not decide final federal judgments under Article III.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, June 18, 1993: preserves the distinction between open direct review and final judgments; judicial retroactivity is not a reopening command.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products and Rivers v. Roadway Express, April 26, 1994: preserve the distinction between express legislative temporal direction and constitutional power; ordinary antiretroactivity construction cannot avoid this express command.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371 (1980): distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own res judicata defense from compulsory reopening between private parties.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 27A(b) requires an action commenced on or before June 19, 1991, dismissed as time barred after that date, timely under the jurisdiction's June 19 law including retroactivity principles, and a reinstatement motion within 60 days after December 19, 1991. The January 31 motion met the deadline; the ruling does not rest on a missing statutory condition. Section 27A(a)'s separate June 19 limitations-law direction, other applications of subsection (b), general severability, due process, fraud liability, damages and independently lawful reopening remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit is reversed, 5–3, and the matter remanded with directions to deny §27A(b) reinstatement. The completed August 1991 dismissal remains undisturbed. O'Connor does not participate; no fraud findings or damages trial is ordered.

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### Burson v. Freeman, 504 U.S. 191 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1056.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### What scrutiny governs the campaign-specific restriction on ordinary streets and sidewalks within the polling-place boundary

**Holding:** Tennessee's campaign-specific prohibition is a content-based restriction on political speech, and ordinary public sidewalks and streets within the 100-foot boundary remain public forums. The State must show that this restriction is necessary to serve a compelling interest and narrowly drawn to achieve that end; viewpoint neutrality and the availability of speech beyond the boundary do not by themselves lower that scrutiny.

**Proposition-level authority:** Seven participating Justices expressly adopt this governing proposition at the same level of generality: Blackmun's plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, and Stevens's dissent, joined by O'Connor and Souter. It is an independently majority-supported proposition, not a seven-Justice judgment coalition or an Opinion of the Court. Scalia rejects the forum classification and applies a different standard.

**Material application:** Viewpoint neutrality and opportunities to speak beyond the boundary do not make campaign-specific content discrimination neutral. The Justices applying strict scrutiny disagree about necessity and tailoring in this facial challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The majority-supported scrutiny rule supplies no shared validity rationale or numerical safe harbor. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated political speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment invalidating the 100-foot restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3, by Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy. The governing scrutiny proposition has a different, seven-Justice coalition; Thomas takes no part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Ass'n v. Perry Local Educators' Ass'n, 460 U.S. 37 (1983) — applies its traditional-public-forum and content-based scrutiny framework to the ordinary streets and sidewalks reached here.
- United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171 (1983) — applies its protection of ordinary public sidewalks; government-building proximity does not alone remove that status.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988) — applies strict scrutiny to political subject discrimination; a claim of regulatory purpose does not automatically make this campaign restriction content neutral.
- Mills v. Alabama, 384 U.S. 214 (1966) — preserves its protection of general election-day advocacy and its reservation concerning conduct in and around polls; it does not automatically settle this boundary's validity.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, simulated decision — preserves exacting review of content-based burdens; this case's divided application creates no general exception to that rule.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989) — distinguishes its content-neutral time, place and manner standard; it does not replace strict scrutiny here.

##### Whether this 100-foot restriction survives the facial challenge

**Authority of the judgment:** The judgment invalidating the restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3: Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy support reversal; Stevens, O’Connor and Souter oppose it. Thomas takes no part. No controlling validity rationale exists under *Marks*: Blackmun’s plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, treats the place as a public forum and finds strict scrutiny satisfied; Scalia treats the polling environs during voting as a nonpublic forum subject to reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality. The different forum predicates are not established logical subsets, and Scalia does not adopt the plurality’s necessity or tailoring conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds this facial challenge to the 100-foot boundary during voting. Neither the four-Justice application nor Scalia’s nonpublic-forum theory becomes a controlling exception. The separately majority-supported scrutiny rule appears above. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

#### Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1200.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### The selected commercial category lacks the required fit

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading commercial publications concerning lawful activity and newspapers cause the same asserted physical harms through their newsracks, the City must justify why its selected commercial category reasonably fits its safety and aesthetic interests. Cincinnati has not done so: removing respondents' sixty-two racks while retaining approximately 1,500–2,000 physically similar newspaper racks violates the First Amendment; a small reduction is not invalid merely for being small, and no least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven, throughout.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S.557 (1980): applies the existing commercial-speech test to the accepted classification.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S.469 (1989): applies reasonable proportional fit and government burden; preserves rejection of least restrictive means.
- Metromedia, Inc. v. San Diego, 453 U.S.490 (1981): distinguishes the billboard treatment; does not permit an unrelated content distinction without the required fit.

##### The selective prohibition is not content neutral

**Holding:** A newsrack prohibition determined by whether its publication is a commercial handbill is content based when the asserted safety and aesthetic harms do not justify that distinction without reference to the publication's content. Cincinnati cannot sustain this selective ban as a content-neutral time, place and manner restriction merely because its objective is benign; otherwise lawful neutral regulation of newsracks remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S.781 (1989): applies justification without reference to content; a benign objective alone does not validate the selective line.
- Clark v. Community for Creative Non-Violence, 468 U.S.288 (1984): preserves valid content-neutral time, place and manner regulation.
- Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc., 475 U.S.41 (1986): distinguishes different secondary effects; no such difference justifies this rack classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Central Hudson and Fox remain governing. Incremental regulation remains possible; alternatives inform fit but do not impose least restrictive means. No strict-scrutiny rule for all truthful commercial speech, all-sources-at-once duty or commercial/noncommercial equality rule is adopted. Other lawful neutral rack controls remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit and the injunction against the selective commercial-handbill newsrack prohibition.

#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fowler v. Rhode Island, 345 U.S.67: discrimination in the treatment of religious exercise prohibited.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S.872: targeting falls outside the neutral-law rule; that rule is not reconsidered here.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S.618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S.693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

##### Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. Each challenged ordinance fails that minimum requirement here, including 87-72’s express state-law exemption for small-number hog or cattle operations; the Court does not define every boundary of general applicability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Smith, 494 U.S.872: general-applicability requirement applied, without reopening its neutral-law holding.
- Fowler, 345 U.S.67: unequal treatment of religious exercise applied.
- Hobbie v. Unemployment Appeals Commission, 480 U.S.136: protection against unequal treatment of religious observers preserved.

##### Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance

**Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. These ordinances fail narrow tailoring independently of whether the asserted ends could be compelling because comparable harms are left unregulated and neutral, narrower controls on disposal, care, killing methods and location could address the city’s concerns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S.205: rigorous justification of restrictions on religious exercise applied.
- McDaniel, 435 U.S.618: demanding scrutiny of religious disabilities applied.
- Arkansas Writers’ Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S.221: lack of narrow tailoring independently suffices under strict scrutiny.
- Schneider v. State, 308 U.S.147: collateral public harms cannot justify unnecessary suppression when direct regulation is available.

##### The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S.524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S.205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

#### [Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Turner_Broadcasting_System_Inc_v_FCC_merits_1994-06-27.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-44.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Cable editorial expression receives ordinary First Amendment protection

**Holding:** Cable operators' editorial selection and cable programmers' expression are protected speech, and compulsory carriage burdens those interests. Cable's physical transmission capacity and market power do not justify importing broadcasting's spectrum-scarcity standard or reducing review of this expressive burden to rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-A and II-B as relevant to this proposition, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens recognizes protected cable expression but does not join these complete portions and is not counted for their formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock, 499 U.S. 439: applies its recognition of cable speech and press; its approval of a broad content-neutral tax does not resolve compulsory carriage.
- City of Los Angeles v. Preferred Communications, Inc., 476 U.S. 488: applies its protection of editorial selection and attention to cable's characteristics; it had left the precise standard open.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC, 395 U.S. 367: confines its scarcity rationale to the broadcasting setting; it supplies no diminished cable standard here.

##### Content classification depends on what the law selects and why

**Holding:** A law burdening protected expression because of its content ordinarily must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest; a burden justified without reference to content is subject to the appropriate intermediate review. A benign purpose or absence of hostility does not neutralize a content-based operation, while a distinction among speakers or media does not by itself establish content discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens does not join that portion; his agreement with the case-specific classification below is distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991: applies its actual content-based escrow holding; neutral collection remedies remain outside that holding.
- Arkansas Writers' Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: applies its scrutiny of differential burdens dependent on a publication's content.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content as the classification inquiry.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: applies its rejection of benign-purpose neutrality for an unexplained content distinction; its commercial-speech fit holding remains bounded.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its distinction between permissible neutral differential treatment and censorial or content-based burdens.

##### The general full-power carriage requirements are content neutral

**Holding:** The general commercial and noncommercial full-power must-carry obligations regulate cable's distribution bottleneck and preservation of independently available broadcast service without selecting the stations' individual messages; intermediate scrutiny therefore governs those obligations. This classification does not decide the distinct low-power local-programming criteria, the market-adjustment provision, or a materially different content-based administration, and creates no general right to compel private editorial publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-C and II-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this application.

**Material application:** The classification addresses the general full-power core of §§4–5, whose commercial and noncommercial eligibility, capacity, duplication, signal-quality and channel-position provisions remain distinct. It does not adjudicate the separately reserved low-power programming criteria or market adjustments, authorize an editorial-balance mandate for another medium, or confer both must-carry and retransmission-consent entitlement at once.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241: distinguishes compelled reply and interference with newspaper editorial judgment; its protection is not displaced.
- Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. Public Utilities Commission, 475 U.S. 1: distinguishes its fractured compelled-access reasoning concerning speech-responsive access and attribution; no unanimous categorical rule is attributed to it.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its allowance for justified neutral distinctions between media; the cable-specific burden still requires constitutional scrutiny.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC: supplies no basis for the classification; broadcast scarcity is not extended.

##### Intermediate scrutiny requires advancement and a proportionate speech burden

**Holding:** A qualifying content-neutral carriage requirement must further an important or substantial governmental interest unrelated to suppressing expression and must not burden substantially more speech than necessary to further that interest. The Government need not establish that the law is the least restrictive conceivable alternative, but the actual expressive burden and relevant alternatives bear on whether its fit is sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. This common standard does not establish common agreement about whether the existing record satisfies it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367: applies the important-interest, nonsuppression and incidental-burden requirements to this content-neutral carriage burden.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism: applies its requirement against substantially excessive burdens while preserving its rejection of least-restrictive-means review.

##### Preserving broadcast availability, diverse sources and fair competition are substantial interests

**Holding:** Preserving free local broadcast service, maintaining diverse sources of information and promoting fair competition in television distribution are important content-neutral interests supporting consideration of the general full-power carriage scheme under intermediate scrutiny. Their importance does not itself prove threatened harm, advancement, adequate fit or the scheme's final constitutionality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. The separate opinions disputing the neutrality of the Act's localism and programming preferences do not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock: applies the distinction between a medium's economic characteristics and selection of protected ideas.
- United States v. O'Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism: apply the requirement of a substantial nonsuppressive interest without treating that element as proof of advancement or tailoring.

##### Predictive judgments receive substantial deference and remain subject to review

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** In reviewing Congress's predictive judgments relevant to the carriage scheme, a court must give substantial deference while independently assuring that Congress drew reasonable inferences from substantial evidence. Congress need not compile the formal evidentiary record required of a court or agency; this general principle neither decides whether the evidence here suffices nor joins the four-Justice application requiring additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, supported by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Souter, together with Stevens's express agreement in his concurrence at this limited level of generality: five Justices. Stevens does not join Part III-B, its real-harm formulation as a complete passage, or its record-insufficiency application. This is direct agreement on the stated proposition across writings, not a Marks inference or a fifth join in the plurality portion.

**Current force:** Controlling at this express five-Justice level of agreement only; no majority adopts Part III-B's present-record insufficiency application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landmark Communications, Inc. v. Virginia, 435 U.S. 829: applies the judiciary's continuing responsibility to review an expressive burden despite legislative findings.
- Sable Communications of California, Inc. v. FCC, 492 U.S. 115: applies the refusal to make congressional findings conclusive; its strict-scrutiny setting does not impose that standard here.

##### Authority of the remand on evidentiary sufficiency

**Authority of the judgment:** The summary judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter. No controlling rationale exists under Marks for the present-record insufficiency conclusion: only Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter adopt it; Stevens agrees in remand without adopting it. The separate grounds do not establish a logical subset. The six controlling propositions above retain only their separately stated support and scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final constitutionality of the general scheme remains open. The district court must consider the separate low-power criteria in the first instance and may address the market-adjustment provision; neither is independently upheld or judicially severed. The initial home-shopping transition clause does not establish current FCC treatment. The remand orders no replacement quota, damages, blanket injunction against the Act or particular interim relief. Any interim relief requires its own properly presented legal basis. The legal standards and the judgment govern; the four-Justice explanation of why further proof is needed remains identified as such.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The summary judgment sustaining the challenged carriage scheme is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, under the controlling intermediate framework. Final constitutionality remains open. The district court must consider low-power eligibility in the first instance and may consider market adjustments; any interim relief needs its own properly presented basis. No new channel quota, damages or blanket injunction against the Act is ordered.

#### [Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, Board of Accountancy, 512 U.S. 136](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Ibanez_v_Florida_Board_of_Accountancy_merits_1994-06-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-639.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### An active CPA credential is not false because its holder disputed regulation

**Holding:** The Board may not reprimand Ibanez for truthfully identifying her active Florida CPA credential on these law-practice communications on the unsupported theory that the title falsely implies submission to regulation. Her earlier legal challenge to the Board's authority does not falsify the credential or prove misleading advertising; the CPA ground for the reprimand must be set aside, with otherwise lawful professional regulation preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the CPA ground and its limited remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies protection for truthful, nonmisleading commercial information and preserves regulation of actual deception.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: applies the distinction between factual professional information and misleading advertising; no immunity from appropriately justified regulation follows.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies the requirement of justification in the relevant professional setting, without imposing an invariable empirical-study requirement.

##### The private CFP credential is not inherently deceptive on this record

**Holding:** The Board may not sustain this reprimand merely because Ibanez's authorized private CFP credential, displayed beside CPA without naming the private certifier, could be mistaken for government recognition. On this record the Board has not established actual or inherent deception or a constitutionally adequate justification for suppressing that truthful certification information; the CFP prohibition ground must be set aside, without validating every private credential or every form of its advertisement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices directly adopt this as-applied holding; O'Connor dissents from it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the distinction between protected factual information and actually or inherently misleading speech.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: preserves regulation of deception without treating possible confusion as automatically justifying suppression.
- Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91: applies its protection against the certification ban within the distinct plurality and concurrence rationales; no unanimous rule against certification regulation is attributed to it.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies its real-harm and material-advancement inquiry to the actual asserted professional deception.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: preserves reasonable fit between the selected restriction and its asserted harm without requiring regulation of every source at once.
- United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co., June 25, 1993: preserves supported incremental regulation and reasonable fit; it supplies no presumption that this credential is misleading.

##### The actual immediate disclosure demand does not justify this reprimand

**Holding:** Paragraph (j)'s demand for an immediately adjacent disclaimer that the certifier is neither affiliated with nor sanctioned by state or federal government, together with the certifier's recognition requirements including but not limited to education, experience and testing, cannot sustain this reprimand without adequate justification for that actual burden. The Board has not justified it on this record; the holding preserves supported, proportionate factual clarification and neither prescribes a replacement disclaimer nor creates an exception to paragraph (i)'s separate prohibition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices reject this disclosure justification as applied; O'Connor would independently sustain it if the advertisement is merely potentially misleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Paragraph (g) addresses stated or implied recognition as a public-accountancy specialist unless the recognizing agency is Board-approved; paragraph (i) independently prohibits recognition by another entity using certified; paragraph (j) separately imposes the actual disclosure just analyzed. Compliance with (j) is not an exception to (i). The Court resolves the sanction on this record, not all applications of those provisions, the withdrawn unlicensed-firm charge, future advertising, licensing jurisdiction, or the validity of a differently justified shorter disclosure. An expired credential, fictitious certifier or express false claim of state endorsement presents a different case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, 471 U.S. 626: applies the distinction between reasonably related factual clarification and unjustified or unduly burdensome disclosure.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: preserves the possibility of adequately justified clarification of potentially misleading professional information.
- Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91: preserves the separate concurrence's limited-disclosure rationale without converting its permission into a duty to supply exhaustive standards.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies record-sensitive justification without requiring empirical studies in every professional-regulation case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The CPA basis for the reprimand is reversed unanimously, and the CFP basis and actual disclosure justification are rejected by eight Justices. The case is remanded to set aside the reprimand on both challenged grounds. Licensing jurisdiction remains intact; no new disclaimer, damages, credential revocation or finding on the withdrawn unlicensed-firm charge is ordered.

#### [City of Ladue v. Gilleo, 512 U.S. 43](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/City_of_Ladue_v_Gilleo_merits_1994-06-13.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1856.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### The replacement prohibition leaves no adequate residential channel

**Holding:** A city violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments when, even assuming content neutrality, it virtually eliminates residents' signs expressing their own views at home without adequate alternative channels preserving that distinctive communicative opportunity. Ladue's replacement prohibition fails that requirement; the judgment protecting Gilleo's residential expression is affirmed, while otherwise lawful controls of size, number, placement, illumination and safety remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same residential-channel holding and limited remedy; no fractured-opinion inference is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that the exemptions are content neutral. It does not decide their independent validity, a formal severability determination concerning the exemptions, the unpreserved six-square-foot substitute, or any new ordinance. The old variance procedure is not restored. The separate fee adjustment is outside this merits review. No absolute right to any size, number, illumination or placement of sign is recognized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Linmark Associates v. Willingboro, 431 U.S. 85: applies its inquiry into whether costlier or less effective alternatives adequately replace signs; its reservation concerning genuinely neutral regulation remains.
- Members of City Council v. Taxpayers for Vincent, 466 U.S. 789: distinguishes its public-property setting and preserved private channels; its recognition of substantial aesthetic interests remains intact.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies the requirement of adequate alternative channels while preserving its rejection of a general least-restrictive-means requirement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: preserves attention to actual burden and fit without importing a rule against incremental regulation; the present holding does not resolve every exemption's content classification.
- Metromedia v. San Diego, 453 U.S. 490: preserves its fractured billboard decision and the distinction between regulating a medium's physical effects and suppressing protected expression; no unified rationale is attributed to all its writers.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's judgment and the injunction protecting Gilleo's residential expression are affirmed. No exemption is deleted, old variance procedure restored, replacement ordinance imposed or separate fee ruling altered.

Source: `state/holdings/tort-law.md`, heading containing `Easterwood`.

#### CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood, 507 U.S. 658 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-790; No. 91-1206.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### Coverage requires substantial subsumption of the actual duty

**Holding:** Section 434 reaches common-law railroad-safety duties, but preempts them only when operative federal rules substantially subsume the subject of the relevant state requirement, not merely touch it. A covered additional or more stringent requirement survives only when necessary to eliminate or reduce an essentially local safety hazard, not incompatible with federal requirements and not an undue burden on interstate commerce.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cipollone, June 24, 1992 — applied at its common-law-duty and text-specific scope; its tobacco predicates do not decide FRSA coverage.
- Rice, 331 U.S. 218, and Shaw, 463 U.S. 85 — applied to identify congressional purpose through text and structure.
- Morales, Gade, D.C. Board of Trade and Boston Harbor — distinguished: different statutory language and regulatory settings supply no substitute FRSA test.

##### Warning-device preemption requires the operative project predicates

**Holding:** General crossing-safety planning and MUTCD compliance do not displace this warning negligence claim. Sections 646.214(b)(3)–(4) do displace independent device-selection duties when applicable, but planned and abandoned Cook Street gates, redirected funds and circuitry that does not warn motorists do not establish federal participation in installation of warning devices here; the separate subsection (b)(2) highway-project route is not presented.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cipollone, June 24, 1992 — predicate-duty method preserved; this application turns on FRSA and its own regulations.
- Rice, 331 U.S. 218 — applied against inferring displacement from general planning provisions.

##### The ordinary excessive-speed claim is covered

**Holding:** Section 213.9’s track-class speed regulation, read in its safety-regulatory setting, covers the pleaded ordinary claim that this train should have traveled more slowly for the time and place despite compliance with the class-four 60-mile-per-hour maximum. That general negligence claim does not qualify merely by its local facts for section 434’s local-hazard saving clause; a duty to slow or stop for a specific individual hazard is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, III–IV; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Section 213.9 under FRSA — construed to cover ordinary operating-speed negligence in this setting.
- Rice, 331 U.S. 218 — applied through the statutory coverage inquiry; does not defeat demonstrated coverage.
- Cipollone — no extension into a universal preemption rule; actual regulated subject controls.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The device provisions remain distinct. Under §646.214(b)(3)(i), adequate warning devices for a covered project include automatic gates with flashing lights when there are multiple main-line tracks; multiple nearby tracks allowing a train to obscure another; high speed combined with limited sight distance; high speed combined with moderately high highway and rail traffic; high vehicular or train volume, substantial schoolbus or hazardous-material truck traffic, unusually restricted sight distance, continuing accidents, or a combination; or a diagnostic-team recommendation. Under (b)(3)(ii), when a diagnostic team justifies that gates are inappropriate, FHWA may find those requirements inapplicable; that permission is not a command to omit gates. For covered installations to which (b)(3) does not apply, (b)(4) requires FHWA approval of the device choice. These qualifications do not establish that the Cook Street project predicate was met. Section (b)(2)’s separate provision for crossings within or near the terminus of a federal-aid highway construction or improvement project is not adjudicated because CSX did not invoke that predicate here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial coverage differs from merely touching the subject; compatibility alone does not save a covered requirement. Warning-device installation predicates and the specific individual-hazard speed issue remain distinct. No blanket crossing-negligence exemption, actual-conflict-only test, negligence finding or gate-installation command follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both reviewed appellate components: warning-device negligence may proceed, while ordinary excessive-speed negligence is preempted. The speed coalition excludes Stone-Zsela, Souter and Thomas; no new individual-hazard claim is adjudicated.
