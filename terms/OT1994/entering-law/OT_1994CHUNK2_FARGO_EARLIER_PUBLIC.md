## Event

United States v. Shabani, 513 U.S. 10, No. 93-981. Merits decision, November 1, 1994. Argued October 3, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The question is whether 21 U.S.C. §846 requires proof of an overt act in furtherance of a narcotics conspiracy. Shabani was convicted after the district court refused his requested overt-act instruction. The Ninth Circuit, 993 F.2d 1419, reversed under its circuit precedent and expressly left sufficiency and other appellate claims undecided. The indictment omitted an overt-act allegation; its sufficiency on that ground follows from the same statutory construction.

Render form: compact. Basis: 9–0 judgment, a single common statutory ground and a bounded appellate remand.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand for the reserved appellate issues, 9–0. Supporting Justices: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice opposes.

The appellate court must apply the rule that §846 has no additional overt-act element and address the remaining preserved issues. No new factfinding is required on the overt-act question. The Court does not order acquittal, a new trial or unconditional reinstatement of every aspect of the conviction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act

**Controlling proposition:** Section 846 of Title 21 requires neither an allegation nor proof of an overt act beyond the unlawful narcotics agreement. The Government must still prove the unlawful agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt; neither mere association nor awareness of another person’s plan is enough.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this construction and its application; no fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress made the unlawful agreement the completed conspiracy offense. Section 846 contains no additional overt-act language, while 18 U.S.C. §371 expressly requires an act to effect the conspiracy’s object. Nash supplies the common-law understanding of conspiracy, and Singer shows why Congress’s omission of an extra act requirement matters when it uses that settled term. This reading does not punish a thought: the agreement itself is conduct, and the Government must prove knowing participation in it. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary element cannot rest on circuit precedent when the governing statute supplies none. Felix’s treatment of successive prosecutions did not decide this statutory question and cannot add the omitted element. Nor does lenity supply it; ordinary construction resolves the question without the residual ambiguity addressed in Ratzlaf. The refused overt-act instruction therefore supplies no ground for reversal of the conviction. The Court leaves the appeal’s reserved sufficiency and other claims to the court of appeals.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Nash v. United States*, 229 U.S. 373 (1913), applies the common-law meaning under which the agreement completes conspiracy without a further overt act.
- *Singer v. United States*, 323 U.S. 338 (1945), applies the significance of Congress’s omission of an additional overt-act requirement from a statutory conspiracy offense.
- *United States v. Felix*, 503 U.S. 378 (1992), remains a double-jeopardy decision distinguishing agreement from substantive conduct; it did not decide §846’s overt-act element. Its narrower separate position likewise supplies no contrary statutory element.
- *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), preserves lenity for genuine ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction; that condition is absent here. Its distinct willfulness rule is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule for every conspiracy statute is announced. The Court does not decide credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, the remaining appellate objections or the ultimate validity of the conviction. Current double-jeopardy law is unchanged.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 1, 1994, §846 conspiracy requires the unlawful agreement and knowing participation, but no further overt act in allegation or proof. This adds an offense-specific statutory construction. It does not alter the knowledge requirements of other crimes, create a universal rule about omitted statutory language, or change Felix, Dixon, Grady or Ratzlaf beyond the distinctions stated above.

## Event

U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18, No. 93-714. Decision on mootness and vacatur, November 8, 1994. Argued October 4, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The Ninth Circuit, 2 F.3d 899, affirmed the district court’s reversal of a bankruptcy ruling and remanded for further bankruptcy proceedings. Its decision recognized the availability of the new-value doctrine; it did not find a particular plan confirmable. A consensual settlement while review was pending mooted the underlying controversy. Bancorp requests vacatur of the appellate judgment; Bonner opposes it. The live questions are the Court’s continuing authority to dispose of the judgment and whether the voluntary settlement justifies vacatur. The bankruptcy merits are not reached.

Render form: compact. Basis: unanimous disposition on two directly supported grounds, with the appellate judgment preserved and a single uncomplicated Court opinion.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court denies the motion to vacate and dismisses the case as moot.

## Judgment & Remedy

The motion to vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment is denied and the Supreme Court proceeding is dismissed as moot, 9–0 on both components. Supporting Justices: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice opposes either component.

The appellate judgment remains undisturbed, without endorsement of its bankruptcy merits. The Court neither dismisses the underlying Chapter 11 case nor vacates any other order. No settlement-enforcement order, plan-confirmation ruling or Rule 60(b) remand is entered.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ending a controversy and disposing of the pending review are distinct judicial acts. Section 2106 authorizes a reviewing court to affirm, modify, vacate, set aside or reverse a judgment lawfully before it, and to direct further proceedings as justice requires. Walling recognizes the authority to make an appropriate disposition even when merits review cannot continue. Munsingwear likewise rests on power to address the consequences of a judgment that has become unreviewable. A categorical rule stripping all dispositional authority after mootness would contradict that practice. Article III still forbids an advisory ruling on the bankruptcy dispute; the Court therefore does not decide whether the new-value doctrine survives or whether a former proposed plan could be confirmed. Kokkonen concerns jurisdiction over a separate contract-enforcement controversy after dismissal, which no party obtains here. The existing review supplies the occasion for considering vacatur; the parties’ consent does not create jurisdiction or dictate the equitable result.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute.

### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Controlling proposition:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a losing party’s voluntary surrender of review ordinarily leaves the existing judgment in place. Munsingwear protects against an unreviewable judgment when review fails through happenstance; Karcher shows why a party’s own decision to end review receives different treatment. Bancorp accepted a consensual resolution while review remained available and has identified no further equitable reason to erase the appellate judgment. Settlement is lawful and valuable, but that value does not turn a completed judicial act into a term the litigants can remove by agreement. The judgment also has a public significance beyond their immediate bargain. Exceptional circumstances may justify relief in another settlement case; a clause requesting vacatur alone merely repeats the private preference and cannot supply the missing equity. This is no heightened evidentiary test or finding of settlement misconduct. The Court denies the requested alteration and dismisses this proceeding as moot, without affirming the Ninth Circuit’s bankruptcy reasoning or determining the judgment’s effect on every outsider.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 8, 1994, §2106 continues to authorize a proper disposition after mootness, and voluntary settlement ordinarily weighs against vacatur absent a further sufficient equitable reason. The Court adds this settlement-vacatur rule to the prior mootness framework; Izumi had left it unresolved. Exceptional relief is reserved, without a heightened proof standard. Bankruptcy new-value doctrine, settlement enforcement, collateral-order review and the merits of the judgment below remain unchanged by this disposition.

## Event

Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., including Walsh’s employee action, 513 U.S. 30, No. 93-1197. Merits decision, November 14, 1994. Argued October 3, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.

The question is whether PATH, the Port Authority’s bistate railway subsidiary, is an arm of New York and New Jersey entitled to Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employees’ federal suits. Hess and Walsh alleged separate workplace injuries and brought separate FELA actions; Hess also invoked the Boiler Inspection Act. The district courts dismissed under the Third Circuit’s arm-of-State precedent and the unmet one-year condition attached to the States’ consent. The reviewed appellate disposition left those dismissals in place. The two actions remain distinct; this decision resolves their common immunity premise.

Render form: full. Basis: a divided judgment with a material disagreement about the entity-status rule and the operation of conditional consent; the component judgment and opinion tables preserve both actions and the dissent’s separate ground.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Third Circuit’s judgment and remands both employee actions.

| Walsh action: dismissal on PATH’s claimed state immunity | Reverse and remand, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas | The same removal of the immunity bar, with the separate action’s ordinary defenses and merits reserved. |

## Holdings

### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Controlling proposition:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that PATH’s actual relationship to New York and New Jersey does not make these suits actions against the States. Lake Country Estates requires examining the compact structure, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes separate public entities from the State itself. Gubernatorial appointment and veto, legislative restrictions and public transportation responsibilities favor PATH’s position, but do not overcome this enterprise’s separate identity, revenues, assets and obligations. Article XV requires equal state appropriations only while operating revenues are inadequate, only for salaries, office and other administrative expenses, in amounts recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, with each State obligated only up to $100,000 annually. It is no promise to pay judgments. Financial independence therefore carries great weight in this mixed setting, without making treasury liability immunity’s sole condition. Feeney assumed status when deciding consent; it did not resolve the antecedent question now presented. Because PATH is not an immune arm, its conditional consent is unnecessary to overcome an Eleventh Amendment bar. The Court leaves ordinary defenses and liability for the alleged injuries to proceedings on remand.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

## Law After Decision

Effective November 14, 1994, PATH is not an arm entitled to the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity from these employee actions. Lake Country’s compact inquiry is clarified through the actual separate legal and financial structure, with treasury exposure carrying great weight but no universal sole-treasury test. The Third Circuit’s contrary PATH-status rule cannot sustain these dismissals. FELA coverage, consent of an entity that actually is immune, immunity abrogation and collateral-order appealability remain separate and unchanged.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. She would treat PATH as an arm of the States because the governors and legislatures retain substantial control over its commissioners, actions, projects and spending. Financial exposure matters, but the protection of a State from federal suit also safeguards its institutional authority. A self-supporting state enterprise need not lose that protection merely because it can pay judgments from its own revenues. Lake Country Estates permits an entity-specific finding of arm status; it does not compel treating PATH’s state controls as equivalent to the more local structure there. Puerto Rico Aqueduct recognizes protection from suit for qualifying arms while leaving the qualification question separate.

The dissent does not read Article XV as an unlimited state guarantee. Its equal, conditional administrative appropriations, recommended by the Authority and approved by both governors, bind each State only up to $100,000 annually while operating revenues are inadequate. The claimed sovereign relationship rests on the combined legal controls, not an invented obligation to pay judgments. Having found arm status, the dissent treats consent as a separate requirement. Feeney’s federal-forum consent did not eliminate its conditions; the district courts found both actions outside the one-year period running from accrual. Hilton’s state-court FELA holding does not revive constructive waiver in federal court. The dissent therefore would affirm these dismissals without deciding liability, damages or a new exception to the consent legislation. This position does not control the Court’s status ruling or remand.

## Procedure After Action

Both federal employee actions may proceed against PATH, subject to ordinary defenses and adjudication of the underlying claims. The Eleventh Amendment premise of dismissal is removed. The Court awards no damages and decides no negligence, Boiler Inspection Act merits or independent nonimmunity limitations question.

## Event

United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64, No. 93-723. Merits decision, November 29, 1994. Argued October 5, 1994, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The granted question is whether knowingly in 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1)–(2) reaches performer minority and the sexually explicit character of the depiction. The reviewed judgment, 982 F.2d 1285, reversed convictions following a bench trial because the panel construed the statute to lack the knowledge it considered constitutionally necessary. Respondents also renew two grounds rejected below: facial overbreadth arising from the under-eighteen definition and facial vagueness or overbreadth from the use of lascivious in the exhibition category. The panel left the indictment-scienter objection and the contention that these particular tapes were not child pornography unresolved.

The reported trial finding states that defendants knew the performer was underage when she made the films transported or shipped in interstate commerce. That finding is not a ruling on every element, every count or the reserved content challenge.

## Holdings

### The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character

**Controlling proposition:** To convict under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1) or (2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knew the performer was a minor, as well as the applicable knowing handling act. It need not prove knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition; the actual statutory production, depiction and commerce-or-mail conditions remain distinct, and the Court does not decide whether knowledge of the jurisdictional nexus is required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven Justices directly adopt the factual-knowledge construction and its reasoning. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that knowingly reaches the factual features that make these depictions criminal. Morissette supplies the presumption of culpability, applied to the whole statutory setting rather than confined by the distance between the adverb and the later conditions. Staples supports requiring awareness of the characteristics that distinguish a prohibited object from an otherwise familiar one, while Posters preserves the difference between factual knowledge and legal classification. Reading §2252 to punish a distributor who lacks awareness of the material’s incriminating character would also raise the speech concerns identified in Smith and Ferber. The fair contextual reading avoids that unnecessary constitutional ruling without rewriting a federal default of recklessness from Osborne’s Ohio statute. The reported finding that respondents knew the performer was underage remains relevant, but does not resolve every count, the indictment objection or whether these tapes contain the conduct the statute defines.

**Controlling proposition:** Respondents may defend the existing appellate reversal on the renewed age-eighteen and lascivious-wording grounds that appear in the record, although the court of appeals rejected those grounds, because they seek no enlargement of their rights or reduction of the Government’s rights under that judgment. The Court reaches those two alternatives; the separate actual-or-simulated bestiality and sadistic-or-masochistic-abuse objection is not established as renewed and is not decided.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this reach ruling. Stone-Zsela joins only the dispositions on the two alternatives and adds no reach ground. Scalia and Thomas accept the legal availability of such defenses but do not decide either alternative because their independent ground would sustain the appellate judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that respondents may offer these two grounds to preserve the judgment they already obtained. American Railway Express distinguishes defending a judgment from asking the Court to enlarge it or overturn a separate adverse judgment. Respondents seek the same appellate reversal, not greater relief, and the two renewed objections were considered in the record below. Izumi therefore does not require treating each rejected reason as a separate adverse judgment that respondents must overturn. ### The asserted age-eighteen line does not establish substantial facial overbreadth

**Controlling proposition:** The renewed objection that §2256 defines a minor as a person under eighteen, rather than under sixteen, does not establish substantial First Amendment overbreadth in §§2252(a)(1)–(2) as construed here. Difficulty estimating age near eighteen, together with decisions permitting adult treatment of some older adolescents in other settings, does not require facial invalidation of this protective category.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part III and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the asserted difficulty of distinguishing older adolescents from adults does not establish the substantial overbreadth needed to invalidate this statute on its face. Ferber supplies that demanding facial standard and recognizes the governmental interest in preventing the sexual exploitation of children. Its particular statute protected children under sixteen, but the decision did not constitutionalize that age as an upper boundary. Permission to treat some sixteen- or seventeen-year-olds as adults in other settings likewise does not require adult treatment for sexual exploitation. The concern that distributors may avoid protected adult expression deserves consideration; the Court does not equate it with proof of a substantial unconstitutional sweep. The required actual knowledge of minority further narrows that concern, although the age-line ruling does not rest on scienter alone. **Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the stated ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of Part IV and adds no ground; Scalia and Thomas do not reach its merits.

Ferber’s treatment of lewd exhibition supplies a meaningful comparison, and the ordinary terminology considered in Miller does not become constitutionally indeterminate merely through the substitution of lascivious. ## Holdings

### The Commission lacks independent certiorari authority for this FECA enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 437d(a)(6)'s authority to litigate and appeal civil enforcement actions through the Commission's general counsel does not displace 28 U.S.C. §518(a)'s allocation of Supreme Court representation in this FECA corporate-contribution action. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains available, and the express certiorari powers in 26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d) remain confined to actions in which the Commission appears under those respective funding sections.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule. Stone-Zsela and Stevens dissent; Ginsburg does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress did not authorize the Commission to petition independently in this enforcement action. Providence Journal establishes that authority to conduct litigation below does not necessarily include authority to represent the Government here. Congress may create an exception, and ordinary construction determines whether it has done so. Section 437d(a)(6) gives the Commission substantial enforcement autonomy, but its general appeal language must be read with section 518(a)'s specific allocation and the express certiorari grants in the presidential-funding statutes. Those grants apply only to actions brought within their respective sections; this corporate-contribution action is not one of them. **Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the immediate respiratory emergency is the condition to be stabilized. Section 1395dd(e)(1)(A) addresses acute symptoms sufficiently severe that absence of immediate attention could reasonably cause serious jeopardy to health, serious impairment of bodily functions, or serious dysfunction of an organ or part. Uniform screening under subsection (a) therefore cannot replace the separate stabilization obligation after an emergency is found. **Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that refusal provisions are conditional compliance routes, not exceptions triggered by disagreement alone. ## Holdings

#### The private repo return is outside the federal-obligation exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3124(a) does not exempt the return on these repurchase agreements because it is owed under a separate private financing obligation, rather than on the United States' securities; actual book-entry delivery does not change the source of the negotiated earnings. The statute continues to protect direct and indirect taxation of federal obligations and their interest, except nondiscriminatory corporate franchise or substitute corporate nonproperty taxes and estate or inheritance taxes; the State prevails because this income is outside the protected subject, not because an exception covers this individual income tax.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the obligation producing the taxed return controls. Rockford distinguishes the Government's payment obligation from a private obligation supported by federal credit, and Smith v. Davis supplies the same direct-obligation boundary. Here the Trusts advance cash for a privately negotiated return unrelated to Treasury yield. The Trusts transmit the issuer's payments to the Seller-Borrower; the Seller-Borrower maintains collateral under the margin provisions; and the Trusts retain recourse for a deficiency after default liquidation. Those arrangements identify secured financing for this exemption even though securities actually passed through the Federal Reserve system and no separate note was signed. Frank Lyon respects genuine business arrangements; it does not determine whether the Government owes these particular earnings. The State therefore may tax the private return. The removal of surplus language during recodification did not abolish indirect-tax protection. The Court decides neither repo ownership under other bodies of law nor a transaction conveying genuine beneficial ownership and federal coupon income.

Interstate Commerce Commission v. Transcon Lines, No. 93-1318; 513 U.S. 138. Merits decision, January 10, 1995. Argued November 1, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit; judgment under review: 9 F.3d 64 (October 26, 1993), following 990 F.2d 1503.

The District Court granted summary judgment for respondents, and the court of appeals sustained denial of the credit-rule collection injunction, reasoning that the filed-rate obligation barred it and that alternative enforcement measures remained. The question is whether the ICC's express credit and enforcement provisions authorize this injunction and whether it is appropriate for the challenged loss-of-discount liquidated damages. The separate earlier coded-rate component is outside review.

**Render form: compact.** The unanimous decision has a margin greater than one vote; no material change in judgment, controlling propositions, non-Chief alignment, remedy, mandate, reserved issues or precedent treatment requires the full form.

## Participation

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision. No Justice is disqualified or otherwise nonparticipating.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded, 9-0, for implementation of an injunction confined to the admitted-unlawful loss-of-discount liquidated-damages class, with genuine scope, inclusion and amount questions beyond the concessions preserved. Ordinary unpaid freight principal and other estate receivables are excluded from the restraint.

## Holdings

### The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** Under the applicable pre-reform 49 U.S.C. §§10743(b)(1) and 11702(a)(4), the ICC may seek a judicial injunction against collection violating valid credit regulations, and §§10761–10762's filed-rate commands do not categorically defeat that enforcement. Section 11702(a)(4) excludes an action under a provision governing the reasonableness or discriminatory character of rates; this action enforces specified credit and billing requirements rather than asking the court to determine rate reasonableness or discrimination.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's credit delegation and its filed-rate commands must operate together. Section 10743 permits credit under Commission regulations governing payment and preventing discrimination; §11702 supplies the public enforcement channel. The anti-discrimination purpose of credit regulation does not itself turn this suit into the rate-review action excluded from that channel. Commercial Metals rejected an implied private consignor defense while recognizing the Commission's express enforcement tools. Maislin rejected a negotiated-rate policy inconsistent with the filed-rate scheme, not every independently authorized statutory remedy. Reiter likewise preserves claims and defenses supplied by the Act without creating a general equitable exception. MCI requires an actual delegation, which these credit and enforcement provisions provide; Gardner's insistence on statutory meaning before deference does not eliminate that delegation. The trustee therefore cannot defeat this action merely by expressing the challenged damages as a difference between filed rates. Statutory authority still does not establish every violation or entitle the Commission to every requested injunction. The Court decides no general private defense, constitutional question or entitlement under later transportation reforms.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Southern Pacific Transportation Co. v. Commercial Metals Co., 456 U.S. 336 (1982): applies its distinction between an implied private credit defense and the ICC's express public enforcement tools; the private-defense holding remains intact.
- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990): distinguishes its invalid negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy from enforcement under the separate credit delegation; no general equitable departure from filed rates is permitted.
- Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993): applies its preservation of specifically enacted statutory routes alongside filed-rate duties; counterclaims, recoupment, primary jurisdiction and separate-judgment discretion retain their own conditions.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218 (1994): applies the requirement of actual delegated authority; its limit on replacing mandatory tariff filing does not erase Congress's distinct credit and enforcement provisions.
- Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115 (1994): applies statutory construction before deference while preserving actual delegated discretion; its veterans-benefit causation and eligibility rules remain confined to that statute.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): preserves deference within a lawful statutory delegation; neither a litigation demand nor general agency expertise supplies power Congress withheld.

### The admitted collection class violates the credit conditions

**Controlling proposition:** The challenged loss-of-discount liquidated-damages collections violate 49 C.F.R. §1320.3(c) because the original bills omitted the required late-payment warning, §1320.2(g)(2)(vi) because revised billing occurred years later rather than within ninety days after authorized-credit expiration, and §1320.2(g)(2)(iii) because the demands aggregate past-shipment balances. The concessions establish those violations and the charge's liquidated-damages character for the challenged class, without establishing every account's inclusion or amount, every regulatory exception, statutory authority, remedial entitlement or a distinct contractual collection route.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the admitted charge and violations supply the factual predicates for this confined enforcement action. Commercial Metals requires attention to the difference between lawful freight charges and the consequences of a particular credit violation; it does not convert every charge into unconditional freight principal. Here the request concerns additional collection damages measured by the loss of a discount. The original bills omitted the warning, the revised billing came years after the regulatory deadline, and the trustee demanded aggregate past-shipment balances. The concession of noncompliance does not rest merely on bankruptcy or on the existence of an undercharge demand. Nor does the tariff's ninety-day payment condition measured from shipment replace the regulation's period measured from expiration of authorized credit. The missing complete account file leaves genuine applications beyond the concessions open; it does not reopen the conceded class's violation or character. No omission of the loss-of-discount term from the filed tariff is alleged, and no independent contractual entitlement is established.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Commercial Metals, 456 U.S. 336: preserves its private-defense rule and distinction between lawful freight principal and enforcement of particular credit obligations; it does not immunize the admitted liquidated-damages class.
- Security Services, Inc. v. Kmart Corp., 511 U.S. 431 (1994): distinguishes an effectively canceled essential tariff component from this violation of credit conditions; no absent tariff component or wholesale tariff cancellation is found here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All operative credit qualifications remain. Authorized credit begins the day after bill presentation; absent a published alternative the period is fifteen days including weekends and holidays, and permitted combined credit cannot exceed thirty days. Service charges within the standard period are prohibited. Published additional-period charges and published post-expiration charges are distinct; post-expiration charges neither authorize delay nor remove the carrier's good-faith reassessment obligation before another credit extension. The tariff's ninety calendar days after shipment for discount eligibility is separate from the revised-billing deadline measured from authorized-credit expiration.

Section 1320.2(g)(1) permits reasonable, certain collection-expense liquidated damages through an identified dollar or percentage charge or loss of an identified discount after a stated payment date no earlier than authorized-credit expiration. Section (g)(2) requires clear terms and conditions, prohibits unlawful prejudice or unjust discrimination, permits only original separate independent bills rather than aggregate past-shipment balance-due demands, excludes clear clerical or ministerial errors such as nonreceipt, a lost payment check or misaddressing, and prohibits duplicate collection fees already provided by the bill of lading for the same transportation. Damages arise only after authorized credit expires, with a revised bill or notice issued within ninety days after that expiration. Section (g)(3)'s separate contractual bill-of-lading collection-charge route outside the tariff is not an entitlement established here. Section 1320.3(c)'s original-bill or accompanying-notice requirements concerning penalties, time limits, service and collection charges, and discount terms remain distinct from revised billing. No unreported exception, account inclusion or amount is inferred.

### A confined injunction is appropriate and must be implemented on remand

**Controlling proposition:** On the admitted violations and the defined loss-of-discount liquidated-damages request, an injunction preventing collection of that class is an appropriate exercise of the ICC's public enforcement authority, and the case must be remanded for its confined implementation. The lower court must specify the order's terms and may resolve genuine scope, inclusion and amount questions beyond the concessions or a properly presented distinct lawful collection basis, but may not reopen the admitted violation or charge character, restrain ordinary unpaid freight principal or other estate receivables, or treat this decision as a universal discharge.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this injunction fits the violation and the protections Congress authorized. Commercial Metals recognizes public enforcement, and ABF Freight supports respect for a reasoned remedial choice within its statutory limits, subject to judicial review. The aggregation restraint directly prevents prohibited demands. The notice and billing protections guard shippers against collection penalties imposed without timely warning; permitting the admitted demands would realize the advantage those requirements forbid. A future warning cannot supply notice on the original bill, and a new bill cannot make years-late billing timely. An order forbidding these same demands would accomplish the confined restraint; other sanctions or an order addressing only future carrier conduct would not themselves stop collection. Judicial preference for another permissible tool therefore does not justify denial on the grounds presented. The Commission's selection is reasoned and appropriate, not binding merely because the agency requests it. The court retains responsibility for statutory boundaries and its order's fit. ABF's Board-order setting supplies no transfer of remedial power or universal deference formula. Genuine account questions remain, while bankruptcy supplies neither tariff immunity nor permission to collect these unlawful penalties.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Commercial Metals, 456 U.S. 336: applies its recognition of public enforcement to a confined injunction protecting the intended beneficiaries of the violated credit rules; no new automatic private defense follows.
- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, 510 U.S. 317 (1994): applies by analogy its respect for explained statutory remedial discretion and meaningful review; its review of a Board remedy neither transfers that power to the ICC nor binds a court to every injunction request.
- Maislin, 497 U.S. 116: preserves the ban on a general nonstatutory negotiated-rate exception; the filed-rate objection cannot be recast as an equitable balance that defeats these expressly authorized credit conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court independently examines authority, established violation, the restraint's legal character and its fit. Review remains available for an unsupported premise, insufficient explanation, arbitrariness, excessive scope or inconsistency with the statute. No automatic Chevron deference to litigation demands, universal agency-remedy standard or broader limitation on Chevron is adopted. The prior distinct coded-rate component, individual private defenses, other statutory claims, estate distribution, exact dollar relief and unpresented reform-statute relief remain outside the disposition. A distinct contractual route requires its own presentation and proof; nothing here accepts its availability for these claims.

## Precedent Treatment

Commercial Metals preserves the private-defense/public-enforcement distinction; Maislin preserves the filed-rate rule against a general negotiated-rate policy; Reiter preserves separately enacted claims and judicial procedures. MCI and Gardner require actual statutory authority while leaving valid delegated discretion intact. Chevron is neither displaced nor converted into deference to a request for litigation relief. Kmart's effectively canceled tariff component remains a distinct ground, unused here. ## Event

Tome v. United States, No. 93-6892. Argued October 5, 1994; decided January 10, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, reviewing 3 F.3d 342 (1993), which affirmed an aggravated-sexual-abuse conviction. ## Holdings

### Substantive prior consistent statements require the premotive relationship

**Controlling proposition:** Under original Federal Rule of Evidence 801(d)(1)(B), a prior statement offered substantively to rebut an express or implied charge of recent fabrication or improper influence or motive must have been made before the particular charged fabrication, influence, or motive arose. The declarant must testify and be subject to cross-examination concerning the statement, and the statement must be consistent with that testimony and offered for that specified rebuttal purpose; general relevance or repetition alone does not satisfy this nonhearsay rule.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the same rule and explanation; no aggregation of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Rule carries forward the premotive requirement associated with its specific rebuttal terms. Salerno requires satisfaction of the chosen evidentiary provision's actual conditions; general fairness cannot replace them. Daubert displaces common-law requirements incompatible with enacted rules but permits compatible inherited meaning to illuminate their terms. Here the words describing rebuttal of recent fabrication or improper motive invoke the distinction between answering the source of an accusation and merely repeating an account. The defense asserted that A.T. accused her father to remain with her mother. The challenged accounts followed that asserted custody motive and therefore do not qualify under this subparagraph. A later statement may sometimes possess indirect probative force, as the Government argues, but relevance alone does not establish this particular condition for substantive admission. The Court decides neither that A.T. fabricated her account nor that the statements are inadmissible for every purpose. Other exceptions and properly limited nonhearsay use retain their separate requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): applies its requirement that the selected hearsay provision's conditions be satisfied; its similar-motive and forfeiture holdings remain distinct.
- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993): applies its distinction between incompatible common-law additions and compatible interpretive guidance; no expert-evidence factor is transferred to this Rule.

### Alternative admissibility and ordinary error review remain for remand

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal of admission under Rule 801(d)(1)(B) leaves the Court of Appeals to decide other preserved grounds of admission and, if the statements are not admissible on such a ground, the consequences under ordinary harmless-error rules. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness, and neither immediate retrial nor acquittal follows solely from the Rule-construction error.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt these remedial limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying the wrong admission rule does not complete appellate review of the conviction. Williamson separates an exception's proper construction from the admissibility of particular evidence and from the relief an established error requires. The Court of Appeals left the medical-diagnosis and residual-exception grounds undecided; those grounds remain available only within their own terms and ordinary preservation limits. The reviewing court may not assume their satisfaction from the present holding. If no lawful ground sustains admission, Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos require evaluation of the whole record under the Government's harmlessness burden. The rejection of the temporal limitation below did not supply that evaluation. Chapman would apply only if a constitutional trial error were actually reached and found; this decision finds none. The Court also does not revive an unpreserved aggregate Rule 403 objection or reopen every separate appellate issue. Remand permits the necessary bounded inquiry while leaving both guilt and the ultimate need for a new trial undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594 (1994): applies its separation of admissibility, preservation, and judgment consequences.
- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record substantial-influence standard to preserved nonconstitutional error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves its beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard only for constitutional trial error actually reached and found.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): distinguishes forfeited error from this preserved construction question; no new preservation finding is made.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 803(4) requires statements for medical diagnosis or treatment describing history, symptoms, pain, sensations, or their inception or general cause insofar as reasonably pertinent to diagnosis or treatment. Then-Rule 803(24) applies to a statement not specifically covered by any of the foregoing exceptions and requires equivalent circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness, a material fact, greater probative value on the offered point than other evidence obtainable through reasonable efforts, service of the Rules' general purposes and the interests of justice, and advance notice affording a fair opportunity to prepare, including the intended statement's particulars and the declarant's name and address. The Court finds no such condition satisfied or unsatisfied. Rehabilitation-only use, other properly limited nonhearsay purposes, and factbound questions outside the admitted temporal issue remain undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

Salerno and Daubert supply the enacted-rule and compatible-background principles. Williamson and Kotteakos govern the separate remedial inquiry; Chapman and Olano retain their distinct constitutional-error and forfeiture scopes. No earlier decision is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 10, 1995, original Rule 801(d)(1)(B) has the stated premotive condition for this substantive use. The Court adds no general exclusion of postmotive statements and changes no other hearsay exception, rehabilitation rule, preservation rule, or harmless-error burden.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia joins the Court's text-and-context construction and explains that Advisory Committee Notes can persuade as expert commentary without obtaining special authority from their authors' role as draftsmen. He does not adopt a rule that committee expectations themselves establish an omitted condition. The Court's explanation rests on enacted language and compatible inherited usage, so his concurrence does not subtract from the six votes for either holding.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Thomas, would affirm. The Rule requires genuine rebuttal, but its words do not expressly make chronology conclusive in every case. A statement's circumstances can weaken an accusation of fabrication even when an asserted motive existed earlier. The Tenth Circuit relied on the initial disclosure's spontaneity and its assessment of the alleged custody motive, rather than on consistency alone. Breyer's approach retains the declarant's testimony and cross-examination, a true rebuttal purpose, and Rules 402 and 403. The dissent finds no separate exception satisfied and offers no independent harmlessness determination; its disagreement is the construction supporting the judgment below.

## Holdings

### Ministerial selection claims encounter a constitutional defense

**Controlling proposition:** The Religion Clauses bar the application of Title VII to Young's race, sex, and retaliation claims challenging her advancement and retention as a probationary minister, because deciding those claims or imposing the requested relief would subject the church's choice of its religious leadership to civil review. The defense covers reinstatement, compelled reconsideration, and financial liability resting on that same selection decision; it does not exempt every religious employee or independently actionable secular conduct from civil law.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this claim-specific rule; their formal descriptions of the judgment are distinguished below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these claims would place a civil court in control of a ministerial selection. Kedroff protects church government and the selection of ecclesiastical authorities from state control. Milivojevich forbids civil reconsideration of ecclesiastical decisions through an inquiry into whether the church followed its own internal rules. Young seeks review of denied advancement and termination of her ministerial service, including alleged departures from church procedure. Reinstatement or compelled reconsideration would directly control that office; damages for the same decision do not make the underlying adjudication independent. Race and sex discrimination and retaliation are serious civil wrongs, but calling their asserted motives secular does not remove this inquiry's control of religious leadership. Section 702's religious-preference permission is not enlarged to include those categories. Jones v. Wolf permits neutral adjudication of genuine property obligations; this record establishes no independently pleaded secular claim of that kind. The Court does not find discrimination, religious motivation, or procedural compliance, and leaves disputed ministerial status and unrelated civil obligations for appropriate cases.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kedroff v. St. Nicholas Cathedral, 344 U.S. 94 (1952): applies its protection of church government and selection of ecclesiastical authorities to this ministerial-employment claim.
- Serbian Eastern Orthodox Diocese v. Milivojevich, 426 U.S. 696 (1976): applies its bar on reviewing internal ecclesiastical decisions for arbitrariness or compliance with church procedures; its unresolved fraud-or-collusion possibility remains unresolved.
- Jones v. Wolf, 443 U.S. 595 (1979): distinguishes neutral property adjudication from review of ministerial selection and preserves the former within its own limits.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990): distinguishes regulation of ordinary conduct by neutral general law from civil control of the choice of religious leaders; its rule is not replaced.

### The defense defeats the claims on their merits, not statutory jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** A federal court with jurisdiction under Title VII §706(f)(3) and 28 U.S.C. §1331 over a substantial Title VII claim exercises that jurisdiction when it decides that the Religion Clauses defeat the claim; it must enter a substantive dismissal rather than characterize this defense as a withdrawal of subject-matter jurisdiction. Young's pleaded claims are substantial federal claims, and the remand is for entry of a merits dismissal of the stated ministerial advancement-and-retention claims, not a discrimination trial or an award of relief.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the jurisdiction-and-merits distinction; eight vacate the jurisdictional judgment as a whole, while the Chief would vacate the jurisdictional characterization and affirm the dismissal on the merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to decide a federal claim does not depend on the claimant ultimately overcoming a constitutional defense. Bell v. Hood separates a substantial federal question from the legal sufficiency of the asserted right to relief. Congress supplied jurisdiction over Title VII claims, and Young's discrimination allegations invoke that statutory subject. The church's successful defense therefore supplies the reason for a merits judgment, not a reason to deny that the court possessed adjudicatory authority. Ecclesiastical autonomy limits the permissible application of civil law; deciding that limit is itself an exercise of jurisdiction. Bell's exceptions for immaterial claims made solely to obtain jurisdiction and wholly insubstantial and frivolous claims are not presented here. The Court does not decide their treatment. The correction changes the legal basis and preclusive character of the dismissal without requiring a trial about church motives. No reinstatement, promotion, back pay, or damages follows from recognition that the federal court had power to decide the defense.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678 (1946): applies the distinction between a substantial federal claim's jurisdictional basis and its legal defeat; the immaterial-jurisdiction-device and wholly insubstantial-or-frivolous exceptions are not reached.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375 (1994): preserves the need for a statutory or otherwise lawful jurisdictional basis; Title VII and §1331 supply that basis here, unlike the separate enforcement claim in Kokkonen.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional defense rests on Young's undisputed reported ministerial role and her selection-dependent claims, not a comprehensive definition of minister. No fraud or collusion is alleged; the Court neither adopts nor rejects an exception for it. The relevant church decision is that of the highest body to which this dispute was actually carried, without a new exhaustion requirement reaching the denomination's ultimate hierarchy. RFRA's invocation, preservation, and application to this suit remain undecided. A genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim remains outside this adjudication only if independently pleaded and resolvable without deciding doctrine, ministerial fitness, or the selection of religious leaders; no such claim is found to exist here.

## Precedent Treatment

Kedroff and Milivojevich supply the claim-specific selection defense. Jones preserves neutral independent property adjudication. Bell supplies statutory jurisdiction despite a defeating defense. Smith, Lukumi, Kiryas Joel, Lamb's Chapel, and Clearwater remain within their different subjects; no RFRA application or new §702 race/sex exemption is adopted. Kokkonen's requirement of a jurisdictional source is satisfied, not displaced.

## Law After Decision

The ruling creates no general immunity from civil law for religious institutions, does not enlarge Title VII §702, and leaves RFRA applicability and independently pleaded secular claims outside its holding.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's ministerial-selection defense and jurisdictional distinction. He would vacate the jurisdictional characterization, affirm dismissal of the ministerial-employment claims on the merits, and direct dismissal with prejudice insofar as those claims challenge ordination, ministerial promotion, discipline, assignment, or continued ministerial employment. The Court's mandate also requires substantive dismissal, but he would expressly affirm that disposition while correcting its basis.

In his view ministerial autonomy follows genuine function rather than title: who preaches, leads worship, teaches doctrine, and represents a faith belongs to the religious body. Even substantial evidence that sex entered a selection decision cannot authorize judicial review of whether doctrinal or ministerial reasons were valid or pretextual. That limit does not excuse discrimination in ordinary secular employment and supplies no general church immunity. An independently pleaded and genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim may proceed only if adjudication avoids religious doctrine, ministerial fitness, and the selection of religious leaders; he identifies no such claim in Young's complaint as reported. Public money, tax benefits, licensing, or other governmental contact alone does not waive ministerial autonomy. Government may impose lawful, clearly stated conditions on secular uses of public funds and recover diverted funds, but cannot purchase control of clergy selection. Those additional public-funding observations are his own; the Court does not decide a funding controversy in this case.

No new discrimination trial, ordination, reinstatement, promotion, reconsideration, money, or costs is ordered. The Chief would additionally affirm dismissal on the merits and expressly direct dismissal with prejudice. Review here is complete; entry of the corrected judgment remains below.

## Holdings

#### Section 2 continues to govern covered agreements in state court

**Controlling proposition:** FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court, making them valid, irrevocable and enforceable save upon grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. The Court retains Southland's state-court holding; Alabama may apply generally applicable contract defenses but may not deny enforcement solely because its law singles out predispute arbitration agreements for a categorical disability.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the established state-court application of §2 remains governing law. Southland supplies that substantive construction, and Perry confirms that a State cannot withhold enforcement by assigning a covered dispute to a judicial forum while preserving generally applicable contract defenses. Respondents and supporting States ask the Court to reject that interpretation in light of the Act's federal procedural structure. The statutory-construction objection deserves consideration, but repetition of that original disagreement does not establish a sufficient reason to overturn the settled rule and the reliance it permits across forums. Congress remains able to alter the statute. Alabama's categorical prohibition is directed at arbitration itself, not a defense to contracts generally. Volt does not save that prohibition: it enforces an actual contractual choice of state rules, and no such choice is established by the forum's location alone. Retaining §2's substantive command does not decide whether every direction in §§3 and 4 must operate identically in state courts or settle the assent and scope questions of every claim here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1: retains the substantive state-court application of §2 against the express request to overrule it; its commerce and general-contract-defense conditions remain.
- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483: applies its rejection of a state judicial-forum exception directed at arbitration while preserving generally applicable contract defenses.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Junior University, 489 U.S. 468: preserves enforcement of an actual contractual choice of state arbitration rules; a forum-wide hostility to arbitration is not itself that choice.

#### Coverage turns on actual commerce within Congress's power

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2's phrase involving commerce reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full commerce authority Congress constitutionally possesses, and coverage turns on the transaction's actual interstate commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity when contracting. The multistate service operations and interstate procurement of treatment and repair materials establish the necessary commerce here, requiring reversal of Alabama's coverage ruling; this statutory construction does not define the outer constitutional boundary or make remote interstate history alone conclusive.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven of nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and explanation. Justices Scalia and Thomas would affirm on the state-court ground and do not reach the alternative commerce questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §2 describes the transaction actually made and carried out. Its broad commerce language does not confine coverage to goods moving in an interstate stream, and its reference to a contract evidencing a transaction adds no private expectation or foresight requirement. Bernhardt preserves the need to show a transaction involving commerce; its failure-of-proof setting does not supply Alabama's contemplation test. Here a commercial termite-service arrangement involved firms operating across state lines and actual materials obtained outside Alabama. Local work on the house does not erase those commercial connections. The competing reading would make enforceability turn on what parties predicted about commerce instead of what their transaction involved, although Congress supplied no such mental-state condition. These facts establish genuine interstate commercial operation, rather than a talisman based on the remote history of one object. The ruling resolves statutory coverage under the commerce authority Congress possesses. It neither fixes that authority's furthest constitutional reach nor determines who agreed to arbitrate every pleaded claim or which ordinary contract defenses may succeed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bernhardt v. Polygraphic Co. of America, Inc., 350 U.S. 198: distinguishes a record failing to establish the requisite commerce; its transaction predicate survives, without a newly attributed contemplation requirement.
- Southland, 465 U.S. 1: applies its substantive enforceability rule after identifying the actual covered transaction; general hostility to arbitration does not substitute for the statutory commerce inquiry.

**Remedy and limits:** The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded under the actual-commerce rule. Formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other generally applicable contract defenses remain for ordinary adjudication to the extent properly presented and preserved below, together with party assent, clause scope and appropriate implementing procedure. Coverage is not a final order compelling all parties to arbitrate every claim. Section 1's separate employment exclusion is not presented by this service bond. The Court does not decide the tort merits, damages, a particular fraud-in-the-inducement defense, a contractual choice of state rules not established here, or wholesale state-court incorporation of §§3–4.

## Precedent Treatment

Southland's substantive application in state court is retained, and Perry continues to reject arbitration-specific state barriers while preserving ordinary contract defenses. Volt protects an actual choice of state rules, not an assumed adoption of the forum's antiarbitration policy. Bernhardt retains the commerce predicate but supplies no contemplation requirement. No earlier decision is overruled, and §§3–4's entire state-procedure reach is not decided.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, §2 reaches actual transactions within Congress's constitutional commerce power without subjective contemplation or a stream-only limitation. State courts remain bound by its substantive enforceability rule and full general-contract-defense qualification. The genuine multistate operations and interstate materials suffice here; the outer constitutional ceiling, every party's assent, clause scope and particular contract defenses remain for their proper settings. The decision adds no special immunity from ordinary contract law.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor joins the Court's opinion and concurs separately. She continues to believe that Southland originally misconstrued the Act's application in state courts, for the reasons set out in her Southland and Perry dissents. Statutory stare decisis nevertheless weighs strongly against reopening an interpretation that has governed for a decade without a demonstrated inability to administer it. Once that interpretation is retained, a broad and uniform actual-commerce rule is appropriate; narrowing commerce coverage is not a substitute for overruling Southland. Her original-construction reservation does not subtract from her join in the Court's present rule or mandate.

In his view, reliance on a statutory interpretation cannot by itself preserve a state-court command the statute does not establish. He joins Thomas's state-court ground and would affirm without deciding the Court's commerce construction. These proposed changes to Southland do not control; the Court retains that precedent.

## Holdings

### Saved seed means seed retained for the farmer's own planting

**Controlling proposition:** Under former 7 U.S.C. §2543, a farmer satisfying the statutory conditions may sell for reproductive purposes only seed genuinely saved for replanting the farmer's own acreage; intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing does not become exempt merely because the seller primarily grows food or feed crops. Qualifying later disposition of genuinely retained seed remains possible when planting plans change, without a fixed percentage or mechanical ceiling based on the previous year's acreage.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt the construction, explanation, and application that these sales fall outside the exemption. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela independently accepts the stated construction but does not join the Court's final application; his separate remand position is not counted as an additional application vote.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the farmer-sales proviso protects a limited category of seed, rather than every crop produced by a person whose principal occupation is farming. The opening sentence reserves infringement through multiplication for reproductive marketing, while the proviso permits sale of such saved seed. Those provisions operate together when the farmer initially retains seed for planting and later changes plans. The proviso's direction to disregard §2541(3) prevents the qualifying later sale from retroactively converting lawful production into infringement; it does not erase the opening reservation for production intended for the planting market. The contrary reading gives the sales proviso work only by depriving saved seed and the reservation of their limiting function. Reasonably anticipated acreage expansion and crop-appropriate retention remain possible, so this construction creates no rigid prior-acreage quota. These reproductive-production sales, greatly exceeding the Winterboers' own planting needs, fail the necessary saved-seed condition. Their asserted food-and-feed business proportion cannot cure that failure, and no finding about disputed state-law compliance is needed to establish it.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: follows its respect for the enacted condition in statutory context, without importing the veterans statute's protective construction or inventing a numerical PVPA condition.

**Operative qualifications:** Saved seed must be produced by the person from seed obtained, or descended from seed obtained, with the owner's authority for seeding purposes. Seller and purchaser must each have as their primary farming occupation growing crops for sale for nonreproductive purposes; the sale must comply with applicable state seed-sale laws. The retained §2541(4) restriction on using the variety to produce, rather than develop, a hybrid or different variety remains separate. Bona fide nonreproductive sales in customary nonreproductive channels retain their independent statutory protection; a purchaser who diverts that seed to planting is deemed to have notice under §2567 that the diversion infringes. That diversion provision is distinct from §2541(6)'s notice claim. No general ruling on a buyer's later authorized-source lineage is adopted.

### Marketing does not require an advertising or distribution apparatus

**Controlling proposition:** Former §2541(3)'s multiplication as a step in marketing for growing purposes includes multiplication directed toward holding protected seed forth for sale and the preparatory activities of selling; extensive advertising, intermediaries, or coordinated merchandising are not required. The proviso's primary-farming-occupation condition concerns the farmer's business as a whole, not a judicial rule allowing reproductive sale of less than half of each protected variety's crop, and that occupation condition does not dispense with the distinct saved-seed limitation.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt these related constructions; Justice Stevens rejects the marketing premise. The Chief's separate opinion rejects an advertising prerequisite and a judicial fifty-percent quota without joining the final application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ordinary marketing encompasses offering seed for reproductive sale without the elaborate commercial machinery demanded by the Court of Appeals. The statute regulates multiplication as a step in marketing, so its protection reaches production directed toward the later sale as well as the completed transfer separately described in §2541(1). Using different words for the transfer and its preparatory reproductive activity does not require limiting the latter to wholesalers, cooperatives, or advertising campaigns. The enacted context, rather than a preference for plant breeders, supplies that distinction. The lower court's variety-by-variety percentage rule likewise has no textual foundation. Congress described a person's primary farming occupation, not the percentage of each variety marketed for planting. Reading occupation at the whole-business level does not authorize all reproductive sales by that farmer: the seed itself must still satisfy the own-planting condition. Stevens's narrower commercial-distribution reading would preserve the lower judgment, but it would remove ordinary reproductive-sale production from the reserved protection without a statutory basis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: applies contextual attention to the enacted terms rather than an additional condition; its separate VA-benefits rule remains unchanged.

### The former Act governs these transactions

**Controlling proposition:** The 1994 Plant Variety Protection Act amendments do not govern these transactions or supply the meaning of the former saved-seed provision: Pub. L. 103-349 §15 makes the amendments effective April 4, 1995, after this decision, and §14 preserves former-law treatment for specified older certificates and pending applications subject to its express exceptions. The later deletion of the farmer-sales proviso therefore creates no retrospective liability here and does not establish that every later sale involving an older protected variety loses former-law treatment.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's temporal-text portion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: seven direct opinion votes. The Chief and Justice Stevens also apply former law in their separate writings, without joining every broader temporal rationale of one another or of Landgraf.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Congress's actual temporal directions before considering a general presumption about retrospective consequences. Landgraf and Rivers preserve that starting point, although their broader formulations received different joins. The amendment became law on October 6, 1994 and specified effectiveness 180 days later. The statute therefore has not yet displaced the former law for this January decision, much less retroactively changed the consequences of completed sales. Congress also addressed older certificates and pending applications separately, which forecloses a universal inference that the new farmer-sales restriction immediately reaches all protected varieties. The pendency of this appeal does not change the relevant statutory dates or turn later legislation into conclusive evidence of the prior text's meaning. The Court neither approves nor condemns the new policy. It construes the former Act on its own terms and leaves any future transaction to the provisions that actually govern it, including the transition conditions Congress expressly enacted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies Congress's express temporal direction as the starting point; does not convert Scalia, Kennedy, or Thomas into joiners of its broader general framework.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298 (1994): preserves the separation of later legislative change from the law governing completed conduct; the new amendment is not a conclusive interpretation of old text.

**Transition qualifications:** Section 14(a), except as otherwise provided in §14, preserves prior law for varieties with certificates issued before April 4, 1995 and applications pending then. Section 14(b) permits refiling a pending application on or after that date under amended eligibility and protection terms, while §42 uses the original filing date. Section 14(c) requires a notice claiming amended protection to identify that protection and applies §128 sanctions to false or misleading claims or labels. No relevant refiling or transition exception is found.

**Notice and remedial limits:** The seven-Justice Court opinion does not decide whether a sale actually authorized by §2543 must nevertheless satisfy §2541(6), because these sales fail the saved-seed condition. It makes no separate notice-infringement finding or damages award. Section 2541 concerns unauthorized acts in the United States or regulated or affected commerce, before protection expires but after certificate issuance or distribution with §2567 notice, subject to the subchapter's exceptions. Section 2541(6) concerns dispensing propagable protected material without the protected-variety notice under which it was received; failure of the saved-seed defense alone does not establish those elements. Former §2567 permits owners to give public notice by associating a label with seed or affixing it to its container or the variety, stating that unauthorized propagation or seed multiplication is prohibited and, after the certificate issues, adding a protected-variety designation. When an owner-authorized distribution reaches the infringer without that marking, the owner cannot recover damages unless the infringer has actual awareness that propagation is prohibited or the variety is protected; damages then extend only to infringement after such notice. For both damages and an injunction, a court may be lenient concerning disposal of material acquired in good faith through acts before such notice. These are reserved remedial conditions, not findings of marking, actual awareness, good faith, or entitlement to leniency in this case. Actual relief and precise lawful injunction implementation remain for the receiving courts. The adjudicated own-planting failure is not reopened by that limited return.

## Precedent Treatment

Brown supplies contextual respect for enacted conditions without extending its veterans-specific rule. Landgraf and Rivers support obedience to the express temporal directions, with their actual coalition boundaries preserved. No precedent is overruled, no statutory quota is invented, and the hypothetical notice status of a truly exempt sale remains unresolved by the Court.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, the former Act protects qualifying later disposition of seed genuinely saved for the farmer's own planting, while reproductive-market production and these sales fall outside that condition. Ordinary marketing needs no advertising apparatus. The farmer's primary occupation concerns the business as a whole, without a crop-specific half-crop safe harbor. The Court establishes no final §2541(6) liability or damages and no rule resolving notice for a truly §2543-exempt sale. The 1994 amendments retain their actual future effective date and qualified grandfather provisions.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that the Federal Circuit's broad occupation-based exemption must be reversed and that former law controls. Seed genuinely saved to replant the farmer's own acreage may later be sold to qualified farmers when plans change and the statutory conditions are met. Intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing is outside that protected core, but neither high yield alone nor an invented fifty-percent rule establishes infringement. Marketing does not require advertising. The scale and reproductive destination of these sales support applying the multiplication restriction; he would remand for application of the narrower construction and resolution of unresolved infringement and remedial questions, without joining the Court's conclusive application to every challenged sale.

He separately reaches the legal relationship of the exemption to the notice claim. Former §2543 expressly retains §§2541(3) and (4) but does not separately except §2541(6) from its protection. In his view, a sale actually satisfying the correctly construed exemption remains exempt from notice-infringement liability; if it is not exempt, the receiving court must determine §2541(6)'s ordinary elements and lawful relief. Rejection of the broad exemption does not establish absence of notice, satisfaction of every element, or damages. His remand includes that pleaded claim and requires reconsideration of its exemption premise under the narrower rule. He makes no final notice finding, imposes no new notice form, and imports no later amendment backward. This conditional notice proposition is his alone; the Court expressly leaves it undecided.

Justice Stevens would affirm because Congress used marketing in §2541(3) and selling in §2541(1). He reads marketing more narrowly to address commercial distribution through cooperatives, wholesalers, retailers, or comparable extensive coordinated activity, rather than every ordinary farmer sale. Unclear restraints on alienation should not be enlarged by treating those distinct terms as equivalent. He accepts that the Court's own-planting exposition follows if its broad marketing premise is adopted, but rejects that premise. His position is therefore not simply a preference for a different acreage quota or an exemption from every statutory condition. He applies former law and would preserve the Federal Circuit's judgment and eligibility remand; he makes no independent final notice-liability or damages finding.

## Procedure After Action

The Federal Circuit judgment is reversed and the case returns for proceedings consistent with the Court's construction and seven-Justice determination that these sales fail the saved-seed condition. The receiving courts address genuinely unresolved notice elements, §2567 damages limits, and lawful relief; they do not reopen that controlling failure or automatically adopt every term of the earlier injunction. No damages amount or separate notice-infringement finding is entered. The Chief would remand for application and his stated conditional notice treatment; Stevens would affirm.

NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co., No. 93-1612, and Ludwig v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co., No. 93-1613, consolidated. Argued December 7, 1994; merits decision January 18, 1995. ## Participation

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Fifth Circuit in both consolidated dockets, sustaining the Comptroller's conditioned approval against the statutory objections presented.

## Holdings

#### Reasoned approval of annuity brokerage falls within the banking power

**Controlling proposition:** The business of banking under 12 U.S.C. §24 Seventh is not exhausted by its enumerated activities, and the Comptroller's reasonable construction may include financial-intermediary agency sales of the fixed, variable and combined annuity products covered by this approval. The construction must remain within banking and its incidental powers and comply with enacted restrictions; this conditioned brokerage approval authorizes no underwriting, issuance or guarantee of the insurers' obligations and no unrelated commercial enterprise.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight of nine participating Justices directly adopt this rule and its agency-review rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments on an independent statutory ground and does not join this opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Act leaves room for a reasoned administrative judgment about financial services within banking. Section 24 Seventh identifies core activities but also grants the incidental powers necessary to carry on the business of banking. Clarke supplies specific support for substantial respect for the Comptroller's reasonable construction of that banking authority in the brokerage setting. Chevron requires courts first to enforce clear statutory boundaries; MCI and Gardner preserve that obligation rather than eliminating lawful interpretive discretion. Here the bank acts as agent between customers and insurers, offering financial instruments used for saving and investment. It does not issue the products or bear the insurer's actuarial or interest-rate risk. Camp requires genuine expert judgment, not a rationale invented by counsel, and the approval gives the relevant product and agency reasons. The contrary reading treats the enumerated activities as the entire banking business, overlooking the enacted broader language. The holding sustains this approval within its disclosure conditions, without authorizing every profitable financial activity or a bank's own-account underwriting.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Clarke v. Securities Industry Association, 479 U.S. 388: applies its substantial respect for reasonable Comptroller banking-law construction and brokerage analysis to this agency activity.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies deference within the ambiguity left after the enacted banking boundaries are determined; clear prohibitions still govern.
- Investment Company Institute v. Camp, 401 U.S. 617: preserves substantive banking restrictions and the need for an actual reasoned agency judgment, which this approval supplies.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218, and Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115: preserve text-first review and actual statutory limits; neither displaces permissible construction within a genuine delegation.

#### Section 92 does not defeat this annuity classification

**Controlling proposition:** The Comptroller reasonably classifies the annuity agency products covered by this approval as investment instruments outside the insurance category of 12 U.S.C. §92 for this statutory purpose, so §92 does not invalidate the approval. The Court does not decide whether §92 exclusively limits genuine insurance-agency activity to banks in qualifying small places, and it neither repeals that provision's conditions nor determines classification under a distinct state insurance law.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight of nine participating Justices directly adopt this rule and its agency-review rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments on an independent statutory ground and does not join this opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a product's label under other regulatory laws does not compel its classification under this banking provision. National Bank of Oregon establishes that §92 remains in force, but left its reach and the Comptroller's interpretation undecided. Deferred annuities can accumulate funds and permit lump-sum distribution or systematic withdrawal; variable products tie returns to selected investments. A lifetime payout option carries mortality risk, but it does not describe every use or feature of these instruments. The Comptroller therefore reasonably treated these agency sales as investment intermediation. Chevron and Clarke permit that bounded construction where Congress has not required the contrary classification. The Fifth Circuit inferred an exclusive geographic insurance prohibition from §92's specific small-place authorization and treated annuities categorically as insurance. The first inference need not be resolved because the second premise is not compelled. Section 92 begins by adding to existing powers; the Court leaves its application to true insurance sales intact. This judgment neither authorizes bank guarantees nor displaces any otherwise preserved state regulation.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America, Inc., 508 U.S. 439: applies the statutory-survival holding and respects its reservation of scope and agency-construction questions; no repeal issue is reopened.
- Chevron, 467 U.S. 837, and Clarke, 479 U.S. 388: apply permissible, reasoned construction to this statutory classification after examining the text and product features.

**Conditions and limits:** The Comptroller's approval separately requires advertising, promotional materials and customer descriptions to disclose that the annuities are not bank products and are not FDIC insured, together with a signed purchaser acknowledgment of agency status and lack of federal insurance. Brokerage does not issue, underwrite or guarantee an insurer's undertaking. Applicable banking, securities and insurance requirements otherwise remain.

When §92 applies, it requires the national bank to be located and doing business in a place of no more than 5,000 inhabitants under the last preceding decennial census; the insurer must be authorized by the State where the bank is located; and the agency must operate under Comptroller rules. It permits solicitation and sale of insurance and collection of premiums for agreed fees or commissions, but prohibits the bank from assuming or guaranteeing premium payment and separately prohibits guaranteeing the truth of the insured applicant's statements. Those statutory terms are not the same as this approval's disclosure conditions. Section 24's distinction between without-recourse, customer-order/customer-account securities transactions and own-account activity remains; no own-account investment exception is adjudicated. No individual customer's contract, universal annuity classification or separate state-law preemption dispute is resolved.

## Precedent Treatment

Clarke and Chevron govern reasonable banking-law construction within an actual delegation; Camp preserves enacted restrictions and genuine administrative reasoning. National Bank of Oregon settles §92's survival and leaves the present scope question open for decision. MCI and Gardner continue to require obedience to statutory boundaries; no general retreat from their text-first rule or from Chevron is adopted.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, the conditioned annuity agency business is permissible under the reasonable banking and classification constructions adopted by the Court. The listed banking activities do not exhaust the business of banking, but real banking bounds and express restrictions remain. Section 92's survival, small-place conditions and two prohibitions remain intact whenever that provision applies. The Court leaves broader insurance-agency geography, underwriting and separate state-law preemption unresolved. Stone-Zsela's independent statutory reading does not replace the controlling agency-review rationale.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments in both dockets. He independently reads §24 Seventh to permit brokerage of financial investment products closely connected with banking. These annuities serve retirement saving and asset management, while the bank's agency role avoids underwriting the issuer's risk. The Comptroller's expertise and reasoned explanation are persuasive confirmation of the statutory fit; deference cannot supply a power the statute withholds. He does not join the Court's agency-deference rationale.

In his reading, §92's limited insurance authorization neither compels treating these annuities as insurance nor prohibits this incidental banking activity. He would reinstate the conditioned approval subject to applicable banking, securities and insurance rules. The approval gives no general power to underwrite insurance and displaces no state regulation preserved by federal law. His independent construction supplies his judgment votes, not a replacement for the eight-Justice controlling rationale.

Advertising and customer disclosures, the signed purchaser acknowledgment and other applicable law remain conditions of operation. No new agency proceeding, individual contract recovery or additional business permission is ordered.

## Event

United States v. Mezzanatto, No. 93-1340. Argued November 2, 1994; decided January 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 998 F.2d 1452 (1993), which reversed a methamphetamine conviction and ordered a new trial because Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) categorically barred enforcement of the advance impeachment waiver. The question is whether the represented defendant could knowingly and voluntarily permit use of plea-discussion statements to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. He and counsel requested the October 17, 1991 discussion; after counsel consultation he accepted the impeachment condition. He later gave inconsistent testimony and preserved an objection to the Government's impeachment use. Render form: full. ## Holdings

### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule forbidding admission without consent does not necessarily forbid a defendant from giving informed consent. Ricketts enforces the actual terms of a lawful agreement, while Rumery rejects categorical invalidity without dispensing with review of voluntariness and overreaching. The evidentiary Rules contain no express antiwaiver command. Their carefully limited exceptions do not themselves permit ordinary impeachment, but that does not answer the separate question of voluntary relinquishment. Congress's interest in candid negotiation deserves respect; the record does not establish that this counseled, limited agreement necessarily defeats it. Mezzanatto consulted counsel, accepted the impeachment condition, and presented no individualized claim that the acceptance was unknowing or involuntary. Predictions of universal prosecutorial abuse do not invalidate that agreement categorically. Godinez keeps competence distinct from actual informed assent, and Olano keeps intentional relinquishment distinct from failure to object. Neither supplies a universal waiver rule. The Court enforces only the agreement governing this defendant's inconsistent testimony, leaving supported validity challenges and broader uses to their own facts and legal requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Application and remedy:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver rule and the new-trial order resting on it are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the limited waiver decision; no new mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction, or sentencing ruling is ordered. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supports reversal and remand under his separately stated Government-proof requirement. Stevens and Souter would affirm the new-trial judgment. A preserved nonconstitutional error actually found remains subject to Rule 52(a), Williamson, and Kotteakos: the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt precludes harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and the ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error under Olano is not substituted for that rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

## Precedent Treatment

The Court uses Ricketts, Rumery, Brady, and Peretz within their actual consent settings, distinguishes Brooklyn Savings's particular public protection, and preserves Salerno's enacted conditions. Godinez and Olano retain separate capacity, actual-assent, and forfeiture inquiries. Williamson and Kotteakos continue to govern any preserved nonconstitutional error actually found. No universal waiver rule, ordinary nonconsensual impeachment exception, or broader-use rule is adopted.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, a knowing and voluntary advance agreement can waive Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) for impeachment of the defendant's own inconsistent testimony. Six Justices adopt enforcement absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, subject to supported individual challenges. The Chief's distinct Government-proof requirement remains noncontrolling. The Rules' express exceptions and independent evidentiary safeguards remain in force; other uses and fundamental nonwaivable protections are reserved.

## Separate Writings

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justices O'Connor and Breyer, joins the Court and emphasizes the limited issue before it. Impeachment follows the defendant's own decision to give inconsistent trial testimony. The Government did not seek to use the proffer as proof in its case in chief, a use that could affect the incentive for candid negotiations differently. Agreement with the limited waiver therefore supplies no answer about that different agreement or other broader uses. The concurrence preserves a boundary of the Court's holding, without imposing a separate public-interest burden or requiring another hearing on an unraised individual validity issue.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal and would remand under a distinct allocation of proof. A represented defendant may knowingly and voluntarily agree before the proffer that the Government may use his statements solely to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. The Government invokes that waiver and controls the proposed condition, so it must prove knowing and voluntary acceptance. Representation and competence do not settle whether the actual term was understood and freely accepted; clarity, prosecutorial threats or deception, and the circumstances of the demand matter to that inquiry. He does not presume actual coercion from bargaining or require a new hearing when the existing record permits the necessary determination. Enforcement remains subject to ordinary constitutional and evidentiary safeguards.

His ground does not approve case-in-chief or sentencing use, or a waiver effectively eliminating the right to trial. Defense counsel's cross-examination, independent defense evidence, and argument of another theory do not ordinarily trigger this personal-testimony waiver. Any broader trigger would require a separate explicit agreement and a distinct validity analysis, neither of which is decided. Existing rule-based perjury, false-statement, and comparable-abuse exceptions retain their own prerequisites. He therefore joins the judgment rejecting categorical nonwaivability while declining the Court's affirmative-challenge presumption. His Government-proof requirement is not the Court's controlling allocation.

Justice Souter, joined by Justice Stevens, would affirm the Ninth Circuit's new-trial judgment. General waiver doctrine does not resolve whether Congress protected plea discussions partly for the judicial system's benefit. Brooklyn Savings demonstrates that private consent cannot always defeat a public statutory policy, and these Rules deliberately encourage candid bargaining through enumerated, limited exceptions. Allowing an advance waiver to become the price of entering discussions can remove the protection before its function begins. Peretz's consent to allocation of a trial function and ordinary evidentiary waiver do not decide that distinct statutory design. The dissent neither denies all criminal waivers nor finds Mezzanatto personally coerced. It would sustain the preserved objection, and the lower court already found the admission nonharmless; its result is a new trial rather than acquittal or a rule of automatic reversal for every violation.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment and the retrial direction based on categorical nonwaivability are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the bounded impeachment decision. The Court orders no additional validity hearing, automatic retrial, acquittal, or sentencing change. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand with enforcement conditioned on Government proof of knowing and voluntary acceptance; Stevens and Souter would affirm the existing new-trial judgment.

## Event

McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., No. 93-1543. Argued November 2, 1994; decided January 23, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for the employer, 9 F.3d 539. The question is whether wrongdoing discovered after discharge bars every remedy for an age-discriminatory discharge and, if not, how it bears on available relief. Render form: compact. Basis: unanimous judgment and a common statutory liability-and-remedy rationale.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision. The Court has nine participating members.

## Public Action

Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

The Sixth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and the case remanded, 9–0. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer support both rejection of the complete bar and the stated remedial rule. No Justice opposes either component. The district court must adjudicate liability without treating unknown misconduct as a cause of the original discharge and determine relief under the employer’s actual-would-discharge showing and the statutory limits. No damages amount or final liability judgment is entered.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Every participating Justice joins each holding, its stated limits, and the judgment. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation

**Controlling proposition:** Misconduct unknown to the employer when it discharged an employee cannot defeat liability for an age-discriminatory discharge or categorically bar every remedy. The employee must still establish an ADEA violation under the governing liability rules; an employer’s assumption of discrimination for summary judgment is not a final liability finding.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this proposition and explanation; it is a direct majority holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The ADEA forbids discharge because of age and gives courts authority to grant relief that serves the statute’s purposes. Hazen Paper requires age actually to motivate the challenged decision and have a determinative influence on it; a reason the employer did not know cannot have motivated it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse address lawful and unlawful reasons operating in the actual decision, so their same-decision inquiry does not transform later discovery into an original cause. The Banner assumed discrimination only to obtain summary judgment and relied on documents discovered after McKennon’s dismissal. That discovery cannot erase an otherwise proved violation. The contrary rule would remove compensation for an unlawful period and weaken enforcement whenever later investigation uncovered a sufficient ground for termination. The employee’s misconduct nevertheless matters to remedy, which the next holding addresses. The Court does not find age discrimination, change the employee’s ultimate burden, or decide whether the violation was willful.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper’s actual-motive requirement remains controlling; Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse are distinguished because they concern reasons involved in the challenged decision. St. Mary’s Honor Center retains its existing proof framework and does not make rejection of an employer’s explanation an automatic liability judgment.

### Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief

**Controlling proposition:** To limit relief through after-acquired evidence, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone had it known, rather than merely showing that discharge was permissible. Once that showing is made, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate, and backpay’s starting measure is the period from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the proof requirement and bounded remedial rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 626(b) authorizes appropriate legal and equitable relief to effectuate the ADEA, which requires attention to both discrimination and the employment consequences of serious misconduct. An order requiring continued employment or its prospective monetary substitute ordinarily makes no sense when proved wrongdoing would have ended that employment lawfully. But the employer must establish what it actually would have done; an available pretext, minor infraction, or merely possible discharge does not establish that limit. Backpay ordinarily compensates the interval before actual discovery, when the later ground had not supplied a lawful termination basis. The court may account for extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests, consistently with the statutory purposes. The Banner’s affidavits and McKennon’s acknowledgment supported its motion-specific would-discharge showing, but the erroneous complete bar prevented proper adjudication of liability and relief. The Court awards no amount, imposes no reinstatement decree, and leaves statutory willfulness and other distinct remedial predicates for their proper determination.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper’s knowledge-or-recklessness standard continues to govern any claim of willfulness; this remedial ruling does not lower it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse supply no complete forfeiture rule for a reason discovered after the challenged discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine protected opposition or participation cannot supply a lawful discharge ground; this case announces no general ban on obtaining evidence of discrimination. No final discrimination, willfulness, compensation, liquidated-damages, fee, or reinstatement finding is made. Section 626(c)(1)’s termination of the individual right to bring an action when the EEOC commences an action to enforce that employee’s right remains distinct; no such agency action is established here. The 1991 Title VII amendments supply no new rule for this ADEA discharge.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins preserves actual age motivation and determinative influence as the liability inquiry and knowledge or reckless disregard as the willfulness inquiry. After-acquired evidence changes neither requirement.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle addresses actual mixed reasons for the challenged action; it does not make an unknown later reason a complete defense.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins likewise concerns contemporaneous decisionmaking and supplies no after-acquired-evidence forfeiture rule.
- St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks preserves the governing burden of persuasion and rejects automatic liability from disbelief of an employer’s explanation; this decision does not alter that allocation.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 23, 1995, later-discovered misconduct supplies a possible limitation on ADEA remedies, not erasure of an otherwise proved discriminatory discharge. The employer’s actual-would-discharge burden, ordinary limits on reinstatement and front pay, and discovery-based backpay measure with extraordinary-equity consideration govern the remand. Actual age motivation, the employee’s ultimate persuasion obligation, and the separate willfulness rule remain intact.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion was filed.

## Procedure After Action

The Sixth Circuit must return the case for proceedings consistent with the liability/remedy distinction. Liability remains to be adjudicated; any relief must rest on its statutory and factual predicates. This Court enters no amount, no final willfulness finding, and no order of reinstatement or front pay.

## Source Notes

The record and lower-court grounds appear in [9 F.3d 539](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/009/9.F3d.539.92-5917.html), reviewing 797 F. Supp. 604. The remedial authority and its qualifications appear in [29 U.S.C. §626, 1994 edition](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-1994-title29-section626&num=0&edition=1994). The motion-specific assumption of age discrimination is not a trial finding; the opinion decides the legal effect of later-discovered misconduct and leaves unresolved liability and relief to the lower courts.

## Event

Schlup v. Delo, No. 93-7901. Argued October 3, 1994; merits decision January 23, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, reviewing 11 F.3d 738, which sustained denial of a second federal habeas application without the requested evidentiary hearing. Schlup offers crime-innocence evidence as a gateway to otherwise barred ineffective-assistance and suppression claims arising from his conviction for participation in a prison murder. The questions concern the gateway quantum, the augmented evidentiary inquiry, statutory refusal authority and the proper remand. Render form: full; judgment, gateway rationale and statutory-refusal rationale have distinct coalitions and partial joins.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Eighth Circuit judgment and remands with instructions to return the matter to the District Court for proceedings under the probability crime-innocence gateway. It does not find Schlup innocent or find the gateway satisfied.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rejection of the innocence gateway and denial of renewed review | Vacate and remand, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Eighth Circuit to remand to District Court for the correct whole-record probability assessment and a reasoned determination of needed factual development. |

The three dissenters would affirm under the stricter crime-innocence showing; Scalia and Thomas also support a bounded statutory-discretion ground. The mandate orders no automatic hearing, constitutional-merits adjudication, retrial, writ, release or stay.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Stone-Zsela in the gateway/all-evidence portion only | Vacatur and remand | Six directly support the gateway rule; five directly support the statutory-refusal/remand rationale. Stone supports the same judgment on his stated ground. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins gateway portion and vacatur/remand | Adds nonexclusive reliability guidance; no join to separate §2244(b) analysis. |

## Holdings

### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the probability inquiry concerns innocence of the crime, not whether a concededly guilty person qualified for a death sentence. Carrier supplies the exceptional route where constitutional error probably convicted an innocent person; Sawyer preserves its different eligibility inquiry without deciding this crime question. McCleskey retains the innocence exception while enforcing finality through barriers to repetitive and abusive claims. The prisoner's burden remains demanding because the question concerns reasonable jurors considering the augmented evidentiary record, not the habeas judge's personal doubt. Kuhlmann's plurality discussion supports examination of all probative evidence, but its formulations are not converted into a separate majority holding. Jackson's trial-record sufficiency question cannot replace that task: evidence sufficient when credited at trial may have a different effect when reliable new evidence is considered with it. The State's request for one uniformly higher burden disregards the different question and the gateway's limited function. Passing this threshold permits further consideration within the governing claim rules; it does not award a writ or adjudicate innocence.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Operative evidentiary qualifications:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Controlling proposition:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that discretion to refuse renewed review must be exercised under the proper governing law. McCleskey explains both the statutory permission and its qualifications; neither the label second petition nor the filing of new affidavits decides the result. The earlier federal consideration of guilt-phase ineffective assistance supplies a real legal-merits proceeding, and the lower court's additional discussion of that claim remains relevant. It does not establish an independently sufficient ground disposing of every claim despite today's legal correction. The new accounts concern the meaning of the existing videotape, identification and the interval between attack and response. The lower analysis used a stricter burden and treated credibility too categorically as the original jury's task. Those features prevent accepting it as the required probability assessment. Even assuming the ultimate refusal is discretionary, reconsideration is required here; the Court need not settle the broader discretion question. Keeney and Townsend preserve necessary factual development of properly available claims, but their predicates are not established merely by this remand. The District Court must decide what further development this gateway assessment needs.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Governing statutory terms:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

**Application, reservations and remedy:** The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

Carrier supplies the crime-innocence route, while Sawyer retains its distinct clear-and-convincing eligibility rule. McCleskey's statutory qualifications, abuse pleading, cause/prejudice framework and innocence exception remain. Kuhlmann's relevant discussion retains plurality force, and Jackson's trial-record sufficiency rule remains separate. Keeney and Townsend govern necessary factual development within their actual available-claim conditions. Herrera retains only its record-specific insufficiency holding, Delo v. Blair supplies no precedent from equal division, Brecht remains a preserved-error merits decision, and McFarland supplies no automatic gateway or stay. No prior controlling holding is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 23, 1995, reliable new evidence of crime innocence is assessed with the whole probative record under the more-likely-than-not/no-reasonable-juror gateway. The prisoner retains that demanding burden and must separately establish any underlying constitutional claim. Section 2244(b)'s qualified permission, lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain; the Court decides neither a universal mandatory-review rule after a successful showing nor a general appellate standard for refusal. The judgment corrects the legal inquiry and creates no freestanding innocence remedy.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's probability gateway and all-evidence rule and concurs in vacatur and remand. Finality is weighty, but the procedural route remains available where reliable new evidence indicates that constitutional error may have convicted an innocent person. Sawyer's death-eligibility threshold answers a different question. The lower courts must evaluate the videotape, timing and eyewitness accounts together with the trial record; passage through the gateway authorizes consideration of otherwise barred constitutional claims within their governing rules, not an innocence judgment or automatic release.

He adds guidance for the reliability inquiry. The habeas court should explain the new evidence's reliability, any independent corroboration, why the evidence was unavailable at trial, whether delay impairs credibility, whether state misconduct or ineffective counsel explains its absence, and exactly how it changes the evidentiary case the jury heard. These are nonexclusive considerations relevant to reliability and probability, not additional elements, a checklist or a new diligence requirement. He does not decide the witnesses' truthfulness or shift the underlying claims' ultimate burden. He does not join the Court's separate statutory-refusal analysis or announce a general rule governing residual refusal discretion.

Justice Kennedy dissents. Justice Thomas joins his opinion in full; Justice Scalia joins its stricter-gateway rule and application, but not its alternative discussion of the probability formulation. Kennedy would require clear and convincing evidence, on the probative record as a whole, that no reasonable juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. He proposes extending Sawyer's quantum to crime innocence; he does not claim that Sawyer already decided that question. McCleskey's treatment of abusive litigation and the exceptional nature of renewed review support the more rigorous showing in his view. The continuing identifications, uncertain timing and delayed or inconsistent accounts leave the submitted evidence insufficient under that quantum. This is not a finding that every new witness lied or that any inculpatory trial evidence invariably defeats an innocence claim.

Kennedy and Thomas additionally question the Court's predictive formulation if a probability rule is used. They would adapt Jackson's reasonable-juror approach to the enlarged evidentiary record, with limited factual inquiry where the submissions indicate that the necessary innocence showing may be made. They do not substitute the unchanged trial-record-only sufficiency test, order a hearing automatically, or establish an independent failure under a correctly applied Carrier rule. Their proposed stricter rule and application would sustain the judgment; their alternative discussion does not control.

Justice Scalia also dissents in an opinion joined by Justice Thomas. Section 2244(b), in their reading, preserves real authority to decline successive or abusive review, subject to its two qualifications and review for abuse. The earlier federal legal-merits adjudication satisfies a hearing alternative despite the lack of the now-requested factual hearing. They rely on the accepted repeat/abuse posture, the actual consideration and refusal below, and the evidentiary submission to find no abuse; they also regard the stricter gateway as correct. They do not convert permission into mandatory dismissal, classify every present claim as previously adjudicated, or find an unexamined ground deliberately withheld.

Salinger and Sanders support consideration of prior litigation within their respective settings, while McCleskey preserves the statute's conditions and judicial limits on discretion. Wong Doo shows that an adequate independent discretionary ground can sometimes preserve a judgment despite a legal error. Scalia and Thomas do not use that proposition to declare these affidavits necessarily insufficient under a correctly applied probability rule. Their statutory rationale is additional to the stricter-standard objection, but its application here shares their conclusion that the lower court used a legally proper quantum. They recognize that misconception of law or arbitrary reasons can constitute abuse and announce no unconditional affirmance should the proper gateway later be satisfied. These views do not control the remand.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated. That court must remand to the District Court for the proper gateway assessment, including a reasoned decision about factual development. The existing videotape and new timing and identification accounts must be considered together; the Court predetermines no credibility finding, gateway success or hearing requirement. Otherwise barred constitutional claims may proceed only within the gateway and other governing conditions, and passing the gateway proves none of their merits. Supreme Court review ends; the habeas proceeding continues below without an automatic writ, retrial, release or stay.
