## Standards and Tests selection: Timely substantive reconsideration

### Timely substantive reconsideration in a Government criminal appeal

**Governing question:** When a timely Government motion for reconsideration suspends appellate finality of a suppression ruling.

**Current rule:** A single timely motion seeking substantive alteration of a district court's suppression ruling renders the ruling nonfinal for appeal purposes until the court decides the motion, including when the motion renews a ground the Government earlier abandoned. The full appeal period begins on disposition of the motion. This finality rule does not determine whether the renewed ground remains waived on the merits.

**Authority by component:**

- Suspension of finality, restart of the full appeal period, and absence of an abandoned-ground exception: [United States v. Ibarra — Timely substantive reconsideration in a Government criminal appeal](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Ibarra_merits_1991-10-15.md), October 15, 1991; unanimous participating per curiam opinion.

**Present operation effective:** *Ibarra*, October 15, 1991.

**Open question:** *Ibarra* leaves bad-faith and successive motions outside its decision.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Ibarra, 502 U.S. 1 (1991) — Appellate finality](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-ibarra-502-us-1-1991).

## Standards and Tests selection: APA limits on exhaustion

### APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals

**Governing question:** What instructions govern APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals?

**Current rule:** In an APA action, an otherwise final agency decision is reviewable without an optional superior-agency appeal unless a statute expressly requires exhaustion or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the action inoperative pending it. Courts cannot add exhaustion beyond those alternatives. APA finality, reviewability and adequate-remedy conditions remain; express statutory exhaustion and non-APA judicial exhaustion retain their own operation.

**Authority by component:**

- Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review: [Darby v. Cisneros — Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Darby_v_Cisneros_merits_1993-06-21.md), 1993-06-21. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-21; Darby v. Cisneros, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** [Darby v. Cisneros, 509 U.S. 137 (1993)](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#darby-v-cisneros-509-us-137-1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Disclosure of known direct-negation

### Disclosure of known direct-negation evidence and supervisory dismissal

**Governing question:** A federal prosecutor's duty to disclose known evidence before seeking an indictment, and the distinct conditions permitting supervisory dismissal for nondisclosure.

**Current rule:** Before seeking an indictment, a federal prosecutor must present or otherwise disclose to the grand jury personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when omission would materially distort the presentation. This requires neither a search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every potential defense. Violation alone does not establish entitlement to dismissal. Supervisory authority permits dismissal without prejudice when the prosecutor withholds such personally known substantial direct-negation evidence, the omission materially distorts the presentation, the evidence would plainly preclude probable cause, and the violation substantially influenced the indictment or leaves grave doubt that it was free of that influence. Those additional conditions identify a sufficient, nonexclusive dismissal class: plain preclusion of probable cause is not part of the disclosure trigger, and the decision does not forbid relief in every other circumstance. The Government may seek a lawful new indictment.

**Authority by component:**
- Disclosure command, timing, recipient and material-distortion qualification: [United States v. Williams](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Williams_merits_1992-05-04.md), May 4, 1992, “Disclosure of known evidence directly negating guilt.” The record's Marks determination identifies the shared rule of Stevens's Parts II–III, joined by Blackmun, O'Connor and Thomas, and Stone's separate merits opinion; Stone's material-distortion qualification remains operative.
- Sufficient dismissal class, independent prejudice and without-prejudice remedy: the same record, “Confined supervisory power over a materially misleading presentation.” The four-Justice direct-negation/plainly-no-probable-cause ground, with material distortion and charging prejudice, is the record's controlling logical subset of Stone's broader known-element-undermining/material-distortion rule with the same prejudice and remedy. Stone does not formally join the narrower qualification, and no single majority merits opinion exists.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Costello's evidentiary-competence and sufficiency restrictions remain for otherwise lawful presentations. Calandra's grand-jury independence and freedom from trial evidence rules remain. Brady's trial obligation is not transplanted. The particular accounting evidence was outside the accepted authority-only question; affirmance creates no general factual conclusion that evidence bearing on intent necessarily satisfies the controlling sufficient dismissal class.

**Open question:** Applications outside the sufficient dismissal class, a constitutional disclosure duty, state grand juries, negligent discovery and unpresented immunity or fabrication claims remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Williams, 504 U.S. 36 (1992) — Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-williams-504-us-36-1992-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence error

### Preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review?

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. For preserved Doyle error properly before a federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the verdict, considering the whole record. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error: [Brecht v. Abrahamson — The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/brecht_merits_1993-04-21.md), 1993-04-21. White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas: [Brecht v. Abrahamson — Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/brecht_merits_1993-04-21.md), 1993-04-21. White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-04-21; Brecht v. Abrahamson, for the components identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial-speech advancement

### Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions?

**Current rule:** For truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech about lawful activity, the government must establish real harms, material advancement and a reasonable fit between its chosen restriction and its interests. Where commercial and newspaper racks cause the same physical harms, the commercial category requires justification tied to those harms; benign objectives alone do not make content-selected regulation content neutral. Incremental regulation is not invalid merely because its reduction is small, but alternatives bear on fit without a least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement. In professional solicitation the government must demonstrate advancement in the relevant professional setting, without an invariable empirical-study requirement. Deceptive or coercive solicitation remains regulable. Central Hudson and Fox remain the framework; no general equality of commercial and noncommercial speech or categorical vice exception follows.

**Authority by component:**

- 1. The selected commercial category lacks the required fit: [Cincinnati v. Discovery Network — 1. The selected commercial category lacks the required fit](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Cincinnati_v_Discovery_Network_merits_1993-03-24.md), 1993-03-24. Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven, throughout.
- 2. The selective prohibition is not content neutral: [Cincinnati v. Discovery Network — 2. The selective prohibition is not content neutral](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Cincinnati_v_Discovery_Network_merits_1993-03-24.md), 1993-03-24. Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.
- Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting: [Edenfield v. Fane — Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/edenfield_merits_1993-04-26.md), 1993-04-26. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Present operation effective:** Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993, for the selective-newsrack fit and content distinction. Central Hudson and Fox supply the retained advancement requirement applied in Edenfield, April 26, 1993; that application creates no separate test.

**Holdings navigation:** [Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#cincinnati-v-discovery-network-507-us-410-1993); [Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761 (1993)](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#edenfield-v-fane-507-us-761-1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Retrospective withdrawal

### Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

**Governing question:** Whether a later law may double the interval between mandatory discretionary-parole hearings for an earlier offender.

**Current rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining intervening review is established. That binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence and ultimate release discretion; the prisoner need not prove the omitted hearing would have produced release. Relief restores the consideration opportunity, not an entitlement to parole.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory annual-to-biennial withdrawal, proof and relief boundary: [Cavanaugh v. Roller — The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Cavanaugh_v_Roller_merits_1993-11-30.md#public-projection), 1993-11-30. Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

**Present operation effective:** Cavanaugh v. Roller, 1993-11-30.

**Open question:** Individualized postponement based on no material change warranting release, meaningful interim review, changed voting or suitability rules, abolition of parole and the reach of extraordinary clemency remain unresolved. No general probability formula governs other changes.

**Holdings navigation:** [The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#the-binding-loss-of-annual-reconsideration-increases-punishment-on-this-record).

## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective parole-consideration

### Prospective parole-consideration claims under §1983

**Governing question:** Whether a prisoner must use habeas to restore the required frequency of discretionary parole consideration.

**Current rule:** A state prisoner may seek prospective declaratory and injunctive relief under §1983 against responsible officials to restore the legally required frequency of discretionary parole consideration when the judgment neither orders release nor establishes entitlement to release or shorter custody. Habeas is not the exclusive channel for that opportunity-only claim, and no general state-remedy exhaustion requirement attaches to the properly brought §1983 action.

**Authority by component:**

- Opportunity-only channel and absence of general exhaustion: [Cavanaugh v. Roller — Section 1983 permits this opportunity-only claim](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Cavanaugh_v_Roller_merits_1993-11-30.md#public-projection), 1993-11-30. Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the same rule and affirm this remedial channel.

**Present operation effective:** Cavanaugh v. Roller, 1993-11-30.

**Related current rules:** “Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation” governs the separate in-custody damages prerequisite; Cavanaugh’s opportunity-only relief remains available.

**Holdings navigation:** [Section 1983 permits this opportunity-only claim](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#section-1983-permits-this-opportunity-only-claim).

## Standing State selection: 3. Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). It ordinarily issues without opinion; a brief per curiam and noted dissents are available. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the [opening Standing State](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/13b19ee463d16fb4377d846e5b058599f886bed3/state/STANDING_STATE.md#3-standing-practices) and the [OT1993 Term Continuity Note](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/workspace/continuity.md#standing-practices). Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`; heading: United States v. Ibarra

#### United States v. Ibarra, 502 U.S. 1 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1713.  
**Decided:** October 15, 1991.

##### Whether a timely reconsideration motion renewing an abandoned suppression ground postpones appellate finality

**Holding:** A single timely motion asking a district court to alter a suppression ruling renders that ruling nonfinal for appeal purposes until the motion is decided, even when the motion renews a ground the Government earlier abandoned. The full appeal period begins upon disposition of the motion; the rule does not decide whether the renewed ground remains waived on the merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; unanimous eight-Justice participating Court.

**Material application:** The Government's January 30 notice of appeal was filed within thirty days after the January 3 denial of reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Bad-faith and successive motions, the substantive effect of abandonment, and the Fourth Amendment validity of the search are unresolved. Existing appeal periods and preservation rules are unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the appeal is vacated, and the case is remanded for further appellate proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** United States v. Healy supplies suspension of finality on a timely criminal rehearing request. United States v. Dieter forecloses an exception based on the kind or strength of the asserted error. Department of Banking v. Pink is distinguished because its filing sought no substantive alteration of adjudicated rights.


## Verbatim Holdings selection

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`; heading: United States v. Williams

#### United States v. Williams, 504 U.S. 36 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1972.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Williams*, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1972, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (reviewability) and Criminal Procedure — Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal (disclosure duty and dismissal authority).

##### What known evidence a federal prosecutor must disclose to avoid materially distorting a grand-jury presentation

**Holding:** Before seeking an indictment, a federal prosecutor must present or otherwise disclose to the grand jury personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when its omission would materially distort the grand-jury presentation. This duty requires neither an investigative search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every possible defense; a violation alone does not establish entitlement to dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, adopt the personally-known/direct-negation duty. Stone’s separate merits opinion adopts that duty where omission materially distorts the presentation. Under Marks, these five judgment supporters establish the stated common requirement; the four-Justice formulation does not remove Stone’s material-distortion qualification from the controlling rule.

**Material application:** The duty concerns substantial evidence personally known to the prosecutor, not an investigative search or a preliminary trial of every defense. Misconduct and charging prejudice are separate inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns authority, not the accounting documents’ specific character. Affirmance does not establish that every intent-related item meets the duty or that the prosecutor fabricated evidence. Negligent discovery, state-grand-jury duties, constitutional disclosure, immunity and fabrication claims remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — its protective function and enforceable process limits support the confined duty; ordinary evidentiary freedom remains.
- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its misconduct and prejudice inquiries remain separate; violation does not automatically warrant dismissal.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — preserved for competence and sufficiency challenges to an otherwise lawful presentation; distinguished from the specified prosecutorial omission.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the judgment supporters’ shared direct-negation duty with the material-distortion qualification retained.

##### When a materially misleading omission permits supervisory dismissal without prejudice

**Holding:** Federal supervisory authority permits dismissal without prejudice when a prosecutor withholds personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense, the omission materially distorts the presentation, the evidence would plainly preclude a finding of probable cause, and the violation substantially influenced the indictment or leaves grave doubt that the indictment was free from that influence. This establishes the stated sufficient ground for dismissal; it requires neither a search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every potential defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s merits Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, supply the narrower judgment-supporting rule. Stone concurs in affirmance under the broader material-distortion formulation, covering known evidence substantially undermining an essential element with established Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, the four-Justice rule is the controlling logical subset: every known, directly negating omission that plainly defeats probable cause, materially distorts the presentation and causes the stated prejudice also satisfies Stone’s broader rule. The five judgment supporters together establish its force. Stone is not represented as formally joining the narrower qualification. The four-Justice and broader one-Justice applications are not combined to remove that qualification from the sufficient ground recognized here. Neither the plurality nor the five judgment supporters adopt an exclusive rule forbidding relief in every other circumstance.

**Material application:** The narrower rule adds evidence plainly precluding probable cause to the shared known-evidence, direct-negation and material-distortion requirements, and independently requires Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. The Government presented a categorical authority objection rather than renewed review of the particular evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a sufficient dismissal class, not an exclusive prohibition on relief outside it. The plurality does not find the accounting documents plainly defeat probable cause; the evidentiary application was outside the grant. Stone’s broader application does not become general law. Guilt, negligent discovery, state grand juries and constitutional disclosure remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its prejudice requirement is retained; limited supervisory power is extended to the specified known-evidence omission. Its evidence-reliability prohibition survives for lawful presentations.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — distinguished: the confined misconduct inquiry is not a challenge to the competence or sufficiency of evidence in an otherwise lawful presentation. Its hearsay and ordinary adequacy rules remain.
- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — preserved for grand-jury independence and freedom from trial evidence rules; no exclusionary-rule extension occurs.
- United States v. Mechanik, 475 U.S. 66 — its completed-trial harmlessness holding is not extended to erase a prejudicial violation challenged and resolved before trial; the charging-prejudice formulation adopted in Bank remains operative.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 — not transplanted into grand-jury proceedings; its trial disclosure duty remains unchanged.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the genuine logical subset of the five judgment supporters’ rules; the shared authority supports the sufficient dismissal ground, without creating a single majority merits opinion or an exclusive boundary on other relief.
- United States v. Page, 808 F.2d 723 — the judgment applying it in Williams is sustained against the categorical no-authority challenge; Page’s full disclosure formulation and any rule requiring every fact relevant to a possible defense are not adopted.

#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to this preserved Doyle claim with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman here; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. Exhaustion, preservation, other habeas gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

Source: `state/holdings/administrative-law.md`; heading: Darby v. Cisneros

#### Darby v. Cisneros, 509 U.S. 137 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2045.
**Decided:** June 21, 1993.

##### Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review

**Holding:** In an APA action, an otherwise final agency decision is reviewable without an optional superior-agency appeal unless a statute expressly requires exhaustion or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the challenged action inoperative pending it. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the Court's opinion and judgment and writes separately to state the broader finality principle he would apply. Congress may change substantive law, govern pending cases, alter the rules for prospective relief, create generally applicable judicial remedies, and waive federal claims or defenses when otherwise constitutionally permissible. It may not command an Article III court to reopen and replace a final judgment in a private civil action after the judicial process for revising that judgment has ended. The defect is another department's assertion of authority to revise a conclusive judicial disposition, not principally unfairness or disappointed reliance. Morgan Stanley supplies the controlling rule for this statute, and §27A(b) orders restoration of cases already finally dismissed. Wheeling Bridge concerns continuing prospective relief, which remains distinct. His position preserves prospective legislation, nonfinal cases, lawful alteration of continuing equitable relief, federal waiver, and generally applicable reopening rules administered by courts consistently with Article III. No other Justice joins this separate broader formulation.

Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justice Ginsburg. They expressly advocate reconsidering Morgan Stanley's treatment of this limited statutory class; they do not deny that it is binding, recent, and directly applicable. Section 27A(b) changes the limitations law and restores actions timely under the earlier filing regime while leaving fraud, defenses, and relief for courts to adjudicate. Robertson supplies the genuine-law-change principle, and Schor supports attention to the actual judicial function retained. They acknowledge completed finality and McCullough's concern but regard the confined correction as preserving essential judicial adjudication. Chase Securities supplies a revival analogy, not a controlling Article III answer; Sioux Nation illustrates changed legal effect without pretending that the Government waived a private defendant's defense here. They would reverse the denial and remand for reinstatement and adjudication, without finding fraud, awarding damages, deciding every due-process question, or recognizing unrestricted legislative reopening power.
