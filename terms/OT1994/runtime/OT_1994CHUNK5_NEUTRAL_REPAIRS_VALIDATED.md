Review concerns the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of three false-statement convictions, Counts V–VII, in *United States v. Hubbard*, 16 F.3d 694 (February 15, 1994). The remaining convictions are separate: Counts I–IV for bankruptcy fraud under §152; Counts VIII–X for mail fraud under §1341. ### Facts and what the lower court actually decided

Hubbard filed a Chapter 7 petition on September 25, 1985. During bankruptcy litigation, a successor trustee filed an amended complaint and a motion for surrender of business books and records. Through counsel, Hubbard filed an answer and a response. Count V concerns the assertion that records previously had been produced to the former trustee; VI and VII concern denials of allegations about the locations of a well-drilling machine and its parts. These were unsworn filings. The jury convicted on all ten counts. The lower court found the evidence sufficient to establish the charged false statements and their capability of affecting identification and collection of estate assets. Neither civil pleading form nor an absence of oath was treated as absence of factual falsity.

The Sixth Circuit rejected the Government's Rule 12(f) objection: an argument that the conduct cannot constitute the charged offense falls within then-Rule 12(b)(2)'s exception for failure to charge an offense, rather than a waived formal defect in the indictment. That ruling concerns preservation below; it does not decide the separate Supreme Court presented-question objection.

On the merits, the majority treated *Bramblett* as foreclosing the plain-language exclusion of courts. It declined to recognize the judicial-function exception. It regarded *Erhardt*'s concern about preserving the perjury statute and two-witness rule as weakened and observed that these unsworn statements were not prosecutable as perjury. It distinguished *London*'s private fraud on clients and declined to follow *D'Amato* insofar as that decision would govern. It rejected materiality and exculpatory-no arguments separately. The court **expressly did not reach** the Government's alternative contention that locating and collecting bankruptcy assets is administrative rather than adjudicative. Nelson dissented only from affirmance of V–VII: he read *Erhardt* as binding circuit precedent excluding conduct involving judicial machinery, including unsworn filings, and would reverse those counts. He concurred as to the other seven.

The original candidate's assertion that no preservation issue exists must not be repeated. The source record includes both the resolved Rule 12 objection and the live Supreme Court scope argument.

### Statute in terms and temporal limits

The offense-date §1001 text, reproduced identically in the substantive party briefs and lower opinion, is:

> Whoever, in any matter within the jurisdiction of any department or agency of the United States knowingly and willfully falsifies, conceals or covers up by any trick, scheme, or device a material fact, or makes any false, fictitious or fraudulent statements or representations, or makes or uses any false writing or document knowing the same to contain any false, fictitious or fraudulent statement or entry, shall be fined not more than $10,000 or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.

Section 6 supplies **two distinct definitions and qualifications**:

> As used in this title:
>
> The term “department” means one of the executive departments enumerated in section 1 of Title 5, unless the context shows that such term was intended to describe the executive, legislative, or judicial branches of the government.
>
> The term “agency” includes any department, independent establishment, commission, administration, authority, board or bureau of the United States or any corporation in which the United States has a proprietary interest, unless the context shows that such term was intended to be used in a more limited sense.

The reference to former Title 5 §1 was reenacted as §101; the official Code's reference note confirms that change. Do not omit the department definition's contextual expansion or convert the agency definition's narrowing proviso into the same exception. A choice about department coverage does not silently decide every possible agency theory.

The official 1994 Code records a September 13, 1994 amendment replacing §1001's old $10,000 fine clause with “fined under this title.” The underlying conduct predates that change; no new penalty applies by inference. No later amendment creating a judicial-party exception may enter this May 1995 event. Neither §1001 nor §6 as supplied contains an express judicial-function exception.

### Party positions and the distinct scope question

Hubbard argues that the ordinary meaning and §6 definition exclude courts; the department-context proviso is not a license to disregard the enacted definition. He asks the Court to recede from *Bramblett*'s reasoning or confine its legislative-branch holding, observes that the judiciary language was not necessary to its result, and invokes text, criminal lenity, notice, and the separate statutes and court rules addressing litigation misconduct. Alternatively, he seeks recognition of a judicial-function exception for adjudicative pleadings and discovery responses. A still narrower argument invokes *D'Amato* and *London*: this is private bankruptcy litigation in which the trustee represents the estate, not an executive department or the United States as litigant. He seeks vacatur and remand with directions to vacate V–VII.

The Government argues that *Bramblett*'s context and statutory-history rationale encompasses all three branches. It expressly acknowledges that the judicial-branch statement was, formally, dictum, but argues the underlying rationale affords no principled executive/legislative/judicial distinction. It invokes statutory stare decisis, the absence of an adequate reason to discard the precedent, its subsequent use, and Congress's ability to alter the statute. It rejects a judicial-function exception as absent from the text, inconsistently administered, unnecessary to protect lawful advocacy, and inconsistent with the coverage of authorized government functions. It distinguishes a not-guilty plea or legal argument from a knowing false factual statement; ordinary concealment also requires a disclosure duty. It says statutory overlap does not repeal a general false-statement prohibition and that prosecution policy does not bind construction. It seeks affirmance of V–VII.

The Government separately objects that the petition asked about a judicial-function exception and did not fairly present complete exclusion of the Judicial Branch or overruling *Bramblett*. Its preserved excerpt acknowledges that presented-question limits are nonjurisdictional and that the Court may consider an antecedent, dispositive issue, but argues that discretion need not be used for every antecedent issue and is especially unwarranted where a statutory precedent already supplies the premise. These statements are **party advocacy**, not an admitted limitation on the user-established question and not an actual Court ruling. The supplied question and the source's characterization of the petition must be reconciled by the model under the actual admitted scope and governing review rules. The lower court did address the plain-language point. ### Live alternatives, counterarguments, and remedy boundaries

1. **Resolve the department/agency question under the statutory text.** Excluding courts could dispose of V–VII without developing a judicial-function exception. The hard counterarguments are the §6 context proviso, *Bramblett*'s rationale and statutory stare decisis, and the Government's presented-question objection. A narrow rationale confined to courts must explain its relationship to the legislative-branch holding; merely calling a passage dictum does not answer every precedent argument.
2. **Retain branch coverage and adopt a judicial-function exception.** The proposed protection concerns the court's adjudicative machinery, with an administrative/housekeeping boundary. It has circuit authority and *Rodgers*'s reported reservation, but must answer the absence of express statutory language, varying applications, ordinary elements protecting lawful advocacy, and the independent force of overlapping offenses. If the exception is adopted, classification of these filings is a separate step; the Sixth Circuit did not resolve the Government's administrative-asset-collection alternative. A reasoned remand on that preserved alternative is distinct from immediate vacatur of the convictions.
3. **Retain branch coverage and reject an extra-textual exception.** This could sustain V–VII if the statutory elements and proper scope are satisfied. It must answer text and context, the reserved nature of the prior judicial-function question, the criminal-law notice objection, and the private-civil-litigation argument. It cannot rest on a claim that *Rodgers* already decided the exception's validity.
4. **Adopt a narrower statutory limit for the actual pleadings or private proceeding.** Hubbard expressly offers these paths. They require a rule distinguishing knowingly false factual averments from a mere issue-joining denial, or an explanation of why government involvement solely as adjudicator differs from an agency matter. The Government's counterarguments concern the factual assertions actually made, the court's authorized functions, the trustee's asset administration, and Congress's use of “any matter.” Do not transform the record into a harmless misunderstanding or assume that the trustee's role conclusively resolves every federal-jurisdiction nexus.
5. **Decline a broad antecedent inquiry while deciding a properly presented narrower issue.** This is a scope choice, not a merits holding excluding or including courts. It requires the actual governing presented-question doctrine and admitted scope; briefing alone does not decide it. No fictional grant limitation is supplied.

If V–VII are invalid under the selected statute-wide or as-applied rule, the remedy must identify those convictions and the lawful implementing remand. If the Court recognizes an exception but lacks the classification necessary to apply it, vacatur and a bounded remand differ from an outright direction to acquit. If the statute reaches the conduct and no preserved defense succeeds, affirmance concerns the challenged convictions. Nothing here adjudicates the other seven convictions, constitutional innocence, release, a precise sentence, or a new prosecution.

### Entering law already verified and Justice-specific source boundaries

The current sources include *Heintz v. Jenkins* (April 18, 1995), unanimously joined by all current Justices: it rejects a categorical attorney-litigation exemption from the FDCPA's actual text and repeal history, while reserving application-specific defenses. It is a useful comparison for proposed judge-made litigation exclusions, **not** a holding about §1001 or criminal fair warning. *Brown v. Gardner* (December 12, 1994), also unanimously joined, rejects a regulatory prerequisite that the statute did not enact and preserves the actual causal conditions. Neither case answers whether Congress's §6 context proviso includes a court.

*Allied-Bruce* (January 18, 1995) retained *Southland*'s statutory state-court rule in a Breyer opinion joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg (and Stone-Zsela). O'Connor's separate writing accepts statutory stare decisis despite continuing original-construction disagreement. Thomas, joined by Scalia, would overrule that rule; Scalia separately explains that an express overruling request differs from earlier acceptance where reconsideration was not requested. These are direct, date-eligible positions on statutory precedent. Their statute and reliance setting remain distinct from this criminal prosecution; no Justice's Hubbard conclusion follows automatically.

*X-Citement Video* (November 29, 1994) supplies Ginsburg's contextual criminal-knowledge construction joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer (and Stone-Zsela); Scalia and Thomas's dissent insists that constitutional avoidance remain within a permissible construction. Its statutory knowledge holding and the dissent's textual objection are relevant to the reach of text, context, fair warning, and avoidance, not votes in this case. Its separate scope holding permits respondents to defend an existing favorable judgment with preserved alternatives and preserves *Izumi*, *Caspari*, and *Madsen*'s limited-question boundaries; Hubbard is a petitioner seeking reversal, so that prevailing-party rule cannot simply be transferred.

*Mezzanatto* (January 18, 1995) permits the specified knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver; Thomas's rationale is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Ginsburg, O'Connor, and Breyer expressly preserve limits on other uses. Souter, joined by Stevens, emphasizes statutory protection of the judicial process beyond private consent. That decision preserves the plea Rules' separate false-statement exception requiring oath, record, and counsel. It does not establish that ordinary unsworn pleadings enjoy the same rule-based exclusion or a general permission to lie.

*NationsBank* (January 18, 1995) supplies the eight non-Stone Justices' agreement that classification of a product under one statutory scheme does not mechanically control another; it does not decide the meaning of “department.” *MCI*'s enacted-boundary holding likewise remains at its tariff-delegation scope. These current authorities and the published writings are available to test each Justice's actual forks; they are not substitute holdings about this case.
