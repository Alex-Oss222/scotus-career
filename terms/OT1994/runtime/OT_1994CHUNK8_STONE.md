- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not prohibit truthful, nonmisleading targeted direct-mail solicitation for 30 days after an accident merely because recipients or the public may regard prompt solicitation as offensive or undignified, when the State has not established that the categorical ban is a proportionate response to deception, coercion, harassment, or another concrete harm.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Direct mail is materially less coercive than in-person solicitation because the recipient may discard it unread. Florida may require clear identification as attorney advertising, prohibit false or misleading content, forbid repeated contact after an opt-out, regulate misuse of confidential information, and punish harassment or coercion. Those rules target the actual harms. The submitted survey and complaints show that many people dislike immediate solicitation and believe it reflects poorly on lawyers; they do not establish that truthful mail itself deprives recipients of choice or that a blanket 30-day ban materially protects privacy in a way narrower conduct rules cannot. Commercial-speech doctrine does not require the least restrictive conceivable means, but it does require more than suppression based on offense or professional image.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not invalidate supported restrictions on in-person solicitation, deception, coercion, repeated unwanted contact, confidential-record misuse, or narrowly tailored disclosure requirements. A materially different record showing concrete harm and fit remains open.
