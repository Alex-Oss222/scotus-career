### Preserved component rules and qualifications

**A — Unit formation and availability.** A State may apply its benefit schedule to otherwise eligible applicants and recipients grouped under the challenged same-household caretaker rule, consistently with federal inclusion, exclusion, income, and equitable-treatment requirements. The difference between that grant and the sum of hypothetical separate grants does not itself prove assumed income from a legally nonresponsible person. Mere residence does not establish eligibility, membership, income availability, or a support duty. The litigation principally concerns the single-caretaker nonsibling provision; the other specified married-caretaker and common-child configurations are not independently adjudicated in every possible application.

The refreshed commitment's separate treatment of §§233.90(a)(1), 233.20(a)(2)(viii), and 233.20(a)(3)(ii)(D) remains intact. Parental deprivation and the ban on a substitute-parent assumption are both operative. Reduction of a need item solely because a nonresponsible person is present is prohibited, subject to the separately stated stepparent, authorized-proration, and sponsored-alien provisions. Resources must satisfy current-use availability; income may be actually available or meet both the legal-interest-in-a-liquidated-sum and legal-ability-to-use conditions, consistently with the chapter and applicable disregards. Member income legally considered in the unit need not be transferred separately to every other recipient, but the unit label cannot make resources unavailable to every member countable.

King, Lewis, and Van Lare retain their protection against fictitious outside support. Dandridge permits bounded state benefit-level choices; it does not repeal those protections. The report's rejection of the categorical rule challenge is not approval of every possible resource attribution.

**B — Mandatory inclusion, nonapplication, and §605.** Section 602(a)(38) requires inclusion of the co-resident parent and qualifying sibling subject to its dependency criteria, exceptions, and Title II proviso; it does not occupy the entire field of assistance-unit definition. The independently modeled rule retains the supplied SSI, alienage, sponsorship, lump-sum, stepparent, and overlapping-unit qualifications. A step-sibling is not automatically included merely because a stepparent is. Optional nonparent caretakers and nonsiblings may decline aid only when federal mandatory membership does not require their inclusion. A mandatory sibling cannot simply withdraw alone while leaving the required sibling grouping intact; the supplied example may require both to decline.

The historical report itself reserves an actual claim that another California provision unlawfully prevents an otherwise permissible opt-out. Nothing in this handoff validates such a provision or finds such a concrete application present. The classwide rejection does not erase a distinct actual-income claim properly before a court. It also does not announce a new general facial-challenge doctrine merely because the report cites Salerno in addressing a hypothetical application.

Section 605 concerns use of the AFDC grant for those for whom it is paid; it creates no mutual duty to transfer a child's or caretaker's non-AFDC income and changes no general state-law support obligation. This limitation is consistent with the report's observation that a caretaker need not spend the grant in mechanically equal per-capita shares and may account for a child's outside income when allocating the grant. It does not require a contrary historical departure. The independently sufficient legal basis remains lawful unit composition and the actual income rules, without a new finding about grant expenditure in this family. Counseling, protective payments, guardianship, and authorized penalties remain qualified permitted responses, not mandatory cumulative remedies.

**C — Equitable treatment.** Both §§233.10(a)(1) and 233.20(a)(1)(i) are decided on the supplied classwide record. Objective measurement of the needs of a lawful aided unit through the same grant schedule does not violate them merely because separate-unit totals would be larger or because an unaided caretaker's own needs are absent from a grant. The hardship finding and the argument that lower aid can discourage care by relatives are accepted and answered, not erased. Section 601's purposes do not prescribe the sum of separate grants as the required measure. Classifications must remain reasonable, statewide, objective, equitable in light of the Act, and consistent with eligibility requirements. Administrative convenience cannot justify an arbitrary exclusion.

The uniform-income clause and its federal-statutory exception remain distinct from the equity command; neither resolves an unpresented actual income-classification dispute. Section 233.10's opportunity to apply and assistance in qualifying remain operative, and its title XVI uniformity provision is not converted into an additional AFDC holding. As in the comparator, rejection of the equity merits assumes without deciding that the cited provisions supply an enforceable §1983 right. This is not a holding for or against enforceability and supplies no alternative dismissal ground.

**D — Proration.** Section 612's optional reasonable proration of shelter, utilities, and similar needs for a household containing people not claiming aid together does not require fragmentation of this otherwise lawful unit. It excludes an SSI recipient subject to the statutory one-third reduction from the authorized household calculation. Section 233.20(a)(5) separately prohibits proration with respect to such a recipient and in a bona fide landlord-tenant relationship. If the State elects the option, both need and payment standards must be prorated on a reasonable basis, with the plan specifying allowances, procedure, and relevant circumstances. Applying a lawful unit-size schedule is not by itself an election of this outsider-proration option.
