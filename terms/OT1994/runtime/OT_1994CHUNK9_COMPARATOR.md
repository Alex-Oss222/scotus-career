## 97. Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION I -->

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. Historical case, questions, and proceedings

**Status:** Historical comparison only. This section supplies no simulated law, lower-court premise, vote, or prediction for the reconstruction.

**Historical Identity and Posture:** Miller v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900 (1995), consolidated Nos. 94-631, 94-797, and 94-929, on direct appeals from the three-judge District Court for the Southern District of Georgia, Johnson v. Miller, 864 F. Supp. 1354 (1994). Historically argued April 19, 1995, and decided June 29, 1995. These facts do not establish the reconstructed review channel or argument date.

**Historical Questions, in Paraphrase:** Whether Georgia's Eleventh District gave rise to the racial-gerrymandering claim recognized in historical Shaw; whether an unusually shaped district was a necessary threshold or racial predominance could be established by other evidence; whether the legislature subordinated traditional districting principles to race; and whether the plan survived strict scrutiny, particularly in light of the asserted need to satisfy VRA preclearance demands. Racial separation, stereotyping, and representational harm informed the constitutional dispute. The four independent-injury categories in this reconstruction are not a claim that the historical Court separately adjudicated four such causes of action.

**Historical Record and Disagreement:** After the 1990 census increased Georgia's congressional delegation from ten to eleven seats, the legislature submitted two plans containing two majority-Black districts. DOJ objected; a later plan with three received preclearance. Five white Eleventh District voters challenged the plan. The district-court majority found racial predominance and rejected the statutory-necessity justification. The Supreme Court majority accepted that predominance finding and characterized DOJ's conduct as maximization. The dissenting opinions disputed important inferences concerning districting practice, injury, and the treatment of racial inclusion. These historical assessments do not enter the reconstructed record automatically. The findings expressly adopted for this reconstruction, including racial predominance, are identified in Section I; other historical characterizations remain comparator material. The official report recounts the proceedings at 515 U.S. 905-910 and the relevant disagreements at 917-928 and 929-949.

#### B. Historical outcome and opinion structure

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The district-court judgment was affirmed, and the cases were remanded for further proceedings consistent with the decision. Vote: 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion of the Court:** Kennedy, joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas. The Court treated racial predominance and subordination of traditional principles as triggering strict scrutiny without a mandatory threshold showing of bizarre shape; it upheld the predominance finding and rejected the asserted justification based on the VRA and DOJ demands. 515 U.S. 910-928.
- **O'Connor concurrence:** Emphasized that the predominance threshold was demanding and did not cast doubt on ordinary race-aware districting consistent with customary practices. 515 U.S. 928-929.
- **Stevens dissent:** Joined Ginsburg's dissent and separately challenged cognizable injury and standing. He disputed inferred representational harms and the equation of inclusion with exclusion. 515 U.S. 929-934. His jurisdictional conclusion is distinct from Stone's proposed merits answer.
- **Ginsburg dissent:** Joined by Stevens and Breyer, and by Souter except Part III-B. She opposed the predominance standard, emphasized traditional districting considerations and legislative responsibility, and addressed minority political inclusion. 515 U.S. 934-949. Stone's reconstruction does not depend on rejecting the premise of predominance, importing every part of this dissent, or predicting its historical joiners' positions.
