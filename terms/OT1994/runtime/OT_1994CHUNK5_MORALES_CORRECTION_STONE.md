The supplied record establishes *Cavanaugh*’s protection of mandatory consideration opportunities and its rejection of an actual-release proof requirement. Earlier preparation statements identifying no relevant simulated rule are superseded on that point.

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** **AFFIRM the Ninth Circuit’s judgment on the ex post facto hearing-frequency claim.**
- **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Preserve the relief requiring parole consideration at the frequency applicable when Morales committed his offense. The Board retains authority to determine suitability under lawful standards. The judgment requires consideration, not release, and does not reopen the previously rejected false-information or sentence claims.

#### Issue 1: Retrospective withdrawal of meaningful parole consideration

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the admitted ex post facto question. No jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle obstacle is established in the supplied record. The question concerns the retrospective operation of the 1981 hearing-deferral provision.

**Stone’s Legal Position:** The Ex Post Facto Clause protects against retrospective increases in punishment, including changes that withdraw a legally guaranteed, meaningful avenue to earlier release. A procedural label, unchanged sentencing range, or continued abstract eligibility does not resolve whether the amendment makes punishment more onerous in operation.

A State may reorganize parole administration, but when it removes a mandatory consideration opportunity, any asserted replacement must preserve that opportunity in substance. The substitute must be legally available, practically accessible, and timely enough to permit consideration before the postponed hearing when earlier release remains legally possible. Discretionary authority to advance a hearing does not, by itself, establish an adequate substitute.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** California’s application of the 1981 amendment to Morales violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because it withdraws offense-date annual consideration during a period of parole eligibility without an established, adequate replacement. An individualized prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that initial prediction do not preserve the subsequent opportunity to present rehabilitation, changed circumstances, or grounds showing that the prediction was materially mistaken.

Morales need not prove that a canceled hearing would have resulted in parole. His claim concerns the retrospective withdrawal of a meaningful opportunity capable of shortening confinement.

**Required Showing and the State’s Responsibility:** The prisoner must identify the earlier legal entitlement, its retrospective withdrawal, and its concrete connection to an opportunity for earlier release. A scheduling inconvenience without that connection is insufficient.

Once that showing is made, the State must substantiate any replacement mechanism on which it relies. It must identify the mechanism’s legal basis and establish its practical availability and capacity for timely consideration. Where operation matters, assertions of theoretical authority require supporting evidence.

Uncertainty about the existence or effectiveness of the State’s asserted substitute cannot supply a reason to deny relief. Courts may not fill a gap in the State’s showing by imagining that officials could reopen a case. This allocation requires the State to support its own answer to the demonstrated loss; it does not relieve the prisoner of the ultimate burden of establishing an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Stone’s Reasoning and Record Application:** The amendment contains genuine constraints. Annual consideration remains the default; postponement is permissive, applies to a defined class, requires separately directed reasons, and cannot exceed three years. The Board made an interval prediction concerning Morales’s offense, prior murder, criminal history, and need for further observation, evaluation, or treatment. Stone accepts those features rather than treating postponement as automatic or the findings as nonexistent.

Those constraints govern the initial postponement. They do not establish a continuing opportunity to obtain consideration when the forecast becomes outdated or proves mistaken. Morales became eligible for parole during the postponed interval. The findings did not establish that earlier release was legally impossible.

*In re Jackson* requires reasons supporting postponement and recognizes review of that decision. Its discussion of advancing a hearing in extraordinary circumstances establishes no ordinary, enforceable replacement for annual consideration. The Court must distinguish authority to act from a prisoner’s usable opportunity to obtain timely action.

On the supplied record, California has not established an adequate substitute. The protected annual opportunity therefore cannot be displaced by predictions about its likely outcome or assumptions about discretionary advancement. The Ninth Circuit’s consideration-only relief should remain in place.

**Boundary and Reserved Questions:** This position does not constitutionalize every administrative detail or require identical procedures in every parole system. A different arrangement may be permissible if it actually preserves the relevant opportunity through an adequate, enforceable substitute. The Court does not approve a hypothetical replacement system, prescribe a universal filing procedure or numerical response deadline, determine Morales’s suitability, or establish a right to release.

#### Research and drafting notes — authority available in April 1995

- **Guaranteed opportunities and discretionary substitutes:** *Weaver v. Graham*, 450 U.S. 24, 29–36 (1981), rejected the argument that discretionary opportunities for additional credits compensated for reducing previously guaranteed credits. It supports the proposed distinction between an enforceable opportunity and official discretion, although it did not prescribe this parole-review standard. [Weaver](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/450/24/)

- **No requirement to prove the favorable outcome:** *Lindsey v. Washington*, 301 U.S. 397, 401–402 (1937), and *Miller v. Florida*, 482 U.S. 423, 431–435 (1987), establish that the possibility of receiving the same punishment under the former law does not defeat an ex post facto claim. Their application to this hearing restriction is supported by the supplied simulated *Cavanaugh* rule. [Lindsey](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/301/397/), [Miller](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/482/423/)

- **Punishment remains the constitutional boundary:** *Collins v. Youngblood*, 497 U.S. 37, 41–46 (1990), prevents treating every retrospective procedural disadvantage as unconstitutional. The proposed rule therefore requires a concrete connection between the withdrawn consideration opportunity and possible additional confinement. [Collins](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/37/)

- **California authority requires careful treatment:** *In re Jackson*, 39 Cal.3d 464, 473–479 (1985), upheld the related 1982 biennial-hearing amendment. Its separate-reasons requirement and hypothetical advancement discussion are relevant, but it did not establish an ordinary reopening system or decide the precise 1981 provision presented here. [Jackson](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/39/464.html)

- **The evidentiary allocation is a proposed clarification:** Pre-1995 precedent did not establish a general presumption shifting the ultimate burden of an ex post facto claim to the State. This formulation instead requires substantiation of the State’s asserted safeguard after the prisoner demonstrates the withdrawal of a meaningful release opportunity. The historical *Morales* majority’s contrary discussion in footnote 6 identifies the objection this distinction addresses; that same-day historical opinion supplies comparison material, not entering simulated law. [Historical discussion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93-1462.ZO.html)
