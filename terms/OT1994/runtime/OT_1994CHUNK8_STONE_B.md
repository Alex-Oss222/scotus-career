- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM in part, REVERSE in part, and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The reversal of the § 1503 conviction is affirmed. The § 2232(c) conviction may be reinstated, subject to remaining preserved claims and ordinary sentencing proceedings.

#### Issue 1: Section 1503 obstruction nexus

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory sufficiency question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1503 requires a nexus between the act and the judicial or grand-jury proceeding: the defendant must know his conduct is likely to affect that proceeding, and the conduct must have the natural and probable effect of interference. The proof here showed only false statements to investigators who might later testify.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** False statements to investigators do not violate § 1503 absent proof that the defendant knew they were likely to reach and obstruct a pending judicial or grand-jury proceeding.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The omnibus clause cannot become a general false-statement offense. Pettibone’s knowledge and nexus principles provide a text-grounded boundary. The agents were not shown to act as an arm of the grand jury, and transmission was speculative.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Direct lies to the grand jury, destruction of subpoenaed evidence, witness tampering, and statements knowingly channeled to the proceeding remain within the statute.

#### Issue 2: Section 2232(c) wiretap disclosure

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory construction question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The statute requires knowledge of an application or authorization, intent to obstruct the interception, and notice of the possible interception. It does not require the authorization to remain formally pending at the instant of disclosure.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2232(c) reaches a disclosure made with the required knowledge and obstructive intent even after the identified authorization expires, so long as the notice concerns the possible interception authorized or sought.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** “Such interception” links intent to the known application or authorization; “possible interception” anticipates uncertainty about whether interception will occur. Adding an in-force requirement would narrow text Congress did not write.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Government must still prove the defendant’s knowledge and specific obstructive purpose; ordinary disclosure unrelated to an interception is not covered.

- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The injunction against the 30-day targeted-mail prohibition remains in force. Florida may regulate false, misleading, coercive, harassing, or improperly disclosed solicitation and may adopt proportionate privacy protections consistent with the First Amendment.

#### Issue 1: Truthful targeted direct mail

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Truthful, nonmisleading commercial communication about lawful legal services is protected for speakers and recipients. Florida’s interests in protecting accident victims’ privacy and maintaining confidence in the profession are substantial, but neither offense, poor taste, nor public disapproval establishes that a categorical period of enforced silence directly and proportionately serves those interests. The State must show the actual harm and fit of the restriction it chose.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not prohibit truthful, nonmisleading targeted direct-mail solicitation for 30 days after an accident merely because recipients or the public may regard prompt solicitation as offensive or undignified, when the State has not established that the categorical ban is a proportionate response to deception, coercion, harassment, or another concrete harm.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Direct mail is materially less coercive than in-person solicitation because the recipient may discard it unread. Florida may require clear identification as attorney advertising, prohibit false or misleading content, forbid repeated contact after an opt-out, regulate misuse of confidential information, and punish harassment or coercion. Those rules target the actual harms. The submitted survey and complaints show that many people dislike immediate solicitation and believe it reflects poorly on lawyers; they do not establish that truthful mail itself deprives recipients of choice or that a blanket 30-day ban materially protects privacy in a way narrower conduct rules cannot. Commercial-speech doctrine does not require the least restrictive conceivable means, but it does require more than suppression based on offense or professional image.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not invalidate supported restrictions on in-person solicitation, deception, coercion, repeated unwanted contact, confidential-record misuse, or narrowly tailored disclosure requirements. A materially different record showing concrete harm and fit remains open.

## 94. Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton

#### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Ninth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The judgment invalidating the suspicionless athlete-testing policy remains in force. The District may continue ordinary disciplinary rules, testing based on individualized reasonable suspicion, and any narrower search practice that independently satisfies the Fourth Amendment.

#### Issue 1: Suspicionless urinalysis of student athletes

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Collection and chemical analysis of urine are searches. The school setting and a genuine non-law-enforcement safety need permit departures from the warrant and probable-cause defaults, but they do not eliminate the requirement of reasonableness. Student athletes accept coaching, physical examinations, communal changing, and sport-specific safety rules; they do not thereby waive privacy in excretory functions or medical information. A documented student drug problem and the possibility of athletic injury establish a serious concern, but not that suspicionless testing of every athlete is necessary or proportionate when individualized suspicion and ordinary school tools remain workable.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public school may not condition participation in interscholastic athletics on random urinalysis of every athlete when the record does not establish that individualized reasonable-suspicion testing or materially less intrusive measures are impracticable and the program subjects a broad class of students to recurring bodily and medical searches without individualized cause.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The program is not ordinary law enforcement, uses random selection, and limits some disclosure, which reduces its burden. Those features do not answer whether suspicionless testing is justified. The District’s evidence establishes drug use and disciplinary difficulty, but it does not show that athletes as a class caused the problem, that sport-specific risks could not be addressed through observation and reasonable-suspicion testing, or that every athlete must surrender bodily privacy to participate. Voluntary participation in a public-school activity is not a blanket waiver of the Fourth Amendment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not bar testing based on individualized reasonable suspicion, testing after a specific safety incident, ordinary physical examinations directed to athletic fitness, or a materially narrower safety-sensitive program supported by evidence that individualized methods cannot address the risk. He does not authorize law-enforcement use or testing for unrelated medical information.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Fourth Circuit and require viewpoint-neutral access to the printing benefit on the program's existing terms.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Remove the religious-viewpoint exclusion from eligibility for the generally available printing benefit. Apply the program's otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms without ordering unrestricted support for religious activity or treating the publication's message as the University's own.

#### Reasoning and record application

The University supports student expression through a generally available arrangement while leaving the publications' editorial choices private. It does not adopt every publication's message. Excluding an otherwise eligible publication because its viewpoint is religious denies equal access to that benefit. Payment of printing costs through this program does not itself make the publication governmental religious speech.

The independent character of the publication and the limits on the printing benefit do constitutional work. Equal access to this arrangement differs from specially delegating governmental power to a religious enclave. This is not an unrestricted authorization of direct governmental sponsorship of worship, nor a decision that every public expenditure is a private-speech forum.

#### Preservation and boundaries

Stone reaches the Free Speech and Establishment Clause questions concerning this printing program. Recognition, meeting space, distribution and funding remain distinct benefits requiring analysis of their own operation; the older categorical conclusion that this particular payment may be denied as financing religious exercise is not the selected position. Other eligibility disputes and any relief beyond removing the viewpoint exclusion retain their ordinary procedural predicates. Earlier simulated religion holdings control within their actual scope; no other Justice's vote is supplied.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone independently reads “take,” “harm,” the statute’s definitions, and the conservation scheme to permit coverage of habitat modification that actually kills or injures protected animals. The agency’s ecological expertise and reasoned narrowing of the rule earn substantial persuasive weight, but authority comes from the statute.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The ESA permits the Secretary to define “harm” to include significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures endangered wildlife by materially impairing essential behavioral patterns.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** “Harm” adds work beyond direct hunting or capture, and Congress authorized protective regulations while creating habitat-focused exceptions and permit mechanisms. The regulation requires actual death or injury and proximate causal connection, preventing conversion of every land-use effect into a take.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not reach habitat modification causing no actual injury, eliminate ordinary causation, or authorize regulation outside the ESA’s statutory species and permit structure.
