## United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258 (1947)

### What the Court decided and the operative distinctions

Chief Justice Vinson's Court opinion holds in Part I that the Norris-LaGuardia Act did not bar this Government suit against workers at mines in Government possession and operation, where the relevant relation was that of governmental employer and employee. It separately gives alternative grounds in Part II, pp. 289–295, for sustaining **criminal contempt** even if the Act barred the underlying injunctive relief. The dispute over the Government's contract was properly before the district court; the immediate jurisdictional question concerned power to give ancillary injunctive relief under the labor statutes. The two inquiries must remain distinct.

1. **Temporary preservation while substantial jurisdiction is decided.** The Government had seriously urged a legally unresolved basis for injunctive power. In those circumstances, the district court could issue an order preserving existing conditions pending decision on its own jurisdiction. The defendants did not move to vacate before allowing the strike to occur. The Court relied on *United States v. Shipp*, which permitted preservation of the subject of a pending habeas appeal while jurisdiction was considered, and on the analogous treatment of a temporary order in *Carter v. United States*. UMW, pp. 289–293. The source expressly states that a different result would follow if the question of jurisdiction were “frivolous and not substantial,” while rejecting that description here. Id., p. 293. The underlying labor-statute question was novel and substantial, as its language and legislative history showed. This is not a declaration that merely asserting jurisdiction necessarily creates temporary coercive authority.

2. **Orders of a court with jurisdiction remain binding while unreversed.** The second formulation is that an order issued by a court with jurisdiction over subject matter and person must be obeyed until reversed through orderly proceedings, even where the error concerns the constitutionality of the law supplying the merits rule. Id., pp. 293–294, quoting *Howat v. Kansas*. The Court then states its actual circumstances: “the subject matter of the suit, as well as the parties, was properly before the court”; federal jurisdiction was clearly shown; and authority to issue ancillary relief depended on a statute whose scope and applicability were subject to substantial doubt. Id., p. 294. After the district court affirmatively resolved the statutory objection, outstanding and subsequent orders were to be obeyed “until they expired or were set aside by appropriate proceedings, appellate or otherwise.” Id. This identifies both duration and an orderly means of relief; it does not make an interlocutory order permanently immune from challenge.

3. **Criminal and civil contempt have different consequences after reversal.** Criminal punishment for disobedience may survive an order's being set aside on appeal or the basic action's becoming moot. Id., p. 294, citing *Worden* and *Gompers*. But the Court immediately rejects the inference that the opposing litigant can thereby retain remedial civil relief: “The right to remedial relief falls with an injunction which events prove was erroneously issued,” and “a fortiori” when the order exceeded jurisdiction. Id., p. 295. Had Norris-LaGuardia applied, the Court would have set aside the preliminary injunction and the **entire civil-contempt judgment**, while sustaining criminal-contempt judgments subject to defects in procedure or fines. Thus criminal survival after reversal is not a basis for preserving substantive rights, compensatory recovery, or civil coercion under an invalid injunction. Conversely, that post-reversal distinction does not itself authorize disregard of an order still in force.

4. **Substantial jurisdiction is not final jurisdiction.** The phrase “plain usurpation” appears in footnote 57, pp. 292–293, within the Court's quotation from *Carter*. Frankfurter separately uses “indisputable want of authority” and a court acting outside its orbit, pp. 309–310. The controlling Court text itself states the “frivolous and not substantial” limit and its particular properly pending suit. Do not merge the separate writing and quoted authority into an invented single verbal test, or describe arguable jurisdiction as a determination that all substantive relief is lawful.

5. **Contempt procedure and remedy remain reviewable.** Part III examines notice, criminal safeguards, the claimed statutory jury right, and prejudice from mixing criminal and civil proceedings; Part V reviews and modifies the fines. The Court required awareness of the criminal charge and the associated protections, and found no substantial prejudice on this record. The alternative authority-to-preserve ground expressly remains subject to infirmities in the contempt proceedings or fines. Id., pp. 295–301. The decision therefore does not immunize a contempt judgment from independent procedural or remedial objections.

6. **Civil compensation and civil coercion are not interchangeable.** Compensation requires evidence of actual loss and depends on the complainant's rights in the underlying controversy; coercion requires attention to the harm threatened and the sanction's likely efficacy. The burden relative to the contemnor's financial resources matters. Id., pp. 303–305. The Court sustained Lewis's $10,000 criminal fine and changed the union's $3.5 million unconditional fine to $700,000 punishment plus $2.8 million conditional on failure to comply fully within the stated five-day period. Those remedies illustrate the distinctions; their amounts, labor-emergency premises, and factual findings supply no sanction or fact in Celotex.

The references to mootness above concern *Gompers* and the general survival of criminal punishment. They establish no mootness, payment, completion of reorganization, or other later event in Celotex.

## Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307 (1967)

### Rule, constitutional reservations, and accessible review

Justice Stewart's Court opinion upholds Alabama's application of an established collateral-obedience rule to **criminal contempt** for knowingly violating an injunction against unpermitted street demonstrations. It approves the *Howat* principle as consistent with federal practice. The question was whether the Federal Constitution compelled Alabama to let these defendants bypass judicial review, not whether every alleged error in every injunction is unreviewable. The Court expressly frames its conclusion by the circumstances. Id., pp. 313–315.

- **Jurisdiction and substantial validity were present.** The Court says that the issuing state court unquestionably had jurisdiction over the parties and subject matter. It adds: “And this is not a case where the injunction was transparently invalid or had only a frivolous pretense to validity.” Id., p. 315. This wording marks a reservation: Walker does not decide the full boundaries, procedure, or consequences of a universal transparent-invalidity exception. It also does not equate any substantial merits objection with transparent invalidity. The Court recognized substantial constitutional concerns about the ordinance and injunction while requiring an orderly challenge in the circumstances before it.

- **Error, including constitutional error, did not itself eliminate jurisdiction.** The ordinance and injunction were potentially overbroad or vague; those objections were to be presented in a motion to modify or dissolve. The particular prohibition violated—mass parading without a permit—was clear and understood. A possible narrowing construction of the ordinance and legitimate street-regulation interests made it inappropriate to assume facial nullity on this record. Id., pp. 315–317. This is not an adjudication that every provision of the injunction or ordinance was constitutionally valid.

- **Timely relief was available on the record as the majority read it.** The majority expressly states: “This case would arise in quite a different constitutional posture if the petitioners, before disobeying the injunction, had challenged it in the Alabama courts, and had been met with delay or frustration of their constitutional claims.” Id., p. 318. It found no showing that a timely motion would have met that fate, noted the two-day interval before the first march, and noted that Alabama procedure provided expedited appellate review if the issuing court did not modify or dissolve. Id., pp. 318–319 & n. 12. This does not hold that merely filing a motion automatically licenses disobedience, that every review route is sufficiently prompt, or that delay invariably creates an exception. It leaves the materially different posture open.

- **No surprise procedural forfeiture was found.** The Court relied on Alabama precedents predating the conduct and rejected an analogy to a newly announced procedural bar or a departure without notice from a practice of entertaining claims. Id., pp. 319–320, distinguishing *Barr* and *Wright*. Its reasoning thus includes fair notice of the method of challenge; it does not authorize an unforeseen procedural device to extinguish a federal right.

- **The federal-preemption qualification was not erased.** Footnote 6, p. 315, describes *In re Green* as requiring the state courts to hear a claim that federal labor law deprived the issuing state court of jurisdiction. Walker distinguishes Green because Green had promptly sought vacation before violating and offered to prove that the issuing court agreed to violation as a means of testing validity. That is Walker's treatment of Green; it is not a new holding that these are invariably cumulative prerequisites to every jurisdictional challenge. The dissents dispute the majority's narrowing description, as detailed below. The full independent Green opinion has not been validated by this assignment.

- **Notice, actual violation, and proof still matter.** The Court separately addressed knowledge of the injunction, sufficient evidence, and the use of press-conference statements as evidence of willfulness rather than a separately punishable speech offense. Id., p. 312 nn. 3–4. The Alabama courts had rejected other convictions for missing proof of notice or violation. Obedience doctrine did not substitute for proof that a person knew of and violated the operative order.

Walker supplies no finding about Celotex's injunction, Edwards's conduct, insolvency, available relief, or the scope of bankruptcy jurisdiction. Its findings of deliberate announced defiance, two days available before the march, expedited Alabama review, and legitimate street-management interests belong to Walker. Its original dispute involved criminal contempt and constitutional limits on a state procedural rule; translating its principle to a still-operative bankruptcy injunction and a second federal court's bond-enforcement proceeding requires legal reasoning beyond labeling the situations identical.

## Boundaries for neutral use in case 47

These authorities establish that merits error, final jurisdiction, interim authority to preserve a substantial jurisdictional question, and the continuing force of an unreversed order are different issues. They also preserve the distinction between criminal punishment for disobedience and civil remedial relief after invalidation. A proposed argument must specify which proposition it uses and carry its qualifications.

They do not interpret 28 U.S.C. §§1334, 157, or 158 under the bankruptcy framework at issue in Celotex. In particular, they do not decide whether a proceeding is core, whether noncore procedure requires proposed findings, whether all parties consented, or which direct-review route was actually available. Those matters require the separately validated statute and record sources. Nor does an available route to seek relief establish an unconditional right to immediate appellate review.

The original authors and joins above are evidence about these prior decisions. Neither source provides an OT1994 participating Justice's own authored or joined opinion. They must not be recast as personal-position evidence for a current Justice without a separate, date-eligible writing. The current model remains responsible for its own source-grounded analysis, with no present-case outcome supplied by this audit.
