## Selected conditional disposition and immediate scope

All eight associates select the following **within the stated condition**, for the Justice-specific reasons recorded below:

**Decided insurer/HMO applications.** Reverse the reviewed judgment insofar as it holds the 13% hospital differential, the additional 11% commercial-insurer measure and the conditional HMO assessment of up to 9% preempted on the rejected economic-effects theory in the decided commercial-insurer/HMO coverage applications. Set aside the associated ERISA hospital-surcharge injunction to that extent. The mandate must direct conforming treatment of the actual operative injunction, rather than merely removing an opinion's rationale while leaving the same final relief supported by it.

**Reserved direct self-funded applications.** Insofar as the reviewed judgment or associated ERISA hospital-surcharge injunction adjudicates or protects a direct self-funded application left unresolved by the Court, vacate that portion and remand it for consideration under the announced law and any properly presented independent ground. That limited vacatur does not reject the reserved claim on its merits, find deemer protection applicable, or dismiss the claim. It does remove reliance on the vacated adjudication as a continuing final entitlement to direct-plan protection. The qualifier identifies the precise reach of the mandate where the original/current implementation is incompletely supplied; it is not a finding that a separate direct-plan injunction exists. Neither 9% nor 11% is newly declared directly payable by self-funded plans merely because the 13% application is reserved.

**Independent components.** Leave the separate FEHBA and Actuarial Letter dispositions undisturbed. The FEHBA setting is the experience-rated coverage before the amended appellate court; community-rated plans were reserved, and the district court did not decide a FEHBA challenge to 9%. The Actuarial Letter ruling involves stop-loss insurance, with paragraphs 1, 2, 3 and 5 protected below and paragraph 4 additionally held preempted by the amended appellate judgment; paragraphs 6–7 were not challenged on that appeal. This surviving stop-loss relief is not direct-plan hospital-surcharge protection. When two grounds concern the same payment, removal of the reviewed ERISA ground does not eliminate independently surviving FEHBA protection.

**Interim and monetary implementation.** No distinct direct-plan injunction, automatic stay, mandatory escrow continuation, escrow disbursement, reimbursement, accrued-liability determination or new payment directive is ordered by this disposition. Any lower-court request for interim protection on the reserved direct-plan claim must be assessed under applicable law on its own supported grounds; the fact of reservation does not require protection. The lower courts must conform existing implementation orders to the limited mandate, identify any independent surviving basis, and resolve properly presented interim or fund-administration issues without treating the rejected economic-effects rationale or vacated final direct-plan adjudication as sufficient by itself. No precise stay-termination or mandate-issuance date is supplied or invented. These are the operative scope and instructions of the selected disposition, not a claim that collections change automatically on the announcement date.

Thus the formal choice is **reversal in part, limited vacatur in part, and remand**. The disposition is not a blanket reversal of the entire amended lower judgment, preservation of a supposed separate direct-plan injunction, or a merits denial of the reserved claim. The distinction between the reversed decided application and the vacated unresolved application must appear expressly in any subsequent record or projection adopting this conditional branch.

## Adversarial remedial comparison

**Alternative: retain direct-plan final relief while remanding the alternative claim.** The strongest argument is that a prevailing respondent may defend relief on an alternative ground and FMC makes a direct-plan/deemer distinction legally substantial; dissolving protection before that ground is decided may expose payors to hard-to-recover charges. Actual X-Citement confirms that a properly renewed alternative can defend an existing favorable judgment. The problem is that availability of the claim does not establish entitlement to the injunction. Here the merits premise sustaining the broad relief has been rejected, the direct-plan issue is expressly reserved, the original operative injunction does not identify a separate direct-payor command, and its later implementation is not fully established. The 1993 stay required 13% payment, and the party-quoted later direction likewise reports payment by the identified claims fiduciary, so continuing nonpayment protection cannot be justified as an established status quo. These points favor limited vacatur and renewed lower-court evaluation, rather than unconditional survival of final protection. This does not extinguish the alternative ground or prevent supported interim relief.

**Alternative: reverse every direct-plan claim and dissolve all lower relief outright.** The strongest argument is that the rejected relation theory was the basis for surcharge preemption and a clean reversal avoids lingering effects of an erroneous judgment. Under the stated condition, however, that argument exceeds the merits decided. The direct-plan alternative is not adjudicated against respondents; FMC remains available at its actual scope, and factual/statutory implementation may matter. Partial vacatur removes the undecided component's final adjudicative force while preserving its lawful consideration. Separate FEHBA and Actuarial Letter relief is outside review, independently supported, and cannot be removed for convenience.

**Alternative: require interim direct-plan protection or continuation of the old escrow arrangement.** The strongest argument invokes potential collection/recovery difficulties identified in the 1993 stay opinion and avoidance of loss before the reserved merits are considered. But the old order separates 13% payment from the 9%/11% escrow, supplies no independently verified direct-plan injunction, and concerns a particular appeal. Its findings do not establish current irreparable harm for every reserved direct-payor application or the conditions for a new Supreme interim command. The attributed payment account is especially inconsistent with assuming blanket direct-plan protection. No current interim application, full later-order record, or separately established entitlement supports compulsory protection here. Accordingly all eight decline to impose it while preserving the lower courts' ability to decide a supported request. No inference is made that interim relief would necessarily be denied.

**Selected alternative: reject decided final relief, vacate the reserved adjudication, and remand without an invented interim regime.** Section 2106 supplies authority for this tailored separation. It fits the merits reach, retains the independent claim, and does not substitute a new remedial factual premise for the missing implementation record. The costs of renewed litigation and possible collection are genuine considerations, but they do not establish a present injunction entitlement or justify deciding the reserved merits silently.
