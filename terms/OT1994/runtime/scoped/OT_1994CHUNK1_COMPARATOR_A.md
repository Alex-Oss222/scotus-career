## 1. United States v. Shabani

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION III -->

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** 993 F. 2d 1419, reversed. Vote: 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor, J., delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** In order to establish a violation of § 846, the Government need not prove the commission of any overt acts in furtherance of the conspiracy. The statute’s plain language does not require an overt act, and such a requirement has not been inferred from congressional silence in other conspiracy statutes, see, e. g., Nash v. United States, 229 U. S. 373. Thus, absent contrary indications, it is presumed that Congress intended to adopt the common law definition of conspiracy, which “does not make the doing of any act other than the act of conspiring a condition of liability,” id., at 378. Moreover, since the general conspiracy statute and the conspiracy provision of the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 both require an overt act, it appears that Congress’ choice in § 846 was quite deliberate.

- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 513 U.S. 10, and Supreme Court Database vote metadata.

## D. HISTORICAL HOLDINGS BY COMPONENT

### Component 1: Power
Section 2106 permits an appropriate disposition after mootness; Article III does not forbid addressing the motion to vacate.

### Component 2: Equitable entitlement
Settlement does not ordinarily justify vacatur because the losing party voluntarily surrendered review. Exceptional circumstances remain possible, but an agreement to vacate is not enough. The motion failed on the circumstances presented.

## E. HISTORICAL DECISIONAL PREMISES

**Unanimous Court:** The dispositive premises were voluntary causation of mootness, the continued availability of review before settlement, and the public character of judicial judgments. The Court distinguished a loss of review through happenstance from a deliberate choice to settle.

## 4. United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION III -->

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** 982 F. 2d 1285, reversed. Vote: 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, JJ., joined. Stevens, J., filed a concurring opinion, post, p. 79. Scalia, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which Thomas, J., joined, post, p. 80.
- **Historical Holding:** Because the term “knowingly” in §§ 2252(1) and (2) modifies the phrase “the use of a minor” in subsections (1)(A) and (2)(A), the Act is properly read to include a scienter requirement for age of minority. This Court rejects the most natural grammatical reading, adopted by the Ninth Circuit, under which “knowingly” modifies only the relevant verbs in subsections (1) and (2), and does not extend to the elements of the minority of the performers, or the sexually explicit nature of the material, because they are set forth in independent clauses separated by interruptive punctuation.
- **Historical Authority:** The official opinion structure stated above controls only as historical comparator; it is not simulated law entering Stone’s vote.
