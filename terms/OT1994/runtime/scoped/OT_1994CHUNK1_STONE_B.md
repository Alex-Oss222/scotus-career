# OT1994 chunk 1 STONE, scope B

## 5. Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater +

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION I -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Eleventh Circuit’s judgment affirming in part, vacating in part, reversing in part, and remanding.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall complete the remand ordered by the Eleventh Circuit. The broad prospective and retrospective financial, operational, and organizational disclosure and recordkeeping requirements and the mandatory sixty-day refund requirement may not be enforced against churches as presented here. Limited identifying registration requirements and the requirement to provide a complete written statement of the terms of a voluntarily offered refund policy may remain enforceable subject to the unresolved sectarian-purpose challenge and severability. The district court shall resolve the purpose issue and then severability. It shall also apply RFRA to any materially different prospective enforcement application not conclusively resolved by this judgment, with Clearwater carrying the statutory burdens once a substantial burden is established.

#### Issue 1: Religious Freedom Restoration Act

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches RFRA first because it became governing law while this prospective challenge remained pending. Section 6 expressly reaches implementation of preexisting state and local law, and applying it to prospective enforcement does not attach new liability to completed conduct. The Church’s failure to invoke a statute not yet enacted when the court of appeals entered judgment does not forfeit the intervening governing rule. Stone applies RFRA where the existing record permits and remands any materially different application requiring additional factfinding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Church bears the threshold obligation to identify a substantial burden on religious exercise. Once that showing is made, RFRA places on Clearwater both the burden of going forward and the burden of persuasion to establish that application of the particular burden furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. Preventing fraud in charitable solicitation is compelling, but the interest must justify the particular regulation rather than religious supervision in general. On this record, the broad compulsory disclosure and recordkeeping regime substantially burdens religious exercise and association by requiring extensive disclosure of internal financial operation, use of funds, member-directed solicitation information, and supporting records under criminal sanction. The mandatory sixty-day refund provision also substantially burdens exercise by displacing the Church’s own religiously connected refund process. Clearwater has not shown those broad measures to be the least restrictive means because targeted anti-fraud law, limited identifying disclosures, transaction-specific investigation, and accurate disclosure of promised refund terms address the demonstrated secular harms more narrowly. The limited identifying registration requirements do not establish a substantial burden on this record. The contemporaneous written statement of the terms of a refund policy, when a refund is voluntarily promised, survives even assuming a substantial burden because the record identifies concrete deception concerning the content and availability of refunds and the provision requires disclosure of the Church’s own policy rather than a policy dictated by government.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** RFRA governs Clearwater’s prospective implementation of the challenged ordinance. Broad compulsory disclosure and recordkeeping of internal religious financial and organizational affairs, and a municipal rule mandating refund within sixty days whenever a church offers refunds, substantially burden religious exercise and fail because Clearwater has not shown those requirements to be the least restrictive means of preventing fraud. Limited identifying disclosures do not establish a substantial burden on this record, and a requirement that a solicitor accurately state in writing the terms of a voluntarily offered refund policy survives RFRA on the established record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** RFRA restores a statutory compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means inquiry even for burdens imposed through generally applicable law. Clearwater has legitimate authority to prevent affirmative deception. But the statute does not permit the City to substitute a broad continuing inspection and disclosure regime for narrower tools capable of addressing the demonstrated wrong. The record already identifies ordinary fraud prohibitions, limited identification rules, complaint-triggered investigation, and written disclosure of promised refund terms as materially narrower means. The sixty-day mandate goes further still by changing the substance of the Church’s policy rather than requiring truth about the policy it chose. By contrast, the written-policy provision responds directly to evidence that contributors may have been misled about refund conditions and does not require civil officials to determine religious doctrine or the merits of a request.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** RFRA does not exempt a church from generally applicable fraud, contract, tax, restitution, or criminal law; does not bar a targeted subpoena or audit supported by a concrete secular enforcement need and lawful process; and does not authorize government to determine whether religious beliefs are true. Stone reserves materially different investigative applications not established by this record.

#### Issue 2: Purpose, neutrality, and targeting

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional purpose question because RFRA does not resolve whether Clearwater enacted or administers the ordinance for a forbidden sectarian purpose, a question that could affect the ordinance as a whole. He resolves only the summary-judgment issue and leaves the disputed factual determination to the district court.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Government may regulate charitable solicitation through religion-neutral law, but it may not design or administer an ostensibly general ordinance to burden a named religion. Evidence of sustained official focus on Scientology is legally material. A city cannot convert concern about religion into authority to supervise or disadvantage a disfavored church.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Summary judgment for Clearwater cannot stand on the sectarian-purpose claim where the record contains evidence from which a factfinder could conclude that religious hostility or denominational targeting materially shaped the ordinance’s design or enforcement.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The First Amendment does not immunize religious fundraising from ordinary fraud law. It does require the court to determine whether the asserted fraud regime is genuine and evenhanded. The record contains evidence bearing on anti-Scientology purpose, so Clearwater is not entitled to judgment that the ordinance is neutral as a matter of law. Stone does not himself decide the disputed motive.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not find on the unresolved record that every provision arose from forbidden purpose, and he does not disable a neutral replacement ordinance directed to fraud rather than religion.

#### Issue 3: Registration, disclosure, recordkeeping, and investigation

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** RFRA supplies the sufficient ground for invalidating the broad disclosure and recordkeeping provisions on this record. Stone therefore does not add an unnecessary constitutional holding concerning those same provisions. He separately reaches the challenge to the limited identifying registration provisions because RFRA does not invalidate them.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Clearwater may require the limited identifying information necessary to identify the solicitor and charitable organization and may enforce truthful representations through neutral law. The surviving registration requirement does not empower the clerk to adjudicate doctrine or prohibit solicitation while judicial review proceeds. The broader compulsory internal disclosure regime is already disposed of under RFRA and receives no additional constitutional holding.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The limited identifying registration provisions sustained below may remain, subject to the unresolved sectarian-purpose and severability questions. No additional constitutional ruling is necessary concerning the broader disclosure and recordkeeping provisions invalidated under RFRA.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The surviving identifying provisions ask who is soliciting, the organization’s legal form and tax status, and where it has registered. Those requirements perform ordinary civil identification work. The invalid provisions, by contrast, are removed on the antecedent statutory ground. Constitutional avoidance counsels against using them to announce a broader rule than the judgment requires.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve standardless religious licensing, theological review, or an investigative demand untethered to a concrete secular violation. A materially different subpoena, audit, inspection, or disclosure order must be judged on its own source, scope, record, and RFRA consequences.

#### Issue 4: Refund-policy requirements and severability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves the refund provisions under RFRA first. Because the written-policy disclosure survives RFRA, he reaches the remaining First Amendment objection to that provision. The sixty-day mandate is invalid under RFRA, so no additional constitutional ruling concerning that mandate is necessary. Florida severability remains for the remand court after the purpose determination.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Clearwater may require a solicitor who voluntarily promises refunds to provide a complete and contemporaneous written statement of the terms it actually offers. That rule regulates the accuracy of a secular representation and does not authorize officials to prescribe doctrine, require a church to offer refunds, or decide whether a member is spiritually entitled to one. Clearwater may not require every church that offers refunds to return money within sixty days regardless of the church’s own policy because RFRA supplies a narrower statutory ground for invalidating that mandate. A donor’s private payment to a church remains private money; regulation of truthful solicitation does not transform it into public financing or create governmental authority over religious use.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Clearwater may require truthful written disclosure of the terms of a voluntarily offered refund policy, but the mandatory sixty-day refund requirement may not be enforced against churches on this record under RFRA. The surviving provisions remain subject to the unresolved sectarian-purpose determination and Florida severability law.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The written-policy rule answers a demonstrated deception problem by requiring the solicitor to disclose the terms it chose at the moment it makes the refund representation. It does not tell the church what those terms must be. The sixty-day requirement does exactly that and is unnecessary given narrower fraud and disclosure tools. If the district court finds that the ordinance itself was enacted with a forbidden sectarian purpose, the broader constitutional consequence follows from that finding. If not, the court should preserve only provisions that can operate independently under Florida law without reconstructing the ordinance or enlarging municipal authority.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not constitutionalize a general law of charitable refunds, prevent enforcement of ordinary fraud, contract, restitution, tax, or reporting law, or create a governmental financing entitlement for religion.

## 6. Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION I -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the D.C. Circuit’s separation-of-powers judgment and REMAND for consideration of the FECA issues left unresolved by that court.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The court of appeals shall address any preserved statutory or enforcement issues not resolved because of its structural disposition. The district court’s enforcement judgment does not revive automatically if additional appellate issues remain. Because Stone finds no defect in the Commission’s composition, no severability or ratification inquiry is required.

#### Issue 1: Independent litigating authority

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory antecedent and concludes that the FEC timely invoked this Court’s jurisdiction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress must actually grant an agency authority to conduct Supreme Court litigation, but no particular verbal formula is required. Section 437d(a)(6) affirmatively authorizes the FEC, through its general counsel, to initiate, defend, or appeal any civil enforcement action. In this statutory setting, “appeal” carries the Commission’s own enforcement action through appellate review, including discretionary review in this Court. The more explicit phrasing in the presidential-fund provisions does not negate the affirmative grant Congress enacted for FECA enforcement.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 437d(a)(6) authorizes the FEC to petition for certiorari through its general counsel in a civil FECA enforcement action. The petition filed within § 2101(c)’s period was therefore timely without later authorization from the Solicitor General.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute assigns the FEC exclusive civil-enforcement responsibility and authorizes it to carry an action through appeal in its own name and through its own counsel. Section 518(a)’s use of “appeals” for Supreme Court litigation confirms that the term is not confined to appeals as of right. Stone is construing the authority Congress granted, not allowing the Commission to generate authority from its mission or policy importance.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not recognize independent Supreme Court litigating authority in every agency. The holding rests on the text, structure, and assigned enforcement role of the FEC statutes.

#### Issue 2: Nonvoting congressional officers on an executive commission

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the granted separation-of-powers question because jurisdiction is secure and the court of appeals invalidated the Commission’s structure.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Separation of powers governs who may exercise legal power and what legal consequence that power produces. Congress may not give its officers a vote, veto, quorum power, binding directive authority, or another legally operative role in execution merely by labeling them ex officio. But the Constitution does not prohibit congressional officers from observing, advising, communicating, or attempting to persuade executive decisionmakers when they possess no legal authority to bind the agency. On this record, the Secretary and Clerk had no vote, could not serve as chair, call or adjourn meetings, or count toward a quorum, and possessed no identified power to authorize, block, suspend, or direct Commission action.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The presence of congressional officers as nonvoting ex officio members of an executive commission does not violate separation of powers when those officers possess no vote, veto, quorum power, binding directive authority, or other legally operative control over execution.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The six appointed Commissioners alone exercised the Commission’s enforcement powers. The possibility that an ex officio officer might influence discussion does not convert advice into execution. Buckley and Bowsher involved constitutionally significant governmental authority, and the prior Airports Authority case involved a statutory veto over operational decisions. The arrangement here supplies no comparable legal power.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress may not use an advisory label to conceal operative control. A materially different arrangement involving approval, disapproval, delay, compulsory direction, decisive participation, or another legal consequence would require separate analysis.

## 7. Reich v. Collins

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION I -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Supreme Court of Georgia and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Georgia must provide Reich a clear and certain opportunity to obtain the refund or equivalent relief due under federal law, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies McKesson’s due process rule. A State may choose a predeprivation remedy, a postdeprivation remedy, or both. It may not hold out a postpayment refund route, require payment, and later reinterpret that route away after the taxpayer has relied on it.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a State appears to provide a clear postdeprivation refund remedy for unlawfully collected taxes, due process bars the State from denying that remedy after taxpayers have paid in reliance on it.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Georgia’s refund statute and prior administration represented an available postpayment route. The State’s later construction converts compliance into forfeiture and leaves no clear and certain remedy for a tax collected in violation of federal law.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not dictate the form of Georgia’s tax system or prevent prospective procedural rules that provide an adequate prepayment remedy with fair notice.

## 8. Brown v. Gardner

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION I -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Federal Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Gardner’s claim proceeds under the statute’s causal standard without the invalid fault requirement; no broader remedial instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone independently construes § 1151. The statute requires additional disability resulting from VA hospitalization or treatment and contains no negligence, fault, or accident element. The agency may exclude the natural progress of disease and consequences certain or intended from proper treatment, but it may not add a fault prerequisite Congress omitted.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1151 requires a causal connection between VA treatment and additional disability; it does not require proof of negligence, accident, or other fault.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The text is plain, the neighboring provisions show Congress knew how to require fault, and the longstanding veteran-protective interpretive rule confirms rather than creates the result. The regulation’s fault language changes coverage rather than filling a gap.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make VA an insurer for the underlying disease or for consequences not caused by treatment.
