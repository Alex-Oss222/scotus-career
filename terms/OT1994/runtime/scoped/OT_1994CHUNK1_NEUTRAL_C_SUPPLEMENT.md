## 12. Interstate Commerce Commission v. Transcon Lines

### Exact requested injunction and verified party concessions

The reviewed ICC request is to enjoin collection of loss-of-discount liquidated damages imposed contrary to the credit rules. The trustee's broader collection program includes unpaid freight principal, but this reviewed injunction is directed at the additional liquidated-damages claims. The distinction matters even though the damages are expressed as the difference between the discounted and nondiscounted filed rates. The request is not a universal discharge of the estate's ordinary shipping receivables or an adjudication of every separate private defense.

The ICC identifies three asserted regulatory defects: the original bills did not warn of late-payment consequences under section 1320.3(c); revised bills were not issued within ninety days after expiration of authorized credit under section 1320.2(g)(2)(vi), with the challenged later billing occurring years afterward; and the trustee applies loss-of-discount damages to aggregate balance-due claims on past shipments contrary to section 1320.2(g)(2)(iii). The ICC does not allege failure to state the loss-of-discount provision in the filed rules tariff. The tariff's ninety calendar days from shipment remains distinct from the regulation's period measured from authorized-credit expiration.

The respondents acknowledge that the liquidated-damages collection at issue violates the credit regulations. Original filing locator: Brief in Opposition pages 4–5. They also acknowledge that the charge the requested injunction would prevent is liquidated damages. Original oral-argument locator: transcript page 24. These are bounded concessions about the challenged collection and charge; they do not establish every account's amount, entitlement under a separate contractual collection route, or applicability of every regulatory exception. ### Existing adversarial remedial issue

The parties dispute more than whether an injunction is imaginable in the abstract. The ICC seeks this injunction under the credit and enforcement provisions and argues that denying it would leave its credit rules without effective enforcement in this bankruptcy setting. The trustee argues that such relief permits circumvention of the filed-rate obligation and that other enforcement means remain: cease-and-desist measures, compliance injunctions, and statutory sanctions. The Ninth Circuit's published October 1993 opinion records both positions and its alternative balancing analysis.
