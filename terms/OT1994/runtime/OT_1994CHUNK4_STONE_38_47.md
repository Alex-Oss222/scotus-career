## 47. Celotex Corp. v. Edwards

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Fifth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The execution against the bond shall be set aside. The respondents may challenge or seek modification of the injunction through the bankruptcy appellate process.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone separates the validity of an injunction from the duty to obey it while it remains in force. The bankruptcy court had at least a colorable basis for related-to jurisdiction over an action threatening the debtor’s reorganization and surety obligations. Respondents could appeal the injunction but could not obtain a contrary order from another court and execute anyway.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Parties subject to a bankruptcy injunction issued by a court with at least colorable jurisdiction must obey it until it is stayed, modified, or reversed through authorized review; they may not collaterally execute in defiance of the order.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Orderly judicial administration requires one review path. The injunction was not transparently void, and respondents had notice and appellate remedies. The Fifth Circuit therefore erred by authorizing collateral noncompliance.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not validate an injunction entered without even an arguable jurisdictional basis and does not decide the ultimate statutory power to enjoin every action against a nondebtor surety.
