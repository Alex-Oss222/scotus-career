### Preserved component rules and remedial limits

For Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg, the independent refresh's five proposition blocks are incorporated unchanged in substance:

1. Physically present carrier operations and the completed local sale of locally originating transportation establish substantial nexus, without extending the remote-collection or wholly out-of-state-travel rules.
2. An identical tax on that same local-sale application taxes this retail sale once; later service segments do not themselves duplicate the ticket sale. Jefferson's contrary statutory-definition argument is rejected, not declared waived.
3. The full price measures a localized consumer sales event even when purchased transportation crosses state lines. Central Greyhound retains its distinct apportionment rule for the carrier's multistate business receipts. Buyer/seller labels alone do not do the constitutional work. No hypothetical credit or actual second levy is found.
4. Equal rates plus the absence of an established additional discriminatory burden satisfy nondiscrimination in this application; facial equality is not a universal answer. The lower practical-effect conclusion depended on its rejected external-apportionment characterization.
5. Broad services supporting the taxable local activity establish fair relation without dollar-for-dollar or mile-for-mile public-cost accounting. Out-of-state performance does not defeat the connection to this local transaction.

Scalia and Thomas's sufficient reversal basis is the absence of established discrimination and of an indistinguishable contrary precedent under their narrower method. It is not a join in the full alternative analysis. O'Connor and Breyer regard Central Greyhound as governing the transport-receipts substance; their acceptance of nexus/internal consistency is not a vote to reverse or approval of external consistency.
