## 39. Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission +

#### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Supreme Court of Alaska.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The state enforcement judgment remains in force. No separate remedial instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Religious Freedom Restoration Act

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone assumes without deciding that application of the housing law substantially burdens Swanner’s religious exercise and reaches RFRA’s compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means requirements.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Government may not prescribe or punish religious belief, including religious moral judgments expressed publicly. But RFRA does not require an exemption from a neutral commercial housing-nondiscrimination law when the requested exemption would operate by withdrawing from an identified applicant the statutory protection the law confers and no accommodation preserving that protection in the regulated transaction has been identified. The compelling interests are protecting each member of the covered class from being denied an otherwise available commercial housing opportunity because of the prohibited status and preserving equal access to the covered rental market without forcing applicants to absorb the religious objections of individual landlords.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** RFRA does not require an exemption from a neutral commercial housing-nondiscrimination law when the exemption would authorize denial of the otherwise available tenancy on the prohibited ground, remove the applicant’s statutory protection in the transaction, and no less restrictive accommodation preserving that protection has been shown.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Commission does not regulate Swanner’s belief, teaching, worship, or public expression. It regulates the legal terms on which residential property is offered in the commercial rental market. Referral or notice would still leave the rejected applicants denied the equal treatment the statute guarantees from the landlord who made the unit available. On this record, the requested exemption is not a burden-lifting accommodation that leaves third-party rights intact; its legal effect is the loss of those rights in the transaction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide an accommodation that preserves the applicant’s full statutory protection without material transferred cost or loss of access.

#### Issue 2: Free Exercise Clause

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional claim independently after resolving RFRA.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The challenged provisions neither target religion nor tolerate comparable secular refusals to rent. They regulate the commercial denial of housing on the prohibited ground without making religious motivation an element or maintaining a system of individualized secular exemptions. Smith therefore controls. The Free Exercise Clause does not require an exemption permitting a commercial landlord to deny an otherwise available tenancy on a ground the neutral law forbids.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Because the housing provisions are neutral and generally applicable and contain no comparable secular exemption, the Free Exercise Clause does not require a religious exemption from their prohibition on marital-status discrimination in commercial housing.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Swanner remains free to hold, teach, and express his religious judgment. The constitutional question is whether the State must convert that judgment into a legal authority to deny another person an offered commercial tenancy. Smith answers no when the law regulates the same refusal without regard to religious motivation and does not selectively burden religious conduct.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Nothing here decides regulation of religious teaching or advocacy, housing operated as part of a religious institution’s religious mission, owner-occupied arrangements implicating intimate association or privacy, or a statutory scheme that grants comparable secular exemptions while withholding a religious one.

## 40. Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Ninth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower courts shall recognize color as potentially registrable and determine infringement, secondary meaning, and functionality under ordinary Lanham Act rules.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the statute’s broad definition of a mark. A color can identify source after acquiring secondary meaning, and trademark protection then serves the same informational function as words or symbols. The functionality doctrine prevents control of useful or competitively necessary color features.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A color may be protected as a trademark when it has acquired source-identifying significance and is not functional.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The green-gold dry-cleaning press-pad color can distinguish Qualitex’s goods without performing a utilitarian function. Existing doctrines address shade confusion, depletion, and competitive need rather than imposing a categorical ban absent from the Act.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect color in the abstract, functional colors, or colors lacking secondary meaning, and it leaves ordinary likelihood-of-confusion proof intact.
