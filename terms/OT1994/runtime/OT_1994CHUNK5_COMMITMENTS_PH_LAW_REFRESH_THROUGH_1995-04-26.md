## Actual legal additions and their proper limits

**Kyles, April 19.** Collective lawful defense uses define Brady materiality; police acting for this prosecution fall within the prosecutor's responsibility. A completed material violation is not defeated by a second duplicative prejudice inquiry. Its Souter opinion and the Scalia dissent's actual shared premises leave neither an innocence prerequisite nor an item-by-item threshold. This does not create a general suppression rule, shift Pinson's privacy burden, resolve an affidavit not challenged on appeal, or dispense with the remedial predicates of a Fourth Amendment claim. Souter, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the stated majority grounds. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas disputed the cumulative application, not those invented additional hurdles. The distinction between governing standard, actual evidentiary showing and remedy is relevant method; Brady's particular answer is not transferred to Pinson or Hubbard.

**Rubin, April 19.** The truthful label ban fails both material advancement and independently reasonable proportional fit. The state-assistance argument is rejected assuming it may be considered, without an independent forfeiture holding. Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join those controlling grounds. Stevens supplies a distinct truthful-information ground and does not join the commercial-speech framework or Fox alternative. Predictive judgment remains permissible, but the actual restraint needs responsive support; no universal empirical-study requirement or all-sources-at-once rule follows. These limits reinforce careful use of policy arguments in Hubbard and record facts in Pinson. They do not create First Amendment immunity for knowingly false pleadings or make a theoretical chilling effect decisive under §1001.

**Stone v. INS, April 19.** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, finds a specific INA modification of the ordinary reconsideration rule; Breyer, O'Connor and Souter would retain that incorporated rule on their reading of the consolidation provision. No earlier case is overruled. This is competing construction of a modified statutory system, not permission to discard Bramblett merely upon preferring another original reading. The distinction among review objects, their filing periods and nonreach limits corroborates the need to keep Pinson's preserved search question distinct from affidavit sufficiency and Hubbard's admitted entity question distinct from the unadjudicated asset-collection alternative.

**Morales, April 25.** The hearing-frequency judgment is reversed without a single controlling rationale. O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, distinguishes the annual default, limited class, separately directed interval prediction, initial review and three-year cap from Cavanaugh's automatic removal of alternate hearings. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent because the later consideration opportunity is lost without an established ordinary interim substitute. Cavanaugh remains controlling at its existing scope. Neither the plurality nor the Chief Justice's separate sufficient-risk rationale supplies a new general test; votes cannot be added into an unshown logical subset under Marks. The published positions illuminate the associates' treatment of actual statutory operation and missing institutional protections. They decide neither Fourth Amendment information acquisition nor the meaning of §6, and an analogy cannot import an unproved interim remedy or a generalized risk formula.

**Williams, April 25.** Ginsburg's express-refund-waiver ground, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer, reaches the supported lien-backed collection claim while preserving administrative-claim and timing requirements. Her independently sufficient taxpayer-definition ground has direct joins from Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Scalia withholds that unnecessary alternative and expresses no contrary view of it. Kennedy and Thomas dissent on the integrated claimant restriction and unequivocal consent. Neither construction creates an equitable waiver; a lien is not a levy, the substitute-fund agreement is permissive, and jurisdiction is not a present refund entitlement. Section 7701's own compatibility proviso belongs to that definition and cannot substitute for §6's different department-expansion and agency-narrowing provisions. The new case requires the Hubbard paths to answer the strongest contextual reading rather than equate fidelity to a definition with mechanical isolation of its words. It neither overrules Bramblett nor supplies a blanket rule that all defined entities must be read broadly or narrowly.

**Lopez, April 26.** Kennedy's controlling opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas among the associates, rejects the generalized downstream-effects basis for the original stand-alone possession offense and refuses to insert an absent commerce element. It preserves commercial aggregation precedents, does not adopt a categorical noneconomic exclusion, and does not decide the conditional indictment alternative or reserved sentencing objections. Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would sustain the enacted offense on rational substantial class effects, without adding a commerce element, and remand for the unresolved sentencing issues. No associate joins the Chief Justice's additional six-route framework. The holding distinguishes interpretation of an existing term in X-Citement from insertion of a new one. In Hubbard, §6 actually contains contextual provisions: consideration of those provisions is not the forbidden insertion of a missing statutory term. Conversely, a litigation exception needs a lawful construction, not simply a desirable policy. Lopez's constitutional invalidation and existing dismissal mandate do not dictate the different remedy or preservation posture in either assigned case.
