## 56. United States v. Harris — No. 94-297; prospective April 27, 1995

### Reach, record and independent components

All eight Associates provisionally reach the three supplied questions through the established merits channel. The lower venue and sufficiency rulings do not enlarge those questions. The supplied case involves one trial, two convictions, and 41 months on count one plus a consecutive 60 months on count two for each defendant. No established disqualification affects the roster. Neither the sawed-off description nor an absent government sentencing challenge supports changing the five-year term to another weapon tier.

The offense is the original December 1992 armed-taking offense. It requires taking or attempting to take a vehicle previously transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce, from another's person or presence, by force and violence or intimidation while possessing a §921 firearm. Its 15-year ordinary maximum and distinct serious-injury/death tiers remain separate. The 1994 intent-to-cause-death-or-serious-injury phrase is not applied to this conduct. A profit motive is not an enacted condition. Federal/state cooperation and prosecution discretion do not become additional elements.

### Commerce: supported ground and limits

The Commerce Clause permits Congress to protect an instrumentality of interstate commerce against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the federal offense requires proof of the particular instrumentality’s interstate connection. Original §2119 is constitutional as applied to the forcible taking of the Buick proved here; the judgment is affirmed without adopting the Fifth Circuit’s aggregate-effects rationale.

Here the vehicle’s transportation function, the direct attack and the enacted interstate element each perform a distinct task. An automobile is capable of carrying persons and property through interstate transportation channels. The prohibited taking attacks possession and control of that instrumentality itself. Section 2119 requires the Government to prove in every prosecution that the particular vehicle was transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Buick’s Michigan manufacture and Arkansas and Mississippi titling establish that condition. The Court neither supplies a missing statutory element nor finds that the Buick was traveling interstate when taken.

The defendants’ reliance on the taking’s local character does not defeat this protective authority. Unlike an offense in which an interstate object appears incidentally, this offense directly seizes control of the protected transportation instrumentality. No profit motive or intended interstate resale is necessary to that connection. The Fifth Circuit’s chain through violence, travel, insurance and the national economy does not supply the governing ground.

The holding does not make every crime in or near a vehicle federal, and past interstate movement alone creates no universal jurisdictional rule. Allied-Bruce’s reservation about remote object history remains intact because the direct attack and transportation function do constitutional work independent of that history. Wickard and Perez remain effective within their economic-class scope; the Court does not need that separate route to decide this application. Both the carjacking conviction and the dependent firearm conviction survive the commerce challenge.

### Fault and fair notice: alternative paths

**Supported construction and disposition.** Read the original offense to require knowing armed participation in the forcible taking, with intentional aid to that taking for an aider and abettor. Statutory silence does not authorize punishment for mere association or an unwitting role. The traditional forcible-taking terms supply fair notice of the charged conduct; disagreement about motive, profit or gang retaliation does not make the offense vague. The express interstate vehicle condition is objectively intelligible. No knowledge of federal law or intent to kill is added, and the Court need not decide a separate knowledge-of-jurisdictional-nexus question to reject the supplied challenge.

**Strongest contrary path.** A reading that punishes someone who merely stood nearby, possessed a firearm without knowingly participating, or knew another's plan without helping it would raise the serious fault concerns recognized in Staples and X-Citement. The remedy for a proved omitted-fault instruction would need the actual charge and preservation record. Those are absent; an ordinary mens rea construction does not establish that the lower court gave a contrary instruction. Its account instead expressly treats factual knowledge as implied and intentional assistance as required. Facial invalidation or an automatic new trial therefore lacks the needed premise. The 1994 amendment cannot supply that premise retroactively.

### Cumulative punishment: competing paths and individual commitments

**Cumulative-text path.** Section 924(c) reaches any federally prosecutable crime of violence, expressly includes armed-enhancement predicates, adds punishment to the predicate punishment, and commands consecutive service. The inclusion is not an exclusion of a crime in which firearm possession is an original element. Original §2119's later enactment and silence do not repeal or except the earlier express general direction. Hunter makes that clear authorization sufficient for cumulative punishment in a single trial even assuming Blockburger overlap. The actual Smith requirements remain separate; this ground need not adopt Singleton's universal assertion that every possible §2119 violation necessarily satisfies every §924(c) condition.

**Strongest contrary path.** Congress might be read as addressing added punishment only where a gun increases an otherwise complete offense's penalty, leaving a later offense defined from inception by a firearm outside that direction. The original carjacking statute contains no separate cross-reference. But “any,” the nonexclusive “including” phrase, “in addition to,” and the consecutive command answer that statutory objection. Residual lenity remains available only if genuine ambiguity survives the whole text; it does not automatically follow from judicial disagreement. Deal's conviction-sequence holding is supportive context, not an answer to this different question.

**Distinct Stevens constitutional fork.** His Hunter dissent join is real contrary evidence: multiple convictions impose independent stigma and collateral consequences, and on that view the legislature cannot make included offenses separately punishable merely by commanding cumulative terms. That objection must not be concealed. It also does not itself establish that the two offenses are necessarily the same on every application; Smith preserves the separate relation requirement. The narrow provisional conclusion below applies Hunter without deciding an unnecessary universal-overlap proposition or reopening Hunter. If a later proposed opinion requires Stevens to repudiate his prior constitutional view or expressly adopts universal overlap as a newly adjudicated premise, his join and remedy must be revisited before final freeze. Current Dixon retains Grady for successive prosecutions and does not resolve this single-trial fork.

## 57. United States v. Robertson — No. 94-251; prospective May 1, 1995

### Scope and competing statutory paths

All eight Associates provisionally reach whether this enterprise was engaged in interstate commerce under §1962(a). The statute separately prohibits investment of the specified racketeering-derived income in an enterprise engaged in commerce or whose activities affect commerce. Its principal-participation and income conditions do not disappear. The mine and drug predicates are distinct; movement in the drug activity does not automatically prove enterprise commerce. The complete open-market securities exception supplied in the neutral packet remains intact but is inapplicable to this mine's establishment and operation.

**Direct-engagement path provisionally preferred.** The mine obtained equipment, supplies and workers across state lines for its own operations. Those are the enterprise's acquisition of goods and services, not just the owner's unrelated travel, a predicate crime, or local purchase of an item whose interstate journey ended earlier. American Building Maintenance distinguishes precisely the direct acquisition inquiry from purchases from an independent local distributor after interstate flow has ceased. Nothing requires the enterprise's output to cross a state line as well. Small production and a local gold market do not negate its own interstate procurement. Allied-Bruce's actual-operation analysis is consistent with that distinction without replacing the differently worded RICO conditions.

**Strongest insufficiency objection.** The lower opinion describes a small local mine and characterizes the Government's contacts as incidental; not every asserted transportation is enterprise commerce. A ruling cannot invent invoices or rely only on a car's history, the owner's private trip, or the assumption that every Alaskan business is interstate. That objection is met only by the lower record's actual out-of-state purchases and hiring for the mine. The panel's own reason for rejecting those facts was their supposedly incidental nature, not an adjudicated absence of procurement across state lines. The holding should rest on that legally material category and preserve independent proof questions outside the supplied record.

**Alternative path not adopted preliminarily.** A full affecting-commerce rule could require deciding the constitutional sufficiency of de minimis or aggregate effects. That is unnecessary if actual direct engagement is established. An insufficiency disposition would require confronting the procurement/hiring rather than treating “incidental” as a self-executing legal exemption. The actual Lopez and Harris decisions must be read before deciding whether either rationale changes that analysis or its required formulation. No predicted nexus rule is supplied.
