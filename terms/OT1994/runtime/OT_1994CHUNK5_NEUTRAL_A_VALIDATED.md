## 49. Stone v. Immigration and Naturalization Service — No. 93-1199

**Event:** Merits, April 19, 1995. Supplied argument date: November 28, 1994. **Question:** Whether the agency's disposition of a motion for reconsideration filed before judicial review starts the review period for the original deportation order, under the governing former INA review provisions. The question concerns statutory finality and the deadline, not an equitable enlargement of an expired period.

### Judgment, record, and scope

The Sixth Circuit's January 6, 1994 judgment, 13 F.3d 934, dismissed the petition to the extent it sought review of the original deportation order and denied the separate challenge to the refusal of reconsideration. Those are distinct components. Reversal of the first does not by itself reverse the second or decide deportability, suspension of deportation, or ultimate immigration relief.

Marvin Stone, a Canadian citizen, had lived continuously in the United States without proper immigration documentation since 1977. He was convicted of mail fraud on January 3, 1983, and served eighteen months. The immigration judge found him deportable in January 1988 and denied suspension under former §244(a), because the more-than-180-day confinement rule in §1101(f)(7) prevented the necessary good-moral-character showing for the pertinent seven-year period. These are background findings, not the merits now presented for this Court to decide.

The BIA dismissed his administrative appeal on **July 26, 1991**. In August 1991 he filed a motion labeled reopening and reconsideration. Because it offered no new facts, the BIA treated it as reconsideration, and denied it on **February 3, 1993** as frivolous. He filed the petition for judicial review on **March 25, 1993**: within ninety days of the latter order, well beyond ninety days of the original order. The precise petition date is established, replacing the candidate's suggestion that only approximate sequencing was available.

The Sixth Circuit read the Immigration Act of 1990's consolidation amendment as requiring separate finality and review periods. It rejected reliance on administrative misinformation: the alleged imperfect advice was not affirmative misconduct establishing estoppel, and the court had an independent obligation to examine jurisdiction; appellate Rule 26(b) did not authorize an enlargement. It separately rejected the attack on reconsideration under abuse-of-discretion review. These lower rulings are findings and reasoning to review, not this Court's adopted holdings.

### Governing provisions and distinct functions

Use former **8 U.S.C. §1105a**, including the 1990 amendments applicable to this proceeding, together with its express incorporation of the judicial-review procedure in **chapter 158 of Title 28**. Do not substitute later removal-review legislation. The official 1994 Code reproduction is useful but includes a 1994 amendment applying only to proceedings begun after September 13, 1994; that addition is not the operative version for Stone's earlier proceeding.

- Section 1105a(a)'s incorporation of chapter 158 makes the ordinary administrative-review finality rules an important textual starting point, subject to the INA's stated exceptions and modifications. It is not merely a free-floating analogy to the Hobbs Act.
- Section 1105a(a)(1) requires the review petition no later than **ninety days after issuance of the final deportation order**, with a **thirty-day** period for an alien convicted of an aggravated felony. The lower court applied the ninety-day period here; do not reclassify Stone into the shorter category.
- Section 1105a(a)(6): **“whenever a petitioner seeks review of an order under this section, any review sought with respect to a motion to reopen or reconsider such an order shall be consolidated with the review of the order.”** The parties dispute what this commands about finality, not whether consolidation is generally optional.
- Section 1105a(a)(3) makes service of the judicial petition stay deportation unless the court otherwise directs, but an alien convicted of an aggravated felony receives no such stay unless the court directs one. Do not state a universal automatic-stay rule.
- Section 1105a(a)(8) does not require the Attorney General to defer deportation merely because judicial review may be sought, and preserves the relevant custody authority. A right to seek judicial review, service of an actual judicial petition, and an administrative reconsideration motion are different triggering events.
- Section 1105a(c) requires exhaustion of administrative remedies available **as of right**, and separately addresses departure from the United States. Optional reconsideration is not automatically an unexhausted remedy available as of right. No departure fact is supplied as a live bar in this case.
- **8 C.F.R. §243.1** identifies administrative finality following BIA dismissal. **8 C.F.R. §3.8(a)** provides that filing a reopening/reconsideration motion does not itself stay execution, while allowing a stay specifically granted by the Board or authorized officer. Finality for judicial filing, enforceability, a stay, and exhaustion must be analyzed separately.

The exact official statutory unit and the briefs' regulatory appendices are located through the source inventory. The model must preserve the statutory exceptions above rather than collapse them into an unqualified deadline or stay proposition.

### Entering law and competing paths

**Actual Ibarra, October 15, 1991**, treats one timely substantive reconsideration motion directed to a district-court suppression order as postponing finality, even when it renews a ground abandoned in the initial presentation; the full appeal period begins on disposition. It preserves merits waiver questions and reserves abusive successive or bad-faith use. Its eight-Justice per curiam participation predates Thomas's seating. Ibarra is relevant finality reasoning; it does not itself decide the amended INA.

**Actual Darby, June 21, 1993**, holds that the APA does not authorize courts to require an optional superior-agency appeal from otherwise final action unless a statute expressly requires it or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the challenged action inoperative pending review. Scalia and Thomas withheld the legislative-history ground in the published topology. Darby distinguishes finality from exhaustion; it does not itself establish INA reconsideration tolling. Its qualifications must be retained.

The parties also contest the operation of the ordinary administrative reconsideration rule described in **ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. 270 (1987)**, including its distinction between a challenge to an original order after reconsideration and review of an agency's refusal to reopen for asserted material error. Treat an agency's refusal to revisit claimed legal error separately from a motion founded on new evidence or changed circumstances. A present holding should identify which review object it governs.

**Path favoring original-order review:** The incorporation of chapter 158 brings its ordinary reconsideration/finality rule with it unless the specific INA language displaces it. Consolidation remains useful when judicial review is first sought and administrative reconsideration follows; the provision therefore need not imply that a pre-review reconsideration motion leaves the original order final for the petition deadline. A timely motion may defer finality without compelling an administrative stay or requiring exhaustion of an optional remedy. This is a construction of the statute, not an equitable exception. A late motion cannot simply resurrect an already expired review right. The strongest objection is that this reading weakens the apparent two-order, two-petition structure of the consolidation amendment and Congress's shortening of review time.

**Path favoring separate deadlines:** The amendment anticipates review of both the original order and reconsideration denial, to be consolidated when sought, and gives each a separate filing period. Its structure, the shortened deadline, and the administrative finality/no-stay regulations support review without indefinite postponement through later motions. The strongest objection is that consolidation also has work under the contrary reading, and enforceability does not by itself decide judicial-review finality. The Government's invocation of agency deference must confront whether the relevant question is a delegated administrative determination or the judicial review jurisdiction established by Congress; the regulation cannot supply deference merely by its existence.

The Government also raises the nonreviewability of a pure-error reconsideration refusal as an alternative concerning that separate review object. That issue must not silently replace the admitted original-order timeliness question. If the model proposes to change the lower court's separate denial component on that basis, it must first resolve scope and the motion's legal character from the sources.

### Available dispositions and limits

Affirm the threshold dismissal if the original period ran independently. If the agency motion postponed finality, reverse or vacate that dismissal and remand for the court of appeals to consider the original-order challenge within its lawful scope. A ruling on timely review neither grants suspension nor resolves deportability. The separate reconsideration component requires its own stated ground if touched. No proposed disposition requires an invented equitable extension, automatic administrative stay, or new agency merits determination by this Court.

**Source sufficiency:** The complete lower opinion and the material statutory and merits arguments support this bounded timeliness issue. No current merits outcome is used. Any expanded ruling on the underlying immigration merits, different reconsideration facts, or a distinct unreviewability ground requires its own scope and source check.

## 50. Kyles v. Whitley — No. 93-7927

**Event:** Merits, April 19, 1995. Supplied argument date: November 7, 1994. **Question:** Whether the prosecution's suppression of favorable evidence, assessed on this record as a whole, undermines confidence in the conviction under Brady and Bagley. The supplied operational scope does not independently authorize disposition of every ineffective-assistance, intimidation, knowing-false-testimony, sentencing, or later-evidence claim appearing in a filed brief.

### Judgment and material trial record

The Fifth Circuit's October 14, 1993 decision, **5 F.3d 806**, affirmed denial of federal habeas relief. Judge Higginbotham wrote for himself and Judge Jones; Judge King dissented. The appellate decision reproduces earlier district proceedings in appendices; recovery of that file is not itself complete reading of every appended proceeding.

Dolores Dye was shot at about 2:20 p.m. on September 20, 1984, in a grocery parking lot, and her red Ford LTD was taken. The first trial in November 1984 ended in a hung jury; the second produced a guilty verdict and death sentence on December 7, 1984. The reason for the first jury's disagreement is not established and must not be invented as an innocence or penalty finding.

The State relied on eyewitness identifications and physical evidence. Three witnesses selected Kyles's photograph four days after the offense, and four identified him at trial. Police found the murder weapon behind a stove at the apartment where Kyles regularly stayed, matching ammunition and a holster, the victim's purse and papers in garbage, and pet food of brands the victim had bought. A fingerprint linked Kyles to a grocery receipt in the victim's automobile, but processing destroyed the receipt's readable date; no temporal link may be invented from that print. The defense stressed the absence of his fingerprints on other key objects and challenged the physical evidence's provenance.

Kyles's defense was that **Joseph “Beanie” Wallace** was the killer or otherwise implicated in the crime and framed him. Defense witnesses placed the victim's car with Beanie on the day of the murder. Evidence indicated Beanie changed the plates, had possible access to the apartment and garbage, and had possible romantic or self-protective motives. Johnny Burns testified that Beanie stooped near the stove before the police search; adverse credibility findings and the countervailing State evidence remain part of the record. The jury heard a framing theory; that does not settle whether the suppressed information would have made it substantially stronger.

### Suppressed material and its possible uses

The principal disclosed-after-trial categories established in the lower discussion are: **six contemporaneous eyewitness statements; Beanie's recorded September 22 statement; his September 23 written statement; prosecutor Strider's interview notes; the police memorandum concerning garbage retrieval; and the parking-lot license-plate list.** Assessment must include their relationships and any evidence they would have led competent counsel to develop, with lawful-use and factual limits stated.

- **Identification and observation:** Smallwood's initial account placed the sound of the shot before his observations of the fallen woman and departing car, while his trial account described seeing the shooting in detail. Williams's description of a short, medium-built man did not fit Kyles's height and slender build and could support comparison with Beanie. These are potential impeachment and alternative-suspect uses. Do not convert inconsistencies into an adjudicated finding that a witness knowingly lied. The majority stressed that Territo's identification was not contradicted in the same way and that other identification and physical evidence remained.
- **Beanie's accounts and investigative direction:** The accounts contain differences about acquiring the car, picking up Kyles, grocery events, times, and places, and statements relevant to the Sunday visit and changed plates. They could corroborate defense witnesses, impeach Beanie if called, and support examination of police reliance on him. Some uses arise through police cross-examination concerning the investigation and do not depend entirely on putting Beanie on the stand or admitting every assertion for its truth. The State responds that contradictions also harm the defense version, overlap known information, or concern matters whose admissible use would be limited.
- **Physical evidence and garbage:** The statements and memorandum bear on Beanie's knowledge of and possible access to evidence used to implicate Kyles. They do not establish planting as a fact. The contrary credibility findings and the prosecution's chain of inferences must be considered alongside the possible new cross-examination.
- **Parking-lot plate list:** A list prepared on the evening of September 20 contained seventeen plates and did not include Kyles's car. Its completeness is disputed. The State relied on testimony that police did not record every car; Judge King emphasized the accompanying memorandum's reference to the parking lots without that qualification. Neither the list's completeness nor Kyles's absence from the lot is an established fact simply because his plate is missing.
- **Other allegations in the briefing:** Information about Beanie's prior arrest, a different unsolved killing, an automobile-accident report, compensation, and alleged pressure on witnesses requires item-specific status and scope. For example, the State contests whether the accident report described the victim's car at all. Do not turn an allegation or arrest into guilt, identify a different car as this car without proof, or use a disputed compensation figure as an established finding. These claims may reinforce a supported Brady assessment only after their exact factual and procedural foundation is established; independent claims are not silently admitted by their presence in a brief.

The timing of Strider's notes is described differently or with different precision in the materials. Do not supply an exact interview date by inference. The State's account and the underlying source should be identified if that timing becomes dispositive.

### Lower reasoning, governing law, and distinct inquiries

The majority said that the suppressed evidence, considered individually and collectively, did not establish a reasonable probability of a different result. It treated much of the Beanie material as cumulative or of limited utility because Beanie did not testify, emphasized the defense's actual framing presentation and the remaining identification/physical evidence, and relied in part on its distinct conclusion that counsel was not ineffective for declining to call him. It did not find that every constitutional suppression claim requires a separate showing of factual innocence.

Judge King's dissent examined the evidence's interacting effects on identification, police investigation, physical-evidence provenance, and counsel's handling of Beanie. Her discussion connects Brady and the separately presented Strickland claim, including the counterfactual possibility that disclosure would change the decision whether and how to interview or call Beanie. The present model may evaluate disclosure's effect on preparation and presentation under Brady without deciding an unadmitted standalone Strickland claim. It must confront both the majority's remaining-evidence argument and the dissent's explanation of why apparently separate weaknesses may reinforce one another.

**Brady/Bagley materiality** asks whether there is a reasonable probability that disclosure would have produced a different result, a probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the outcome. It is not a requirement to prove that acquittal was more likely than conviction, and it is not a sufficiency-of-the-evidence test applied after subtracting tainted evidence. The whole disclosure counterfactual matters. Individual items need not each independently satisfy materiality before their cumulative effect is considered. The scope of the prosecution's disclosure responsibility must address favorable information held by police participating in the investigation, rather than assume that a trial prosecutor's personal ignorance removes it from the constitutional inquiry.

The actual **Williams** rule concerns disclosure in federal grand-jury practice and personally known evidence directly negating guilt within its stated conditions. Its personal-knowledge and no-investigative-search qualifications are not a trial Brady limitation. Use the date-eligible opinion's actual domain and each Justice's own public position.

**Actual Brecht** retains Chapman for the specified preserved Doyle claim on habeas. **Actual O'Neal** extends that rule to its preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category; its proposed extension to ordinary constitutional trial error generally had only three supporters. Neither supplies a universal Chapman rule for every habeas claim, and neither replaces a prejudice element that defines another constitutional violation. Conversely, the historical universal Brecht/Kotteakos premise discussed in the lower opinion is not governing law in this timeline.

Accordingly, separate: (1) whether favorable evidence was withheld within the prosecution's constitutional responsibility; (2) whether the suppression was material under the defining Brady/Bagley standard; (3) any independently presented constitutional claim with its own prejudice or harmlessness rule; and (4) ordinary habeas gates. A finding of a completed material Brady violation cannot be defeated merely by repeating the same prejudice assessment under a weaker harmless-error label. Any proposed additional harmlessness step needs an independent, applicable legal basis, not an assumption that all habeas cases use historical Brecht. **Schlup** supplies a gateway for otherwise barred claims, not an additional innocence condition or the materiality test for an available Brady claim.

Date-eligible Justice-specific public positions include O'Connor's joinder in Bagley's reasonable-probability discussion and Stevens's Bagley dissent concerning disclosure/request distinctions, together with the participating Justices' actual Williams, Brecht, O'Neal, and Schlup positions. Ginsburg and Breyer's reservation of O'Neal's generalization must be distinguished from their joinder in its limited holding. Those positions inform legal reasoning; none determines a present vote by label or assumed historical alignment.

### Record boundaries, alternative paths, and remedy

The state collateral hearing occurred in 1989; the federal district court denied relief on March 24, 1992. A later Rule 60(b) attempt involving additional material followed a separate procedural route. Later Cahill/Kersh recantation material discussed by litigants is not automatically admitted evidence in the judgment now reviewed. The model must not strengthen either side with a later allegation whose availability and admission remain unresolved.

**Affirmance path:** Apply cumulative Bagley review to all properly established suppressed material and reasonable lawful uses, then conclude that the consistent identification evidence, physical evidence, weaknesses of the framing account, and cumulative nature of particular disclosures leave confidence in the verdict intact. Its strongest objection is that the suppression affects the apparent independence of those very strands and may make the framing defense substantially different from the one the jury heard.

**Relief path:** Find that the interacting impeachment, corroboration, investigative, and provenance uses undermine confidence in the verdict despite evidence sufficient to convict. Its strongest objection is that the counterfactual must remain grounded in admissible or otherwise legitimate uses and the actual record, and cannot presume Beanie's confession, planting, completeness of the plate list, false testimony, or the truth of later disputed recantations.

**Remand path:** If the lower review improperly atomized materiality, conflated noncalling of Beanie with the absence of useful disclosure, or imported an inapplicable harmlessness standard, vacate for a proper whole-record assessment if that application remains for the lower court. The lower majority expressly used cumulative language; a remand must identify the actual analytical defect rather than misquote it as disclaiming cumulative review.

If a material violation requiring relief is established, the ordinary remedy is a conditional habeas writ allowing a new trial within a reasonable time, not an acquittal, automatic unconditional release, or this Court's declaration of factual innocence. A penalty-only disposition requires a separately supported sentencing-materiality ground and scope determination; the fact that Louisiana permits residual-doubt argument does not by itself create a new federal constitutional residual-doubt instruction right.

## 51. Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co. — No. 93-1631

**Event:** Merits, April 19, 1995. Supplied argument date: November 30, 1994. **Question:** Whether the federal prohibition on truthful numerical statements of malt-beverage alcohol content on labels satisfies the First Amendment's commercial-speech requirements. The independently sustained advertising prohibition is not before the Court for invalidation.

### Litigation path and factual dispute

Coors sought federal approval in 1987 for alcohol-content statements in labels and advertisements. BATF denied approval under **27 U.S.C. §205(e)(2) and (f)(2)**. The first district judgment invalidated both restrictions; **Coors I, 944 F.2d 1543 (10th Cir. 1991)**, remanded for factual development on material advancement and fit. Truthful, lawful commercial expression and the substantial interest in preventing competitive escalation of beer strength were accepted as the starting premises.

After trial on remand, the district court upheld the advertising restriction but invalidated the labeling restriction. **Coors did not appeal the advertising ruling.** The Government appealed the labeling ruling, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed on August 23, 1993, **2 F.3d 355**. The district court found defects in both advancement and fit; the court of appeals resolved the advancement requirement and **did not reach the separate fit question**.

The Government relies on the history of competition over alcohol strength, modern malt-liquor promotion, Coors's interest in correcting a perception that its beer is weaker, and reasonable inferences about the possible effect of truthful numerical labels. Its position does not require a finding that most beer purchasers currently prefer higher strength; it argues that existing preferences may partly reflect the restriction. Coors responds that accurate information may support lower consumption or preferences for lower strength, that the record does not demonstrate the asserted strength war where disclosures are allowed, and that the regime's different treatment of advertising, other beverages, and state-required labels weakens the claimed connection. Neither a future strength war nor harmlessness of disclosure is an established trial fact merely because one party asserts it.

### Exact statutory architecture and exceptions

Read **§205(e) and (f) together with the provision following the advertising clauses**, not just the brief's shorthand. The official statutory unit is [available locally](../../../tmp/ot1994_chunk5_a/uscode1994_27_selected_section_FULL.txt); the filed statutory and regulatory appendix is [separately preserved](../../../tmp/ot1994_chunk5_a/coors_statutory_regulatory_appendices_FULL.txt).

- **Section 205(e)(2), labeling:** regulations must provide adequate information about identity, quality, and alcohol content, **“except that statements of, or statements likely to be considered as statements of, alcoholic content of malt beverages are prohibited unless required by State law.”** The same clause states that, for wines, alcohol-content statements are **required only** above fourteen percent alcohol by volume. “Required only” is not a prohibition on disclosure below fourteen percent.
- **Section 205(f)(2), advertising:** its alcohol-information exception prohibits statements of, or likely to be considered statements of, the alcohol content of **malt beverages and wines**. It does not itself contain the labeling clause's “unless required by State law” exception. Do not transfer that textual exception from (e)(2) into (f)(2).
- A later paragraph expressly addresses both **subsection (e) labeling** and **subsection (f) advertising** for malt beverages shipped or introduced into a State from outside it, conditioning application on the receiving State's imposing similar requirements on comparable intrastate transactions. Its textual reference to both provisions must be acknowledged. The agency's longstanding construction applies the label ban except where state law affirmatively requires disclosure, but applies the advertising restriction only where the State has a corresponding advertising prohibition. Text and administrative construction are distinct propositions. Coors's constitutional challenge does not itself present a statutory attack on that construction.
- The wider section also contains separate institutional and transitional exceptions, including the provision for qualifying outdoor advertising already in place on June 18, 1935 until replacement, restoration, or renovation, and the publisher/radio treatment subject to involvement as producer, importer, wholesaler, bottler, or warehouseman. These are not the state-law or numerical-label exceptions. If a proposed rationale depends on the exact reach of those ancillary provisions, consult their complete text before using them.

In the challenged regulatory scheme **27 C.F.R. §7.26(a)** prohibited alcohol-content and original-extract statements unless required by state law. Where state law required alcohol content but did not specify the manner, the statement was to use percentage by weight or volume, not proof, maximums, or minimums; if state law specified the manner, that manner governed. Section 7.26(b) permitted **“low alcohol” or “reduced alcohol” only below 2.5 percent alcohol by volume**. Section 7.26(c) permitted “non-alcoholic” with the directly conjoined, readily legible, contrasting-background statement **“contains less than 0.5 percent (or .5%) alcohol by volume.”** Section 7.26(d) permitted **“alcohol-free” only for products containing no alcohol**. Preserve these separate triggers.

The numerical-disclosure challenge is distinct from **§7.29(f)'s strength-descriptive wording restrictions** and **§7.29(g)'s numerical/design restrictions suggesting strength**. Coors does not ask this Court to invalidate those separate restrictions. They may nevertheless bear on whether the numerical ban reasonably fits the asserted harm.

BATF's April 19, 1993 interim regulatory response complied with the district injunction by suspending enforcement of the regulatory label restriction. That litigation compliance is not a permanent statutory repeal or a concession that the statute is unconstitutional. The older reproduced regulation should be identified as the challenged regime, not presented without qualification as the unaltered enforcement position throughout the litigation.

### Actual entering law and public writings

**Central Hudson** supplies protection for truthful, nonmisleading speech concerning lawful activity, a substantial governmental interest, material advancement of that interest, and appropriate fit. **Fox** requires a reasonable, proportionate fit, not the least restrictive conceivable means. The Government must demonstrate real harms and material advancement in the relevant setting, but the actual precedents do not demand controlled studies in every case or regulation of all sources at once.

**Actual Discovery Network** rejects a commercial category unrelated to the physical harm that supposedly justifies the restriction; it does not invalidate every incremental measure. Stevens's opinion was joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; Thomas joined White's dissent. **Actual Edenfield**, Kennedy for eight, distinguishes supported professional prophylaxis from insufficient justification for the actual solicitation ban; O'Connor's dissent invokes the professional setting and preventive regulation. **Actual Ibanez**, Kennedy, supplies the truthful-credential application and the distinct burden of the prescribed disclaimer; O'Connor's partial dissent identifies the particular disclosure alternative and credential concern, not a general rule that unsupported bans are valid.

**Actual Edge** controls only at the level of the rationale that obtained a majority: meaningful reduction and reasonable fit for the state-based lottery-broadcast scheme on that record. Broader categorical or vice-based deference discussed by fewer Justices did not become controlling law. The majority there was Stone, White, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; O'Connor and Scalia's wider category-level position must be treated as their public reasoning rather than silently imported as the Court's rule.

**Actual Lanphere, April 18, 1995**, is date-eligible and materially relevant. Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer, separately applies material advancement and reasonable fit to a restriction selected by intended commercial expression. It rejects the challenged record-access/use conditions on their demonstrated operation, preserves neutral confidentiality/access rules, imposes no all-sources-at-once requirement, and does not adopt least-restrictive means. O'Connor and Scalia's dissent emphasizes official supply of information and privacy in that setting while accepting First Amendment review. Neither the outcome nor the setting alone determines this label case. The copied holdings and public positions are in the validation supplement.

### Competing applications and remedies

**Path sustaining the label restriction:** The evidence and reasonable predictive judgment may establish that numerical strength comparisons would stimulate strength competition, while the State's public-health and federalism interests justify the boundaries. Advertising, other beverages, and compelled disclosures may present different institutional or market problems. The numerical and descriptive restrictions can operate together. The strongest objection is the need to show how this scheme materially works despite those distinctions; an abstract interest or a vice label does not satisfy the actual test.

**Path invalidating the label restriction:** The regime's operation and the trial evidence may fail to demonstrate material advancement; accurate percentages are not equivalent to exhortations to buy stronger beer, while the treatment of other channels and beverages may undermine the claimed effect. Independently, the numerical prohibition may lack reasonable fit given supported alternatives such as targeted promotional restrictions or direct strength regulation. Those alternatives are arguments bearing on proportionality, not mandatory replacement legislation or proof that the Constitution demands the least restrictive means. The strongest objection is that a court must assess the evidence and explanation actually offered and may not require complete coverage, uniform state policy, or empirical certainty.

**Federalism and preservation:** The Government also frames the scheme as facilitating state alcohol policy, with reference to the Twenty-first Amendment. Coors contests both the rationale and its presentation below. Do not presume either that it was conclusively forfeited or that it exempts the restriction from the First Amendment. Address preservation if that independent rationale is necessary to the disposition.

Affirmance may preserve the injunction against the challenged label restriction on a supported advancement or fit ground. Reversal on advancement may require remand to address the fit ground the court of appeals did not reach, unless the record and lawful scope permit its resolution here. Vacatur and remand is available if a lower court imposed an incorrect evidentiary or tailoring standard. Each disposition must identify its ground. No available path automatically invalidates the unappealed advertising judgment, mandates a uniform nationwide disclosure format, or adjudicates the separate descriptive-strength restrictions.

Party state-by-state surveys and disputed market predictions are attributed submissions, not independently verified fifty-state findings. A ruling depending on a particular unverified state enactment or on the complete reach of an ancillary statutory exception needs that source checked first.
