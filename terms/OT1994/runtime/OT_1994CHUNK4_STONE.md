- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Complete Auto to the taxable event Oklahoma selected: the in-state sale of transportation. Apportionment is not required merely because the service later crosses state lines.

#### Case-specific reasoning

Congress can change prospective law and sometimes govern pending cases. Simulated Morgan Stanley already establishes the Article III bar against §27A(b)’s command to reopen the completed, unappealed private securities judgment it reviewed; its reserved questions remain open. Reopening final Article III judgments crosses a distinct separation-of-powers boundary. A remedial aim cannot dissolve finality. Later modification of a continuing injunction must be analyzed separately.

#### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the judgment of the Sixth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The final dismissal remains binding. Section 27A(b) may not compel its reopening. No separate remand instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Legislative reopening of a final Article III judgment

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Article III’s allocation of judicial power. Congress may change substantive law, apply new law to pending cases, alter the rules governing prospective relief, create generally applicable judicial remedies, and waive federal claims or defenses when otherwise constitutionally permissible. It may not direct an Article III court to reopen and replace a final judgment in a private case after the judicial process for revising that judgment has ended. The defect is not principally unfairness or disappointed reliance. It is that another department has asserted legal authority to revise the judiciary’s conclusive disposition of a case.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress violates Article III when it commands federal courts to reopen final judgments in private civil actions and enter new dispositions under a subsequently enacted rule.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Morgan Stanley supplies the existing source for the final-judgment boundary in this statutory setting. Section 27A(b) does not merely change the law applicable to pending litigation. It identifies cases already dismissed by final judgment and orders their restoration. If Congress may require a completed Article III judgment to be replaced, the original judgment was not a conclusive exercise of judicial power. Wheeling Bridge is different because new law changed what continuing prospective relief could lawfully regulate going forward; it did not legislate a substitute disposition for a completed damages or dismissal judgment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar prospective legislation, new law governing nonfinal cases, lawful alteration of continuing equitable relief, waiver of a federal claim or defense, or generally applicable judicial reopening rules administered by courts consistently with Article III.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Compliance with both state duties and federal law was possible. Regulatory inaction, without a demonstrated federal choice to forbid the safety feature or state duty, is not obstacle preemption.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A valid federal standard or an affirmative federal policy requiring a uniform no-regulation result could present a different conflict.

- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Colorado may maintain neutral access rules, fees, privacy redactions, and use restrictions supported under the governing speech standard. #### Issue 1: Constitutional right of access

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the issue and rejects the broad constitutional entitlement asserted.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The First Amendment does not create a general right to obtain executive criminal-justice records. The scope of ordinary access begins with enacted state law and the specific historical access doctrines.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** There is no general First Amendment right to inspect all state criminal-justice records merely because the information may support protected speech.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide access to a court filing or proceeding covered by the separate experience-and-logic doctrine.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches this distinct claim because Colorado has created an access regime and then denies records by reference to the requester’s intended protected speech.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Colorado may protect privacy, prevent harassment, charge retrieval costs, and redact sensitive data through neutral rules. It has not shown that a categorical denial to every requester intending direct commercial solicitation materially advances those interests with the fit Central Hudson requires. The rule distinguishes identical requests solely by the message and audience the requester plans to pursue.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a State makes criminal-justice records otherwise available, it may not categorically withhold them solely because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation unless the use-based restriction satisfies the governing commercial-speech standard.

A wholesale denial based on intended speech burdens more protected communication than the evidence supports.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require disclosure of confidential records, prevent neutral limits on access, or protect false, deceptive, coercive, or harassing solicitation.

Broad identification mandates require a justification fitted to the regulated speech. The state's interests do not justify this application to an individual's political leaflet. #### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Supreme Court of Ohio.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The enforcement order and fine against McIntyre shall be set aside. Ohio may enforce valid disclosure rules not inconsistent with the holding.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone treats anonymous political advocacy as core speech. The identity ban is content based because it applies to election advocacy and burdens the author’s choice to speak without attribution. Ohio’s interests in fraud prevention and campaign information do not justify a categorical prohibition reaching truthful, independently produced leaflets.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not require every person distributing campaign literature to identify herself on the face of the communication when the rule broadly prohibits anonymous core political advocacy and is not narrowly fitted to fraud or corruption.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Anonymous pamphleteering has a deep constitutional history and can protect speakers from retaliation while preserving public debate. Existing laws against fraud, false attribution, and undisclosed campaign organizations are materially narrower.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate source disclosure for candidates, committees, coordinated spending, large financial contributions, or fraudulent impersonation under appropriately tailored rules.
