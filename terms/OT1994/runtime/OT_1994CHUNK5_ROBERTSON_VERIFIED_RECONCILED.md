## Explicit reconciliation repair and shared join

All eight associates retain reversal of the commerce-based Count Six disposition and the bounded further-proceedings remedy. All eight can also join the following **single cumulative ground**, directly and at the same stated generality:

**R1 — Operative rule:** An enterprise is engaged in interstate commerce under §1962(a) when it itself participates directly in interstate acquisition, production or distribution of goods or services. Actual cross-border procurement and mine-work labor movements, together with carriage outside the State of gold produced by the same mining enterprise, establish direct engagement on this record; no separately substantial interstate effect of this individual mine is required under that alternative.

**R2 — Cumulative application and attribution:** Some equipment and supplies were obtained outside Alaska and transported there for the mine, employees were repeatedly brought from outside Alaska for its work, and the proprietor carried approximately $30,000 of the mine's gold outside the State. These are actual interstate connections of the identified continuing mining operation. Calling its activity local mining and local sales does not erase its interstate acquisition of goods and labor or the direct movement of its own product. The proprietor's carriage of the mine's product belongs in this cumulative assessment because the carried goods are the enterprise's actual output and he carried them as its sole proprietor; no further finding of export-sale purpose is needed to use that connection on this record. The Court rejects the contrary attribution objection at this record-specific level. It does not hold that all private travel, unrelated drug activity, all distributions to owners, or every removal of retained assets automatically becomes enterprise commerce. It decides no standalone sufficiency rule for private carriage considered without the established interstate mining operations.

**R3 — Distinct alternatives and limits:** American Building Maintenance's direct-engagement distinction applies by comparison while its Clayton Act setting remains distinct. Gulf Oil supports the difference between actual interstate engagement and local activity with interstate effects. The case does not require deciding the separate affecting-commerce route, its substantial-effects requirement, all RICO applications, or Congress's outer constitutional ceiling. The enterprise's own commerce condition remains separate from the predicates' commerce and from §1962(c)'s direction requirement. Allied-Bruce confirms attention to actual commercial operation but does not merge its broader FAA language with RICO's two alternatives. Lopez and Harris remain operative at their bounded grounds.
