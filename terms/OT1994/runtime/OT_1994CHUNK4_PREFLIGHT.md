## Factual corrections and qualifications, by matter

### 37. Newport News

The judgment below has two distinct components. The Director's petition regarding temporary-total versus permanent-partial disability concerned the interval May 6, 1988 through February 16, 1989; the lower court found no pleaded administrative or economic injury supporting that challenge. The Special Fund issue was separately reviewable and was reversed/remanded. The disability-award component does not bring the Fund disposition into review automatically. The lower opinion's footnote 1 uses constitutional standing language; its actual rationale must be described accurately even if the question presented is statutory. The opinion distinguishes possible concrete administrative burden and Fund exposure rather than resolving every conceivable agency injury. There is no basis here to decide a new medical or earnings fact about Harcum.

### 38. Anderson

The Ninth Circuit source is the short report at **12 F.3d 154–155**, not pages 155–159. It establishes the class, regulations, adoption of Beaton, and affirmance. The class includes households with two or more nonsibling dependent children and either a single caretaker relative **or a relative married couple**. MPP §44-205.31 and successor §82-824.1 are identified. The appellate opinion does not itself supply household-specific payment amounts or the text of ACF-AT-94-6.

Beaton supplies a complete adopted rationale and pertinent federal-regulation quotations. Its Washington figures ($397 for two persons together and $314 for each separate one-person unit) must not be attributed to the California class. Its anti-presumption holding is distinct from the district court's additional proration, uniformity and equal-protection issues, which Beaton expressly did not reach. Edwards does not establish those unaddressed grounds as Ninth Circuit holdings.

The complete ACF-AT-94-6 of March 16, 1994, the complete California regulations, and an authenticated district-court record with California payment amounts were not obtained. If disposition depends on the precise agency interpretation, deference to it, or a household arithmetic finding, that source must be supplied or the unsupported premise omitted. No district citation is invented.

### 39. Swanner

The operative lower opinion is the **May 13, 1994 substituted opinion on rehearing**, replacing the February 11 opinion. The source includes both the substitution order and Moore's dissent. Three complainants—Bowles, Harper and Moose—were refused solely because of the stated policy. The policy included even a nonsexual opposite-sex roommate arrangement, on the stated appearance-of-immorality ground. The Commission's order became final January 22, 1991 after neither side objected within the regulatory ten-day period; the superior court affirmed August 31, 1992.

The municipal text quoted in the lower opinion has an **individual-home exception when the renter would share common living areas with the owner, lessor, manager, agent or other person**. That qualification cannot be dropped. The lower court addressed the marital-status construction, federal and Alaska free-exercise claims, and federal/state due process separately. Its RFRA footnote assumes constitutional validity and applicability for its analysis; it does not resolve RFRA's constitutional validity. Exact damages or penalties are not supplied by this opinion and must not be invented.

### 40. Qualitex

The lower judgment did not eliminate all relief. It reversed the registered-mark infringement ruling and required cancellation on the counterclaim, while affirming the independent §43(a) unfair-competition trade-dress judgment, monetary relief, and injunction. The opinion treats the unregistered trade dress separately from the registered color mark. Registration no. 1,633,711 issued February 5, 1991 covers the green-gold pad color as described in the source. The district findings on secondary meaning, nonfunctionality and confusion are findings under review, not new findings here. The opinion distinguishes a competitive need for color generally from a need for this particular shade. Passing off was an alternative theory not reached because trade dress supported the unfair-competition ruling. Any request to set aside the judgment must specify the registered-mark component and preserve the separate surviving relief unless independently before the Court.

### 41. Jefferson Lines

The bankruptcy proceedings and district review were in **Minnesota**; the challenged tax is Oklahoma's. The lower opinion reproduces the 4% gross-receipts provision for transportation for hire with the qualification **not otherwise exempted in the Oklahoma Sales Tax Code**. The lower court accepted internal consistency but rejected external consistency; those are distinct issues. Its description of the general ticket-sale statute must be kept distinct from the record's application to tickets sold in Oklahoma for travel originating there. This source does not support expanding the actual taxable transaction to an unpresented transaction.

### 42. Plaut

The suit was filed November 25, 1987 and involved the plaintiffs' 1983 stock purchase. The district court dismissed with prejudice August 13, 1991; no appeal followed. Congress enacted §27A December 19, 1991; the reinstatement motion came February 11, 1992. The district court found the statutory reinstatement predicates satisfied. The lower opinion quotes §27A(b) and states that subsection (a) is not challenged. Keith's separate writing distinguishes final unappealed judgments from cases still on appeal. The clean candidate's references to 1984 fraud or an undifferentiated attack on all of §27A require source correction or qualification. The entering-law slice, including the earlier simulated finality decision, controls its present legal effect.

### 43. Whitecotton

The reported appeal runs **17 F.3d 374–377**; source pinpoints to 378–380 are unsupported. The child is Margaret (Maggie), and the vaccination at issue was her third DPT dose, August 18, 1975. The petition was filed August 2, 1990. The first doctor that evening did not observe seizures; another doctor observed seizures the next day. These facts should retain their evidentiary source and must not become an independent medical finding.

The lower court reversed/remanded **for determination of compensation**, not merely unspecified reconsideration. The two consolidated appeals include the Rule 60(b)(2) new-evidence matter. Its onset reasoning, comparison with the residual-seizure exclusion, and analysis of proposed unrelated birth trauma must be represented from the full opinion; the fresh validator is independently restoring those distinctions. The opinion's footnote 2 separately quotes the Table's encephalopathy exclusion on proof by a preponderance that infection, toxins, trauma or metabolic disturbance caused the condition. This is distinct from §13's unrelated-factor treatment of idiopathic or unexplained causes and from the initial onset showing. The source states the Secretary did not argue one of the enumerated Table causes there.

**Regulatory transition:** the complete February 8, 1995 rule is effective March 10, 1995. Section 100.3(c), pp. 7695–7696, applies both the revised Table and revised qualifications to petitions filed on or after March 10. Petitions filed before that date remain governed by §2114(a) and (b) **as in effect on February 8, 1995**. The 1990 Whitecotton petition falls in that earlier-petition provision. The preamble encourages use of scientific findings where appropriate; that encouragement is not retroactive substitution of the revised Table. The operator has been notified of the March 10 source transition for separate chronological admission.

### 44. Freightliner/Myrick

The consolidated common-law claims involve the Myricks against Freightliner and Lindsey's estate-related action against Navistar. The Myrick Georgia RICO claim was abandoned on appeal, so the disposition of that separate claim is not reopened by the appellate common-law reversal. The lower majority and Hill dissent use an effective manufacturer-choice characterization. That characterization is an attributed lower-court premise; it must not silently become a finding that NHTSA affirmatively adopted a policy precluding common-law liability.

Paccar invalidated the stopping-distance requirements from 60 mph and suspended the portions requiring heavier axles and antilock pending sufficient safety/reliability evidence, while recognizing agency authority to propose replacement standards. It did not erase every air-brake provision. The later precise operative Standard 121 amendment/CFR text has not been retrieved here. If the current disposition turns on whether the actual agency text deliberately protected a choice rather than left an area unregulated, the fresh validator must verify that text or keep the legal consequences of the two readings separately qualified. The lower court's historical treatment of post-1991 Supreme Court cases is not a substitute for current entering law.

### 45. Heintz

The loan agreement authorized charges for loss/damage automobile insurance. The challenged $4,173 premium was alleged to be financial/default-protection insurance instead. Both §1692f(1)'s unauthorized-amount allegation and §1692e's false/misleading allegation appear. At the dismissal stage, allegations about regular debt collection and the insurance are not final liability findings. Removing the attorney exemption is established by the full 1986 act; the ultimate application of the remaining provisions still requires their own terms and statutory qualifications. The corrected argument date is February 21, 1995.

### 46. Lanphere

The complete quoted §24-72-305.5 has two sentences with different terms: criminal-justice records shall not be used to solicit business for pecuniary gain; the custodian must deny inspection unless the requester signs a statement against **direct** solicitation of business for pecuniary gain. Preserve the difference between the use prohibition and certification condition. The lower opinion supplies the relevant definitions in §24-72-302(3), (4) and (7). The plaintiffs were the legal practice seeking misdemeanor/traffic information and the treatment center; the record involved El Paso County court records and previous access to names and addresses.

The majority did not simply stop at the absence of a generic access right: it treated the use-based restriction as implicating the First Amendment and applied Central Hudson. Aldisert's dissent addresses qualified access, the commercial-speech classification, and fit; the State's concession that the information also appeared in the El Paso County News bears on the source record. No finding of misleading or unlawful proposed solicitation appears. No substantive legal framing is certified by this preflight.

### 47. Celotex

The underlying judgment was $281,025.80; Northbrook's bond was $294,987.88. The mandate and bankruptcy filing occurred October 12, 1990; the §105 order was October 17. The enforcement motion was May 3, 1991, and the district enforcement order May 27, 1992. The majority has two grounds—order interpretation and jurisdiction/equitable power. Jones joined only the order-interpretation ground and expressly withheld agreement on the broader power issue. The December 22 rehearing order is part of the available lower record.

The injunction text reproduced in note 1 refers to proceedings involving a Debtor, including appeals/supersedeas bonds. The subsequent Florida clarification at 128 B.R. 478, 484 is cited but has not been independently retrieved. The actual collateral agreement was not before the appellate court (note 7); generalized representations do not establish a definitive estate-property finding. The automatic §362 stay and a discretionary §105 injunction must remain distinct. If exact Florida-order construction beyond the quoted October 17 text is material, obtain the clarification order itself.

### 48. McIntyre

The commission imposed a $100 fine for leaflets distributed April 27 and 28, 1988; some bore identifying information, others did not. The lower opinion quotes §3599.09(A)'s exception for personal correspondence **not reproduced by machine for general distribution**. The identification requirement permits residence **or business** address and specifies the responsible individual or an organization's chairman, treasurer or secretary. These terms cannot be replaced with an unqualified requirement to reveal a home address. The opinion does not supply a finding that the speech was fraudulent. The Wright dissent addresses the individual author's activity separately from organized campaign financing; the fresh validator must make its own framing from the full text and current law.
