##### Issue 1: Territorial design and personal constitutional deprivation

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated Shaw, effective June 28, 1993, holds that deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial districting, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the asserted equal-protection deprivation without exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, inferior civic membership, or another independently cognizable constitutional wrong. Its holding expressly preserves ordinary pleading, noneconomic injuries, and independent statutory claims. See [Election Law Holdings, Shaw](../../../state/holdings/election-law.md#shaw-v-reno-509-us-630-1993).
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** [Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#territorial-racial-design-and-independently-cognizable-injury), present operation effective June 28, 1993, Shaw. Predominance alone supplies no new scrutiny trigger or general replacement for the law governing racial classifications elsewhere.
- **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Fifteenth Amendment for its distinct prohibition. Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1, 22 (1948), identifies equal protection as personal. Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339, 341-348 (1960), rejects geographic insulation of racial municipal disenfranchisement. Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399, 402-404 (1964), addresses official racial ballot labels without requiring proof of a changed winner. White v. Regester, 412 U.S. 755, 765-770 (1973), and McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents, 339 U.S. 637, 641-642 (1950), show that formal access need not establish equal participation.
- **Material Other Authority:** Wright v. Rockefeller, 376 U.S. 52 (1964), resolved failure to establish the alleged racial purpose, not the constitutionality of proven racial predominance. United Jewish Organizations v. Carey, 430 U.S. 144 (1977), supplies qualified support through fractured opinions, not one comprehensive injury test. The exact current force of Croson must be checked after any earlier simulated Adarand decision; neither a historical 1995 opinion nor a planning proposal changes entering law.
- **Open Point:** The fuller explanation of political membership, application to the asserted stereotyping and representational harms, and the extent of voluntary-inclusion permission beyond the decided Shaw holding.

- **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act §§2 and 5, then codified at 42 U.S.C. §§1973 and 1973c, and §14(b), then §1973l(b). Beer v. Proportional electoral opportunity is relevant under the totality, not a safe harbor or burden-shifting defense; disproportionality alone does not establish liability, and opportunity elsewhere cannot excuse proved local dilution. No universal population denominator or geographic scale, racial floor or ceiling, or proportional-election entitlement follows.
- **Open Point:** Whether lack of a statutory requirement for a particular district itself defeats the State's constitutional permission to adopt it. No direct challenge to a particular DOJ objection or independent §2/§5 claim is established by the present record description.

#### C. Decision-material litigation positions

**Position supporting the challenge:** Deliberate racial sorting may itself deny personal equality by assigning citizens an official racial political identity. Subordination of ordinary districting principles can support that characterization; an unusual shape is evidence, not necessarily an indispensable element. The challenge need not be reduced to economic loss or inability to elect a preferred candidate. A State's description of its purpose as inclusion cannot eliminate personal rights, and obedience to an administrator cannot authorize an otherwise unconstitutional act.

**Position defending the district:** The reconstructed Shaw rule forecloses predominance alone as the deprivation. A common territorial constituency may recognize demonstrated voting conditions without imposing different legal terms of membership by race or making its representative the agent of a single racial group. The distinction must survive the finding of predominance rather than depend on finding that race was merely incidental. Statutory obligations need not exhaust the State's lawful choices. Any allegation of actual exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, or official inferior status must nevertheless be considered under its own law.

**Necessary qualifications:** Formal eligibility is not conclusive proof of equality; a racial restriction may itself injure. Conversely, an allegation that a boundary reflects race does not automatically establish every asserted consequence of that design. Neither statewide racial political strength nor the race of an elected representative settles a particular plaintiff's claim. Lack of dilution does not foreclose every equality injury. Independent statutory claims have their own elements. The precise reasoning below, alternative grounds, concessions, and preservation are now fixed by the reconstructed judgment above.
