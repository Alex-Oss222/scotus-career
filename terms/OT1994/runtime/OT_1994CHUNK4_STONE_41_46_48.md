## 41. Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc.

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Position and scope

**Approval Status:** USER-SUPPLIED POSITION. The supplied disposition, reasoning and express alternatives are recorded as preparation instructions. No Court judgment, vote, rationale or coalition is created by this supplement.

**Build:** Commerce Clause and Federalism.

#### Case-specific reasoning

The taxable event is the local sale of the ticket, not every mile of the interstate journey. A conventional state sales tax may apply to the entire sale price when the sale has a legitimate local situs, interstate and intrastate sales are treated equally, the structure is internally and externally consistent, and the tax relates to state-provided services.

#### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Tenth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The bankruptcy court shall allow the tax claim to the extent otherwise valid and conduct remaining proceedings under the ordinary tax and bankruptcy rules.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Complete Auto to the taxable event Oklahoma selected: the in-state sale of transportation. The sale has substantial nexus, is fairly related to state services, is nondiscriminatory, and is internally consistent because every State applying the same rule would tax each sale once. Apportionment is not required merely because the service later crosses state lines.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nondiscriminatory sales tax on the full price of an interstate bus ticket sold in the taxing State satisfies the Commerce Clause when the sale is a discrete local transaction and the tax is internally consistent and fairly related to state services.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The tax attaches to the completed sale, not to mileage or gross receipts earned in several States. Oklahoma treats local and interstate tickets alike, and the purchaser receives the protections and market infrastructure associated with the in-state transaction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize multiple taxation of interstate receipts, origin-based discrimination, or taxes failing external or internal consistency.

## 46. Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado +

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Tenth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Colorado may maintain neutral access rules, fees, privacy redactions, and use restrictions supported under the governing speech standard. It may not deny the covered records solely because the requester intends protected commercial solicitation under the categorical rule before the Court.

#### Issue 1: Constitutional right of access

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the issue and rejects the broad constitutional entitlement asserted.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The First Amendment does not create a general right to obtain executive criminal-justice records. The scope of ordinary access begins with enacted state law and the specific historical access doctrines.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** There is no general First Amendment right to inspect all state criminal-justice records merely because the information may support protected speech.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Constitution protects speaking with lawfully obtained information, but that does not obligate government to create or disclose every record. The judicial-proceeding cases rest on a distinct history and structural function not established for the records at issue.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide access to a court filing or proceeding covered by the separate experience-and-logic doctrine.

#### Issue 2: Use-based restriction on commercial speech

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches this distinct claim because Colorado has created an access regime and then denies records by reference to the requester’s intended protected speech.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Colorado may protect privacy, prevent harassment, charge retrieval costs, and redact sensitive data through neutral rules. It has not shown that a categorical denial to every requester intending direct commercial solicitation materially advances those interests with the fit Central Hudson requires. The rule distinguishes identical requests solely by the message and audience the requester plans to pursue.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a State makes criminal-justice records otherwise available, it may not categorically withhold them solely because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation unless the use-based restriction satisfies the governing commercial-speech standard.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The State’s interests are substantial, but the record does not show why neutral redaction, delayed release, opt-out protections, regulation of deceptive or coercive solicitation, or other direct measures are inadequate. A wholesale denial based on intended speech burdens more protected communication than the evidence supports.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require disclosure of confidential records, prevent neutral limits on access, or protect false, deceptive, coercive, or harassing solicitation.

## 48. McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Position and scope

**Approval Status:** USER-SUPPLIED POSITION. The supplied disposition, reasoning and express alternatives are recorded as preparation instructions. No Court judgment, vote, rationale or coalition is created by this supplement.

**Build:** First Amendment and expression.

#### Case-specific reasoning

Broad identification mandates require a justification fitted to the regulated speech. The state's interests do not justify this application to an individual's political leaflet. Preserve separate analysis of candidate finance disclosure, fraud, and organizations whose spending creates distinct informational concerns.

#### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Supreme Court of Ohio.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The enforcement order and fine against McIntyre shall be set aside. Ohio may enforce valid disclosure rules not inconsistent with the holding.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone treats anonymous political advocacy as core speech. The identity ban is content based because it applies to election advocacy and burdens the author’s choice to speak without attribution. Ohio’s interests in fraud prevention and campaign information do not justify a categorical prohibition reaching truthful, independently produced leaflets.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not require every person distributing campaign literature to identify herself on the face of the communication when the rule broadly prohibits anonymous core political advocacy and is not narrowly fitted to fraud or corruption.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Anonymous pamphleteering has a deep constitutional history and can protect speakers from retaliation while preserving public debate. Existing laws against fraud, false attribution, and undisclosed campaign organizations are materially narrower.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate source disclosure for candidates, committees, coordinated spending, large financial contributions, or fraudulent impersonation under appropriately tailored rules.
