## 26. United States v. National Treasury Employees Union — No. 93-1170

**Argument:** November 8, 1994. **Event cutoff:** February 22, 1995, common pre-day law. **Judgment under review:** 990 F.2d 1271, affirming an injunction covering all Executive Branch employees. The certified class covers affected employees below GS–16; named plaintiff Peter G. Crane is GS–16 and outside that class. Not every plaintiff's expressive activity is unrelated to official work.

### Statutory and record premises common to the commitments

Section 501(b) provides: “An individual may not receive any honorarium while that individual is a Member, officer or employee.” Section 505(2) covers government officers and employees **except special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202**. Section 501(a)'s separate outside-income ceiling for specified high-level noncareer officers is not decided.

Section 505(3) defines an honorarium as a payment of money or anything of value for an appearance, speech, or article, including a series **if the subject matter is directly related to official duties or payment is because of government status**. That parenthetical nexus qualification expressly concerns a series; it is not already a general nexus qualification governing every single appearance, speech, or article. The definition excludes actual and necessary travel expenses of the individual and one relative to the extent paid or reimbursed by another person and otherwise reduces the amount by those expenses to the extent not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses are transportation, lodging, and meals while away from residence or principal place of employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment instead made on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received by the individual, **only up to $2,000 for each payment**, and forbids this route to an organization from which the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child, or dependent relative derives financial benefit. It therefore preserves a charitable outlet, not compensation for the employee's expressive labor. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and permits a penalty no greater than $10,000 or the compensation received, whichever is greater. Under §504(b), a §503 entity may render a written advisory opinion. Protection from the subsection (a) sanction covers the recipient and another covered individual whose facts are indistinguishable in all material aspects **for action after issuance, in good faith, in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings**. Neither advisory availability nor charitable payment is a discretionary wholesale exemption from the substantive rule.

The lower record describes protected expression on subjects including religion, history, art, dance, and the environment. Many payments lack a duties, status, or payor-dealings connection; some activities are closer to official work. The GAO and commission materials support the Government's genuine corruption and appearance concerns. A conclusion that they do not justify this ban's reach is a judgment about constitutional fit, not a finding that corruption never occurs. Conversely, preventive authority is not proof that every unrelated payment causes disruption. Reimbursement does not replace payment for expressive effort, and the continued availability of unpaid speech does not establish zero speech burden.

All eight associates reach the admitted statutory/constitutional question and remedial scope. None adds a damages or civil-penalty adjudication, a congressional/judicial-employee ruling, or a constitutional ruling on §501(a). All decline to transplant the series parenthesis into every payment as an avoidance construction: the plausible-interpretation limit cannot be satisfied by writing a new general nexus rule into §505(3). This agreement on text is not agreement on validity or relief.

## 27. Harris v. Alabama — No. 93-7659

**Argument:** December 5, 1994. **Event cutoff:** February 22, 1995, common pre-day law. **Judgment under review:** Alabama Supreme Court affirmance, 632 So. 2d 543. ### Actual record and controlling law

The jury recommended life without parole seven to five on July 13, 1989. The judge imposed death after a further hearing and entered the amended order reproduced in the predecision appendix. That order states that the evidence, presentence material, further hearing, counsel's contentions, and jury recommendation were considered. It excludes victim-impact material; finds one aggravator, pecuniary gain; finds no significant criminal history as statutory mitigation and further family, work, church, and community mitigation; and treats Harris as a principal planner, financier, and beneficiary rather than a minor participant. The wording of its weighing passage prompted a statutory-mitigator objection, but the appellate court, considering the order and Rule 10(f) clarification, found that mitigator had been considered. The appellate court also reviewed arbitrary influence, independently weighed the circumstances, and considered proportionality and the roles/cooperation of other participants.

These are record facts and lower findings. They are not an agreement that the override was constitutionally adequate. The judge did not assign a number to the recommendation or separately label a comparative explanation for rejecting it. The record nevertheless contains actual findings and weighing; no commitment may call it wholly unexplained or find ignored mitigation contrary to the stated lower findings.

Section 13A-5-47(e) requires the judge to determine whether found aggravation outweighs found mitigation and, in doing so, **shall consider** the advisory recommendation **unless waived under §13A-5-46(a) or (g)**. The recommendation is not binding. This case has an actual unwaived recommendation. The complete text of other sentencing subsections is not a premise for an additional rule here; their reported operation and the actual review are sufficient for the selected question.

Spaziano permits a judicial final sentencer notwithstanding contrary advice; Florida's Tedder protection is an important fact of that case but was not declared a universally compulsory federal formula. Actual simulated Tuilaepa/Proctor rejects required numerical weighting while preserving relevant individualized consideration, effective mitigation, and separate selection errors. Actual Espinosa concerns an invalid aggravator transmitted through advice to which state law assigns **great weight**; neither that factor error nor that statutory great-weight command is present here. Actual Romano invalidated a substantial invitation to surrender sentencing responsibility to an irrelevant separate death decision; it did not establish a universal jury-sentencing requirement. No harmlessness shortcut is used: the affirming commitments find no federal error on this admitted issue.

## 28. Grubart and City of Chicago v. Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. — Nos. 93-762 and 93-1094

### Common factual and legal premises

The actual closure corroborates the apt description but is not made a necessary condition for jurisdiction in every case. Sisson reserved mixed instrumentalities. The present rule must therefore explain, rather than pretend already settled, why an alleged tortfeasor's substantial maritime activity can connect this casualty despite another alleged actor's nonmaritime conduct.

**Definition-based coverage:** the negotiated purchase agreement is a written communication offering or confirming the stock sale. ## 31. Arizona v. Evans, No. 93-1660 — March 1, 1995

### Threshold, record, and separable remedial propositions

All eight exercise review of the federal question. The Arizona Supreme Court finally disposed of the State's suppression appeal after dismissal without prejudice; the supplied record identifies no continuing lower proceeding that must determine this suppression issue before that state judgment can be reviewed. This is a conclusion about this admitted review posture, not a general §1257 exception for every interlocutory suppression ruling. The majority's stated exclusion analysis rests on federal cases and a distinction from Leon. Its discussion of §13-3925 rejects the State's statutory characterization; it does not expressly adopt an independently sufficient state-constitutional suppression rule. Neither the dissent's account of state law nor citation of state cases supplies such a majority ground. Long, as preserved in the actual Sochor and Middlebrooks holdings, permits review of this federal/interwoven ruling. No additional state-law exception is construed from text that has not been recovered.

The warrant had been quashed; its entry remained in the database. The trial judge did not find whether court personnel, police personnel, or both caused that failure. The intermediate court's inference is not a substitute finding. For this review the associates accept the lower court's unlawful-arrest premise and address exclusion. They do not create a rule that an officer's subjective intent always controls the legality of an otherwise authorized traffic arrest. Nor do they reopen the rejected alternative discovery theory or hold the nonexistent warrant valid.

The following propositions separate the possible grounds:

- **Remedial inquiry:** Leon makes objective reasonableness and the exclusionary rule's relevant deterrent function a separate question from the underlying violation. The arresting officer's personal good faith alone does not decide the institution's recordkeeping issue.
- **Court-employee extension:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would hold that exclusion is not justified solely by an established negligent recordkeeping error of court staff functioning outside law enforcement when the arresting officer's reliance is objectively reasonable and no causally contributing police recordkeeping failure is established. The absence of a finding that this is such a case requires vacatur and remand. This does not decide police error, combined error, knowingly unreliable systems, or errors so pervasive that reliance cannot be objectively reasonable.
- **Narrower institutional application:** Souter and Breyer support vacatur and remand to determine the source, relevant recordkeeping responsibility and objective reliability of reliance. They accept nonexclusion for an established isolated court error in a reasonably maintained system where the relevant law-enforcement actors neither caused nor controlled the defect and exclusion would supply no appreciable corrective incentive. They refuse to make court payroll or the word “clerical” alone decisive. No such complete predicate has yet been found.
- **Administrative-function distinction:** Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm suppression. The existing judgment concerns a quashed warrant carried into an arrest by a failure of the ministerial warrant-record system, not a magistrate's legal judgment that probable cause justified a warrant. Excluding the resulting evidence can give those maintaining and using the arrest-record chain a reason to ensure that quashing orders are transmitted and acted upon. Identity of the particular clerk does not remove that function on this record. This is not a decision overruling Leon's protection for its judicial probable-cause setting or resolving every government computer error.

### Enhanced alternatives and remedies

**Affirm on administrative function:** its strongest support is the difference between an erroneous judicial decision and the failure to carry out a correct order quashing a warrant. The state opinion found that distinction sufficient and did not need to choose between competing fault inferences. The strongest objection is that Leon's deterrence rationale concerns the actors susceptible to the evidentiary sanction; court staff may have no law-enforcement stake, while an objectively reasonable officer cannot prevent a defect outside police control. Stevens and Ginsburg treat the arrest-record chain as a connected administrative operation whose accuracy can be improved by the incentive; the six remanding associates reject categorical irrelevance of who maintained and controlled it.

**Apply a court-clerk good-faith rule and reverse suppression outright:** Leon supplies the legal analogy, and the intermediate court inferred court error. But no trial finding establishes that premise; the Arizona Supreme Court expressly rejected treating the conflicting proof as settled. All six who accept some extension therefore refuse outright admission of the evidence or an instruction requiring denial of suppression. Their result is vacatur of the state supreme judgment and remand for proceedings under the adopted remedial distinction. No conviction is directed.

**Remand on a narrower institutional inquiry:** this avoids imputing all ministerial errors to police or categorically insulating all court records. Its counterargument is that record expansion may postpone a legal issue already sufficiently presented and adds factual demands beyond a simple court/police distinction. Souter and Breyer nevertheless regard responsibility and actual objective reliability as necessary to deciding whether the Leon analogy fits. The other four remanding associates can support the narrower nonexclusion proposition as a sufficient application without treating its additional facts as necessary in every case. Their broader employee-category proposition remains separately identified.

**Threshold dismissal for an independent state ground:** that route would be required if the majority actually supplied such a sufficient basis, as Middlebrooks did. It has not. Inferring independence from the mere presence of §13-3925 would evade the state opinion's federal distinction and transform a rejected statutory argument into an affirmative separate holding. All eight reject that inference. A later authoritative clarification would be new neutral material requiring proper review, not an assumed fact here.

## 32. Swint v. Chambers County Commission, No. 93-1636 — March 1, 1995

### Components and common disposition

All eight reach the appellate-power question and would vacate the Eleventh Circuit's county portion of the judgment, remanding with directions to dismiss the county's interlocutory appeal for want of appellate jurisdiction. That restores ordinary district proceedings on the county's surviving federal claims without deciding their merits. The individual qualified-immunity appeal dispositions are not disturbed by this county-jurisdiction decision. No appellate jurisdiction is created over the separately declined City or state-law appeals.

- **Collateral order:** the county's denial of summary judgment is not independently appealable under §1291. Its final-policymaker defense to liability is reviewable after final judgment; it is not the individual officers' immunity from suit. The cumulative Cohen requirements are not satisfied merely because a legal ruling could eliminate the county from trial. Digital Equipment's adequate-vindication alternative directly supports that conclusion without needing to decide every other collateral-order element.
- **Statutory routes:** no §1292(b) district certification and timely permitted appeal are established. That section requires the written controlling-question/substantial-disagreement/material-advancement determinations, application within ten days and appellate permission; application alone does not stay proceedings. Sections 1292(e) and 2072(c) supply rulemaking channels for additional categories, not an operative rule authorizing this particular appeal. They reinforce the need for lawful authority; their existence alone does not conclusively abolish every possible form of ancillary review.
- **Pendent power asserted here:** a proper individual-immunity appeal does not allow the court of appeals to decide a separate party's nonappealable liability defense merely for efficiency. The lower court's actual rationale was that county resolution would end its case. That is not necessity to resolve the officials' appeal.
- **Narrow reservation:** no decision whether some issue genuinely inseparable from, or necessary to meaningful resolution of, an independently appealable issue can be examined with it. Here the officers' alleged conduct, constitutional violation and clearly established law can be evaluated without deciding whether Alabama assigns final county policymaking authority to the sheriff. Shared facts do not make the county attribution issue indispensable. No general pendent-review category is affirmatively created by this reservation.
- **Remedy:** vacatur is limited to the county's §1983 and §1985(3) appellate disposition. The county is neither held liable nor granted summary judgment by this Court. There was no remaining county state-law count. The appellate merits discussion about Alabama authority supplies no Supreme Court holding or substitute district judgment.

### Enhanced two-path test

**Retain pendent review for efficiency:** the lower court's prior circuit authorities and the prospect of ending all litigation against the county offer the strongest argument. The official-immunity appeals properly place related events before the appellate court, and final-policymaker attribution may be a legal question. But those features do not make a separate liability defense effectively unreviewable after judgment or satisfy §1292(b). If eliminating a party were enough, ordinary summary-judgment denials would become appealable whenever an immunity question appears in the same order. All eight reject that rationale.

**Exclude all conceivable pendent review:** §1291, Cohen's cumulative conditions and Congress's express interlocutory/rulemaking channels strongly favor restraint. The strongest objection to a universal prohibition is that deciding a properly appealed issue may sometimes require addressing a connected question; the existence and limits of that necessity do not follow merely from the convenience rule used here. All eight therefore choose the narrower path: this county issue is independently resolvable and not required to decide the immunity issues, so the actual jurisdiction asserted fails. They reserve genuine necessity without recognizing it as a new jurisdictional category.

### Separable components and supported paths

**A0 — reach:** Reach those admitted constitutional questions in review of the dismissal after a bench trial and denial of injunctive relief. No new grant, waiver, valuation, compensation demand, or modern takings-ripeness rule is invented. The report of the trial's inverse-condemnation disposition does not establish the preservation or merits of every possible parcel-specific theory.

**A1 — speech classification and justification.** Path **A1-S** sustains the reported application under Renton's specific secondary-effects framework. The enacted locational scheme, cited studies, development history, and available relocation distinguish suppression of expression from regulation of its associated land-use effects; the attorney's abandoned early objective and service of enforcement notices do not establish that the enacted ordinance is censorship. The strongest contrary argument is the trial court's express adverse purpose statement and the appellate tendency to privilege declared purpose. The sustaining ground must answer that evidence and cannot say that any locational law or any benign preamble is necessarily neutral.

Path **A1-R** vacates the First Amendment disposition for renewed classification/justification under the correct legal standard. The trial court's contrary purpose assessment and the lower courts' use of the fact that businesses may relocate require a legally adequate examination of whether the law actually regulates secondary effects. Turner preserves examination of operation and justification, and an asserted benign purpose alone does not cure content selection. The strongest objection is that Renton already permits precisely this form of adult-use zoning and the appellate court addressed the actual evidence. A1-R therefore orders neither strict scrutiny automatically nor invalidation; it requires reasoned treatment of the contrary purpose assessment without treating it as conclusive merely because it was made after trial. It does not require a local-effects study or least-restrictive means.

**A2 — alternative avenues.** On the source-supported challenge, the 97 available-site finding, the precise 6.75-percent denominator, and United Arcade's obtained conditional-use permit support adequate alternative avenues. Unaffordable preferred premises or relocation expense alone do not show a First Amendment denial. The competing concern that nominal sites may be unusable is legally valid, but no parcel-specific disqualification of these 97 sites is established here. This component preserves the lower finding and rejects the asserted cost-only objection; it does not declare every parcel practically available under every unexamined constraint. A Justice supporting A1-R can agree with A2 without upholding the whole speech disposition.

**A3 — existing-business due process and transition.** Reject the categorical proposition that a locational ordinance can apply only prospectively or that established businesses have an absolute right to remain at their current sites. The reported substantial land-use relation, three-year transition, and possible reasonable extension for undue financial hardship support rejection of that claim. The extension is permissive upon the statutory showing, not automatic, and no application or denial is found. The contrary concern about a confiscatory or arbitrarily administered transition remains a different, record-dependent claim; three years is not a universal constitutional safe harbor. A3 does not substitute rational-basis review for the separate First Amendment inquiry.

**A4 — admitted takings/property question.** Reject a categorical compensation entitlement based solely on the requirement that an existing adult business relocate or cease that particular use at its present location. Neither the duration of the prior use nor a valid police-power label conclusively resolves compensation. Under actual **Lucas**, June 29, 1992, complete loss of the relevant property's economically beneficial use must be established, then the State must substantiate a background property/nuisance limit; no such complete loss follows from use relocation alone. Under **Concrete Pipe**, June 14, 1993, and Penn Central, partial deprivation requires consideration of economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations, and governmental character together, without a numerical or amortization-period safe harbor. No dedication is demanded, so Dolan does not supply the test. The strongest opposing path seeks a remand because the trial court's reported recoupment-period rationale is insufficient for a developed compensation claim. That concern cannot be rejected by declaring three years categorically adequate; it is preserved if such an independent, properly presented claim is actually established.
