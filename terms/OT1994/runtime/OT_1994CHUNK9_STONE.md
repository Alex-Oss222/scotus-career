#### C. Issue positions

##### Question 1: Predominance and equal political membership

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the admitted legal question once jurisdiction is established. Accept the reconstructed finding that race predominated and ordinary criteria were subordinated, as addressed in Question 5. Stone does not avoid the question by imagining a predominantly nonracial legislative compromise. His answer applies simulated Shaw and explains the equality principle that makes its distinction defensible.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Equal protection secures persons' equal civic standing and participation in political institutions. Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency does not, by itself, deny those interests, even when traditional districting considerations are subordinated. Governmental exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, and other independently cognizable deprivations remain subject to their governing constitutional rules. This enumeration is illustrative, not exhaustive.

**Essential Reasoning:** Equal membership identifies what government owes each person. Antisubordination explains why the history and continuing operation of exclusion matter to that obligation: identical formal access can coexist with structures that impair participation, while attention to racial conditions can help remove barriers. Individual rights constrain corrective methods, and lawful authority constrains which institutions may act and what remedies they may impose. These are complementary parts of Stone's reasoning, not interchangeable tests or a license to select whichever rationale favors the policy under review.

Districting necessarily assembles residents into constituencies. Recognizing demonstrated racial voting conditions does not necessarily assign every resident a racial political identity, make voters of one race interchangeable, or create a representative legally accountable only to that race. The constituency includes all residents. This is why demographic composition alone does not establish unequal terms of membership. It is not because boundaries have no effects, because political institutions are exempt from personal equality, or because the government has described its purpose favorably.

Formal access is therefore a starting fact rather than a complete defense. A boundary can implement expulsion, unlawful dilution, or inferior civic treatment. Courts must examine the actual operation relevant to the asserted legal interest. Equal protection protects persons of every race; aggregate white political strength or a group's past dominance cannot answer an individual's present deprivation. The term civic membership expresses equal standing and does not limit the Equal Protection Clause to citizens.

**Boundary:** The present holding concerns territorial districting. It creates neither a universal constitutional action for unequal social outcomes nor a general replacement scrutiny tier for every governmental use of race. Antisubordination informs the common equality inquiry; it does not establish a separate immunity for measures called benign.

**Operative consequence:** Predominance alone supplies neither liability nor a new burden on the State to justify an independently unproved constitutional wrong. Once a distinct deprivation is alleged and proved as its governing doctrine requires, the court applies that doctrine's burden and justification rules. A map is not immune because it assigns residents territorially; Gomillion shows that a boundary itself can implement racial exclusion. This is an account of what the governmental action accomplishes, not a declaration that geographic assignment can never constitute racial treatment.

##### Question 2: Sorting, stereotyping, representation, and independent deprivation

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does the racial design established here itself establish unconstitutional segregation, an official racial identity, or a legally inferior relationship to the elected representative? Stone answers no on these allegations and explains why each asserted inference fails. He does not simply rename the claim design-only and decline to confront it.

**Separation and stereotyping:** The strongest argument is that government itself assigns political identity when it deliberately sorts voters by race, so the assignment is already the unequal treatment. Stone accepts that imposed racial separation can be an injury without a second loss. He rejects the inference that every predominantly racial arrangement of a shared electorate necessarily has that character. The challenged demographic design does not itself make anyone ineligible to vote, associate, seek office, form a cross-racial coalition, or belong to the representative's constituency because of individual race. These features distinguish the asserted design from racial restrictions on institutional membership or participation. They do not insulate a geographically implemented restriction that actually imposes such inequality.

Recognition of observed voting patterns is different from prescribing a person's political beliefs or civic worth. Gingles permits relevant evidence of political behavior within its statutory inquiry; it does not authorize assuming that every person of a race thinks alike. Purpose evidence and official statements can expose an independently cognizable discriminatory practice. The State cannot rely on racial stereotypes as a justification for an actual burden. But the constitutional character of the governmental treatment must be explained, not conclusively inferred from the weight given to race in selecting boundaries.

**Representation:** A majority-minority district does not make its representative the legal representative of only minority residents. The prediction that the representative will neglect other residents does not follow necessarily from the racial composition or design of the district. A racially discriminatory denial of an otherwise available participation or constituent-service opportunity would require examination under the law governing that denial. No such practice is established by this reconstructed record. Conversely, the opinion creates no general right to command a representative's attention, policy agreement, or favorable vote.

**Status and expression:** Noneconomic harm can be a constitutional deprivation. Anderson's state-imposed racial ballot cues and McLaurin's restrictions within an institution refute any requirement that a plaintiff prove financial loss or a different election result. A status claim need not await an official's later denial of service if the challenged governmental act itself establishes the prohibited unequal status. What fails here is the assertion that predominant demographic design necessarily announces that residents have racially inferior standing. General disagreement with that design's perceived racial message, unsupported by an independently cognizable form of governmental inequality, does not establish the asserted violation.

**Application and limit:** On the stated premise, the historical separation, stereotyping, and representational arguments do not supply a distinct basis for invalidation. This is a substantive legal answer under the reconstructed record, not a finding that no conceivable injury existed in historical Georgia and not a dismissal of expressive harms as categorically unreal. An allegation's substance controls; a court must not reject facts establishing a recognized deprivation merely because the pleader also invokes racial predominance.

**Application to exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, and civic status**

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** An independent deprivation means one legally identifiable apart from predominance alone. It requires neither a second governmental act nor an additional downstream injury after unequal treatment is established. The challenged boundaries themselves can accomplish the wrong. Each claim retains its own elements, burdens, and remedy.

Gomillion's municipal disenfranchisement supplies the concrete boundary example. | Predominant composition of a common congressional district does not alone establish expulsion or racial ineligibility. No such additional operative fact is supplied here. |

**Reasoning and history:** The Reconstruction Amendments and voting-rights enforcement responded to racial exclusion from political membership, including devices that preserved formal rights while frustrating their exercise. That history explains why meaningful participation and lawful correction matter. It does not prove this particular map effective, erase the rights of white residents, or turn equal membership into an entitlement to proportional seats. Recognizing four protected interests does not establish that four separate claims were pleaded, preserved, and tried in the historical Miller litigation. This brief answers their legal relationship to the reconstructed case without manufacturing those proceedings.

**Administration:** Apply ordinary pleading and proof at the actual stage. No universal expert report, replacement map, statistical threshold, electoral defeat, or economic loss is required. Shape, departures from ordinary criteria, voting evidence, legislative statements, and practical operation remain available for the legal work assigned by the particular claim. Racial motive is not irrelevant merely because predominance alone is insufficient.

**Administration of status claims:** Identify the governmental act said to impose the status and explain the relevant legal operation or official racial designation. Official statements, rules, patterns of treatment, and institutional practice may bear on that inquiry; none is a universal evidentiary prerequisite. The court cannot demand a second loss where the governmental act itself imposes a recognized deprivation, but must explain why the asserted status injury is established rather than infer it conclusively from racial predominance. The equal-membership account is therefore broader than formal eligibility and more specific than an unbounded inquiry into offense at a map's message.

##### Question 3: Voluntary inclusion, statutory duties, and lawful authority

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may take account of race in territorial districting to improve minority electoral opportunity even when no adjudicated prior violation or federal statutory command requires the precise district selected. Absence of such compulsion does not itself make the State's choice unconstitutional. The permission remains subject to every independently applicable constitutional and statutory limit and supplies neither a duty to maximize majority-minority districts nor an entitlement to proportional representation.

**Reasoning and Application:** The Constitution's protection of equal membership permits lawful efforts to make participation meaningful. Government need not wait until a particular response becomes judicially mandatory before considering obstacles to participation. The contrary inference would turn a statute's minimum obligations into a ceiling on constitutional permission. This conclusion rejects the asserted legal prohibition; it makes no finding that Georgia's policy actually achieved inclusion or that its historical predominant purpose was remedial.

**Historical preclearance question answered:** Compliance with a DOJ demand is not equivalent to compliance with the statute, and neither automatically establishes constitutional validity. Beer does not convert §5 into a requirement to create every possible majority-minority district. A federal administrator must act within delegated statutory authority, and independent judicial review of a properly presented constitutional claim cannot be surrendered to an administrator's approval. Conversely, the fact that DOJ could not lawfully require a particular district would not by itself establish that the State could not choose it.

**What this answers and what it does not:** Stone rejects the legal equivalence between constitutional permission, statutory duty, and an administrator's demand. He does not adjudicate the lawfulness of a particular historical objection letter on an unadmitted record or bypass Morris and the designated review channel. Nonretrogression does not eliminate the distinct statutory purpose inquiry. This opinion does not decide every relationship between §§2 and 5, the full reach of §5's purpose provision, or later disputes concerning federal enforcement authority. Those questions require their own statutory grounds and records. This reservation does not reopen the proposition actually answered here: lack of federal compulsion alone is no constitutional prohibition.

**Burden and administrative consequence:** A design-only challenge does not oblige every legislature to commission a study, establish that its map maximizes representation, exhaust every alternative, or prove a prior constitutional violation before considering racial voting conditions. An established racial burden requiring justification is different: its governing doctrine supplies the government's obligation. A statutory claim retains its own elements. Participation, transparent reasons, and revisable choices may improve decisionmaking, but their absence is not a new freestanding constitutional violation under Miller. The statute may require, authorize, or leave room for a measure; those are different propositions.

##### Question 4: District shape and admissible proof

**Stone's answer and proposed rule:** Bizarre shape is not a necessary threshold for the asserted constitutional challenge, and neither unusual shape nor a compactness score independently establishes the violation. Direct and circumstantial evidence may prove the facts made material by the governing claim. Shape, demographics, departures from customary considerations, legislative statements, line-drawing instructions, and the map's operation remain available for that work.

**Reasoning and application:** An ordinary-looking boundary can implement actionable exclusion or dilution, while an unusual district can arise from lawful geography, population distribution, political subdivisions, or attention to voting conditions. A geometric measurement identifies a geometric property; the court must explain its relationship to purpose or the particular deprivation. Here the lower court did not have to establish a threshold degree of bizarreness before examining the broader evidence of predominance. Accepting that evidentiary proposition does not reinstate the historical predominance-to-strict-scrutiny rule.

**Lower-court direction and limits:** State what each material item helps prove and consider supported competing explanations. There is no universal compactness cutoff, commissioned study, expert report, or replacement-map requirement created by this opinion. Where an independent statute or constitutional claim requires a comparator or particular proof, that requirement remains. Motive evidence cannot be discarded merely because predominance alone is insufficient, and it cannot stand in for every element of a different claim.

##### Question 6: Strict scrutiny and the conditional statutory-compliance justification

**Primary answer and governing consequence:** No. Under simulated Shaw, even conclusive proof of racial predominance and subordination of traditional criteria does not by itself trigger strict scrutiny in this territorial-design challenge. Stone affirms because the asserted constitutional predicate fails and the related status and representation arguments do not establish a distinct deprivation on this record. He does not affirm by applying rational-basis review to a conceded racial burden, finding strict scrutiny satisfied, or treating inclusion as immunity.

**Approved conditional answer:** Assume, solely to answer the opposing approach, that predominance triggers strict scrutiny. On the approved reconstructed statutory-compliance record, Georgia's asserted §5-compulsion justification does not sustain the challenged district. Even assuming compliance with a valid statutory duty can furnish a compelling interest, the State must connect the challenged district to that duty. The admitted lower-court conclusion is that the particular district was not reasonably necessary to satisfy the substantive §5 obligation and that reliance on DOJ's demands did not establish that connection. Stone accepts that limited conclusion for this conditional inquiry. An administrative demand, without the necessary statutory and evidentiary support, does not itself establish a compelling justification or the required fit between the district and the statutory duty.

**Why this is not a prohibition on voluntary inclusion:** The absence of an exact statutory command, standing alone, does not prove a lack of reasonable justification and does not invalidate every discretionary means of compliance. Nor does defeat of the asserted compulsion defense dispose of an independently supported corrective or prospective objective. The conditional conclusion rests on the admitted insufficiency of this compliance justification, not a universal rule that only federally required maps are constitutional. Georgia's separate defense that the Constitution leaves room for otherwise lawful voluntary inclusion succeeds against the plaintiffs' asserted prohibition under Question 3; it does not invent a different, factually established compelling interest under the hypothetical strict-scrutiny inquiry.

**Section 5's distinct requirements:** Beer supplies nonretrogression, not an automatic requirement to maximize majority-minority districts. Pleasant Grove prevents the analysis from treating absence of retrogression as conclusive compliance with the separate discriminatory-purpose requirement. Nothing here presumes that the creation of fewer majority-minority districts proves discriminatory purpose, or that an ameliorative plan necessarily resolves every purpose issue. Do not import the answers to later Bossier disputes or merge §2's results test with §5's requirements.

**Institutional authority and remedy:** Review of the State's constitutional justification is not a decree setting aside DOJ's administrative action. Morris and the designated review channel remain in force. No separate statutory count or claim against federal officials is decided. If an independently cognizable racial burden were established in a different case, the law governing that burden would determine justification; the historical or proposed Adarand rule does not enter this case silently.

#### D. Common reasoning, administration, and fit across settings

**Stone's Position:** Equal membership supplies a common constitutional purpose; the legal treatment, protected interest, institutional authority, and remedy determine how it applies. History helps identify subordination and the significance of corrective action. It cannot substitute for justification of an actual personal burden or authorize an institution to exceed its powers.

In contracting, a racial eligibility rule or incentive can affect a bidder's competitive opportunity directly. A lawful corrective objective does not erase that burden. Stone's separately selected Adarand position requires an important objective within lawful authority, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between means and objective, and justification of coverage and actual burdens, including concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. That remains his position, subject to the actual earlier adjudication; Miller does not adopt it by implication or create a benevolent-purpose exemption. His Stevens fallback in Adarand remains the separately authorized alternative for nonadoption of the replacement standard, not a means of excusing failure under an adopted rule.

An adjudicated school-segregation violation supplies a remedial predicate that voluntary districting does not require. Stone's Jenkins preference for effective correction, joint development with independent review, and a meaningful route to release addresses administration of that remedy. It creates no indefinite obligation to achieve ideal racial or educational outcomes. Equally, the absence of a decree does not itself prohibit every voluntary integration policy. An actual restriction on an individual's access must be justified under the law governing that restriction. A legitimate inclusion objective and an unlawful particular means can coexist.

**Lower-court direction:** Identify the challenged action and the source of the asserted right; distinguish jurisdiction from merits; determine the legally material facts and apply the governing burdens; assess any required justification; and confine relief to the established violation and lawful authority. These are directions for reasoned adjudication, not a mandatory factor count or a new branded test. Participation, transparent reasons, independent review, and revisable measures may improve institutional decisions. Miller does not make them universal constitutional prerequisites, automatic defenses, or substitutes for rights.

**Future-case boundary:** These propositions constrain later reasoning if adopted, but do not decide later outcomes. Standing, statutory causes of action, congressional enforcement power, agency authority, immunity, and remedial release each retain their governing sources. No future Vera, Boerne, Bossier, school-transfer, contracting, or crossover-district result is imported into the holding. Nor may a later court evade a proposition actually settled here merely by calling it reserved. The distinction is between applying this rule to a new claim and pretending that its already answered legal question remains open.

**Standing consistency:** Warth's separation of injury from success on the merits applies in both Miller and the planned Vera. A merits defeat does not automatically eliminate Article III injury, and residence does not automatically establish it. #### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the judgment of the Eleventh Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Georgia shall provide Chabad access on the same neutral terms governing comparable private expression. The State may use a neutral identification or disclaimer rule, or may prospectively close or redefine the forum through viewpoint-neutral rules, but may not exclude this display because its viewpoint is religious.

#### Issue 1: Viewpoint discrimination in the Capitol Rotunda forum

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the issue and applies Pinette as controlling same-term law.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Georgia opened the Rotunda to private unattended displays and excluded Chabad’s otherwise eligible expression because it conveyed a religious message. That is viewpoint discrimination. The State may close the forum or impose neutral operational rules, but it may not preserve the forum for secular perspectives on subjects for which religious perspectives are excluded.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State that opens a government-building forum to private displays on neutral terms violates the Free Speech Clause when it excludes an otherwise eligible display solely because the private speaker’s viewpoint is religious.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The display is privately owned, privately financed, and privately sponsored, and the access policy makes room for private expression rather than selecting or paying for a government message. Equal access does not prefer Chabad; it removes a disability imposed because of religion. Administrative concerns associated with size, duration, safety, identification, or unattended displays may be addressed by rules applying equally to comparable speakers.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not require Georgia to maintain the forum, authorize a display that violates neutral physical or scheduling limits, or give Chabad preferential placement, duration, or governmental assistance.

#### Issue 2: Establishment Clause attribution

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the State’s asserted constitutional defense because it is the sole justification for the viewpoint exclusion.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Under Pinette, the location inside the Capitol is relevant but not dispositive. The Rotunda’s established use for private displays, neutral access rules, private ownership, and an identifying sign or disclaimer make clear that the menorah is Chabad’s expression. Permitting it therefore does not constitute governmental worship, sponsorship, or adoption of religious doctrine.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Neutral access for a clearly identified private religious display in a genuine public forum does not violate the Establishment Clause merely because the forum is located inside a state capitol.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A reasonable and informed observer would encounter the display in a setting used for private expression and could be told directly who sponsored and financed it. No public funds pay for the menorah or its religious message. The Constitution does not require government to discriminate against religion in order to avoid endorsement. It requires the State to keep its own speech, coercion, funding, and authority distinct from the private speaker’s message.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves a display selected, designed, financed, controlled, or presented by the State as its own, a forum whose asserted openness is pretextual, or circumstances in which government obscures private sponsorship and affirmatively adopts the religious message.
