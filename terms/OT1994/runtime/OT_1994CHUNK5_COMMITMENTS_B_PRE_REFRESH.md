## 54. United States v. Lopez — No. 93-1260

### Question, statutory scope and remedy common to the rows

The charged law is original 18 U.S.C. §922(q)(1)(A): knowing firearm possession at a place known, or reasonably believed, to be a school zone, including elementary/secondary school grounds and the area within 1,000 feet. The indictment contains no commerce allegation. The record reports an unloaded handgun, five bullets, a proposed paid delivery, and stipulated proof of out-of-state manufacture. Those facts are not additional statutory elements or a substitute grand-jury allegation. The lower court reversed and ordered dismissal, also explaining that if a nexus were necessary, trial proof could not cure its omission from the indictment.

The possession exemptions remain: private property outside school grounds; a state/local license issued under a law requiring prior law-enforcement verification of qualifications; an unloaded firearm in a locked container or locked vehicle rack; a school-approved program; a school contract covering the individual or employer; official law-enforcement possession; and authorized crossing of school premises with an unloaded gun to reach hunting land. The lower court found none applicable. Their preservation does not turn the law's remaining possession class into an economic class. The distinct discharge prohibition has only four listed exceptions: private property outside school grounds, an approved program, the school-contract situation, and official law enforcement. Its separate culpability and discharge/attempt elements are not adjudicated by these possession commitments. The statute preserves state/local school-zone law; that clause neither supplies commerce power nor commandeers the States.

Original §924(a)(4) permits up to five years, a fine not exceeding $5,000, or both; imprisonment cannot run concurrently with another provision's term; except for the authorized maximum, the offense is deemed a misdemeanor for other-law purposes. The actual six-month prison term does not resolve the reserved challenges to two years' supervised release and the special assessment. The 1994 general-fine wording is not applied backward. September 1994 findings and renumbering are not a new jurisdictional element, a new indictment, or an automatic cure of the original prosecution. Their admission as effective noncase law remains an operator prerequisite if assembly relies on the enactment itself.

### Supported paths and operative proposition boundaries

**Sustaining path.** Congress could rationally treat school gun possession as a class producing a substantial national commercial effect through school disruption, workforce preparation and interstate travel, and select a prohibition as a means of addressing that class. Wickard and Perez permit examination of a class rather than the isolated instance; Heart of Atlanta, McClung and Hodel, as mapped in the validated packet, do not impose a universal formal-findings prerequisite. This path does not infer an unexpressed jurisdictional element. Its limiting premise is a rationally substantial relation of the identified class and a reasonable means, not that any undesirable local act is federal commerce. The strongest objection is that an education/productivity and crime-cost chain can connect almost anything to commerce, erasing an enumerated-power boundary. The sustaining rows resolve that objection through deference to the described class-wide effects under received doctrine; they do not announce that mere interstate history of every object is always enough.

**Invalidating path.** Congress's broad authority over economic activity and an interstate commercial scheme does not sustain this stand-alone possession offense on the Government's chain from guns to crime, education, productivity and national costs. The original provision supplies neither a commercial-activity limitation nor a case-specific commerce condition. On this record, that connection is too attenuated to support the charged offense. This conclusion rejects a compulsory findings rule, leaves Wickard/Perez and actual Allied-Bruce intact in their commercial settings, and does not revive traditional-governmental-function immunity. The strongest objection is that it adds an economic/non-economic boundary to precedents employing rational class-wide effects and discounts Congress's ability to address a national problem. The invalidating rows answer that aggregation cannot itself supply the missing legal connection where the proposed chain would leave no meaningful distinction between federal commerce power and a general police power. They support this bounded application, not a categorical decision of every noncommercial federal offense.

**Construction and indictment alternative.** Bass permits a federalism-sensitive reading when the enacted words admit it; Gregory and actual New York likewise use a plausible construction. Neither authorizes adding a jurisdictional element merely because one would save a clear statute. Actual X-Citement construes the reach of an existing knowledge term and does not automatically authorize inserting a new commerce requirement. No row affirmatively adopts an added-element construction of §922(q)(1)(A). If reconciliation or lawful circulation later proposes one, the independent Stirone/Russell ground must be decided: proof that this gun crossed state lines does not by itself cure an indictment omitting the necessary nexus. An affirmance on that narrower construction would preserve dismissal, not reinstate the conviction. Conversely, under the sustaining rows' no-element construction there is no omitted required commerce allegation, so that alternative cannot independently preserve dismissal. This is a conditional consequence of the chosen rule, not an independently adopted indictment holding by every Justice.

### Supported paths, saving/deemer forks and remedial reach

**No sufficient relation on this record.** Under Shaw and Mackey, the breadth of connection/reference does not make every consequence for a plan preemptive. These payor/provider measures do not depend on a covered ERISA plan's existence or use its benefit content as the legal measure, unlike actual Greater Washington Board of Trade. They alter provider/payor prices and HMO participation incentives, including for customers outside ERISA, without prescribing covered plan benefits, claims administration or a particular insurer. The 13% and additional 11% can influence insurance choices; the HMO assessment can influence Medicaid participation. Intended economic influence alone does not show the kind of connection established in Shaw's benefit requirements, FMC's subrogation restriction or Board of Trade's mandatory plan-content benchmark. The same §514(a) analysis applies to the represented direct self-funded-payor application; its presence is acknowledged, not assumed away. This is not an immunity for every price law or every law with an indirect effect.

**Strongest preempting path.** The differentials are deliberately selective, and a sufficiently severe financial penalty could effectively require an ERISA plan to select or reject a coverage arrangement. The HMO participation conditions affect organization and enrollment more directly than an ordinary sales tax. Kennedy's Mackey dissent and the FMC majority require attention to real administrative burdens, not just whether the law names ERISA. The provisional rows reject preemption on this supplied record because no compulsory covered-plan term or claims-administration duty is identified, nor an established economic effect equivalent to such a command. They do not find as a fact that premiums are lower, that switching occurred, or that no cost could ever coerce plan design. No numerical safe harbor is created. A materially different record of effective compulsion or direct plan administration would need its own analysis.
