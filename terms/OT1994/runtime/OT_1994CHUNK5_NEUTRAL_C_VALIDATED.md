## 57. United States v. Robertson — No. 94-251

Merits submission; supplied argument February 27, 1995, event May 1, 1995. The inventory's public-form planning label supplies no vote, author or rationale. Authorized question: whether the Alaska mine was an enterprise engaged in interstate commerce under §1962(a). Keep direct engagement and activities affecting commerce distinct; resolving the first need not determine the full constitutional reach of the second.

### Lower judgment and remaining issues

The Ninth Circuit, 15 F.3d 862 (February 1, 1994), reversed **Count Six**, the RICO conviction, for insufficient enterprise-commerce proof. It affirmed the cocaine convictions, **Counts One–Four**, but **vacated their sentences** for noncompliance with then-Rule 32(c)(3)(D), directing actual resentencing after compliance. Counts Five and Eight ended in acquittals; the jury did not decide Count Seven (forfeiture), which was later dismissed. A commerce ruling cannot silently undo the independent drug-sentence vacatur or revive acquitted/dismissed counts.

The Government seeks relief from the RICO reversal. If that ground is rejected, six unresolved RICO appellate grounds remain: refusal of a special verdict; venue; severance of RICO and CCE counts; exclusion of Agent Devetko's testimony; denial of CJA funds to locate the claimed owner of the Cadillac used for predicate act six; and RICO vagueness (appendix p.11a n.3). A remand for those grounds differs from unconditional final reinstatement of conviction and sentence.

The Government's separate sentencing appeal relied on alleged RICO activity after November 1, 1987. The Ninth Circuit did not reach that straddle argument because it reversed RICO; the affirmed drug counts concerned pre-Guidelines conduct (pp.26a–27a). Reversal of the commerce ruling could reopen that unresolved RICO sentencing argument, not establish its merits. The separate drug resentencing requires an accuracy finding or express nonreliance determination for each disputed PSR fact and an appended written record; a retrospective assertion of nonreliance alone would not satisfy the lower mandate (pp.25a–26a).

The panel rejected spillover prejudice, the remaining evidentiary challenges or their claimed prejudicial effect, continuance and bill-of-particulars claims, and the wiretap challenge. It found admitting testimony concerning Marco Guarderas's disappearance erroneous but harmless; it did not find Robertson guilty of murder. Reinhardt joined the judgment and panel opinion and separately criticized overcharging and bad-act evidence. Those concerns are neither a separate ground invalidating the RICO commerce proof nor a reversal of the drug convictions (entire appendix pp.1a–36a read).

### Facts, legal text, and application limits

The lower opinion describes financing Alaska mining claims for $125,000, equipment for $100,000 and travel for seven workers, paid nearly entirely in cash; a subsequent $300,000 house-secured loan financed the 1986 season. The Government relied on out-of-state equipment, supply purchases, recruitment and travel, and the Cadillac's movement to Alaska. The panel treated these contacts as incidental and emphasized locally sold gold and about $200,000 production. These are the lower court's account and legal treatment; do not replace them with invented detailed purchase invoices or a finding about exactly how much gold crossed a state line. The mine was a distinct enterprise from the predicate drug activity (pp.4a–10a).

Section 1962(a) prohibits a person who received income derived directly or indirectly from a racketeering pattern or collection of an unlawful debt, in which that person participated as a principal within §2, from using or investing any part of that income or its proceeds, directly or indirectly, in acquiring an interest in, establishing, or operating an enterprise engaged in interstate or foreign commerce or whose activities affect it. Predicate commerce is not automatically enterprise commerce.

The subsection's securities qualification remains explicit: an open-market securities purchase for investment, without intent to control or participate in controlling the issuer or assist another in doing so, is not unlawful under this subsection if the purchaser's, immediate family's and racketeering/unlawful-debt accomplices' aggregate holdings after purchase are less than one percent of the outstanding securities of any one class and confer no power, in law or fact, to elect a director. This qualification is not factually implicated by ownership and operation of this mine. Sections 1962(b), (c) and (d) separately address acquisition/control through racketeering, conduct of enterprise affairs, and conspiracy; their distinct elements cannot replace §1962(a)'s investment and commerce requirements. Official 1994 Code §1962 text was checked; its 1988 amendment concerns only subsection (d)'s reference grammar.

Entering law: Scheidler (1994-01-24), 'No economic-motive element in §1962(c),' preserves commerce as an independent condition and does not decide §1962(a)'s income element. Reves (1993-03-03), 'Direction of enterprise affairs under §1962(c),' is not an additional management element in subsection (a). Holmes (1992-03-24), 'Direct relation between a civil RICO violation and injury,' concerns civil damages, not this prosecution's commerce element. Allied-Bruce (1995-01-18) construes the FAA's 'involving commerce' through actual multistate operation; it neither makes remote interstate history conclusive nor defines Congress's outer constitutional power. These exact operative rules and their qualifications are in the entering-law slice.

Surviving American Building Maintenance, 422 U.S. 271, 283–286 (1975), distinguishes direct participation in interstate goods/services transactions from local purchases after the flow has ended. Its statute and 'engaged in commerce' limitation must be compared, not merged with §1962(a)'s alternative affecting-commerce language. The entire opinion, separate writings and footnotes were read.

### Supported paths and Justice-specific materials

**Direct-commerce path:** The Government can contend that the mine itself obtained goods and labor across state lines; a small local output market does not erase actual interstate procurement. The strongest objection is that predicate movements, owner's private travel and merely local purchases of once-interstate goods cannot substitute for enterprise activity. Resolve that objection on the actual evidence, not a presumption that all Alaska businesses are interstate. A favorable Government ruling can set aside the commerce ground and remand remaining RICO grounds while leaving the independently vacated drug sentences intact.

**Insufficiency path:** Robertson can contend that the enterprise proof established only local operation plus incidental contacts, applying the panel's account and distinguishing direct market participation under American Building Maintenance. The strongest objection is that direct out-of-state procurement and hiring are enterprise commerce even though output is local. A decision adopting insufficiency must address those contacts separately and identify the applicable statutory standard; the word 'incidental' cannot itself do the legal work. An alternative affecting-commerce rationale is available only within authorized scope, evidence and actual refreshed law; no predicted Lopez/Harris rule is supplied.

For individual assessment: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined Allied-Bruce's actual-commerce rationale; Breyer authored it. Scalia and Thomas did not reach its commerce alternatives, so their dissents supply no contrary commerce holding. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Souter and Ginsburg joined Scheidler's preservation of enacted RICO conditions. Reves supplies a further distinction: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopted its statutory direction requirement and application; Souter rejected the requirement. These are issue-specific public materials, not Robertson votes. Each must be reconsidered with any actually effective Lopez/Harris law.

## 58. United States v. Pinson — assigned Supreme Court No. 94-164

### Event, facts and lower judgment

Established merits review; supplied simulated argument March 20, 1995 and event May 8, 1995. The question is whether outside thermal imaging directed at heat patterns associated with activity inside a home is a Fourth Amendment search requiring a warrant. The Eighth Circuit judgment is 24 F.3d 1056, May 23, 1994; its docket is 93-2851, distinct from the assigned Supreme Court docket. It affirmed conviction and sentence for manufacturing over 100 marijuana plants. Its separately affirmed sentencing issue does not enlarge the supplied search question.

The investigation began after officers learned in June 1991 of three packages sent to Pinson in February 1989 by known indoor hydroponic-equipment suppliers. Subpoenaed electrical records showed unusually high use compared with nearby homes. On July 25, 1991 at approximately **1:20 a.m.**, police used a **FLIR mounted under a helicopter**. The affidavit reported excess heat from a covered third-floor window, roof and skylight. The subsequent **July 30 warrant search** found the indoor growing operation in the attic/third floor and seized marijuana, cash, equipment and cultivation publications. The scan itself was warrantless.

At suppression, Officer Patterson said the device compares radiated **surface** heat; it does not measure absolute temperatures or see through walls. High-output grow lights create heat that must be vented to maintain growing temperatures. The lower court accepted a heat-discard theory and found no intimate details or physical intrusion. Those are findings and reasoning to evaluate, not premises establishing the constitutional conclusion. The opinion supplies **no altitude, flight-frequency finding, FLIR public-availability finding, thermal image itself, or detailed noise/wind disturbance evidence**. Do not import these from aerial cases or future thermal-imaging litigation.

### Preservation and remedy must remain separate

The search challenge is preserved. **Footnote 2 expressly states that Pinson did not challenge the affidavit's sufficiency on appeal and the court made no determination of it.** This does not establish that thermal imaging was unnecessary to probable cause; nor does it establish that the existing constitutional challenge is waived. An independent demand to excise the thermal statements and redetermine probable cause cannot be treated as preserved without additional source support. Any remand must identify what lawful preserved suppression issue remains, and any appellate forfeiture rule actually being invoked. Olano distinguishes intentional waiver from failure to assert, requires plain error and ordinarily outcome prejudice for Rule 52(b), and makes correction discretionary only after the fairness/integrity/public-reputation inquiry; it does not automatically authorize a fresh factual suppression challenge.

Part II separately rejected a lesser-harms departure based on Pinson's claimed asthma treatment. The lower court held that the five-year statutory minimum under §841(b)(1)(B)(vii) could not be reduced without a Government §3553(e) substantial-assistance motion; none was made. Section 5K2.11 permits a reduced Guidelines sentence where avoiding a perceived greater harm significantly diminishes society's punishment interest, but says reduction is unwarranted if punishment/deterrence interests are not reduced. That discretionary policy was not an independent authority to go below the statutory minimum. No new Supreme Court sentencing issue or medical finding is supplied.

The affidavit itself, suppression transcript and thermal image have not been independently recovered. The complete appellate account supports adjudicating the legal search issue and a bounded procedural disposition. A final finding that untainted evidence suffices, that reliance was reasonable, or that a particular suppression issue was preserved beyond what footnote 2 says requires additional record sources.

### Entering rules and competing legal paths

Katz, 389 U.S. 347, protects reasonable privacy without requiring physical penetration. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III, protects information about an object within a private home that ordinary outside observation cannot obtain; its warrant presumption preserves recognized exceptions, including truly exigent circumstances. Part IV separately tests whether a warrant had sufficient untainted information. Pinson supplies no exigency finding. The existence of an earlier warrantless search does not itself invalidate every subsequent warrant. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321, distinguishes observing what is exposed from an additional search revealing concealed matter, and rejects an 'unimportant information' exception to search coverage. No physical manipulation like Hicks occurred here; its informational principle must be applied by analogy.

Dow Chemical, 476 U.S. 227, concerns outdoor industrial premises, commercial aerial photography and substantially weaker privacy than a home; it does not make every external sensor use permissible. Riley, 488 U.S. 445, addresses **naked-eye** helicopter observation of a partially open greenhouse. Its opinions do not prove the missing altitude or public-observation facts in this case. Place's dog-sniff treatment concerns its limited contraband-information character; the heat detector supplies information consistent with lawful as well as unlawful activity. Greenwood's discarded-garbage analogy supplies the Government's abandonment argument but does not resolve whether involuntary heat escape abandons privacy in domestic activities.

Current rules in the entering-law slice: Soldal/Padilla's personal property/privacy interest requirement remains; Dickerson (1993-06-07), 'Contraband recognized through a lawful protective patdown,' permits detection through authorized contact but forbids additional probing to create cause; Olano (1993-04-26) controls forfeited-error limits at its scope. Evans (1995-03-01) permits nonexclusion for a qualifying **non-law-enforcement court employee's clerical error plus objectively reasonable reliance**. Police-generated or mixed errors and unreasonable reliance remain unresolved. An officer's deliberate warrantless scan is not the court-employee error Evans addressed.

Leon, as retained in Evans, excludes protection where the affidavit knowingly or recklessly misleads the magistrate, the magistrate abandons neutrality, probable-cause indicia are so absent that reliance is wholly unreasonable, or the warrant is facially deficient so that reasonable reliance is unavailable. Its inquiry includes officers obtaining or supplying the information, not merely the executing officer, and assumes execution within the warrant's scope. These conditions cannot become a generalized technology good-faith exception without a separately supported legal holding and record.

**No-search path:** Affirm the no-search ground if passive detection of exposed surface emissions, at the demonstrated level of detail, invades no reasonable domestic privacy. Strongest objection: sensing lawful domestic heat patterns is not limited to contraband and can acquire information protected by Karo even without penetrating a wall. The path must answer that objection and avoid treating lawful aerial access or exterior measurement as automatically dispositive.

**Search path:** Reject the no-search ground if the device obtains otherwise unavailable information concerning concealed home activities. Strongest objection: the device only compares external surface emissions rather than revealing people, objects or conversations, so Karo's information must be distinguished or explained. A supported search rule must state its actual trigger and qualifications without adopting a future categorical technology test. Search coverage, warrant necessity, exclusion and effect on conviction are separate decisions. A bounded remand for preserved remedial questions may be lawful; automatic suppression or a fresh affidavit-sufficiency proceeding is not established by this record. An affirmance on a supported independent remedial ground likewise requires its actual preservation and facts.

Both paths require enhanced issue-specific comparison under Engine §7 because technology, analogy, remedy and preservation can alter scope or outcome. No exact threshold about commercial availability, resolution, flight altitude or heat amount can be inferred from the missing record.
