## Effect of the new actual decision

**No change for any associate Justice.** Swanner requires application-specific RFRA consideration of substantial burden, compelling interest, and least restrictive means. It neither construes the Lanham Act nor decides source identification, color distinctiveness, functionality, confusion, registration, or unfair-competition remedies. Its rejection of commercial participation as categorical forfeiture of a statutory religious defense is not a general rule protecting every commercial feature against ordinary intellectual-property limits.

The Qualitex commitment already applies the enacted trademark criteria without inserting a categorical color exclusion. Swanner adds no statutory exception to those criteria and no ground for treating a useful or competitively necessary color as protectable merely because it identifies a source. Conversely, Swanner's attention to direct third-party harm does not create a new categorical color-depletion rule. Competition concerns remain addressed through the trademark authorities and the actual functionality inquiry, not through RFRA's different strict-scrutiny framework.

The new decision's remedy also makes no difference. Swanner orders the appropriate statutory inquiry without requiring a new evidentiary hearing in all circumstances. The Qualitex record already contains the findings described in the independent commitment: secondary meaning, likely confusion, and no competitive need for this particular shade. No new source undermines those findings or calls for importing Swanner's burden-of-proof allocation. The basis for reversing the color-only bar remains distinct from the separately affirmed §43(a) relief.

Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer's published Swanner dissent concerns the adequacy of a concrete RFRA justification on that record; their support for affirmance there does not change their trademark commitments. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas's complete joins in the Swanner Court opinion concern the distinct statutory inquiry required in that case; they establish no general preference for remand over decision of an adequately presented legal classification. Neither group's position furnishes a material changed premise here.

## Confirmed commitments and remedy

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain their independent votes to reverse the categorical color-only registrability bar and associated cancellation direction and remand under the ordinary trademark requirements. All eight continue to support protection for acquired source-identifying, nonfunctional color at the bounded scope already stated. No Justice treats color as inherently distinctive, eliminates confusion where required, grants control over useful or competitively necessary features, or adopts a new universal rule about aesthetic functionality.

The separately affirmed §43(a) unfair-competition judgment, damages, and injunction remain preserved; no double recovery, newly quantified remedy, new factual survey, or market definition is supplied. Each Justice's previously stated legal ground and join limitation remains unchanged. The original alternative-path comparison remains sufficient on the unchanged trademark record.
