#### Other governing law

- **42 U.S.C. §§ 1983 and 1988; Dennis v. Higgins, 498 U.S. 439, 443–451 (1991):** A Commerce Clause violation can impair an individual right protected by § 1983. Section 1988 permits a fee award in an eligible enforcement action; it does not independently create a claim or remedy. Dennis does not answer every tax-specific equitable limitation.

- **28 U.S.C. § 1341; Felder v. Casey, 487 U.S. 131, 138–153 (1988):** The Tax Injunction Act limits federal district-court interference when a plain, speedy, and efficient state remedy exists. It does not directly withdraw state-court authority. A State may not frustrate an otherwise valid federal remedy through a conflicting local condition; the source of any limitation must therefore be federal law, not a free-standing preference for uniformity.

- **Dows v. Chicago, 11 Wall. 108, 110–112 (1871); Matthews v. Rodgers, 284 U.S. 521, 525–530 (1932); Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. v. Huffman, 319 U.S. 293, 298–301 (1943):** Equity ordinarily does not interfere with tax collection where an adequate legal remedy exists, and declaratory relief cannot be used to evade the same principle. Adequacy includes whether recovery truly protects the claimant; irreparable harm or an established need to prevent repeated suits concerning the same injury can alter the analysis. No categorical rule forecloses relief where the legal remedy fails.

- **Fair Assessment in Real Estate Association, Inc. v. McNary, 454 U.S. 100 (1981); Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982):** The special federal restraint in state taxation coexists with the general rule against imposing an unprovided exhaustion requirement on § 1983. A tax-specific equitable limitation is not a universal duty to exhaust state remedies before asserting civil rights.

- **Maher v. Gagne, 448 U.S. 122, 129–133 (1980):** A fee-eligible federal claim need not always receive an express merits judgment when a plaintiff prevails on a related ground. But § 1988 does not make a legally unavailable federal remedial claim sufficient merely because a related state-law claim succeeds.

- **Burden and scope:** The party seeking equitable intervention must establish the circumstances making legal relief inadequate under the governing equitable rules. No special proof quantum is added. The statutory availability question is reviewed independently; the existing refund and separate state-law fee ruling are not reopened.
