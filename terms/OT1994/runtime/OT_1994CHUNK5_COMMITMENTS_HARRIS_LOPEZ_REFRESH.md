## Actual controlling law and the precise comparison

Actual Lopez supplies three controlling holdings, each supported by the Chief Justice's opinion of the Court joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. First, the Court identifies the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting it to commerce among the States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. The original stand-alone school-zone possession offense fails at that connection stage: the offered chain from possession through crime costs, impaired education and productivity to the national economy cannot supply the missing connection, and its enacted class and elements supply none. The holding does not categorically exclude noneconomic intrastate conduct; Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope, and no universal formal-findings requirement is imposed. Second, Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regulatory regime; the Court recognizes that route without fully defining it, and it does not save the possession offense. Third, the Court will not insert an absent commerce element into original section 922(q)(1)(A). It leaves the conditional indictment alternative unresolved because the constitutional disposition is sufficient.

No holding makes every jurisdictional phrase constitutionally conclusive. The majority expressly distinguishes actual multistate commercial operations from an object's remote manufacture. The four public dissenters?Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg?would sustain school-zone possession on a rational substantial class-effects analysis, not on remote manufacture alone, and would remand unresolved sentence issues. Their broader application is noncontrolling but is relevant individual evidence for their approach to the stronger commercial-taking setting here.

Harris's original 1992 section 2119 regulates taking or attempting to take a motor vehicle from another's person or presence by force and violence or intimidation while possessing a firearm, and expressly requires that the vehicle have been transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Michigan-manufactured Buick's Arkansas and Mississippi history satisfies the reported enacted condition. That condition is already in the offense; the Court need not create a missing nexus element, add an interstate-resale intention, or supply an absent allegation. The absence of the original complete indictment in the produced sources does not establish an indictment defect.

The Court holds that Congress may protect the interstate transportation instrumentality itself against forcible seizure. Lopez recognizes authority over instrumentalities and explains that intrastate conduct may be reached when it directly attacks one. The required connection must exist before aggregate magnitude can matter; national crime costs do not establish it.

Here the vehicle’s transportation function, the direct attack and the enacted interstate element each perform a distinct task. An automobile is capable of carrying persons and property through interstate transportation channels. The prohibited taking attacks possession and control of that instrumentality itself. Section 2119 requires the Government to prove in every prosecution that the particular vehicle was transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Buick’s Michigan manufacture and Arkansas and Mississippi titling establish that condition. The Court neither supplies a missing statutory element nor finds that the Buick was traveling interstate when taken.

The holding does not make every crime in or near a vehicle federal, and past interstate movement alone creates no universal jurisdictional rule. Allied-Bruce’s reservation about remote object history remains intact because the direct attack and transportation function do constitutional work independent of that history. Wickard and Perez remain effective within their economic-class scope; the Court does not need that separate route to decide this application. Both the carjacking conviction and the dependent firearm conviction survive the commerce challenge.

## Two paths and eight independent applications

**Sustaining path:** The Commerce Clause permits Congress to protect an instrumentality of interstate commerce against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the federal offense requires proof of the particular instrumentality’s interstate connection. Original §2119 is constitutional as applied to the forcible taking of the Buick proved here; the judgment is affirmed without adopting the Fifth Circuit’s aggregate-effects rationale.

**Strongest contrary path:** This is a local armed taking of a vehicle whose interstate movement may be remote, undertaken for a claimed noncommercial motive. A jurisdictional phrase should not become a device for federal control over every later event involving an old interstate object. If the opinion supported federal power solely by the Buick's manufacture, by all violent crime's aggregate costs, or by the assertion that all cars are instrumentalities, it would not answer actual Lopez. A broader economic-class classification must also not be defined so generally as all crime affecting the economy.

**Resolution at the governing baseline:** The defendants’ reliance on the taking’s local character does not defeat this protective authority. Unlike an offense in which an interstate object appears incidentally, this offense directly seizes control of the protected transportation instrumentality. No profit motive or intended interstate resale is necessary to that connection. The Fifth Circuit’s chain through violence, travel, insurance and the national economy does not supply the governing ground.

## Connections to the other two Harris components

Actual Lopez's refusal to insert an absent commerce element does not turn all statutory silence about culpability into strict liability. The original carjacking construction remains knowing armed participation in a forcible taking and intentional aid for an aider and abettor. Staples and the actual X-Citement holding remain distinct sources for contextual fault; no 1994 intent-to-cause-death-or-serious-injury amendment is applied to 1992 conduct. Motive and intent remain distinct. No original instruction error is established by the fact that the complete instructions are unavailable.
