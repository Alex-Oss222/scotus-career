## Actual controlling law and the precise comparison

Actual Lopez supplies two controlling holdings, each supported by Kennedy's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and the Chief Justice. First, the original stand-alone school-zone possession offense cannot be sustained by the offered chain from possession through crime costs, impaired education and productivity to the national economy. The enacted class and elements supply no adequate commercial connection on that theory. The holding does not categorically exclude noneconomic intrastate conduct; Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope, appropriate intrastate inclusion in a valid interstate commercial regime remains possible, and no universal formal-findings requirement is imposed. Second, the Court will not insert an absent commerce element into original section 922(q)(1)(A). It leaves the conditional indictment alternative unresolved because the constitutional disposition is sufficient.

Neither holding makes every jurisdictional phrase constitutionally conclusive. Neither adopts the Chief Justice's separate complete six-route formulation: no Associate joined that additional framework. The majority expressly distinguishes actual multistate commercial operations from an object's remote manufacture. The four public dissenters?Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg?would sustain school-zone possession on a rational substantial class-effects analysis, not on remote manufacture alone, and would remand unresolved sentence issues. Their broader application is noncontrolling but is relevant individual evidence for their approach to the stronger commercial-taking setting here.

Harris's original 1992 section 2119 regulates taking or attempting to take a motor vehicle from another's person or presence by force and violence or intimidation while possessing a firearm, and expressly requires that the vehicle have been transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Michigan-manufactured Buick's Arkansas and Mississippi history satisfies the reported enacted condition. That condition is already in the offense; the Court need not create a missing nexus element, add an interstate-resale intention, or supply an absent allegation. The absence of the original complete indictment in the produced sources does not establish an indictment defect.

The constitutional analysis must nevertheless do more than point to that past history. Forcible acquisition of a motor vehicle is a transaction in a valuable good and a form of vehicle theft. Congress addresses that economic class in a motor-vehicle theft program connected to the actual interstate vehicle market. The connection is to the regulated taking and market, not merely to insurance costs, fear, policing expenditure or the eventual national consequences of violence. The defendants' asserted retaliatory or gang motive does not change the enacted class into stand-alone possession or supply an unlegislated profit-motive condition. No judicial finding that these particular defendants intended to resell the car interstate is required by the retained economic-class doctrine.

This is a qualitative commercial connection, not a newly found dollar amount or a claim that every local crime involving valuable property is automatically federal. The reported interstate movement performs the statute's expressly enacted limiting work; economic-class authority supplies the constitutional ground selected here. Remote history standing alone is not approved. Nor does the word automobile automatically settle an instrumentality theory: whether this application protects an interstate instrumentality on a sufficient independent ground would need its own justification. None of the eight preparatory commitments relies on that label alone.

## Two paths and eight independent applications

**Sustaining path:** Apply the economic-class scope that actual Lopez preserves. Armed forcible vehicle acquisition is materially different from the stand-alone possession offense there. The enacted vehicle condition is present and met on the reported record; it is not judicially inserted. No broader downstream-cost rationale or output/resale finding is necessary.

**Strongest contrary path:** This is a local armed taking of a vehicle whose interstate movement may be remote, undertaken for a claimed noncommercial motive. A jurisdictional phrase should not become a device for federal control over every later event involving an old interstate object. If the opinion supported federal power solely by the Buick's manufacture, by all violent crime's aggregate costs, or by the assertion that all cars are instrumentalities, it would not answer actual Lopez. A broader economic-class classification must also not be defined so generally as all crime affecting the economy.

**Resolution at the now-refreshed baseline:** The regulated conduct is acquisition of the vehicle itself; that concrete economic class supplies a connection absent from the Lopez possession prohibition. No separate profit intention is necessary to classify theft of the traded good as economic conduct. The narrower economic-taking ground is therefore sufficient for the provisional disposition below, while the constitutional sufficiency of past movement alone and the independent instrumentality theory remain open. ## Connections to the other two Harris components

Actual Lopez's refusal to insert an absent commerce element does not turn all statutory silence about culpability into strict liability. The original carjacking construction remains knowing armed participation in a forcible taking and intentional aid for an aider and abettor. Staples and the actual X-Citement holding remain distinct sources for contextual fault; no 1994 intent-to-cause-death-or-serious-injury amendment is applied to 1992 conduct. Motive and intent remain distinct. No original instruction error is established by the fact that the complete instructions are unavailable. The supplied lower account recognizes knowing conduct and intentional assistance. The sources do not support a new trial based on an invented omitted-fault instruction.

The new Lopez decision creates no cumulative-punishment rule. Hunter's single-trial authorization rule, section 924(c)'s express cumulative and consecutive language, and the separate Smith use/carrying-and-relation requirements remain the relevant baseline. The existing eight preliminary dispositions reject the cumulative challenge on clear authorization, with Stevens's actual Hunter dissent reservation preserved and no universal holding that every section 2119 violation automatically satisfies every section 924(c) element. The final Harris file must independently restate that reservation and every component's join boundaries; this preparatory passage does not erase them.

Because the refreshed provisional commerce disposition sustains the predicate on the selected ground, it does not presently require vacating the linked section 924(c) conviction. If the final permitted law or a genuine source correction instead defeats the federal predicate, section 924(c)'s need for a federally prosecutable predicate must be addressed rather than ignored. On the available narrowed path, the reported 41-month carjacking term and consecutive 60-month firearm term for each defendant survive these three presented challenges. No new weapon tier, sentence enhancement, adjudication of unpresented sufficiency claims, or approval of unknown instructions is implied.
