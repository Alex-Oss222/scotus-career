#### E. ISSUE-BY-ISSUE LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reconsideration and judicial-review finality

#### Question

Does a timely motion for agency reconsideration filed before judicial review postpone the original deportation order’s review deadline?

#### Controlling reasoning below

The Sixth Circuit inferred separate continuing finality from a provision contemplating consolidation of two petitions and dismissed the original-order challenge.

#### Petitioner / Appellant / Excepting party

- **Proposed Rule and Application:** Petitioner argues that incorporation retains the ordinary reconsideration rule and that a direction to consolidate available petitions does not silently abolish it.
- **Requested Judgment and Limiting Principle:** Reverse and remand for original-order review; limit the position to timely agency reconsideration initiated before judicial review, without reviving deadlines through untimely motions.
- **Principal Authorities:** Former INA § 106(a)(1), (a)(6), 8 U.S.C. § 1105a(a)(1), (6); ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. 270, 279, 284–285 (1987); Cheng Fan Kwok v. INS, 392 U.S. 206, 212 (1968); 8 C.F.R. §§ 3.8, 243.1, applicable versions; Review and consequences; the published lower opinion identified in Section I.H supports the attributed position.
- **Necessary Concessions:** No express concession is attributed. As an analytical limit, this position remains subject to the independent threshold, factual, and remedial requirements identified above.

#### Respondent / Appellee / Opposing party

- **Proposed Rule and Application:** INS argues that consolidation requires two reviewable final orders and would have little work if reconsideration always rendered the original order nonfinal.
- **Requested Judgment and Limiting Principle:** Affirm the dismissal; permit separately timely petitions for the original order and the reconsideration denial, consolidated when both are pending.
- **Principal Authorities:** Former INA § 106(a)(1), (a)(6), 8 U.S.C. § 1105a(a)(1), (6); ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. 270, 279, 284–285 (1987); Cheng Fan Kwok v. INS, 392 U.S. 206, 212 (1968); 8 C.F.R. §§ 3.8, 243.1, applicable versions; Review and consequences; the published lower opinion identified in Section I.H supports the attributed position.
- **Necessary Concessions:** No express concession is attributed. A favorable answer does not itself establish the separately reserved merits or remedial questions.

#### Strongest answer to petitioner

A construction that erases the independent finality of an order after a judicial petition is already filed may conflict with Congress’s instruction to consolidate pending reviews.

#### Strongest answer to respondent

The statute can preserve an already-filed judicial petition while retaining the ordinary finality rule when reconsideration is sought first. Consolidation has work without an unstated abolition of the incorporated rule.
