## Exact majority-ground boundary and controlling qualifications

**Six associates share the following ground:** The applicable June §105 restraint cannot be displaced through this collateral bond-enforcement proceeding merely because the creditors have substantial objections to its issuance. Section 1334(b) reaches a bankruptcy relation beyond ownership of the bond itself. The described bond, collateral, insurance, and reorganization setting supplies the specific jurisdictional connection here, and §157(c)(1)'s final-order limitation does not establish that this interlocutory protection was transparently beyond all bankruptcy adjudicative competence. The creditors must pursue relief in the issuing proceeding and proper review while the restraint remains operative. The Court need not approve its final core classification, every finding concerning estate impact, or the continued injunction's ultimate merits to reject this collateral route.

This is not a rule that merely uttering an arguable jurisdiction claim immunizes an order. United Mine Workers permits temporary preservation while a substantial jurisdictional question is resolved, expressly excluding frivolous and insubstantial jurisdictional claims. It separately requires obedience to unreversed orders of a court with jurisdiction over the parties and subject matter. Its civil-remedial relief falls with an erroneously issued injunction; criminal punishment may stand differently. No contempt judgment or surviving civil award after invalidation is at issue here. The six associates apply its orderly-review principle to this still-operative civil restraint with those distinctions intact.

Walker preserves the qualification concerning an injunction that is transparently invalid or has only a frivolous pretense to validity. It also expressly reserves a materially different posture involving timely challenge frustrated by delay. It does not fully define either universal exception. No such frustration is established in the supplied Celotex record. The explicit motion route, debtor's continuation burden, and statutory reviewing courts provide the concrete review basis here; a fictitious unconditional immediate appellate right is unnecessary. No Walker facts of announced defiance, clear individual notice, or a particular available two-day review period are imported, and no sanction follows. Its treatment of Green is not turned into a general ban on jurisdictional objections.

Stevens and Ginsburg accept that legal error alone does not license disobedience. Their objection concerns the antecedent allocation of adjudicative power to this non-Article III judge, not general impatience with lawful review or denial that the injunction speaks to the bond. They would permit the bond court to exercise its conceded authority on that structural ground. Their view supplies no controlling law unless a later final coalition independently adopts it. The two grounds are incompatible on the present authority question; no Marks synthesis or artificial shared validity rule is possible or necessary.

## Relief and review routes preserved

For the six reversal Justices, reverse the Fifth Circuit judgment and remand so that the Texas enforcement authorization cannot operate contrary to the §105 restraint while it remains effective. This decision does not itself dissolve the injunction or order it to remain forever. The creditors may seek relief from the issuing bankruptcy court; the June order assigns Celotex the burden to establish that continued restraint is warranted. Coverage, subject-matter jurisdiction, §157 allocation and procedure, statutory authority, supporting evidence, and duration remain available questions. Nothing here awards the bond to the estate, changes the underlying tort judgment, or extinguishes Northbrook's undertaking.

Under the applicable §158(a), final bankruptcy orders are reviewable by the district court in the district where the bankruptcy judge serves, and interlocutory review requires leave. Here that is the Middle District of Florida. Section 158(d) provides Eleventh Circuit review of qualifying final appellate decisions, not every interlocutory bankruptcy action. Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route from a district court's bankruptcy appellate order; where §1292(b) is invoked, written statutory findings, timely application and circuit permission remain necessary, and application alone supplies no stay. A bankruptcy appellate panel is not assumed. Section 157(d) withdrawal of reference is separate and retains its own cause, timely-motion and statutory prerequisites.

Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm the authorization to enforce Northbrook's independent bond obligation. Their ground neither changes the amount of that undertaking nor awards damages, contempt relief, or an avoidance recovery. No new injunction, refund, payment, release, reorganization completion, or other later event is invented for either side.
