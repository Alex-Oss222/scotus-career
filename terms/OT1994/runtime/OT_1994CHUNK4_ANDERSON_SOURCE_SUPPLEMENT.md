## Full question and the independent grounds actually briefed

The petition asks whether a State, consistently with federal AFDC law, may require the needy caretaker relative and all needy children, siblings and nonsiblings, living in the same household to be included in one AFDC assistance unit. The merits brief states the question as whether all needy children, siblings and nonsiblings alike, living with one relative caretaker may be grouped into one unit. The certified class covers applicants or recipients in households with two or more nonsibling dependent children and a single caretaker relative **or relative married couple**. These formulations must be read with the actual rules and application conditions; they do not authorize compulsory inclusion of every resident regardless of eligibility or application.

The recipients' availability objection includes both presumed caretaker-to-child support and presumed support between nonsibling children. Verna Edwards herself is not receiving AFDC; consequently the case is not exhausted by deciding how to treat an aided caretaker's income. The recipients separately contend that federal mandatory parent/sibling inclusion under §602(a)(38) does not authorize wider state-compelled consolidation, that §612's regulated proration option limits economies-of-scale reductions, and that the agency interpretation cannot overcome its regulations. Their merits brief, printed pp. 74–77, independently invokes equitable treatment under §§233.10(a)(1) and 233.20(a)(1)(i), measured against §601's family-support purposes. They argue that consolidation discourages care by poor relatives, lowers assistance to children living with aided relatives compared with children living with non-needy relatives, and can attribute one child's non-AFDC income to children whom that child has no duty to support.

California answers that its rule treats similarly sized aided families alike, defines units of applicants before calculating need, and leaves nonapplicants' income subject to actual/legal availability restrictions. It argues that §602(a)(38) supplies mandatory inclusions rather than an exclusive unit definition, that its flat-grant schedule is not a §612 proration plan, and that HHS's interpretation is consistent with the governing law. California's reply, printed p. 18 (PDF 26), expressly acknowledges that neither the Ninth Circuit in this case nor *Beaton* reached the equitable-treatment question and that respondents raise it as a ground for affirmance. Rejection of the lower court's availability ground therefore does not by itself dispose of that independent ground.

## Rules, membership, and application conditions

The district court reproduces successor MPP §82-824.1 in full as relevant here, petition App. 17 (PDF 49): two or more assistance units in the same home shall be combined when (1) a caretaker relative is married to a caretaker relative in another unit; (2) two caretaker relatives have separate children and a child in common; or (3) there is only one caretaker relative. The litigation and injunction focus on the nonsibling consolidation rule, particularly .13. The earlier §44-205.31 places all eligible children in one family-budget unit when there is one caretaker; where there is more than one caretaker, their eligible children have separate units except under the rule's further conditions. App. 56–57 reproduces the application/caretaker-determination provisions and .311: one unit where the caretaker is a parent who could be included as an eligible child in another caretaker's unit, with deprivation established for all eligible children. The supplied old-rule exhibit ends there; it does not justify an assertion that every possible exception to the earlier section has been reproduced.

The §602(a)(38) text in respondents' appendix, App. 2 (PDF 96), is:

> provide that in making the determination under paragraph (7) with respect to a dependent child and applying paragraph (8), the State agency shall (except as otherwise provided in this part) include—(A) any parent of such child, and (B) any brother or sister of such child, if such brother or sister meets the conditions described in clauses (1) and (2) of section 606(a) of this title or in section 607(a) of this title, if such parent, brother, or sister is living in the same home as the dependent child, and any income of or available for such parent, brother, or sister shall be included in making such determination and applying such paragraph with respect to the family (notwithstanding section 405(j) of this title, in the case of benefits provided under subchapter II of this chapter);

The exception for other provisions of the part, the same-home requirement, the sibling dependency conditions, and the Title II proviso are operative qualifications. The supplied 1986 instructions require co-resident otherwise-eligible parents and dependent blood-related or adoptive siblings within the State's age limit. They identify excluded SSI recipients, aliens failing citizenship/alienage requirements, aliens ineligible because of sponsor income/resources or agency/organization sponsorship, and individuals ineligible because of lump-sum income. They define parent to include natural and adoptive parents and qualifying stepparents whose support duty under a generally applicable state law equals that of a natural parent. They distinguish stepbrothers/stepsisters, who need not be included merely because the qualifying stepparent must be included. In incapacity cases, and in States with an AFDC-UP program for unemployment of the principal earner, both parents are included if otherwise eligible. Those are the reproduced administrative instructions, not new judicial findings.

The 1986 excerpt also distinguishes Title II benefits paid to a representative payee in the unit's household, which must be counted, from payment to an out-of-household payee, counted only to the extent made available for the beneficiary's support. It mandates consolidation where one person is required in two or more co-resident units. Its minor-mother/grandchild example expressly says the family need not seek assistance for the grandchild; **if** it does, the linked units consolidate. Outside those requirements, it leaves States policy over how many units are formed when another relative files for aid.

California's merits brief p. 40 n.19 and reply p. 6 expressly condition nonapplication/opt-out on §602(a)(38). An otherwise optional nonparent caretaker or nonsibling child may refrain from applying; that does not mean a parent or sibling already required by federal law may withdraw alone. Respondents' pp. 61–63 give the precise counterexample: a child with income and an included sibling cannot individually opt out while the sibling remains; avoiding the child's inclusion could require both siblings to decline AFDC. The hypothetical personal-injury award and Social Security examples are counsel's illustrations, not adjudicated facts about any named child receiving such money. Any narrower income-attribution challenge must identify the actual income, membership, availability, applicable disregards, and federal inclusion conditions.

## Exact availability provisions reproduced in the primary appendix

Petition App. 30 (PDF 62), §233.20(a)(2)(viii):

> Provide that the money amount of any need item included in the standard will not be prorated or otherwise reduced solely because of the presence in the household of a non-legally responsible individual; and the agency will not assume any contribution from such individual for the support of the assistance unit except as provided in paragraphs (a)(3)(xiv) and (a)(5) of this section and §233.51 of this part.

The cross-references concern stepparent income, the authorized proration option, and sponsored-alien eligibility, respectively; they are separate provisions and cannot be collapsed into an undefined general exception. No named claimant is shown here to present a disputed stepparent or alien-sponsor application. The §612 proration objection is presented, and the complete §612 text supplied in respondents' App. 4 permits a State plan to prorate shelter, utilities, and similar needs on a reasonable basis where a claimant lives as a household with other individuals **not claiming aid together**; an SSI recipient subject to the statutory one-third reduction is excluded from household membership for that proration. The State's assertion that its plan is outside §612 and respondents' assertion that §612 is the exclusive permissible method are competing legal positions.

Petition App. 31 (PDF 63), §233.20(a)(3)(ii)(D):

> Income after application of disregards, except as provided in paragraph (a)(3)(xiii) of this section, and resources available for current use shall be considered. To the extent not inconsistent with any other provision of this chapter, income and resources are considered available both when actually available and when the applicant or recipient has a legal interest in a liquidated sum and has the legal ability to make such sum available for support and maintenance.

The legal-interest and legal-ability conditions qualify the second stated route to availability; neither actual availability nor the chapter-consistency qualification may be omitted. The parenthetical reference to (a)(3)(xiii), the application of disregards, and current-use limit remain part of the quoted command. The official 1994 edition confirms the entire quotation on PDF pp. 6–7. Its full paragraph (a)(3)(xiii), PDF p. 10, supplies the cross-referenced gross-income qualification:

> Under the AFDC plan, provide that no assistance unit is eligible for aid in any month in which the unit's income (other than the assistance payment) exceeds 185 percent of the State's need standard (including special needs) for a family of the same composition (including special needs), without application of the disregards in paragraph (a)(11)(i) (except to the extent provided for under paragraph (a)(3)(xix)), paragraph (a)(11)(ii) and paragraph (a)(11)(viii) of this section.

Paragraph (a)(3)(xix), PDF p. 11, permits a State to disregard earned income of dependent children who are full-time students for this gross-income limit, with the State plan specifying amounts and a period no longer than six months per calendar year; earned income from JTPA participation may be disregarded under that paragraph, paragraph (a)(3)(xvii), or both for a combined maximum of six months per calendar year. Paragraphs (a)(11)(i), (ii), and (viii), fully read on PDF pp. 15–17, address earned-income eligibility disregards, benefit-calculation disregards, and earned-income tax credits. No named claimant's actual income is adjudicated under these provisions in this supplement; the quotation preserves the qualification rather than presuming that all income invariably enters every calculation.

Petition App. 32 (PDF 64), §233.90(a)(1), reproduces:

> The determination whether a child has been deprived of parental support or care by reason of the death, continued absence from the home, or physical or mental incapacity of a parent, or (if the State plan includes such cases) the unemployment of his or her parent who is the principal earner will be made only in relation to the child's natural or adoptive parent, or in relation to the child's stepparent who is married, under State law, to the child's natural or adoptive parent and is legally obligated to support the child under State law of general applicability which requires stepparents to support stepchildren to the same extent that natural or adoptive parents are required to support their children. Under this requirement, the inclusion in the family, or the presence in the home, of a 'substitute parent' or 'man-in-the-house' or any individual other than one described in this paragraph is not an acceptable basis for a finding of ineligibility or for assuming the availability of income by the State;

Do not describe only the first sentence as the entire regulation. Respondents specifically invoke the second sentence's income-availability clause (printed pp. 29–30); HHS's contrary account emphasizes parental-deprivation determination. Both sentences and the State-plan, marriage, generally applicable support-duty, and equal-support-duty qualifications must enter consideration.

## What the administrative documents do and do not establish

ACF-AT-94-6 instructs States to determine unit composition before eligibility and need; it identifies mandatory inclusion and overlapping-unit consolidation, then allows statewide policies consolidating or separating other co-resident units. It says the income/resources available to individual members are considered together within the established unit, after statutory disregards and allocations, without proof of actual transfers between members. Its actual/legal-availability limitation protects against attributing funds unavailable to any unit member. Its Example A consolidates a grandmother and five children after another caretaker dies but excludes the surviving male friend's resources unless actually made available. Example B keeps two units separate under a different state policy and forbids presumed cross-unit sharing, again excluding the friend's resources absent actual availability. Thus the instrument does **not** purport to make every resident's money available regardless of membership or legal relationship.

The transmittal quotes and analyzes the three availability regulations, discusses the 1981 deletion of a prior sentence from §233.90(a)(1), and addresses objective/equitable determination under §233.20(a)(1). It does not quote or separately analyze §233.10(a)(1)'s Social Security Act purposes requirement. It ends by identifying possible protective-payee/legal-representative remedies and state-law penalties for misuse of aid; it does not determine that any named caretaker misused aid. The transmittal's consistency with the regulations and the weight it deserves remain questions, rather than conclusions supplied by this supplement. The 1992 Barnhart declaration, App. 53–55, is an agency official's declaration supporting the California policy; it is not a lower-court adoption of every asserted administrative premise.

California's reply pp. 15–17 and n.5 expressly concede that §605 does not affect non-AFDC income of caretakers or children, does not create a mutual support duty among nonsiblings, and does not create or alter state-law general support obligations. California relies on the caretaker's use of the **AFDC grant** for all persons for whom it is paid, and on separate provisions for unit composition and income treatment. Any disposition resting on §605 must preserve that distinction. Section 605's supplied text authorizes the State to provide counseling/guidance and advice about protective payments, guardianship, and authorized penalties under its stated conditions; it must not be rewritten as a command to impose every listed remedy.

## Actual lower findings and the judgment to be reviewed

The district court's April 24, 1992 filed order, App. 15–27, finds a loss of aggregate household benefits and resulting hardship, and gives Edwards's then-current $901 versus $630 example. It holds the rule invalid because *Beaton* binds it and expressly declines to decide that precedent's persuasiveness. The later 1994 merits-brief payment figures reflect intervening benefit changes and must not be merged with the 1992 findings. Detailed allegations about food, borrowing, foster care, and hypothetical outside income in respondents' brief are attributed to declarations or counsel; not every assertion is a separate lower finding merely because hardship was found generally.

The permanent injunction was filed May 8, 1992 (signed May 7), App. 5–12. It declares §§44-205.31 and 82-824.13 invalid, bars the nonsibling-consolidation policy, requires separate grants for children not required together by §602(a)(38), and preserves a needy nonparent caretaker without eligible children of the caretaker's own choosing to join a related child's unit. It orders implementation, correction of ongoing grants, corrective payments retroactive to the order, and specified county notices and timing. It preserves court involvement over implementation disputes and attorney fees. The operative paragraph contains an apparent “44-205.21” textual inconsistency with the repeatedly identified challenged .31; a quotation must preserve it or identify it, not silently claim a different challenged rule. The December 1, 1993 Ninth Circuit judgment affirms on *Beaton* and rejects purported distinctions; it supplies no independent equitable-treatment disposition.

This is a classwide attack on the consolidation rule. A distinction between the rule's categorical validity and specific income-attribution applications is legally material, but it cannot be converted into a factual finding that every as-applied claim has already been adjudicated or waived. The existing classwide injunction and the independent equitable-treatment ground must be addressed in any disposition; a reservation of a distinct, fact-dependent resource dispute does not silently dispose of the preserved equity contention.

Section 233.10(a) states that a State plan under titles I, IV–A, X, XIV, or XVI must satisfy the following complete paragraph (a)(1):

> (1) Specify the groups of individuals, based on reasonable classifications, that will be included in the program, and all the conditions of eligibility that must be met by the individuals in the groups. The groups selected for inclusion in the plan and the eligibility conditions imposed must not exclude individuals or groups on an arbitrary or unreasonable basis, and must not result in inequitable treatment of individuals or groups in the light of the provisions and purposes of the public assistance titles of the Social Security Act. Under this requirement:
>
> (i) A State shall impose each condition of eligibility required by the Social Security Act; and
>
> (ii) A State may:
>
> (A) Provide more limited public assistance coverage than that provided by the Act only where the Social Security Act or its legislative history authorizes more limited coverage;
>
> (B) Impose conditions upon applicants for and recipients of public assistance which, if not satisfied, result in the denial or termination of public assistance, if such conditions assist the State in the efficient administration of its public assistance programs, or further an independent State welfare policy, and are not inconsistent with the provisions and purposes of the Social Security Act.
>
> (iii) There must be clarity as to what groups are included in the plan, and which are within, and which are outside, the scope of Federal financial participation.
>
> (iv) Eligibility conditions must be applied on a consistent and equitable basis throughout the State.
>
> (v) A plan under title XVI must have the same eligibility conditions and other requirements for the aged, blind, and disabled, except as otherwise specifically required or permitted by the Act.
>
> (vi) Eligibility conditions or agency procedures or methods must not preclude the opportunity for an individual to apply and obtain a determination of eligibility or ineligibility.
>
> (vii) Methods of determining eligibility must be consistent with the objective of assisting all eligible persons to qualify.

The permission to impose administratively useful or independent state-welfare conditions is expressly conditioned on consistency with the Act's provisions and purposes. It does not eliminate the separate ban on arbitrary or unreasonable exclusions. Mandatory eligibility conditions, permitted narrower coverage, and the equal-treatment provisions are distinct. The title XVI qualification in (v) remains in the complete quotation without being treated as an AFDC-specific rule.

The official 1994 edition confirms that the above primary-source quotation of §233.90(a)(1) is complete: its terminal semicolon is followed by “and” joining **separate paragraph (a)(2)**. Paragraph (a)(2) concerns suspected injury, sexual abuse/exploitation, neglect or maltreatment threatening a child's health or welfare, and the agency's reporting and cooperation duties. It does not continue or qualify the income-availability wording within (a)(1). Nothing in these sources makes that separate provision a pleaded ground here. The official heading and lead-in are: “Factors specific to AFDC. (a) State plan requirements. A State plan under title IV–A of the Social Security Act shall provide that:”.

Section 233.20(a)'s official 1994 text begins: “Requirements for State Plans. A State Plan for OAA, AFDC, AB, APTD or AABD must, as specified below: (1) General.” The complete clause (i) is:

> (i) Provide that the determination of need and amount of assistance for all applicants and recipients will be made on an objective and equitable basis and all types of income will be taken into consideration in the same way except where otherwise specifically authorized by Federal statute and

The final “and” introduces separate clause (ii); it is not an instruction that some unidentified exception governs equity. The complete uniform-income command and express Federal-statute qualification are now directly verified in the 1994 official source. The 1994 petition p. 7, merits brief pp. 12 and 38, respondents' pp. 74–77, reply pp. 18–20, and ACF-AT-94-6 App. 42 independently corroborate the contemporaneous **objective and equitable basis** requirement. The earlier source limitation concerning the final words is therefore resolved. The uniform-income command does not itself settle whose income belongs to an assistance unit, whether resources are available, or what statutory disregard or mandatory-inclusion rule applies; those distinct questions retain their supplied conditions.

The directly implicated proration regulation, §233.20(a)(5), is fully verified on PDF pp. 13–14:

> (5) Proration of shelter, utilities, and similar needs in AFDC. (i) Provide that the State agency may prorate allowances in the need and payment standards for shelter, utilities, and similar needs when the AFDC assistance unit lives together with other individuals as a household; except that, the State shall not prorate with respect to any person receiving SSI to whom the statutory one-third reduction (section 1612(a)(2)(A)(i) of the Act) is applied, or prorate when a bona fide landlord-tenant relationship exists. If the State chooses to prorate under this paragraph, it must prorate both the need standard and payment standard.
>
> (ii) If the State agency elects to prorate allowances for shelter, utilities, and similar needs the State plan must:
>
> (A) Indicate which allowances will be prorated, and describe the procedure which will be used to prorate the allowances;
>
> (B) Provide that the allowances will be prorated on a reasonable basis; and
>
> (C) Specify the circumstances under which proration will occur, including a description of which individuals are considered to be living with an AFDC assistance unit as a household.

That regulation's landlord-tenant bar is distinct from the statutory SSI one-third-reduction condition and must not disappear if the regulatory proration option is applied. The choice to prorate is permissive; proration of both standards and the stated plan requirements follow only if the State elects it. Whether California's challenged consolidation is such proration remains the parties' dispute, not a determination supplied here.

The other cross-references in §233.20(a)(2)(viii) have also been read in their complete contemporaneous setting: §233.20(a)(3)(xiv), PDF pp. 10–11, and §233.51, PDF pp. 29–30. The former applies to co-resident married stepparents in States lacking a generally applicable equal support duty, and counts their income after these disregards: first $90 of gross earned income; the State need-standard amount for a family of the composition of the stepparent and other co-resident persons whose needs are not included in the AFDC eligibility determination and who are or could be claimed as the stepparent's dependents for federal personal income tax, excluding sanctioned or required but noncooperating persons; amounts actually paid to persons outside the home who are or could be claimed as the stepparent's federal personal income tax dependents; and alimony or child support paid for persons outside the household. When the stepparent is included in the unit, paragraph (a)(11)(i) and (ii) disregards instead apply. Section 233.51 is a separate sponsored-alien regime; its full definitions, temporal predicates, financial calculations, and categorical exclusions are available in the now-read official source. One express limit relevant to identifying the nature of the cross-reference is paragraph (d): “Income and resources which are deemed to a sponsored alien shall not be considered in determining the need of other unsponsored members of the alien's family except to the extent the income or resources are actually available.” No claimant-specific stepparent or sponsorship application is established here; these distinct regimes do not furnish a general authority to presume every co-resident's contribution. This source completion does not apply the sponsored-alien regime to an unproved set of facts.

The full-source read also included §233.90's separate plan-approval and federal-participation provisions and all other Part 233 sections. Their mere presence in the source does not expand the pleaded claim. No new factual premise, claim, or adjudicated application is inferred from completing the regulatory source.
