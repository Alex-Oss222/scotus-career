## 86. Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Approval and scope

**Approval Status:** APPROVED IN SUPPLIED SOURCE. The existing approval label is preserved; consolidation supplies no new approval, vote or Court holding. Revalidate only against the actual event-date record, lawful scope and effective law.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM in part, REVERSE in part, and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Oklahoma may not enforce the motor-fuels tax against the Tribe under the current statute and may not tax income of tribal members residing in Indian country. It may tax wages of members residing outside Indian country, subject to ordinary law.

#### Issue 1: Motor-fuels tax

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved legal-incidence question and declines to decide the Hayden-Cartwright Act argument first raised on the merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Absent clear congressional authorization, a State may not impose a tax whose legal incidence falls on a Tribe or tribal members in Indian country. The statute’s language and structure place legal liability on the tribal retailer.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Oklahoma’s motor-fuels tax, as currently written, may not be enforced against the Tribe because its legal incidence falls on the tribal retailer in Indian country and Congress has not clearly authorized that tax.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The State does not require pass-through to consumers and imposes reporting, payment, and liability on the retailer. Legal incidence supplies the administrable first question and respects Congress’s primary role. Oklahoma remains free to amend the tax prospectively within federal limits.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide the late-raised Hayden-Cartwright argument or a tax whose legal incidence is lawfully shifted to nontribal distributors or consumers.

#### Issue 2: Income tax on tribal members

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved income-tax question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A State lacks authority to tax a tribal member’s income earned from the Tribe when the member resides in Indian country. Residence outside Indian country changes the territorial and sovereign basis, and the ordinary state income tax may then apply absent federal law to the contrary.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Oklahoma may not tax the tribal wages of members residing in Indian country, but may tax members residing outside Indian country under its generally applicable income tax.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** McClanahan protects reservation Indians within Indian country from state income taxation. The categorical territorial rule does not follow members to off-reservation residences where Oklahoma provides ordinary governmental services and no federal enactment supplies exemption.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide nonmember employees, income from nontribal sources, or a federal statute expressly altering state authority.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Ninth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall determine whether the Hawaii rules genuinely constrained officials’ authority to impose punitive segregation, whether the challenged deprivation was meaningful rather than routine, and what process was due. No liability finding is made.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone clarifies and narrows Hewitt rather than replacing it with a new framework. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient, but state law creates a liberty interest when substantive law meaningfully restricts official authority to impose a substantial deprivation. Routine incidents of confinement do not become entitlements through drafting conventions; genuine legal constraints do not disappear merely because they operate in prison.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State creates a protected prison liberty interest when substantive law meaningfully limits officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation; mandatory language alone is insufficient, but a court may not disregard a genuine legal constraint on punitive segregation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Hawaii’s rules may have conditioned segregation on a supported misconduct finding, and material facts remain disputed. Summary judgment is therefore premature. Materiality belongs to ordinary judgment about whether liberty was meaningfully restrained, not to a new multi-factor constitutional test.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not constitutionalize every prison regulation, routine transfer, brief inconvenience, or discretionary management choice. Security and institutional judgment remain entitled to the weight the native due-process analysis permits.

## 90. Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION II -->

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### Position and scope

**Approval Status:** USER-SUPPLIED POSITION. The supplied disposition, reasoning and express alternatives are recorded as preparation instructions. No Court judgment, vote, rationale or coalition is created by this supplement.

**Build:** First Amendment and expression.

#### Case-specific reasoning

A parade is expression even without a single narrow message or rigorous editorial selection. The application of the public-accommodations law compelled inclusion within that expression. This does not establish that every private institution can evade antidiscrimination law by asserting a viewpoint. A different expressive-association dispute requires evidence of a comparably material expressive burden.

#### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Supreme Judicial Court of Massachusetts and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The injunction compelling GLIB’s inclusion shall be dissolved. The public-accommodations law remains enforceable in applications that do not compel private expression.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone separates equal access to commercial services from compelled editorial selection. A parade is expressive even when it carries many messages and lacks a single precise theme. Requiring the organizers to include an identified contingent and its message alters the communication the private speakers choose to present.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Applying a public-accommodations law to compel private parade organizers to include a contingent conveying a message they do not choose violates the First Amendment.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The independent record shows that selecting marchers is part of the parade’s expressive composition. The organizers’ broad admissions policy does not surrender editorial autonomy, and the State may not force a private speaker to affirm or include a message as the price of using public streets under a neutral permit.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate public-accommodations laws generally, authorize exclusion from ordinary nonexpressive goods and services, or make customer identity itself a message.
