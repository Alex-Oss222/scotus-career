#### Issue 1: Undue burden and the facial challenge

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing facial standard. He does not decide disputed operational facts that the lower courts did not lawfully resolve under that standard.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Casey, not Salerno, supplies the governing standard for a facial challenge to a regulation of previability abortion. The challenger need not prove invalidity in every conceivable application. The relevant denominator is the persons for whom the particular provision operates as a restriction. Geographic distance, provider availability, scheduling limitations, financial constraints, and comparable access conditions are not constitutional violations merely because they exist independently of the enactment, but they cannot be disregarded. The court must determine the additional burden attributable to the challenged provision as it operates against those conditions. Requirements that operate together may not be insulated from review by analyzing each resulting burden in artificial isolation.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Casey governs an abortion-specific facial challenge rather than Salerno’s no-set-of-circumstances formula; the relevant class consists of the persons for whom the challenged provision operates as a restriction; existing access conditions must be considered in measuring the additional burden attributable to state law; and interacting statutory requirements must be evaluated together when their combined operation determines whether a substantial obstacle exists in a large fraction of the relevant cases.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Eighth Circuit treated constitutional applications outside the relevant class as a reason the challenge could not succeed and treated similarity to Pennsylvania’s text as an adequate substitute for examining operation in North Dakota. Casey requires the opposite sequence. The district court must identify the persons actually burdened by each provision, determine what the provision adds to the conditions they face, and decide whether that attributable burden creates a substantial obstacle in a large fraction of those cases. The existing affidavits and statutory constructions may inform that inquiry, but this Court does not turn them into untested findings.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that a twenty-four-hour period is always valid or always invalid, that every practical difficulty is attributable to the State, or that cumulative review permits relief against a provision that does no constitutional work in the combined burden.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal adequacy required of the emergency provision and remands any application requiring medical factfinding or an authoritative state-law construction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The medical-emergency language must permit prompt action in a physician’s good-faith clinical judgment when compliance would create a material risk of death, serious physical injury, substantial irreversible impairment, or another grave medically established danger to the patient’s health. A construction forcing the patient to approach death or requiring delay incompatible with the emergency would be unconstitutional. The penal provisions must also incorporate the culpability required by North Dakota law.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An abortion regulation backed by penalties must preserve a practically usable emergency route based on good-faith medical judgment when delay creates a serious health risk, and any saving construction must be one the enacted text and controlling state law fairly bear.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower court relied on statutory similarity and a saving interpretation without resolving whether North Dakota law authoritatively supplied the required clinical latitude. On remand, the court must determine whether the statute fairly bears the constitutionally sufficient construction and how the State’s scienter law applies. If the text cannot bear that construction, the defective provision may not be enforced.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not create immunity for negligent practice or permit invocation of an emergency unsupported by good-faith medical judgment.

Government cannot escape constitutional obligations merely by choosing a corporate form for an entity it created and controls in the relevant manner. This is an attribution decision, not a merits ruling approving the advertisement.

#### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Second Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower courts shall adjudicate Lebron’s First Amendment claim treating Amtrak as governmental for constitutional purposes; the Court makes no final merits ruling on the advertising display.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies settled attribution principles. The Federal Government created Amtrak by special law to pursue governmental objectives and retains permanent authority to appoint a majority of its directors. Congress may define Amtrak for statutory purposes but cannot disclaim constitutional responsibility for the entity it created and controls.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A corporation created by special federal law to advance governmental objectives and permanently controlled through federal appointment authority is part of government for constitutional claims, notwithstanding a statutory declaration that it is not an agency.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The controlling facts establish creation, purpose, and enduring governmental control, not merely funding or regulation. Treating the disclaimer as dispositive would allow government to relocate public power beyond constitutional limits by corporate form.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not convert federally chartered, subsidized, or heavily regulated private corporations into state actors without comparable governmental creation and control.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the enacted amortization formula. The statute directs calculation as if the first payment were made on the first day of the plan year after withdrawal. Interest therefore accrues over the subsequent payment period, not during a year before the statutory debt and schedule begin.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For the statutory installment calculation, withdrawal-liability interest begins on the first day of the plan year following the withdrawal year.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** “Amortize” assumes interest but does not answer when the debt is outstanding. The explicit assumed first-payment date and the rejection of a pre-withdrawal funding gap resolve the timing question.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding concerns the statutory calculation method and does not alter separate rules governing delinquency, assessment, or actual payment dates.
