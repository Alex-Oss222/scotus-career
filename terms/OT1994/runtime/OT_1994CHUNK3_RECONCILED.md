### Exact rule and remedial premises retained

Section 501(b) prohibits receipt of an honorarium by a Member, officer, or employee. Section 505(2) excludes special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202. Section 505(3) covers payment of money or anything of value for an appearance, speech, or article and includes a series only if the subject directly relates to official duties or the payment is because of governmental status. That nexus parenthesis does not already qualify every single payment. Actual and necessary travel expenses for the individual and one relative are excluded to the extent paid or reimbursed by another and otherwise deducted to the extent not paid or reimbursed; travel expenses mean transportation, lodging, and meals away from residence or principal employment.

Section 501(c)'s charitable route treats a qualifying payment on the individual's behalf as not received, with a **$2,000 limit on each payment** and no payment to an organization financially benefiting the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child, or dependent relative. It does not remunerate the employee's expressive labor. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and a civil penalty up to the greater of $10,000 or compensation received. Section 504(b) protects the recipient of a written opinion by a §503 entity and another covered individual with materially indistinguishable facts only for action **after issuance, in good faith, in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings**, from subsection (a)'s sanction. Neither mechanism is a wholesale exemption. Section 501(a)'s different high-level outside-income ceiling is not adjudicated.

## 28. Grubart; City of Chicago v. Great Lakes — Nos. 93-762, 93-1094

Argument October 12, 1994; event February 22, 1995. Stevens and Breyer do not participate. The admitted controversy concerns admiralty jurisdiction over the alleged vessel-based piling casualty and ensuing land flooding. The two dockets receive coordinated treatment.

### Final common scope

All six participating associates support affirmance of the appellate jurisdictional judgment and continuation of the limitation proceeding. The alleged negligent vessel operation on navigable water can support jurisdiction despite injury consummated on land. The Admiralty Extension Act's “caused by” boundary is ordinary proximate causation, not a new immediate-impact, fixed-distance, or fixed-time cutoff. No independent tort negligence, indemnity, contribution, corporate privity/knowledge, contract scope, cap amount, or damages determination is made.

Four associates—O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg—adopt the complete Sisson locality-and-connection route. The incident is damage by a vessel in navigable water to an underwater structure, a general type capable of disrupting maritime commerce. The activity is repair or maintenance work on a navigable waterway performed from a vessel, substantially related to traditional maritime activity. Actual closure corroborates the characterization but is not a universal element. At least one alleged tortfeasor's relevant maritime activity must be an alleged proximate cause of this incident; nonmaritime contributing conduct does not erase that relationship. This resolves Sisson's mixed-activity reservation without declaring that mere joinder of a vessel owner creates jurisdiction over unrelated land conduct. The four reject replacing Sisson with an open-ended multi-factor balancing inquiry.
