## Official provision in full

The Office of the Law Revision Counsel's official [1994 edition of 28 U.S.C. §151](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-1994-title28-section151&num=0&edition=1994), “Designation of bankruptcy courts,” states:

> In each judicial district, the bankruptcy judges in regular active service shall constitute a unit of the district court to be known as the bankruptcy court for that district. Each bankruptcy judge, as a judicial officer of the district court, may exercise the authority conferred under this chapter with respect to any action, suit, or proceeding and may preside alone and hold a regular or special session of the court, except as otherwise provided by law or by rule or order of the district court.

The source identifies laws in effect on January 4, 1995. Its source credit is Pub. L. 98-353, title I, §104(a), July 10, 1984, 98 Stat. 336. Section 122(a) of that Act makes title I and its amendments effective July 10, 1984, except as otherwise provided in §122. Section 122(b) concerns the nonapplication of 28 U.S.C. §§1334(c)(2) and 1411(a) to title 11 cases pending on July 10, 1984, or proceedings arising in or related to those cases; it does not except §151. Section 122(c) gives §§108(b), 113, and 121(e) of the Act a June 27, 1984 effective date; §104(a), which enacted §151, is not among them. Thus the quoted designation took effect July 10, 1984, before the packet's September 25, 1985 bankruptcy petition. The short-title and separability notes were also read; neither changes this designation or supplies a §1001 construction.

## Institutional clarification and its limits

The provision makes the bankruptcy judges **in regular active service** collectively a **unit of the district court**, designated the bankruptcy court for that district. It identifies each bankruptcy judge as a judicial officer of the district court. It permits exercise of authority **conferred under chapter 6**, with respect to any action, suit, or proceeding; it permits a judge to preside alone and hold regular or special court sessions. These permissions remain subject to what is otherwise provided by law or by a district-court rule or order. They are not an unrestricted grant of every district-court power, and permission does not become a duty. This source establishes no case-specific reference, rule, order, or allocation of authority beyond the quoted text.

This institutional description remains separate from the construction of the offense-date 18 U.S.C. §§6 and 1001. Section 151 neither defines “department” or “agency” for title 18 nor says how those terms apply to this unit. The separate department definition and its contextual expansion, and the agency definition and its contextual narrowing, remain as quoted in the validated packet. The bankruptcy court's designation does not itself resolve either definition, every other Judicial Branch entity's status, the §1001 jurisdiction requirement, or whether these particular filings performed adjudicative or administrative functions. The packet's distinction between the court, its judge, and the trustee remains necessary. No additional entity classification is inferred from the trustee's involvement.
