## Seven exceptions: exact retained grounds and comparison

### Colorado's laches exception

**Retained position:** Overrule the exception seeking protection against monetary liability for periods before Kansas initiated the Administration process in March 1985. It does not request cancellation of prospective Compact compliance. Colorado has not shown inexcusable delay: knowledge that wells increased or gross flow declined did not itself establish knowledge of a material loss of usable Compact water amid drought, changing reservoir operation, imports and developing regulation. The neutral report and briefing support that distinction; historical pp. 687–689 confirm it.

The model also finds no demonstrated causal connection between the alleged delay and the missing records, testimony or development reliance. Preserve that assessment and the distinction between reliance on Colorado law and reliance on Kansas silence. Do not convert missing old records into presumed misconduct or a duty that the Compact did not enact. The sufficient disposition ground remains lack of inexcusable delay; no independent prejudice holding or categorical prohibition of interstate laches is released. Illinois (1991)'s general observation and actual Nebraska's concrete acquiescence holding do not decide an absolute Compact rule. Texas (1987) rejects good-faith uncertainty as a complete answer to past performance while retaining remedial flexibility. The eventual form of relief and supported remedy equities remain open; no final damages cutoff is fixed.

### Colorado's protected pre-Compact pumping exception

**Retained position:** Overrule both the legal challenge based on later decrees or maximum possible pumping and the factual challenge to the 15,000 acre-foot benchmark. Article IV-D includes improved or prolonged functioning of existing works when it materially depletes covered usable water; Article VI preserves Colorado administration only subject to the Compact's express qualifications. Later state decrees cannot enlarge the protected interstate allowance. Both parties accept an allowance for established pre-Compact use, but that acceptance does not establish Colorado's claimed amount.

The accepted highest negotiation-period estimate is preferable on this liability record to the single wet-year 11,000 figure and to Colorado's decree, power-use and acreage reconstructions. Shared Hurr estimates are one foundation, not five independent measurements. Missing original calculations remain a limitation; the competing reconstructions also depend on disputed coverage, nonelectric use, efficiency and equipment assumptions. The model's comparative evaluation, rather than automatic trust in agency publication, supports its result. Historical pp. 689–691 adopt actual use, the estimate, and the Court's independent factfinding responsibility. The additional source qualifications in the neutral packet do not contradict that result. Pumping volume is not Stateline depletion; the protected allowance is neither a final damages figure nor an unconditional pumping cap.

### Colorado's 1980 Operating Plan exception

**Retained position:** Overrule the asserted independent legal credit for benefits Kansas obtained in the separately bargained Plan. Colorado does not press a complete settlement or bar in this exception. The separate exchange of release and timing rights, the absence of an agreement purchasing a general well-liability offset, and the Plan's preservation of claims defeat the free-standing legal credit. Historical pp. 691–693 confirm the same bargain-based ground; their discussion of settlement does not enlarge the actual exception into a request for a complete release.

Actual physical mitigation remains distinct. Hydrologic comparisons must include actual Plan operation and actual imported return flows that reduce loss. Denying a legal credit does not authorize modeling a different river. The 60/40 account shares concern designated conservation storage, not every river flow. Imported water is outside the apportioned source, but its actual return flows can reduce the actual loss; this creates no right to future imports. Article V-E's prohibition on accumulated credits or debits supplies no general credit bank. No numerical offset is awarded, and none of the Plan's account, transit, spill, evaporation or termination qualifications is displaced.

### Colorado's proof-standard exception

**Retained position:** Overrule the objection as a reason to deny pumping liability while reserving the general choice between preponderance and clear and convincing proof of contested Compact-enforcement facts. Substantial material pumping depletion is established under either proposed standard; WWSP's distinct claim fails even the lesser one. Historical pp. 693–694 expressly confirm this narrow resolution rather than adopting the Master's general preponderance rule.

Actual Nebraska distinguishes enforcement from obtaining a new allocation; it does not settle every disputed factual quantum in Compact enforcement. Colorado (1984)'s high-probability approach concerns a proposed new diversion and requires more than speculative proof, not certainty. Here materially different models show a large pumping effect, with actual operation, imports and usability addressed separately. The gross comparison figures are not awards or independently certified usable losses. The retained conclusion concerns the existence of a material pumping violation, not every intermediate estimate. Greenwich, Daubert and Whitecotton preserve distinctions among admissibility, production, persuasion, degree of conviction and separately allocated defenses; none imports its statute's burden into this Compact. No evidentiary tie is resolved in Kansas's favor.

### Kansas's Trinidad Operating Principles exception

**Retained position:** Overrule and accept dismissal of the presented Compact claim. The 1967 Principles do not establish the claimed independent Compact command under which any deviation is a Compact violation without material depletion. Article VIII-B authorizes consistent implementation; it does not allow administrative enlargement of the Article IV-D bargain. For this claim the comparison is operation with and without the project, rather than actual operation against an ideal project following every Principle. The presented showing does not establish material Compact depletion on the former comparison.

Historical pp. 681–683 confirm that conclusion, and note 3 expressly reserves a separate agreement or third-party-beneficiary cause of action. The model preserves those claims and the report's unresolved question about compelling Administration duties; it does not recognize either claim as established. Unanimous approval and mandatory language warrant identifying the actual source and scope of authority, not a conclusion that valid implementing rules are categorically impossible or that a document's title controls. Assumed inconsistency with the Principles is not an adjudicated factual breach of every term. A federal project has no blanket immunity from IV-D. The current qualification that a genuine consistent implementation rule could have legal force does not authorize enforcement of these Principles as an independent Compact prohibition.

### Kansas's WWSP depletion exception

**Retained position:** Overrule for failure to prove material depletion, not because the program is found harmless. Historical p. 684 confirms failure of proof after a full reasonable opportunity to present the claim. The neutral packet provides the necessary specificity behind that conclusion: paired-run errors can cancel in principle, but these runs have an inadequate common operational base, nonparallel winter-evaporation and storage errors, and unreliable timing of modeled spills and accretions. The omitted actual Plan operation and artificial storage buildup matter to the inference sought.

An effect smaller than absolute calibration error is not automatically unprovable; the model is not rejected by that arithmetic alone. Nor does reliability for large aggregate pumping establish precision for the smaller program effect. Conditional model output is not a concession accepting all assumptions. Accretions are neither all usable nor all unusable without supported timing. Defects in the United States' evaporation proof do not affirmatively prove Kansas's competing calculation. Article VIII-G's conditional information powers and VIII-H's mandatory investigation but discretionary reporting remain in their own terms; unequal data control does not create an automatic persuasion reversal, universal well-metering duty or spoliation finding. No further liability trial follows simply because additional science is possible, and no remedial quantity is awarded.

### Kansas's usable-water methodology exception

**Retained position:** Overrule and accept Durbin's approach with Larson's modifications for this liability record. Article IV-D protects usable quantity and availability, including supported timing and recharge. Spronk's method seeks a finer reconstruction than the monthly depletion model reliably supplies, and its recharge attribution can charge Colorado for effects of later Kansas pumping. Historical pp. 684–687 confirm the fit problem and acceptance of the selected approach; the neutral packet supplies the precise method and the additional attribution limit.

The method retains Larson's 72 percent summer and 25 percent winter coefficients, a 9.9 percent pre-Compact recharge component, and monthly/seasonal ceilings of 40,000/140,000 acre-feet in summer and 7,500/40,000 in winter. These are record-specific method features, not permanent Compact formulas. Spronk's Garden City reconstruction is not rejected as inherently defective; the 20 c.f.s. power-plant threshold concerns 1970–1973, and the discussed ordinary threshold is 5 c.f.s. A reliable daily analysis remains possible in principle. Dismissal of Colorado's Kansas-well counterclaim does not prevent considering Kansas pumping when estimating the water Kansas could actually have used. Neither gross depletion nor a report estimate becomes final compensable acre-feet.

**Retained position:** Confirm dismissal on failure to trace protected John Martin releases to unauthorized retention, rather than the short retention necessary to move water through the approximately 3,600 acre-foot pool. Article V-E(2) requires prompt beneficial use unless the Administration authorizes downstream storage; the Plan does not erase either the rule or that exception. Pool size and observed level alone do not establish origin, retention duration or unauthorized use. The proof fails even the lesser quantum, without deciding a universal counterclaim burden. Historical note 2, p. 680, expressly adopts dismissal, but does not supply a separate published hydrologic rationale. The source for that rationale remains the neutral report. No general downstream-storage permission follows.

### Kansas well counterclaim

**Retained position:** Confirm dismissal for failure to demonstrate injury to Colorado's protected supply through additional calls, transit/account effects or lost surplus. After the Plan Kansas draws on its own conservation account; the earlier additional-call claim lacks the necessary attribution, and asserted lost surplus is unquantified. Historical note 2 expressly confirms dismissal.
