## Travelers — complete material qualifications in the reproduced state text

Source: docket93-1414 appendix A99–A113, mechanically cleaned [statutory text](../../../tmp/ot1994_chunk5_b/clean/travelers1414_03_substantive.txt). It was read in full. The [Archive collection](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0497) supplies the underlying PDF. The original page numbers remain in the clean text. These are the appendix's versions, not an asserted verbatim compilation of every amendment effective April26,1995.

**ERISA.** Appendix A99–A100 supplies §514(a), §514(b)(2)(A) and (B), with §4(a)/§4(b) coverage qualifications and the deemer exception for a plan established primarily for death benefits. The exemption for a separate workers' compensation/unemployment/disability plan is not an exemption for every health plan touched by a hospital bill. Neither the saving clause nor the deemer clause creates a general exemption from ordinary state law unrelated to the covered plan.

**Section2807-c(1), reproduced period January1,1988–December31,1993.** The general-hospital inpatient rate system excludes Medicare payments. Alternative reimbursement under subdivisions4(e)–(i) may be authorized; the statute does not make every reimbursement arrangement identical.

- Paragraph(a) covers state governmental agencies, qualifying article43 Blues payors (including the described subscribers of foreign corporations paying on an expense-incurred basis), and article44 HMOs. Local governmental payments for local correctional inmates receive the state-agency basis under (a-1).
- Paragraph(b)'s expense-incurred category includes workers' compensation, volunteer firefighters' and volunteer ambulance workers' benefits, no-fault coverage, self-insured funds **except those under a subdivision2-a rate-method agreement**, licensed commercial accident/health insurers covering inpatient services, and authorized out-of-plan HMO benefits under §4406 **except those of not-for-profit or Internal Revenue Code §501(c) organizations**. Its rate uses the Blues reimbursement basis with adjustments for services not included in that basis, adds13%, and adds the paragraph11(i) adjustment where applicable.
- Hospitals cannot refuse qualifying direct-pay arrangements without due cause. The reproduced text also precludes use of a lower-of-charges-versus-DRG method and specified apportionment excluding treatment of a preexisting or acquired condition with the admission. These are payment-method qualifications, not ERISA plan-design commands.
- Paragraph(c) permits the other, charge-paying category and the stated short-stay treatment. Its maximum is120% of the paragraph(b) DRG rate **without the paragraph11(i) adjustment**; charges must follow a uniform schedule. The hospital-charge category must not be described as simply another application of the full24% differential.

**Additional11%.** Paragraph11(i), reproduced from1992 Laws ch55 §348, expressly addresses commercial-insurer expense-incurred discharges from April1,1992 through March31,1993. The quoted11% is additional to the13%, making24% for that category/time. The appendix uses an ellipsis after that statement. Paragraph14(e) directs hospitals to remit the11% component into the designated pools and ultimately the state general fund, with related income. The record's descriptions and the Blues' reply assertion that the differential was continued through1995 do not replace the missing full extension law. No current1995 discharge liability or rate calculation is certified. A disposition of the supplied ERISA issue can leave amendment effects, rate implementation and live prospective relief to remand.

**HMO assessment under subdivision2-a(a).** The reproduced text reaches article44 HMOs or qualifying article43 arrangements for discharges after July1,1992, **excluding Medicaid patients and patients in the regional pilot program under1988 chapter703**. The basis is the applicable DRG/per-diem subdivision4 amount or approved negotiated subdivision2-b amount, with an addition up to9% adjusted under paragraph(b). The commissioner's initial notification deadline was June1,1992; the later annual deadline is December1. An appeal by May1 can produce the July1 half-year adjustment. The amount is not automatically9% for every HMO or every inpatient discharge.

**Elimination and partial reductions under2-a(b).** Elimination requires participation as a managed-care provider under Social Services Law §364-j in **each** social-services district in the service area and enrollment of at least90% of the summed target number of eligible, nonexempt Medicaid recipients. Initial1992 calculations use the specified May1 date; the annual1993-and-later determination uses November1 of the preceding year, subject to the specified May1 appeal for the following July half-year. Reaching25%,50% or75% of target produces a25%,50% or75% reduction respectively. A75% reduction does not equal elimination; the elimination threshold is90% with the participation requirement.

The commissioner may exempt districts from the participation requirement on timely application—May1,1992 or October1 of the preceding year for1993 and later—under the statutory good-faith/commissioner criteria. The listed good-faith alternatives for1992 and1993 are not interchangeable or indefinite future exemptions:

- An implemented participation plan meets refusal by the local district to contract.
- Letters of intent cover all required districts, with contracts covering at least half by July1992 or two-thirds by January1993, and Department-agreed explanations for remaining delays.
- The corresponding half/two-thirds threshold is met by contracts covering that share of the nonexempt Medicaid population in the districts, with agreed explanations for remaining delays.
- Enrollment equals the total nonexempt district target despite incomplete contracts. This route counts fee-for-service Medicaid activity converted by the annual average commercial visits and an approved viable capitation schedule. **It grants only a75% reduction, not complete elimination.**

An exempt district is removed from the service-area calculation. An HMO with noncontiguous regions may apply across its whole service area or the separately aggregated regions under the text. The target formula compares an HMO's commercial subscribers with all HMOs' commercial subscribers in the district, excluding Medicaid, Medicare and regional-pilot participants from both numerator and denominator, and multiplies by the district Medicaid objective. The district objective is the applicable maximum of the approved enrollment goal as of July1 of the previous year;5% of the nonexempt population if the first full year has not ended; and the actual enrolled nonexempt population on the prescribed date, excluding the described non-HMO/non-§4403-a providers. Initial1992 uses its May1 determination. The most recent relevant statistics govern; qualified affiliated prepaid-health-plan Medicaid enrollment can count under the statutory arrangement.

**Remittance and annual cap,2-a(c).** HMOs pay the assessment directly to the pool monthly; estimated payments are due15days after the month unless actual payment is made. For the fiscal year ending March31,1993 the specified total cap is$31million. Collection stops when the commissioner, with the budget director, determines that the amount has been or will be collected; excess is returned proportionately. Arrearages carry subdivision20 interest/penalties. The administration contract calls for an annual audit and limits the specified personnel cost paid from the pool to$200,000. That first-year cap is not certified as the cap for all future years.

**Point-of-service transfers,2-a(d).** Where an article43 corporation had an HMO certificate on April1,1992 and later transfers from that HMO to its point-of-service product reduce enrollment by more than20%, the pre-April1 ratio is used and the assessment also reaches the point-of-service inpatient business. The exception requires satisfactory evidence that the transfers were beyond the corporation's control. Remittance uses the same pool. Paragraph(e) requires the prescribed annual county enrollment data by May1, including Medicaid, Medicare and pilot-program categories.

These provisions explain why the economic connection, HMO participation incentives, insurer saving, and self-funded applications cannot be analyzed as a single unqualified24% or9% rule. If a disposition depends on a different statutory year or the appendix's omitted11(i) text, obtain that exact law first.

**Harris §2119.** [Official Pub.L.102-519](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-106/pdf/STATUTE-106-Pg3384.pdf), §101(a),106Stat.3384, is the original enactment, saved as `carjacking1992.pdf/.txt`. Its text is:

> Whoever, possessing a firearm as defined in section921 of this title, takes a motor vehicle that has been transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce from the person or presence of another by force and violence or by intimidation, or attempts to do so, shall—
>
> (1) be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than15years, or both,
>
> (2) if serious bodily injury (as defined in section1365 of this title) results, be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than25years, or both, and
>
> (3) if death results, be fined under this title or imprisoned for any number of years up to life, or both.

Spacing is normalized; spelling artifacts in OCR are not statutory changes. The packet decides no serious-injury or death-tier application. Those tiers' statutory predicates and the reference to §1365 remain part of the text. The1994 replacement of the firearm-possession phrase by an intent phrase is not applied to the reported December1992 conduct.

**Harris §924(c), reconstructed offense-date reading from official Code and amendment notes.** The verified operative core reads: “Whoever, during and in relation to any crime of violence or drug trafficking crime (including a crime of violence or drug trafficking crime which provides for an enhanced punishment if committed by the use of a deadly or dangerous weapon or device) for which he may be prosecuted in a court of the United States, uses or carries a firearm, shall, in addition to the punishment provided for such crime of violence or drug trafficking crime, be sentenced to imprisonment for five years”. This core is reproduced in the official1994 edition and in the predecision *Singleton* judicial opinion; the official amendment notes identify no1993/1994 change to that core.

The1992 version also supplied ten years for a short-barreled rifle/shotgun, thirty for a machinegun/destructive device or firearm equipped with a silencer/muffler, twenty for a second/subsequent conviction and life without release for that repeat conviction involving the listed machinegun/destructive-device/silencer/muffler category. No probation or suspended sentence was permitted and the §924(c) term could not run concurrently with any other imprisonment, expressly including the predicate term. The original no-parole sentence remained: “No person sentenced under this subsection shall be eligible for parole during the term of imprisonment imposed herein.” Section924(c)(2) defined drug trafficking by the enumerated federal felony statutes; §924(c)(3) defined a crime of violence as a felony that either has as an element use, attempted use or threatened use of physical force against another's person/property, or by its nature involves a substantial risk that such force may be used in committing it.

Version control matters: the1994 edition adds a semiautomatic-assault-weapon tier under §110102(c)(2) and deletes the parole sentence under §110510(b); neither is treated as original1992 text. The1994 technical amendment §330011(j) corrects the1990 directory language deleting redundant “imprisonment for” before “life imprisonment without release,” expressly effective as of the1990 amendment; this is not a new substantive tier for the present five-year count. The1990 amendment notes identify introduction of the short-barrel/destructive-device terms. This is an openly reconstructed historical reading, not a claim that the1994 PDF itself is a1992 compilation. No unverified weapon-tier finding or new sentence is derived from it.
