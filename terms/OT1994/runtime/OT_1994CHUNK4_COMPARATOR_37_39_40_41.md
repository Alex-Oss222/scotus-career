#### B. HISTORICAL JUDGMENT AND VOTE

- **Historical Judgment:** Affirmed the reviewed statutory-standing ruling.
- **Vote:** 9–0 in judgment.
- **Named Coalition:** All nine historical Justices; Ginsburg concurred in the judgment.
- **Remedy / Remand:** The Director could not independently litigate the employee-award challenge on a general administrative-interest theory.

#### C. HISTORICAL OPINION TOPOLOGY

- **Opinion of the Court:** Scalia, joined by Rehnquist, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer.
- **Concurrence in the judgment:** Ginsburg.
- **No-Majority Components:** None in the Court’s statutory holding; the narrower concurrence did not adopt every generalization about agency aggrievement.

#### D. HISTORICAL HOLDINGS BY COMPONENT

### Component 1: Employee-award review
The Director was not a person adversely affected or aggrieved within § 921(c) merely because of his general administrative interest in benefits adjudication.

### Component 2: Boundary
The claimant’s own appeal and distinct statutory roles were not silently transferred to the Director. The Special Fund setting was materially different.

#### E. HISTORICAL DECISIONAL PREMISES

**Court opinion:** The Act did not expressly authorize general Director appeals, and his supervisory interest was not the aggrievement the provision required. **Ginsburg:** Agreed with the result while avoiding an unnecessarily categorical exclusion of governmental agencies from statutory aggrievement.

- [Director, OWCP v. Newport News, 514 U.S. 122, 124–136 (1995)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/122/): historical outcome and majority/concurrence boundaries.

## 40. Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION III -->

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** 13 F. 3d 1297, reversed. Vote: 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Breyer, J., delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** The Lanham Act permits the registration of a trademark that consists, purely and simply, of a color. (a) That color alone can meet the basic legal requirements for use as a trademark is demonstrated both by the language of the Act, which describes the universe of things that can qualify as a trademark in the broadest of terms, 15 U. S. C. § 1127, and by the underlying principles of trademark law, including the requirements that the mark “identify and distinguish [the seller’s] goods . . . from those manufactured or sold by others and to indicate [their] source,” ibid., and that it not be “functional,” see, e. g., Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U. S. 844, 850, n. 10. The District Court’s findings (accepted by the Ninth Circuit and here undisputed) show Qualitex’s green-gold color has met these requirements.
- **Historical Authority:** The official opinion structure stated above controls only as historical comparator; it is not simulated law entering Stone’s vote.
- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 514 U.S. 159, and Supreme Court Database vote metadata.

## 41. Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc.

<!-- BEGIN_SECTION III -->

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** 15 F. 3d 90, reversed and remanded. Vote: 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Rehnquist, C. J., and Stevens, Kennedy, and Ginsburg, JJ., joined. Scalia, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment, in which Thomas, J., joined, post, p. 200. Breyer, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which O’Connor, J., joined, post, p. 201.
- **Historical Holding:** Oklahoma’s tax on the sale of transportation services is consistent with the Commerce Clause. (a) Under Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, 430 U. S. 274, Oklahoma’s tax is valid if it is applied to an activity with a substantial nexus with the State, is fairly apportioned, does not discriminate against interstate commerce, and is fairly related to the services provided by the State.
- **Historical Authority:** The official opinion structure stated above controls only as historical comparator; it is not simulated law entering Stone’s vote.
- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 514 U.S. 175, and Supreme Court Database vote metadata.
