## Qualitative relevance check

* **Kyles v. Whitley:** The completed Brady violation concerns collective favorable suppression, prosecution-team responsibility, and the absence of a second duplicative prejudice test after claim-defining materiality is established. Its reasonable-probability standard is not a newly universal test for retrospective punishment. It neither changes Cavanaugh nor establishes a parole-board reopening practice. Morales's rejected false-information claim is not an admitted Brady question here. The Williams opinion cited in Kyles is the distinct 1992 grand-jury matter, not the current tax-refund case. Kyles supplies no answer to the latter's statutory waiver question.
* **Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co.:** Its material-advancement and proportional-fit requirements govern the challenged commercial-speech restriction. Its treatment of predictive judgment does not create a general rule about evidentiary burdens in parole administration or sovereign immunity. It neither proves nor disproves California's claimed practice and supplies no construction of the tax-refund statutes.
* **Stone v. Immigration and Naturalization Service:** The independent filing periods result from the particular former INA scheme, including its consolidation command. The decision preserves the distinction among finality, enforceability, exhaustion, and stays; optional reconsideration is not an added prerequisite. It does not establish that another statute's alternative remedy is exclusive, that Williams had to seek substitute collateral, or that filing an administrative claim cures a statutory plaintiff limitation. Those issues still require the tax provisions' own construction. Neither its majority nor its dissent establishes a generally available procedure for reopening a parole decision on changed circumstances.
* **Celotex Corp. v. Edwards:** Its qualified requirement of orderly relief and review addresses a still-operative judicial bankruptcy restraint with established relatedness, sufficient interim authority, and concrete relief routes. It does not transform an IRS lien into such an injunction, establish the validity of Williams's liens, or turn a discretionary substitute-fund agreement into a mandatory and available exhaustion step. Its jurisdiction and adjudicative-authority holdings do not enlarge an express federal immunity waiver. It supplies no interim parole mechanism.
* **McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission:** Its exacting scrutiny and limits on source-identification requirements govern individual political leaflets. It creates no tax-refund jurisdiction, parole standard, universal proof requirement, or presumption about the parties' disputed facts.
