## Scope and preservation now established

The Supreme Court merits briefs expressly contest **all four Complete Auto factors**. Nexus, nondiscrimination, and fair relation are not conceded, waived, or absent merely because the Eighth Circuit stopped after external consistency. The joint appendix also establishes that the bankruptcy court actually decided all four factors: it found nexus and fair relation satisfied; it found external apportionment and discrimination unsatisfied. The district court and Eighth Circuit resolved apportionment only. A remand limited by an assumed absence of adversarial presentation of the remaining factors is no longer compelled by this source gap. Whether to decide a fully presented legal issue or remand remains a judicial choice under the actual governing law and record.

The petitioner's question concerns an in-state purchaser and in-state vendor, tickets sold within Oklahoma, and a destination or route beyond its borders. The respondent asks whether taxing all receipts from transportation partly furnished outside Oklahoma is valid. The parties' February 10, 1992 stipulation, J.A. 3–6, is conclusive for this bankruptcy case and its appeals, not for all future disputes between these parties. Its specified issue is tickets sold in Oklahoma for a trip originating in Oklahoma and terminating elsewhere. Hypotheticals about tickets for entirely out-of-state travel are developed in the respondent's argument but are not the stipulated transactions at issue. Its addenda concern a separate Greyhound/Eagle Bus record containing those broader transactions; that record must not be imported into Jefferson's findings.

## Verified record additions

- Jefferson is a Minnesota corporation qualified to do business in Oklahoma on April 11, 1988 and operating there as a common carrier. The stipulation confirms local ticket sales and in-state-origin interstate routes. The bankruptcy court found an Oklahoma presence facilitating intrastate and interstate sales, and local services at the places of sales and loading and on Oklahoma roads. J.A. 3–5, 11–12, 15–16.
- The disputed periods are September and October 1989 and January and February 1990 under sales-tax permit 246600. The amount in controversy is **$46,659.15**. This is a stipulated disputed claim amount, not a new Court award. Other permit/withholding amounts were withdrawn; no disposition should revive them. J.A. 4–5, 8 n.2.
- The stipulation says the same rate applies to intrastate and interstate tickets sold in Oklahoma regardless of origin or destination. The petitioner's statute footnote states that the rate increased from four percent to four and one-half percent effective February 1, 1990. Thus a statement that every disputed month used four percent would be inaccurate. The amount is stipulated; no new computation is required. Pet. Br. 2 n.1; J.A. 5 ¶15.
- The petition was filed October 27, 1989, and the plan was confirmed August 3, 1990. Oklahoma asserts priority and administrative claims; the materials do not adjudicate an unrestricted right to payment outside normal bankruptcy treatment. J.A. 4–5.
- Section 1354's levy is expressly qualified by sales otherwise exempt under the Sales Tax Code. Section 1361(A) directs the consumer/user to pay the vendor as trustee and makes the required collector liable. Section 1361(B) adds the tax to the price, makes it a consumer-to-vendor debt, and permits ordinary debt recovery. Pet. Br. 2–3. The respondent additionally quotes §1352(F)'s gross-consideration definition and §1352(L)(4)'s inclusion of furnishing/rendering taxable services within sale. Those texts support a contested characterization argument, not an agreed answer. Resp. Br. 1–2, 8–10.
- The district court expressly found no Oklahoma credit mechanism for the hypothesized other-state tax, and the briefs dispute whether a credit is necessary here. No actual second assessment is shown; multiple taxation is argued as a potential. J.A. 20–23; Pet. Br. 17–18; Resp. Br. 23–24, 32–34.

## Remedy and limits

The State expressly requests reversal and remand with instructions to allow and direct payment of its unpaid-sales-tax claims. Pet. Br. 22. Jefferson requests affirmance and full disallowance; it argues that this statutory levy is not judicially apportionable in its present terms. Resp. Br. 8 n.3, 19, 43. Neither request is itself a ruling or an authorization to resurrect withdrawn claims.

The available source-supported routes now include: affirming disallowance on a supported constitutional ground; rejecting external inconsistency while returning a truly unresolved necessary question; or deciding the other fully briefed factors on the stipulated and lower-found record and issuing the corresponding bounded bankruptcy remand. A decision reaching all four factors must state its actual rule and application for each disputed factor rather than claim the others were conceded. It must keep a holding on these local-sale/local-origin transactions distinct from tickets for transportation wholly outside Oklahoma, remote purchasers, different credit schemes, or another State's actual levy. The lower nonreach is not a factual vacuum after the appendix and merits briefs have supplied the material issues and their support.

The additional public sources also state a historical grant date of June 13, 1994. That is now source-supported historical chronology, but this supplement does not add a separate simulated grant event. No merits opinion or historical merits lineup was retrieved or supplied to this validation context.
