### Statute in terms and temporal limits

The offense-date §1001 text, reproduced identically in the substantive party briefs and lower opinion, is:

> Whoever, in any matter within the jurisdiction of any department or agency of the United States knowingly and willfully falsifies, conceals or covers up by any trick, scheme, or device a material fact, or makes any false, fictitious or fraudulent statements or representations, or makes or uses any false writing or document knowing the same to contain any false, fictitious or fraudulent statement or entry, shall be fined not more than $10,000 or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.

Section 6 supplies **two distinct definitions and qualifications**:

> As used in this title:
>
> The term “department” means one of the executive departments enumerated in section 1 of Title 5, unless the context shows that such term was intended to describe the executive, legislative, or judicial branches of the government.
>
> The term “agency” includes any department, independent establishment, commission, administration, authority, board or bureau of the United States or any corporation in which the United States has a proprietary interest, unless the context shows that such term was intended to be used in a more limited sense.

The reference to former Title 5 §1 was reenacted as §101; the official Code's reference note confirms that change. Do not omit the department definition's contextual expansion or convert the agency definition's narrowing proviso into the same exception. A choice about department coverage does not silently decide every possible agency theory.

The official 1994 Code records a September 13, 1994 amendment replacing §1001's old $10,000 fine clause with “fined under this title.” The underlying conduct predates that change; no new penalty applies by inference. ### Competing legal paths and precedent treatment

**Textual exclusion with express precedent treatment.** A federal court is not an enumerated executive department or, in the relevant statutory usage, an agency of the United States. The broad words “any matter” and “jurisdiction” describe the functions of an included entity; they do not independently establish which entities are included. Section 6's context qualification is genuine, but general antifraud purpose and the absence of a demonstrated narrowing intent in 1934 do not establish that every branch is a department under the enacted formulation. The separate agency words likewise cannot be read to make every body exercising governmental authority an agency without regard to their setting. On this path, Bramblett's all-branch construction must be rejected expressly, including its reasoning supporting legislative-branch coverage; the objection cannot be evaded merely by calling the judicial passage dictum. The present judgment decides courts and these filings, not the outcome of an unpresented prosecution involving another institution on an independently established agency theory.

The strongest objection is statutory stare decisis: Bramblett considered the same proviso, settled its meaning, was later used in Rodgers, and may be revised by Congress. The exclusion path requires more than repeating disagreement. Its sufficient justification is the combined defect of displacing the express default with general predecessor-purpose reasoning, the resulting need to improvise unstable function-based criminal boundaries, and the absence here of contract, property, or institutional-benefit arrangements comparable to Hilton and Allied-Bruce that depend on the broader coverage. Prosecution expectations are relevant but do not by themselves cure a materially overextended definition of a crime. Rodgers's jurisdiction holding survives for an actual department or agency; its footnote 4 reserved rather than decided the judicial-function issue. No generalized right to make false statements follows.

**Retain Bramblett and reject a judicial-function exception.** The §6 proviso expressly allows branch-wide meaning, Bramblett exercised that contextual judgment after examining this statute's development, and the subsequent statutory system supplies no sufficiently concrete reason to overrule it. Once that construction is retained, excluding judicial functions from “any matter” lacks an enacted basis. Rodgers supports authorized-function coverage and ordinary knowledge/materiality protections while expressly leaving the exception open. Heintz supplies a current, bounded analogy against an invented litigation exemption; it does not itself construe this criminal statute. Factual lies in these pleadings are not treated as innocent legal argument or a not-guilty plea. The Government's asset-collection characterization is unnecessary to this path, not a lower-court finding.

The strongest objection is that the ordinary definition excludes courts and overlapping perjury/obstruction provisions protect judicial proceedings with deliberately different limits. The retention path answers that Congress wrote a context proviso and that Bramblett resolved it; statutory overlap does not itself exclude unsworn factual lies. Lenity does not undo a meaning these Justices regard as settled. This path supplies no universal knowledge-of-illegality rule, approval of every prosecution of litigation conduct, or retroactive penalty increase.

**Intermediate exception or private-litigation path.** The circuit tradition and Nelson's dissent provide a serious narrower alternative. The court's adjudicative machinery differs from administrative/housekeeping acts; a private trustee is not automatically the United States as litigant. Against it stand the absence of a textual exception, inconsistent boundaries, and the fact that the actual counts charge factual assertions rather than merely testing legal sufficiency. On either intermediate theory, classification must be performed expressly. The lower court did not reach the Government's administrative-asset-collection alternative. A remand to address that preserved issue would be distinct from a direction to vacate the counts. None of the eight selects this intermediate path as a sufficient ground on the present provisional record; the individual barriers below explain why. No finding that the claims were harmless, truthful, sworn, or incapable of affecting estate administration is invented.

## Shared legal comparisons after the source refresh

**Precedent and special justification remain separate from first-instance construction.** Bramblett addressed §6 and the statutory history; it cannot be rejected on the factually incorrect assertion that it overlooked them. The exclusion path says its treatment was unsound and identifies what subsequent experience adds: judicial attempts to confine the broad construction, uncertain function boundaries, and the acknowledged enforcement guidance. The retention path says those developments reflect contestable lower-court exceptions, not failure of Bramblett's basic all-branch rule. The disagreement is substantive. Statutory stare decisis has force even when no private contract or property arrangement is identified, and criminal statutes are not categorically exempt from it.

**Reliance has more than one subject.** The recommendation weakens an inference that the Executive has built an indispensable, uniformly exercised enforcement program on using §1001 for litigation statements. It does not eliminate prosecutorial reliance everywhere, establish how often the advice was followed, or prove that Hubbard knew it. Lawful reliance of advocates on procedural rules differs from reliance on permission to lie; those rules themselves require honesty. Neither side gets congressional ratification from silence. The source limits prevent an empirical conclusion that practice has been uniform in either direction.

**Institutional chill is not answered simply by condemning lies.** Knowledge, falsity, and disclosure-duty limits reduce the prospect of convicting lawful advocacy. They do not make the costs of an allegation, investigation, or disputed classification disappear. Conversely, possible chilling cannot by itself defeat clear criminal coverage: perjury, obstruction, and contempt also affect litigation incentives. The legal inquiry is whether the proposed scope is supported by the enacted terms, the relevant prior construction, and their coherent operation—not whether false pleadings are socially desirable. The Government's executive-agency adjudication example must be answered on the entity definition or a defensible statutory distinction; a general claim that all adjudication must be exempt would reach beyond the present paths.

**The manual supplies no standalone defense.** All eight decline a ground that would vacate the convictions merely because charging allegedly conflicted with guidance. This record establishes no enforceable assurance to Hubbard, estoppel, selective-prosecution claim, or prejudicial breach of a procedural entitlement. The manual's descriptive significance does not transform it into law.

**Lenity cannot silently bypass Bramblett.** Those using residual lenity must first state the treatment of the precedent that otherwise supplies the broader construction, and then identify genuine uncertainty remaining after the ordinary tools. Neither competing briefs, circuit disagreement, nor a cautious prosecution policy automatically establishes ambiguity. The operative Ratzlaf and Granderson holdings supply a confined residual rule, not a universal least-punishment command. A decision rejecting one proposed judicial-function exception also does not settle whether a court is an included entity.
