## Compact terms to carry into the eventual entry

Use the validated section 3 and the complete `runtime/OT_1994CHUNK5_KANSAS_COMPACT_SOURCE.txt` when drafting the final statute-in-terms passage. The underlying official source is 63 Stat. 145–152, reproduced at `tmp/ot1994_chunk5_c/validation_sources/arkansas_compact_63stat145.txt`. Preserve the full operative words and qualifications; these reminders are not substitutes for that text.

- Articles I, III-B and IV-A concern the defined naturally originating basin waters above the Stateline, excluding imports, together with John Martin conservation benefits. The 1980 account division is not a 60/40 riverwide Compact allocation. IV-B preserves the stated New Mexico interests. III-C and VI-B retain the official gage and Frontier/similar canal qualifications.
- Article IV-D permits future beneficial development, including the improved or prolonged functioning of existing works, subject to no material depletion in usable quantity or availability to the Compact's water users. Neither all development nor all gross flow reduction is the enacted rule.
- Article IV-C preserves the Corps' distinct flood-control and maintenance operations with their actual conditions. Article IX-A and the congressional consent preserve federal rights and jurisdiction; the Chief of Engineers is authorized to operate consistently with the Compact, subject to exceptions jointly approved by the Chief and Administration. These provisions do not supply unlimited project immunity.
- Article V-A/B's winter and summer dates, available-capacity condition, demand rights, flow thresholds and avoidance of waste remain distinct. V-C's stored-water demand rates change below 20,000 acre-feet, and extraordinary increases are permitted through the Administration. V-D's restriction on upstream calls remains subject to V-F/G.
- Article V-E requires prompt beneficial use unless the Administration authorizes downstream storage, uses the distinct release/delivery measurement points and travel-time allowances, and forbids cumulative credits/debits. V-F/G's predicted exhaustion within fourteen days, notice, cancellation/modification, resumed-storage finding, decree priorities and Kansas rights to water actually crossing the Stateline must accompany any reliance on that transition.
- Article V-H's material-depletion or adverse-effect condition, Administration finding, covered transfers/enlargements and state-proceeding notice remain together. Article VI's Colorado administration begins with its express exception for other Compact provisions.
- Article VIII-B authorizes consistent rules and procedures, with District Engineer approval for John Martin operations. Each State has one vote under VIII-D; unanimity and the separate subsequent unanimous arbitration referral are not interchangeable.
- Article VIII-G's cooperative data and gaging arrangements do not create a universal well-metering duty. The Administration may require ditch measuring devices at users' expense when it deems them necessary. VIII-H commands prompt investigation of brought violations or prejudicial acts, but permits a report of findings and recommendations after investigation if advisable. VIII-I makes factual findings prima facie, not conclusive.
- Article IX-B permits unanimous modification or termination while preserving established rights. An administrative agreement does not silently amend that condition.

The 1980 Plan likewise requires its actual qualifications if it enters a holding or explanation: the separate conservation accounts; other-water rules; the 35% transit deduction, priority for the 1,700-acre-foot Kansas reserve, and division of the excess; evaporation and spill accounting; the annual notice/termination provisions and treatment of water already stored; and the retained right to object before the Administration or a court. ## Entering-law use limits

- Nebraska supplies the distinction between enforcement and new allocation/modification, and the actual Rule 56 guidance. It does not establish that every disputed factual issue in Compact enforcement uses a particular quantum, or that silence universally creates water rights.
- Daubert separates Rule 702 reliability/fit and preliminary admissibility from ultimate persuasion and sufficiency. Its factors are nonexclusive and are not a mandatory scientific checklist for every technical judgment. Stevens's published reservation is evidence of his earlier position, not an automatic current join barrier.
- Greenwich distinguishes production from persuasion and assigns the APA's burden under the statutory scheme there. It does not itself establish an interstate Compact's burden or create an inference from data control.
- Hess directs attention to an entity's actual compact structure in the immunity setting. Its Treasury and institutional-control analysis supplies no independent Arkansas Compact interpretation or present immunity issue.
- MCI preserves delegated authority within the enacted structure. Its tariff holding is an analogy only if the final frozen grounds actually adopt it; it is not a general prohibition against consequential administration.
- The actual April releases through Harris have been read as public law. Travelers separates a reserved claim from the continued force of its reviewed final adjudication but announces no universal vacatur, injunction, or remand rule. Its particular relief supplies no Kansas remedy by analogy alone. Lopez and Harris address their actual commerce and criminal-law components, not a new rule for Compact enforcement.
