## Controlling holdings

### Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver

**Holding and operative rule:** COGSA §3(8) does not invalidate the Tokyo arbitration clause merely because the selected forum increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA does not override nonwaivable COGSA substantive protection; the statutes operate together while that protection remains available.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven participating Justices adopt this statutory coexistence ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a change in forum is not itself a surrender of the carrier obligations Congress protected. COGSA invalidates contractual relief from or lessening of specified liability; it does not guarantee the cheapest forum for enforcing those obligations. The FAA separately enforces maritime arbitration agreements. Those commands can coexist without treating either as an implied repeal or giving arbitration automatic priority over statutory rights. Mitsubishi preserves the distinction between submitting a federal claim to arbitration and prospectively waiving the protected entitlement. Indussa’s increased-cost inference for foreign courts is not adopted for this arbitration clause; its arbitration reservation matters here. Allied-Bruce, Mastrobuono and First Options retain their actual coverage, agreement and judicial-review limits, without deciding COGSA. The certified question permits rejecting the categorical foreign-arbitration objection. It permits no reopening of the excluded adhesion defense or adjudication of cargo liability. True foreign-court selection clauses, practical foreclosure on a materially different record and demonstrated substantive waiver remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mitsubishi Motors Corp. v. Soler Chrysler-Plymouth, Inc.: applies forum/substantive-right distinction and preserves its prospective-waiver reservation.
- Indussa Corp. v. S.S. Ranborg: rejects its costs inference for this arbitration clause; reserves its application to true foreign-court clauses.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson; Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton; First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan: preserve actual FAA coverage, consent, choice-of-law and review limits, without overriding COGSA.

### Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection

**Holding and operative rule:** The present record does not establish that arbitration will diminish nonwaivable COGSA protection. The district court must retain the action during arbitration; the stay neither authorizes prospective waiver nor forecloses a properly supported statutory objection through the remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices support the retained-jurisdiction rule and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an unrendered award and an uncertain choice of substantive law cannot be treated as an adjudicated surrender of federal protection. No arbitrator has yet selected the liability rule or decided the claim. The Japanese-law and stowage-defense arguments therefore do not establish actual foreign law, an arbitral finding or diminished carrier liability. Retaining the domestic action distinguishes enforcement of a forum agreement from acceptance of whatever substantive limit an award might later impose. Mitsubishi’s public-policy reservation requires confronting a proven tandem waiver when no lawful later protection remains; an already unambiguous surrender could not be cured merely by postponing review. That premise is not established here. The district court may consider a concrete statutory objection through the enforcement authority properly invoked. The Court promises neither plenary reconsideration of arbitral merits nor COGSA recovery. It determines no hypothetical award, foreign defense, final cargo liability or nondelegable-duty question on this record.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mitsubishi Motors: preserves a concrete prospective-waiver/public-policy objection while distinguishing the presently speculative reduction.
- First Options: retains the actual bounds on judicial review and agreement; does not guarantee unlimited review of arbitral merits.

COGSA’s enumerated duties and notice/suit periods do not lose protection merely because ordinary forum expense differs. The Court neither dismisses the action, rewrites the agreement, determines cargo-loss merits nor decides a future award.
