## Law of the decision

### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule forbidding admission without consent does not necessarily forbid a defendant from giving informed consent. Ricketts enforces the actual terms of a lawful agreement, while Rumery rejects categorical invalidity without dispensing with review of voluntariness and overreaching. The evidentiary Rules contain no express antiwaiver command. Their carefully limited exceptions do not themselves permit ordinary impeachment, but that does not answer the separate question of voluntary relinquishment. Congress's interest in candid negotiation deserves respect; the record does not establish that this counseled, limited agreement necessarily defeats it. Mezzanatto consulted counsel, accepted the impeachment condition, and presented no individualized claim that the acceptance was unknowing or involuntary. Predictions of universal prosecutorial abuse do not invalidate that agreement categorically. Godinez keeps competence distinct from actual informed assent, and Olano keeps intentional relinquishment distinct from failure to object. Neither supplies a universal waiver rule. The Court enforces only the agreement governing this defendant's inconsistent testimony, leaving supported validity challenges and broader uses to their own facts and legal requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Application and remedy:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver rule and the new-trial order resting on it are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the limited waiver decision; no new mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction, or sentencing ruling is ordered. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supports reversal and remand under his separately stated Government-proof requirement. Stevens and Souter would affirm the new-trial judgment. A preserved nonconstitutional error actually found remains subject to Rule 52(a), Williamson, and Kotteakos: the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt precludes harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and the ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error under Olano is not substituted for that rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

### Enhanced two-path comparison

**Limited consensual-waiver path:** The Rules provide a defendant a protection without expressly forbidding its relinquishment. Brady, 397 U.S. 742, Ricketts, 483 U.S. 1, Peretz, 501 U.S. 923, and Rumery, 480 U.S. 386, support giving effect to an informed choice within their actual scopes, rather than inferring that every mandatory protection is unwaivable. The specific agreement, counsel consultation, and impeachment-only use matter. The strongest counter is that Congress's rejection of an ordinary impeachment exception and its bargaining-protection policy could be routinely defeated if waiver becomes the price of discussion. This path distinguishes admission without assent from enforcement of a limited informed agreement; the packet establishes no coercion or prosecutorial misconduct, and speculative universal effects do not establish that this particular bargain defeats the public policy. It preserves legal challenges to actual coercion, fraud, ignorance, scope, and independent evidentiary restrictions.

**No advance bargaining-entry waiver path:** The Rules' deliberately protected discussion space and carefully enumerated exceptions reflect an institutional interest in candid negotiation, not only an individual's privilege. Brooklyn Savings, 324 U.S. 697, establishes that consent cannot always displace a protection enacted for public purposes; Rumery's public-interest limitation prevents a universal waiver analogy. The strongest counter is the absence of an express no-waiver clause and the ordinary enforceability of many constitutional and statutory waivers. This path answers that the particular prospective condition defeats the function of the protected discussion before it begins; its ground is statutory policy and structure, not an invented finding that Mezzanatto was coerced or lacked capacity. It leaves agreements at other procedural stages and other uses undecided.

**Individual validity:** Godinez (June 24, 1993), **Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver**, separates capacity from actual knowing, intelligent, voluntary assent. It does not decide this protection's waivability. Olano (April 26, 1993), **Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)**, distinguishes valid intentional relinquishment from failure to object, but expressly preserves right-specific requirements. Salerno requires actual Rule conditions and rejects independent contradiction as selective disclosure; it does not decide express consensual waiver. These rules cannot be used to shortcut the statutory fork.

The Rules exclude withdrawn guilty pleas, no-contest pleas, related plea-proceeding statements, and statements in unsuccessful or withdrawn-plea discussions with the prosecutor within their terms. The fairness exception applies when another statement from the same plea or discussion has been introduced and fairness requires contemporaneous consideration. The perjury/false-statement exception requires a criminal proceeding for that purpose and a statement made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence. Ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision concerns voluntary relinquishment of the exclusion for impeachment of Mezzanatto's inconsistent trial testimony, not a new nonconsensual exception.

The six reversing associates support enforcement absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, with supported fraud/coercion challenges considered individually. Counsel consultation and the absence of a live individualized challenge matter here; competence or a signature alone would not decide a contrary record. The separate perjury-use term is not adjudicated. No case-in-chief, derivative-use, or witness-other-than-defendant agreement is validated. Ordinary independent evidentiary restrictions and actual scope remain distinct. No empirical prediction about all future bargaining becomes a fact finding or operative test.

The final judgment removes the Ninth Circuit's categorical waiver bar and its resulting retrial order by **reversal**. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation; there is no new sentencing holding, acquittal, mandatory validity hearing, or independent new-trial direction. Stevens and Souter would affirm the retrial judgment because the preserved objection should have been sustained and the panel found the error nonharmless. Neither side converts the preserved objection into forfeiture. For any nonconstitutional error actually found, Rule 52(a)/Kotteakos governs, rather than Olano's ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error; no constitutional error is newly adjudicated.

The three-Justice broader-use concern is a confirmed rationale boundary, not an assignment of historical authorship. **Research cutoff:** immediately before January 18, 1995, subject to the actual intervening-law refresh. ## Public Projection

## Event

United States v. Mezzanatto, No. 93-1340. Argued November 2, 1994; decided January 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 998 F.2d 1452 (1993), which reversed a methamphetamine conviction and ordered a new trial because Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) categorically barred enforcement of the advance impeachment waiver. The question is whether the represented defendant could knowingly and voluntarily permit use of plea-discussion statements to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. He and counsel requested the October 17, 1991 discussion; after counsel consultation he accepted the impeachment condition. Basis: distinct waiver-proof rationales and explicit broader-use reservations.

## Holdings

### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Controlling proposition:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule forbidding admission without consent does not necessarily forbid a defendant from giving informed consent. Ricketts enforces the actual terms of a lawful agreement, while Rumery rejects categorical invalidity without dispensing with review of voluntariness and overreaching. The evidentiary Rules contain no express antiwaiver command. Their carefully limited exceptions do not themselves permit ordinary impeachment, but that does not answer the separate question of voluntary relinquishment. Congress's interest in candid negotiation deserves respect; the record does not establish that this counseled, limited agreement necessarily defeats it. Mezzanatto consulted counsel, accepted the impeachment condition, and presented no individualized claim that the acceptance was unknowing or involuntary. Predictions of universal prosecutorial abuse do not invalidate that agreement categorically. Godinez keeps competence distinct from actual informed assent, and Olano keeps intentional relinquishment distinct from failure to object. Neither supplies a universal waiver rule. The Court enforces only the agreement governing this defendant's inconsistent testimony, leaving supported validity challenges and broader uses to their own facts and legal requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Application and remedy:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver rule and the new-trial order resting on it are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the limited waiver decision; no new mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction, or sentencing ruling is ordered. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supports reversal and remand under his separately stated Government-proof requirement. Stevens and Souter would affirm the new-trial judgment. A preserved nonconstitutional error actually found remains subject to Rule 52(a), Williamson, and Kotteakos: the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt precludes harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and the ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error under Olano is not substituted for that rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

## Precedent Treatment

The Court uses Ricketts, Rumery, Brady, and Peretz within their actual consent settings, distinguishes Brooklyn Savings's particular public protection, and preserves Salerno's enacted conditions. Godinez and Olano retain separate capacity, actual-assent, and forfeiture inquiries. Williamson and Kotteakos continue to govern any preserved nonconstitutional error actually found. No universal waiver rule, ordinary nonconsensual impeachment exception, or broader-use rule is adopted.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 18, 1995, a knowing and voluntary advance agreement can waive Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) for impeachment of the defendant's own inconsistent testimony. Six Justices adopt enforcement absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, subject to supported individual challenges. The Chief's distinct Government-proof requirement remains noncontrolling. The Rules' express exceptions and independent evidentiary safeguards remain in force; other uses and fundamental nonwaivable protections are reserved.

## Separate Writings

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justices O'Connor and Breyer, joins the Court and emphasizes the limited issue before it. Impeachment follows the defendant's own decision to give inconsistent trial testimony. The Government did not seek to use the proffer as proof in its case in chief, a use that could affect the incentive for candid negotiations differently. Agreement with the limited waiver therefore supplies no answer about that different agreement or other broader uses. The concurrence preserves a boundary of the Court's holding, without imposing a separate public-interest burden or requiring another hearing on an unraised individual validity issue.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal and would remand under a distinct allocation of proof. A represented defendant may knowingly and voluntarily agree before the proffer that the Government may use his statements solely to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony. The Government invokes that waiver and controls the proposed condition, so it must prove knowing and voluntary acceptance. Representation and competence do not settle whether the actual term was understood and freely accepted; clarity, prosecutorial threats or deception, and the circumstances of the demand matter to that inquiry. He does not presume actual coercion from bargaining or require a new hearing when the existing record permits the necessary determination. Enforcement remains subject to ordinary constitutional and evidentiary safeguards.

His ground does not approve case-in-chief or sentencing use, or a waiver effectively eliminating the right to trial. Defense counsel's cross-examination, independent defense evidence, and argument of another theory do not ordinarily trigger this personal-testimony waiver. Any broader trigger would require a separate explicit agreement and a distinct validity analysis, neither of which is decided. Existing rule-based perjury, false-statement, and comparable-abuse exceptions retain their own prerequisites. He therefore joins the judgment rejecting categorical nonwaivability while declining the Court's affirmative-challenge presumption. His Government-proof requirement is not the Court's controlling allocation.

Justice Souter, joined by Justice Stevens, would affirm the Ninth Circuit's new-trial judgment. General waiver doctrine does not resolve whether Congress protected plea discussions partly for the judicial system's benefit. Brooklyn Savings demonstrates that private consent cannot always defeat a public statutory policy, and these Rules deliberately encourage candid bargaining through enumerated, limited exceptions. Allowing an advance waiver to become the price of entering discussions can remove the protection before its function begins. Peretz's consent to allocation of a trial function and ordinary evidentiary waiver do not decide that distinct statutory design. The dissent neither denies all criminal waivers nor finds Mezzanatto personally coerced. It would sustain the preserved objection, and the lower court already found the admission nonharmless; its result is a new trial rather than acquittal or a rule of automatic reversal for every violation.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment and the retrial direction based on categorical nonwaivability are reversed. The case returns for ordinary lawful implementation of the bounded impeachment decision. The Court orders no additional validity hearing, automatic retrial, acquittal, or sentencing change. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand with enforcement conditioned on Government proof of knowing and voluntary acceptance; Stevens and Souter would affirm the existing new-trial judgment.
