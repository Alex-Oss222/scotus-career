## Controlling holdings

### The Table onset route requires the first manifestation of the injury

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant using the applicable Table's onset route must establish that the first symptom or manifestation of onset of the claimed Table injury occurred within the prescribed period after vaccination; for the DPT encephalopathy claim here, that period is three days. A later manifestation of the same already manifested injury does not satisfy first onset, but no finding that Maggie had that injury before vaccination is made or insulated from review by this construction.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that first onset means the beginning of the claimed injury, not simply its first manifestation after vaccination. The statutory timing requirement would lose its work if any later symptom of the same injury sufficed. Gardner requires respect for an enacted condition while preserving the protection that follows its satisfaction. The Table relieves a qualifying claimant of proving the vaccine's actual causal mechanism; it does not eliminate the qualifying onset requirement. The lower court's comparison with the residual-seizure provision does not overcome the first-onset language. Nor does the separate unrelated-factor defense require the contrary reading: a prior cause need not have produced an earlier manifestation of the Table injury. Significant aggravation remains a separate route for an injury already present. The Court therefore reverses the compensation direction without deciding whether the master's preexisting-injury finding is factually sound. A small head circumference or other prior abnormality alone does not establish the identity, onset, cause, or aggravation of this child's injury.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, December 12, 1994: applies respect for distinct statutory conditions without inserting a universal actual-causation requirement into the Table route; its veterans-benefits rule is not transplanted.
- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., June 28, 1993: leaves expert-evidence admissibility separate from the Vaccine Act's entitlement predicates; no new admissibility determination is made.

### Preserve significant aggravation and the separate rebuttal stage on remand

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualifying significant-aggravation claim and an otherwise available actual-causation claim remain distinct from first onset. The claimant's statutory entitlement showing by a preponderance precedes the Secretary's separate burden to establish an unrelated factor by a preponderance; the disputed preexisting-injury finding, preserved alternative routes, and consolidated new-evidence proceeding return for lawful consideration without an award, final denial, or hypothetical rebuttal ruling.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct rationale votes on the sequence and bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that correcting the onset construction does not decide the family's remaining entitlement. Congress separately provided significant aggravation and actual causation, and the lower courts must consider properly preserved routes under their own requirements. Greenwich Collieries distinguishes persuasion, presumptions, and separately allocated defenses; the Vaccine Act itself supplies the governing allocations here, not the APA. The Secretary's unrelated-factor burden arises only after the necessary claimant showing. Section 13's exclusion of idiopathic, unexplained, unknown, hypothetical, and undocumentable causes from that defense does not dispense with the initial predicate. Conversely, a prior abnormality cannot automatically defeat compensation or shift the Secretary's burden to the claimant. The Court does not decide whether a specifically identified underlying condition may qualify as unrelated although its ultimate origin is unknown. That dispute and the sufficiency of any rebuttal are premature without a qualifying claim. The new-evidence procedural component likewise returns without an invented mootness or Rule 60 determination. The lawful remand preserves factual review and statutory alternatives, not a predetermined medical outcome.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Director, OWCP v. Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: applies only the distinction among persuasion, presumptions, and separately allocated defenses; the Vaccine Act's own burdens govern.
- Brown v. Gardner, December 12, 1994: preserves the distinction between an eligibility condition and additional grounds not supplied by the statute; no compensation follows automatically from removing a legal error.

**Operative qualifications and nonreach:** Section 13(a)(2)(A) excludes any idiopathic, unexplained, unknown, hypothetical, or undocumentable cause, factor, injury, illness, or condition from unrelated factors. That provision is distinct from §14(b)(3)(B), which excludes an encephalopathy from the Table when a preponderance establishes that its cause is infection, toxins, trauma, or metabolic disturbances; those specified Table causes were not asserted by the Secretary in this appeal. No application of either provision is decided. The August 2, 1990 petition uses §2114(a) and (b) as in effect February 8, 1995: the March 10, 1995 revision applies only to petitions filed on or after March 10. The earlier Table and its qualifications both remain applicable. No new definition of significant aggravation, final preexisting-injury finding, actual-causation determination, or new-evidence merits ruling is adopted.

A qualifying claimant retains the statutory Table benefit without proving a vaccine mechanism; the Secretary retains the assigned preponderance rebuttal burden subject to the actual unrelated-factor exclusions. This decision corrects the onset construction without deciding medical entitlement or every rebuttal issue. The 1990 petition remains under the Table and qualifications effective February 8, 1995.

## Separate Writings

The Court issues one unanimous opinion. Its common reservations preserve factual review, significant aggravation, and the unresolved rebuttal question.

## Procedure After Action

The matter returns to the Federal Circuit for review of the preexisting-injury finding, the preserved significant-aggravation claim and any other properly preserved entitlement route under the correct statute, together with lawful consideration of the new-evidence procedural component. Any further special-master proceedings must preserve the statutory burdens and applicable earlier Table. No final compensation award, denial, new-evidence ruling, or hypothetical rebuttal decision is ordered.
