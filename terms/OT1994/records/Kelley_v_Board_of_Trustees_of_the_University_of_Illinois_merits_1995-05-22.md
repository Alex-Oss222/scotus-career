#### Case-specific reasoning

Title IX permits institutions to address unequal athletic opportunity in an overall program. It does not require maintaining every existing men's team, nor does proportionality become a universal command to cut men. The actual budget and participation circumstances matter. Sex-conscious restructuring receives the established important-objective/substantial-relationship inquiry. It does not depend on the separate analysis governing governmental racial treatment and does not resolve whether any imaginable team elimination would be lawful.

#### Disposition, application and preserved limits

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the judgment of the Seventh Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The judgment for the University remains in force. No separate remedial instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Title IX participation opportunities

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory claim and rejects the premise that Title IX guarantees continuation of a particular men’s team.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Title IX protects equal participation opportunity, not a vested right to the continued existence of a particular varsity team. For selection of sports and participation opportunities, the institution is evaluated across the athletic program rather than through sport-for-sport symmetry. Substantial proportionality may be relevant to compliance, but it is not a quota and is not the exclusive route. Title IX does not itself require elimination of men’s teams, and a university may add, eliminate, or restructure teams for legitimate fiscal and programmatic reasons so long as its participation opportunities satisfy the statute.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title IX does not entitle athletes to preservation of a particular intercollegiate team. Participation opportunities are evaluated across the athletic program rather than by requiring a men’s and women’s counterpart in every sport, and proportionality is neither a rigid quota nor the exclusive means of compliance.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The loss of men’s swimming is a concrete injury to its athletes, but the statute does not convert that injury alone into sex discrimination. The University administered a broader program in which women remained materially underrepresented in participation opportunities. Retaining women’s swimming while eliminating men’s swimming did not itself establish that the program denied men equal athletic opportunity. The plaintiffs identify no statutory rule requiring sport-for-sport symmetry or continuation of every existing men’s team.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This holding concerns participation opportunities and selection of sports. It does not allow inequality in facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling, scholarships, or another separately regulated component to be offset by participation elsewhere. Nor does it approve a quota, make proportionality exclusive, or require an institution to reduce men’s opportunities.

#### Issue 2: Equal protection and sex-conscious athletic restructuring

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional claim because a public university’s express sex-conscious treatment is state action.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Correcting a demonstrated inequality in athletic participation opportunity is an important governmental objective. Compliance with Title IX confirms the University’s lawful remedial responsibility but is not an independent constitutional justification. The University must show a substantial relationship between the sex-conscious choice and the documented inequality. Mere numerical imbalance is insufficient by itself. The record must show a meaningful disparity, a genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring, and a real connection between preserving the underrepresented sex’s opportunities and correcting or avoiding continuation of that inequality.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public university may consider sex when restructuring a financially constrained athletic program to avoid perpetuating a demonstrated inequality in participation opportunities, but only when the sex-conscious choice is substantially related to that remedial objective. Numerical disparity alone is insufficient, and the classification may not rest on stereotype, pretext, or a measure materially broader than the demonstrated remedial need.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Women were approximately 44 percent of the undergraduate population but only 23.4 percent of intercollegiate athletes. The University faced a substantial athletic-department deficit and eliminated several programs rather than adopting a general rule that men were ineligible for athletics. Preserving women’s swimming while women remained markedly underrepresented was substantially related to avoiding further contraction of their already unequal participation opportunity. The decision did not rest on an assumption that men or women were unsuited to swimming and did not use exact numerical parity as an automatic command.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The principle is symmetrical and applies to whichever sex is demonstrably underrepresented. This case does not decide classifications based on sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status, or an individual’s eligibility for a sex-designated team.
