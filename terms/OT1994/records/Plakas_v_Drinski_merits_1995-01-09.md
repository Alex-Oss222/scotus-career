## Controlling holdings

#### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding and operative rule:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 9 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that reasonableness requires the actual encounter, not a choice among tactics imagined afterward. Graham supplies the on-scene, objective inquiry into immediate danger, resistance, flight and the surrounding circumstances, while Garner preserves the serious-threat boundary and its necessity and warning conditions. Plakas had struck an officer, threatened death and advanced with the poker raised as Drinski retreated until further retreat was blocked. Police testimony receives no conclusive credit by status, but critical review identifies no supported contradiction requiring trial. The absent photograph and the body's movement cannot furnish an invented reconstruction. Perras possessed CS repellant and explained the distance and risk to other officers; a dog was offered earlier, not shown at Drinski's side. Hunter preserves ordinary summary-judgment constraints and the supported nonmovant account. Those constraints do not require a trial on speculation. The Court rejects a least-dangerous-option mandate while leaving a properly developed claim concerning earlier unconstitutional provocation for another case.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994): preserves consideration of relevant law on a preserved immunity issue, ordinary record limits and conduct-date notice; no immunity disposition or mandatory merits-first sequence is added.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earlier-beating theory was not pleaded and lacks the asserted causal showing; the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. Gilmere's different provocation theory is neither approved nor disapproved. The Court does not decide a case in which the threat ended before the shot or a supported safe alternative makes the force unreasonable. The merits ground supplies no general immunity for tactical decisions.

#### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality's §1983 liability requires a cognizable federal deprivation attributable to municipal policy or training, the applicable culpability and a causal connection; officer personal immunity is not municipal immunity, and reasonable final force alone does not answer every independent municipal-created-danger theory. The county is entitled to summary judgment here because the actual equipment-and-training contention establishes neither a constitutional equipment-list duty nor a developed independent deprivation with the required causal and culpability showing; a purely derivative force theory also lacks a predicate when the force itself is reasonable.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this proposition and its stated qualifications. These 8 rationale votes control without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the county's own claim must be examined separately. Monell requires municipal responsibility for the federal injury, and Canton supplies the failure-to-train attribution, causation and deliberate-indifference requirements rather than a substantive guarantee of every available device. Collins likewise separates the underlying deprivation from its attribution. The estate contends that inadequate equipment or training created the dangerous conditions even if Drinski's final choice was reasonable. The record does not develop that independent wrong or the necessary causal and culpability showing. A list of possible spray, canine or other tactics does not establish a constitutional equipment standard. Heller disposes of a theory resting solely on nonexistent excessive force; it does not confer immunity against every independent municipal wrong. Leatherman rejects heightened municipal pleading, but ordinary pleading is not summary-judgment proof. The county therefore prevails on the actual insufficient theory, while a supported distinct municipal injury remains open. No personal immunity defense is transferred from Drinski to the county.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserves municipal policy/custom responsibility and causation for a federal deprivation; employment or vicarious responsibility alone is insufficient.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): applies the required failure-to-train culpability and causal connection; it does not create a substantive right to every device or training method.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992): applies the separation between deprivation and municipal attribution; its voluntary-employment holding does not decide every affirmative municipal injury.
- City of Los Angeles v. Heller, 475 U.S. 796 (1986): applies to a derivative theory resting solely on constitutionally reasonable force; it does not supply a universal bar to a distinct municipal wrong.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993): preserves ordinary municipal pleading and distinct Rule 56 proof; it neither relaxes Monell/Canton elements nor supplies personal immunity to a municipality.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the officer holding and in the judgment for the county. He emphasizes that the encounter must be assessed as a whole: earlier tactical choices remain relevant when officers unreasonably manufacture the necessity for force. That broader frame does not help this estate because the record shows resistance, an armed assault, an explicit lethal threat, surrender efforts and retreat before the close-range advance. A requirement to employ spray, a dog or another step of retreat on these facts would replace constitutional reasonableness with judicial tactical direction. His position does not protect an officer who needlessly creates the confrontation, fires after danger ends or ignores a clearly safe and available means when the circumstances make deadly force unreasonable. The Court's opinion leaves a developed police-provocation theory undecided; his more affirmative account of earlier conduct is a separate position. For the county, he relies on the absence of the asserted underlying excessive-force violation and would reject a municipal claim resting solely on that force. He does not reach an independent municipal act producing a separate constitutional injury, because none is established here, and reserves a supported training or policy claim causing such an injury. He joins the county judgment without joining the Court's separate equipment-and-training analysis.
