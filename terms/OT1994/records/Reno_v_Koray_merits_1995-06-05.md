**Holding and operative rule:** Time under a federal Bail Reform Act conditional-release order is not official detention under § 3585(b), even when the restrictions are severe, so Koray’s release period requires no conditions-based credit inquiry. This rule concerns the presented federal release/detention distinction, preserves qualifying uncredited detention and judicial review, and does not decide state custody or require physical BOP control for every creditable day.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven directly adopt the status rule and its application without an independently sufficient administrative-deference holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the credit provision must be read with the related release and detention scheme. Sections 3142(c) and 3142(e) distinguish conditional release from detention, and § 3621 supplies the imprisonment context. Restrictive conditions do not convert the established legal status of release into detention under the federal scheme. Wilson assigns initial calculation to the BOP while preserving review and forbidding duplicate credit; it does not answer the present definition by itself. Koray argues that institutional restraint can match jail, but the linked categories resolve this federal period without comparing every alleged restriction or treating allegations as findings. ## Law After Decision

For the presented federal Bail Reform Act conditional-release period, statutory release status does not become official detention under § 3585(b) merely because the conditions are severe. The mandatory credit command for qualifying detention remains intact, including detention resulting from the offense sentenced or another charge for which arrest occurred after that offense, when the time has not been credited against another sentence. The decision does not universally require physical BOP control for every creditable pretrial day and does not decide state custody. No independent administrative-deference ground or new notice holding is adopted.

## Separate Writings

Ginsburg concurs while joining the Court’s opinion in full. She reserves whether an intelligent choice between bail and detention requires notice and comprehension of the consequences for sentence credit. That claim was not presented, so she decides no notice or due-process rule and supplies no new ground for statutory credit.

Stevens dissents without joins. He reads § 3585(b)’s term official detention in light of the restraint actually imposed, rather than treating related Bail Reform Act categories as an exclusive definition for all purposes. Crandon supports considering enacted words, context and purpose before any residual lenity. Court-ordered incarceration in an institution can be official even when a release caption is used, and Wilson allocates calculation without defining qualifying detention. The institutional-control theory does not authorize credit for ordinary curfews or every treatment placement. He would affirm the opportunity for lawful findings, not enter a credit award on allegations.

Stone-Zsela dissents without joins. He would hold that compulsory confinement in an institution under a court order can qualify when equivalent to incarceration, and that the conditional-release label alone cannot defeat the claim. The credit text does not say that only custody committed to the Attorney General counts; Wilson answers who initially computes credit, while Thomas Jefferson University concerns an agency’s own regulation and MCI confines administrative action to enacted authority. Those decisions do not make a program statement conclusive. Stone distinguishes official institutional incarceration from a free-floating hardship comparison. Ordinary home detention, curfew, work-release residence or voluntary rehabilitation is not categorically incarceration. Because the full conditions remain unproved, he would affirm a prompt bounded remand for actual compulsory conditions, the qualifying period and absence of duplicate credit, with the BOP’s computation conforming to § 3585(b) subject to lawful habeas review. He would not automatically award approximately 150 days, order release, revive equality, or replace the BOP as original calculator.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit’s conditions-inquiry judgment is reversed and the matter remanded consistently with exclusion of this federal conditional-release period from § 3585(b) credit. BOP initial computation and exhausted judicial review remain available for qualifying detention. No conditions-based credit award for this release period, exact-day award, resentencing, immediate release or equality adjudication is ordered. State-custody treatment and the unpresented bail-choice notice question remain open.
