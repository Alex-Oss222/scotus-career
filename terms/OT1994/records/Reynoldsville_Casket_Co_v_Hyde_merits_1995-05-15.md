## Controlling law

### Reliance alone does not permit withholding a governing federal rule

**Holding and operative rule:** A state court must apply a controlling federal constitutional rule to a case still open on direct review and cannot withhold that rule merely because a litigant relied on the invalidated statute. Independently valid procedural or remedial rules may determine relief, but cannot reproduce selective prospectivity under a different label.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct votes; Kennedy and O'Connor concur in the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio cannot preserve the legal operation of its unconstitutional tolling provision solely for claims accruing before Bendix. Bendix establishes the interstate-commerce defect in that provision. Harper requires a federal rule applied to the original litigants to govern still-open cases within its substantive scope. Beam supplies the distinction between applying the rule and deciding an independently supported remedy. Ohio's reliance ground is tied to the date of Bendix and continues the invalid tolling rule for earlier claims. Calling that treatment a remedy does not create an independent legal basis. Reliance can matter to an actual equitable doctrine with its own lawful prerequisites; this judgment establishes none. Finality, valid procedural bars, immunity, an independently justified equitable rule or a constitutional remedial requirement can present different grounds. Reich concerns withdrawal of an apparent tax remedy and does not guarantee survival of this tort action. The Court remands without deciding whether another valid ground supports relief.
