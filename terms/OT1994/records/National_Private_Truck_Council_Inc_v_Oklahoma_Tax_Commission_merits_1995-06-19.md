## Controlling holdings

### Federal tax equity and an actually adequate legal remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 does not authorize injunctive or declaratory interference with state tax administration where an adequate legal remedy actually protects the claimant. The limitation arises from federal equitable principles informing §1983, not direct application of 28 U.S.C. §1341 to state courts, and does not restrict otherwise lawful state-law equitable relief.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this remedial rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a federal right and the relief available to enforce it are distinct questions. Dennis v. Higgins recognizes an enforceable Commerce Clause right; it does not abolish the established limits on federal tax equity. Dows, Matthews and Great Lakes supply that tradition: courts ordinarily withhold tax injunctions and parallel declarations when legal relief affords effective protection. Section 1341 directly addresses federal district courts and therefore cannot itself withdraw this state court’s jurisdiction. Nor does a State’s preference for uniform procedure defeat federal law. Here the taxpayers obtained invalidation of the discriminatory taxes and an adequate state-law refund remedy. Additional federal equity is unnecessary on that record. The rule preserves extraordinary relief for irreparable injury, a cloud on title or a genuine risk of numerous suits between the same parties involving the same law or facts. It does not impose general exhaustion, decide damages, or permit withdrawal of an actually promised refund.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Dennis v. Higgins: preserves the Commerce Clause right; distinguishes right from equitable relief.
- Dows v. Chicago; Matthews v. Rodgers; Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. v. Huffman: apply the tax-equity tradition and its adequacy and extraordinary-injury limits.
- Reich v. Collins: preserves an actually honored postpayment remedy; the successful refunds here do not require duplicate equity.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation: preserves application of governing tax law and meaningful correction, without fixing a single remedy.
- Livadas v. Bradshaw: applies the separation of federal right, enforcement channel and available relief.
- Felder v. Casey; Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguish hostile state conditions and general exhaustion from this intrinsic federal equitable limitation.

### Fees follow an available fee-eligible federal claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the invoked §1983 equitable action affords no relief on this record, §1988 supplies no fee entitlement through that action. Success on state law does not categorically defeat fees where an independently viable fee-eligible federal claim exists.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that section 1988 does not create the federal action whose availability must be established first. Maher v. Gagne permits fees in appropriate federal enforcement proceedings without an express adjudication of federal merits, including when a court resolves the dispute on another ground. The decisive defect here is different: the federal tax-equity limitation leaves no available relief under the section 1983 action the taxpayers invoked. Their state-law refund victory therefore cannot supply a fee entitlement through an unavailable federal remedial route. The Court does not hold that every state-law judgment, or every absence of an express federal merits ruling, defeats fees. No independently viable alternative federal fee-eligible claim is established in this case. The tax invalidity and refund entitlement remain undisturbed. The independent state statutory fee ground is outside review, as are a reasonable fee amount, an unpresented damages action and any case in which the legal remedy fails to afford adequate protection.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Maher v. Gagne: distinguishes an available fee-eligible action resolved without federal merits from this unavailable federal equitable action; its rule remains intact.

An inadequate remedy, including continued unconstitutional taxation after a refund judgment, may require different equity. Mere parallel claims by many taxpayers do not themselves establish the same-party multiplicity exception. The general duty of state courts to entertain §1983 is assumed rather than decided.
