## Controlling holdings

### The Commission lacks independent certiorari authority for this FECA enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 437d(a)(6)'s authority to litigate and appeal civil enforcement actions through the Commission's general counsel does not displace 28 U.S.C. §518(a)'s allocation of Supreme Court representation in this FECA corporate-contribution action. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains available, and the express certiorari powers in 26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d) remain confined to actions in which the Commission appears under those respective funding sections.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices directly adopt the rule. Stone-Zsela and Stevens dissent; Ginsburg does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress did not authorize the Commission to petition independently in this enforcement action. Providence Journal establishes that authority to conduct litigation below does not necessarily include authority to represent the Government here. Congress may create an exception, and ordinary construction determines whether it has done so. Section 437d(a)(6) gives the Commission substantial enforcement autonomy, but its general appeal language must be read with section 518(a)'s specific allocation and the express certiorari grants in the presidential-funding statutes. Those grants apply only to actions brought within their respective sections; this corporate-contribution action is not one of them. Enforcement exclusivity and the separate power to defend actions do not settle who may affirmatively invoke this Court's review. Germain permits the grants to coexist with their distinct conditions. The Court adopts neither a rule that appeal invariably excludes certiorari nor a required formula for statutory exceptions. Lawful particular-case direction remains possible; no timely direction is established here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Providence Journal Co., 485 U.S. 693 (1988): applies its separation of lower-court litigation authority from Supreme Court representation while retaining ordinary statutory construction of congressional exceptions; no special verbal formula is imposed.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249 (1992): applies its preservation of coexisting statutory routes and their conditions; enforcement authority and Supreme Court representation can operate together.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns this affirmative FECA enforcement petition. It does not deny the Commission the express powers supplied for funding litigation, construe every agency's litigation statute, or eliminate section 518(a)'s particular-case direction exception.

### Authorization after the statutory petition period does not ratify this filing

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming that the Solicitor General's May 26, 1994 authorization fully adopted the Commission's January 18 petition, it cannot relate back to validate this unauthorized invocation after the period in which an authorized petition could be filed had expired. Section 2101(c) allows ninety days and permits a Justice, for good cause, to extend the application period by no more than sixty days; the asserted authorization falls outside even that maximum under either supplied judgment date.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: six direct votes. Together with Part I, this ground requires dismissal for want of jurisdiction; it is not a constitutional merits disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that subsequent authorization cannot cure this petition after lawful filing became impossible. The Commission filed on January 18, within ninety days of the October 22 judgment, but lacked authority to invoke review for the Government. Cook permits ratification to relate back only when the principal could perform the act both when first attempted and when adopted. By May 26 the ninety-day period and even the maximum possible sixty-day extension had elapsed. Treating the October 25 amendment as the starting point would not change that conclusion; the Court finds neither a restart nor an extension. The fullest claimed authorization therefore fails without an inquiry into finer wording of the letter. Dodge concerned adoption of a seizure in a forfeiture proceeding, not this expired review period. Smith v. Barry preserves functional compliance by a timely notice document, not missing governmental authority. Ordinary timely corrections and lawful delegation are not foreclosed by this bounded decision.

**Precedent treatment:**

Because he finds no defect here, he would undertake no severability or structural-ratification inquiry. His proposed remand would not automatically revive the district court's enforcement judgment while other appellate issues remain. He distinguishes legally operative executive control from advice, observation, communication, or persuasion. Congressional officers cannot receive a vote, veto, quorum power, binding directive, or other legal authority over execution by being called nonvoting or ex officio. On the arrangement presented, the appointed Commissioners alone exercise the enforcement powers; the nonvoting officers have no identified authority to authorize, block, suspend, or direct Commission action. The operating account reported below also excludes chairmanship, meeting calls or adjournment, and quorum participation; those operating descriptions are not additional words of the statute.

Buckley concerns officers exercising significant governmental authority; Bowsher concerns executive authority lodged in an officer controlled by Congress; and the Airports Authority decision concerns a legally effective veto over operational decisions. In his view, potential influence without comparable legal power does not establish the constitutional defect those decisions forbid. An advisory label concealing approval, disapproval, delay, compulsory direction, decisive participation, or another legal consequence would present a different case. Because he finds no defect here, he would undertake no severability or structural-ratification inquiry. His proposed remand would not automatically revive the district court's enforcement judgment while other appellate issues remain. No other Justice joins these merits views, and they establish no rule of the Court.

## Procedure After Action

The existing writ is dismissed for want of jurisdiction. The lower judgment ending this enforcement action remains undisturbed; the district court's penalty and injunction are not reinstated. There is no Supreme Court merits remand or new adjudication of the unresolved enforcement defenses. This review proceeding is terminal; no additional Court act or filing condition is ordered.
