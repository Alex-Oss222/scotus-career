## Controlling law

### A household-composition definition is not this occupancy exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** The FHA exemption for reasonable restrictions regarding the maximum number of occupants permitted to occupy a dwelling does not cover Edmonds' family definition, which permits an unlimited number of related residents while limiting unrelated residents to five. The definition regulates household composition, so the absolute exemption does not foreclose the ordinary FHA discrimination and accommodation inquiry.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Breyer: six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exemption addresses crowding rather than the membership of a household. Section 3607(b)(1) preserves reasonable restrictions on the maximum number of people occupying a dwelling, while section 3604(f) supplies protection against disability discrimination and requires reasonable accommodation within its terms. Edmonds permits any number of people related by genetics, adoption or marriage, yet limits unrelated people to five. That operation identifies who may live together, not how many occupants the dwelling can safely hold. The City's strongest response is that five is a real maximum for the unrelated household. But a numerical feature does not change the subject of this rule: unlimited related occupancy remains permitted. Reading the exception in its enacted setting keeps genuine crowding controls distinct from household definitions. The Court decides only the exemption. It does not invalidate single-family zoning, find discrimination, order a particular accommodation or determine ultimate relief.
