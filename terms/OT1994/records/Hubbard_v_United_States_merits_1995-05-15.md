### II. A federal court is not an included department or agency under this statute

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the offense-date §§6 and 1001, a federal court is neither an enumerated executive department nor an agency within the statute's contextual meaning; the department definition's branch-wide contextual alternative does not extend this statute to the court, and the distinct agency definition does not do so either. Counts V–VII therefore lack the asserted court nexus, without deciding whether another Judicial Branch entity could qualify as an agency or whether an independently established nexus to a different included institution would support a different prosecution.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer: six direct votes. Stone-Zsela reaches the same count disposition under his narrower Article III/adjudicative rule but does not join this broader court-entity construction. O’Connor and Souter would retain Bramblett's contrary construction. No combination of these different grounds is needed for the six-Justice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress supplied separate definitions of department and agency, and both must be read with their own qualifications. Rowland gives an enacted default genuine force while permitting the context to do its stated work. Here the broad references to any matter and jurisdiction describe the functions of an included body; they do not independently identify which bodies are included. Rodgers construed authorized agency functions, not away the separate entity requirement. A general antifraud purpose and the inconclusive predecessor history do not establish the branch-wide department meaning here. Nor does the word authority in the agency list turn every body exercising governmental power into an agency in this setting. Section 151 identifies the bankruptcy court as a unit of the district court, without independently answering either Title 18 definition. The knowing factual falsehoods found below cannot supply the missing entity nexus. This construction resolves these court filings without creating a judicial-function exception, classifying the trustee, or deciding the status of every Judicial Branch institution.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Rowland v. California Men's Colony, decided January 12, 1993: applied for an enacted definition operating subject to a genuine contextual qualification; its association-eligibility holding supplies no categorical result under a different statute.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: preserved; jurisdiction includes the authorized functions of an included agency. Footnote 4 reserved the judicial-function question and did not decide these filings or eliminate the entity requirement.
- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: its incompatible all-branch construction is expressly overruled in Part III, not avoided as merely an unnecessary judicial passage.
- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: consistent with enforcing enacted conditions without adding a category the statute does not establish.
- Heintz v. Jenkins, decided April 18, 1995: distinguished in statutory setting; rejection of an added FDCPA litigation exemption does not resolve §1001's entity definition or supply an exception here.

### III. Bramblett's incompatible all-branch construction is overruled

**Holding and operative rule:** Bramblett's construction of department in §1001 to include the executive, legislative, and judicial branches is overruled, including the rationale supporting its legislative-branch result. The decision does not adjudicate the independent House Disbursing Office agency or Treasury-nexus theories Bramblett left open, classify every other judicial entity, or alter §287's separate false-claims coverage.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer: six votes for this express precedent treatment. Stone-Zsela supports overruling only to the extent stated in his narrower concurrence and does not join the broader disposition of Bramblett. The six associates do not share a controlling extended stare decisis framework: Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer adopt one account; Scalia and Kennedy another; Thomas joins neither complete account. No Marks synthesis supplies a further rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Bramblett's treatment of department cannot remain the governing interpretation alongside the construction adopted today. It considered §6 and the predecessor statutes; the defect is its expansion of the definition, not failure to notice those materials. Its actual dispute involved representations to the House Disbursing Office, but its reason for coverage treated all three branches alike. Calling the reference to courts unnecessary would leave that incompatible interpretive reason in force. The Court therefore rejects the all-branch construction expressly, including the rationale supporting the legislative result. Bramblett declined to decide whether the office separately qualified as an authority or whether Treasury jurisdiction supplied another nexus. Those questions do not become resolved by today's rejection of its chosen ground. Rodgers's meaning of jurisdiction for an included agency remains intact. Six Justices agree on this construction and express precedent treatment, although their additional explanations for departing from statutory precedent differ. The decision establishes this overruling, not a general standard assembled from those separate explanations.

Correcting the entity boundary preserves their essential protection while dispensing with a function line the enacted language does not contain.

The statute's definitions and actual context supply the first-instance construction, but disagreement with Bramblett is not the entire precedent inquiry. Hilton and Allied-Bruce show why statutory precedent has institutional force and why settled arrangements matter. Here the combination of the particular definitional defect, the entrenched limiting development, and the qualified reliance record supports correction. The parties agree that prosecution guidance favored other charges in the described court-statement setting, but differ about its scope. That evidence weakens an assertion of indispensable, uniform reliance on litigation prosecutions; it proves neither total nonenforcement nor Hubbard's personal reliance. The Government's account of the petition's narrower focus does not transform this inquiry into review of another judgment. Independent Insurance permits consideration of necessary antecedent governing law with a fair opportunity for adversarial presentation; it does not impose a duty to decide every omitted issue. Yee preserves the difference between a supporting argument and an independent question. Izumi refused to reach a distinct intervention denial, while Caspari permitted its confined subsidiary defense and Madsen required its own supported basis for exceptional review. None makes later briefing sufficient by itself. The entity inquiry is also the premise of the proposed judicial-function exception. The circuit's separate Rule 12 ruling resolved the failure-to-charge objection below; it did not decide this Court's question-presentation issue. The Court reaches coverage without reopening other convictions or unrelated claims.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido, decided April 1, 1992: applied; new supporting arguments and independently presented questions remain distinct.

### II. A federal court is not an included department or agency under this statute

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the offense-date §§6 and 1001, a federal court is neither an enumerated executive department nor an agency within the statute's contextual meaning; the department definition's branch-wide contextual alternative does not extend this statute to the court, and the distinct agency definition does not do so either. Counts V–VII therefore lack the asserted court nexus, without deciding whether another Judicial Branch entity could qualify as an agency or whether an independently established nexus to a different included institution would support a different prosecution.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer: six direct votes. Stone-Zsela reaches the same count disposition under his narrower Article III/adjudicative rule but does not join this broader court-entity construction. O’Connor and Souter would retain Bramblett's contrary construction. No combination of these different grounds is needed for the six-Justice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress supplied separate definitions of department and agency, and both must be read with their own qualifications. Rowland gives an enacted default genuine force while permitting the context to do its stated work. Here the broad references to any matter and jurisdiction describe the functions of an included body; they do not independently identify which bodies are included. Rodgers construed authorized agency functions, not away the separate entity requirement. A general antifraud purpose and the inconclusive predecessor history do not establish the branch-wide department meaning here. Nor does the word authority in the agency list turn every body exercising governmental power into an agency in this setting. Section 151 identifies the bankruptcy court as a unit of the district court, without independently answering either Title 18 definition. The knowing factual falsehoods found below cannot supply the missing entity nexus. This construction resolves these court filings without creating a judicial-function exception, classifying the trustee, or deciding the status of every Judicial Branch institution.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Rowland v. California Men's Colony, decided January 12, 1993: applied for an enacted definition operating subject to a genuine contextual qualification; its association-eligibility holding supplies no categorical result under a different statute.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: preserved; jurisdiction includes the authorized functions of an included agency. Footnote 4 reserved the judicial-function question and did not decide these filings or eliminate the entity requirement.
- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: its incompatible all-branch construction is expressly overruled in Part III, not avoided as merely an unnecessary judicial passage.
- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: consistent with enforcing enacted conditions without adding a category the statute does not establish.
- Heintz v. Jenkins, decided April 18, 1995: distinguished in statutory setting; rejection of an added FDCPA litigation exemption does not resolve §1001's entity definition or supply an exception here.

### III. Bramblett's incompatible all-branch construction is overruled

**Holding and operative rule:** Bramblett's construction of department in §1001 to include the executive, legislative, and judicial branches is overruled, including the rationale supporting its legislative-branch result. The decision does not adjudicate the independent House Disbursing Office agency or Treasury-nexus theories Bramblett left open, classify every other judicial entity, or alter §287's separate false-claims coverage.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer: six votes for this express precedent treatment. Stone-Zsela supports overruling only to the extent stated in his narrower concurrence and does not join the broader disposition of Bramblett. The six associates do not share a controlling extended stare decisis framework: Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer adopt one account; Scalia and Kennedy another; Thomas joins neither complete account. No Marks synthesis supplies a further rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Bramblett's treatment of department cannot remain the governing interpretation alongside the construction adopted today. It considered §6 and the predecessor statutes; the defect is its expansion of the definition, not failure to notice those materials. Its actual dispute involved representations to the House Disbursing Office, but its reason for coverage treated all three branches alike. Calling the reference to courts unnecessary would leave that incompatible interpretive reason in force. The Court therefore rejects the all-branch construction expressly, including the rationale supporting the legislative result. Bramblett declined to decide whether the office separately qualified as an authority or whether Treasury jurisdiction supplied another nexus. Those questions do not become resolved by today's rejection of its chosen ground. Rodgers's meaning of jurisdiction for an included agency remains intact. Six Justices agree on this construction and express precedent treatment, although their additional explanations for departing from statutory precedent differ. The decision establishes this overruling, not a general standard assembled from those separate explanations.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: expressly overruled as to its incompatible all-branch department construction, including the rationale supporting its legislative result; no new ruling resolves the independent agency or Treasury theories it declined to decide.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: retained at its authorized-function jurisdiction holding and express reservation of the judicial-function issue; its citation of Bramblett does not preserve the displaced entity construction.
- Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission, decided December 16, 1991: its retained FELA construction and specialized reliance setting remain intact; the separate writings explain its significance without creating a new majority stare decisis framework here.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, decided January 18, 1995: its retention of Southland and its actual contractual setting remain intact; disagreement about this precedent does not displace that holding.

### IV. Reverse only the affirmance of Counts V–VII and remand to vacate them

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's affirmance of Counts V, VI, and VII is reversed, and the case is remanded for lawful proceedings to vacate those §1001 convictions on the asserted court-nexus ground. Counts I–IV and VIII–X remain undisturbed by this review; no exact prison, probation, supervised-release, fine, immediate-release, general resentencing, retrial, or new-prosecution command follows, and any ordinary implementation requires lawful proceedings and the actual criminal judgment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer: seven votes for the exact count-specific mandate. Stone-Zsela's narrower coverage and precedent position supplies his vote; he does not join Parts II–III. O’Connor and Souter would affirm these three convictions. The other seven convictions receive no new merits adjudication.

**Controlling explanation:** Count V concerns the account of records supplied to a former trustee, while Counts VI and VII concern denials about the locations of machinery and parts. These findings of factual falsehood are not set aside as unproved or converted into permissible legal argument. The defect is the statutory court nexus supporting these three convictions. Their affirmance must therefore be reversed and the convictions vacated through the implementing remand. This disposition does not reach the separate bankruptcy-fraud and mail-fraud counts or the rejected ineffective-assistance claim. Nor does it require a decision on the Government's administrative-asset-collection characterization, which the circuit left open. The Court does not adopt a function exception as the common basis of its mandate. The descriptions of Count X's sentence differ, and the criminal judgment is necessary before any exact sentencing consequence can be determined. The remand accordingly commands removal of V–VII, not release, a new trial, a specified sentence, or a new prosecution.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: no longer supports these court-based §1001 convictions; the seven mandate votes differ in the broader extent of their precedent treatment as stated in Part III and Stone-Zsela's concurrence.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: its jurisdiction holding does not supply a new entity nexus or an independent basis to affirm these convictions.

## Law After Decision

Under the offense-date law, the court is neither a department nor an agency within §§6 and 1001. The department contextual alternative and agency qualification remain real and distinct; no blanket conclusion about every Judicial Branch body or trustee follows. Bramblett's inconsistent all-branch construction no longer governs, including the rationale supporting its legislative result. Six Justices directly support those holdings. No additional entity nexus or outcome under an independent agency theory is established. Rodgers's jurisdiction rule operates only once an included institution is identified. The decision creates neither a right to lie nor an independent judicial-function or private-litigation exception. Other enacted offenses retain their scope. Seven Justices require vacatur of V–VII alone. No extended stare decisis account, generic Marks synthesis, or independently sufficient lenity alternative changes current doctrine.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer.** These Justices join the Court's construction, full Bramblett treatment, and count-specific remedy. They supply an additional case-specific explanation for correcting the precedent. The judicial-function decisions have become an entrenched legal development attempting to protect adversarial proceedings from Bramblett's broad language. Those lower decisions do not authorize vertical disobedience or themselves overrule this Court. Their sustained development nevertheless deserves respect in assessing the consequences of the original construction. Correcting the entity boundary preserves their essential protection while dispensing with a function line the enacted language does not contain.

The statute's definitions and actual context supply the first-instance construction, but disagreement with Bramblett is not the entire precedent inquiry. Hilton and Allied-Bruce show why statutory precedent has institutional force and why settled arrangements matter. Here the combination of the particular definitional defect, the entrenched limiting development, and the qualified reliance record supports correction. The parties agree that prosecution guidance favored other charges in the described court-statement setting, but differ about its scope. That evidence weakens an assertion of indispensable, uniform reliance on litigation prosecutions; it proves neither total nonenforcement nor Hubbard's personal reliance. Congress's silence is not ratification. Crandon supports attention to criminal statutory setting and fair warning, without supplying a rule that every criminal precedent may be discarded.

The ordinary knowledge, falsity, and disclosure-duty requirements protect lawful advocacy but do not remove every cost of threatened prosecution or disputed classification. These Justices recognize that concern without finding an empirically established chilling effect or a right to lie. They adopt no independent lenity holding: genuine residual uncertainty after ordinary construction and precedent treatment is not an operative premise of this decision. Their complete explanation has three votes and is not the Court's general stare decisis standard.

**Scalia, concurring, joined by Kennedy.** These Justices also join the Court's construction, express overruling, and precise remedy. They reject treating the lower courts' limiting decisions as a competing body of authority entitled to displace this Court's statutory construction. Those cases instead demonstrate the serious litigation risks generated over time by the erroneous expansion and the absence of a rational repair grounded in the text. That combination supplies the reason beyond statutory error for overruling here. It would be incoherent to preserve Bramblett's legislative rationale by treating its judicial language as dispensable, because the same all-branch construction produced both.

Patterson requires a special justification; neither Kennedy's Hilton opinion nor his Allied-Bruce join imposes unconditional retention of an erroneous criminal construction. Scalia's Crandon concurrence, which Kennedy joined, takes cautious legal advice and inconsistent executive practice seriously but expressly distinguishes such practice from established judicial precedent. The additional justification here must therefore come from the demonstrated legal difficulties and consequences, not from treating the prosecutor's recommendation as law. The guidance remains qualified, and no assured restraint, universal nonenforcement, or personal reliance is found.

Ordinary offense elements reduce the risk to legitimate adversarial work without logically eliminating investigation and classification costs. Conversely, concern about litigation costs alone cannot invalidate an enacted crime. These Justices conclude that this erroneous construction can be retained only by irrational or textless limitations, and that the statutory boundary should be corrected directly. They do not adopt an independently sufficient lenity ground or a policy-made judicial-function exception. Their complete additional account has two votes. Thomas joins neither this framework nor the competing three-Justice account; his joins remain the Court's statutory construction and express precedent treatment, with practical concerns corroborative rather than independently controlling.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The Chief joins the conclusion that the question presented may be reached and joins the count-specific mandate. He would overrule Bramblett only to the extent it treats an Article III court acting in its adjudicative capacity as a department or agency under §1001. He applies that rule to these adversarial bankruptcy filings and would vacate the resulting §1001 convictions. Section 151 identifies the bankruptcy court as a unit of the district court; that institutional relationship accompanies his application without a new classification of bankruptcy judges as Article III judges or of every Judicial Branch body as a court. His characterization of these filings is his legal application, not a finding that the circuit resolved the Government's administrative-asset-collection theory.

Patterson, Square D, and Illinois Brick give statutory stare decisis unusual force because Congress can amend the law. A different preferred first reading is insufficient. The required special justification is the combination present here: Bramblett's actual facts involved the House, its broad definition was extended to adjudicating courts despite Congress's express statutory usage, and decades of attempts to carve a judicial-function exception show that its broadest language cannot be administered without exceptions the text does not supply. Those lower decisions do not displace Bramblett by attrition. They demonstrate the unworkability of retaining its broadest formulation. A direct correction of the statutory boundary is more candid and administrable than continuing textless exceptions.

Concrete reliance on applying §1001 to adversarial court filings is slight, while perjury, contempt, bankruptcy fraud, obstruction, and other enacted offenses remain available on their own terms. The Chief creates no immunity for falsehoods to executive agencies merely because litigation is pending and does not affect statutes expressly covering court filings or sworn statements. Congress remains free expressly to extend a false-statement offense to all three branches and define safeguards and exceptions appropriate to adversarial proceedings. He supplies no sentence-wide relief and leaves unaffected counts and proceedings subject to the lower court's judgment. He does not join the associates' broader entity rule, their full displacement of Bramblett, or either complete extended stare decisis account. His narrower formulation has his vote alone.

**O’Connor, dissenting, joined by Souter.** These Justices join the Court's conclusion and complete analysis that the entity question is properly presented, but would affirm Counts V–VII. Section 6 expressly allows context to expand the ordinary department meaning. Bramblett considered that provision and the statute's history, and its construction has the institutional force of a statutory precedent Congress may revise. Their disagreement is not a denial of the force of the ordinary definition; it concerns whether the reasons now offered suffice to unsettle a construction already made. O’Connor's Allied-Bruce concurrence and Souter's Hilton and Allied-Bruce joins distinguish a preferred initial reading from the duty to treat statutory precedent seriously. Their Rowland dissent likewise gives actual context substantive work.

They acknowledge the prosecution guidance and the possible burden on legitimate advocacy. The recommendation is neither irrelevant nor binding, and its qualified scope does not establish abandonment of the statute. The difficulties of an added judicial-function exception do not demonstrate that the basic retained coverage rule is unworkable; refusing that extra exception can produce a coherent rule. Neither Congress's silence nor assured prosecutorial restraint supplies an additional ground.

On the retained construction, the proposed judicial-function and private-litigation exclusions lack an enacted basis. Rodgers supports authorized-function coverage but expressly reserved the judicial-function question; it did not already decide the exception's validity. Heintz is a limited analogy against adding a litigation category, not an answer to this criminal statute. The actual counts involve knowing factual falsehoods, not a not-guilty plea or a mere demand for proof. Knowledge and falsity requirements, and a disclosure duty for concealment, protect lawful conduct without eliminating every process cost. These Justices adopt no administrative classification of the filings, no universal legal-knowledge element, and no independent lenity ground. Their affirmance reaches only the three challenged convictions.
