## Controlling holdings

### Congressional agents may not control the legal effectiveness of airport decisions

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may advise, oversee, obtain reports and enact a fixed waiting period, but may not vest a congressional agent with selective, legally operative control over execution outside the constitutional allocation of power. The revised Board is such an agent and its ability to trigger an additional restraint on covered Authority actions makes the challenged control provisions unconstitutional; mere influence, nominations considered alone, consultation, reporting and nonvoting attendance are not independently invalidated.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt the congressional-agency-plus-operative-control ground. Stone's independent Article I ground is not an additional Court holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that removing the old Board's formal veto did not remove its legally operative control. Citizens for the Abatement of Aircraft Noise requires a functional examination of congressional agency and the federal power exercised. Mandatory leadership lists constrain selection even though the Authority formally appoints the members and may request additional names or remove members for cause. Bowsher forbids Congress's agents from executing the law, and Buckley preserves constitutional accountability for governmental power. Here a recommendation that the Authority declines to accept triggers sixty legislative days during which a covered decision cannot proceed. The Authority therefore lacks the freedom that it retains when Members merely offer advice. The contention that the Board only creates time for lawful legislation overlooks that selective statutory restraint. Chadha supplies the constitutional process for Congress's responsive prohibition; it does not make every enacted reporting interval invalid. The Court does not decide the separate Appointments Clause, incompatibility or ineligibility grounds, nor prohibit ordinary interbranch communication.

**Precedent treatment:** Citizens for the Abatement of Aircraft Noise, 501 U.S. 252, is applied to the revised agency and retained operative control. Bowsher v. Synar is applied to congressional execution. Buckley v. Valeo supplies constitutional accountability for governmental power without independently deciding appointments here. INS v. Chadha preserves bicameralism and presentment for responsive congressional prohibition; no broader independent Article I holding is adopted.

### The enacted contingency limits the remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** When a judicial order prevents the Board from carrying out its statutory functions, 49 U.S.C.App. §2456(h) withdraws the Authority’s power thereafter to perform actions required to be submitted to that Board. The restraint is affirmed subject to that enacted consequence: unaffected functions, the lease and compact survive, past Board actions are not automatically invalidated, and severance cannot authorize prohibited covered actions.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt the precise statutory remedial limit.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that effective structural relief must follow the statute's own contingency. The defect lies in congressional legal control, so the existence of the interstate Authority, compact and lease does not itself become unlawful. But Congress expressly addressed the consequence of a judicial order disabling the Board. Section 2456(h) removes the Authority's power to take the actions that must be submitted to that Board. A general appeal to continued administration cannot erase that limitation or turn prohibited decisions into unaffected ordinary operations. Citizens for the Abatement of Aircraft Noise identifies the constitutional defect; it supplies no authority to disregard this later statutory command. The reviewed judgment also expressly protects past Board actions against automatic invalidation, leaving particular reliance, finality and retrospective issues for their proper proceedings. This distinction answers the contention that affirmance must dismantle every airport function. The Court grants no new stay and authorizes no further unconstitutional control. Its affirmance preserves the existing judicial stay's actual terms until their applicable final-disposition condition operates.

**Precedent treatment:** Citizens for the Abatement of Aircraft Noise is applied to the constitutional defect, not extended into permission to ignore §2456(h). The earlier FEC v. NRA jurisdictional disposition supplies no merits or severability rule.

Members must have aviation and airport-user experience, frequently use the metropolitan airports and be registered voters outside Virginia, Maryland and the District of Columbia. The Authority appoints from mandatory Speaker and President-pro-tempore lists, may request additional names but cannot select outside them, and may remove a member for cause by a two-thirds directors’ vote. Covered actions include adoption or amendment of an annual budget; bond authorization and an annual bond plan or its amendments; adoption, amendment or repeal of a regulation; adoption or revision of a master plan; appointment of the chief executive; award of a directors-approved contract; approval or modification of terminal design or airport layout; and authorization to acquire or dispose of land or grant a long-term easement. The recommendation period is the longer of thirty calendar days or ten legislative days. Silence through that period permits immediate effectiveness on expiration; earlier notice that no recommendation will be made permits immediate effectiveness then. Acceptance permits the proposed action with the recommended changes; rejection triggers sixty legislative days of review. Legislative days exclude Saturdays, Sundays, holidays, and days neither House sits because of sine-die adjournment or recess or adjournment longer than three days. Congressional prohibition must proceed by constitutional lawmaking. No general urgent-and-compelling-circumstances override exists. More than four vacancies after congressional lists have been supplied independently disables covered actions under §2456(f)(2)(D). Those statutory branches do not eliminate the selective control or the §2456(h) remedial consequence.
