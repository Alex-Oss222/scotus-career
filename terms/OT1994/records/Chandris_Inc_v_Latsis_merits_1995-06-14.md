## Controlling holdings

### Contribution and substantial employment-related vessel connection

**Controlling proposition:** Jones Act seaman status requires work contributing to a vessel's function or mission and an employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation or identifiable fleet that is substantial in both duration and nature. The actual assignment governs; a job label or isolated moment aboard is not conclusive, and a genuine change of essential assignment is assessed on the new position rather than mechanically diluted by earlier shoreside service.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined at this level by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six support the general connection rule. Stevens, Thomas and Breyer concur only in the judgment on their different actual-crew approach.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that contribution and substantial employment connection are distinct requirements. Wilander removes any requirement that a seaman perform navigational duties, while retaining the line between a vessel's workforce and land-based employees incidentally aboard. Gizoni rejects occupational labels and voluntary compensation as conclusive substitutes for that employment inquiry. Nature and duration together identify whether the relationship ordinarily exposes the worker to the vessel's work as part of its company. The lower court's duration-or-nature formula does not preserve both dimensions. The parties stipulated that Latsis contributed to the mission, but disputed the nature and extent of his shipboard assignment. Neither that stipulation nor his presence on the injury voyage settles the remaining connection. A genuinely new essential assignment is evaluated on its own activities; prior shoreside years cannot mechanically defeat a newly established shipboard role. Conversely, later employment cannot retroactively create a relationship absent when injury occurred. The Court leaves actual status to proper factual evaluation and decides neither negligence nor damages.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McDermott International, Inc. v. Wilander, 498 U.S. 337 — applied and clarified: mission contribution is necessary but does not eliminate substantial employment connection.
- Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni — applied to reject categorical occupational exclusions and preserve genuine connection disputes; conclusive-record summary judgment remains possible.

### A nonabsolute temporal guide for ordinary assignments

**Controlling proposition:** For an ordinary employment arrangement, about 30 percent of service in connection with a vessel in navigation supplies a rule of thumb for substantial duration, not an inflexible cutoff or jurisdictional condition. Justified departures and genuinely changed essential assignments remain possible; courts may resolve status as a matter of law only when the properly assessed facts permit one reasonable conclusion.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, this temporal-guide discussion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; five directly support it. Stone-Zsela does not join this discussion. No judgment-concurrence vote is aggregated into its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a temporal guide may make the duration inquiry usable without replacing the actual employment relationship. Wilander supplies the statutory distinction between seamen and land-based workers; it does not provide a numerical answer to every arrangement. Gizoni preserves factfinding when work and connection remain genuinely disputed, and leaves room for judgment on a conclusive record. The approximate proportion reflects the practical difference between substantial vessel service and incidental presence in an ordinary assignment. Its purpose fails if the number becomes an automatic bar regardless of a worker's newly established essential duties or other justified circumstances. Nature remains an independent dimension, so passing the guide does not prove seaman status and falling below it is not invariably dispositive. The competing estimates of Latsis's vessel time are not reconciled by selecting one as fact. The Court accordingly preserves ordinary judicial definition and genuine jury application, rather than converting a useful guide into an immutable threshold or deciding this worker's ultimate status.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wilander — clarified through a practical nonabsolute guide to duration; mission contribution remains separately necessary.
- Gizoni — preserved: disputed status ordinarily requires factual resolution, while conclusive-record judgment remains open.

### The preserved categorical drydock exclusion requires a new trial

**Controlling proposition:** Temporary repair does not invariably withdraw a vessel from navigation, although major reconstruction can do so on a sufficient record; withdrawn-vessel time is not automatically qualifying navigation service but may illuminate the actual employment relationship. Here the record did not justify a single categorical navigation conclusion and the instruction excluded the drydock period for every purpose, prejudicing the preserved status issue and requiring a new trial without deciding ultimate status or negligence.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined on this preserved instructional holding by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six support this rationale. The three judgment concurrences supply no full-framework join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the preserved drydock objection independently supports retrial. Senko and Butler reject treating ordinary immobility or repair as a mechanical withdrawal from navigation; they do not establish that a major conversion is temporary repair. Galileo underwent substantial reconstruction, including removed bottom plates and propellers, engine work and passenger-space rebuilding. A properly developed conclusive record could therefore support withdrawal as a matter of law. This record supplied no adequate findings or stipulations making the categorical instruction unavoidable. The instruction also barred consideration of the period for every purpose, confusing qualifying navigation time with evidence of the nature and continuity of an assignment. Later work may support or undermine an inference about the earlier relationship without creating retroactive status. Because the verdict addressed status alone, that broad exclusion materially affected the contested issue. Rules 51 and 61 require a preserved, prejudicial error; the specific drydock objection supplies it. The Court need not excuse every unpreserved objection to the broader charge or ratify that charge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Senko v. LaCrosse Dredging Corp., 352 U.S. 370 — applied against mechanical repair/immobility exclusions; no finding that every reconstructed vessel remains in navigation follows.
- Butler v. Whiteman, 356 U.S. 271 — applied to genuine navigation/status fact disputes, not to eliminate conclusive-record rulings.
- Wilander and Gizoni — applied to distinguish contribution, connection and factual status from the occurrence of an injury aboard.
