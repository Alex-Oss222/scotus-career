## Controlling holdings

### Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing

**Holding and operative rule:** A bidder able and ready to compete suffers a personal injury when a governmental racial preference denies equal competitive opportunity; it need not prove that it would have received the contract. Prospective relief also requires a real, sufficiently imminent renewed exposure to that preference, which Adarand's repeated bidding and continuing opportunity to encounter the challenged incentive establish here.

**Authority:** Standing portion of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt the holding and its reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that unequal opportunity to compete is an injury even when the award remains uncertain. Northeastern Florida identifies the governmental barrier, rather than proof of the award that would follow its removal, as the relevant competitive injury. Adarand submitted the low bid, but the prime contractor selected a certified disadvantaged business in response to the financial incentive. The contractor could decline that incentive; its optional character does not remove its effect on competitive opportunity. Lujan requires the connection to be concrete and the requested relief appropriate to a continuing or threatened injury. Adarand's recurring bids and the likelihood of encountering the challenged clause again satisfy that prospective requirement on this record. The Court does not infer a guaranteed future award, an entitlement to damages, or a waiver of sovereign immunity. Nor does accepting this competitive injury establish that the challenged preference is unconstitutional; justification remains a separate question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville (June 14, 1993): applies its equal-opportunity injury rule; no guaranteed contract award is required.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): retains concrete injury, causal connection and stage-appropriate proof; prospective relief requires sufficiently imminent renewed exposure.

### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding and operative rule:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a common equality obligation requires substantial justification for racial treatment by federal, state and local government. The incentive burdens an opportunity to compete even though the prime may decline it; that burden requires justification, not automatic invalidation. Corrective purpose matters because overcoming supported exclusion differs from maintaining subordination, but purpose alone cannot establish validity. Evidence must connect credit, bonding, subcontracting relationships or performance opportunities to the relevant market, institution, program or lawful legislative category. General small-business difficulties do not justify every racial category; neither may courts assume that differences in experience are unaffected by documented exclusion.

Croson supplies the demand for relevant evidence; Wygant supplies attention to actual burdens; Paradise supplies flexibility and fit; Fullilove recognizes supported congressional remedial action without furnishing a universal rationale from its fractured opinions. These principles require examination of purpose, operation and burden rather than a classification's benign label. Metro's lower federal tier and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation obscure that inquiry and are displaced to the extent specified below. A uniform obligation does not require ignoring relevant differences between subordination and supported correction.

The objection that common review either erases Congress's role or weakens individual protection fails. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure. An agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project, but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. Courts retain responsibility for constitutional sufficiency. Conversely, the affected competitor's actual burden must be justified even when correction is important. Supported barriers may warrant action before discrimination against every beneficiary has been adjudicated. This decision supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures, no constitutional duty to adopt preferences, and no power over every private inequality. It leaves the arrangement's validity for application of the standard on remand.

### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved. Those concrete issues may affect coverage, fit and burdens; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence.

The Tenth Circuit recorded a stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and a concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority. Their ordinary force is preserved in their actual scope. The appellate statement accepted evidence for discussion while disputing tailoring, and the merits brief disputes the delegation characterization. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings. A stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice. Vacatur is not a finding that the arrangement is unconstitutional, and interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Remand directions and administration in later cases:**

Identify the actual program and preserved challenge. Distinguish the subcontracting incentive, the applicable certification rules, and any separately challenged statutory provision. Do not substitute a generic percentage set-aside or a different federal-aid program for the arrangement before the court. Resolve legal standards independently and apply the ordinary rules governing factual findings, preservation, and further proceedings.
Assess the supported objective and reach. Identify the barriers or conditions the government relies on and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories, and scope to them. Relevant national or shared evidence can suffice where its application is explained. A statewide population percentage, a generalized account of societal discrimination, or a category borrowed without support does not by itself justify the racial treatment.
Examine presumptions in operation. Determine which certification route applies, what social or economic disadvantage must be established, how presumptions can be challenged, and whether firms outside the presumptive categories have a meaningful route to qualify. Group evidence may support a presumption without requiring each applicant to litigate personal discrimination. Individual review and rebuttability matter to justification but are not automatic constitutional safe harbors. A nominal review process that cannot meaningfully correct unsupported eligibility does not supply the flexibility the government claims.
Examine burdens and concrete alternatives. Consider how the incentive affects competition, whether genuine qualifications remain required, and whether persons outside assisted categories retain meaningful opportunity. Government must answer a concrete, practicable alternative that would comparably achieve its supported objective with materially less racial disadvantage. It need not exhaust every imagined alternative or accept a less effective measure simply because that measure uses no racial classification.
Examine continued justification. Determine whether the program can respond when the conditions supporting its coverage or a firm's eligibility materially change. Continued need cannot be established solely by restating the original purpose. Conversely, improved participation while a program operates does not itself prove that its justification has disappeared. The constitutional inquiry requires neither a universal expiration date nor proof that all racial inequality has ended.
State the consequence of a failure. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship, or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails. Relief must address the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. A court does not acquire authority to redesign all procurement policy or supervise every future contract merely because it finds a defect.
These directions require judgment about evidence and operation, not a numerical score. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure, or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Existing records may suffice. Participation, transparent reasons, and independent review can improve administration, but cannot replace the substantive justification owed to an affected bidder.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

## Precedent treatment and current law

Metro Broadcasting's selection of a lower tier for benign federal classifications is overruled. Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced by the common standard while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. Wygant's attention to actual burdens, Paradise's attention to flexibility and fit, and Fullilove's recognition of supported congressional remedial action are retained. Shaw's design-only rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched. This decision does not replace sex-equality doctrine or other source-specific protections and does not decide the validity of later school, employment or contracting programs.

From June 12, 1995, the common substantial-justification standard governs federal, state and local racial classifications affecting competitive opportunity: an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage, operation and actual burdens, and a reasonable response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Supported correction may proceed without proof that every beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, but corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro's lower federal tier no longer governs, and Croson's categorical formulation is displaced as stated above. The arrangement's validity remains for remand; the decision does not resolve every certification route, all future applications, sovereign immunity, damages, or any program-wide injunction. The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

## Holdings

### Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing

**Holding and operative rule:** A bidder able and ready to compete suffers a personal injury when a governmental racial preference denies equal competitive opportunity; it need not prove that it would have received the contract. Prospective relief also requires a real, sufficiently imminent renewed exposure to that preference, which Adarand's repeated bidding and continuing opportunity to encounter the challenged incentive establish here.

**Authority:** Standing portion of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt the holding and its reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that unequal opportunity to compete is an injury even when the award remains uncertain. Northeastern Florida identifies the governmental barrier, rather than proof of the award that would follow its removal, as the relevant competitive injury. Adarand submitted the low bid, but the prime contractor selected a certified disadvantaged business in response to the financial incentive. The contractor could decline that incentive; its optional character does not remove its effect on competitive opportunity. Lujan requires the connection to be concrete and the requested relief appropriate to a continuing or threatened injury. Adarand's recurring bids and the likelihood of encountering the challenged clause again satisfy that prospective requirement on this record. The Court does not infer a guaranteed future award, an entitlement to damages, or a waiver of sovereign immunity. Nor does accepting this competitive injury establish that the challenged preference is unconstitutional; justification remains a separate question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville (June 14, 1993): applies its equal-opportunity injury rule; no guaranteed contract award is required.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): retains concrete injury, causal connection and stage-appropriate proof; prospective relief requires sufficiently imminent renewed exposure.

### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding and operative rule:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a common equality obligation requires substantial justification for racial treatment by federal, state and local government. The incentive burdens an opportunity to compete even though the prime may decline it; that burden requires justification, not automatic invalidation. Corrective purpose matters because overcoming supported exclusion differs from maintaining subordination, but purpose alone cannot establish validity. Evidence must connect credit, bonding, subcontracting relationships or performance opportunities to the relevant market, institution, program or lawful legislative category. General small-business difficulties do not justify every racial category; neither may courts assume that differences in experience are unaffected by documented exclusion.

Croson supplies the demand for relevant evidence; Wygant supplies attention to actual burdens; Paradise supplies flexibility and fit; Fullilove recognizes supported congressional remedial action without furnishing a universal rationale from its fractured opinions. These principles require examination of purpose, operation and burden rather than a classification's benign label. Metro's lower federal tier and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation obscure that inquiry and are displaced to the extent specified below. A uniform obligation does not require ignoring relevant differences between subordination and supported correction.

The objection that common review either erases Congress's role or weakens individual protection fails. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure. An agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project, but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. Courts retain responsibility for constitutional sufficiency. Conversely, the affected competitor's actual burden must be justified even when correction is important. Supported barriers may warrant action before discrimination against every beneficiary has been adjudicated. This decision supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures, no constitutional duty to adopt preferences, and no power over every private inequality. It leaves the arrangement's validity for application of the standard on remand.

### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved. Those concrete issues may affect coverage, fit and burdens; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence.

The Tenth Circuit recorded a stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and a concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority. Their ordinary force is preserved in their actual scope. The appellate statement accepted evidence for discussion while disputing tailoring, and the merits brief disputes the delegation characterization. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings. A stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice. Vacatur is not a finding that the arrangement is unconstitutional, and interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Remand directions and administration in later cases:**

Identify the actual program and preserved challenge. Distinguish the subcontracting incentive, the applicable certification rules, and any separately challenged statutory provision. Do not substitute a generic percentage set-aside or a different federal-aid program for the arrangement before the court. Resolve legal standards independently and apply the ordinary rules governing factual findings, preservation, and further proceedings.
Assess the supported objective and reach. Identify the barriers or conditions the government relies on and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories, and scope to them. Relevant national or shared evidence can suffice where its application is explained. A statewide population percentage, a generalized account of societal discrimination, or a category borrowed without support does not by itself justify the racial treatment.
Examine presumptions in operation. Determine which certification route applies, what social or economic disadvantage must be established, how presumptions can be challenged, and whether firms outside the presumptive categories have a meaningful route to qualify. Group evidence may support a presumption without requiring each applicant to litigate personal discrimination. Individual review and rebuttability matter to justification but are not automatic constitutional safe harbors. A nominal review process that cannot meaningfully correct unsupported eligibility does not supply the flexibility the government claims.
Examine burdens and concrete alternatives. Consider how the incentive affects competition, whether genuine qualifications remain required, and whether persons outside assisted categories retain meaningful opportunity. Government must answer a concrete, practicable alternative that would comparably achieve its supported objective with materially less racial disadvantage. It need not exhaust every imagined alternative or accept a less effective measure simply because that measure uses no racial classification.
Examine continued justification. Determine whether the program can respond when the conditions supporting its coverage or a firm's eligibility materially change. Continued need cannot be established solely by restating the original purpose. Conversely, improved participation while a program operates does not itself prove that its justification has disappeared. The constitutional inquiry requires neither a universal expiration date nor proof that all racial inequality has ended.
State the consequence of a failure. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship, or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails. Relief must address the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. A court does not acquire authority to redesign all procurement policy or supervise every future contract merely because it finds a defect.
These directions require judgment about evidence and operation, not a numerical score. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure, or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Existing records may suffice. Participation, transparent reasons, and independent review can improve administration, but cannot replace the substantive justification owed to an affected bidder.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

Metro Broadcasting's selection of a lower tier for benign federal classifications is overruled. Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced by the common standard while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. Wygant's attention to actual burdens, Paradise's attention to flexibility and fit, and Fullilove's recognition of supported congressional remedial action are retained. Shaw's design-only rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched. ## Law After Decision

From June 12, 1995, the common substantial-justification standard governs federal, state and local racial classifications affecting competitive opportunity: an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage, operation and actual burdens, and a reasonable response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Supported correction may proceed without proof that every beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, but corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro's lower federal tier no longer governs, and Croson's categorical formulation is displaced as stated above. The arrangement's validity remains for remand; the decision does not resolve every certification route, all future applications, sovereign immunity, damages, or any program-wide injunction. The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor concurs in part and concurs in the judgment, joined by Justice Kennedy throughout and by Justices Scalia and Thomas in the uniform-scrutiny and bounded-remand portions. She joins the Court's standing holding but rejects the common standard in favor of a compelling interest and narrow tailoring for every racial classification, federal, state or local. She supports overruling Metro's lower federal tier and concurs in vacatur and remand for actual application of strict scrutiny. Congress's institutional competence may inform evidence and fit, but does not replace individual judicial protection. Strict scrutiny need not invalidate every racial remedy. O'Connor and Kennedy support her particular stare-decisis explanation; Scalia and Thomas do not join it, and it is not the Court's ground.

Justice Scalia concurs in part and concurs in the judgment. He joins the Court's standing holding and supports uniform strict scrutiny and bounded remand, but rejects compensation or entitlement based merely on shared racial ancestry and does not join an affirmative endorsement of group catchup remedies. Justice Thomas concurs in part and concurs in the judgment, joins the Court's standing holding, and supports strict scrutiny and bounded remand while rejecting a benign or paternalistic exception and racial entitlement by group ancestry. His broader equivalence and paternalism reasoning is his own; neither separate position makes the unknown certification route invalid or issues an injunction.

Justice Souter concurs, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, and joins the Court's opinion in full. Constitutional stare decisis requires reasons for departing from Metro, attention to Fullilove's actual supported result, and preservation of Congress's differentiated remedial role. The Court supplies those reasons: a common obligation still considers supported correction, legislative authority, relevant evidence and real burdens, without assigning a lower tier merely because a program is federal and described as benign. Fullilove's recognition of supported congressional action remains. The remand identifies concrete certification and economic-disadvantage issues, preserves concessions and findings in their actual scope, and does not invent a lack of evidence or an unconditional waiver. Those grounds support the common rule and remand while preserving Souter's concern for precedent and the actual challenge.

Justice Ginsburg concurs, joined by Justice Breyer, and joins the Court's opinion in full. Continuing exclusion and barriers to meaningful access may warrant supported repair; they cannot be equated without examination to racial subordination. Congress's remedial competence and relevant national evidence remain important under the common standard, while evidence, operation and outsiders' actual burdens receive meaningful judicial review. Genuine-disadvantage safeguards, preserved concessions and the bounded inquiry on remand prevent either remedial purpose or a racial category from becoming an automatic entitlement. These concurrences add no unrestricted quota entitlement or universal benign-program exemption.
