## Controlling holdings

### Source and application of the WARN limitations period

**Holding and operative rule:** For these WARN claims, courts must borrow an appropriate analogous state limitations period because NLRA § 10(b) is neither a clearly closer analogy nor shown significantly more suitable through federal policy and litigation practicalities. Both claims are timely under the state candidates presented; the Court need not select an exact statute or decide the WARN merits.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly support the rule and application. Scalia joins only the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that WARN’s notice and compensation action follows ordinary state borrowing. DelCostello supplies the default and a narrow exception requiring both a clearly closer federal analogy and significantly greater suitability in light of federal policy and litigation practicalities. Reed confirms that employment-related litigation does not by that label satisfy the exception. WARN protects a substantive right independent of a direct attack on a collective-bargaining settlement; Fort Halifax explains why such employment protections need not be equated with bargaining machinery. The employers’ desire for nationwide certainty and rapid resolution is understandable, but those ordinary effects of borrowing do not demonstrate that the available state periods frustrate this right. The presented state candidates permit both suits, so choosing among them would not affect these judgments. Courts still must select an appropriate analogy when that selection matters. The Court decides no liability, damages, accrual, tolling, arbitration requirement, or statutory merits exception.

**Precedent treatment:**

- DelCostello v. Teamsters, 462 U.S. 151: preserves the ordinary state-borrowing rule and confines its federal exception to a genuinely closer analogy and significantly greater federal suitability; its hybrid labor-settlement application is distinguished.
- Reed v. United Transportation Union, 488 U.S. 319: applies its restrictive account of the exception; a labor setting alone remains insufficient.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1: applies its separation of independent substantive employment protections from bargaining mechanisms; no arbitration question is decided.
