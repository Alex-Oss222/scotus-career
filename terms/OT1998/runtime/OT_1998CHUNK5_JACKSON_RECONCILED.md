### The five-Justice common ground

Wisconsin cannot justify financing the demonstrated devotional instructional function merely by routing its educational operating-and-debt-cost payment through a parent's selection and restrictive endorsement. Agostini requires examination of what the governmental expenditure purchases. The supported integration of devotional instruction into the reimbursed educational work, coupled with the absence of reliable separation for that work, defeats categorical approval based solely on neutral eligibility and private selection.

This is an actual legal and record-based application, not a direction to begin all constitutional analysis anew. The five reject treating the parent-payable check, the opt-out, or ordinary accounting oversight as a sufficient answer to the shown financing issue. They do not decide that every religious school, every lesson, every religious viewpoint, or every independently separable expense is devotional. They preserve genuinely independent private-benefit relationships and lawful separately identifiable secular services, subject to the governing controls. O'Connor and Breyer join this confined ground, not a broader institutional-support prohibition. Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg can apply the controlling Agostini limit without newly endorsing its entire method as their preferred test.

The lower court must identify the appropriate provider/payment and remedial scope under that rule, consider the supported integrated-curriculum evidence, and resolve state-law severability where necessary. An existing record sufficient for a particular determination may be used without a compulsory new trial or new submissions. The Court neither erases the integration findings nor presumes missing source material establishes the State's failure. The remand is not permission to repeat categorical parental-choice approval without addressing the governmental function purchased.

The five froze **vacatur and remand** of the federal disposition and its dependent blanket dissolution direction. Reconciliation does not replace that disposition with an outright reversal declaring the entire amended program invalid, an instruction to exclude every religious provider, or an immediately final statewide funding prohibition. Their substantive rejection of categorical approval supplies the ground for their limited vacatur; it does not by itself expand the remedial judgment.

The three affirming Justices froze a **program-specific affirmance**. Their willingness to respect narrow relief if they lose the financing question is not a supported alternative vote for vacatur, reversal, or the five's substantive application. No non-Stone Justice has a frozen alternative permitting a result selected merely to accommodate another vote. A proposed material judgment or ground change requires its own supported circulation and, where applicable, reconciliation process; it cannot be supplied by tallying agreement with abstract remedial limits.

Independent Wisconsin religious-benefit, public-purpose, local-bill, uniformity, and racial-equality dispositions are not redecided. The August 25, 1995 educational-status qualification for affected pupils remains at its lawful scope: it preserves the relevant enrollment-status date, not a waiver of income limits, caps, or other conditions and not a guarantee of unconstitutional payments. No school closure, pupil transfer, recoupment, damages, compulsory theological restructuring, or court-written replacement program is ordered. A permissible secular-services remedy regulates public expenditures rather than the school's private theology. No constitutional right to a subsidy or duty of a private school to participate is created.

## Current law, source facts, and adversarial alternatives

Agostini's modified Lemon inquiry governs. Purpose, effect, and institutional relationships remain evidence, not three independent surviving or fallback prongs. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when applicable; an established permissible relationship governs its actual category; unresolved legally sufficient claims require attribution and function analysis. The question for an expenditure is what government purchases or performs, not whether every act of the recipient becomes government speech. Ordinary monitoring of secular work is not forbidden merely because the provider is religious. Parental selection alone does not answer the reserved voucher/provider-redemption question.

Both sides preserve Witters and Mueller at their actual scopes. Both address Rosenberger as an actual controlled printing benefit, not merely free space and not blanket authority to buy devotional instruction. Bronx Household concerns genuinely private access and comparable instruction, not this payment for educational costs. Zobrest supplies no current Establishment Clause merits holding. Agostini displaced the corresponding Ball Shared Time rationale, not its distinct Community Education judgment. No side may silently import the historical lower court's altered post-divergence authorities.

The strongest permissible-relationship argument combines neutral income/enrollment eligibility, genuine parent selection, the supported public and private alternatives, independent curriculum and the payment controls. The strongest financing objection combines the restricted provider-cost payment, the possibility of payment beyond ordinary tuition and replacement of church subsidy, supported integrated devotional instruction, and the absence of enforceable separation for the purchased work. Those are the operative alternatives; neither a religious name nor a parent's signature resolves them mechanically.

The exact statute pays the lesser of the public per-member aid amount and the selected school's per-pupil educational operating/debt cost, in four installments after proof of enrollment. It does not cap payment at charged tuition. The exemption from religious participation requires a written parental request; it does not automatically render all funded instruction secular. Performance criteria, accounting, audit, nondiscrimination, health and safety duties remain; they are not absent, and they are not secular-use controls. The supplied record of integrated teaching exceeds affiliation-only evidence but does not prove every activity or expense indivisible. No newly stipulated school practice or taxpayer fact is introduced.

The actual March 3 Sullivan public decision was already read in this reconciliation context and was independently incorporated by the model. Its carrier-attribution rule preserves scrutiny of government's own acts; Jackson concerns Wisconsin's own expenditure. Its limited compensation-payment holding does not decide Establishment Clause finance or create a school-financing safe harbor. The later financing question therefore receives its own Agostini inquiry. No pending election-finance or administrative-law matter is presumed to have supplied a new rule.
