# AT&T section 208 — official order validation

**Scope and cutoff:** January 25, 1999 AT&T event; narrow source supplement to [ATT_208_NEUTRAL](OT_1998CHUNK4_ATT_208_NEUTRAL.md). The earlier frozen text remains intact. No vote, procedural premise or new event is supplied.

The [official First Report and Order extract](../sources/chunk4_a/ATT_208_FCC_ORDER_EXACT.md), paragraphs 121–129 and all accompanying footnotes, supports the following more precise account of the challenged announcement:

- Paragraphs 121 and 124 identify judicial review of arbitrated agreements and the FCC's possible participation, and paragraph 124 argues that section 252(e)(6) does not make judicial review exclusive. The agency contrasts Congress's express exclusivity language for state failure to act with the different language concerning a state determination. That is the agency's construction, not an adjudicated conclusion.
- Paragraphs 126–127 expressly retain section 208 authority. A complaint may allege breach of sections 251–252 and implementing rules **even when the carrier complies with a state-approved agreement**, or breach of the agreement's own terms. The agency plans a later proceeding for expedited complaint procedures; it does not say the asserted jurisdiction awaits that proceeding.
- Paragraph 128 disclaims direct review of the state commission's decision and confines the proposed complaint inquiry to the carrier's acts or omissions under the Communications Act. It nevertheless treats a carrier complaint as an alternative for a person aggrieved by a state determination and identifies the separate section 207 damages route. It says the FCC is unlikely to revisit issues already judicially determined, which is a qualification rather than an absolute preclusion rule.
- Footnote 236 says the FCC **might decline, in some instances**, to impose financial penalties when a carrier acts under state requirements or authorization. It creates neither immunity nor a mandatory penalty; it must not be restated as either. Paragraph 129 identifies other enforcement powers in appropriate circumstances and preserves possible recourse under other law, without initiating a case or making a carrier-specific finding.

These passages sharpen both ripeness paths. The express prospect of a statutory complaint despite compliance with an approved agreement, together with the conditional penalty statement, is relevant to whether the announcement has current legal consequences or alters a carrier's actual conduct. The declaration is settled enough that the planned expedited-procedure rulemaking does not alone make its legal position tentative. Conversely, no passage orders a particular carrier to change conduct, adjudicates a violation, imposes a sanction, or identifies an actual section 208 complaint. The record must still distinguish the substantive duties created elsewhere from the claimed enforcement forum and identify any present hardship attributable to the challenged position. Neither a categorical absence of all effect nor a proved immediate compliance burden follows simply from these paragraphs.
