# Jones v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the reviewed appellate judgment, vacate the twenty-five-year sentence, and remand for sentencing within the offense authorized by the indictment and verdict.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The aggravated injury element was not charged and found by the jury. No acquittal or automatic new aggravated prosecution is ordered; any further prosecution must satisfy its own procedural and constitutional requirements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Injury and death as aggravated-offense elements

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory element question and uses the sufficient statute-specific ground rather than restating a broader constitutional rule already supplied by *Almendarez-Torres*.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Read §2119’s serious-injury and death provisions as elements of aggravated carjacking offenses, applying the enacted structure and the constitutional avoidance concern reflected in Almendarez-Torres. Stone also maintains that a fact increasing authorized punishment must receive the jury protection owed an element; that broader constitutional position is not an inherited majority holding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The governing version of § 2119 created aggravated offenses whose serious-bodily-injury and death facts had to be charged and admitted or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Jones's twenty-five-year sentence on an indictment and verdict establishing only the base offense must be vacated.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The aggravated provisions alter the maximum punishment for a completed carjacking and identify concrete injury consequences traditionally treated as offense elements. Construing them as elements gives effect to the graded offense structure and avoids allowing a judge to authorize punishment the charged verdict did not permit. Almendarez-Torres is a statutory analogue rather than an already adopted universal constitutional command. Jones was charged and convicted only of the base offense; the judge’s later serious-injury finding cannot supply the omitted aggravated charge or jury determination. Watts does not authorize that increase.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not restrict judicial findings used to select a sentence within the maximum authorized by the conviction or decide charging questions under later statutory text.

# Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band of Chippewa Indians

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit’s judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The declaratory and injunctive framework recognizing the treaty rights remains in force. Minnesota may enforce nondiscriminatory conservation measures only under the standards governing treaty-reserved rights and demonstrated necessity.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Continuing force of the 1837 treaty rights

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads the treaties as the tribes would reasonably have understood them and requires clear federal action to terminate a reserved right. Executive preference, statehood, demographic change, and nonuse do not silently accomplish abrogation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Mille Lacs Band’s 1837 usufructuary rights survived because the 1850 Executive Order lacked lawful authority to revoke them, the 1855 Treaty did not clearly relinquish them, and Minnesota’s admission to the Union did not itself extinguish federal treaty rights.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1850 order’s removal command could not lawfully cancel the separate reservation of use rights, and the 1855 text conveyed new land without the clear surrender Minnesota asserts. Statehood is subject to the Supremacy Clause and supplies no repeal. The Band therefore retains the reserved rights within the treaty territory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding permits necessary, nondiscriminatory conservation regulation and does not determine private land access, criminal conduct unrelated to the treaty, or rights beyond the ceded territory.

# Brower v. Washington

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm state court’s rejection of the facial challenge absent an established discriminatory rule, excluded voter, unequal franchise or actionable abridgment of campaign expression.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm state court’s rejection of the facial challenge absent an established discriminatory rule, excluded voter, unequal franchise or actionable abridgment of campaign expression.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Elections and Voting

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Distinguish source of election expenditures, objective time to organize, ballot access, actual suppression and state control over election administration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A private payment to reimburse publicly administered election costs does not, standing alone, convert an election into private government or change who counts votes. A short schedule can matter if challengers prove genuine inability to campaign or an intent to disable the opposition, but the record supplies no finding of such effects. Remedy cannot be based simply on one side having greater money.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may schedule and publicly administer a referendum while accepting statutorily authorized private reimbursement for election costs if franchise equality, viewpoint neutrality, and meaningful legal participation remain intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A private payment to reimburse publicly administered election costs does not, standing alone, convert an election into private government or change who counts votes. A short schedule can matter if challengers prove genuine inability to campaign or an intent to disable the opposition, but the record supplies no finding of such effects. Remedy cannot be based simply on one side having greater money. The summary-judgment record identifies the challenged reimbursement condition and compressed campaign period, but no fee on an individual voter and no supported discriminatory purpose. Alternative petition routes also remained available. Those features support rejection of the federal challenge without endorsing every privately reimbursed election or revisiting the State’s separate constitutional holdings. Burdick’s calibrated rule applies despite Stone’s disagreement with its particular write-in result. On this summary-judgment record, reimbursement does not impose a voter qualification, and the asserted campaign disadvantage lacks evidence of exclusion or a deliberately disabling schedule. The State’s administration and financing interests consequently support this arrangement without establishing that any schedule, however restrictive, would survive.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No license for secret election rules, selective voter exclusions, private vote tabulation, intimidation or proven deliberately prohibitive campaign timing.
