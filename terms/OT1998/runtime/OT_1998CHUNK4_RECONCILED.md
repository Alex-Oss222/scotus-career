### Final statutory and constitutional grounds

**C3-P — Prohibition: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Sections 141 and 195 prohibit the challenged NRFU/ICM use to determine interstate House apportionment. The specific retained apportionment exception qualifies §141(a)'s general sampling language; §141(b)'s reporting requirement creates no contrary grant. The 1976 feasibility-qualified command broadened required sampling elsewhere while retaining the apportionment boundary. This reading preserves the subject-specific provision within the continuous statutory scheme. An exception from a command is not invariably a prohibition; that general observation does not settle this statute's integrated structure and history.

These four affirm the Glavin statutory judgment and apportionment injunction for the challenged methods and leave the constitutional validity question undecided. They do not ban all statistical work, declare sampling inaccurate, treat feasibility as an unconditional duty, or confuse §141(e)(2)'s distinct mid-decade restriction with §195. O'Connor and Kennedy accept the legislative-history corroboration that the amendments did not announce a reversal of the retained restriction; Scalia and Thomas do not adopt silence in legislative debates as a sufficient textual ground. Scalia and Thomas also retain serious constitutional doubt as a supplementary reason to choose the available restrictive construction; Kennedy accepts that supporting avoidance argument. It concerns the historically ordinary meaning of enumeration and Congress's long practice, not a holding that every estimate violates the Constitution. Franklin's residence ruling and Wisconsin's express reservation do not remove that doubt. No literal census-day physical-presence rule is adopted.

**C3-D — Broad statutory permission: Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.** Section 141(a) expressly permits sampling in the decennial census, and §141(b) uses that population tabulation for apportionment. Section 195 excepts apportionment from the otherwise feasible-method command, preserving discretion rather than imposing a ban. The exception therefore has operative work: the Secretary need not sample for apportionment even when feasible. The prior statutory setting and the retained exception are substantial contrary arguments, but they do not turn the changed command into a prohibition contrary to §141's express methods language. Section 209 authorizes review and unsampled preparations, not the contested method. These three would reverse the statutory injunction; they establish no obligation to sample, immunity from review or permission for a constitutionally defective method.

**C3-B — Breyer's narrower statutory permission.** Breyer would reverse the statutory injunction for these two methods on a distinct ground. Read in its historical and operational context, §195's apportionment exception bars replacing the enumeration with a sample census, but does not bar sampling that supplements an actual enumeration effort to correct its omissions. ICM's comparison and adjustment supplements the initial count. NRFU must be considered separately because it estimates the remaining housing units rather than merely checks completed returns. Its bounded role after direct information from at least ninety percent of each tract's housing units, the practical collection difficulty and the Secretary's explained accuracy/cost judgment keep this proposal within permissible supplementation, although this degree may approach the discretionary boundary. Ninety percent concerns housing units, not an asserted ninety percent of all persons. No fixed percentage safe harbor or permission for any amount of substitution follows.

Breyer gives the statutory purpose, administration and context weight without joining a general rule that §141(a) permits every apportionment sampling design. The strongest objection is that any statistical contribution helps determine the final total and that NRFU itself substitutes estimates for particular missing households. He finds the degree and role of the supplement material in this statutory setting, where actual enumeration remains the plan's organizing effort and statistical methods address its residual limitations. He recognizes no arbitrary cost exception to an express command. The broad C3-D rationale and this narrower rationale must not be added together as though they were the same explanation; final proposition-level support depends on actual joins in assembly.

**C4 — Constitutional position of Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** These four reject a categorical claim that the Constitution forbids the challenged sampling methods. The requirement of an actual enumeration demands a count grounded in the population, not an allocation based on political bargaining or unsupported estimates; Congress may prescribe methods that use an actual enumeration effort and reasoned statistical supplementation. Franklin rejects a literal physical-presence condition without deciding sampling; Wisconsin supplies the reasonable-relationship standard and respects explained method choice while requiring attention to geographic accuracy and uncertainty. Its earlier reservation left this question open for decision, not permanently reserved.

They also reject the presented constitutional attack on this explained design. The plan combines initial collection with tract-based follow-up and a coverage measurement intended to correct differential omissions. Its account distinguishes random block error from systematic bias, considers state and congressional-district accuracy and explains aggregation and uncertainty. The strongest objection is that national accuracy can improve while representation becomes less accurate at the relevant geographic scale. These four accept that legal proposition but find the actual geographical explanation and method sufficiently responsive to establish a reasonable relationship to enumeration and population-based representation. They do not find perfect accuracy, certainty that every State or district improves, or that every possible implementation is constitutional. The dissenters' constitutional conclusion concerns the designed methods on this record, not a guaranteed eventual count; unauthorized, discriminatory, unsupported or materially different implementation remains reviewable.

## 3. AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board — eight consolidated dockets — January 25, 1999

**A1 — General implementation power.** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would reverse the categorical lack-of-authority premise. Section 251(d)(1) expressly directs implementation of the requirements, and §252(c)'s duties to apply §251 and the Commission's rules confirm that national standards coexist with state resolution of individual negotiations, arbitrations and rates. Section 152(b) does not remove the local competition provisions from the regulatory authority the amendments themselves create. Operational-support systems, operator and directory services, and vertical-switching functions are not excluded merely because they are not detachable physical objects or also can be described as services. Eligibility is distinct from compulsory access under §251(d)(2). No conclusion that every eligible function must always be unbundled follows.

**A4 — Necessary, impair and Rule 51.319.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would set aside the challenged required-element list and return it for application of the enacted access criteria. The Commission must give independent limiting work to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, taking account of realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Treating any increased cost or diminished quality as sufficient impairment, while categorically excluding external alternatives, does not supply that limiting inquiry. The proprietary formulation of significant impairment or thwarting is not rejected merely because it is less than absolute indispensability; the defect includes the cramped comparison limited to the incumbent's nonproprietary elements. Neither standard commands an abstract indispensability rule or access only to an irreplaceable natural monopoly. The agency retains room to explain genuine entry impairment, actual availability, costs, quality, service needs and workable criteria under the statute. The Court supplies no invented numerical cost threshold and does not direct which element must be removed.

**Souter's A4 contrary commitment:** Uphold the agency's construction and resulting listed access requirements against this statutory attack. The Act makes unbundled access a means of competitive entry, not a last resort after replication. Cost and quality affect the ability to provide the selected service; the separate higher proprietary threshold and nonproprietary alternative give the clauses distinct operation. An outside-source requirement can let incumbents demand duplication of facilities that is theoretically possible but commercially disables entry. Chevron and his MCI dissent support the actual competition rationale within an express delegation. He does not favor absolute mandatory access to every technically available element or endorse the separately invalidated technical-feasibility presumption. His sufficient ground concerns the reasonableness of this interpretation, not independent judicial findings about every administrative submission.

The strongest challenge to the seven-Associate A4 position is that it risks substituting judicial economic judgments for the agency's authorized competitive-entry judgment. The seven accept the agency's policy domain but require the words Congress selected to perform the limiting task; expertise cannot turn every disadvantage of purchasing elsewhere into statutory impairment. The strongest challenge to Souter's position is that marginal cost or quality differences are virtually ubiquitous. He finds the statutory context and retained proprietary and access restrictions sufficient, and does not claim that breadth alone exempts the rule from review. The technical-feasibility presumption receives its own disposition in A9, not an automatic consequence of either A4 vote.

**Stevens's reconciled A4 ground.** His MCI dissent and Allentown Mack join preserve reasoned agency choice within actual statutory limits; neither makes every competition-promoting rule reasonable. The current rule excludes external alternatives altogether and supplies no genuine selection standard when any cost or quality advantage suffices. The unchanged specific §251(d)(2) predicates supply the limiting distinction missing from the provisional analogy to MCI. He joins the stated invalidation and remand without an essential-facilities-only rule, numerical threshold or direction selecting replacement elements.

**A5 — No freestanding own-facilities prerequisite.** All eight reject a categorical requirement that a requesting carrier own some local facilities before seeking unbundled access. Section 251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combination into a service. The separate resale route does not insert a minimum-property requirement absent from the unbundling route. A4 still determines which elements must be supplied, and combining or purchasing elements retains distinct obligations and commercial consequences from buying a finished retail service for resale. No holding promises the entrant the incumbent's entire network regardless of the statutory access criteria or decides rate differences assumed in the parties' policy arguments.

**A6 — Existing combinations, Rule 51.315(b).** All eight would reverse its invalidation. Unbundled access requires separate availability and valuation, not destruction of a configuration already joined in the incumbent's network. Paragraph (b) permits separation when the requesting carrier asks; otherwise a nondiscriminatory duty to leave requested existing combinations intact reasonably implements §251(c)(3). The sentence allowing the requester to combine does not command the incumbent first to dismantle. This holding does not restore paragraphs (c)–(f)'s affirmative new-combination duties, require construction of an unestablished configuration or decide their validity; their separate requested reconsideration is addressed in A11. The technical and operational content of a future particular request remains governed by the Act and valid rules.

**A7 — Section 252(i), Rule 51.809.** All eight would reverse its invalidation. The statutory access to any interconnection, service or network element under an approved agreement permits selection of an individual arrangement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. It is not selection of an isolated price with its relevant obligations discarded. The rule preserves the incumbent's proof that providing the particular arrangement to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing. Those safeguards, nondiscrimination and the agency's assessment of negotiation incentives support the construction. The challengers' strongest tradeoff objection does not establish that Congress required every entrant to take an entire agreement or that the agency's contrary practical assessment is irrational. No limitless cherry-picking right follows.

**A8 — California.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would reverse the intraLATA dialing-parity vacatur based on absent federal authority and remand any actual remaining issues. The five adopting A1 apply its general implementing grant. O'Connor independently accepts implementation of §251(b)(3)'s express national substantive dialing-parity duty under §251(d)(1); her specific state-pricing objection does not extend to this duty. Thomas and Breyer would affirm the intraLATA authority vacatur: their A1-P construction does not treat the implementation deadline as a general jurisdictional grant, and the dialing-parity provision does not itself specifically assign this regulatory task to the FCC. This is a jurisdictional-authority position, not a conclusion that Congress left substantive parity optional.

All eight leave the separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge unripe on this record: its disadvantage depends on particular rate decisions permitting recovery, none of which is reviewed. Reversing or sustaining the parity authority ground does not settle those rates or manufacture an accrued numbering injury. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain; no complete second-judgment reversal follows merely from A1.

**A9 — Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption, separate from its definition and Rule 51.319.** The opening brief expressly seeks corrected-standard reconsideration; this is a supported remedial request, not proof that the lower court decided nothing but jurisdiction. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the partial vacatur of the presumption and corresponding Order paragraphs 278 and 281 on the following independently sufficient ground. Feasibility can be relevant to access, and the words at a minimum allow the Commission to consider additional matters. But this rule presumptively requires additional elements on a feasibility finding and permits refusal only through the actual incumbent-network-only proprietary alternative or the rule's no-quality-loss/no-cost-increase condition. Those restrictions reproduce the failure to give the required necessity and impairment criteria independent limiting force identified in A4. The result does not invalidate every portion of Rule 51.317 or the separately sustained technical-feasibility definition, and does not hold that the Commission may never use a lawful presumption. A corrected-standard remand cannot cure this already resolved substantive defect without agency reconsideration of the defective criteria.

Souter would vacate the lower partial invalidation and remand this presumption for review under the proper delegated-authority standard, without unconditional reinstatement or a final validity ruling. The lower premise that technical feasibility is unmentioned among the minimum criteria is insufficient; the rule has actual exceptions, not feasibility alone. His A4 position removes the seven Associates' independently sufficient objection, but does not decide whether the actual presumption and burdens are reasonably explained or valid in every application. The strongest challenge is that its burden can effectively replace a statutory determination; his remand requires that objection to be considered rather than assuming technical feasibility can never inform selection.

**A10 — Superior quality, Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c).** All eight would vacate the reviewed invalidation and remand for reconsideration under the corrected authority and substantive-standard distinction, without deciding final validity or directing immediate unconditional reinstatement. Section 251(c)(2)'s at-least-equal language states a mandatory floor; absence of an express command to provide superior quality does not itself establish that a reasonable implementing rule may never require it. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict grounds remain real objections, not erased by the advocate's claim that nobody challenged statutory consistency. On return, the court must consider the actual requested and technically feasible improvement, the relationship to the statutory duty and the agency's own justification. Compensation alone supplies no authority, and the statutory nondiscrimination floor does not itself require every requested upgrade.

Thomas and Breyer accept this corrective remand if the Court adopts A1, without abandoning their contrary general-authority position. They have not supplied an independently sufficient substantive defense of these invalidations comparable to Thomas's A11 ground. This is agreement in a remand under the Court's governing framework, not a complete join in A1 or unconditional merits reinstatement.

The two rules remain distinct: Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns interconnection superior to that provided to the incumbent, subsidiary, affiliate or another interconnection recipient; Rule 51.311(c) concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, with the incumbent's technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission. The rule preserves lesser quality at the requester's sole request. The lower necessary-modification exception remains relevant: modifications necessary to accommodate lawful interconnection or access were not forbidden by its ruling. Neither an unconditional right to superior facilities nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

**A11 — Affirmative new combinations, Rule 51.315(c)–(f).** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the invalidation and remand for review under the correct implementing-power standard. The sentence requiring provision in a manner allowing requesting carriers to combine elements describes the access capability; it does not unambiguously assign every physical combining task exclusively to the requester. The lower court's substantive reading must therefore be assessed with the available delegation, the incumbent's control, the actual conditions and the agency's explanation. Unlike A6, this does not decide these affirmative duties valid. Breyer accepts the remand under the Court's A1 framework without joining the general-authority rationale he rejects; this independently presented remedial request has no published historical merits vote to import. Subsection (c) reaches requested combinations not ordinarily made only if technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) concerns combination with elements possessed by the requester in any technically feasible manner. Subsections (e) and (f) assign the incumbent the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission. The (c) other-carrier condition must not be rewritten as an express condition in (d). The remand does not approve each burden or compel a particular configuration.

Thomas would affirm the invalidation of (c)–(f) on a substantive ground independent of the pricing dispute. He reads Congress's direction to make elements available so that the requesting carrier can combine them, together with the distinct resale route, as assigning the new-combination undertaking to the requester; a general implementation grant cannot shift that affirmative task to the incumbent on demand. Necessary accommodation remains distinct from requiring the incumbent to create combinations it does not use. His strongest counterargument is that the statute speaks in terms of enabling, not exclusive labor assignment. His MCI majority and additional-condition joins give decisive weight to the specific allocation; compensation and the rule's feasibility safeguards do not supply the obligation he finds absent. This position permits preservation of an existing combination under A6, which demands no new combining work, and does not forbid voluntary new-combination agreements.

The request to reconsider these three groups is expressly presented in the sanitized opening brief. No certified grant limitation excludes it in the approved neutral packet. The above dispositions neither accept that brief's characterization of the lower reasoning as fact nor resolve every residual regulatory merit simply because general authority has been corrected.

## 4. Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson — No. 97-1287 — January 25, 1999

### Common result and required claim-specific grounds

**Every Associate Justice would reverse the Ninth Circuit's revival of the six original claims and restore their dismissal on the following limited legal grounds.** The judgment concerns the original complaint reviewed under Rule 12(b)(6); it does not decide later merger allegations merely because an amended complaint appears in the joint appendix. Factual allegations receive the applicable favorable treatment, but the labels separate plan, termination and diversion do not themselves establish the legal predicates. The defined-benefit arrangement continues to fund its promised benefits from the common fund under the contributory and noncontributory benefit structures. No loss of an accrued promised benefit, actual reversion to Hughes, sham payment to a nonparticipant or completed statutory termination is established by the original theories. That conclusion follows from the actual acts alleged and the incorporated plan characterization, not from an assertion that sponsor capacity immunizes everything done with assets.

**H1 — Section 403(c)(1), original first claim and related fifth-claim ground.** Plan assets remain plan property, including surplus attributable to employee contributions. Paying genuine benefits to participants under the amended single plan does not make the assets inure to Hughes merely because Hughes's required future contributions are lower or an early-retirement program reduces payroll. The amendment adds a benefit structure and eligible participants; it is not a transfer from one legally separate trust to satisfy another employer obligation. A contribution holiday alone does not distribute assets to the employer. The anti-inurement restriction applies independently of fiduciary classification; an actual diversion, prohibited reversion or payment outside the lawful plan would require its own analysis. No new exception to §403 is created.

**H2 — Sections 3(21)(A), 404 and pleaded 405 derivative responsibility.** Design and adoption of the 1989 and 1991 amendments are sponsor acts under Spink, even though employees contributed. Contributions do not make every amendment a fiduciary function or participants co-settlors entitled to control the plan's design. Asset management and disposition in implementation remain separately subject to the functional statute, including authority or control over disposition without an additional discretion requirement. Assuming fiduciary implementation of the challenged genuine payments, the original complaint identifies no distinct imprudent management or disloyal diversion beyond the rejected surplus-ownership and separate-plan theories. The derivative cofiduciary label supplies no underlying breach. John Hancock's asset/function/breach distinctions and Varity's separate administration holding remain intact; neither turns amendment design into administration nor exempts actual administration.

**H3 — Sections 203(a)(1), 204(c), and claimed benefit deprivation.** Accrued employee-derived benefits are nonforfeitable, and a contributory defined-benefit plan must satisfy the statutory employee-contribution floor. For mandatory contributions, §204(c) uses accumulated contributions with the statutory interest treatment, expressed as an annual benefit commencing at normal retirement age; a different benefit form requires the prescribed actuarial equivalence. The statute does not treat all actual investment appreciation on mandatory contributions as the employee's separate property. Section 204(c)(4)'s different treatment of voluntary employee contributions remains distinct and is not erased by the defined-benefit label; the original allegations describe required payroll contributions, not an unrecognized voluntary-contribution account. The original claim identifies no reduction below the promised or statutorily protected accrued benefit and no forfeiture merely by use of pooled surplus for lawful plan benefits. No accrued-benefit reduction, anti-cutback violation or recalculation amount is judicially invented. An actual violation of the statutory floor or an independently promised greater benefit is not insulated.

**H4 — Sections 4041 and 4044, original fourth claim.** Closing new enrollment in the contributory structure while continuing the plan, its participants' claims and its ongoing obligations does not constitute the statutory termination that triggers §4044 distribution. Section 4041 specifies standard or distress termination for a single-employer plan, apart from PBGC proceedings under §4042; a common-law wasting-trust label does not supply a substitute termination. If the statutory distribution trigger occurs, the liabilities and §4044 order matter, including its protection for residual assets attributable to employee contributions. Employer residual reversion has its own conditions: satisfaction of all liabilities, consistency with law and authorization by the plan, with the separate enacted limits on a reversion amendment. None creates a present right to distribute all surplus before termination. This is not an unpresented general partial-termination holding and does not use the lower opinion's deleted common-law or mixed-question language.

**H5 — Section 406(a)(1)(D), related fifth claim, and §406(b)(2) argument.** The pleaded asset use is payment of pension benefits to participants in the continuing plan, not a transfer to Hughes or to strangers solely renamed as beneficiaries. Spink's binding genuine-benefit-payment rule defeats the inference of a prohibited use solely from employer savings; it assumes an otherwise lawful plan and preserves sham transfers, kickbacks and independent statutory violations. The original theory supplies no additional transaction of that kind after H1–H4 are resolved. Section 406(b)(2)'s separate bar on a fiduciary acting in a transaction on behalf of a party whose interests are adverse to the plan or its participants is not made out merely because the sponsor adopted a benefit design advantageous to itself. No blanket benefit-payment exemption or general net-benefit defense follows, and no §408 exception is needed to cure an otherwise found prohibited transaction. Souter and Breyer's prior Spink reservation does not constitute a holding that these genuine pension payments are prohibited; they apply the controlling rule to this narrower asserted transaction.

**H6 — Original sixth claim, §404(a)(1)(D), §502(a)(1)(B), and Article V §5.2.** The plan's fair-and-equitable administration, interpretation and application clause does not promise every retired participant an incentive later offered to induce current eligible employees to retire. The actual alleged difference is created by the 1989 amendment's eligibility design, not unequal implementation among participants satisfying that design. Spink disposes of fiduciary liability resting solely on creating that eligibility condition, but that alone is not the answer to §502(a)(1)(B). The benefits-enforcement theory fails separately because the quoted administrative clause supplies no contractual entitlement to an incentive for a retiree outside its eligibility class. The complaint alleges no failure to pay benefits due under the applicable plan terms. Curtiss-Wright preserves actual amendment-procedure requirements and authorized adoption; the original six theories do not allege a distinct defect in who adopted this amendment. The Court decides no case in which an amendment violates a specific anti-discrimination, allocation, benefit or amendment restriction. Selective design is not synonymous with unfair administration, and a broad fairness clause is not judicial authority to redesign the benefit structure.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Contributory-plan distinction.** The retirees' strongest point is that employee-derived money is protected expressly, rather than being property the sponsor may treat as its own. H1 and H3 accept that premise. It does not follow that participants own every dollar of an ongoing plan's investment surplus, that contributions turn design into fiduciary conduct, or that all surplus must be distributed before the statutory termination trigger. John Hancock preserves plan-asset status and function; it did not adjudicate individual ownership of actuarial surplus. The contrary blanket employer-ownership ground is unavailable.

**Implementation remand.** The strongest return path invokes Spink's express preservation of independently pleaded anti-inurement and implementation claims and Rule 12's treatment of allegations. That path would be required for an actual asset transfer outside the amended plan, a sham, unpaid protected benefits, unauthorized amendment or administrative departure from operative terms. The original complaint's asserted recipient and transfer rest on the claim that the amendment created a legally separate plan, while the early-retirement theory rests on disfavored eligibility design. Resolving those legal predicates leaves no separate supported implementation wrong in these six claims. No dismissal is justified merely by saying that everything Hughes did was settlor conduct.

**Termination path.** The proposed wasting-trust construction would force a distribution because no new entrants can join the contributory structure. Its strongest support is protection of employee-generated residual value against indefinite employer capture. The plan still owes and pays benefits and continues through the new structure; the statutory termination and residual-asset provisions, rather than a judicially invented termination trigger, protect the identified interests. The Court neither finds an actuarial shortage nor treats keeping the assets in the plan as a reversion.

**Plan-enforcement path.** The strongest H6 objection is that an enforceable fair-administration clause has work independent of fiduciary classification. The unanimous commitment expressly gives it that work for administration under actual eligibility terms; it does not turn it into an equality-of-benefit-design promise. Rejection of §502(a)(1)(B) is therefore a claim-specific merits ruling rather than a remedial-category bar under Mertens or an assumption that all contract claims fail when fiduciary design liability fails.

## John Crane, Inc. v. Abate — No. 98-873 — February 18, 1999

### Components and common sufficient grounds

**A1 — Channel and scope.** Reach the preserved Fourteenth Amendment challenge through §1257 as it bears on Crane's final judgments in the five-trial-plaintiff litigation. The Maryland Court of Appeals denied discretionary review. The unresolved typography concerning that denial's precise 1998 date establishes no filing defect. Common findings concerning roughly 1,300 other claimants are not themselves 1,300 final judgments. Neither the broad inventory description nor the consolidation grants advisory jurisdiction over future applications. Review does not reopen judgments or separate issues belonging to other defendants.

**A2 — Constitutional rule.** A State may decide genuinely common issues together if the defendant has a meaningful opportunity to contest the evidence and every material defense to those issues and the remaining claimant-specific elements remain to be proved. Neither the number of parties nor the absence of a brand name on a verdict form is by itself a due-process violation. Efficiency cannot excuse substituting general asbestos exposure or the experiences of selected plaintiffs for proof of a particular claimant's product exposure, legally sufficient causation, injury and damages. Rule 23's settlement requirements are not constitutional rules governing Maryland Rule 2-503.

**A3 — Present record.** Reject the constitutional attack on the reviewed Crane judgments. Common state-of-knowledge evidence, identification of the examining lawyers' clients, explanations of the cross-plaintiffs' roles, instructions distinguishing earlier liability from contested liability, defendant-specific verdicts, and the actually retained individual questions support a meaningful adjudication here. The five plaintiffs illustrated asbestos issues; their injuries were not statistically extrapolated into recoveries for the untried claimants. Combining evidence across phases did not itself prevent Crane from contesting its meaning.

The category finding requires its actual evidentiary limit. The lower court relied on testimony connecting visible asbestos dust to excessive exposure, the absence of directed contrary proof for the products implicated in the appeal, and the need to prove actual exposure to that dust and resulting harm. It expressly recognized that products vary. It did not find that a nonemitting or materially different product could never exist. The jury distinguished Crane packing from Crane gaskets. The alleged contradiction in Theis and Glensky's judgments is answered by the admitted catalog identifying sheet packing from which gaskets were cut, together with testimony about cutting sheets and dust; the colloquial description of the cut article as a gasket did not compel a finding of exposure only to the separately exonerated gasket product.

The record contains errors. Starting dates were corrected for Stebbing and Hampshire; Harbison-Walker findings were set aside; other exposure findings were reversed because the fiber-drift inference did not satisfy Maryland causation law. Those corrections do not prove that Crane lost a meaningful defense or that every remaining finding was the product of an incapable jury. Conversely, their correction is not a universal cure for a constitutionally defective aggregation. The presently asserted structural inference fails on the concrete record, including the differentiated Crane verdict and catalog evidence.

**A4 — Remedy and future limits.** Affirm the judgment insofar as challenged by Crane on this consolidation/category due-process ground, leaving the lower court's distinct reversals, reductions, vacaturs and remands intact. No new trial, damages calculation, universal asbestos-liability determination or instruction governing every future mini-trial is ordered. In later individual proceedings, negligence and defect questions actually resolved will not ordinarily be retried; exposure, disease, sufficient causation and damages remain individual. The decision approves no extension of a common finding to a materially different product or defense that was not fairly adjudicated. Whether a particular later application exceeds that limit must be decided on its actual record. The reply-stage Seventh Amendment reference supplies no separately decided federal jury claim here.

### Enhanced comparison and individual commitments

No new evidentiary burden or numerical ceiling for consolidation is adopted.

## National Collegiate Athletic Association v. Smith — No. 98-84 — February 23, 1999

### Components and common sufficient grounds

**N1 — Dues and recipient status.** Ordinary membership dues paid by institutions receiving federal financial assistance do not, without more, make their association a recipient of that assistance under Title IX. Assistance may reach a recipient through an intermediary; the legal distinction is receipt of assistance rather than direct versus indirect delivery. Money paid by a recipient in an ordinary downstream transaction is not for that reason itself federal assistance extended to the payee. Grove City preserves coverage where federal student aid is intended for payment to the college; Paralyzed Veterans preserves the distinction between an assisted recipient and an entity economically benefiting from assisted operations.

Section 1687 broadens the operations covered after assistance reaches part of an identified entity. Even its provision for an entity established by two or more listed institutions retains the condition that some part receive assistance. The Restoration Act does not erase that condition. The whole sentence of §106.2(h) requires assistance extended directly or through another recipient; its subsequent reference to an educational activity receiving or benefiting from assistance does not turn every beneficiary into a recipient. No de minimis dues or revenue-percentage exemption is created. The NCAA's importance to collegiate athletics does not alone identify a transfer of assistance.

**N2 — Alternative routes.** Do not decide actual receipt through the NYSP/NYSP Fund or independently sufficient coverage based on delegated control. The dues holding does not establish that the NCAA never receives federal assistance, resolve the grant mechanics, or prove that its governance role is or is not the legally relevant form of control. The lower court's surrogate language supported its dues rationale; it was not a separately completed adjudication of either alternative. Preserve ordinary lower-court consideration of properly preserved contentions, the actual pleadings, the Marshall affidavit's procedural significance, and any permissible amendment. No determination of waiver or automatic amendment entitlement is supplied.

**N3 — Judgment, pleading and remedy.** Vacate the Title IX portion of the Third Circuit's judgment resting on dues and remand for further proceedings consistent with N1–N2. The District Court's dismissal did not by itself make the amendment request moot. Foman's grounds, including futility, undue delay, bad faith, dilatory motive and prejudice, remain available under the applicable procedure; an amendment relying only on ordinary dues cannot cure the recipient defect. The remand permits the lower courts to determine the preserved alternative coverage and pleading issues rather than commands a finding of coverage or leave to amend again. Consequences for supplemental contract jurisdiction follow the resulting federal-claim posture. The concluded antitrust disposition is not reopened.

This is statutory coverage at the pleading/amendment stage, not an Article III dismissal. The compensation demand prevents treating the expired athletic seasons alone as dispositive of the entire controversy; its legal prerequisites remain unproved. No discrimination, recipient responsibility, causation, compensable loss, prospective-relief entitlement or money award is adjudicated. The proposed addition of the universities is not independently granted or rejected by this Court.

## Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala — No. 97-1489 — February 23, 1999

### Components and common sufficient grounds

**H1 — Board route and review of its jurisdictional ruling.** A refusal to reopen the final 1989 reimbursement determinations is not a new final determination as to the amount of total program reimbursement within §1395oo(a)(1)(A)(i). It leaves the earlier amount determination in place. The 180-day original appeal route and a timely request within the regulation's ordinary three-year reopening period are distinct. Section 1395oo(d)'s authority over cost-report matters operates after the conditions for a hearing are met; it does not create jurisdiction over every refusal to revisit a report. Judicial review of the Board's final conclusion that it lacks jurisdiction permits deciding that question under §1395oo(f)(1); it does not empower the Board to hear the underlying refusal or authorize direct judicial calculation of reimbursement.

The Secretary's construction fits the statutory final-determination language and the distinct regulatory reopening scheme. Section 405.1885(c) places reopening jurisdiction exclusively in the body that last decided the matter; §405.1889 separately provides review when reopening actually produces a revision in the reimbursement amount. The manual explains this arrangement; it does not create statutory jurisdiction or have independent legislative force. Auer and Thomas Jefferson support the Secretary's considered reading of the regulation within its text. This conclusion does not depend on a blanket rule that a manual may amend law or that an agency's characterization of jurisdiction is conclusive.

**H2 — Direct federal-question route.** The claim seeks reopening to obtain additional Medicare reimbursement and draws its standing and substantive basis from that scheme. Incorporated §405(h) therefore forecloses the asserted §1331 route notwithstanding the procedural formulation of the requested order. Ringer supplies the arising-under analysis. Michigan Academy's review presumption does not guarantee a new district-court route where the statute provided an original Board appeal and judicial review. The missed original appeal is relevant to the statutory arrangement; it is not misdescribed as failure to exhaust the later reopening challenge.

**H3 — APA alternative.** Sanders remains controlling that the APA is not an independent grant of subject-matter jurisdiction. Its review standards cannot supply the absent jurisdiction over this refusal. The provider's express request to reconsider that rule is rejected: the Medicare corrective-adjustment command neither amends the APA's jurisdictional status nor creates an exception to it when §1331 is barred. Section 1395oo(f)'s use of APA review standards for a properly authorized action does not establish every action's authorization. No distinct colorable constitutional claim is presented, and no constitutional-review exception is decided away.

**H4 — Mandamus.** Assume without deciding that incorporated §405(h) does not universally bar §1361. The provider has pursued the available remedies concerning the refusal to reopen; failure to appeal the original amount decision does not establish nonexhaustion of that separate claim. Mandamus nevertheless fails because no clear nondiscretionary duty to reopen these determinations is established. The duties governing reasonable costs and compensation comparable to services at comparable institutions govern lawful reimbursement; they do not by themselves command reopening after finality. Good Samaritan's corrective-adjustment holding likewise operates within the lawful cost-method system and does not require reopening every disputed closed payment. Regions' authorization to correct a historical input for future payments, while preserving closed annual payments, supplies no contrary symmetry rule.

The reopening provisions must retain their separate terms. Section 405.1885(a) ordinarily permits reopening on a timely motion within three years; it does not require it whenever the provider alleges new evidence. Under (b), an intermediary determination or hearing decision shall be reopened and revised when HCFA gives the specified inconsistency notification within that period. Under (d), procurement by fraud or similar fault of a party requires reopening and revision at any time. Neither trigger is established here; a provider's asserted legal error is not HCFA's notification and does not establish fraud. Paragraph (e)'s application to reporting periods ending on or after December 31, 1971, and its rule for earlier periods reopened after May 27, 1972, do not alter this 1989 case. Sections 405.1887's written notice, explanation upon revision, and reasonable opportunity for additional evidence or argument apply to an actual reopening; §405.1889 makes an actual post-reopening amount revision separately reviewable. The manual's new-material-evidence, clear-error and inconsistency considerations do not override these distinct regulatory commands.

**H5 — Judgment and reservations.** Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment on these distinct grounds. No compensation finding, reimbursement, reopened cost report, Board merits hearing or additional remand is ordered. The possible absence of the intermediary as a party is not an independent ground. Review concerning an established mandatory reopening trigger, an actual post-reopening revision, an independently supported constitutional claim, or another genuinely presented regulatory violation remains subject to its own lawful channel and requirements; none receives automatic review or automatic relief here. The decision adjudicates the failed routes presented, not all conceivable review of every reopening-related action.

### Enhanced comparison and individual commitments

**Board-review path:** A refusal finally determines that the existing amount will remain unchanged, and review of abuse of discretion need not mean that the Board itself initially reopens. Its strongest counterargument is that Congress specified a final determination as to the amount and an original deadline, while the regulation separately authorizes an additional reopening opportunity and explicitly identifies a revision after reopening as a distinct reviewable event. Treating every refusal as a new appeal defeats that distinction. Broad §1395oo(d) power does not answer the threshold.

**Alternative-court path:** A claimed violation of mandatory compensation and corrective-adjustment duties should remain judicially enforceable. Its strongest counterargument is the difference between a duty governing the original lawful calculation and a clear present duty to reopen it. Ringer bars relabeling an arising-under claim for §1331, Sanders defeats APA-created jurisdiction, and the actual mandatory reopening triggers are not established. Those are separate answers; neither regulatory discretion nor the expired original appeal alone supplies them all.

**Affirmance path:** The statutory distinction, considered regulation and absence of a demonstrated mandatory reopening duty sustain the denial of the requested review. Its strongest objection is insulation of an agency's legal error. The answer is the actual original statutory channel, the duty to honor the regulation's specific mandatory commands, the reviewability of an actual revision, and the limited question decided. No universal immunity from legal challenge is announced, and no unspecified future channel is promised as an affirmative ground.

## Final A2-208 rule, application and limits

**Operative rule.** A settled agency position asserting a possible enforcement forum is not ripe for judicial review merely because the interpretation is complete and the disputed allocation of authority is predominantly legal. Where the challenged announcement itself directs no present change in primary conduct, makes no carrier-specific liability determination and leaves its disputed authority for concrete application and meaningful judicial review, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice rather than decided on the enforcement merits.

This rule does not make commentary categorically unreviewable. An agency statement that actually changes legal protection, presently directs compliance, or imposes another supported immediate legal constraint presents a different question. Neither the absence of a criminal penalty nor the possibility of later review categorically defeats a challenge to a presently burdensome civil duty. Final agency action, a lawful statutory review channel, Article III standing, and fitness and hardship remain distinct. This disposition rests on ripeness and decides neither the independent final-order question nor standing.

**The actual announcement.** Paragraphs 126–127 assert that the Commission already possesses complaint authority; the planned later proceeding concerns expedited procedures and does not make that legal assertion tentative. Paragraph 127 contemplates a complaint alleging statutory or implementing-rule violations even when a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplates enforcement of agreement terms. Paragraph 128 describes a carrier-focused inquiry while presenting it as an alternative for someone aggrieved by a state determination. These are substantial claims about authority. Their definiteness does not itself make the general challenge ripe.

The announced position does not amend a carrier's substantive section 251 or 252 obligation, identify a present conflict in an operative agreement, revise a state commission's determination, order a carrier or commission to act, or adjudicate a violation. It announces how the agency understands a possible complaint route. A carrier-specific application would identify the challenged acts or omissions, the alleged statutory or contractual violation and the relief claimed. Those particulars can determine whether the Commission is enforcing a carrier duty, enforcing an agreement or using a nominal carrier proceeding to revisit a state determination. Deciding the entire allocation now would answer those materially different settings in the abstract.

**Penalties and state approval.** Footnote 236 says that the Commission might decline financial penalties in some instances of conduct pursuant to state requirements or authorization. It supplies neither immunity nor a command to penalize. The Court therefore does not rest on a promise that state-approved compliance protects a carrier from a complaint or sanction. Nor does the Court treat the announced lack of an absolute safe harbor as a present adjudication of liability or as withdrawal of an established protection comparable to Bennett's operative incidental-take conditions. The statement preserves possible future exercise of asserted authority; it does not itself resolve whether any carrier violated a federal duty or owes a penalty. The qualified statement that the Commission is unlikely to revisit judicially decided issues likewise is not an absolute preclusion rule.

**Later review and hardship.** Section 208(a) supplies a complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer, and investigation under its enacted conditions. Section 208(b)(3) makes an order concluding an investigation covered by paragraphs (1) or (2) final and appealable under section 402(a). A concrete application therefore permits the asserted enforcement authority to be challenged in its actual setting through the applicable review route. Nothing in this announcement requires a presently commanded act to be performed irreversibly while awaiting that process, or forecloses a challenge to the Commission's authority when it is applied. The possibility of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about the general forum allocation do not alone create the present primary-conduct hardship needed here.

The Court creates no universal exhaustion requirement, guarantees no particular later filing's timeliness or standing, and decides no automatic entitlement to interlocutory relief. A later review must satisfy its own statutory conditions. The conclusion concerns this general announcement and its contingent applications, not a rule that all enforcement proceedings can safely be postponed until penalties are collected. Paragraph 129's identification of other possible enforcement mechanisms does not initiate one or enlarge the present challenge into a decision on every such mechanism.

**Controlling-law application.** Ohio Forestry distinguishes a completed policy from a presently consequential authorization and permits deferral while further application would clarify the claim and meaningful review remains. Toilet Goods supplies the same distinction between completion and a ripe dispute. The particular statement here has that deferred-application character. Franklin's completed census determination and Bennett's conditional change in legal protection involved consequences already attached to an actual determination; the settled opinion about jurisdiction here does not perform either function. Pic-A-State involved an existing business actually stopped by a law reaching its conduct and a sufficiently real enforcement risk, not only uncertainty about the forum for a future claim.

Lee's present-civil-duty rule remains intact. Its participation remand addressed challenged substantive obligations and an incomplete inquiry into their application. Here the challenged instrument is the general forum announcement itself, whose operative text is available and establishes its prospective complaint character. It is not a substantive participation duty mistakenly dismissed only because it lacks a criminal penalty. NAACP forbids assumed jurisdiction before contested merits and does not turn every erroneous lower threshold explanation into an obligatory factual remand. WRTL requires examining the actual claim and remedy; it likewise permits a supported threshold disposition when the pertinent legal character is ascertainable.

**Why no renewed threshold remand.** The independent model correctly identified the lower court's incomplete reliance on finality and legal disagreement, but it treated incomplete knowledge about possible carrier-specific effects as a reason to return this general statement challenge. The result here does not find that no relevant fact exists anywhere in the administrative record, infer a litigant default or invent a missing complaint. It determines the present effect of the actual instrument under review and confines the disposition to that instrument. Hypothetical presently operative conflicts cannot transform its general authority announcement into an applied directive. ## Propositions common to all eight Associates

### 1. The transition and named-action rules apply

Yang fixes April 1, 1997 commencement; corrected section 306(c)(1) then reaches past, pending and future proceedings. These older deportation proceedings are not exempt merely because they began before enactment. Reyes-Hernandez distinguishes the three identified executive decisions or actions from the particular legal challenges to completed adjudication it preserved. The present objection to association-based selection attacks commencement and the requested restraint of adjudication; continuing proceedings is not a fourth statutory act. Calling the claim constitutional or attaching it to an eventual final-order petition does not by itself change its source in the named decisions.

The saved former procedure remains subject to the actual transitions. No section 309(c)(2) election, thirty-day notice, section 309(c)(3) termination/reinitiation, final deportation order, or wholesale application of the permanent section 1252(b)(9) scheme is presumed. Section 306(c)(2)'s protection of actual former-section-106 judgments is not an independent grant and does not automatically protect these collateral injunctions. The individual exception in section 1252(f)(1) qualifies a prohibition on relief; it supplies no independent subject-matter jurisdiction. The separate subsection (f)(2) condition for enjoining removal under a final order does not govern this pre-final-order posture.

The constitutional-review question below was not resolved by Reyes-Hernandez. Preserving its named-action boundary does not predetermine that additional question or authorize a general constitutional exception.

### 2. The Government's proposed transfer is not an established adequate route

Former section 1105a(a) incorporates chapter 158 for review of final deportation orders subject to its specified exceptions. Paragraph (4) requires determination solely on the administrative record, with the identified nationality exception. Section 2347(a) uses the agency record when a hearing was held. Subsection (b) applies when no hearing preceded the action reviewed; it then requires agency remand if a hearing was legally required, decision on pleadings and affidavits if no hearing was required and no genuine material factual dispute exists, and district-court transfer if no hearing was required and such a dispute exists. Subsection (c)'s materially additional agency evidence and reasonable-omission conditions are separate.

The eight Associates reject using the Government's proposal as the ground for finding this later channel adequate. The incorporation is for review of a final deportation order, which follows a required hearing, and the specific immigration record limitation cannot simply be restated as governing only facts the agency happens to be competent to decide. Relabeling the earlier charge as the action reviewed for subsection (b), while invoking the final order as the jurisdictional trigger, does not overcome both conditions. The Act's express nationality mechanism makes the omission of a general extra-record transfer particularly significant. A separate selection claim does not become legally reviewable merely because the charging decision involved no prior hearing.

This rejects the proposed statutory mechanism for the selection evidence at issue, not every judicial power to consider a constitutional legal argument on a properly reviewable administrative record. Nor does it decide a future constitutional remedy. Any prohibition of subsection (c) must satisfy its own permanent or transitional trigger; no such prohibition proves subsection (b)(3) available. The parties' competing accounts of a deleted Senate proposal are not adopted as legislative facts. Rule 48, a hoped-for comparable procedure, an unfiled habeas proceeding or the Government's advocacy cannot confer missing authority. Kolster's conditional section 2241 holding against section 440(a) does not decide subsection (g), custody, forum, exhaustion, factual scope or relief here.

The already developed district-court record does not make the problem disappear: the former statute does not permit that record to be imported merely because it now exists. Conversely, the Court does not require a new round of discovery or assume that every factual dispute must be tried. The question is lawful capability to receive and decide the material selection evidence.

### 3. A statutory defense and a selection claim do different work

The immigration proceedings can decide genuine defenses to the actual charges, develop facts relevant to deportability and sometimes avoid constitutional adjudication. That is a substantial reason for ordinary channeling. It is not proof that defeating deportability adjudicates retaliation or repairs every supported interim injury. A favorable administrative result might also leave no adverse final order through which to seek the promised review. That possibility does not establish a new future charge, a continuing controversy after every favorable result, or a right to an advisory declaration.

The Six's status grounds and the Two's association/material-support grounds remain distinct. Barakat and Sharif's temporary-resident legalization defeats the original status grounds only. No universal admission of deportability, permanent-residence grant, replacement charge, intended future prosecution or general exemption is inferred. Their individual continuing interests and the scope of any remaining protection require separate attention.

### 4. Eventual review and immediate protection are separate questions

Thunder Basin permits channeling notwithstanding the agency's inability finally to decide constitutional law when the actual route can supply meaningful review. Its particular review-before-collection, expedition and temporary-relief features are not universal statutory requirements, but its conclusion cannot rest on an unidentified remedy. Catholic Social Services recognizes an actual government-created barrier to the prescribed channel, while rejecting a mere policy label. McNary involved different statutory language and a surrender-to-obtain-review obstacle not present for persons already in proceedings; its independent concern about an unavailable record remains relevant.

Ordinary defense costs, anxiety and undifferentiated chill do not establish a right to stop adjudication. Hollywood Motor Car distinguishes a claim whose remedy may be dismissal from a right never to undergo proceedings. It involved a trial court already able to hear the constitutional motion and does not settle whether every competent initial forum may be withheld here. Younger and Dombrowski distinguish ordinary good-faith process from sufficiently supported ongoing suppression; federal-state comity does not become an independent immigration jurisdictional bar. Oestereich's unequivocal statutory exemption is absent, and Harlan's broader timing analysis remains a concurrence.

Here the association-based selection, disparate treatment and present chill are preliminary judicial findings, not merely pleadings. They make the access claim substantial without making the constitutional merits or every equitable requirement true as a matter of law. Immigration authority, foreign-affairs concerns and the Government's conduct-based justification require consideration, not categorical dismissal of constitutional scrutiny or automatic identity between the rights of every alien and every citizen.

### 5. No underlying merits shortcut

Whren separates lawful objective grounds from a distinct forbidden discriminatory purpose. Dawson protects against treating abstract association as a sufficient adverse ground while permitting relevant association evidence. Armstrong addresses authority and a preliminary discovery showing for its indirect racial-selection theory, not a complete merits standard or an automatic answer to this developed association record. R.A.V.'s controlling overbreadth rationale cannot be replaced by its noncontrolling content-selection proposal. None establishes ultimate selection liability, a specific-intent rule for every material-support charge, immunity from valid immigration enforcement or the absence of any judicially cognizable claim here.

The present constitutional contention is sufficiently substantial to require the appropriate jurisdictional analysis. Neither an assessment that it may fail nor the Government's assertion of a facially legitimate ground permits deciding the contested selection merits on assumed jurisdiction. The limited question whether excluding a competent forum is constitutional is also distinct from whether the officials actually violated the First Amendment.

## The two proposed opinion grounds and their coalition limits

### A. Confined review-preserving construction — five Associates

**Joining:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Proposed operative rule:** Section 1252(g) does not foreclose existing section 1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim when the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate the claim and its applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive the factual record necessary to decide it. The claimant must have a concrete personal controversy and an actual review barrier; a constitutional or collateral label, disagreement with prosecutorial discretion, or mere preference for immediate review is insufficient. Original access does not itself establish entitlement to an injunction against the immigration proceeding.

**Permissible-construction basis and answer to the strongest objection:** The named-action and notwithstanding language strongly reaches this suit; these Justices do not evade that fact by calling the final-order version a different completed-adjudication claim. They apply Webster's requirement of clear congressional intent to exclude judicial consideration of colorable constitutional objections, reinforced by Lincoln's preservation and the actual-barrier reasoning of McNary, Catholic Social Services and Kolster. Generic reference to any claim arising from named executive acts does not, in their view, unmistakably resolve the distinct question of eliminating every capable forum for this substantial constitutional claim. The statute's express attention to constitutional issues elsewhere and its preservation of possible individual relief provide context, not direct jurisdiction or wholesale application of the permanent scheme. Section 1331 and the existing action for constitutional relief against federal officers supply the original basis; subsection (f) does not.

The strongest contrary argument is that this qualification removes a class of claims from an unusually express withdrawal and therefore rewrites it. The five accept the qualification only at the point of otherwise unavailable meaningful constitutional adjudication, not for every constitutional challenge to the named acts. Ordinary review-channel restrictions continue to operate when the specified channel can receive the claim and necessary proof. Reyes-Hernandez remains controlling on scope and timing and is supplemented only on its reserved constitutional-access issue. This is a contested statutory construction, not an assertion that the statute never reached selection claims.

**Application and timing:** The agreement that the IJ and Board cannot decide selection, the applicable record restriction and the failure of the proposed section 2347 mechanism establish the review barrier. The preliminary findings of present association-based coercion make it inappropriate to postpone even access to the only competent forum merely on the possibility that a statutory defense may succeed. The finding of substantiality and the availability of present access do not adopt every lower-court merits ground or make every First Amendment injury irreparable automatically. The Court need not decide whether the Constitution would require the same access if no permissible construction existed, or if an actual adequate later route were available.

**Remedy supported by all five:** Affirm the jurisdictional result on this confined ground, reject subsection (f) as a jurisdictional grant, and remand for individualized treatment of continuing controversy and relief. Set aside the appellate judgment's unqualified preservation of relief to the extent necessary for that reconsideration. The existing district-court orders remain provisionally effective in their existing, properly construed scope pending the lower courts' action on remand; this limited preservation rests on the extant preliminary findings of present injury and retained constitutional jurisdiction, not on automatic continuation from a jurisdictional label. No new charge, party, protected conduct or injunction term is added.

For the Six, the court must distinguish denial of dissolution based on newly presented but previously available evidence from an initial entitlement ruling. The new statutory jurisdiction question and the bounded status changes require their own treatment; the Government does not receive a universal right to relitigate already decided preliminary questions by resubmitting old material. For the Two, their later initial injunction requires its own basis and scope. The disputed conduct and immigration arguments, actual present injury, and need for stopping proceedings rather than narrower protection must be assessed under the proper law without treating a threshold ruling as a final merits finding. The Supreme Court's jurisdictional disposition decides none of those ultimate applications.

For Barakat and Sharif, the original status grounds no longer support deportation, but respondents' interpretation that the injunction prevents new charges establishes neither such charges nor a concrete continuing threat. The lower courts must determine the actual surviving dispute and modify or dissolve individual relief if the relevant requirements are absent. Provisional preservation supplies no adjudicated protection against hypothetical charges and no new non-disavowal rule. No permanent injunction, removal order, legislative invalidation, mandatory discovery or hearing, fixed deadline or scheduled Supreme Court return follows.

### B. Express withdrawal with limited review of its constitutionality — three Associates

**Joining:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Scalia and Thomas rely directly on their public Thunder Basin formulation; Kennedy reaches the confined constitutional-review remand without adopting that formulation as a universal rule.

**Proposed operative rule:** The express withdrawal in subsection (g) reaches the present selection attack on commencement and adjudication, and no statutory exemption for this original action can be drawn from a general constitutional-review presumption or subsection (f). Section 1331 nevertheless permits consideration, in this case, of the already presented substantial contention that application of the jurisdictional exclusion itself is unconstitutional and therefore ineffective. That limited question is not a merits adjudication of the selection claim and does not itself establish an entitlement to halt proceedings.

**Answer to the strongest objection:** The absence of an established later factual route and the preliminary present-injury findings defeat reliance on a merely promised remedy. They do not make the phrase covering any claim arising from the named acts reasonably mean that every otherwise inaccessible constitutional claim is outside it. Webster's avoidance principle chooses among permissible constructions; it does not replace the enacted withdrawal. McNary's individual-determination text and Catholic Social Services' application-channel text supplied narrower statutory objects. The three would confront the constitutional objection through the limited original inquiry rather than announce either an implied selection-claim exception or an actual holding sustaining exclusion without deciding it.

The strongest objection to this limited path is that it postpones relief from a supported ongoing injury and may reproduce the very absence of a forum it rejects. Their answer is that the court on remand has a present, identified judicial question about the exclusion's constitutional effectiveness, not a speculative future petition. That inquiry can address the actual missing factfinding route, timing, nature of the injury and claimed necessity of a forum. A court with that limited jurisdiction may consider properly grounded incidental interim relief; its source and conditions must be established rather than inherited from an injunction resting on different jurisdictional reasoning. The three do not hold that a claimant must await a final order before this exclusion question can even be heard.

**Remedy supported by all three:** Vacate the appellate judgment sustaining original selection-claim jurisdiction and direct that both existing preliminary injunctions be set aside. Remand the expressly presented as-applied objection to the jurisdictional exclusion for limited section 1331 consideration, together with the required individual continuing-controversy inquiry. Do not dismiss every claim while leaving that objection unanswered. The existing selection-based injunctions do not automatically become injunctions against an unconstitutional review restriction. Any new interim protection must rest on the limited surviving claim, established authority and the requisite equitable showing; none is granted or forbidden categorically by this handoff.

The Six's earlier injunction cannot obtain statutory insulation from the ordinary no-changed-circumstances ruling, and the Two's later injunction cannot rest on jurisdiction withdrawn by the statute. Setting them aside is jurisdictional, not a finding that either set of preliminary selection findings is false. No alien is ordered removed, no deportation ground is adjudicated, and no assumed habeas petition or future judicial procedure is supplied. Barakat and Sharif's individual live-dispute issues remain for actual determination, not dismissal on an invented new-charge premise.

## Historical comparison and departure audit

### Historical result and distinctions that remain material
