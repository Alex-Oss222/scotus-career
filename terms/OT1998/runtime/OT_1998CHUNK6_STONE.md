## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equal Protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Rational-basis scrutiny; challenger bears burden to negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate fit. Separate any Ohio Constitution state-law ground.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Legislature may rationally find uniform faculty teaching standards a legitimate management goal and exemptions from contractual veto an effective way to further it. No record evidence that bargaining previously caused every faculty workload change is constitutionally demanded under ordinary rational basis. Nothing suggests forbidden animus or a deliberate proxy for a suspect classification on the presented record.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ohio may exempt university workload standards from collective bargaining while retaining bargaining for other public employees because improving classroom instruction is a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis for the occupational line.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Legislature may rationally find uniform faculty teaching standards a legitimate management goal and exemptions from contractual veto an effective way to further it. No record evidence that bargaining previously caused every faculty workload change is constitutionally demanded under ordinary rational basis. Nothing suggests forbidden animus or a deliberate proxy for a suspect classification on the presented record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Leaves independent state constitutional limitations and bargaining rights created elsewhere untouched; this does not establish an employer’s power to discriminate against protected union activity outside the statute.

# Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Eleventh Circuit shall review the district court’s reliability ruling for abuse of discretion under the complete Rule 702 framework and reinstate summary judgment if no admissible evidence supports defect and causation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Rule 702 gatekeeping beyond scientific testimony

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 702 to the knowledge offered, not the expert’s professional label. The trial judge selects the reliability considerations that perform useful work for the particular method and explains the connection between method and conclusion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical, and other specialized expert testimony; Daubert’s considerations are flexible, nonexclusive tools rather than mandatory elements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The tire expert’s experience was relevant, but he did not reliably explain how his inspection method distinguished defect from abuse or why his observations supported the asserted cause. The district court was entitled to reject the unsupported analytical bridge.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require peer review or testing in every technical field, replace the jury on disputed but reliable evidence, or authorize exclusion because the judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

# South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject Alabama's Eleventh Amendment objection, reverse the preclusion and Commerce Clause judgments, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Alabama courts shall provide a constitutionally adequate remedy for the discriminatory tax, choosing among lawful refund, corrective assessment, or other state-law mechanisms consistent with federal law. The Court does not select the remedial formula.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Supreme Court review and nonparty preclusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves reviewability and preclusion before the tax merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The Eleventh Amendment does not prevent Supreme Court review of the federal questions decided in Alabama's own courts. *Richards* forecloses binding these taxpayers through litigation they neither controlled nor were adequately represented in.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** These taxpayers are not bound by litigation in which they were neither parties nor adequately represented, and the Court may review the federal judgment entered by Alabama's courts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The earlier action involved different taxpayers and tax years. Shared taxpayer status and a common public issue supplied neither privity nor legally accountable representation under *Richards*.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding leaves ordinary party preclusion, actual control, adequate class representation, fiduciary representation, and authorized public representation intact.

### Issue 2: Discrimination against foreign corporations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Commerce Clause merits after rejecting preclusion.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Alabama's franchise-tax structure burdens foreign corporations on actual capital employed while permitting domestic corporations to set par value well below actual value. The domestic shares tax does not compensate because it reaches a different event and is not a substantially equivalent intrastate burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Alabama's tax structure discriminates against foreign corporations, and the domestic shares tax does not qualify as a compensatory burden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The practical tax bases favor domestic corporations competing with foreign corporations. Under *Oregon Waste* and *West Lynn Creamery*, labels cannot substitute for equivalence in event, purpose, and burden, which Alabama failed to establish.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding permits evenhanded franchise taxation and genuinely equivalent compensatory charges.

### Issue 3: Remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the need for a constitutionally adequate state remedy and leaves its precise form to Alabama in the first instance.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The remedy must cure the discriminatory exaction consistently with *McKesson*, but federal law does not require the Court to choose among all lawful state remedial mechanisms on this record.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The judgment is reversed and the refund and corrective-tax details are remanded to the Alabama courts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** State law may supply a refund, retrospective equalization, prospective correction, or another adequate mechanism, provided it does not preserve the unconstitutional competitive disadvantage.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No particular refund amount or corrective assessment is ordered by this Court.

# Jones v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the reviewed appellate judgment, vacate the twenty-five-year sentence, and remand for sentencing within the offense authorized by the indictment and verdict.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The aggravated injury element was not charged and found by the jury. No acquittal or automatic new aggravated prosecution is ordered; any further prosecution must satisfy its own procedural and constitutional requirements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Injury and death as aggravated-offense elements

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory element question and uses the sufficient statute-specific ground rather than restating a broader constitutional rule already supplied by *Almendarez-Torres*.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Read §2119’s serious-injury and death provisions as elements of aggravated carjacking offenses, applying the enacted structure and the constitutional avoidance concern reflected in Almendarez-Torres. Stone also maintains that a fact increasing authorized punishment must receive the jury protection owed an element; that broader constitutional position is not an inherited majority holding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The governing version of § 2119 created aggravated offenses whose serious-bodily-injury and death facts had to be charged and admitted or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Jones's twenty-five-year sentence on an indictment and verdict establishing only the base offense must be vacated.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The aggravated provisions alter the maximum punishment for a completed carjacking and identify concrete injury consequences traditionally treated as offense elements. Construing them as elements gives effect to the graded offense structure and avoids allowing a judge to authorize punishment the charged verdict did not permit. Almendarez-Torres is a statutory analogue rather than an already adopted universal constitutional command. Jones was charged and convicted only of the base offense; the judge’s later serious-injury finding cannot supply the omitted aggravated charge or jury determination. Watts does not authorize that increase.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not restrict judicial findings used to select a sentence within the maximum authorized by the conviction or decide charging questions under later statutory text.

# Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band of Chippewa Indians

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit’s judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The declaratory and injunctive framework recognizing the treaty rights remains in force. Minnesota may enforce nondiscriminatory conservation measures only under the standards governing treaty-reserved rights and demonstrated necessity.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Continuing force of the 1837 treaty rights

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads the treaties as the tribes would reasonably have understood them and requires clear federal action to terminate a reserved right. Executive preference, statehood, demographic change, and nonuse do not silently accomplish abrogation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Mille Lacs Band’s 1837 usufructuary rights survived because the 1850 Executive Order lacked lawful authority to revoke them, the 1855 Treaty did not clearly relinquish them, and Minnesota’s admission to the Union did not itself extinguish federal treaty rights.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1850 order’s removal command could not lawfully cancel the separate reservation of use rights, and the 1855 text conveyed new land without the clear surrender Minnesota asserts. Statehood is subject to the Supremacy Clause and supplies no repeal. The Band therefore retains the reserved rights within the treaty territory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding permits necessary, nondiscriminatory conservation regulation and does not determine private land access, criminal conduct unrelated to the treaty, or rights beyond the ceded territory.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm state court’s rejection of the facial challenge absent an established discriminatory rule, excluded voter, unequal franchise or actionable abridgment of campaign expression.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Elections and Voting

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Distinguish source of election expenditures, objective time to organize, ballot access, actual suppression and state control over election administration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A private payment to reimburse publicly administered election costs does not, standing alone, convert an election into private government or change who counts votes. A short schedule can matter if challengers prove genuine inability to campaign or an intent to disable the opposition, but the record supplies no finding of such effects. Remedy cannot be based simply on one side having greater money.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may schedule and publicly administer a referendum while accepting statutorily authorized private reimbursement for election costs if franchise equality, viewpoint neutrality, and meaningful legal participation remain intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A private payment to reimburse publicly administered election costs does not, standing alone, convert an election into private government or change who counts votes. A short schedule can matter if challengers prove genuine inability to campaign or an intent to disable the opposition, but the record supplies no finding of such effects. Remedy cannot be based simply on one side having greater money. The summary-judgment record identifies the challenged reimbursement condition and compressed campaign period, but no fee on an individual voter and no supported discriminatory purpose. Alternative petition routes also remained available. Those features support rejection of the federal challenge without endorsing every privately reimbursed election or revisiting the State’s separate constitutional holdings. Burdick’s calibrated rule applies despite Stone’s disagreement with its particular write-in result. On this summary-judgment record, reimbursement does not impose a voter qualification, and the asserted campaign disadvantage lacks evidence of exclusion or a deliberately disabling schedule. The State’s administration and financing interests consequently support this arrangement without establishing that any schedule, however restrictive, would survive.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No license for secret election rules, selective voter exclusions, private vote tabulation, intimidation or proven deliberately prohibitive campaign timing.

# United States v. Rodriguez-Moreno

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Third Circuit’s judgment and reinstate the § 924(c) conviction, subject to any unresolved claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case for further proceedings consistent with the reinstated conviction and sentence.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Venue for § 924(c) tied to a continuing predicate offense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone identifies the complete offense Congress enacted. Section 924(c) punishes using or carrying the firearm during and in relation to a predicate crime; the continuing predicate is part of the conduct connection, not merely background.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When § 924(c) is predicated on a continuing kidnapping, venue lies in any district where the kidnapping continued, even if the firearm was physically used in another district, because the offense’s essential conduct includes the firearm’s use during and in relation to that kidnapping.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The kidnapping continued in New Jersey while the victim remained confined, and §924(c) requires the firearm use to occur during and in relation to that crime. Unlike the origin of proceeds in Cabrales, the predicate is ongoing conduct integral to the charged firearm offense. Venue therefore follows an essential conduct component rather than the remote consequences of an earlier offense. The conclusion does not make every place touched by a preparatory act or evidentiary circumstance a proper trial venue.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit venue wherever consequences are felt or for a predicate offense that was completed before the firearm conduct began.

# Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc.

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The federal district court shall treat the notice of removal as timely and address subject-matter jurisdiction and any other preserved remand ground.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Trigger for the 30-day removal period

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads “receipt through service or otherwise” in statutory context. The phrase covers lawful variants of service and jurisdictions allowing service without summons; it does not make an unsolicited courtesy copy coercive process.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The § 1446(b) removal period begins when formal service, waiver, or a legally equivalent process subjects the defendant to the court’s authority, not upon informal receipt of a courtesy copy before service.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Murphy was not obliged to appear or default when it received the fax. Its notice was filed within 30 days after formal service, so it was timely.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not extend the period after valid service, excuse gamesmanship in evading service, or create subject-matter jurisdiction where none exists.

# Mitchell v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the sentence and remand for resentencing.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall determine drug quantity from admissible affirmative evidence without treating Mitchell’s silence as evidence against her. Her guilty plea and admitted offense remain undisturbed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Privilege and adverse inference at sentencing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone separates admission of the offense from unresolved sentencing facts. A plea waives trial on guilt but does not compel testimony about disputed facts that can increase punishment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A guilty plea does not waive the Fifth Amendment privilege at sentencing as to disputed facts bearing on punishment, and the sentencing court may not draw an adverse factual inference from the defendant’s silence.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mitchell admitted the conspiracy, not the Government’s asserted quantity. Her testimony could expose her to a higher sentence and related liability. The court therefore had to decide quantity from evidence rather than convert silence into proof.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent denial of an affirmative acceptance-of-responsibility reduction based on failure to carry a lawful burden, decide immunity-backed testimony, or bar use of voluntary statements.

# Wyoming v. Houghton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Wyoming Supreme Court's suppression judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Propose affirmance of suppression through the expressly stated passenger-container modification of Labron. This brief neither enters that judgment nor declares the modification effective before the Court adopts it.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Search of a clearly passenger-owned container

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the scope of the automobile exception and treats Houghton's personal privacy interest in the purse as established under *Wellons*.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Affirm suppression by expressly adopting a limited passenger-container exception to Labron. A known passenger’s closed personal effects do not fall within a driver-derived automobile search solely because they can hold the suspected contraband; the officer needs facts connecting those effects or that passenger to the object sought, or another valid search authority.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When police know before entry that a closed personal effect belongs to a passenger, and vehicle probable cause rests on another occupant’s conduct or possessions, location and capacity alone do not authorize its search. Particularized facts connecting that effect or passenger to the suspected contraband, or an independent exception, remain sufficient. Adopt this limited modification of Labron’s container-scope rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Labron permits this search under its existing object-based rule if the purse can conceal the drugs sought; Wellons does not already create an exception. Stone would change that part of the rule while retaining Labron’s rejection of a separate exigency requirement and its ordinary treatment of vehicle compartments and unclaimed or shared containers. A personal purse carries private effects independently of its temporary location in someone else’s automobile. Di Re’s distinction between a car and an occupant’s person does not decide the purse question, but it supports refusing to transfer suspicion without examining whose privacy is invaded. The proposed line is knowledge before entry, not ownership discovered after contraband is found. Here the officer knew Houghton claimed the purse before opening the wallet. Young’s syringe and admission supplied vehicle probable cause but no reported fact then tied her purse to his drugs. Her claimed lack of identification did not by itself create probable cause for narcotics; her false identity became apparent through the very intrusion requiring justification. Nor can the later pouch, track marks or admission about the black container justify the first search retroactively. Wyoming’s substantial counterargument is that occupants may share drugs, conceal them in another person’s belongings or falsely assign ownership, and that a uniform container rule is easier to administer. Stone would require concrete indicators of those circumstances rather than presume them from companionship. Unknown or shared ownership and evidence of transfer or common concealment remain within ordinary scope. This limited exception changes the legal treatment of identified personal effects and imposes a real constraint on police; it is justified by that distinct privacy interest, not by a claim that Labron never reached containers. Because this purse search rested on the broader rule, affirm the suppression judgment. The proposal becomes governing law only if adopted in a completed Court decision.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No blanket passenger immunity, new warrant requirement for every container, or restoration of a separate automobile-exigency test follows. Independently supported probable cause reaching the passenger’s effect, consent or another established exception can authorize a search. The limited exception is Stone’s proposed modification; the existing Labron rule remains entering law until the Court changes it.

Rule23(b)(2) cannot convert differential personal medical treatment into one indivisible injunction. Classwide claims remain viable only when common answers and representation can lawfully resolve them.
