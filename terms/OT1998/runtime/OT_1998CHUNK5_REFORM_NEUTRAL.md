## National Committee of the Reform Party v. Democratic National Committee

### Verified posture and statutory corrections

The complete Ninth Circuit opinion, 168 F.3d 360, February 9, 1999, is saved as [reform_ninth.txt](../sources/chunk5_c/reform_ninth.txt), from the Public.Resource.Org reporter reproduction and cross-checked against OpenJurist. District Judge Walker dismissed on March 2, 1998. Plaintiffs include the national committee, 1996 campaign committees, California party, and voter John Place; **Perot himself is not a plaintiff**. Defendants include the national/state major-party organizations, campaign entities, and FEC. The opinion distinguishes damages, FEC membership, financing, and certification. The approved grant includes financing and FEC membership; the other damages judgments remain background and do not become new questions.

The financing record reports approximately $29 million for the 1996 Reform campaign and $62 million for each major party, based on prior presidential support. The asserted as-applied defect is inability, with the $1,000 individual candidate-contribution limit, to make up the funding gap. The appellate court says plaintiffs did **not** allege that these constraints reduced their voting strength or ability to field candidates in the different manner reserved in Buckley. Preserve this as its description of the pleaded claim, not a judicial finding that the program can never impair minor parties.

The official **1998 Code, enacted through January 5, 1999**, is saved; exact sections [9002](../sources/chunk5_c/usc1998_26_9002_exact.txt), [9003](../sources/chunk5_c/usc1998_26_9003_exact.txt), [9004](../sources/chunk5_c/usc1998_26_9004_exact.txt), and [9011](../sources/chunk5_c/usc1998_26_9011_exact.txt) are mechanically extracted. These correct two significant shorthand errors in the lower opinion:

* A major party has **25 percent or more** of the preceding presidential vote; a minor party has **at least 5 percent but less than 25 percent**. New parties are neither. The minor pre-election amount uses its prior votes divided by the average prior major-party votes, multiplied by the major entitlement. Section 9004(a)(2)(B) supplies a candidate-based continuity route; (a)(3) permits post-election entitlement for a minor/new candidate reaching five percent, with the stated credit for pre-election entitlement. Do not say a new party can never obtain public funds.
* Section **9003(c)** permits a minor/new-party candidate's qualified expenses up to the **major-party entitlement**, with private contributions covering expenses beyond public receipts. It does not cap that candidate's total spending at its smaller subsidy. The $1,000 individual-candidate contribution limit is a separate FECA rule. Section 9004(b) separately caps combined entitlements after accounting for received/expended/retained contributions; public money remains restricted to qualified expenses and designated restoration/loan repayment. Section 9004(d)'s $50,000 combined candidate/immediate-family spending certification is a condition of public funding and includes the vice-presidential candidate's expenditures. The dispute is inability to raise the difference under the contribution rule, not a nonexistent statutory command to stop at $29 million.

Section **2 U.S.C. 437c(a)(1)** requires presidential appointment with Senate consent of six voting members and says no more than three may affiliate with the same party. It names no party and reserves no seat to Democrats or Republicans. The reported equal major-party division is practice, not the statute's command. The Code still prints ex-officio legislative members; do not treat that printing as authority for present unconstitutional participation. **Legi-Tech** already addresses a lawfully reconstituted Commission after congressional participation ended and preserves real reconsideration requirements. This case challenges the affiliation limitation, not that earlier defect. Current text and original pages are saved as `usc1998_2_437c_exact.txt` and the associated HTML.

### Threshold, channel, and available relief

The lower court **does not reach the affiliation-cap merits**. It finds no showing that invalidating a cap designed to prevent one party's domination would make minor-party representation more likely: the same six persons could be appointed without it. Personal concrete injury, traceability, and likely redress must be assessed separately. An asserted exclusion from an actual appointment opportunity or a concrete defective enforcement adjudication would pose different questions, but neither may be invented. A political disagreement with the Commission's makeup does not alone establish a right to a seat, nor does its makeup alone establish partisan enforcement.

The funding claim has an identified financial disadvantage; that does not establish success on equal protection. Facial support-based funding and this pleaded as-applied gap claim are separable. Buckley's reserved future challenge requires attention to actual pleaded harm and statutory operation, not an irrebuttable rule that enacted financing always survives or that every unequal subsidy fails.

The original channels matter if a constitutional claim is found substantial. **Section 437h**, saved from the 1998 Code, authorizes the national committee and an eligible presidential voter among the specified plaintiffs and directs certification of FECA constitutional questions to the circuit en banc. **Section 9011(b)** authorizes appropriate implementation/construction suits by the Commission, national committees, and presidential voters; ordinarily those proceedings go to a three-judge district court with direct Supreme Court appeal. The lower court relies on California Medical's standing/frivolousness exception and distinguishes these channels. Review of a single judge's refusal to convene or certify does not authorize bypassing a necessary statutory tribunal after rejecting that refusal's premise. Section 9011(a) separately governs review of Commission action in the D.C. Circuit with its thirty-day filing condition; it is not a substitute for the subsection (b) implementation/construction suit at issue. A tribunal ruling must identify whether the claim arises under FECA or the Fund Act and which route follows from that classification. Certification is not a new ungranted merits controversy, but an antecedent remedial consequence if the Court finds a live substantial statutory constitutional claim. Lack of standing on one component does not decide the other. No FEC damages cause of action, election replay, seat appointment, payment award, or invalidation of unrelated party practices follows from these two questions.

### Governing law and two-path test

**Buckley v. Valeo, Part III** permits demonstrated-support allocation and voluntary funding; it rejects facial equal-subsidy demands while reserving a properly supported as-applied challenge. **Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith (September 21, 1998)** is particularly close current law: all eight Associates adopt the objective demonstrated-support funding rationale at the actual burden; the Court preserves review for substantial suppression, viewpoint favoritism, or discriminatory administration and rejects a blanket funding exemption from constitutional review. Ballot access and subsidy are distinct; a qualified candidate does not automatically acquire a right to equal institutional support. **Day**, **Kentucky Right to Life**, and **Colorado Republican** require meaningful association and actual transaction-specific scrutiny but do not erase Buckley's support-sensitive public-financing holdings. **Kruse/Suster** concern mandatory expenditure cutoffs; this voluntarily accepted program must be analyzed with its actual supplementation route.

For funding, one supported path affirms the facial and presently pleaded as-applied dismissal: the formula tracks demonstrated electoral support, private supplementation exists, the individual-contribution restriction remains independently reviewable, and disparity alone is the very feature Buckley considered. The strongest counterargument is that decades of operation and the combined subsidy/contribution system can entrench incumbents or suppress practical competition notwithstanding formal neutrality; Buckley's reservation and Florida's limits forbid categorical dismissal of a materially supported claim. A contrary path identifies a genuinely sufficient alleged suppression/constitutional burden, distinguishes the mere equalization claim, and sends that substantial question through the statutory channel; it must not invent missing allegations or equate lower funding with inability to field a campaign. The published account is sufficient for a bounded claim-specific decision under Engine §14 without the original complaint, but not for declaring unreported facts proved.

For affiliation, one supported path affirms jurisdictional dismissal for failed redressability, leaving appointment/separation-of-powers/equal-protection merits open. The strongest alternative asks whether the challenged legal cap actually excludes or impairs a concrete opportunity to compete and whether removal would redress that injury even without guaranteeing appointment. Northeastern Florida's opportunity principle distinguishes certain injury from certainty of ultimate success, but the cap does not by its terms exclude Reform affiliates. That alternative needs an actual causal theory supported by the pleadings; “major-party commission” is not enough. Lujan and current Raines/Commerce standing distinctions prohibit replacing the plaintiff's missing connection with general institutional dissatisfaction.

## Exact statutory and source checks

The independent source check confirms the corrections above. For minor and new parties, section 9003(c)(1) uses the major-party entitlement as the qualified-expense ceiling. Section 9003(c)(2) separately limits acceptance and expenditure or retention of private contributions to the excess of qualified expenses over public receipts. These are eligibility certifications under penalty of perjury; they do not promise successful private fundraising. Section 9003(a) also requires furnishing requested evidence and records, an audit and examination, and required repayments. Section 9003(d) preserves payment for qualified expenses incurred while actively seeking election in more than one State after a candidate withdraws, while requiring unused payments to be returned. Section 9003(e) retains its television closed-captioning certification. None of those additional conditions is separately challenged in the authorized questions.

Section 9004(a)(2)(B)'s candidate-continuity route avoids treating a change in party name as necessarily losing all pre-election eligibility: a returning nonmajor presidential candidate with at least five but less than twenty-five percent in the previous election may qualify, subject to section 9003(a) and (c), using all his prior votes and the statutory offset against paragraph (2)(A). Section 9004(a)(3)'s post-election entitlement uses the current election's vote ratio and credits the paragraph (2) entitlement. Section 9004(b) separately limits aggregate payments to the lower of qualified expenses less received-and-expended-or-retained contributions, or the major-party entitlement less those contributions. Section 9004(c) permits only qualified expenses and the specified repayment/restoration uses; its restoration route excludes contributions already received and expended for qualified expenses. Section 9004(d)'s personal-funds certification includes both members of the ticket and immediate family as defined in subsection (e). These statutory qualifications are not a separate holding or a factual finding that this campaign satisfied every condition.

Section 437c(a)(1) sets a maximum of three appointees affiliated with one party; it does not allocate three seats to each named major party or legally disqualify Reform affiliates. Its printed ex-officio provision does not supersede the actual current Legi-Tech law. The constitutional membership claim here concerns the affiliation maximum, not a newly asserted defect in reconsideration of a particular enforcement matter.

Section 9011(b)(2) expressly grants the implementing/construction tribunal jurisdiction without requiring exhaustion of administrative or other remedies. Section 9011(a)'s separate D.C. Circuit review of Commission action within thirty days does not replace that route. Section 437h separately requires immediate circuit en banc certification for its FECA constitutional questions; its text supplies neither injury nor a substitute three-judge district tribunal. If the Court rejects the substantiality or standing premise that permitted a single judge to dismiss, the ensuing procedure must follow the correct claim-specific statute. No appointment, payment amount, damages award or new private enforcement cause follows merely from rejecting that threshold premise.

## Chronology and actual overlap

### Sullivan - March 3, 1999

The completed [public decision](../entering-law/american_manufacturers_v_sullivan_merits_1999-03-03.md) has two distinct rules. Part I requires a governmental basis for responsibility for the private carrier's particular withholding choice; statutory permission, regulation and public support do not alone suffice. Part II rejects an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service inferred from general program eligibility, while preserving fair adjudication and separately supported service-specific or continuation interests.

Neither rule decides Reform's constitutional objection to the Government's own allocation statute or appointment limit. The challenge does not depend on attributing a private carrier's conduct to the State. Its subsidy amounts and conditions are expressly defined by sections 9003-9004; there is no general-eligibility inference that every desired subsidy must be paid immediately. Conversely, statutory definition of a payment entitlement does not itself defeat a First Amendment or equal-protection challenge to that definition. Sullivan adds no immunity for public subsidies and no election-financing holding. Its actual scope therefore **does not displace the existing Reform premise**. The original packet's caution is satisfied for this decision.

### NFFE / FLRA v. Interior - March 3, 1999

The completed [public decision](../entering-law/nffe_and_flra_v_interior_merits_1999-03-03.md) rejects categorical statutory prohibition and categorical statutory compulsion of one comprehensive midterm-bargaining regime. It recognizes a bounded, reasonably explained Authority choice and remands the particular contractual-negotiability determination because the existing alternative explanation remains tied to an erroneous premise of statutory necessity. Genuine statutory authority, a discernible agency ground and a reasoned exercise of the authorized choice remain distinct.

Reform's challenged funding formula and affiliation maximum are enacted provisions, not a demonstrated FEC choice among competing constructions. The record identifies no FEC interpretive order whose explanation supplies the disputed allocation, no contractual-negotiability question, and no agency ground that depends on a displaced labor-law premise. NFFE does not confer deference to the FEC on a constitutional question or validate a statutory classification merely because an agency administers it. Its preservation of Congress's actual allocations is consistent with keeping sections 437h, 9011(a) and 9011(b) distinct, but changes none of their texts or tribunal requirements. Its statutory-entitlement/contractual-negotiability distinction supplies no right to equal funding or a Commission seat. **No existing Reform decisional premise is displaced.** If a later proposed path introduces a particular FEC construction or administrative reconsideration, its actual source and authority must be supplied; NFFE cannot supply those missing facts.

### Jackson v. Benson - March 16, 1999

The completed [public decision](../entering-law/jackson_v_benson_merits_1999-03-16.md) rejects categorical approval of the demonstrated provider-cost purchase of integrated devotional instruction based solely on neutral eligibility, parental choice and restrictive endorsement. Its rule preserves genuinely independent private benefits and reliably separated secular services and expressly supplies no constitutional subsidy entitlement. Its separate remedy requires relief confined to the actual federal conflict and lawful severable applications; it adopts no new general facial-challenge formula.

Reform concerns the Government's support-sensitive allocation for presidential campaigns and an affiliation maximum, not the Establishment Clause limits on purchased devotional instruction. Jackson does not replace Buckley or Florida's election-financing standard, prohibit demonstrated-support allocations, establish a right to equal subsidies, or decide Article III appointment injury. Its rejection of automatic immunity from a neutral/private-choice label is consistent with the existing packet's requirement to examine the actual burden of the funding classification; it does not decide what that burden is here. Its conflict-specific remedy likewise permits no payment award or appointed seat without an established claim and lawful channel. **No existing Reform decisional premise is displaced.** Jackson's substantive and remedial coalitions remain confined to that decision and supply no proxy for a vote in this case.
