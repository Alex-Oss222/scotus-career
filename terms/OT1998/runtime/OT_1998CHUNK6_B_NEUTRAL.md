### Issue 1: Venue for § 924(c) tied to a continuing predicate offense

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Cabrales (June 1, 1998) distinguishes the charged offense’s conduct from a circumstance supplied by earlier crimes: Florida laundering cannot be tried in Missouri merely because unlawful proceeds originated there. Section 924(c) here incorporates an ongoing kidnapping, so venue turns on whether that predicate conduct is part of the firearm offense rather than merely the historical origin of an object. Continued confinement in New Jersey is materially different from Cabrales’s completed antecedent crime.
* **Other Governing Law:** Constitution Article III venue clause; Sixth Amendment Vicinage Clause; 18 U.S.C. §§ 924(c), 1201, and 3237(a).
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Cabrales requires identification of the essential conduct elements rather than effects alone.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Third Circuit vacated the § 924(c) conviction for improper venue because the firearm use occurred outside New Jersey.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The defendant places venue where the firearm was used, while the government includes the district through which the ongoing kidnapping continued. Cabrales distinguishes conduct from an antecedent circumstance; the statutory relationship between kidnapping and firearm use determines the comparison.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If continuing kidnapping is an essential conduct component, venue may lie where it continued despite firearm use elsewhere. If it is only a circumstance for this charge, venue must rest on another legally sufficient conduct connection. The ruling creates no venue based on remote effects alone.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Wyoming Supreme Court suppressed methamphetamine found in Houghton’s purse, holding that probable cause directed to the driver and car did not justify the passenger-container search.

Labron presently supplies an object-based container rule, and Wellons does not itself establish an ownership exception. Later discoveries cannot retroactively justify the first intrusion; any independent justification must be supported at the time it operates.

### Issue 1: Search of a clearly passenger-owned container

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Labron / Kilgore (July 1, 1996) allows a probable-cause search of a readily mobile automobile without additional exigency and limits its scope by the object sought, including containers capable of holding it. Wellons (May 30, 1995) preserves a separate privacy interest in personal luggage but expressly leaves a valid exception capable of authorizing that search and does not restore the displaced Sanders automobile rule. A privacy interest is therefore not itself a categorical owner-specific exemption from an otherwise valid automobile search.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; *United States v. Ross*; *California v. Acevedo*; *United States v. Di Re*.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ross and Acevedo support the object-based container rule; Di Re distinguishes the authority to search a vehicle from authority to search an occupant’s person. A purse is an effect rather than the passenger’s body, so that analogy does not itself settle its treatment. Wellons recognizes the passenger’s privacy interest but leaves valid search exceptions operative.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Court should adopt a limited exception for a closed personal effect known to belong to a passenger when vehicle probable cause derives from another occupant and no independent nexus to the effect is established. The exception is a proposed modification of Labron, not a rule already supplied by the entering law.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Wyoming Supreme Court suppressed methamphetamine found in Houghton’s purse, holding that probable cause directed to the driver and car did not justify the passenger-container search.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Wyoming invokes probable cause to search the vehicle and the object-based container scope of the automobile exception. Houghton emphasizes the purse’s identified separate ownership and absence of individualized suspicion. Wellons recognizes her privacy interest but does not decide whether an otherwise valid search exception reaches it.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Apply Labron’s object-based scope to the actual probable cause and container. Suppression requires an established defect or a candidly justified change in governing law; Wellons does not supply an automatic passenger-ownership exception.
