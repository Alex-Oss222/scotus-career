## Effect of the counsel source: two paths and their limits

**Accepted fact and legal significance.** Walter was offered an additional or replacement lawyer to examine Burke's performance before Burke's federal appointment. Walter declined the offered examination; Burke later sought withdrawal upon learning of proposed sentencing-ineffectiveness evidence in Karl's litigation. The courts denied withdrawal, and the appellate court treated the then-asserted claim as within Walter's waiver of new representation and its possible benefits. This prevents an account that Walter was never offered another lawyer or that Burke never later sought withdrawal. The District Court's separate assessment that the alleged sentencing failure was not ineffective assistance remains a reported ruling; it is not an independently adopted Supreme Court determination of the later gas claim.

**Path A — give the earlier representation choice conclusive effect against the present ineffective-assistance cause route.** The strongest supporting argument is that the earlier appellate court expressly included the possible discovery of previously unknown attorney errors within the benefits of new representation that Walter declined. The source therefore has greater force than a bare absence of a request for counsel. Its limit is equally concrete: the described withdrawal dispute concerned the particular then-asserted sentencing issue. The file does not establish identity with the later gas-related claim, the scope or adequacy of the March 2 state bar, or every applicable exception. The representation choice occurred after the original state omission. No Justice adopts a universal later-claim waiver or uses it as an independently sufficient final default ground on this record.

**Path B — retain the separate cause inquiry while giving the earlier choice its actual scope.** Carrier permits constitutionally ineffective assistance, distinguished from ordinary error, to supply cause under its own prerequisites; it ordinarily requires presentation as an independent state claim. Coleman and the actual March 2 bar remain separate. Trest does not make default jurisdictional. Current Gray v. Netherland keeps related evidence and distinct claims separate. The strongest objection is that this approach might disregard Walter's actual refusal of offered assistance. It does not: the refusal and later withdrawal ruling are admitted and potentially material to a properly identified argument, but a final conclusion must connect them to the claim and default actually at issue. None of these Justices requires a new hearing or a factual reconstruction merely because that connection is not yet established.

The five vacatur supporters continue to correct the actual general-unavailability cause rationale: Reed, Engle and Carrier distinguish a reasonably unavailable claim from unfavorable precedent or counsel's failure to use an available theory; Gray v. Lucas's 1983 gas litigation demonstrates the existence of the general legal/factual theory before 1987. A later circuit gas-invalidity decision supplies no missing cause, and later election-based ripeness cannot excuse omission during state proceedings when gas was the sole method. The new counsel facts neither cure that identified error nor establish every alternative ground against Walter. Their remand permits a genuinely supported independent cause theory and any lawfully justified new interim order, without treating one as already established.

Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer continue to oppose immediate removal of protection while the actual claim-specific cause inquiry remains. Their objection is not an assumption that another lawyer was unavailable: the new source defeats that premise. Their narrower objection is that the earlier representation waiver and sentencing ruling do not themselves dispose of the later gas-related ineffective-assistance route or establish every relevant factual-availability predicate. They accept the available-basis rule and would require prompt lawful consideration, without affirming the circuit's categorical no-basis conclusion.
