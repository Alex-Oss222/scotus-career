## I. City of Cincinnati v. Kruse — No. 98-454

**Research cutoff:** December 16, 1998. This is the authorized merits date; no argument date is invented.

### Record and judgment commitment

The reviewed ceiling governs a candidate's or candidate committee's campaign expenditures, including campaign use of contributed and personal/immediate-family funds. It excludes independent expenditures, uncompensated volunteer services and the specified party slate/sample-ballot publications. It is therefore neither a limit on independent third-party spending nor merely a restriction on a source of candidate receipts. The amount is three times annual council compensation, approximately $140,000 at the relevant time.

For summary judgment, the challengers accepted the City's factual assertions. The conclusions below accept its described fundraising burden, survey evidence and campaign experience for that purpose; they do not reject the affidavits as false or turn the assumption into findings after trial. The legal sufficiency and fit of those facts remain questions for decision.

**All eight Associates vote to affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment invalidating the mandatory ceiling.** They support the following common sufficient propositions and ordinary affirmance consequences. No Associate requires a further evidentiary hearing to decide this law's validity on the accepted record.

|---|---|---|
| K1 — Rule applicable to this expenditure ceiling | A compulsory ceiling on the total quantity of a candidate's campaign expression is subject to the demanding First Amendment review preserved by Buckley. The source of campaign funds does not turn the candidate's spending into a donor's contribution. | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |

The timing of its evidence matters because the 1995 spending experience preceded those controls; it cannot, by itself, establish why the available, more directly fitted controls leave this total-spending restraint necessary. Proof that an effective campaign can be run below the cap does not authorize the government to select the permissible quantity of advocacy. The accepted evidence may establish serious concerns without establishing the constitutional sufficiency of this means.

### Competing paths, sufficient propositions and remedies

**B1 — Registration.** The invalidating path applies Meyer's protection of the available speaker pool: excluding at least 400,000 otherwise qualified but unregistered voters substantially restricts circulation. Registration is not a sufficient proxy for residence, accessibility or honesty. Sworn identifying/address information, signature review and direct fraud enforcement address traceability more closely. The operative conclusion invalidates registration as a condition of circulation; it does not invalidate the age condition or decide whether a properly presented residency rule would be lawful.

The strongest sustaining path applies Anderson–Burdick to an ascertainable qualification within the legally operative petition process. The State can maintain a usable identification/enforcement system without proving a separate fraud by each excluded speaker, and an otherwise qualified voter can enroll. Its strongest objection is that electing not to register does not forfeit political advocacy, the excluded class is substantial, and the State's own affidavit/address mechanisms serve the claimed interest without that exclusion. O'Connor and Breyer nevertheless regard the enrollment step and enforcement connection as proportionate on this facial record. The other six regard the lost speakers and mismatch as decisive. No Justice treats Timmons's candidate-nomination holding as automatically controlling this speech setting.

**B2 — Personal name at the encounter.** All eight support the operative rule that Colorado cannot compel every circulator to expose a personal name on a badge during the protected solicitation on this record. The personal-attribution demand operates at the moment of political advocacy; the reported deterrent effect, especially for unpopular issues, is an actual burden. McIntyre supplies protection against compelled individual attribution, while Meyer and the relevant Riley principles preserve the protection of a compensated speaker.

The strongest contrary argument is that a circulator procures legally operative signatures and a contemporaneous name assists witnesses in reporting wrongdoing. It does not establish the necessary added value of this exposure over the retained identifying affidavit, signature assurances, fraud penalties, protests and invalidation mechanisms. These safeguards are relevant alternatives, not a finding that fraud is impossible. A later, accessible affidavit differs in immediacy and exposure from compulsory display to every person approached. The judgment does not establish anonymity from legitimate investigation or adjudicate the separate paid/volunteer and employer fields.

**B3 — Final individual report.** Six Associates support the operative rule that the reviewed demand to publish each paid circulator's identifying information and individual compensation is insufficiently fitted to the asserted information/enforcement interests. Identifying proponents and aggregate spending reveals who finances the initiative and its paid effort; each messenger is not thereby a financial source or candidate contributor. Payment does not alone strip that speaker's protection. The final report's later timing reduces the badge burden but does not end the separate disclosure inquiry. No monetary threshold distinguishes a consequential financing source from any compensated messenger. The retained amount-per-signature/aggregate information is distinct from the invalidated individual identity/payment package.

The strongest contrary path preserves public information about payees as well as payers: identifying compensated circulators and individual payments can permit checking claimed spending and the extent of paid qualification activity. O'Connor and Breyer accept that substantial relation and the later reporting mechanism on this record. The other six find that the available sponsor and aggregate information and verification machinery leave the asserted added need insufficient to justify this individual exposure. They do not hold that all referendum disclosure lacks a legitimate informational interest or that a demonstrated-reprisal claim is the only way to contest an inadequately fitted disclosure demand.

**B4 — Monthly individual report.** The six reach the same invalidity conclusion separately for monthly individual identifying and paid/owed information. Publication during the circulation campaign creates an additional timely exposure, without remedying the source-versus-messenger mismatch. O'Connor and Breyer separately sustain monthly timing because contemporaneous expenditure information can serve its purpose before qualification and the vote; they do not equate it with a personally worn badge or deny any chilling possibility.

**B5 — Limits on the judgment.** All eight agree that the Court should state the disposition at the reviewed component scope. The lower court invalidated §1-40-121(2) in its entirety, but the reviewed question concerns circulator identity and payment amounts. The independent validity of sponsor, measure and other monthly prescriptions remains open. The six invalidating votes do not independently adjudicate every field of subsection (2), direct their conclusive reinstatement, or create a replacement reporting statute. The two sustaining votes likewise do not convert an unpresented field into a separately decided constitutional holding. Any remaining remedial/severability implementation follows the actual lower judgment and ordinary law; it is not a grant of automatic future enforcement of unadjudicated requirements.

### Alternative-path test and individual commitments

The strongest alternative is to preserve dismissal because state law eliminated County discretion or because the 1983 nonobjection necessarily approved the operative arrangement. The first theory contradicts L1's already-decided statutory question. The second requires the actual submission terms and cannot clear the earlier unidentified changes contrary to L2. A narrower affirmance of a genuinely supported final-ordinance clearance determination could coexist with nonuse of remaining uncleared changes, but the attachment/nonobjection recitation alone does not support that conclusive determination. Remand addresses the unresolved legal scope without treating the research gap as the County's failure to produce a document.

The remedy fork is between automatic restoration of the extension arrangement and a bounded reconsideration of actual tenure circumstances. The existing mandate expressly reserved tenure and selected no interim plan. Consequently the latter is the supported path. Administrative difficulty does not itself satisfy Clark's confined extreme-circumstance reservation or authorize ordinary future elections under the uncleared governmental system.
