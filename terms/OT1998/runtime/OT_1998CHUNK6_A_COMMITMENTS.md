## 2. Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael — March 23, 1999

### Issue-specific conclusions

**K1 — Common Rule 702 obligation.** All eight Associates would hold that original Rule 702's preliminary reliability-and-assistance requirement reaches technical and other specialized knowledge as well as scientific knowledge. The nature of the particular expertise and the proposition offered govern the screening inquiry. Calling an opinion experiential does not exempt its inferential connection from scrutiny, while calling a method untested or unpublished does not alone establish that a particular technical proffer is unreliable.

**K2 — Flexible method and review.** All eight would permit the judge to use Daubert's considerations when they help evaluate this particular proffer, without requiring every consideration for every kind of expertise. The inquiry must address a reliably supported method and its application to the proposition offered, not demand that the proponent prove the ultimate tort claim at the admissibility stage. Joiner's abuse-of-discretion allocation governs both the reasonable choice of how to evaluate reliability and the ultimate evidentiary ruling; categorical legal exemptions remain independently correctable. Flexibility is a duty to fit the inquiry, not permission to dispense with it. No new Rule 702 amendment, universal checklist, expert appointment, hearing requirement or independent-identical-study condition is adopted.

**K3 — Actual exclusion and judgment.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would sustain the exclusion as within the District Court's discretion. Reconsideration expressly acknowledged flexible inquiry and possible accepted uses of visual inspection. The remaining concern was Carlson's particular inference from the tire's observed condition, including the significance assigned to the wear and damage, to exclusion of misuse and identification of a defect. Credentials and a four-sign scheme do not themselves establish the reliability of the threshold that treated the observed signs as insufficient. The court was permitted to find that connection inadequately supported. These seven reverse the Eleventh Circuit and remand for a conforming disposition sustaining the exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment on the supplied record, which identifies no remaining defect evidence. They do not hold exclusion mandatory for every reasonable judge, discredit all visual tire inspection, or find the tire's actual cause of failure.

Stevens agrees with K1–K2 and with rejection of the Eleventh Circuit's categorical experience exemption, but would vacate and remand for that court to review the actual exclusion under the correct legal standard. His commitment is not a vote to admit Carlson, reopen every foundation submission or require a new hearing.

### Common commitments by component

Richards forecloses treating shared taxpayer interests, awareness of earlier litigation or vigorous advocacy there as a substitute for consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representative authority or another constitutionally sufficient binding relationship. No unreported control, consent, waiver, notice finding or distinct immunity claim is invented.

**S2 — Actual discrimination and compensation.** Each would hold the presented foreign/domestic franchise-tax structure discriminatory because the respective bases impose the supported heavier burden on comparable foreign corporations. Comparison of the practical burden, rather than rate labels alone, governs. A compensatory defense requires the State to identify an actual domestic burden, demonstrate a roughly equivalent nonexcessive interstate burden, and establish substantially equivalent taxable events functioning as mutually exclusive proxies. Invoking other domestic taxes or an aggregate fiscal balance does not satisfy those cumulative predicates. On the stated record the defense does not establish the matching burden and equivalence necessary to justify this discriminatory structure. No numerical tax ratio, exact refund, universally equivalent tax pair or categorical prohibition of different nominal taxpayers is supplied.

The Court does not choose a replacement tax base, require an assessment against particular domestic firms, declare every taxpayer entitled to a refund, or decide severability without its applicable state predicates.

## 4. Jones v. United States — March 24, 1999

### Statutory issue, constitutional boundary and remedy

The original firearm-possession version of section 2119 governs the December 1992 offense. The later serious-harm-intent language construed in Holloway does not enter this charge. The operative alternatives are a basic fifteen-year offense, an injury provision authorizing twenty-five years, and a death provision authorizing up to life. The injury issue is concrete; death helps explain statutory structure but is not a factual finding about Jones.

**J1 — Element construction supported by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** Read the injury and death conditions as elements of aggravated carjacking offenses. Their placement in penalty paragraphs is relevant but not dispositive: the text remains fairly capable of describing graded offenses, and the sharply different maximum punishments turn on harm resulting from the present offense. The traditional offense-defining role of that harm and the serious constitutional doubt attending removal of it from indictment and jury support this construction. Almendarez-Torres's element construction is controlling for its own statute and supplies a substantial statutory analogy; its four-Justice avoidance opinion is not mislabeled a universal constitutional holding.

**J2 — Allocation and disposition under that reading.** Hamling's indictment requirement and Gaudin's jury/beyond-reasonable-doubt rule follow once the fact is an element. The charge and general verdict omitted serious bodily injury; the existing conviction therefore cannot support the aggravated range. These five would reverse the Ninth Circuit's sentence-sustaining judgment, vacate the aggravated sentence and remand for sentencing within the original basic offense's fifteen-year maximum on this charge and verdict. No exact replacement sentence, acquittal, withdrawal of the conviction or automatic authority for a further aggravated prosecution is supplied; availability of a properly charged further proceeding is reserved.

**J3 — Penalty construction supported by O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.** These three would affirm the reviewed treatment and increased sentence on the presented classification objection. The opening description identifies the completed offense, followed by a common punishment command and dependent penalty clauses; the gradation of penalties is not by itself a statutory direction to create additional elements. The original provision's structure differs from the reciprocal-cross-reference and aggravated-reentry construction adopted in Almendarez-Torres. Their constitutional ground is that the charged firearm-taking offense remains fully subject to indictment, jury decision and reasonable-doubt proof, while the consequences of the already proved crime may perform the traditional sentencing function of measuring its gravity under this enacted penalty structure. The injury finding neither supplies a missing fact of that defined offense nor shifts the burden of proving its guilt. They recognize that legislative labels alone cannot remove an actual element and that the higher ceiling creates the strongest objection to this allocation, but do not find the bounded harm-based grading here constitutionally forbidden. They do not claim that Almendarez-Torres's dissent controls or that McMillan's unchanged ceiling itself resolves this higher-ceiling question. Their proposed constitutional permission is bounded to the present statutory classification and does not authorize taking an independently established offense element from the jury.

## 5. Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band of Chippewa Indians — March 24, 1999

### Separately tested extinction grounds

**M1 — 1850 order.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that this combined revocation-and-removal order did not terminate the retained use rights. The supplied lower-court account establishes an unauthorized compelled-removal component under the Removal Act's consent structure and a connected removal purpose. On that supported account, the revocation cannot be severed into an independently chosen executive action without changing the order's operation. They need not decide that the President lacked every power to revoke under the treaty's pleasure clause, that every revocation requires a new substantive good-faith test, or that subsequent nonenforcement independently restores extinguished rights.

Kennedy and Scalia take the contrary legal view of severability: the treaty independently authorizes revocation and the revocation command can operate apart from the unauthorized removal direction. A shared policy purpose does not by itself deprive a legally authorized and independently operative command of effect. They would reverse on that ground. They do not defend compelled removal or invent evidence that the President privately would have chosen a different order. Thomas reserves this ground because M3 supplies his sufficient reason for reversal.

**M2 — 1855 treaty.** The five affirming Associates conclude that this land cession did not surrender the separately retained off-reservation hunting, fishing and gathering use rights. The agreement must be read in the sense reasonably understood by the tribal parties and against its supported land-purchase negotiations, which did not address surrender of these independent privileges. Dion permits abrogation without an invariable express-word formula, but requires clear evidence that the disputed right was considered and relinquished. Menominee and Fishing Vessel resist casual implication. Klamath's reservation-dependent rights do not make every land cession extinguish independent use rights; Bourland distinguishes loss of exclusion-derived nonmember regulation from retained personal use. The three reversing Associates do not reach this independent ground and are not counted as agreeing or disagreeing with M2.

**M3 — Statehood and Race Horse.** The five affirming Associates conclude that Minnesota's admission did not itself extinguish these federal treaty rights. Reserved use rights can coexist with state authority to impose lawful, necessary nondiscriminatory conservation regulation; state sovereignty does not require their cancellation. Race Horse's treaty-specific temporary-right analysis must be distinguished from the right retained here: termination at the President's pleasure does not itself identify state admission as the terminating event. Its broader suggestion that equal footing alone extinguishes such treaty rights is rejected to that extent; that proposition cannot govern after the compatible state-regulation approach recognized in Winans, Tulee and Fishing Vessel. They do not displace Race Horse's distinct construction of its own unoccupied-lands condition without deciding that separate treaty.

Thomas would reverse under Race Horse. He reads the pleasure-qualified privilege as a temporary treaty use right rather than an indefinite right intended to burden the State's ordinary regulatory authority after admission. In his view Race Horse's treatment of such temporary hunting rights remains the controlling analogy; the later cases concerning differently reserved rights do not erase that holding. His ground does not assert that equal footing nullifies all federal treaties or that all tribal property or land rights disappear at statehood. Kennedy and Scalia reserve M3 once the 1850 order is dispositive; no statehood join is inferred from their common judgment.

**M4 — Remaining scope and remedy.** The five affirm the recognition of surviving treaty rights against the extinction grounds properly presented. They do not reopen every lower-court ruling about other Bands, allocation, private-land access or detailed conservation administration merely because those subjects appeared in the larger appeal. No supplied later event establishes a distinct termination premise. No unrestricted harvest, numerical allocation, universal right to enter private lands or tribal veto over every state rule follows. Any nonparty preclusion or prospective-officer question retains Richards and the actual simulated Coeur d'Alene rule at its own scope. No title conveyance, retrospective compensation or general immunity from procedure is entered. The two M1 dissenters would reverse the declaration of survival because of the effective 1850 revocation; Thomas would reverse it because of statehood. None supplies an affirmative disposition of ungranted resource-management questions.

## 6. Brower v. Washington — March 25, 1999

### Separate reimbursement and timing conclusions

**B1 — Reimbursement.** All eight Associates would affirm rejection of the presented federal equal-protection and speech challenge. A private beneficiary's reimbursement of a special election's public costs does not itself impose a voter fee, exclude a voter, transfer administration or counting to the beneficiary, or establish a constitutionally suppressive campaign burden. The actual arrangement retained public administration, left eligible voters able to participate, and left ordinary initiative and referendum petition routes available. Those routes are relevant to the complete access picture, not a conclusive answer to every preferential-access claim. Reform Party rejects resource parity as an independent constitutional entitlement while preserving actual suppressive or discriminatory applications.

**B2 — Compressed campaign.** All eight would separately affirm on the supplied summary-judgment record. A campaign period shorter than sixty days is not, solely by its duration and the beneficiary's greater resources, a severe burden on opponents' political expression. The supported account identifies no exclusion, restriction on opponents' own speech or funds, or evidence creating a material dispute about the asserted purpose to drown them out. Reimbursement of public election costs and public administration are legitimate interests fitted to the arrangement actually shown; no established severe burden here requires strict scrutiny. This conclusion adopts no universal minimum campaign duration and supplies no immunity for deliberately prohibitive timing or an actually supported inability to organize and respond.

**B3 — Judgment and limits.** Affirm the Washington Supreme Court's summary judgment on the federal questions within the supplied grant. Do not decide the independent Washington constitutional challenges, order a new statewide election, require the beneficiary to fund opposition, or presume a later election has mooted the concrete maintained claim. Yoshina preserves relief for fundamentally unfair administration; Gable requires an actual continuing-stake dispute before a jurisdictional detour. Neither supplies an unreported factual dispute here. Summary judgment remains improper when evidence supports a genuine dispute of material fact; an unsupported allegation does not itself establish one, and this ruling does not convert lower-court findings into statutory commands.
