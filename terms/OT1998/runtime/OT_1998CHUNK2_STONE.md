* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Fourth Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Fourth Circuit shall return the case to the district court for adjudication of the ADA claim unless another lawful, independently established ground disposes of it.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Clear waiver of the federal statutory forum

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the received clear-and-unmistakable waiver rule. A union may bargain over procedures, but a court will not infer surrender of an individual federal judicial forum from general language covering contractual disputes.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A collective-bargaining agreement requires arbitration of an employee’s federal antidiscrimination claim only when the agreement clearly and unmistakably identifies that statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agreement broadly covered disputes under the contract but never identified the ADA, federal discrimination statutes, or statutory claims independent of the agreement. Wright therefore did not lose his federal forum.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide whether a clear union-negotiated waiver would be enforceable in every setting, or whether Wright will prevail on the ADA merits.

# Minnesota v. Carter

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Minnesota Supreme Court's suppression judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The suppression judgment remains in force, subject to any independent issue not resolved by that judgment under ordinary state procedure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Privacy interest of invited occupants

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches respondents' personal Fourth Amendment interests because the suppression judgment cannot be sustained for them solely through Thompson's rights.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone extends the relationship-based application in *Fields* to invited occupants whom the resident admitted for private use of a deliberately shielded home. Brief duration, commercial purpose, and the illegality of the activity do not, separately or together, eliminate the occupants' personal interest in excluding police observation of the home's interior.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Invited occupants who use a resident's deliberately screened home for a private activity possess a personal Fourth Amendment interest against police acquisition of interior information; short duration, commercial purpose, and illegality do not automatically defeat that interest.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Thompson admitted Carter and Johns and permitted them to use the apartment behind closed blinds. Their claim rests on host-authorized private use of the home, not on ownership, tenancy, or a freestanding right to conceal crime. Treating the visit's purpose or length as categorically dispositive would replace the individualized relationship inquiry with labels that do not measure whether the interior was held private from police.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not give every casual visitor privacy in every part of a host's premises, protect a person present without permission, or decide observation of conduct plainly exposed to ordinary public view.

### Issue 2: Deliberate observation through closed blinds

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether the officer's observation was a search after recognizing respondents' personal privacy interests.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The deliberate window-side observation is a search because the officer used a residential vantage outside the ordinary public approach to obtain information the occupants had screened from public view. This conclusion applies the protection of home information to these facts; it is not a search holding already made in Fields.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Police conduct a Fourth Amendment search when they leave the ordinary public approach and deliberately use a narrow gap in closed blinds from a residential window-side vantage to obtain interior information not exposed to ordinary public observation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The closed blinds and movement from the public walkway to the residential window distinguish observation during ordinary public passage. A private passerby’s earlier view does not itself authorize a police intrusion into a protected vantage. Fields prevents equating imperfect screening with publication, while Pinson explains why concealed home information remains protected. Because Fields reserved the actual search question and Pinson concerned a technological scan, the conclusion here depends on the established access and screening facts, not on attributing either reserved question to those decisions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding leaves ordinary unaided observation from a street, walkway, common approach, open window, or other position genuinely available to public observation untouched. It does not require a fence or posted sign whenever the residential vantage is otherwise shown not to be an ordinary public observation point.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm Sixth Circuit’s rejection of a per se insolvency-based fraud finding and its dischargeability disposition insofar as supported by the actual intent findings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Bankruptcy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Proof by preponderance of a knowing material false representation or actionable omission, actual intent to deceive, justifiable reliance, and consequential loss; subjective intent is proved circumstantially, not conclusively by financial condition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Presenting a card implies an undertaking to honor the account, but §523(a)(2)(A) excludes debt from discharge for actual fraud, not high-risk consumption as such. An intent not to repay may be inferred from conduct and statements, but objectively improbable repayment cannot substitute for the subjective scienter question. Field’s reliance rule remains independently necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A credit-card charge is not nondischargeable solely because the debtor knew repayment was uncertain or relied on unlikely gambling winnings; creditor must prove the contemporaneous deceptive intent and other actual-fraud elements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Presenting a card implies an undertaking to honor the account, but §523(a)(2)(A) excludes debt from discharge for actual fraud, not high-risk consumption as such. An intent not to repay may be inferred from conduct and statements, but objectively improbable repayment cannot substitute for the subjective scienter question. Field’s reliance rule remains independently necessary. Her repayments and second-mortgage proceeds are concrete evidence of an intention to repay; they must be weighed with her testimony. The bankruptcy court’s objective inability-to-pay premise cannot substitute for subjective fraud. Field’s reliance requirement remains separate even if intent could otherwise be proved.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical immunity for gamblers, no requirement to prove defendant admitted deceit, and no abandonment of statutory nondischargeability for proved intentional fraudulent card use.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Iowa courts shall suppress the fruits of the citation-only search and conduct any further proceedings consistent with the remaining admissible record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Full search after a traffic citation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the exception according to its justifications. A citation permits the limited precautions supported by particularized safety concerns, but not an automatic evidentiary search.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Issuing a traffic citation, without custodial arrest, does not authorize a full search incident to arrest; an officer needs consent, probable cause, particularized protective grounds, or another recognized exception.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Second Circuit's per se § 1 ruling and the derivative § 2 ruling, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts shall reject per se group-boycott treatment of the alleged vertical supplier substitution. Any adequately preserved rule-of-reason, monopolization, fraud, or other theory may proceed under its own elements and burdens.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Per se boycott treatment of a vertical supplier switch

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the limited antitrust classification question presented by the grant.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A single buyer's substitution of one supplier for another does not enter the per se group-boycott category without horizontal concerted exclusion or a comparably established exclusionary structure. Alleged fraud or favoritism does not itself supply the missing horizontal feature.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A single buyer's substitution of one supplier for another, without horizontal concerted exclusion, is not a per se group boycott under Sherman Act § 1. The contrary § 1 classification and derivative § 2 ruling are vacated, with other adequately preserved theories left for remand.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The complaint alleges a vertical purchasing decision. Under *Klor's*, *Indiana Federation of Dentists*, and *Business Electronics*, per se condemnation is confined to categories whose structure and predictable anticompetitive operation are established. The Court therefore removes the per se classification without finally adjudicating every possible competitive-injury theory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not immunize horizontal boycotts, purchaser cartels, monopolistic conduct, fraud, or conduct proved unreasonable under ordinary antitrust analysis.

# Calderon v. Coleman

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for whole-record constitutional harmless-error review with the State bearing the Chapman burden for this sentencing-instruction error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** If the available constitutional error is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, provide conditional relief from the death sentence allowing a lawful new penalty proceeding within a reasonable period. Guilt and independent habeas barriers remain unaffected.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Habeas Corpus

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the preserved commutation-instruction error requires the State to prove harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone would apply Chapman to this preserved unconstitutional instruction affecting the jury’s capital selection. That resolves a category not mechanically covered by the narrow Brecht and O’Neal holdings; collateral posture supplies no affirmative reason to lower the standard here.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For the available constitutional commutation-instruction claim, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the death sentence; a generic instruction to disregard commutation does not prove harmlessness by itself.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The erroneous description could alter the jury’s assessment of what a life sentence means and thereby its choice between life and death. Brecht, O’Neal and Roy reject treating collateral review alone as sufficient to shift their preserved constitutional errors onto a prisoner-borne substantial-influence test, while preserving limits on their exact categories. Stone would apply the same constitutional harmlessness burden here because the error concerns the reliability of the jury’s actual punishment choice. The court must examine the complete instructions, mitigation, prosecution theory and any jury questions. A direction not to consider commutation may matter but is not a conclusive cure for earlier misinformation. No present finding establishes the error’s actual effect, so the remand preserves both lawful denial upon the State’s showing and conditional sentencing relief if it fails.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This position does not invalidate all Briggs instructions, decide guilt, or make every capital instructional error structural. The remand applies the Chapman burden to the actual penalty record; independent habeas barriers remain distinct.
