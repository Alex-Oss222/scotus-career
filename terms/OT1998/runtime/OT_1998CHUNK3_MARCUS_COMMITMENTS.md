# Marcus v. Iowa Public Television — independent non-Stone commitments

**Matter and event:** No. 98-710; supplied post-grant merits consideration, January 13, 1999.
**Research cutoff:** January 13, 1999.
**Status:** Supported final provisional commitments for every Associate Justice, ready for the separate historical-reconciliation stage. No material factual premise is adopted to create jurisdiction. No Court disposition, opinion assignment, or aggregate vote is entered here.
**Scope:** The frozen Marcus neutral packet, Marcus entering-law slice, current neutral projection through December 14, 1998, and Engine §§3, 5–9 and 14. No private Stone position, same-matter Supreme Court comparator, other commitments, reconciliation, or raw Decision Record was received. Public prior decisions in the supplied law remain available notwithstanding their public references to the Chief Justice.

## Record and legal premises retained

The July 30, 1998 en banc judgment affirmed the trial judgment after the District Court independently adopted the advisory findings, assessed credibility, credited Miller, and found genuine editorial selection rather than political-affiliation or viewpoint exclusion. The findings include failures of proof, which are not affirmative findings of every conceivable opposite fact. Neither their existence nor the general newsworthiness definition independently settles the constitutional reasonableness question.

The actual Iowa Press format was selective. The program had operated weekly for more than twenty years, and the contested joint candidate appearances occupied the pre-election programming described in the packet. Instruction 15 defined newsworthiness through sufficient interest to the audience and public to warrant inclusion in network news programming. Editorial policies identified fairness, objectivity, balance, community needs, independence from pressure and an informed citizenry. Miller described campaign activity, organization, prior performance, fundraising breadth, media recognition, public engagement and efforts to attract voters. Those descriptions are evidence of professional judgment, not a numerical test or an established finding that every candidate lacked every characteristic.

Petitioners' strongest particular application claim is retained: Newell allegedly made exclusions without a meaningful individual inquiry or knowledge of fundraising; Marcus allegedly had substantial press coverage and invitations from opponents; his approximately $13,000 and Gratzon's approximately $25,000 were compared with invited candidate Smith's approximately $17,000. The petition's exhibit and testimony references identify genuine adversarial claims. The packet does not establish their truth as independent findings, equal significance across candidates, an identical district or office comparison, or a dollars-based eligibility rule. No finding about particular polling results is adopted. The ideas-versus-election testimony warrants scrutiny; it does not establish that viewpoint or a disfavored purpose actually caused these exclusions.

The controlling reusable rule is **Editorial selection in public-broadcaster candidate debates**, effective *Forbes*, May 18, 1998. Actual selective editorial access creates a nonpublic forum. Exclusion must reasonably serve the journalistic purpose and remain viewpoint neutral. The rule requires neither a universal numerical formula nor a written or published advance policy. It does not permit sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, selective administration, viewpoint pretext, or an unsupported invocation of journalism to end judicial review. The published Forbes standards dissent remains a noncontrolling position of Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

## Article III conclusion and adversarial comparison

**Conclusion:** The challenge to the candidate-selection policy remains capable of repetition yet evading review for the Natural Law Party committee. This is a conclusion from established facts, not an assumed new candidacy, later exclusion, scheduled broadcast, or stipulation about jurisdiction. The committee's stipulated purpose is generally to promote its candidates for office; it is not limited to the 1996 campaign as the separately named Marcus committee was. It participated in this challenge involving its candidates across congressional districts. That continuing electoral function, the general editorial-selection policy, and the established recurring news program support a reasonable expectation that the same organizational claimant will again confront the disputed selection practice in seeking exposure for its candidates. The short period for pre-election appearances prevents complete ordinary appellate review before the occasion passes.

The recurrence is the committee's own candidate-access controversy with this broadcaster. It is not merely the possibility that some unrelated candidate will someday be excluded. The inference does not require a finding that every future nominee will be rejected or that the party can never acquire greater support. It concerns the reasonable recurrence of the contested discretionary selection and exclusion of candidates the continuing committee promotes. The several exclusions in the established electoral programming, rather than the committee's abstract mission standing alone, provide the concrete application. The ordinary continuation of that organizational activity under the general policy is reasonably expected; it is not certified as a later historical event. The analysis asserts no continuing statutory party-qualification consequence of the 1996 results.

*Norman* supports review of short recurring election disputes involving continuing party interests, but its actual continuing qualification consequences are not imported here. *Chandler* supplies a sufficient way to establish recurrence for an individual candidate; it does not turn an express statement of another candidacy into an indispensable form of proof for an ongoing party committee. *Spencer* requires a reasonable expectation concerning the same claimant, and rejects speculation or merely possible injuries to others. This packet supplies the organizational link that Spencer's unrelated later custody and possible future consequences did not supply. *NAACP v. Jones* requires an actual jurisdictional determination before contested merits; the determination here rests on the identified committee and facts, not hypothetical jurisdiction.

**Strongest alternative:** Only the completed 1996 candidacies and broadcasts are specifically established. A general purpose does not compel a committee to nominate again; a weekly interview program does not guarantee another election debate; the selection rule need not exclude a later candidate with stronger support. Chandler had an express future intention, and Norman had consequences of actual election results. On that view the record would need an objective continuing-access premise before a merits disposition.

**Why that alternative is not adopted:** Reasonable recurrence permits a supported inference from an established continuing actor and the operation giving rise to its injury. It does not demand a scheduled repetition of an inherently brief event. The party committee's non-election-limited purpose, its participation alongside the affected candidates of its party, breadth of the challenged electoral selection and ordinary recurring program together make this more than the hypothetical succession of new events rejected in Spencer. No fact establishes termination of that organizational activity or replacement of the contested general practice. That last absence is not independently treated as proof; the positive established features carry the inference. No universal election exception, presumption of perpetual organizational standing, or burden shift to IPTV follows. No particular committee expenditure, membership arrangement or campaign work beyond the established purpose and litigation participation is found.

The surviving committee controversy permits resolution of the common selection-policy claim. It does not establish a separate future candidacy for Marcus or any other individual, a separate Senate-debate entitlement, or continuing standing for every plaintiff for every distinct demand. The completed-broadcast demand cannot produce a replacement 1996 appearance. The 1996 guide-correction request does not independently supply the recurrence premise and does not establish continuing dissemination or reputational injury. Its treatment follows the distinct remedial commitments below.

## Merits alternatives and remedial boundaries

**Supported affirmance ground:** Apply Forbes to the actual selective format and independently determine reasonableness on the adjudicated record. The program's electoral exchange and genuine news judgments bear a reasonable relationship to an informed audience. A short general instruction is not a complete description of the evidence or the Court's constitutional rule. The policies, selection testimony, actual purpose and independently adopted findings supply more than a label. Courts must examine whether those judgments were genuine and consistent, but need not devise a superior editorial process or impose a campaign-information questionnaire absent an applicable legal requirement.

The information and comparator allegations receive that examination. The absence of particular fundraising information at an earlier point would not, alone, negate all information concerning a candidate or make the selection irrational. Dollar figures and counts of media items do not themselves establish comparable public interest or electoral significance. They would be important evidence of pretext or arbitrariness if the record established those further connections. Here the court credited the editorial explanation, rejected selection solely by party nomination and found no prohibited cause; the supplied particular comparisons do not establish clear error in those findings or compel the different constitutional conclusion. This ground does not declare every factual assertion false or require proof of subjective hostility as the only possible constitutional defect.

*Regan v. Time*, 468 U.S. 641, Part II-A, invalidated a governmental content-based restriction on private publication. It does not make every content-sensitive editorial selection in a genuine nonpublic forum unconstitutional. Forbes provides the directly applicable distinction while preserving viewpoint neutrality. Selection because a candidate advocates unwelcome ideas would fail that rule. Evidence of actual electoral engagement can bear on this program's purpose; a categorical disqualification for introducing ideas cannot be approved merely because Miller mentioned that consideration. The affirmed record is the found exercise of genuine electoral-news judgment, not such a categorical rule. Offers of other programming neither prove neutrality nor cure an otherwise unlawful exclusion.

**Strongest competing ground:** The standard actually given to the factfinder allowed officials to decide which political candidates were sufficiently interesting to their audience. General professional virtues and a list of considerations do not constrain how officials compare candidates or separate popular appeal from the value of their political ideas. The particular information allegations show why reviewability matters even when bad motive has not been proved. Under the Forbes dissent's position, the constitutional defect lies in this insufficiently definite governing standard, not a new factual finding that IPTV disliked the candidates' views. The proper consequence would be vacatur and reconsideration of the policy challenge under adequately constrained, reviewable selection standards, without automatic access for all ballot-qualified candidates.

**Counterargument to that competing ground:** Forbes expressly rejected an additional universal formula, written-policy or publication requirement. This record also contains considerably more than an undefined aspiration: genuine selection, editorial policies, practical evidence of news significance and an independent trial finding. A general rule demanding greater specificity would preserve the Forbes dissent's disagreement, not merely apply Forbes's controlling rule. Any narrower distinction must explain why this record fails reasonable selection independently of imposing the rejected prerequisite. The individual commitments below identify who maintains the standards disagreement and who finds the existing law and record sufficient.

No path grants damages, compels replacement of a past broadcast, directs a future program not shown in the record, finds that every minor-party candidate is unnewsworthy, or decides an independent statutory-access claim. The offered alternative programs do not resolve every possible First Amendment wrong. A directive to correct the guide requires a legal and remedial basis of its own; neither a jurisdictional recurrence finding nor success on a standards objection automatically supplies it.

## Individual commitments

### Stevens

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion. His join in Norman supports an ongoing party controversy beyond a completed election, and his join in Chandler supports prospective relief tied to a continuing claimant. His own participation in Honig's jurisdictional holding supports reasonable factual inference without a guarantee that the next event is scheduled. He does not convert his different Spencer disposition into a general exception. The strongest objection is the absence of a later specific candidate; the actual continuing party claimant and its involvement in the challenge concerning the multiple excluded candidates of its party distinguish that objection from speculation about an unrelated future litigant.

**Merits:** Vacate the reviewed judgment insofar as it finally rejects the committee's selection-policy claim. Maintain the substantive position of his published Forbes dissent: a state platform for candidates' private political speech requires sufficiently definite, reviewable selection standards, while reasonable selection remains permissible. Instruction 15's audience-interest definition, supplemented by general editorial virtues and discretionary considerations, does not sufficiently constrain the candidate-access decision. Iowa's greater descriptive detail does not cure the underlying discretion his Forbes dissent rejected. The media, fundraising and information claims strengthen the need for reviewability but do not establish favoritism as a fact. He does not derive a categorical ban on newsworthiness judgments from Regan. His objection concerns this publicly provided candidate platform under the Forbes dissent's distinction.

**Remedy and joins:** Remand for reconsideration of the live declaratory and prospective claim under adequately constrained selection standards. No automatic inclusion, numerical formula, compelled future broadcast, new damages, or declaration that the adjudicated exclusions were motivated by hostility. The passed 1996 appearances cannot be restored. The actual guide-correction request returns only for its proper relationship to any permissible equitable relief and any continuing injury it can redress; no correction entitlement is established here. Join shared nonpublic-forum classification and the prohibition on viewpoint exclusion, but refuse the proposition that these standards and their adjudicated application suffice. A fuller statement of reviewability without changing the operative discretion would not answer his objection. A rule actually supplying meaningful constraints could be considered; this handoff does not assume that amendment or a new join.

### O'Connor

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion. Her controlling Chandler join and Norman join permit an inference grounded in this claimant. Her Honig dissent requires an actual likelihood of recurrence, not a lawsuit's continuing existence. That dissent expressly recognized inference for continuing organizations exposed to repeated governmental practices; the broad ongoing party purpose and established multiple-candidate activity supply that link here. No equivalent inference is made for an individual's unannounced future candidacy.

**Merits:** Affirm. Her Forbes opinion governs actual selective access and genuine, reasonable editorial judgment. The Iowa policies and testimony bear on the program's journalistic purpose, and the trial findings establish the genuine nature of the selection on this record. She considers the particular alleged information failures and comparisons, but they do not establish that the credited assessment lacked a reasonable basis or that the candidates were materially equivalent under an adopted criterion. A constitutional rule requiring advance individualized questionnaires, financial totals, a particular press-coverage count, or additional published criteria would exceed Forbes. Regan's private-publication restriction does not control the forum-selection question; her Regan join and Forbes opinion can coexist on that distinction.

**Remedy and joins:** Join affirmance of denial of the reviewed forum claim and its equitable remedies. No retraction or correction is warranted as a remedy for a violation the Court does not find, and no independent guide claim is adjudicated. No replacement appearance or future-broadcast obligation follows. Join the full reasonable-selection and no-viewpoint conclusion only with actual pretext review preserved. She will not join a statement that an advisory verdict alone answers constitutional reasonableness, that every use of newsworthiness is lawful, or that an idea-centered campaign is categorically disqualified. Her strongest alternative is a record-specific remand for an inadequately supported editorial judgment; the independent findings and identified evidence make that unnecessary here.

### Scalia

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion on the concrete organizational facts. His Chandler opinion and Spencer join require the same claimant, while his NAACP opinion forbids assumed jurisdiction. His Honig dissent distinguished possible future participation from demonstrated recurrence and accepted ordinary recurring-organization inferences. The committee's established general purpose and its participation in the challenge alongside multiple excluded candidates of its party supply more than the possibility that an individual will someday decide to run. His Paradise dissent required a defendant-specific connection; here this broadcaster actually applied the challenged selection practice to the candidates whose promotion is the committee's stated function. No independent-party enforcement or nonparty redress problem is imported from Paradise.

**Merits:** Affirm under his full Forbes join. Actual selectivity makes this a nonpublic forum; a reasonable editorial choice is not rendered unconstitutional because it considers public interest in the candidates. The litigants' figures would not entitle a reviewing court to reweigh the credited news judgment as a dollars or article-count contest. The legal rule remains separate from factual deference, and the findings do not authorize viewpoint hostility. The strongest alternative is that the instruction conferred unreviewable discretion; Forbes's retained judicial reasonableness and viewpoint review, applied to the findings and testimony, defeats a need to constitutionalize further selection procedures.

**Remedy and joins:** Join affirmance on the reviewed claim and denial of its declaratory, injunctive and derivative corrective relief. No future access command, substitute broadcast or independent guide ruling. Join the same-claimant threshold, actual nonpublic-forum classification and record-specific reasonableness holding. Refuse an additional mandatory procedural checklist or a general power in courts to replace a broadcaster's lawful editorial judgment. Also refuse immunity from pretext review; that would abandon the Forbes rule he joined.

### Kennedy

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion. His Norman and Chandler joins recognize continuing electoral controversies with a personal claimant; his Spencer and unanimous NAACP joins require an actual recurring injury, not only public importance. The general-purpose committee's supported engagement and the general selection policy of the recurring program establish the required relationship. The strongest alternative correctly notes uncertainty about the precise next candidate, but exact identity of a future nominee is not necessary to the same committee's continuing access controversy.

**Merits:** Affirm under his Forbes join, preserving the protection of private candidate speech against viewpoint discrimination. The forum is genuinely selective, and the trial findings and evidence support the reasonable editorial purpose. The distinction between electoral engagement and an idea-centered campaign needs particular care because political advocacy cannot be disfavored simply as advocacy. He nevertheless does not treat that isolated testimony as an established operative exclusion criterion contrary to the trial findings. Petitioners' particular comparisons do not establish equal electoral significance or disparate administration of an actual fixed criterion. The Regan objection identifies a real danger in governmental judgments of political value but does not erase Forbes's selective-forum holding.

**Remedy and joins:** Join affirmance and denial of equitable relief on the forum claim, including no derivative guide correction. No separate defamation, government-speech or statutory claim is resolved. Join the opinion's full constitutional application with genuine viewpoint review and the distinction between findings and allegations express. Refuse blanket approval of preference for established parties, exclusion of campaigns because their ideas lack popularity, or the premise that another program cures discrimination. A narrower corrective remand would be supportable on established pretext or a legally deficient evaluation; those facts are not supplied here.

### Souter

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion. His Norman opinion is the closest party-continuity anchor, without importing its qualification consequences. His Chandler and Spencer joins preserve the need for a concrete claimant. The existing general-purpose party committee, rather than unidentified future candidates as independent plaintiffs, supplies that claimant. The strongest contrary argument is the contingent identity and support of future candidates; it does not defeat reasonable recurrence of the committee's conflict with the general selection standard. His NAACP join requires resolving that question expressly as done here.

**Merits:** Vacate the final rejection of the committee's policy claim, consistently with his Forbes dissent join. General fairness language does not explain what restrains governmental discretion to decide whose political candidacy interests an audience. Miller's illustrative considerations describe judgment but do not sufficiently delimit it under that position. Instruction 15 makes the deficiency material to the adjudication. He accepts the no-viewpoint findings and does not find that the financial or media comparisons prove equivalent candidates. Those specific objections demonstrate that reasonableness must have a reviewable content beyond officials' sincere characterization of their judgment.

**Remedy and joins:** Join remand for the live declaratory and prospective claim under adequately constrained, reviewable standards. The past programs cannot be re-created. Return the actual guide request for assessment of its available remedial basis and continuing redressable consequences, without ordering a correction or presuming such consequences. No damages, automatic ballot-based admission or universal numerical rule. Join actual selectivity and viewpoint neutrality, but refuse sufficiency of the Iowa standard as applied. A proposed accommodation must meaningfully constrain selection rather than merely repeat that judges can review a standard whose operative terms remain unrestricted.

### Thomas

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion under his Chandler, Spencer and NAACP joins. His Paradise dissent join cautions that broad allegations of future enforcement do not identify a personal threat from the defendants. Here the named broadcaster's actual exclusions operated directly on the continuing party's electoral activity. The committee's general function and repeated candidate applications make recurrence reasonably expected without presuming that any one former candidate will run again. Mere organizational purpose would be inadequate absent that established application; the present inference rests on both.

**Merits:** Affirm under his full Forbes join. The actual invitation-only format and credited independent editorial judgment satisfy the governing reasonable, viewpoint-neutral rule. The strongest opposite path treats broad standards as independently unconstitutional; the Forbes rule he joined allows contextual professional judgment without universal quantitative or written criteria. The fundraising and publicity comparisons do not establish a binding criterion administered inconsistently, and an advocate's allegation about deficient inquiry cannot replace the trial findings. A rule approving viewpoint exclusions is neither necessary nor supported.

**Remedy and joins:** Join affirmance of the reviewed forum claim and denial of its equitable remedies, with no derivative guide correction and no adjudication of a separate guide cause of action. No future program, monetary award or resurrected statutory access entitlement. Join the full threshold and merits grounds within those boundaries. Refuse a new mandatory editorial-investigation procedure, a presumption that party status is always a permissible substitute for editorial judgment, or reliance on alternative airtime to cure a constitutional wrong.

### Ginsburg

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion. Her Chandler and Spencer joins require an actual claimant and a supported recurrence expectation. The existing organization promoting multiple candidates under a continuing general purpose has a concrete interest in the same broadcaster's selection policy; its interest is not exhausted with any single candidate's 1996 race. Her NAACP join prevents substituting an abstract interest in speech rights for those facts. The lack of a named next candidate is the strongest objection but does not require disregarding the actual organization and operation already shown.

**Merits:** Vacate the final denial of the committee's policy challenge, maintaining her Forbes dissent join. The combination of Instruction 15 and discretionary editorial factors does not supply sufficiently definite, reviewable standards for allocating this platform for competing candidates. The adverse motive finding remains respected. The candidate-specific information and comparison claims make it especially important not to conflate absence of hostility with constitutional adequacy, but she does not elevate those allegations into findings. The counterargument that Forbes already accepted comparable discretion is substantial; her position retains the disagreement expressed in that published dissent rather than claiming the controlling Forbes holding itself requires her result.

**Remedy and joins:** Join remand for the live declaratory and prospective question, with no compulsory inclusion or prescribed polling/funding threshold. No replacement 1996 broadcast. The guide request receives a separate remedial determination below; no ongoing publication, reputation injury or entitlement to retraction is found. Join nonpublic-forum classification, permission for reasonable selection, and prohibition of viewpoint bias. Refuse a constitutional sufficiency holding that relies on the sincerity findings to cure the standards objection. Genuine reviewable limits could answer it; a disclaimer that discretion must be fair would not.

### Breyer

**Threshold:** Join the same-committee recurrence conclusion under his Spencer opinion and Chandler join. Spencer rejects speculative collateral consequences, but does not make inference from ongoing institutional activity unavailable. The committee is the existing claimant whose general electoral work encountered the general selection policy, and the short pre-election opportunity explains why this controversy evades full review. His NAACP join requires that express analysis before merits. He rejects both an automatic election exception and a requirement to produce an already scheduled repetition where the positive established organizational facts support reasonable recurrence.

**Merits:** Affirm under his Forbes join. Journalistic judgment cannot operate without attention to the audience and subject, and the actual evidence supplies a reasonable connection between candidate selection and the program's informing function. The particular allegations deserve examination, but the court need not transform dollars raised, media-item totals or the chronology of each producer's inquiry into constitutional prerequisites. The trial court's independent judgment and the record's actual editorial considerations answer the reasonableness objection here. The strongest alternative is a limited remand to ensure meaningful factual review; the supplied findings and appellate review show that review occurred, and no identified legal error requires repeating it.

**Remedy and joins:** Join affirmance and denial of the reviewed claim's equitable relief, including no derivative compelled guide correction. No future debate or independent guide claim is decided. Join the full contextual rule and application while preserving review of pretext, arbitrary application and genuine comparators. Refuse an unqualified declaration that professional sincerity ends First Amendment review or that offers of other programming invariably cure exclusion. His full Forbes join does not require adoption of any more categorical broadcaster-immunity rationale.

## Coalition-compatibility limits for the next stage

Every Associate supports the same bounded committee-based jurisdictional proposition and the genuinely selective format; individual renewed candidacies are neither found nor needed for that common policy claim. The affirmance commitments adopt constitutional reasonableness independently of the adverse motive findings, with those findings and other evidence informing the application. They can join one opinion carrying that complete ground and the remedial limits stated above. They cannot join an opinion reducing the case to ballot eligibility, treating every allegation as disproved, approving a bare label irrespective of actual operation, or treating private speech as the government's own message.

The vacatur commitments share the Forbes dissent's standards objection and do not support a merits holding that actual viewpoint discrimination occurred. They can join a separate position preserving that disagreement and directing a confined equitable remand. They cannot be counted as supporting the sufficiency of Iowa's existing selection process merely because they agree on the nonpublic-forum classification or recognize that a broadcaster may select participants. No opinion author or final writing form is selected here.

No final commitment is conditional on obtaining an unestablished current candidacy or broadcast. The individual guide demand's exact ongoing consequences are not adjudicated by inference: affirmance denies its derivative relief on the reviewed claim; the competing remand returns its available equitable treatment without an entitlement. If a later stage proposes to grant corrective relief, identify a distinct current guide injury, decide an independent claim, or order a particular future appearance, that proposal would need a neutral supplement and renewed affected modeling. Those unadopted proposals are not blockers to the present commitments.
