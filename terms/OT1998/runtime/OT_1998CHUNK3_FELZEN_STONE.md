## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Party status for settlement appeal

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Objection before the settlement court does not itself establish appellate party status.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An opportunity to object within an ongoing derivative action does not alone confer independent party status for the appellate court. Rule 24 provides a route for securing that status and review of its denial. The *Izumi* party-status boundary is best applied rather than silently expanded from the fact a settlement can affect shareholders.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Objection before the settlement court does not itself establish appellate party status.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An opportunity to object within an ongoing derivative action does not alone confer independent party status for the appellate court. Rule 24 provides a route for securing that status and review of its denial. The *Izumi* party-status boundary is best applied rather than silently expanded from the fact a settlement can affect shareholders.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No foreclosure of review by actual parties, appeals from denied intervention, derivative due-process claims by genuinely unrepresented individuals, or Rule 23.1 settlement objections below.

### Issue 2: Derivative representation and available review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Intervention under Rule 24 and challenge by an actual party remain distinct lawful channels.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An opportunity to object within an ongoing derivative action does not alone confer independent party status for the appellate court. Rule 24 provides a route for securing that status and review of its denial. The *Izumi* party-status boundary is best applied rather than silently expanded from the fact a settlement can affect shareholders.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Intervention under Rule 24 and challenge by an actual party remain distinct lawful channels.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Notice and objection protect shareholders within the derivative process, but they need not make every objector an appellate party. Rule 24 permits the shareholder to seek an accountable litigating role and challenge a denial through the appropriate appeal. Richards’s protection against unrepresented nonparty preclusion does not itself decide appellate status. Stone would require the lawful party route while leaving the settlement’s fairness and the merits of any properly presented intervention challenge undecided.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No foreclosure of review by actual parties, appeals from denied intervention, derivative due-process claims by genuinely unrepresented individuals, or Rule 23.1 settlement objections below.
