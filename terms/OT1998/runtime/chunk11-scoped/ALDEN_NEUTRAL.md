### Issue 1: Federal wages in competent state courts

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Garcia sustains generally applicable federal wage regulation of state employers. Hilton preserves state-court FELA enforcement against a state-owned railroad on its particular statutory and reliance grounds; Howlett and Testa prohibit discriminatory refusal to enforce federal law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The FLSA extends beyond Hilton’s specific railroad setting. The court must decide whether the express private state-court remedy prevails over Maine’s immunity defense; Hilton supplies a foundation rather than a complete answer.
* **Other Governing Law:** 29 U.S.C. §216(b); Supremacy Clause; Eleventh Amendment.

### Issue 2: Judicial enforcement and procedural vehicle

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** New York and Printz concern compelled legislative or executive administration; they do not erase the distinct duty of competent courts to apply supreme federal law. Seminole does not reproduce the historical categorical Article I immunity result.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The historical federal dismissal cannot be assumed to have occurred here. A live Maine state-court judgment and remaining wage controversy are prerequisites to this planned review.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; ordinary finality, mootness and preclusion rules.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Supreme Judicial Court of Maine affirmed dismissal of state probation officers’ FLSA overtime action on state sovereign-immunity grounds.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The employees invoke the FLSA’s express public-employer terms, earned wages and the duty of state courts to enforce federal law. Maine asserts immunity from a private treasury action in its own courts. Congress’s substantive regulatory power, federal-court abrogation and a compelled state-forum remedy are distinct questions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A state-forum immunity holding would not erase the federal duty or every lawful enforcement channel. A contrary amenability holding requires its constitutional and statutory basis, not an assumed general Seminole rule. Any monetary award still requires proved wage liability and applicable remedial conditions.
