### Issue 1: Disability and mitigating measures

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Bragdon requires an individualized inquiry into impairment and substantial limitation and separates coverage from legitimate safety defenses. Arline supports meaningful statutory coverage; no completed holding decides the mitigation question presented here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Hypertension, its unmedicated effects, and medication must be considered separately from the job’s driving qualification. A coverage ruling does not establish ability to perform essential tasks.
* **Other Governing Law:** Original ADA, 42 U.S.C. §§12102(2),12111(8),12112.

### Issue 2: Working, perceived impairment and qualification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** An individualized substantial limitation cannot be inferred solely from inability to perform one specialized job. Bragdon permits objective assessment of actual safety risk.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The mechanic’s DOT certification issue and the employer’s understanding of it require an actual record; perceived inability to drive one class of vehicle does not necessarily establish exclusion from a broad class of jobs.
* **Other Governing Law:** 29 C.F.R. §1630.2; applicable federal safety qualifications.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for UPS because medication controlled Murphy’s hypertension and the employer did not regard him as broadly unable to work.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Murphy relies on serious untreated hypertension and UPS’s exclusion; UPS emphasizes his functioning with medication and the driving component of the job. Actual disability and regarded-as limitation of a broad class of work require distinct proof.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Effective treatment may leave no substantial limitation on this record without establishing that every controlled impairment is excluded. A perceived inability to perform one particular job configuration is not necessarily broad occupational incapacity. Qualification and any distinct treatment burden remain separate.
