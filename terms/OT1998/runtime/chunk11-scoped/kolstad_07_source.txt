STATEMENT OF THE CASE 

Petitioner commenced this action in 1994 in the United 
States District Court for the District of Columbia, alleging 
that she had been denied a promotion because of her sex in 
violation of Title VII of the 1964 Civil Rights Act. The 
complaint sought back pay, injunctive relief and punitive 
damages. 


The case was tried to a jury, which found that petitioner 
had indeed been the victim of intentional discrimination on the 
basis of sex. (J.A. 109-10). The district court denied 
respondent’s post-trial motion for judgment as a matter of law 
on the merits of petitioner’s discrimination claim. (Pet. App. 
69a-74a). The court of appeals panel which first heard this 
case unanimously upheld the jury’s finding of intentional 
discrimination.’ (Pet. App. 46a-49a). The court of appeals 
declined to grant rehearing en banc with regard to the liability 
determination. This Court denied a cross-petition seeking 
review of this aspect of the case. American Dental Ass'n v. 
Kolstad, _U.S. __ (No. 98-364, Nov. 2, 1998). 


' The panel decision overturned the district court’s decision 
denying injunctive relief and counsel fees, and remanded for further 
consideration of those issues. (Pet. App. 54a-56a). 


3 


The issue which was addressed by the court of appeals en 
banc, and which is the subject of the question presented, is 
petitioner’s claim for punitive damages. The district court 
refused to instruct the jury on punitive damages. (J.A. 104- 
109). A divided panel of the court of appeals held that the 
district court had erred in dismissing petitioner’s punitive 
damage claim. (Pet. App. 42a-68a). The court of appeals 
granted rehearing en banc limited to this issue. (Pet. App. 75a- 
76a). A narrowly divided en banc court sustained the rejection 
of petitioner’s punitive damage claim. (Pet. App. la-41a). 


STATEMENT OF FACTS 


Respondent is a Chicago-based professional organization 
which maintains an office in Washington, D.C. to represent 
its members’ interests before Congress and various federal 
agencies. In September 1992, Jack O’Donnell, the second- 
highest ranking employee in ADA’s Washington office, 
announced his retirement at year’s end. O’Donnell held the 
dual-titled position of Director of Legislation and Legislative 
Policy and Director of the Council on Government Affairs 
and Federal Dental Services. In the fall of 1992 petitioner 
was working in respondent’s Washington office as its Director 
of Federal Agency Relations, a position that she had held for 
four years. (Pet. App. 38a). Petitioner ultimately applied for 
O’Donnell’s position, but was rejected in favor of a male 
candidate, Tom Spangler, who had worked for respondent for 
less than two years. (Pet. App. 43a-44a). 

The jury found that petitioner was denied the promotion 
in question because she was a woman; the correctness of the 
jury’s special verdict finding of intentional discrimination is 
no longer subject to dispute. The evidence adduced in 
support of that verdict, however, remains important to the 
question of whether petitioner was entitled to have the jury 
consider her request for punitive damages.
