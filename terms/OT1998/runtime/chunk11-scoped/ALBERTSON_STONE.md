# Albertson’s, Inc. v. Kirkingburg

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand with instructions to enter judgment for Albertson's on qualification.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enter judgment for Albertson’s on the federal qualification issue. Do not treat experimental waiver availability as a duty to disregard the operative standard, and do not decide disability categorically.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Individualized disability and natural compensation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the legal error in treating monocular vision as automatically disabling; qualification independently supplies the complete disposition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Require individualized evidence of a substantial limitation in seeing, disregarding the beneficial effect of mitigation or adaptation on the impairment. A diagnosis alone does not establish substantial limitation; qualification remains independently necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Monocular vision requires individualized proof of substantial limitation; beneficial compensating measures should not defeat original-Act coverage. The binding occupational qualification independently resolves this case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Monocular vision is not a categorical disability or categorical absence of disability. Bragdon requires the claimant’s actual impairment and substantial limitation to be identified. Beneficial adaptation should not automatically erase coverage, for the same original-Act reasons that support disregarding beneficial correction in Sutton. Qualification remains a separate inquiry: an employer may insist on the operative federal commercial-driving requirement even where the claimant is disabled. No completed Sutton decision is assumed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Natural adaptation does not automatically negate disability, and an employer may not rely on stereotype or an invented safety criterion.

### Issue 2: Operative federal safety qualification and experimental waiver program

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the employer’s entitlement to insist on the binding DOT standard, including the effect of the later experimental waiver.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Albertson's could insist on compliance with the binding federal driving qualification. An experimental waiver program did not amend the operative rule or make every potentially waivable driver presently qualified.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An experimental waiver program does not amend an operative federal safety standard or make every potentially waivable driver presently qualified; an employer may require the certification the binding rule demands.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The operative DOT vision standard supplied the legal qualification for this commercial-driving job. An experimental waiver program does not compel the employer to participate in experimentation or waive that standard. Kirkingburg ultimately obtained an experimental waiver, but its existence does not make Albertson’s earlier reliance on the binding qualification unlawful or require acceptance of the experimental alternative. This conclusion does not authorize employers to invent safety restrictions unsupported by law or objective evidence.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The experimental program did not compel the employer to waive the basic regulatory standard. The conclusion does not decide a different regulation that makes an exemption legally equivalent to basic qualification or authorize unsupported private safety restrictions.
