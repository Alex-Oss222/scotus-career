# Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit’s judgment permitting the patent-infringement action and remand for adjudication of the merits.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the sovereign-immunity obstacle to this expressly authorized action. Resolve patent validity, infringement, defenses and any remedy in ordinary proceedings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Patent Clause enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Congress authorize this action against a State?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Recognize Congress’s power to provide the express federal patent remedy against States and permit the action to proceed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Recognize Congress’s power to provide the express federal patent remedy against States and permit the action to proceed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Uniform patent rights lose an important part of their content if a State may exploit a patented invention while withholding the ordinary federal remedy. Congress created a national patent system, concentrated adjudication in federal court, and unmistakably included States in §296. Union Gas supplies a retained Article I foundation; extending its principle to the Patent Clause is an express doctrinal extension, not a claim that Seminole already decided it. The remedial power remains tied to enforcement of a valid enumerated-power obligation and a clear congressional choice.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding of infringement, patent validity or damages entitlement.

### Issue 2: Section 5

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must ordinary infringement be recast as unconstitutional deprivation?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Do not make §5 the basis for the judgment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Do not make §5 the basis for the judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Boerne prevents Congress from defining all statutory patent infringements as constitutional violations. A State’s ordinary infringement may leave adequate compensation or other process, and negligence alone does not establish the needed deprivation. The Patent Clause rationale allows this action without inventing those predicates or imposing a new claim-by-claim constitutional tort requirement on every patent suit.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Fitzpatrick and Boerne retain their independent force.
