# Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved. The revised position stated below was user-approved 2026-10-30.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit’s judgment sustaining the denial of sovereign immunity, and remand for ordinary adjudication of the patent claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the sovereign-immunity obstacle to this expressly authorized action. Patent validity, infringement, causation, defenses and entitlement to particular remedies remain for adjudication. Affirming jurisdiction to proceed awards no damages and does not preapprove enhanced damages or fees.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Patent Clause enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Congress authorize this action against a State?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Article I independently sustains the action. Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Article I independently sustains the action. Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Uniform patent rights lose an important part of their content if a State may exploit a patented invention while withholding the ordinary federal remedy. Congress created a national patent system, concentrated adjudication in federal court, and unmistakably included States in §296. Union Gas supplies a retained Article I foundation; extending its principle to the Patent Clause is an express doctrinal extension, not a claim that Seminole already decided it. Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly establish state coverage, federal suit and otherwise available remedies. Extend the retained constitutional conclusion of Union Gas into a general Article I enforcement principle; the simulated Seminole Tribe supplies no contrary categorical prohibition. Adopt the federal-question reading of the Eleventh Amendment and clarify that Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. Reach Article I as a preserved alternative ground for affirmance: the Patent Clause argument is an alternative source of congressional authority supporting the same immunity-denial judgment, without enlarging the respondent’s requested relief. The remedial power remains tied to enforcement of a valid enumerated-power obligation and a clear congressional choice.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding of infringement, patent validity or damages entitlement.

### Issue 2: Section 5

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does Section 5 independently sustain the authorized action?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 5 supplies an independent constitutional basis. Under Fitzpatrick and the simulated Boerne, Congress may prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional; patents constitute protected property.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 5 supplies an independent constitutional basis. Under Fitzpatrick and the simulated Boerne, Congress may prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional; patents constitute protected property.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative state remedies support Congress’s preventive judgment that immunity could leave unconstitutional deprivations without adequate redress. The Act supplies an enforcement mechanism for existing patent obligations; its validity does not depend on treating every infringement as a constitutional violation or requiring each plaintiff to establish one. Neither the Article I ground nor the Section 5 ground depends on commercial participation constituting consent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Fitzpatrick and Boerne retain their independent force. Consent inferred from a State's continued commercial participation is rejected; actual consent through a distinct State choice remains available; whether Florida's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided because the Section 5 and Article I grounds sustain the judgment.
