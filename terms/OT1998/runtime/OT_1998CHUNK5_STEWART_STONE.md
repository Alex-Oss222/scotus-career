# Stewart v. LaGrand

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved. The revised position stated below was user-approved 2026-10-27.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** DENY the State's application to lift the gas-specific injunction and oppose summary reversal. Choosing among statutory methods of execution is not a knowing waiver of an Eighth Amendment objection to the chosen method, and the record does not establish that the later gas-related ineffective-assistance claim was subject to an adequate, independent and unexcused state bar, so the procedural-default ground on which Stone would otherwise rest is unavailable on this record. He joins Stevens in dissent from any vacatur or summary disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The existing gas-only protection should remain on its actual terms pending proper consideration with full briefing; the default question, including any applicable exception and its relation to the earlier representation-choice proceedings, should be decided on a developed record. Enter no categorical holding that lethal gas is constitutional or that method selection surrenders Eighth Amendment protection. The separate certiorari disposition is stated separately.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Eighth Amendment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Distinguish waiver of an underlying constitutional guarantee from independently dispositive, unexcused procedural default.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Choosing among methods of execution authorized by law is not an affirmative knowledgeable relinquishment of all objections to the chosen method. But that correction does not reopen a procedurally defaulted habeas claim absent a recognized excuse. Neither the severity of a death sentence nor the incorrect waiver rationale manufactures habeas jurisdiction or automatically excuses default.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** This election among statutory execution methods does not establish knowing waiver of an Eighth Amendment objection; the preserved claim nonetheless fails at the independent procedural-default threshold on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Selecting one state-authorized execution method does not without more show knowledgeable relinquishment of a constitutional claim. The independent default nevertheless controls here. The availability of lethal-gas objections before the direct appeal defeats the assertion that no legal or factual basis existed then; the later method choice does not newly create the underlying objection. The ineffective-assistance excuse is itself barred on the stated state-court history and unsupported by an established cause for that default. Breard’s interim stay inquiry cannot substitute for this claim-specific default analysis or create a right to restrain execution. The precise consequence is to lift Arizona’s gas-specific injunction and reverse the contrary default ruling, while declining the lower court’s broad nonwaivability rule and the State’s automatic-election waiver theory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Does not decide categorical constitutionality of gas, foreclose claims with adequately preserved objections, or adjudicate the Germany original-action request.
