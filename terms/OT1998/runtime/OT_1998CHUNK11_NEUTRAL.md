* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Bragdon v. Abbott* requires claimant-specific proof of substantial limitation and rejects categorical treatment of every person sharing a diagnosis.

### Issue 1: Rule 23(b)(1)(B) limited fund and adequate representation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor requires every settlement class to satisfy Rule 23 and holds that settlement does not relax predominance or adequate representation where present and future claimants conflict.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Settlement-only class certification under Rule 23, under Civil Procedure, supplies the operative requirement and permits narrower subclasses and individual resolution.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(a) and 23(b)(1)(B); Due Process Clause.
* **Material Other Authority:** Traditional limited-fund practice requires a fund fixed independently of the settlement, inadequate to pay all claims, wholly devoted to the class, and distributed through equitable treatment among claimants.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed certification and approval of a mandatory global settlement class against Fibreboard.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Settlement proponents invoke the fund’s asserted insufficiency, insurance uncertainty and the advantages of global resolution. Objectors contest the fund’s independence from the bargain, inclusion of available value and representation of conflicting present and future interests. Amchem forbids relaxing the applicable class requirements merely to achieve settlement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A failed limited-fund or adequacy predicate defeats this certification without prohibiting every mass settlement. A revised class or fund requires its own lawful proof and representation; no release, distribution or additional monetary obligation follows from appellate approval of settlement desirability alone.
