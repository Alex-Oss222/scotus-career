## Components

**C — Commerce authority.** All eight affirm rejection of the commerce challenge as applied to both convictions. The identified mechanism is section 922(d)(1)'s prohibition on sale or other disposition to a recipient known, or reasonably believed, to have the qualifying conviction, coupled with the recipient-possession rule in section 922(g)(1). Exempting the same disqualified recipients' acquisition and retention would allow them to obtain and retain the firearms that the valid supply restriction prevents them from receiving. Congress may include that fitted counterpart to prevent evasion or material impairment of the national supply regime. Each convicted defendant's locally possessed pistol falls within that recipient/weapon relationship; no new finding about a transaction is required by that constitutional rationale. The supplier's specified mental-state requirement is not silently imported as the complete culpability rule of the recipient offense, and no statutory conviction exclusion or relief provision is displaced.

The strongest contrary path is that Connecticut manufacture alone cannot make every subsequent local possession a matter of federal constitutional power. That objection is correct as a statement of the limits preserved in Lopez, Hale, Rybar, Kirk and Wright. The conclusion here does not rely on provenance alone: the independently identified prohibited-recipient supply mechanism supplies the connection, and the enacted interstate element supplies an additional, separate statutory requirement. General criminal dangerousness, the restaurant burglary and downstream crime costs are not alternative commerce grounds. No general firearms police power or automatic analogy from the different machinegun-stock restriction is adopted. Effective Franklyn Part II now directly supplies this section 922(d)(1)/section 922(g)(1) mechanism; the same provisions cover both its ammunition and these firearms, without changing the recipient class or borrowing proof of either pistol's movement from Gonzalez's stipulation.

**I — Requested instruction.** All eight affirm rejection of the instruction demanding a direct interstate effect beyond out-of-state manufacture. Once the covered class is constitutionally included through the identified mechanism, the jury need not find that this particular possession has a contemporaneous or substantial economic effect. Bass and Scarborough's surviving statutory construction does not impose that additional condition; Lopez does not rewrite it. The prosecution must still prove the enacted firearm nexus beyond a reasonable doubt. No verbatim approved replacement charge is supplied and no other instructional challenge is decided.

The competing path would use constitutional avoidance to add a new direct-effect element. That is unnecessary given the valid mechanism and would change the retained statutory requirement. The jury's task remains deciding the offense facts, not resolving the constitutional sufficiency of Congress's national scheme.

**S — Nexus sufficiency.** All eight affirm denial of the two acquittal motions. The expert testified that both pistols were manufactured in Connecticut; their possession in North Carolina permits a rational jury to find that each firearm had crossed a state boundary. Under Jackson, viewing this evidence favorably to the verdict, the enacted nexus is sufficiently proved. The government need not identify the precise shipment date, carrier, seller or defendant's personal crossing. This conclusion is evidentiary and statutory; it does not supply the independent constitutional mechanism merely from the same historic movement. No expert-qualification, authentication or contrary-evidence defect is supplied by the record, and none is invented.

**Remedy and limits.** All eight affirm both separate judgments against the three presented objections. No conviction is reversed, no instruction or trial is ordered, and no sentence is recalculated. No preserved Second Amendment claim appears, so none is added from Wright or FOP. The prior-felony element, statutory conviction exclusions, possession knowledge and each weapon's classification are not newly adjudicated merely because other cases could contest them.
