## 1. In re Sealed Case — No. 98-93 — April 13, 1999

**Reconciled disposition:** All eight Associates retain affirmance of the compulsion order against the asserted Rule 501 protective-function privilege. All retain the independent handoff's recognition-first reasoning and its exact qualifications. No new overcoming-need test, absolute privilege, actual-security finding, contempt consequence or testimony schedule is adopted.

**Historical evidence and limit:** The Supreme Court denied certiorari on November 9, 1998. Ginsburg and Breyer wrote separately against denial. Neither writing adjudicated the merits or supplies a historical merits tally. The court of appeals' unanimous affirmance is the reviewed judgment, not a Supreme Court coalition. Ginsburg specifically treated judicial creation of this privilege as debatable and sought a definitive answer. Her review vote does not imply recognition. Breyer gave substantive reasons to take the claim seriously: Rule 501 permits development; lack of precedent matters little for an unprecedented demand; security depends on close access; criminal law's complexity could make otherwise lawful conversations unexpectedly discoverable; and knowledgeable protective officials' statements should not simply be dismissed as speculation. He sought determination of the existence and contours of a possible privilege, including waiver, without announcing a final merits rule.

**Comparison of live paths:** The frozen handoff acknowledges the Director declaration and former President Bush letter as affirmative support, preserves common-law development without a universal empirical-proof prerequisite, and does not assume Presidents would deliberately refuse all protection. It rejects the asserted broad coverage of information acquired in proximity, including independently observed facts, together with the contemporaneous-felony boundary. The exception covers a felony that had been, was being, or would be committed, assessed at the time of the observation or statement, in a federal investigation or prosecution. An observation that becomes probative only later remains outside that exception. The exception's uncertain coverage does not itself establish a reliable protected relationship. Recognition and overcoming a recognized privilege remain separate inquiries; an extraordinary-need threshold cannot supply the missing recognition predicate.

The no-privilege result does not rest on a general requirement that a threatened assassination or actual impaired protection already have occurred. It also does not deny the plausibility of the access concern. The question is whether the record and proposed scope justify this evidentiary class. Independently supported security accommodations and properly asserted other privileges remain available under their own law. The handoff's reference to speculative future behavior means that this Court cannot find actual impairment or order a factual hearing merely to discover whether some later conflict might arise; it must not be rendered as a finding that the supporting declarations were nonexistent or worthless.
