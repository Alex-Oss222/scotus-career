# Department of the Army v. Blue Fox, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the lien claim against the Army.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lien claim against the Army shall be dismissed. The judgment does not foreclose a properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against a Miller Act surety, or relief supported by another unequivocal waiver. No Miller Act payment bond was obtained for this contract, so the general reservation of a properly founded alternative remedy does not establish an available surety recovery here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: APA waiver and equitable lien against federal funds

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves sovereign immunity before the equitable merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 702 does not authorize substitute monetary recovery from general federal funds. No identifiable retained fund belonging to Blue Fox or specific statutory payment obligation remains for the court to restore; the requested lien would instead attach federal property to replace the prime contractor's default and alter appropriations and creditor priorities without an unequivocal waiver.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When no identifiable retained fund or specific payment entitlement belonging to the claimant remains, § 702 does not waive federal sovereign immunity for an equitable lien whose function is substitute compensation from Government property for a private debtor's default.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Blue Fox seeks payment from Government-controlled property to make good the prime contractor's unpaid debt, not return of a segregated res or enforcement of an enacted payment obligation owed directly by the United States. *Bowen* is therefore materially different, and the Miller Act's remedial structure cannot be supplemented by an implied lien against general federal funds. The absence of a waiver controls even though the Government's administration contributed to the remedial gap. Nordic Village confirms why describing this claim as a lien cannot itself establish consent. The Army’s failure to require any bond explains the lack of a surety remedy here; no existing Miller Act surety is assumed. The actual disbursements and the absence of retained earned payments distinguish restitution of property belonging to Blue Fox from compensation for Verdan’s unpaid debt.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve the Army's conduct or hold that governmental wrongdoing is immaterial. It leaves specific relief concerning an identifiable retained fund, an enacted payment mandate, a properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against a Miller Act surety, and relief supported by another unequivocal waiver to their own governing law.
