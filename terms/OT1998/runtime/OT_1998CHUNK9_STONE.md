# Richardson v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Richardson's continuing-criminal-enterprise conviction shall be set aside unless it may lawfully be sustained through proceedings consistent with the element-specific unanimity rule, subject to ordinary double-jeopardy and retrial principles.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Unanimity on the continuing-series violations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether the particular violations are elements or merely alternative means.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 848 uses 'violations' to refer to independently criminal acts and makes a continuing series of them part of the greater offense. The jury must therefore agree on the same qualifying violations, though not on every evidentiary detail or means of committing each agreed predicate.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Each violation necessary to constitute the continuing series under § 848 is an offense element. The jury must unanimously identify the same qualifying violations beyond a reasonable doubt, though it need not agree on every evidentiary detail or means of committing an agreed predicate.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A conviction without agreement on concrete predicate crimes would permit jurors to find different criminal series and still return one verdict. Statutory text, traditional unanimity, and constitutional avoidance reject that result.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require unanimity on every overt act, item of evidence, participant, or means used to commit an agreed predicate violation.

# Reno v. Goncalves

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the First Circuit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Board of Immigration Appeals shall consider Goncalves's former § 212(c) application under the law applicable to pending applications when AEDPA took effect and exercise the discretion that law supplies. The judgment confers no entitlement to a favorable exercise of discretion and does not adjudicate the excluded equal-protection claim.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Continued availability of § 2241 habeas review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves jurisdiction first. The petition presents pure statutory and constitutional questions, and no other threshold defect defeats the historic habeas channel.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the clear-statement and meaningful-review principles already operating in the simulation. AEDPA and IIRIRA altered immigration review extensively, but the provisions invoked do not clearly eliminate § 2241 for the legal claims presented, and the Government may not derive withdrawal of the writ from implication alone.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AEDPA and IIRIRA did not clearly withdraw district-court jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 to hear the pure statutory and constitutional habeas challenges presented by a covered criminal alien in Goncalves's posture.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Jurisdiction is not assumed and is not created by the seriousness of the claim. It exists because § 2241 supplied a historic channel and Congress did not clearly close it in the provisions governing this pending proceeding. The alternative review structure did not furnish an equally clear route for the specific legal challenge when Goncalves filed. The First Circuit therefore properly reached the statutory issue.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not establish district-court review of every discretionary immigration determination, reopen factual findings, or displace an exclusive review channel Congress states clearly and preserves meaningfully.

### Issue 2: Temporal application of AEDPA § 440(d)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the temporal question because jurisdiction survives and the Board applied § 440(d) to a pending application.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Landgraf*, *Rivers*, and the enacted structure. Congress did not clearly direct § 440(d) to govern already-filed applications, while neighboring provisions contained express temporal instructions. Applying the new categorical bar to a request already filed and adjudicated under former § 212(c) would attach a new legal disability to the pending proceeding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AEDPA § 440(d) does not apply to a former § 212(c) application that was pending when AEDPA took effect, absent a clear congressional direction making the new categorical restriction applicable to such pending applications.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Goncalves had invoked the discretionary process, obtained an immigration-judge decision on its merits, and pursued an administrative appeal before enactment. The new law did not merely change a tribunal's procedure; it removed eligibility for the discretionary judgment already underway. The First Circuit correctly required the Board to decide the pending request under the former eligibility rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern applications first filed after AEDPA's effective date, guarantee discretionary relief, or decide the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal in another posture.

### Issue 3: Interpretive weight owed the Attorney General

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the question because the Board relied on the Attorney General's decision in *Matter of Soriano*.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone works within the then-operative statutory-deference framework and does not silently replace it. The Attorney General cannot receive controlling weight on district-court jurisdiction, and no deference is due when ordinary interpretive tools resolve the temporal question against the agency's construction. At most, *Soriano* receives persuasive consideration for its reasoning, which does not overcome the text, structure, and nonretroactivity principles.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Attorney General's conclusion that AEDPA § 440(d) applies to pending applications receives no controlling weight because the statute's temporal operation is resolved by ordinary interpretive tools and the agency's contrary reasoning is unpersuasive.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agency's immigration expertise is real within delegated policy and factual domains, but the issue here is Congress's temporal command. *Soriano* treated statutory silence as authorization to extinguish pending eligibility and gave insufficient effect to the enactment's express temporal contrasts. Deference cannot make silence carry a legal consequence the statutory structure does not support.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not deny appropriate weight to authorized immigration judgments on genuinely unresolved statutory details, policy choices, or technical administration within the agency's lawful domain.

# Neder v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, vacate the affected convictions, and remand for a new trial on those counts if the United States elects to retry.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Every count on which materiality was removed from the jury shall be vacated. Any retrial must submit materiality and every other offense element to the jury under the ordinary beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. Any count not affected by the omitted-element ruling remains governed by its own preserved issues.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Materiality as a fraud element

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory element identification before the constitutional consequence of omission.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The settled legal meaning of fraud includes materiality unless Congress displaces it. The federal mail, wire, and bank-fraud statutes contain no contrary command.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Materiality is an element of federal mail, wire, and bank fraud.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutes employ fraud as a legal term, and nothing in their wording displaces its settled materiality requirement. A lender’s capacity to be influenced by inflated value, concealed self-dealing or a fictitious down payment supplies the relevant inquiry; successful inducement of every lender is not an additional element. Wells concerns a different enactment and cannot make every false representation immaterial as a matter of statutory definition. Once materiality is identified as an element, Gaudin requires its submission to the jury. The tax counts already expressly require materiality.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide materiality under every differently worded federal offense.

### Issue 2: Complete removal of an element from the jury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the remedial classification because the trial judge completely withheld materiality and the Eleventh Circuit sustained the convictions through harmless-error review.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The complete withdrawal of materiality is constitutional error. Roy prevents treating every omitted element as automatically structural. Stone would nevertheless order a new trial on the affected counts because an appellate conclusion that materiality evidence was strong cannot replace a reliable finding that this error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Where materiality was withheld from the jury and the actual verdict and record do not establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, the affected convictions must be vacated for a new trial. The omission alone does not establish a universal structural-error rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Roy controls: the omission is reviewable trial error, and Chapman permits reliable inferences from the whole record concerning the actual verdict. The Government has a substantial argument. Omitting more than $1 million and $4 million from successive returns plainly bears on tax computation; the alleged inflated appraisals and nonexistent down payments similarly bear on lenders’ financing decisions, and Neder did not contest materiality as a factual matter. Those considerations must be weighed, not excluded by a necessary-equivalence-only test. Stone nevertheless would find the burden unsatisfied. The instructions affirmatively removed tax and bank materiality from the jury and never asked it to assess that dimension of mail or wire fraud. Findings of knowing falsity and fraudulent intent do not themselves show that the jury considered the capacity of each charged representation to influence the relevant decision. The Government’s argument on this record establishes what a properly instructed jury would almost certainly find, but does not reliably isolate the omission’s contribution to the verdicts actually rendered. This is a conclusion about these instructions, count classes and verdicts, not a rule that every omitted element is structural or that whole-record inference is forbidden. Sullivan explains the prohibition on inventing a verdict; Roy supplies the governing review. Johnson’s denial of discretionary correction after forfeiture does not shift Chapman’s burden on Neder’s preserved objections.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Vacate only convictions affected by removal or omission of materiality. No automatic structural-error rule, acquittal for insufficient evidence, or invalidation of differently worded §1014 offenses follows. A valid admission or a record establishing actual-verdict harmlessness could require a different result.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial vagueness question and uses only the narrow adequate standardless-enforcement ground.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The conviction and sentence shall be vacated unless Virginia proves the confrontation error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt under the complete record. The State may retry Lilly without the statement or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Admission of a blame-shifting custodial confession

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the contemporary confrontation framework. The blame-shifting accusation is not within a firmly rooted exception at the asserted scope, and its custodial circumstances supply no adequate particularized guarantee of trustworthiness. This conclusion does not adopt a later categorical doctrine for all testimonial statements.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nontestifying accomplice’s custodial confession that minimizes the declarant’s role and assigns the charged killing to the accused is not admissible merely as a statement against penal interest; absent prior cross-examination or a genuinely applicable established exception, its admission violates confrontation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lilly’s brother minimized his own responsibility while attributing the principal killing to Lilly during separate police questioning. Context must test the specific inculpatory assertions; admitting some misconduct does not make every accusation against another person reliable. The interrogation created concrete incentives to curry favor, shift blame and reduce personal exposure. Independent corroboration cannot cure the absence of the guarantees required for this hearsay accusation, and Lilly had no opportunity to test those incentives through cross-examination. White’s accepted, firmly rooted exceptions do not encompass this different setting. Williamson reinforces the need to separate the assertions without deciding the state evidence rule or the constitutional question by itself.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar genuinely self-inculpatory remarks severable from blame shifting, forfeiture by wrongdoing, dying declarations, or statements admitted for a nonhearsay purpose with effective limiting safeguards.

# Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's judgment and bar enforcement of § 1304 against the advertisements described.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Government may not enforce § 1304 against the truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling at issue in this case. The judgment does not facially invalidate § 1304 or restrict materially different regulation of unlawful, misleading, or narrowly targeted advertising.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Truthful casino advertising

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the as-applied commercial-speech claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Under *Central Hudson* and *Rubin*, the Government must show that the restriction directly and materially advances a substantial interest with a reasonable fit. Extensive comparable exceptions for state, tribal, charitable, and other gambling contradict the claimed demand-reduction mechanism.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1304 may not suppress truthful advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling on this record because the federal scheme's extensive comparable exceptions prevent the ban from directly and materially advancing its asserted interests with a reasonable fit.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Federal law permits substantial advertising that stimulates the same general demand while suppressing the Louisiana broadcasters' truthful information. Direct regulation of gambling remains available and further weakens the asserted need to suppress speech. 44 Liquormart supplies two distinct inquiries: the actual demand-reduction mechanism and the proportional burden on truthful information. The comparable gambling exceptions undermine that mechanism here; available direct controls bear on fit without imposing a least-restrictive-means rule. Edge’s lottery-border setting involved a restriction the Court found meaningfully served the particular allocation between permitting and prohibiting States. That record does not establish that this differently perforated casino-advertising scheme materially serves either asserted federal interest.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect false or misleading advertising, promotion of unlawful transactions, targeting of minors, or a materially narrower evidence-supported restriction; it does not facially invalidate § 1304.
