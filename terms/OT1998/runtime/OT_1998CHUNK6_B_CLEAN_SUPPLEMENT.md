## United States v. Rodriguez-Moreno ? neutral preflight

**Channel and record.** The supplied case is certiorari review of the Third Circuit's venue ruling on the section 924(c) count. The operative issue is the geographic conduct sufficient for that count when kidnapping continues through several States and the gun is used in another district. No preserved additional ground for reversing the kidnapping conviction, new conspiracy charge or independent aiding-and-abetting theory is supplied. Such a charge cannot be invented to avoid the actual venue question. The defendant's denial of New Jersey firearm use and the government's reliance on continuing kidnapping must remain distinct.

**Exact entering law.** Read [Rodriguez-Moreno focused law](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK6_B_RODRIGUEZ_MORENO_LAW.md). Cabrales, effective June 1, 1998, unanimously holds that prior criminal generation of money does not relocate completed laundering transactions. The actual charged conduct, rather than every necessary circumstance or remote causal antecedent, locates the offense. Its reserved transportation, predicate-participation, aiding-and-abetting and conspiracy questions remain reserved. The operative Standard is **Venue for completed money-laundering transactions**, present operation effective June 1, 1998; its exact copied rule carries the same limits. It is not a general statutory-verb-only test.

Article III, the Sixth Amendment, Rule 18 and section 3237(a) constrain venue. Section 3237(a)'s ordinary continuing-offense rule concerns an offense begun in one district and completed in another, or committed in more than one district, absent a separate express enactment. It does not make every prior related event part of the charged offense. The charged version of section 924(c), the predicate kidnapping under section 1201 and their conduct relationship must therefore do the work. No later statutory amendment or new possession theory is substituted for the supplied use-or-carriage count.

**Supported competing paths.** One path treats commission of the ongoing kidnapping as essential conduct incorporated by the firearm offense's required relationship to that crime; if adopted, a district in which that conduct continued can satisfy venue even though the weapon was used elsewhere. The contrary path treats the firearm use or carriage as the conduct charged by section 924(c), with the predicate offense providing the qualifying circumstance; if adopted on these allegations, a kidnapping district without the charged firearm conduct is insufficient. The strength of the first path is the ongoing predicate's statutory role, unlike Cabrales's completed source crime. The strength of the second is the constitutional venue restriction's focus on the particular charged crime, rather than a distinct offense merely incorporated as a condition. Neither path follows merely from counting verbs or from saying every element matters.

**Justice-specific material.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer all joined the actual Cabrales conduct/circumstance distinction and its carefully bounded reservations. That common commitment is a constraint to be applied to this different statute, not evidence that all eight must choose the same characterization here. No matter-specific historical authorship, vote or rationale enters this packet.

**Remedy limits.** A sufficient venue holding removes the stated venue ground. Reinstatement must remain confined to the actual reversed count and ordinary unresolved issues; no new conviction, recharging order or alteration of another count is implied. If venue is insufficient, Cabrales distinguishes venue dismissal from an acquittal and does not itself forbid a properly venued prosecution. Availability of a new prosecution is not an order to bring it.

## Wyoming v. Houghton ? neutral preflight

**Channel and record.** The supplied certiorari question concerns the automobile exception's reach into a closed purse known before the initial intrusion to belong to a passenger. Vehicle-level probable cause is conceded. The identity discrepancy became known only after the officer opened the purse's wallet; later contents and track observations cannot retrospectively justify that first intrusion. Ownership of the purse, separate claimed ownership or disclaimer concerning internal containers, and the timing of each discovery must remain separate. The approved packet's authenticated search chronology is adequate for the identified legal issue; this preflight makes no new factual finding.

**Exact entering law.** Read [Houghton focused law](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK6_B_HOUGHTON_LAW.md), plus the current public copies of [Knowles](../entering-law/knowles_v_iowa_merits_1998-12-08.md) and [Carter and Johns](../entering-law/minnesota_v_carter_and_johns_merits_1998-12-01.md). Labron/Kilgore, effective July 1, 1996, permits a probable-cause search of a readily mobile automobile without an additional urgency showing and limits places and containers by the object sought. Wellons, effective May 30, 1995, recognizes a separate legitimate privacy interest in closed personal luggage but expressly preserves a valid warrant exception reaching that luggage. It does not revive Sanders's displaced automobile warrant rule.

The exact Standard **Privacy in a rented vehicle and personal luggage**, effective May 30, 1995, preserves both the personal interest and valid-exception distinction. **Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop**, effective February 19, 1997, authorizes the limited exit direction but no automatic frisk, search, forcible detention or extended stop. **Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop**, copied in the slice, does not transform initial stop authority into authority for a further intrusion. No separately titled Labron Standard appears in the opening register; its controlling Holdings entry is the direct authority for the automobile rule.

Knowles, effective December 8, 1998, rejects citation alone and unused arrest power as grounds for the full search-incident-to-arrest exception. All eight Associates join its preserved independent probable-cause search grounds and ordinary, properly available remedial return. It supplies no automobile-container ownership exception. Carter and Johns, effective December 1, 1998, preserves the distinction between personal privacy, whether an intrusion is a search, justification and evidence-specific consequences. Its domestic-sharing rule does not decide a purse search in a vehicle. Its eight-Associate conditional remedial rule requires genuine independence where that issue arises and supplies no automatic factual finding of independent source.

**Supported competing paths.** Applying Labron's object-based scope could permit the purse search because it is a container capable of holding the drugs for which the car may lawfully be searched. The competing route would adopt a qualified limitation for an identified passenger's closed personal effect where the accepted vehicle probable cause derives from another occupant and establishes no independent nexus to that effect. That route requires a candid modification or limitation of the present container rule; Wellons alone does not already supply it. Di Re's person-search distinction offers an analogy but does not by itself classify the purse as the passenger's body. Conversely, calling the purse an effect does not eliminate the protected privacy that Wellons recognizes.

An independent ground may justify a later act only on facts and law actually available at that point. No broad consent, abandonment, inventory, protective-search or inevitable-discovery finding is supplied. A court may not invent one. If an earlier unlawful intrusion produces the later information, the resulting evidence requires its own ordinary causal and remedial analysis rather than retrospective validation of the initial act.

**Justice-specific material.** O'Connor authored Labron's controlling automobile rule, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer among the Associates. Stevens and Ginsburg opposed review there on jurisdictional or discretionary grounds, not through an adopted additional-exigency merits rule. All eight joined Wellons's separate luggage interest and preserved valid exceptions. Thomas's Wilson opinion has O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer on limited passenger-exit authority; Stevens and Kennedy's disagreement over that intrusion supplies no automatic purse-search position. All eight join Knowles's independent-grounds distinction. In Carter, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join affirmative shared-home privacy, while O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas oppose that application; the full public copy preserves each ground and prevents treating the split as a generic privacy preference.

Reversal of a passenger-ownership exclusion rule need not announce validity of every later act or automatic conviction reinstatement. A justified limitation must identify which intrusion fails and its legally supported consequences. No acquittal, universal passenger exemption or categorical immunity is inferred.
