# Objective lower-court reasoning recitations for fresh validation

These mechanically copied reported recitations identify the lower courts' grounds. They are not the present Supreme Court's reasons, result, or votes. Karl and Walter are different litigants. The quoted earlier Supreme Court stay order is historical procedural background only: it supplies no current-term Court action or merits precedent. The Walter panel's own reported adoption of its earlier reasoning, not the supposed force of an unrecorded Supreme Court action, is the material fact to validate.

L21: Among the claims raised in Walter LaGrand’s petition for a writ of habeas corpus was the claim that execution by lethal gas constituted cruel and unusual punishment under the 【3†Eighth Amendment】 to the United States Constitution. The District Court found the claim to be procedurally defaulted because Walter LaGrand had failed to raise it either on direct appeal or in his petition for state post-conviction relief, when the sole method of execution was by way of lethal gas.
L22: On appeal, the Ninth Circuit did not reach the issue of procedural default because it found the claim was not ripe until and unless LaGrand chose gas as his method of execution. LaGrand v. Stewart, supra, at 1264. The petition for writ of habeas corpus was denied. Id., at 1269.

L25: The Ninth Circuit held that Karl LaGrand’s lethal gas claim was procedurally barred but found cause and prejudice to excuse the default. The court concluded that Karl LaGrand’s failure to raise the lethal gas claim was excused because there was no legal or factual basis for the claim when he pursued his direct appeal in state court. Prejudice was shown because he was now faced with execution by a method the Ninth Circuit had previously found to be unconstitutional.
L26: The Ninth Circuit also addressed the State’s argument that Karl LaGrand’s choice of execution method constituted a waiver of his current claim. According to the Ninth Circuit, its precedent dictated that “【3†Eighth Amendment】 protections may not be waived, at least in the area of capital punishment.” See LaGrand v. Stewart, No. 99-99004, at 10—11 (CA9 February 24, 1999).

L30: The District Court also denied a certificate of appealability, concluding “that the issue of procedural default of Petitioner’s lethal gas challenge is not debatable among jurists of reason.”
L31: The Ninth Circuit panel granted a certificate of appealability and proceeded to the merits of the case. It concluded that our order lifting the stay of execution in LaGrand v. Stewart, No. 99—99004 (CA9 February 23, 1999), did not pass upon the merits of the panel’s opinion and concluded that its reasoning remained sound. It then denied the stay of execution but restrained and enjoined the State of Arizona from executing Walter LaGrand by means of lethal gas.
