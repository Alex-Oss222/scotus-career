### Issue 1: McCarran-Ferguson reverse preemption

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe (June 11, 1993) applies McCarran-Ferguson functionally: state policyholder protection and necessary liquidation administration prevail over conflicting federal priority, while unrelated creditors’ preferences do not. It does not make every additional federal remedy an impairment. Here Nevada’s prohibition of the alleged discount fraud and nonexclusive remedies bear on whether RICO frustrates or reinforces the State’s actual regulatory choices. Barnett Bank distinguishes the threshold exception for a federal statute specifically relating to insurance from the separate question of operative conflict. RICO is not the insurance-specific authorization examined there, so Humana cannot bypass McCarran-Ferguson on Barnett’s §92 ground. Fabe’s protected state purposes remain the relevant comparison: the question is whether this RICO claim defeats Nevada’s insurance policy, not merely whether federal relief differs from state relief.
* **Other Governing Law:** McCarran-Ferguson Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1012(b); RICO, 18 U.S.C. §§ 1961–1964; Nevada insurance law.
* **Material Other Authority:** Department of Treasury v. Fabe supplies the statute’s protection for state laws enacted for insurance regulation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit permitted the RICO action to proceed, holding that it did not invalidate, impair, or supersede Nevada insurance law.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Policyholders argue that RICO reinforces Nevada’s prohibition of the same fraudulent insurance practice. Humana argues that federal treble remedies impair Nevada’s chosen enforcement scheme. Whether state remedies are exclusive and whether federal liability frustrates actual state policy matter more than the mere existence of two remedies.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Compatible concurrent enforcement permits the RICO claim to proceed subject to its elements. A demonstrated conflict with a protected state scheme could require displacement under McCarran-Ferguson at that scope; neither result decides the fraud allegations.
