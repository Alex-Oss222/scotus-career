FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS 
9. On or about May 21, 1993, at approximately 9:00 
a.m., plaintiffs lived at 531 Balham, La Puente, California, 
in the County of Los Angeles. 
10. 
At said date, time and place, the defendant 
police officers and certain Does broke into the residence 
damaging the front door. There may have been no knock 
notice or other similar notice prior to the police entry by 
la.v enforcement. 
11. 
The defendant deputies proceeded to search 
plaintiffs’ house without such notice and without reason- 
able or probable cause. The deputies ransacked the 
house, destroying property, dumping and scattering 
plaintiffs’ personal effects, and damaging and breaking 
items. 
12. 
Further, items including money were unlawfully 
taken from the home and never returned to plaintiff's 
notwithstanding plaintiffs’ repeated requests. 
13. 
Plaintiffs in ho way consented to defendants’ 
conduct, but have protested the same. Defendants, and 
each of them, had no valid arrest or search warrant, or 
probable or legal cause to arrest the plaintiffs or search 
their home. 
14. 
By reason of the above-described acts and omis- 
sions of defendants, and each of them, plaintiffs sus- 
tained injuries, including, but not limited to, humiliation 
and indignities, and physical, mental and emotional pain 
and suffering all to their damage in an amount to be 
ascertained. Plaintiffs have ‘hus far incurred medical/ 
psychological expenses in a sum to be ascertained. 

15. 
The aforementioned acts of defendants were 
willful, wanton, malicious, oppressive, and done with 
reckless indifference to and/or callous disregard for 
plaintiffs’ rights and justify the awarding of exemplary 
and punitive damages in an amount to be ascertained 
according to proof at the time of trial. 
16. 
By reason of the above-described acts and omis- 
sions of defendants, plaintiffs were required to retain an 
attorney to institute, prosecute and render legal assis- 
tance to plaintiffs in the within action so that they might 
vindicate the loss and impairment of their rights. By 
reason thereof, plaintiffs request payment by defendants 
of a reasonable sum for attorney’s fees pursuant to 42 
U.S.C. Section 1988.

In accordance with the provisions of 28 U.S.C. 
§ 636(c), you are hereby aotified that the full-time United 
States Magistrates of this District Court, in addition to 
their other duties, may, upon the consent of all parties to 
their civil case, conduct any and all proceedings in a civil 
case, including a jury or non-jury trial, and order the 
entry of a final judgment. Copies of appropriate consent 
forms for this purpose (Form number CV-II) are available 
from the Clerk of Court. 
Your opportunity to have your case disposed of by a 
Magistrate is subject to the calendar requirements of the 
court. Accordingly, the district Judge to whom your case 
is assigned must approve the reference of the case to a 
Magistrate for disposition. 
You should be aware that your decision to consent, or 
not to consent, to the referral of your case to a United 
States Magistrate for disposition is entirely voluntary and 
should be communicated solely to the clerk of the district 
court by submitting form number CV-11 after it has been 
completed. Only if all parties to the case consent to the 
reference to a Magistrate will either the Judge or Magis- 
trate to whom the case has been assigned be informed of 
your decision.

DECLARATION OF DAVID MELNYK 
I, DAVID MELNYK, do hereby declare: 
1. 
I am a detective with the West Covina Police 
Department and have been a peace officer since 1978. 
Unless otherwise stated, the following is of my own 
personal knowledge and if called upon to testify thereto, 
I would do so. 

30 
2. 
Il am aware that I am a defendant in Perkins v. 
City of West Covina, et al., Case No. CV 93 7084 SVW 
(CTx). I understand that this lawsuit pertains to the prep- 
aration of a search warrant and the execution of that 
search warrant at the plaintiffs’ home on or about May 
21, 1993. 
3. On May 18, 1993, while on duty as a detective 
with the West Covina Police Department, I learned that a 
male later identified as Julio Alberto Clark had been shot 
and killed within the City of West Covina. I assisted my 
partner, defendant West Covina Police Department Detec- 
tive Michael Ferrari, in the investigation of this homicide. 
4. On May 18, 1993, Marcus Marsh, a.k.a., Marcus 
Winter, became a suspect in the murder of Julio Alberto 
Clark and other crimes. 
5. On May 18, 1993, witness Lashaun McDuffy told 
me that Marcus Winter was her boyfriend and that his 
telephone number was (818) 854-1069. I traced the forego- 
ing telephone number to plaintiffs’ address at 531 Balham 
Avenue in La Puente. 
6. My deposition was taken in this matter on Febru- 
ary 22, 1994. During such deposition, I was asked what I 
had done during our investigation to ascertain that 
Marcus Marsh, a.k.a. Marcus Winter, lived at plaintiffs’ 
residence at 531 Balham Avenue in La Puente. | stated 
that I had conducted computer checks of various records 
regarding Marcus Marsh and found items listing Mr. 
Marsh's address as 531 Balham Avenue. At the time of the 
deposition, I believed the foregoing to be true and cor- 
rect. 
31 
7. Following my deposition, however, I reviewed 
the file in this case and realized that the foregoing por- 
tion of my testimony was in error. Notes contained in the 
file refreshed my recollection and I now recall that West 
Covina Police Department Officer Arthur Marinello 
informed me that he had spoken to Mr. Marsh’s probation 
officer who had informed him that Mr. Marsh’s most 
recent address was 531 Balham Avenue in La Puente. I 
also found documents in Mr. Marsh’s Cadillac which 
identified his address as 531 Balham Avenue. I relayed 
this information to Detective Michael Ferrari. 
8. 
I am informed and believe that on May 20, 1993, 
defendant Detective Michael Ferrari obtained a search 
warrant authorizing a search of plaintiffs’ residence 
located at 531 Balham Avenue in La Puente from the 
Honorable Dan Thomas Oki of the Citrus Municipal 
Court. 
= 
9. On or about May 21, 1993, I assisted in supervis- 
ing the officers who served and executed such search 
warrant at 531 Balham Avenue in La Puente. Prior to 
arriving at plaintiffs’ residence, I had reviewed the search 
warrant. I was fully aware of what items it authorized 
officers to search for and assisted in supervising the 
officers who searched for those items. 
10. 
It is the practice of the West Covina Police 
Department to conduct all premises searches in a reason- 
able manner without any unnecessary destruction of or 
damage to property. It is also the practice of the West 
Covina Police Department to consult the Search Warrant 
Manual drafted by the Los Angeles County District Attor- 
ney’s Office regarding searches. I personally consult such 

32 
Manual and consider it to be authoritative source on legal 
issues regarding searches. Insofar as the return property 
seized during a search, I have relied upon pages 129-130 
of such Manual, true and correct copies of which are 
attached hereto as Exhibit “4”. 
11. 
While supervising officers conducting the 
search, I did not intentionally damage or destroy any 
property. If I did cause any damage to property, it was 
inadvertent. I do not, however, have any recollection of 
damaging or destroying any property during the course 
of searching plaintiffs’ residence and did not observe any 
other West Covina Police Department officer damage or 
destroy any property during the course of such search. 
I declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of 
the United States and the State of California that the 
foregoing is true and correct. Executed this 14TH day of 
March, 1994, at West Covina, California.

IT IS THE POLICY OF THE COURT TO LIBERALLY 
PERMIT THE AMENDMENT OF PLEADINGS 
ESPECIALLY WHERE AN ADDITIONAL CAUSE OF 
ACTION ARISES FROM THE SAME OPERATIVE 
FACTS ALREADY PLED. 
Motions to Amend are freely granted where no preju- 
dice results to the opposing party. Fed.R.Civ.P. 15(a) pro- 
vides: “Leave shall be freely given when justice so 
requires.” (Emphasis added). The Supreme Court, in its 
opinion in Foman 
v. Davis, 371 U.S. 178, 83 S.Ct. 227 
(1962), enunciated the following general standard, which 
is to be employed under Rule 15(a) by the district courts: 
51 
“If the underlying facts or circumstances relied 
upon by a plaintiff may be a proper subject of 
relief, he ought to be afforded an opportunity to 
test his claim on the merits. In the absence of 
any apparent or declared reason 
- such as 
undue delay, bad faith or dilatory motive on the 
part of the movant, repeated failure to cure defi- 
ciencies by amendments previously allowed, 
undue prejudice to the opposing party by virtue 
of allowance of the amendment, futility of 
amendment, etc. — the leave sought should, as 
the rules require, be ‘freely given.’ ” 
Id., 371 U.S. 178, 182, 83 S.Ct. 227, 230 (1962). 
The seminal case in the Ninth Circuit, interpreting 
Foman, is Howey v. United States, 481 F.2d 1187 (9th Cir. 
1973). In Howey, the district court denied a motion to 
amend. The motion to amend had been made five years 
after the complaint had been filed. The Ninth Circuit 
reversed. In doing so, the court observed as follows: 
. “where there is a lack of prejudice to the oppo- 
sing party and the amended complaint is obvi- 
ously not frivolous, or made as a dilatory 
maneuver in bad faith, it is an abuse of discre- 
tion to deny such a motion.” 
Howey at 1191. The court further observed that “[o]nly 
where prejudice is shown or the movant acts in bad faith 
are courts protecting the judicial system 
. 
. 
. when they 
deny leave to amend a pleading.” Id. See also, Hurn v. 
Retirement Fund Trust of Plumbing, Heating & Piping Indus- 
try, 648 F.2d 1252 (9th Cir. 1981); International Association 
of Machinists and Aerospace Workers v. Republic Airlines, 761 
F.2d 1386 (9th Cir. 1985); Poling v. Morgan, 829 F.2d 882 
(9th Cir. 1987). 

52

“An amendment of a pleading relates back to 
the date of original pleading when (2) the claims

of the conduct, transactions, or occurrence set 
forth or attempted to be set forth in the original 
pleading,....

Introduction 
Presently before the Court is a civil rights action 
alleging violations of plaintiffs’ Fourth Amendment 
rights. The claims stem from the allegedly unlawful 
search of plaintiffs’ home as well as the allegedly unlaw- 
ful seizure of plaintiffs’ property, including approxi- 
mately $2,500 in cash and a starter pistol. Plaintiffs have 
named as defendants the City of West Covina and the 
officers present during the search of their home. 
By means of an Order dated May 10, the Court 
granted summary judgment in favor of all defendants on 
the claim that the defendants failed to meet the proper 
knock-notice requirements and the claim that the search 
warrant was issued without probable cause. The Court 
also granted summary judgment in favor of the City of 
West Covina on the Claim for unlawful search. 
The Court presently grants summary judgment for all 
defendants on the claim for unlawful seizure of plaintiffs’ 
property. 

Statement of Facts 
Lawrence Perkins, Clara Perkins and April Perkins 
(“plaintiffs”) reside at 531 Balham Street in West Covina. 
On May 21, 1993, their home was searched by police 
officers of the City of West Covina (the “City”). Among 
the officers present at the search were defendants Detec- 
tive Ferrari, Detective Melnyk, Lieutenant Schimanski, 
Detective Aldecoa and Detective O’Brien (the “individual 
defendants”). 
On May 18, 1993, an individual named Julio Alberto 
Clark had been shot to death at the residence of Lashaun 
McDuffy. The police searched McDuffy’s residence. They 
found evidence linking McDuffy’s boyfriend Marcus 
Marsh (a.k.a. Marcus Winter) to the murder. They also 
found a vial of cocaine and $994.00 in cash. 
Marsh was known by the police department to be a 
member of the neighborhood Crips street gang. Further- 
more, he had prior arrests for carrying a concealed 
weapon, attempted murder, possession of stolen prop- 
erty, sale of cocaine and grand theft. 
McDufty provided the police with Marsh's telephone 
number. This number was traced to plaintiffs’ address, 
where Marsh had been boarding. On May 20, 1993, the 
Honorable Dan Thomas Oki signed a search warrant 
ordering the search of plaintiffs’ house. The warrant 
authorized a search for “any evidence of street gang 
activity” as well as “[a]ny evidence of firearms of the 
following caliber: 9mm.” 
During the search, the police confiscated suspected 
gang clothing, paperwork belonging to Marsh and an 
59 
address book belonging to Mr. Perkins. The police also 
seized numerous pictures depicting gang members, sev- 
eral guns and various types of ammunition. 
Discussion 
City of West Covina 
As a general rule, a municipality is not liable under 
42 U.S.C. § 1983 on the basis of respondeat superior. 
Instead, the municipality will only be held liable if plain- 
tiff shows that the violation resulted from an unconstitu- 
tional policy, practice or custom. See Monell v. Department 
of Social Servs., 98 S.Ct. 2018 (1978); accord St. Louts v. 
Praprotnik, 108 S.Ct. 915, 926 (1988). In order to prevail, 
plaintiff need not produce evidence of a written policy. 
Instead, plaintiff may produce evidence of a long and 
frequent pattern of constitutional deprivations. In the 
alternative, plaintiff may prove that the unconstitutional 
action was taken by an individual with policy making 
authority. Monell, 98 S. Ct. at 2036. Furthermore, a custom 
or practice may be inferred where governmental officials 
act in deliberate indifference in refusing to sanction an 
employee for engaging in unconstitutional conduct. 
McRorie v. Shimoda, 795 F.2d 780 (9th Cir. 1986) (where 
plaintiff alleged that he was deprived of his liberty rights 
by prison guards, the appellate court overruled the dis- 
missal of prison wardens as defendants, finding that 
plaintiff should have the opportunity to amend his com- 
plaint to state that wardens were aware of and were 
deliberately indifferent to the violations); accord Thompson 
v. City of Los Angeles, 885 F.2d 1439 (9th Cir. 1989). 

60 
In the present case, plaintiffs have offered no evi- 
dence that unlawful seizures are condoned by the City of 
West Covina. Nor have plaintiffs produced evidence of a 
pattern of unlawful seizures. Finally, plaintiffs have failed 
to offer evidence that City policy-making officials were 
aware of, but were deliberately indifferent to, the seizure 
of plaintiffs’ cash and starter pistol. Accordingly, the 
Court must GRANT the City’s motion for summary adju- 
dication on plaintiffs’ claim for seizure of the cash and 
th; starter pistol. 
Individual Defendants 
The Court finds that the individual defendants are, as 
a matter of law, entitled to qualified immunity on plain- 
tiffs’ claim for seizure of the $2,469.00 and the starter 
pistol. Accepting the facts in the light most favorable to 
plaintiff, the Court finds that a reasonable police officer 
could have believed that, under clearly established prin- 
ciples of law, he was entitled to seize plaintiffs’ property. 
Act Up/Portland v. Bagley, 988 F.2d 868 (9th Cir. 1993). 
A police officer may seize property under the plain 
view doctrine if the officer is in a lawful position to view 
the item, the discovery of the item is inadvertent, and the 
item reasonably appears to be evidence of a crime. United 
States v. Washington, 797 F.2d 1461 (9th Cir. 1986). 
In the present case, there is no question that the 
officers were in a lawful position when they viewed 
plaintiffs’ cash and pistol. The officers had a valid war- 
rant to search the entire house for guns and gang para- 
phernalia. Both the cash and the starte[r] pistol were 
seized in places where the guns and gang paraphernalia 
61 
could have been found. "urthermore, the discovery of the 
cash and starter pistol was, accordingly to the undisputed 
evidence, inadvertent. Accordingly, the only issue is 
whether a reasonable police officer could have believed 
that there was probable cause to seize the cash and starter 
pistol. 
A reasonable officer could undoubtedly have 
believed that the starter pistol was evidence of a crime. 
Defendants have submitted affidavits stating that starter 
pistols are often used by gang members to frighten, 
harass and inflict bodily harm. Furthermore, the police 
had reason to believe that gang members resided in 
plaintiffs’ house - not only were they informed that 
Marcus Marsh resided there, but they also found pictures 
depicting individuals in traditional gang attire making 
gang “signs” with their hands. In short, a reasonable 
officer, based upon the suspicion that gang members 
resided in plaintiffs’ house and their knowledge regard- 
ing the potential use of the starter pistol, could have 
believed that the pistols were evidence of illegal gang 
activity. 
A reasonable officer could also have believed that the 
cash was evidence of a crime, specifically drug dealing. A 
number of Circuit Courts have upheld the seizure of cash 
as evidence of drug crimes. See e.g., U.S. v. Garcia, 983 
F.2d 1160 (1st Cir. 1993); U.S. v. Vargas, 945 F.2d 426 (1st 
Cir. 1991); U.S. v. Cooper, 942 F.2d 1200 (7th Cir. 1991); 
U.S. v. Gordils, 982 F.2d 64 (2nd Cir. 1992). 
It is true that the cited cases involved greater sums of 
money than the sum involved in the present case, and 
that, in all of the cited cases, the officers performing the 

62 
search discovered additional incriminating evidence such 
as drugs along with the cash. However, considering the 
facts known by the officers in the present case, it would 
be unreasonable to expect an officer acting in the line of 
duty to make the subtle distinction between the facts of 
Garcia, Vargas and Gordils and the present case. First, not 
only did the officers reasonably believe that Marsh resi- 
ded in plaintiffs’ house, but they also had reason 
to 
believe that Marsh was involved in illegal drug activity. 
Marsh had at least one prior arrest for drug dealing. 
Furthermore, Marsh was the chief suspect in a murder 
which took place at the residence of Lashaun McDuffy. In 
searching McDuffy’s residence, the police found cocaine 
as well as approximately $1,000 in cash. In view of these 
facts, an officer could have concluded ‘nat the cash was 
the proceeds of Marsh’s drug dealing. Second, in search- 
ing plaintiffs’ house the police uncovered a number of 
suspicious items such as guns, ammunition, gang photos 
and false identification cards. These items would corrobo- 
rate the officer's belief that the cash was evidence of a 
crime. Third, plaintiffs’ home is an inexpensive home in a 
working class neighborhood, $2,500 is a suspiciously 
large sum of money to find stashed away in such a house. 
Plaintiffs argue that a reasonable police officer could 
not have believed that there was probable cause to seize 
the cash because was seized from the Southwest bedroom 
and, based on the paperwork seized from that bedroom, a 
reasonable officer would have known that the bedroom 
was used exclusively by the plaintiffs and not by Marsh. 
Thus, because there was no reason to connect the South- 
west bedroom with Marsh, a reasonable officer could not 
have believed that the cash was evidence of a drug crime. 
63 
The Court must reject this argument for several reasons. 
First, the house, as depicted in photographs offered by 
both plaintiffs and defendants, is a small house. It does 
not consist of discernible “apartments.” A reasonable 
officer would not necessarily conclude that an individual 
residing in the house did not have access to one particu- 
lar bedroom. Secondly, the paperwork seized in the 
Southwest bedroom is inconclusive in identifying the res- 
ident o. the bedroom. Although the address book was 
inscribed with the name of Lawrence Perkins, a social 
security card with the name of an entirely different indi- 
vidual was discovered. Because of this disparity, a rea- 
sonable officer may not, based upon the paperwork 
a ae, have known that the plaintiffs had exclusive access 
to the room. Finally, several suspicious items, such as 
guns and ammunition, were seized in the Southwest bed- 
room along with the cash. 
In short the Court must find, as a matter of law, that 
a reasonable police officer could have believed that there 
was probable cause to seize the cash and the starter 
pistol. Accordingly, the individual defendants are entitled 
to summary judgment on the grounds of qualified immu- 
nity. 
Conclusion 
The Court grants defendants’ motion for summary 
judgment on plaintiffs’ claim for unlawful seizure of the 
cash and the starter pistol.
