## City of Chicago v. Morales — No. 97-1121 — June 10, 1999

**Threshold and components.** All eight reach the preserved federal vagueness question and accept Illinois's authoritative rejection of the proposed narrowing construction. Enforcement guidance, notice, facial scope and substantive liberty are distinct propositions within one judgment. No Justice supplies a new intimidation element as though Illinois had adopted it. No status-offense, overbreadth or Fourth Amendment judgment is needed for the commitments stated here.

**Entered law doing work.** Grayned supplies notice and enforcement guidance; Papachristou addresses discretionary criminalization of ordinary public activity; Coates rejects an offense turning on variable personal reactions. O'Connor's Kolender opinion, joined by Stevens, establishes an independent minimum-guidance requirement even where some circumstances give actual notice. Hoffman Estates preserves contextual facial-review limits. All eight Associates directly adopted PRO, October 15, 1998, Parts II–VIII and XI: difficult factual proof is distinct from an absent legal classification, fault cannot create a missing criterion, apparent clear examples do not necessarily cure a central classification defect, and remedy must follow the actual defect. PRO does not establish a universal criminal facial-review rule or a fundamental right to all loitering.

**Operative propositions available for joins.**

- M1: An ordinance authorizing mandatory dispersal and punishment of persons remaining together with no apparent purpose must supply a meaningful legal criterion constraining that initial dispersal decision. The reasonable belief that one person is a gang member, geographic and officer restrictions, and a later refusal to obey do not supply the missing criterion concerning the group's otherwise innocent presence. On Illinois's actual construction, this ordinance fails that requirement.
- M2: An order to disperse gives notice that the officer demands departure, but does not retroactively make the standard for demanding departure intelligible. Nor does the text adequately settle the scope and duration of the required removal from the area. This separate notice ground supports invalidity for the Justices identified below.
- M3: The defect concerns the common legal trigger authorizing each dispersal order, not simply uncertainty at the margins of an intelligible prohibition. Facial relief therefore does not require a finding that every conceivable gang gathering is constitutionally immune from regulation. A clearer obstruction, threat or intimidation prohibition presents a different question.
- M4: Ordinary innocent presence in a public place implicates protected liberty against arbitrary governmental displacement; that contextual proposition does not establish an unlimited fundamental right to remain at every place or invalidate reasonable, definite public-order laws. Only the expressly identified Justices support making that liberty explanation part of the opinion.

## Lilly v. Virginia — No. 98-5881 — June 10, 1999

**Threshold and distinct components.** All eight reach the preserved Confrontation Clause challenge to Mark Lilly's custodial accusations admitted substantively at Benjamin's separate trial. This is not Bruton redaction or limiting-instruction adjudication. Firm-rootedness, particularized trustworthiness, scope of constitutional reconstruction and harmlessness are separate. Mark's privilege establishes the supplied unavailability; it does not establish reliability. No express immunity bargain or promise of leniency is found.

**Law entering.** The register Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, present operation effective Hale, February 12, 1996, preserves White's January 15, 1992 exception-sensitive framework and its rejection of a categorical formal-affidavit-only limit. Assertion-specific declarations against penal interest, effective Williamson, June 27, 1994, concerns federal Rule 804(b)(3), not Virginia evidence or a previously decided constitutional firm-rootedness question. Lee v. Illinois supplies the danger of accomplice blame shifting. Idaho v. Wright requires constitutional guarantees from the circumstances surrounding the statement, not independent trial corroboration; its majority was O'Connor's, joined by Stevens and Scalia among present Justices. Kennedy's Wright dissent supports a broader contextual reliability inquiry but is not governing law. Gray, March 9, 1998, separates error from a preservation-qualified Chapman remand.

**Operative propositions and material distinctions.**

- L1: A State's acceptance of an exception for declarations against penal interest does not establish federal constitutional firm-rootedness for the prosecution's use of an unavailable accomplice's custodial, blame-shifting accusations against an accused. The genuinely disserving admissions and the accusation of another cannot simply be treated as one automatically reliable narrative. This rule does not exclude every genuinely self-inculpatory statement or every accusation made outside custody.
- L2: Under Roberts and Wright, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees arising from the circumstances of the actual statement. Independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees. Custodial questioning, pressure concerning charges, intoxication and a narrative minimizing the speaker's violence while assigning it to another do not establish that cross-examination would add little. The offered accusations here fail the constitutional reliability requirement; the statement's inclusion of some genuine self-inculpation does not cure them.
- L3: Vacate the judgment and remand. Where constitutional trial error is found, only a properly available harmlessness contention may be considered under Chapman, with the Commonwealth required to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict. No harmlessness finding, acquittal or unconditional new-trial order follows here.
- L4: A narrower remand may reject the State's categorical firm-rootedness premise while leaving application of corrected trustworthiness standards to the state court in the first instance. Under this path, no present conclusion of constitutional harmlessness or automatic exclusion of every assertion is made; a constitutional error found on remand is governed by L3's Chapman condition.

## Dickinson v. Zurko — No. 98-377 — June 10, 1999

**Threshold and components.** All eight reach whether §559 preserves the Federal Circuit's clear-error review of these PTO factual findings. The lower court expressly found the outcome dependent on that question; no mootness or harmless-standard shortcut is supplied. APA applicability, identification of the applicable §706 provision and patentability are separate. This Court may answer the first while leaving the latter two for proper proceedings.

**Law entering.** Section 559 preserves additional requirements imposed by statute or otherwise recognized by law; later enactments supersede the listed APA provisions only to the extent they do so expressly. Section 706(2)(E)'s substantial-evidence rule applies to §§556–557 cases or other review on the record of a statutorily provided agency hearing; (A)'s arbitrary-or-capricious rule and (F)'s authorized trial-de-novo provision are separate. The whole-record and prejudicial-error instruction applies on its terms. Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard, effective Allentown Mack, January 26, 1998, preserves meaningful scrutiny and agency choice between fairly conflicting views. APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures, effective Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994, requires establishing the actual departure invoked, while preserving its explicit reservations. Haggar, April 21, 1999, distinguishes statutory legal interpretation from factual review; it does not decide direct PTO factual review.

**Competing rules.** Z1 holds that the APA governs this direct agency appeal because the pre-APA sources do not establish a sufficiently identifiable, more searching additional direct-review requirement preserved by §559. Z2 holds that §559 preserves the established judicial practice of independent examination for clear or manifest factual mistake despite variations in its label, supporting the Federal Circuit's clear-error rule. Neither rule may rest only on calling the PTO an agency or on declaring patent cases special.

## American Airlines, Inc. v. Tice — No. 98-1704 — June 10, 1999

**Common sufficient rule.** A judgment does not extinguish a nonparty pilot's individual ADEA claim merely because earlier plaintiffs challenged the same employer policy, had broadly similar interests, and employed counsel later retained by the pilot. Binding requires consent, control, genuine privity, adequate authorized representation or another recognized arrangement consistent with due process. Both claim and issue preclusion need that relationship. **Current authority.** Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants, effective Richards, June 10, 1996, including the retained Matsushita representation component, supplies the prerequisite. All eight joined Richards Parts II–IV. ## Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States — No. 98-387 — June 14, 1999

**Threshold, speech and statutes.** All eight reach the as-applied First Amendment challenge by Louisiana broadcasters to truthful advertising of lawful private casino gambling. The possibility that signals reach Texas or Arkansas does not convert the advertised lawful Louisiana/Mississippi transactions into unlawful activity. The supported public interests in reducing gambling harms and respecting neighboring state policy are substantial for the ordinary Central Hudson inquiry. Direct gambling regulation and advertising of illegal or misleading transactions remain separate.

Section 1304 prohibits the described licensed radio/television broadcasts and knowing permission of them, with each broadcasting day a separate offense. Section 1307(a)(1) exempts information about a State-conducted lawful lottery broadcast from a station licensed in that State or another lottery State. Section 1307(a)(2) requires that the scheme be authorized or not prohibited where conducted and be conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or be clearly occasional commercial promotion ancillary to the organization's primary business. Section 2720 separately exempts gaming conducted by a tribe pursuant to, and consistently with, the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. Section 1307(b)'s transport/mail exceptions and its special lottery definition do not independently exempt §1304 broadcasts. These are conditioned exceptions, not a claim that every other gambling advertisement is legal.

**Law entering.** Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions, present operation effective Lanphere & Urbaniak, April 18, 1995, retains Central Hudson and Fox. Edenfield requires real harm and material alleviation without invariably demanding studies. Rubin, April 19, 1995, supplies independent advancement and fit failures for an unexplained contradictory scheme and requires a supported state-accommodation mechanism. Edge, June 25, 1993, sustained a particular licensing-state restriction with meaningful reduction and a reasonable accommodation; it did not create a vice-speech exception. 44 Liquormart, May 13, 1996, retains ordinary Central Hudson/Fox review; its separate alcohol-Amendment rule creates no general gambling exemption or heightened review. Alternatives matter to fit without a least-restrictive-means requirement.

**Propositions.** G1: Applied to truthful promotions of lawful private casino gambling by these stations, the actual scheme does not directly and materially advance the asserted reduction/accommodation interests. The permitted promotion of materially comparable gambling and the rules' mismatch with the asserted interstate mechanism undermine the justification for suppressing these particular lawful advertisements. G2: Independently, the restriction lacks reasonable proportional fit: it broadly suppresses truthful gaming information while allowing the described competing promotion, and the supported ability to address gambling behavior directly exposes an inadequately justified informational burden. Neither conclusion rests on an automatic rule invalidating every exception, an empirical-study mandate, or the availability of any conceivable less restrictive policy.
