# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Elections and Voting

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Expenditure ceiling regulates candidate political speech directly; anticorruption justification that supports contribution limits does not by itself authorize a cap on candidate expression.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The cap suppresses the quantity of campaign expression, including legitimate advocacy, rather than regulating the source or coordination of contributions. Time-saving and public confidence may be policy benefits, but on this record cannot replace the constitutional rule already governing expenditures. The record supplies no legally supported basis to discard that rule for municipal elections.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A mandatory cap on overall candidate expenditures in municipal campaigns violates the First Amendment under the received contribution/expenditure distinction; legitimate civic interests may be pursued through otherwise lawful tools.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The cap suppresses the quantity of campaign expression, including legitimate advocacy, rather than regulating the source or coordination of contributions. Time-saving and public confidence may be policy benefits, but on this record cannot replace the constitutional rule already governing expenditures. The record supplies no legally supported basis to discard that rule for municipal elections.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No conclusion that contribution ceilings, disclosure, genuine voluntary spending agreements or lawfully structured public financing are invalid.
