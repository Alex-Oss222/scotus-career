# Reno — assembly preparation before Stone exposure

This is a nonauthoritative preparation note. It creates no Court action, final vote, opinion assignment or public handoff. Stone's supplement remains unopened. Final assembly requires the intervening-law receipt and authorization to read the Reno-only approved supplement.

## Form and supported structure before assignment

The supported disposition warrants one signed opinion after plenary review: it resolves the relation between an express immigration jurisdictional restriction and an actual barrier to adjudicating a substantial constitutional claim, then distinguishes jurisdiction from two existing injunctions. Routine summary disposition would conceal the disputed construction. The statutory provisions, record limitations, retained-access ground and remedies are parts of one case; no division into separate opinions is required to manufacture authorship eligibility.

The reconciled five-Associate ground A supplies a full rationale and remedy: Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas share the transition, named-action, subsection (f), transfer and nonmerits limits but reject the retained-selection-jurisdiction construction and provisional preservation. Their separate ground B would instead vacate the jurisdictional judgment and both injunctions, preserving the already raised constitutional challenge to exclusion itself. The two grounds are incompatible, not logical subsets. No Marks aggregation is permissible.

Potential lead structure, subject to Stone's actual approved joins: Part I, named executive actions, transitions and subsection (f); Part II, former section 106 and section 2347 factual-review barrier; Part III, confined review-preserving construction with substantiality, timing and merits limits; Part IV, separate injunction and individual-controversy remand. Assignment is not yet made.

## Required propositions and qualifications

1. Section 1252(g) commenced April 1, 1997 and then reached old, pending and future proceedings. The attack on selection for commencement and requested restraint of adjudication implicates named actions; continuing proceedings is not a fourth statutory act. Yang and Reyes-Hernandez retain their actual force. The latter's completed-adjudication boundary does not decide the additional constitutional-access issue or create a general exemption.
2. The old procedure remains subject to actual amendments and transitions. Section 309(c)(2) requires an evidentiary hearing not yet begun and thirty days' notice; (c)(3) requires no final administrative decision; (c)(4) turns on a qualifying final order more than thirty days after September 30, 1996. No election or final order is established here. Section 306(c)(2) protects actual former-section-106 judgments from paragraph (1) invalidation or compelled reconsideration, but grants no jurisdiction or immunity from ordinary correctness review and does not automatically cover collateral injunctions. The permanent scheme, including section 1252(b)(9), does not apply wholesale.
3. Section 1252(f)(1)'s individual-application exception qualifies a prohibition on relief; it grants no subject-matter jurisdiction. Subsection (f)(2)'s clear-and-convincing condition concerns enjoining removal under a final order, not this posture.
4. Former section 1105a(a)(4) restricts final-order review to the administrative record, subject to the identified nationality-transfer exception in (5)(B). Section 2347(a) uses the agency record when a hearing was held. Subsection (b) first requires that no hearing preceded the action reviewed, then requires agency remand if a hearing was legally required, decision on pleadings and affidavits if none was required and no genuine material factual dispute exists, and district-court transfer if none was required and such a dispute exists. Subsection (c) separately permits additional agency evidence upon materiality and reasonable grounds for omission; applicable prohibitions of (c) require their own temporal trigger. No exclusion of (c) proves availability of (b)(3).
5. The Government cannot use a final deportation order as the jurisdictional trigger while treating the earlier charging decision as the reviewed action to evade both the required hearing and record restriction. The agency cannot decide this selection claim; the developed district-court record is not automatically importable. The proposed transfer is not a lawful established route for the material extra-record proof. This leaves constitutional legal arguments cognizable on a proper administrative record and future constitutional remedies unresolved. Rule 48, advocacy and unspecified procedures cannot confer authority.
6. Ground A preserves existing section 1331 jurisdiction only for a substantial constitutional selection claim with a concrete personal controversy where the actual immigration process cannot decide the claim and the applicable judicial-review channel cannot receive its necessary factual record. Webster's clear-intent rule supports that confined construction despite the named-action and notwithstanding language. Lincoln, McNary, Catholic Social Services and Kolster concern actual capable review; Thunder Basin permits channeling where that capability exists. A label or preference for immediate review is insufficient. Section (f) is contextual, not a jurisdictional grant. No constitutional invalidation of subsection (g) or underlying selection-merits holding follows.
7. A successful statutory defense does not necessarily adjudicate retaliation, repair an independently supported interim injury or produce an adverse final order for review. Preliminary findings of selection, disparate treatment and present chill establish substantiality, not final liability or automatic irreparability. Ordinary defense costs, anxiety or undifferentiated chill do not establish a right to stop proceedings. Hollywood Motor Car had an available trial forum; Younger and Dombrowski preserve their good-faith-process/ongoing-suppression distinction without importing state-federal comity wholesale; Oestereich's unequivocal exemption is absent.
8. The Six's status charges differ from the Two's association/material-support charges. Hamide and Shehadeh are permanent residents. Barakat and Sharif obtained temporary resident status and are no longer deportable on their original status grounds only. No replacement charge, specific threat, universal deportability admission or immunity from every other ground is established. Organizations no longer supply an operative standing premise.
9. Whren separates lawful objective grounds from independently prohibited selection; Dawson distinguishes abstract association from relevant evidence; Armstrong's discovery rule does not decide the complete merits of this developed association record; R.A.V.'s controlling ground remains overbreadth. The lower specific-intent treatment and the Government's conduct/immigration objections remain disputed and preliminary. No all-aliens/all-citizens equivalence or immigration immunity is decided.
10. Ground A affirms only the confined jurisdictional result, sets aside the appellate judgment's unqualified preservation of relief for reconsideration, and temporarily preserves the district orders without enlargement pending lower-court action. The Six's denial-of-dissolution posture does not reopen every issue merely because old evidence is resubmitted; new law, status changes, live controversy, actual injury and scope still matter. The Two's later initial injunction needs its own basis. Barakat and Sharif require individual surviving-dispute review; their allegation of new-charge protection and absence of disavowal prove neither an actual charge nor imminent threat. No permanent injunction, fixed deadline, mandatory new hearing or discovery, removal order, new-charge protection or Supreme Court return is ordered.
11. Kolster's conditional section 2241 rule against AEDPA section 440(a) does not resolve section 1252(g), establish custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion, filing, factual-review scope, stay or relief here. No invented habeas substitute answers the current access problem.
