## Framing and preservation corrections

The original appeal develops the official-use equal-protection claim through chief law-enforcement members. The opinion expressly describes broader relief theories as undeveloped and reaches only standing necessary for that claim. This confines the source's established appellate scope; the grant's reference to the personal arms guarantee permits consideration of its role in the challenged official-use classification, not invention of an individual officer's private-possession record or an across-the-board adjudication of every constitutional theory in the original complaint.

Chief members allege concrete operational interference because officers with disabling convictions cannot be armed even when a chief wishes to deploy them. No state-law requirement compelling the chief to do what federal law forbids is established. The chiefs also fall within the supply prohibition if its knowledge or reasonable-cause predicate is met. The association alleges no injury of its own. The government disputes member standing, not germaneness or the need for individual participation. The original opinion uses Hunt's three requirements and addresses third-party equal-protection rights through Craig and Barrows. Those are its reasons; the Court must apply entering standing law independently.

The association concedes that domestic-violence misdemeanants are not a suspect class. It argues that the arms right is fundamental. The United States' collective-right response cannot substitute for the entering personal-right rule. Recognition of a personal right neither resolves the justification for this particular disability nor establishes a right to government employment, governmental issuance of a firearm, or a favored statutory exception. The relation between the official-use classification and a protected arms interest remains a legal issue within the supported scope.

The original opinion distinguishes two propositions: it accepts rational grounds for treating domestic-violence misdemeanants differently from other misdemeanants, but questions withholding an official-use exemption that can remain available to domestic-violence felons. Its discussion of states' existing felony restrictions as a practical gap-filling justification is **the lower court's own conceivable justification**, expressly not an argument the government made in its brief. The government's identified argument is that Congress can address a problem one step at a time. The packet's shorthand about government reliance on a practical enforcement gap therefore denotes an available analytical defense, not a verified attributed filing. Neither source permits fabricated legislative findings or a universal empirical claim about every State's practices.

The opinion rejects its hypothesized gap-filling justification because of perceived state-law variations and restoration anomalies. That is its reasoning, not a binding factual finding that no rational public-safety rationale is possible. Its descriptions of New Hampshire, Vermont, New York and other States are published source recitations, not independently researched findings by this validation context.

## Complete operative statutory qualifications

Official 1997 Code PDFs and extracted texts are saved under `preflight-sources/18usc921-1997.pdf(.txt)`, `18usc922-1997.pdf(.txt)`, `18usc925-1997.pdf(.txt)` and `18usc928-1997.pdf(.txt)`. The relevant provisions, rather than current amended text, govern this packet.

Section 922(d)'s opening clause prohibits selling or otherwise disposing of a firearm **or ammunition** to a person while **knowing or having reasonable cause to believe** that the person meets a listed condition; paragraph (9) identifies conviction in any court of a misdemeanor crime of domestic violence. The concluding subsection exception concerns a licensed importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector allowed to continue under section 925(b), or a person granted relief under section 925(c). The packet's phrase “knowing supply” is shorthand and must not erase reasonable cause, ammunition, or these textual qualifications.

Section 922(g)(9), together with subsection (g)'s concluding language, prohibits the qualifying person from shipping or transporting a firearm or ammunition in interstate or foreign commerce, possessing one in or affecting commerce, or receiving one that has been shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Section 922(d)(9) has no identical enacted interstate element. The distinct provisions must not be merged or supplied with each other's elements.

Section 921(a)(33)(A) defines the qualifying offense as a misdemeanor under Federal or State law that has as an element use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian of the victim; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting with the victim as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. This packet does not decide unresolved questions about how a particular offense or relationship satisfies that definition.

Under section 921(a)(33)(B)(i), a person does not count as convicted unless represented by counsel or knowingly and intelligently waiving counsel. If the prosecution was for an offense for which the person was entitled to a jury trial in the jurisdiction, the case must have been tried by jury or the person must knowingly and intelligently have waived jury trial by guilty plea or otherwise. This provision creates no jury entitlement where the underlying jurisdiction supplies none.

Section 921(a)(33)(B)(ii) provides that a conviction does not count if expunged or set aside, or if the offense is one for which the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored **if the applicable jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights under such an offense**, unless the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly provides that the person may not ship, transport, possess or receive firearms. The exact wording matters: the express-reservation clause lists pardon, expungement and restoration; it does not separately repeat set-aside. No broader construction is supplied here. The official text begins subsection (A) with a reference to subparagraph (C), although this version supplies no such subparagraph; this packet does not repair that printed reference by inventing an exception.

Section 925(a)(1) provides:

> The provisions of this chapter, except for sections 922(d)(9) and 922(g)(9) and provisions relating to firearms subject to the prohibitions of section 922(p), shall not apply with respect to the transportation, shipment, receipt, possession, or importation of any firearm or ammunition imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of, the United States or any department or agency thereof or any State or any department, agency, or political subdivision thereof.

Thus the challenged exemption is defined by the firearm's governmental channel and use; it is not an unrestricted exemption for anything a police officer privately possesses. The separate section 922(p) limitation must not disappear in a statement that every provision except the domestic-violence clauses is exempt. This section's government-use exception is distinct from section 922(o)(2)'s machinegun exceptions.

Section 925(b) permits specified licensees indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year's imprisonment to continue under their existing license, subject to a timely renewal application where required, during indictment and until a resulting conviction becomes final. Section 925(c) authorizes an application for relief, discretionary administrative relief on the specified public-safety/public-interest findings, and judicial review of an actual denial. This statutory text alone establishes neither an available funded administrative process for any particular claimant in 1998 nor a denial, exhaustion, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement to relief. No result in this case may assume one. The section 921(a)(33) conviction exclusions independently prevent describing the category as literally without every route to relief.

Section 928 provides that invalidity of a provision or its application does not affect the remainder or applications to other persons not similarly situated or other circumstances. The original court used this provision to extend the official-use exemption. The existence of severability text does not itself dictate whether extension, removal, or a narrower remedy follows a particular constitutional defect.

## Neutral competing paths and remedy constraints

- The official-use classification can be defended as a targeted response to an inadequately covered domestic-violence risk; a legislature may address a practical gap without rewriting every old exemption. The strongest objection is the apparent preference for more serious domestic-violence offenders, including variations in existing state barriers. Both positions must address the actual statute and permissible rationality principles, not invented findings or a premise that personal arms rights are unavailable.
- The classification can be challenged as irrationally harsher treatment of the less serious category. The strongest response is that rationality review permits imperfect incremental regulation and asks for a conceivable legitimate connection, not perfect uniformity across overlapping state and federal schemes. The availability and significance of other conviction exclusions must be described accurately.
- If a properly reached arms ground requires a distinct analysis, its scope must remain the official-use claim and supported representative posture. No individual conviction history, weapon characteristics, rehabilitation showing, or private-possession injury is supplied by the abstract classification.
- A defect in the exclusion does not automatically authorize disabling every possession or supply prohibition. The original appellate remedy extends section 925(a)(1)'s official-use exception only; it does not repeal the domestic-violence disability for private possession. Any different remedy must account for Congress's statutory direction and the limits of the plaintiffs' injuries.
- The separate interpretation of restoration for people who never lost civil rights was expressly left unresolved below. The original opinion discusses competing circuit approaches without deciding one. No restoration judgment is supplied by this record. If adjudication of that statutory issue becomes necessary, entering law including the relevant Caron holding must be selected before modeling it.
- Commerce and Tenth Amendment labels in the original complaint are not fully developed appellate claims on this source. No hypothetical commandeering direction, statewide firearm restoration obligation, or universal remote-movement theory is added. Any proposed adjudication beyond the supported official-use claim requires an identified reachability basis and proper entering law first.
