## Verification status and immediate corrections

This is an interim receipt, not a certification that every opinion has received full review. Complete text review finished for Cunningham (Court and Kennedy), Zurko (Court including appendix and Rehnquist dissent), Lilly (lead opinion, footnotes, and all four separate writings), and Greater New Orleans (Court, footnotes, and both concurrences). Morales lead opinion and all three concurrences were fully reviewed; its two full dissents remain saved but await complete reading. O'Sullivan and Amoco syllabuses were consulted and all writings downloaded; complete reading of their opinions remains pending. No source is claimed unreadably missing.

1. **Zurko comparator is overbroad.** The historical Court held that APA section 706 supplies the framework, but expressly reserved which APA standard—substantial evidence or arbitrary/capricious/abuse of discretion—applies to PTO factfinding. The comparator's phrase “including substantial-evidence review for formal adjudicatory records” must not be treated as a holding selecting section 706(2)(E) for this case. Exact reservation is in Part II, 527 U.S. 158. The source paragraph is preserved in `historical-sources/98-377.ZO.compact.txt` beginning “Nor is the absence of the words”.
2. **Cunningham actual posture:** the District Court disqualified Cunningham because she was a material witness. The opinion does not establish that she voluntarily withdrew as counsel. Its reference to refusal to withdraw concerns her improper deposition notice. Both Cornell and official report at 527 U.S. 201 agree. The $1,494 sanction payable to the Hamilton County treasurer remained at issue; separate $2,432 sanctions owed other defendants were satisfied in settlement and were outside the appeal. The Magistrate expressly held no contempt hearing and made no contempt finding. The Court declined certiorari on petitioner's request for prospective-only application of the Sixth Circuit's appealability rule (note 3).
3. **Morales comparator needs coalition precision.** Six Justices joined Parts I, II, and V (minimal enforcement standards), not the separate fair-notice reasoning in Part IV. Parts III, IV, and VI had Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg only. O'Connor/Breyer relied on unbounded discretion; Kennedy additionally expressed notice concerns but did not join Part IV. A description presenting both notice and enforcement grounds as the same six-Justice holding would be inaccurate.
4. **Lilly comparator needs scope and rationale precision.** The judgment was unanimous, but Stevens's Parts III–V (including the broad exception analysis and independent appellate trustworthiness review) had four votes. Six joined Parts I and VI, five Part II. Rehnquist/O'Connor/Kennedy would remand to permit the state courts to consider particularized trustworthiness, whereas six joined the determination of a Confrontation Clause violation and harmless-error remand. The plurality expressly rejected characterization of its position as an absolute ban: failure of the firmly rooted exception route still leaves the Roberts particularized-guarantees route.
5. **Greater New Orleans scope:** the historical holding is as applied to these Louisiana broadcasters' advertisements for lawful private casino gambling, with Louisiana permitting that activity. It does not invalidate every possible application of section 1304 or replace Central Hudson. The evidentiary adequacy question about the causal connection between advertising and social costs was left unresolved because the statutory scheme's exemptions/inconsistencies were independently fatal. A renewed evidentiary remand was rejected in note 6. The main opinion accepted the asserted interests as substantial, with qualifications, then found the third/fourth requirements unsatisfied.

## Reviewed separate writings and material qualifications

**Cunningham.** Kennedy distinguished a mere sanctions award from a contempt adjudication. If denial of a stay of collection produces exceptional hardship likely itself to cause injustice, mandamus might permit review of the issuance of sanctions or denial of a stay. A contempt order against a person without congruent interests with a litigant may be appealable. Neither possibility converts this Rule 37(a)(4) award into a collateral order. The Court's class-based analysis rejects a case-by-case exception for unusually separate discovery issues and rejects an exception based on counsel's cessation of representation. It recognizes legislative/rulemaking solutions and trial-court timing of imposition or collection. It does not adjudicate merits of the sanction or review an actual contempt order.

**Zurko.** The Court found no clearly established pre-APA stricter review requirement saved by section 559; historical phrases such as manifest error did not necessarily express the later Rule 52(a) standard. Agency expertise and agreement within the agency supported deference in those precedents. APA review remains meaningful and includes reasoned decisionmaking; it is not rubber-stamping. Section 145 district-court proceedings can admit new evidence and make the court a factfinder, explaining different appellate review; any anomaly where the district court merely reviews PTO findings was left open to adjustment of related standards. Rehnquist/Kennedy/Ginsburg read section 559 as preserving stricter additional requirements, deferred to the unanimous en banc patent court's historical assessment, and rejected a nontextual clear-statement rule for antecedent common law.

**Lilly.** The plurality distinguished admissions used against their maker, exculpatory declarations offered by the defense, and prosecution use of an accomplice confession against an accused. The third category lacked a firmly rooted exception; the trustworthiness route remained theoretically possible, though especially unlikely for police-produced, retrospective, untested blame-shifting confessions. It separated de novo constitutional evaluation from clear-error review of historical facts. Corroborating trial evidence, Miranda warnings, partial self-inculpation, and the absence of an express leniency promise did not establish the necessary inherent trustworthiness here. The plurality assumed unavailability as framed in the certiorari petition and did not decide the suggestion that prosecution sequencing could have extinguished Mark's privilege. Breyer favored reconsideration of the hearsay/Confrontation linkage in a future case but joined the present analysis. Scalia called custodial interrogation introduced without cross-examination a paradigmatic violation and joined I/II/VI. Thomas maintained his formalized-testimonial-material understanding, joined I/VI, rejected a blanket prohibition, and agreed the state courts had not decided the second Roberts route. Rehnquist/O'Connor/Kennedy treated the blame-shifting portions as plainly outside genuine self-inculpation, left equally inculpatory and noncustodial statements open, preferred deference in trustworthiness review, and wanted both that inquiry and harmlessness considered below.

**Morales reviewed opinions.** Part V invalidated the ordinance's unguided apparent-purpose criterion under the state supreme court's binding construction, with inadequate limits from officer gang beliefs, dispersal before arrest, or unpublished internal enforcement areas. The ordinance extends to nongang members and has an affirmative defense that none of the observed persons was actually a gang member. The penalty text allows a $100–$500 fine, up to six months' imprisonment, or both; up to 120 hours' community service may be imposed in addition to or instead of those penalties. O'Connor/Breyer limited the holding to discretion and described potentially narrower laws or constructions, while rejecting subjective legislative intent as dispositive of vagueness. Kennedy shared notice concerns without joining the notice holding. Breyer explained why standardless discretion infected every application, distinguishing notice-only challenges and First Amendment overbreadth. The plurality did not decide substantive due process, nor did it invalidate the ordinance under First Amendment overbreadth. No summary of the two unread dissents is certified here.

**Greater New Orleans.** Rehnquist emphasized that incremental and inconsistent substantive economic regulation can be permissible even when equivalent commercial-speech restrictions fail Central Hudson. Thomas would reject as illegitimate the governmental purpose of keeping lawful consumers ignorant to influence their marketplace choices; he did not join the Central Hudson analysis. The Court distinguishes the lottery/nonlottery-state accommodation in Edge and discusses the criticism of Posadas in 44 Liquormart without treating section 1304's every application as adjudicated.
