## Identity, scope and verified lower record

Marcus v. Iowa Public Television, Supreme Court No. 98-710; Eighth Circuit No. 96-3645. The scheduled event is January 13, 1999, under the supplied scheduling instruction. No Supreme Court argument date is established. The supplied review question is whether a state broadcaster may exclude ballot-qualified minor-party congressional candidates from debates on newsworthiness grounds. The original petition, opposition and reply have now been read in full under the narrow control authorization below. Their questions and arguments are identified separately from the supplied grant scope; an exact simulated grant instrument was not reviewed.

The judgment under review is the Eighth Circuit's en banc July 30, 1998 affirmance, 150 F.3d 924. Its brief opinion states that the district court did not clearly err in finding no viewpoint exclusion and concludes that the exclusion was otherwise reasonable. It applies the historical Forbes decision; the authoritative law here is this Court's completed May 18, 1998 Forbes decision, supplied in the entering slice. The historical source's reference does not import historical Supreme Court authorship, joins or any different rationale into this Court's law.

The October 11, 1996 interim opinion, 97 F.3d 1137, supplies detailed trial-history and record recitations. Iowa Public Television is a state actor. Its weekly Iowa Press program had interviewed public figures for more than twenty years. In fall 1996 it planned five joint appearances by Democratic and Republican candidates for Iowa's five congressional seats. A host and political reporters asked questions. The district court found that a reasonable viewer would understand the appearances as debates, even though IPTV had not labeled them that way. It also found them bona fide news interview programs. Neither description independently decides their constitutional classification under entering Forbes.

The party list matters. The lower action includes Natural Law Party congressional candidates Marcus (Third District), Cuddehe (First), Dimick (Fifth), Badgett (Fourth), and Lamoureux (Second), and Working Class Party candidate Rusk (Third); it also names a Senate candidate, Gratzon, voter Susan Marcus, Marcus's campaign committee and the Natural Law Party committee. The interim emergency motion directly affected only Cuddehe and Badgett because their two broadcasts remained scheduled for October 13 and 20. The supplied neutral brief's reference to two excluded candidates should not narrow the final merits case to that interim subset. No separate claim or entitlement of the Senate candidate, voter or committees is supplied by their presence in the caption.

The candidates repeatedly requested access. IPTV declined because it did not consider their appearances newsworthy but offered appearances on other programs. The candidates filed suit on September 13, 1996, seeking inclusion of all legally qualified candidates and other injunctive and declaratory relief. After preliminary relief was denied, the district court held a trial with an advisory jury. It independently adopted the jury's findings and added its own. The record is not just an untested complaint, but the advisory verdict is not an independent binding jury resolution of all constitutional issues.

The district court found selection based on independent editorial and journalistic judgments, not political affiliation or views. It found the plaintiffs had not proved their appearances newsworthy, relying in part on weak efforts to raise money and generate public support. The policy sought objectivity, fairness and a better-informed citizenry through important and significant issues. Related editorial-integrity guidelines required decisions free from political or financial pressure, based on fairness, objectivity, balance and community needs rather than funding considerations.

The director's deposition described factors informing professional judgment: activity across the relevant constituency; volunteers and organization beyond staff; previous electoral performance and growth; fundraising breadth; recognition by other media or debates; crowds and public or campaign contacts; advertising and efforts to seek voter support. He also mentioned whether a candidate sought election or primarily sought to introduce ideas. The testimony is evidence of the editorial process; it is not a statutory checklist, a numerical admission threshold, or a finding that every factor was proved absent for each plaintiff. The idea-introduction distinction requires careful viewpoint analysis rather than uncritical conversion into a permissible categorical rule. The district court's no-viewpoint finding remains the actual finding subject to ordinary review, and cannot be displaced simply by describing that testimony.

The original neutral draft's assertion about absent comparable polling is not specifically established by the lower opinions. They support testimony concerning prior electoral performance and findings about fundraising and support efforts, without particular poll results or a numerical admission threshold. The later-read petition supplies specific media and fundraising comparisons as litigant assertions, described in the supplemental source section below; those assertions must neither be omitted nor silently adopted as findings. No universal rule that minor parties lack news value follows.

At the interim stage the panel assumed a limited-public-forum theory for purposes of the emergency motion, applied the circuit's temporary-relief framework, and denied the motion. Judge Beam dissented, reasoning that governmental newsworthiness discretion was not meaningfully different from the viability discretion condemned in the then-operative Eighth Circuit Forbes opinion. That interim classification, remedy and prediction did not become the July 1998 judgment's reasoning, nor does the old circuit Forbes opinion survive this Court's completed Forbes decision as controlling law. The requested emergency prohibition on two broadcasts and IPTV's conditional cancellation statement are 1996 procedural facts, not current relief or an established future cancellation policy.

Forbes holds that a public broadcaster's debate is a nonpublic forum when it actually retains editorial selection rather than opening access generally to qualified candidates. Restrictions must reasonably serve the journalistic purpose and be viewpoint neutral. Public ownership or a journalism label supplies no immunity. Supported assessments of public support and journalistic significance can justify exclusion; ballot qualification alone does not require access. No universal numerical formula, written policy or advance-publication prerequisite applies. Sham criteria, viewpoint pretext, incumbent favoritism and inconsistent denial under genuinely open access terms remain outside the holding.

The exact current reusable provision is **Editorial selection in public-broadcaster candidate debates**, present operation effective Forbes, May 18, 1998. Cornelius and Perry Education supply selective-access analysis; Rosenberger preserves viewpoint equality within the actual opportunity; Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household preserve actual comparators and genuine boundaries; Denver Area supplies no universal broadcaster immunity or complete forum classification.

The temporal controversy question is separate. Norman v. Reed permits a completed-election dispute to continue where actual continuing party consequences and a reasonably recurring short electoral controversy are established. Chandler v. Miller rests on the candidate's stated, uncontested intention to seek office again against a continuing requirement. Spencer v. Kemna requires a concrete continuing controversy; the capable-of-repetition exception requires both a duration too short for review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant again faces the challenged action. A short election cycle by itself does not establish the second element. These bounded holdings are copied into the slice. They authorize neither an invented new candidacy nor an assumption of future broadcasts, exclusions or changes in policy.

## Supported decisional paths

Subject to Article III, one path applies Forbes to the actual selective Iowa Press format, the supported journalistic purpose and the independently adopted no-affiliation/no-viewpoint findings. The district court's general label of limited public forum can be corrected without inventing a different broadcast. Fundraising and public-support efforts may bear on actual news significance without becoming compulsory thresholds. The strongest contrary argument is that broad newsworthiness discretion, including the suggestion that idea-promoting candidates are less suitable, may leave governmental discretion insufficiently constrained or conceal viewpoint selection. The reviewing court must address that argument against the actual findings and governing Forbes rule, not simply assert that journalism is exempt from the First Amendment.

A competing path examines whether the Iowa standards and their application differ materially from Forbes: a wholly general newsworthiness label or a criterion sorting candidates by the political purpose of their campaigns may permit a more specific legal objection. Its strongest counterargument is that Forbes explicitly rejects a universal requirement of more detailed written or numerical criteria, and this record contains policies, testimony and independent findings rather than only the label. A different judgment must identify the actual distinction or a lawful reason to revisit the relevant rule. It cannot rest merely on disagreement with the name newsworthiness, on ballot qualification alone, or on an assumed discriminatory fact contradicting the unchallenged findings.

Another path follows the published Forbes dissent's distinct objection to insufficiently definite, reviewable standards without asserting that political hostility has been found. That position permits reasonable selection and does not require automatic admission of every qualified candidate. Whether the detailed Iowa policies and testimony answer that objection is a Justice-specific issue; the earlier dissent's result is not a mandatory repeated vote.

A threshold route concerns current effective relief or a supported recurrence exception. If the actual record supplies a persistent policy affecting these plaintiffs or concrete renewed candidacy, the relevant source and precise scope of declaratory or prospective relief must be stated. **Specific disputed record arguments.** The petition, pages 13?17, asserts that producer Newell did no meaningful individual inquiry, declined Marcus's requests to identify inclusion criteria, did not know how much the candidates had raised when he excluded them, and first sought organizational and fundraising information after suit began.
