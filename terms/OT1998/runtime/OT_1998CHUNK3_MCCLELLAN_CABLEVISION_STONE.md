## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Telecommunications

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Distinct questions: express statutory duty; whether Congress intended private enforcement; scope of any equitable or damages remedy.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress clearly forbade operator editorial control, but the existence of a protected service does not itself designate private plaintiffs, liabilities or compensation. The Cable Act’s franchise enforcement and separate specified remedies are strong structural evidence against implying damages from §531(e) alone. If Congress provided another actual avenue to enjoin unlawful editing, it remains viable on its own source.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 531(e)’s prohibition on operator editorial control is enforceable through channels Congress authorized; it does not by itself create an implied federal private damages cause of action merely because an operator violated it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress clearly forbade operator editorial control, but the existence of a protected service does not itself designate private plaintiffs, liabilities or compensation. The Cable Act’s franchise enforcement and separate specified remedies are strong structural evidence against implying damages from §531(e) alone. If Congress provided another actual avenue to enjoin unlawful editing, it remains viable on its own source. The complaint also sought injunctive relief and asserted Connecticut law. Rejecting a general implied damages action does not establish that every other remedy is unavailable. On remand the court must identify a preserved source of authority for any surviving relief; neither the requested injunction nor a franchise label creates that authority by itself. Denver Area does not permit the court to assume that every access channel is a governmental forum or that every editorial exclusion is actionable for damages. Its PEG component has no controlling sufficient rationale under Marks, and the surviving 1996 provision requires its own analysis. Stone’s participation in a broader constitutional rationale does not convert that separate view into a statutory private cause of action. The damages question must be resolved from the enacted remedial structure, while independently authorized claims remain available on their own terms.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No merits finding that Cablevision’s refusal complied with the anti-editorial restriction; no conclusion about constitutional forum status, state law, franchise enforcement, or statutory obscenity exceptions.
