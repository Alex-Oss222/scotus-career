## Shared coverage holding and operative qualifications

The FTC Act reaches this nonprofit professional association because it is organized to carry on business for the profit of its members and its activities confer proximate, substantial economic benefits on their for-profit practices. CDA's advantageous insurance and financing arrangements, commercial support and member-oriented business activities satisfy the statute. Nonprofit form, tax status or the absence of profits distributed to the association itself does not eliminate the members'-profit branch of §44.

The reference to substantial commercial activities in the model describes CDA's established activity, not a new percentage or proportional-activity test. Neither exclusive devotion to member profit nor a substantial fraction of the organization's total activity devoted to that purpose is required. The requisite connection to member profit must be real and proximate; presumed or incidental benefit from professional education alone is not decided sufficient. No ruling is made on every charitable or educational organization, nonprofits with annual surpluses, or entities that compete commercially without serving for-profit members. Coverage does not depend on proof that each member earns a net profit from membership.

All eight adopt this construction under ordinary statutory interpretation. They do not decide whether deference is owed to the Commission's view of its jurisdiction. The statutory exception list remains operative; no exception is established for CDA. No professional-association immunity or state-action immunity follows from nonprofit status, the ethical label, or a guideline's unverified account of state law. Statutory coverage permits the competition inquiry and supplies no finding of liability.

Historical Parts I–II and footnote 6 confirm these qualifications. The model's shared statutory construction is reconciled at this scope, without adding a quantitative threshold or transferring the historical author.

## Path R — O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas

### Rule, price application and quality application

An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry can suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make the likely anticompetitive tendency clear enough to assess it and the material competing explanation. Here the professional market's information disparities make the effects of the challenged restrictions insufficiently obvious for the analysis employed below. More examination of the actual price and quality restraints is required. No fixed sequence of labels or mandatory full industry study replaces the rule of reason; the inquiry must fit the circumstances, details and logic of each restraint.

**Price advertising:** Price communication ordinarily supports comparison and rivalry, and the reported enforcement impedes fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements. But an across-the-board discount without an intelligible baseline or scope may convey less reliable information than accurately specified services and prices. Requiring particular disclosure might improve comparison, harm it or do both. Treating the practical restriction on discount advertising as sufficient proof of harmful net competitive tendency, and demanding empirical justification from CDA on that basis alone, does not adequately examine the professional-information explanation. The point is the legal adequacy of the analysis, not a finding that every advertisement is deceptive, every disclosure condition useful, or CDA's justification ultimately correct.

**Quality advertising:** Restrictions can hinder useful comparison and service rivalry, but professional quality can be difficult for patients to verify. Restrictions on unverifiable or misleading claims may improve information. The court below could not resolve that competing account merely by equating fewer advertisements with a supply limitation in dental services. A possible effect on demand is not itself proof of producer-imposed restriction of service output. This does not deny that quality-advertising restrictions may harm competition, require proof of a measured output decline in every antitrust case, or approve a blanket ban. The actual effect and justification need the fuller analysis missing here.

NCAA supplies flexible analysis where competitive tendencies are established. Professional Engineers rejects a defense that competition itself is undesirable. Indiana Federation of Dentists permits direct analysis of a horizontal agreement withholding a desired service; it does not make every professional information restriction identical to that agreement. Bates recognizes the value of truthful professional price information while preserving the distinct problem of unverifiable quality claims. Khan and NYNEX require attention to the actual restraint and competitive mechanism, without converting these horizontal rules into vertical agreements or giving them immunity.

### Remand and proof limits

Vacate the enforcement judgment and remand to the Ninth Circuit for a fuller consideration consistent with the proper inquiry, while resolving FTC coverage. As historical footnote 8 confirms, the Ninth Circuit must determine whether it can adequately assess the Commission's decision on the existing record or should return it to the Commission for more extensive analysis and an enhanced record. No new administrative hearing, comprehensive local-market study, predetermined market-power threshold or automatic adoption of the ALJ's no-market-power conclusion is required by this disposition.

This coalition does not decide whether the evidence supporting the Commission's ultimate conclusion is substantial. Correcting a legal standard does not authorize reweighing supported findings; §45(c) remains effective. Conversely, preserving that statutory obligation does not amount to independently affirming every challenged finding or economic conclusion. The decision finds neither the restraints reasonable nor final antitrust liability. Legitimate restraints on false or misleading advertising remain possible under their actual conditions.

## Path A — Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer

### Complete separate position

These four join coverage and the general need for a context-sensitive rule of reason but would affirm the existing enforcement judgment. They accept the Commission's supported findings about actual enforcement, rather than treating the facial prohibition of material deception as a complete description of the challenged agreement. They find the existing analysis sufficient when the actual restraint, its competitive tendencies, the claimed justification and its practical market reach are considered together. Those are substantive questions, not a newly mandatory four-step formula for every antitrust case.

**Price advertising:** Preventing truthful descriptions of low, reasonable or affordable fees and making broad discount advertising practically unavailable impedes consumer comparison and reduces dentists' incentive to compete on price. A truthful discount can supply useful information without listing every undiscounted service price. Proper disclosure rules may be beneficial, but the supported enforcement goes beyond protection against deception. The claimed reliability benefit has not been shown to justify the practices found. This view does not license deception or condemn every disclosure requirement.

**Quality advertising:** Suppressing useful quality information without determining whether the particular claims are misleading restricts competition to supply qualities patients value. The mechanism is obstruction of communication and matching between provider and patient, not an invented measurement that the total number of dental procedures fell. Verification difficulties are real, but a general assertion of unreliability does not justify the broad actual restraint once the supported findings and competitive tendencies are identified. The coalition does not revive a discontinued fear-allaying policy as a presently enforced restraint.

**Burden and practical effect:** On the showing these four find sufficient, CDA bears the burden of establishing an offsetting procompetitive justification; a merely possible benefit does not force the Commission to refute every conceivable theory. They regard the inquiry and record as adequately answering the reliability argument. Their conclusion about likely practical effect rests on the reported substantial membership, barriers to entry, membership benefits and evidence that dentists value retaining membership, together with the actual enforcement and demonstrated competitive mechanism. The statewide figure is not a finding of identical share in every local market. They do not adopt the ALJ's market-power conclusion or invent a new precise local share.

Indiana Federation's direct-effects analysis and statutory treatment of supported Commission findings are central. NCAA supports an inquiry calibrated to the restraint; Professional Engineers requires a competitive justification rather than rejection of competition itself. The coalition's analysis treats the Commission's factual findings and the reviewing court's legal judgment as distinct. It neither establishes automatic deference to every agency conclusion nor requires every antitrust case to include a full market study.

**Remedy and limits:** Affirm the existing enforcement judgment under the rule of reason, without drafting a new administrative order, expanding sanctions, awarding damages or declaring all professional advertising restraints unlawful. No exact unquoted order provision is independently adjudicated. The position is a complete disagreement with Path R's need for further analysis on both advertising categories, not merely a disagreement over the phrase quick look.

The common ground is rule-of-reason analysis. No coalition here relies on per se condemnation as an additional sufficient holding. The historical affirming writing expressly found it unnecessary to decide whether the Commission's per se alternative was correct. The model's rejection of per se treatment as the selected basis therefore must not become a gratuitous comprehensive ruling about every horizontal professional-price restriction.
