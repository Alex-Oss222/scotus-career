## Record, reach and components

All eight Associates reach the granted federal statutory and associated indictment/jury questions on review of the Ninth Circuit's 1997 judgment. The earlier Oliver opinion supplies the authenticated record recitation, not the judgment presently reviewed. No supported jurisdictional, preservation, participation or vehicle defect prevents review. The superseded neutral packet's court-identity warning has been resolved by the supplement.

The December 1992 offense is governed by the original firearm-possession version of section 2119. The basic maximum is fifteen years, the injury maximum twenty-five years, and the death maximum life. The indictment omitted serious bodily injury; the general verdict did not establish that aggravator; the judge used an injury finding to impose the increased statutory range. Injury is the concrete application. Death is part of the statute's grading structure, not a factual allegation about Jones. Holloway's later-enactment intent element cannot be inserted into this offense.

The distinct components are: (S) whether the statute creates graded offenses; (A) the indictment and jury consequences of that classification; (C) a definitive constitutional rule for additional present-offense conduct or consequences indispensable to an increased maximum; (U) a universal constitutional rule for every maximum-increasing fact, including wholly prior-adjudicated conviction status; and (M) unchanged-maximum mandatory minima. C, U and M must not be collapsed. M is not required to dispose of this record. The remedy is separately assessed.

## Entering authority and actual prior positions

Almendarez-Torres controls its aggravated-reentry statutory classification, charging requirement and bounded remedy. Scalia, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg adopted constitutional avoidance there. No general constitutional maximum-fact rule became Court law. O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer's dissent relied on the recidivist subject and statutory structure; it was not an endorsement of every judicial penalty classification.

Monge is later public law and indispensable to the present assessment. Thomas, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer adopted the Court's classification of its serious-prior inquiry as noncapital recidivist sentencing. They expressly accepted that the generic conviction did not establish the required personal weapon use or infliction of great bodily injury, and that the qualification enlarged the maximum. Their position cannot be reduced to reliance on a previously proved fact. The Court nevertheless reserved independent indictment, jury and due-process claims. Its double-jeopardy classification therefore does not settle Jones.

Scalia, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg adopted Monge's definitive, case-confined element-function dissent. The additional personal-conduct qualification was unestablished by the prior conviction and indispensable to the greater maximum; on that ground the completed failed adjudication received offense-acquittal protection. Their position went beyond constitutional doubt. Stevens separately supported finality even if the proceeding were genuine noncapital sentencing; the other three did not join that extension. Neither dissent established a universal maximum-fact rule.

Gaudin requires jury determination beyond reasonable doubt once a criminal element is identified. Almendarez-Torres and Hamling supply the federal indictment consequence. McMillan concerns a minimum within an unchanged ceiling. Witte and Watts concern ordinary sentencing within conviction-authorized authority and do not independently resolve an increased ceiling. Monge preserves these limits. Holloway supplies each Justice's recent treatment of actual statutory elements and, for Scalia and Thomas, distinct textual objections; it supplies no retroactive amendment.

## Supported alternatives and their strongest objections

**Graded-offense path.** Injury and death identify aggravated forms carrying substantially greater authorized punishment. Traditional grading by the victim's injury, the distinct maximum clauses and the gravity of removing such a fact from the charge and verdict support an element construction. The strongest contrary argument is structural: the opening paragraph states a complete firearm-taking offense, followed by a common punishment command and dependent numbered penalty clauses. The response is that this drafting form does not establish that facts selecting among the authorized offenses are merely discretionary sentencing considerations; the grades can share the opening elements. Almendarez-Torres confirms that statutory placement alone cannot decide classification. This path leaves the existing basic conviction unable to support the injury range.

**Penalty path.** The opening offense paragraph and dependent punishment clauses can be read to create one crime with graduated sentencing consequences. The strongest objection is that the challenged fact describes injury in the charged criminal event and enlarges the maximum, with no indictment or jury determination; neither recidivism nor ordinary within-range sentencing answers that objection. Its proponents must affirmatively defend this statute's constitutional allocation, rather than cite Monge as already deciding the reserved jury question. They do so on the bounded view that the complete firearm-taking offense is independently defined and victim injury may be made a consequence-based sentencing aggravator here. They do not claim unlimited legislative authority to relabel an actual element.

**Constitutional choice.** Avoidance is sufficient to adopt a fairly available graded-offense reading. A definitive present-offense maximum rule also has concrete support in the four Monge dissenters' already published element-function position, now applied to harm in the current charged offense and coupled with Gaudin's allocation rule. That inference does not require treating Monge's double-jeopardy remedy as a jury holding. A rule also covering bare, previously adjudicated conviction status encounters the unresolved distinction left in Almendarez-Torres and Monge. A rule invalidating unchanged-ceiling minimum findings encounters McMillan and cannot be derived from a greater-maximum premise.

## Proposition and disposition accounting

Among the eight Associates, five support reversal of the aggravated sentencing authorization and the S/A statutory rule: Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Three support affirmance and the bounded penalty construction: O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer. These are Associate commitments only; no Stone vote, total Court judgment or author is supplied.

Four independently support C as a definitive constitutional proposition: Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. C concerns an additional fact about the commission or consequences of the charged offense necessary to increase its maximum; a federal indictment and jury proof beyond reasonable doubt are required unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense. Their support is a justified extension of their actual published Monge element-function position, not a claim that Monge already held C or that those Justices adopted a universal rule there. Thomas supplies no fifth constitutional join. No collective commitment to U or to changing M is established. Neither number may be silently enlarged in assembly.

The five statutory proponents can join an opinion that treats serious constitutional doubt as a sufficient reason for the graded construction. The four C proponents' more definite position does not force them to reject that sufficient statutory account. Conversely, counting their joinder in an avoidance opinion does not establish that they renounce C. If an opinion states C definitively, Thomas's join stops at the statutory classification, established-element consequences and remedy. Any proposed wider constitutional proposition requires the separately supported scope test; it is not an editorial synonym for C.

The reversal remedy is confined to the aggravated statutory authorization. On the existing charge and verdict this carjacking count cannot receive more than the basic fifteen-year maximum. The Court does not order fifteen years, prescribe an aggregate sentence, decide all issues concerning another properly charged proceeding, find acquittal, or import Monge's completed-insufficiency bar. Death's classification follows the common statutory construction without finding death in this record. No independently sufficient statutory alternative or remedial exception is silently omitted.
