# O’Sullivan v. Boerckel

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts shall treat claims omitted from the ordinary Illinois Supreme Court petition as unexhausted and, if state review is now barred, procedurally defaulted, subject to cause, prejudice, actual innocence, waiver, and any clear Illinois rule excluding such petitions from the ordinary review process.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Exhaustion through the State’s highest court

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies exhaustion to the review process the State actually makes ordinarily available. Discretionary does not mean nonexistent, but a State may clearly announce that a particular extraordinary petition is unnecessary for exhaustion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state prisoner ordinarily must present each federal claim through one complete round of the State’s established appellate process, including a discretionary petition to the State’s highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step; a clear state rule making the step unnecessary controls.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Illinois provided a regular petition-for-leave-to-appeal procedure and had not told prisoners that federal claims could bypass it. Boerckel therefore did not give the State’s highest court the opportunity that § 2254 requires for the omitted claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require repetitive extraordinary remedies, applications the State expressly labels unnecessary, or futile petitions after the State has finally resolved the claim through another ordinary route.
