## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse Tenth Circuit and hold that the coal reservation does not by its terms include distinct coalbed methane gas.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory Interpretation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Interpret the reserved term as Congress used it at enactment, then consult structure and practical mineral estate operation; distinguish history as evidence of enacted meaning from frozen later factual applications.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The statutory reservation spoke of coal as then understood: a solid mined energy resource. Methane was known and separately treated as an escaping gas hazard. Later discovery of economic worth cannot change the legal substance expressly retained. Although the Tribe later holds the coal estate, a favorable construction cannot create ambiguity contrary to text and contemporaneous understanding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** In the 1909 and 1910 Coal Lands Acts, federal reservation of “coal” does not convey ownership of coalbed methane gas as a separate mineral estate simply because gas occurs in coal seams.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory reservation spoke of coal as then understood: a solid mined energy resource. Methane was known and separately treated as an escaping gas hazard. Later discovery of economic worth cannot change the legal substance expressly retained. Although the Tribe later holds the coal estate, a favorable construction cannot create ambiguity contrary to text and contemporaneous understanding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Does not determine statutory or lease rights in gas that was separately reserved, compensation for trespass to coal seams, surface access, or extraction interference with coal ownership.
