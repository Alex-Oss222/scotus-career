# Lilly v. Virginia

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Supreme Court of Virginia’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The conviction and sentence shall be vacated unless Virginia proves the confrontation error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt under the complete record. The State may retry Lilly without the statement or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Admission of a blame-shifting custodial confession

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the contemporary confrontation framework. The blame-shifting accusation is not within a firmly rooted exception at the asserted scope, and its custodial circumstances supply no adequate particularized guarantee of trustworthiness. This conclusion does not adopt a later categorical doctrine for all testimonial statements.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nontestifying accomplice’s custodial confession that minimizes the declarant’s role and assigns the charged killing to the accused is not admissible merely as a statement against penal interest; absent prior cross-examination or a genuinely applicable established exception, its admission violates confrontation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lilly’s brother minimized his own responsibility while attributing the principal killing to Lilly during separate police questioning. Context must test the specific inculpatory assertions; admitting some misconduct does not make every accusation against another person reliable. The interrogation created concrete incentives to curry favor, shift blame and reduce personal exposure. Independent corroboration cannot cure the absence of the guarantees required for this hearsay accusation, and Lilly had no opportunity to test those incentives through cross-examination. White’s accepted, firmly rooted exceptions do not encompass this different setting. Williamson reinforces the need to separate the assertions without deciding the state evidence rule or the constitutional question by itself.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar genuinely self-inculpatory remarks severable from blame shifting, forfeiture by wrongdoing, dying declarations, or statements admitted for a nonhearsay purpose with effective limiting safeguards.
