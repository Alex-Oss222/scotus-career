* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lonchar v. Thomas requires source-governed disposition of first habeas petitions and recognizes exhaustion and procedural default as distinct governing doctrines rather than generalized finality.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Seventh Circuit held that Boerckel exhausted his claims through the Illinois intermediate appellate court and that failure to seek discretionary review did not bar federal habeas consideration.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The State argues that its highest court must receive an ordinary opportunity to consider each federal claim. Boerckel distinguishes discretionary leave from review that must be exhausted. The State’s actual appellate structure and any clear instruction that a step is unnecessary determine availability.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Failure to use an available ordinary review step may establish default, subject to actual exceptions. A State may define its own review system so that a particular step is unnecessary; the federal court cannot assume such an instruction or automatically reach barred merits.
