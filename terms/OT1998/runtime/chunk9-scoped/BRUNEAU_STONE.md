## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Title IX statutory right through §1983

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do not use §1983 to duplicate the comprehensive Title IX statutory right when Congress foreclosed that route.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1983 cannot be used to evade Congress’s deliberate Title IX funding-recipient liability limits by relabeling the *same statutory right*. But the Fourteenth Amendment imposes independently sourced duties on state actors, so a federal funding statute does not silently eliminate an equal protection claim. Students must still prove purposeful discrimination by the particular defendant, causation and valid relief; negligence or mere sex-based harassment by peers is not automatically unconstitutional official conduct.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Do not use §1983 to duplicate the comprehensive Title IX statutory right when Congress foreclosed that route.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1983 cannot be used to evade Congress’s deliberate Title IX funding-recipient liability limits by relabeling the *same statutory right*. But the Fourteenth Amendment imposes independently sourced duties on state actors, so a federal funding statute does not silently eliminate an equal protection claim. Students must still prove purposeful discrimination by the particular defendant, causation and valid relief; negligence or mere sex-based harassment by peers is not automatically unconstitutional official conduct.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic student victory; Title IX jury verdict stands unless a preserved error supports relief. Equal protection purpose and state action cannot be supplied by Title IX receipt alone.

### Issue 2: Independent Equal Protection claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Allow the separate Constitution-based right with actor-specific purpose, causation and immunity.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1983 cannot be used to evade Congress’s deliberate Title IX funding-recipient liability limits by relabeling the *same statutory right*. But the Fourteenth Amendment imposes independently sourced duties on state actors, so a federal funding statute does not silently eliminate an equal protection claim. Students must still prove purposeful discrimination by the particular defendant, causation and valid relief; negligence or mere sex-based harassment by peers is not automatically unconstitutional official conduct.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Allow the separate Constitution-based right with actor-specific purpose, causation and immunity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Fourteenth Amendment independently binds state officials. A funding statute directed to recipients does not silently repeal that constitutional duty merely because the same events support a Title IX complaint. Each defendant must have the required discriminatory purpose or legally sufficient deliberate conduct, causal responsibility and actionable injury; supervisor title alone is not enough. Municipal liability requires its own policy or final-decision link, and any personal immunity is judged under conduct-date law. Correcting statutory displacement therefore restores only the opportunity to prove the independent constitutional claim.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic student victory; Title IX jury verdict stands unless a preserved error supports relief. Equal protection purpose and state action cannot be supplied by Title IX receipt alone.
