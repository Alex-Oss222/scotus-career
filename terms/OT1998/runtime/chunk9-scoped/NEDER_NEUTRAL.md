* **Question Presented:** (1) Whether removal of an offense element from the jury is subject to harmless-error review and, if so, whether the materiality errors require relief here; (2) whether materiality is an element of the federal mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud statutes.
* **Question Granted:** (1) Whether removal of an offense element from the jury is subject to harmless-error review and, if so, whether the materiality errors require relief here; (2) whether materiality is an element of the federal mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud statutes.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed the fraud and tax convictions. It treated the judge’s determination of tax materiality as harmless because materiality was undisputed, and held that materiality was not an element of mail, wire, or bank fraud. These are distinct grounds, not one undifferentiated harmless-error ruling.
* **Relief Requested:** Neder seeks reversal of the affected convictions, arguing that materiality belongs to the jury and that its removal requires a new trial. The United States disputes the statutory element and alternatively asserts harmlessness.
* **Material Record:** Neder, an attorney and real-estate developer, used controlled entities, inflated appraisals, concealed relationships between buyers and sellers, and misrepresented down payments in parcel transactions financed by lenders. The indictment included nine mail-fraud, nine wire-fraud, twelve bank-fraud, and two false-tax-return counts. The tax counts concerned more than $1 million of unreported income in 1985 and more than $4 million in 1986. The judge told the jury that materiality on the tax and bank counts was for the court, and omitted materiality from the mail- and wire-fraud instructions. Neder objected. His defense disputed fraud and knowing falsity rather than offering evidence that the omitted income or financing representations lacked capacity to affect the relevant decision. The reported instructions and objections permit analysis of the distinct count classes; they do not establish a stipulation relinquishing the jury’s element determination.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The materiality objections were preserved. Chapman review therefore differs from the forfeited-error inquiry in Johnson. The statutory question must be answered before deciding the consequence for the mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud counts. No disposition is assigned to an unreported count or unrelated conviction.

### Issue 1: Materiality as a fraud element

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Gaudin assigns every actual offense element to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Wells construes the differently worded §1014 false-statement offense without a materiality element; it does not remove the settled materiality component from statutes using the legal term fraud. The tax-return materiality requirement under §7206(1) is distinct from the disputed construction of §§1341, 1343 and 1344.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Gaudin assigns actual offense elements to the jury; identifying materiality under these statutes remains a separate interpretive question.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal mail, wire, bank-fraud, and tax-fraud statutes; traditional legal meaning of fraud.
* **Material Other Authority:** Congress's use of the settled legal term “fraud” incorporates materiality unless the enacted text displaces it.

### Issue 2: Complete removal of an element from the jury

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Gaudin requires jury determination of actual elements but reserved universal structural-error treatment. Sullivan v. Louisiana (June 1, 1993) treats a reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the entire verdict as structural. California v. Roy (November 4, 1996) expressly holds that omission of an element does not by itself require automatic reversal; its controlling Chapman inquiry permits reliable whole-record inferences addressing the actual verdict, not a hypothetical correctly instructed conviction. These limits must be addressed before classifying removal of materiality.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth and Sixth Amendments; *Chapman v. California*; *Sullivan v. Louisiana*; *Rose v. Clark*.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chapman requires harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Sullivan addresses a defect vitiating the entire reasonable-doubt verdict; Roy permits actual-verdict review of an omitted element. The materiality omission must be assessed within those distinct categories.
* **Open Point:** Roy permits whole-record inferences concerning the actual verdict and does not require necessary equivalence in every case. The opposing questions are whether the enormous omitted income and financing evidence reliably establish noncontribution of the error, and whether instructions affirmatively removing materiality leave an unresolved effect on the jury’s deliberative task.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed the fraud and tax convictions. It treated the judge’s determination of tax materiality as harmless because materiality was undisputed, and held that materiality was not an element of mail, wire, or bank fraud. These are distinct grounds, not one undifferentiated harmless-error ruling.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Neder disputes removal of materiality from the jury and seeks reversal; the government disputes its statutory role or argues that any omission was harmless on overwhelming or uncontested evidence. Gaudin fixes element allocation, while Roy prevents automatic structural treatment of every omission.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

After identifying materiality as an element, apply the correct error classification and actual-verdict harmlessness inquiry. A new trial follows if the error requires relief; strong evidence alone is not a substitute for the prescribed inquiry, and the omitted element alone does not establish a universal structural rule.
