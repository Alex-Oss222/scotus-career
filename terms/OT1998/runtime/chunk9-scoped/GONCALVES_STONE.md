# Reno v. Goncalves

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the First Circuit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Board of Immigration Appeals shall consider Goncalves's former § 212(c) application under the law applicable to pending applications when AEDPA took effect and exercise the discretion that law supplies. The judgment confers no entitlement to a favorable exercise of discretion and does not adjudicate the excluded equal-protection claim.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Continued availability of § 2241 habeas review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves jurisdiction first. The petition presents pure statutory and constitutional questions, and no other threshold defect defeats the historic habeas channel.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the clear-statement and meaningful-review principles already operating in the simulation. AEDPA and IIRIRA altered immigration review extensively, but the provisions invoked do not clearly eliminate § 2241 for the legal claims presented, and the Government may not derive withdrawal of the writ from implication alone.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AEDPA and IIRIRA did not clearly withdraw district-court jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 to hear the pure statutory and constitutional habeas challenges presented by a covered criminal alien in Goncalves's posture.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Jurisdiction is not assumed and is not created by the seriousness of the claim. It exists because § 2241 supplied a historic channel and Congress did not clearly close it in the provisions governing this pending proceeding. The alternative review structure did not furnish an equally clear route for the specific legal challenge when Goncalves filed. The First Circuit therefore properly reached the statutory issue.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not establish district-court review of every discretionary immigration determination, reopen factual findings, or displace an exclusive review channel Congress states clearly and preserves meaningfully.

### Issue 2: Temporal application of AEDPA § 440(d)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the temporal question because jurisdiction survives and the Board applied § 440(d) to a pending application.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Landgraf*, *Rivers*, and the enacted structure. Congress did not clearly direct § 440(d) to govern already-filed applications, while neighboring provisions contained express temporal instructions. Applying the new categorical bar to a request already filed and adjudicated under former § 212(c) would attach a new legal disability to the pending proceeding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AEDPA § 440(d) does not apply to a former § 212(c) application that was pending when AEDPA took effect, absent a clear congressional direction making the new categorical restriction applicable to such pending applications.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Goncalves had invoked the discretionary process, obtained an immigration-judge decision on its merits, and pursued an administrative appeal before enactment. The new law did not merely change a tribunal's procedure; it removed eligibility for the discretionary judgment already underway. The First Circuit correctly required the Board to decide the pending request under the former eligibility rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern applications first filed after AEDPA's effective date, guarantee discretionary relief, or decide the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal in another posture.

### Issue 3: Interpretive weight owed the Attorney General

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the question because the Board relied on the Attorney General's decision in *Matter of Soriano*.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone works within the then-operative statutory-deference framework and does not silently replace it. The Attorney General cannot receive controlling weight on district-court jurisdiction, and no deference is due when ordinary interpretive tools resolve the temporal question against the agency's construction. At most, *Soriano* receives persuasive consideration for its reasoning, which does not overcome the text, structure, and nonretroactivity principles.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Attorney General's conclusion that AEDPA § 440(d) applies to pending applications receives no controlling weight because the statute's temporal operation is resolved by ordinary interpretive tools and the agency's contrary reasoning is unpersuasive.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agency's immigration expertise is real within delegated policy and factual domains, but the issue here is Congress's temporal command. *Soriano* treated statutory silence as authorization to extinguish pending eligibility and gave insufficient effect to the enactment's express temporal contrasts. Deference cannot make silence carry a legal consequence the statutory structure does not support.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not deny appropriate weight to authorized immigration judgments on genuinely unresolved statutory details, policy choices, or technical administration within the agency's lawful domain.
