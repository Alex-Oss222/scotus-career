### Issue 1: Collateral-order appeal of a Rule 37 sanction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct requires exceptional separateness, importance, and practical unreviewability before allowing a collateral-order appeal.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Collateral-order review of settlement-enforcement rulings, under Article III and Federal Courts, supplies the narrow finality rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 1291; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37.
* **Material Other Authority:** Cohen and Firestone preserve postjudgment review for ordinary litigation-management rulings.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Sixth Circuit dismissed the attorney’s interlocutory appeal for lack of jurisdiction.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Counsel seeks immediate review because the sanctions bind her personally and she no longer represents the litigant. Opponents invoke final-judgment review and the sanctions’ connection to the merits proceedings. Personal inconvenience and litigation cost do not alone establish a collateral-order category.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If later review provides an effective correction and the order lacks the required separateness and importance, dismiss the interlocutory appeal. That does not validate the sanction or eliminate eventual review on a proper record.
