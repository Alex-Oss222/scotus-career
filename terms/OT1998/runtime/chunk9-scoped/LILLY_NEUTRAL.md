### Issue 1: Admission of a blame-shifting custodial confession

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** White v. Illinois (January 15, 1992) retains the existing confrontation framework and permits properly founded firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment statements without a separate unavailability requirement; it leaves broader reconstruction open. Williamson (June 27, 1994) construes federal Rule 804(b)(3) assertion by assertion and reserves confrontation. It is an evidentiary analogue, not a direct federal-rule command to Virginia. The relevant constitutional inquiry concerns the genuinely applicable exception or particularized guarantees inherent in the offered accusation, not a later categorical testimonial rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth Amendment Confrontation Clause; Ohio v. Roberts as the then-received framework; hearsay rules concerning statements against interest.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lee v. Illinois treats accomplice confessions as presumptively unreliable, especially when they shift or spread blame during police questioning.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Supreme Court of Virginia affirmed Lilly’s capital conviction and treated his brother’s confession as sufficiently reliable against penal interest.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Virginia relies on the confession’s self-inculpatory setting and asserted trustworthiness. Lilly identifies the brother’s minimization, accusation of others and custodial incentives. A statement against interest must be examined at the scope offered, and contemporary confrontation rules remain separate from state evidence law.
