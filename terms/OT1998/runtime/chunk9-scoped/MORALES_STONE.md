# City of Chicago v. Morales

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Supreme Court of Illinois's judgment solely on the standardless-enforcement ground.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The dismissals and reversals resulting from invalidation of the ordinance remain in force. Chicago may enact objectively bounded rules governing obstruction, trespass, intimidation, threats, criminal conduct, or emergency dispersal.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Standardless dispersal trigger

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial vagueness question and uses only the narrow adequate standardless-enforcement ground.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An officer's inability to discern another person's purpose is not an objective act or condition. The later duty to disperse cannot cure the antecedent discretion because it converts the officer's uncontrolled judgment into the trigger for criminal liability.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A criminal ordinance is facially vague when every prosecution depends on an officer's unexplained conclusion that persons remain in public with 'no apparent purpose,' and the law supplies no objective criterion governing that conclusion. A later refusal to disperse does not cure the standardless trigger.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trigger governs every order and contains no objective predicate such as obstruction, threat, trespass, intimidation, or criminal conduct. Because the state court rejected a narrowing construction, the ordinance necessarily delegates the decisive boundary to street-level discretion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate objectively bounded loitering or dispersal laws and does not recognize an unlimited substantive right to remain anywhere. Stone does not rely on broader fair-notice or facial-permeation reasoning unnecessary to the judgment.
