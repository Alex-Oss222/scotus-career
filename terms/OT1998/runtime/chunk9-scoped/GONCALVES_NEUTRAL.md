* **October Term:** 1998
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Simulated post-grant merits consideration. This brief enters no Court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1999-06-03 — established simulation assignment preserved from source.
* **Question Presented:**

  1. Whether AEDPA and IIRIRA clearly withdrew district-court jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 to hear statutory or constitutional habeas challenges to deportation by covered criminal aliens.
  2. If habeas jurisdiction survives, whether AEDPA § 440(d)'s restrictions on former § 212(c) discretionary relief apply to applications or proceedings already pending when AEDPA took effect.
  3. What interpretive weight, if any, is owed to the Attorney General's contrary determination that § 440(d) applies to pending applications.

* **Question Granted:**

  1. Whether AEDPA and IIRIRA clearly withdrew district-court jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 to hear statutory or constitutional habeas challenges to deportation by covered criminal aliens.
  2. If habeas jurisdiction survives, whether AEDPA § 440(d)'s restrictions on former § 212(c) discretionary relief apply to applications or proceedings already pending when AEDPA took effect.
  3. What interpretive weight, if any, is owed to the Attorney General's contrary determination that § 440(d) applies to pending applications.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit reversed the District of Massachusetts's dismissal of Goncalves's § 2241 petition. It held that § 2241 jurisdiction remained available for the pure statutory and constitutional questions presented, held that AEDPA § 440(d) did not apply to former § 212(c) applications pending when AEDPA took effect, granted the writ to that extent, and remanded to the Board of Immigration Appeals for a discretionary determination.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the First Circuit, dismiss the § 2241 petition for lack of jurisdiction or uphold application of § 440(d) to Goncalves's pending request for discretionary relief.
* **Material Record:** Goncalves became a lawful permanent resident in 1972. Deportation proceedings began in 1990 after criminal convictions, and an immigration judge denied former § 212(c) relief on January 20, 1995, after considering the merits of discretion. While Goncalves's administrative appeal was pending, AEDPA took effect on April 24, 1996. The Board later applied the Attorney General's decision in *Matter of Soriano* and treated § 440(d) as barring the pending application. Goncalves then filed a § 2241 petition presenting legal challenges to that application of the statute.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant is limited to § 2241 jurisdiction, the temporal reach of § 440(d), and the interpretive weight due the Attorney General. The First Circuit did not decide Goncalves's equal-protection claim, which is outside the simulated grant. The case does not ask whether Goncalves is entitled to favorable discretionary relief, only whether the Board must exercise the discretion available under the former law.
* **Relevant Dates:** Order to show cause December 11, 1990; immigration-judge decision January 20, 1995; AEDPA effective April 24, 1996; BIA decision March 24, 1997; § 2241 petition August 8, 1997; district-court dismissal August 14, 1997; First Circuit decision May 15, 1998; rehearing denied July 31, 1998; simulated grant March 8, 1999; simulation-assigned argument April 21, 1999; simulation-assigned decision June 3, 1999. First Circuit decision: May 15, 1998.

### Issue 1: Continued availability of § 2241 habeas review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich*, 510 U.S. 200, requires statutory channeling intent to be fairly discernible and preserves an initial judicial route when the designated scheme cannot provide meaningful review. No simulated decision holds that AEDPA or IIRIRA eliminated the historic § 2241 writ for the pure legal claims presented here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Specialized statutory review-channel preclusion.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 2241; AEDPA; IIRIRA; the Suspension Clause background; surviving habeas and clear-statement authority including *Felker v. Turpin*.
* **Material Other Authority:** The structure and transitional provisions of the 1996 enactments are used to determine whether Congress clearly displaced the existing habeas channel.

### Issue 2: Temporal application of AEDPA § 440(d)

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Landgraf v. USI Film Products*, 511 U.S. 244, and *Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 298, require a clear temporal command or, absent one, analysis of whether applying the new restriction attaches new legal consequences to pending conduct or proceedings.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registers preserve Landgraf's sequence for temporal reach; no immigration-specific contrary rule is identified.
* **Other Governing Law:** AEDPA § 440(d); former INA § 212(c); IIRIRA's later repeal and transitional structure.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Lindh v. Murphy*, 521 U.S. 320, supplies a structural comparison concerning express and omitted temporal directions in the same enactment.

### Issue 3: Interpretive weight owed the Attorney General

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T*, 512 U.S. 218, provides that deference cannot extend agency authority beyond a meaning the enacted text can bear. *NationsBank v. VALIC*, 513 U.S. 251, gives controlling weight only to a permissible construction within delegated statutory design. *Auer v. Robbins* concerns an agency's own regulation and does not control interpretation of AEDPA's statutory temporal reach.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Delegated power to modify does not authorize elimination; National-bank authority to broker annuities, limited to the general proposition that agency construction must remain permissible within the statute.
* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary statutory-interpretation principles and the Attorney General's delegated immigration authority.
* **Open Point:** The precise weight due *Matter of Soriano* must be resolved within the then-operative statutory-deference framework, without treating the agency as authoritative on the existence of habeas jurisdiction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit reversed the District of Massachusetts's dismissal of Goncalves's § 2241 petition. It held that § 2241 jurisdiction remained available for the pure statutory and constitutional questions presented, held that AEDPA § 440(d) did not apply to former § 212(c) applications pending when AEDPA took effect, granted the writ to that extent, and remanded to the Board of Immigration Appeals for a discretionary determination.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The government argues that the immigration amendments remove the asserted review route and apply the new relief restriction to the pending request. Goncalves invokes surviving §2241 review for a legal question and the temporal distinction for a discretionary proceeding already underway. Agency expertise does not answer Congress’s temporal command by itself.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A surviving review channel permits adjudication of the statutory eligibility question without awarding discretionary relief. Applying the older eligibility rule preserves the administrative choice rather than guaranteeing it. A genuinely closed channel would require its own lawful jurisdictional disposition.
