## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse Ninth Circuit recognition of a generalized substantive-due-process right not to have a lawyer searched during a client’s grand jury testimony; remand for genuinely preserved Fourth Amendment questions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reverse Ninth Circuit recognition of a generalized substantive-due-process right not to have a lawyer searched during a client’s grand jury testimony; remand for genuinely preserved Fourth Amendment questions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Due Process

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Source-specific claim sequencing: search regulation under Fourth Amendment, occupational exclusions under independent due-process rules, official immunity separate.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Government may not search beyond valid authority, but the validity of warrant acquisition and execution is governed by the Fourth Amendment. An incident of temporary interference with client consultation does not establish a free-floating substantive entitlement to be free of lawfully authorized investigation, and no separate professional licensing exclusion occurred.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Prosecutors’ conduct in arranging execution of a search warrant during an attorney’s client’s testimony does not independently violate a substantive Fourteenth Amendment right to practice law; any search injury requires source-bound Fourth Amendment analysis.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Government may not search beyond valid authority, but the validity of warrant acquisition and execution is governed by the Fourth Amendment. An incident of temporary interference with client consultation does not establish a free-floating substantive entitlement to be free of lawfully authorized investigation, and no separate professional licensing exclusion occurred.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This judgment does not rule the search constitutionally valid, foreclose privilege/attorney-client challenges, or create prosecutor immunity from an otherwise proved violation.
