## Wisconsin Right to Life, Inc. v. Paradise - neutral additions

**Cutoff:** 1998-11-24. **Channel:** post-grant appellate review of the Seventh Circuit's March 13, 1998 judgment in No. 97-3668, 138 F.3d 1183; rehearing denial April 16, 1998. **Review scope:** pre-enforcement Article III jurisdiction; no Wisconsin constitutional or statutory merits disposition is supplied by the scheduling.

**Correction of lower procedural shorthand:** The Seventh Circuit did not simply affirm a jurisdictional dismissal. The District Court found standing but abstained under Pullman. The appellate majority vacated that judgment and remanded with instructions to dismiss for want of jurisdiction. Flaum dissented. This matters to the precise disposition under review and any return for consideration of abstention.

**Source-supported facts and positions:** WRTL publishes comparative candidate guides and flyers, maintains a separate PAC for more explicit advertisements, and curtailed its own voter-education activity because of feared registration consequences. No official had threatened WRTL, required its registration, or sued it. The parties agree that ordinary guides and flyers showing candidate positions do not require registration. The Attorney General's longstanding Buckley interpretation is not legally binding on the Board. The Board's 1976 rule addresses expenditures expressly advocating election or defeat and political contributions; the opinion reports no equivalent Board major-purpose regulation.

The record also includes the Board's March 17, 1997 Travis order concerning other organizations' election-season advertisements. Those ads compared positions and called for support without saying vote for a candidate. The Board's order supplied no reasons and stated that failure to register and provide information would lead to subpoenas. It is the statute, not the Board order itself, that imposes a registration duty; an assumption that the Board adjudicated WRTL's materials would be false. The opinion reports actual private candidate suits under Wis. Stat. sections 11.60 and 11.66, temporary speech restraints against other organizations, and ongoing state proceedings. Those candidates and state judges are not defendants here. The only defendants are the Attorney General and Board members. No identity, threat, agency or concert relationship between an absent private plaintiff and these defendants is invented.

**The actual request and disagreement below:** Both the majority's paragraph 5 and Flaum's dissent recognize WRTL's facial attack. The dissent and its footnote report challenges to the breadth of political-committee registration, the lack of protection for certain nonprofit organizations, and the Board-enforcement and citizen-suit provisions as permitting prior restraints. WRTL sought a declaration of invalidity and an injunction against enforcement, arguing that a narrowing construction was unavailable. The appellate majority nevertheless analyzed an instruction to follow Buckley or supervise future applications; it concluded that such a decree would not cure the feared private suits and would be advisory. Flaum accepted the objection to that imagined decree but objected that it was not the facial relief sought. He would return for the merits and abstention questions, not invalidate the statute himself.

**Neutral legal alternatives:** A court may find that concrete intended recurring speech and the Board's treatment of materially similar communications establish threatened official injury that properly confined prospective relief could partly redress. Alternatively, the parties' agreement about WRTL's actual guides and the absence of an individualized official threat may leave only conjectural official harm, while independent private suits cannot be restrained through judgments against nonparty officials. A narrower return can require separate consideration of each actual claim, defendant, injury and proposed remedy without presently finding standing. The facial label alone proves neither jurisdiction nor its absence; predicted loss on the merits does not erase an otherwise substantial controversy. A jurisdictional decision need not resolve the constitutional validity of sections 11.60 or 11.66.

**Entering law and distinctions:** Lujan's ordinary elements require concrete personal injury, traceability and likely redress at the litigation stage. Its absent-funding-agency application is not a controlling rule in this timeline. Steel Co. (June 2, 1997) and NAACP (November 5, 1998) separate jurisdiction from contested merits. Bennett (March 19, 1997) permits supported causal and remedial links despite retained official discretion. Pic-A-State (April 28, 1997) recognizes stopped activity under a credible legal constraint; relief need not eliminate every independent barrier. Lee v. Harcleroad (January 20, 1998) requires actual duty, present burden, proper defendant and likely redress; civil exposure does not categorically preclude present review, but absent concrete exposure cannot be presumed. Maine Right to Life (September 14, 1998) recognizes an actual, ceased-speech controversy over an effective regulation without compulsory advisory-opinion exhaustion; it leaves the Attorney General dismissal intact. Its substantive holding permits tightly bounded contextual express advocacy and leaves vagueness unresolved. It therefore supplies neither a magic-words-only merits rule nor automatic standing against officials who have disclaimed the challenged application. Baker (January 13, 1998) preserves lawful judgment reach and constitutional limits concerning outsiders; it does not make a declaration against officials preclusive against all private litigants. Babbitt, Virginia v. American Booksellers and Pullman retain their respective pre-enforcement and abstention roles.

**Reusable rules consulted:** `Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing`, present operation effective Lujan, June 12, 1992, supplies stage-specific injury, traceability and likely redress. The more specific operative holdings above supply the pre-enforcement, current-law and relief distinctions; no invented composite test is added.
