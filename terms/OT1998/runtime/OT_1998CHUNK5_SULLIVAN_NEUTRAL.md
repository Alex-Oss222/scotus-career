## Identity, channel, participation, and questions

American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan, **No. 97-2000**, on certiorari from Sullivan v. Barnett, 139 F.3d 158 (3d Cir. March 13, 1998), No. 96-2140. The recovered petition and merits covers establish the docket; the supplied 97-1620 is a clerical error. The participating Court at both milestones is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; no case-specific nonparticipation is established. A quorum exists; ordinary judgment requires five of the nine.

The petition asks whether private insurers become state actors by electing state-authorized utilization review and whether due process requires payment of disputed bills before reasonable-and-necessary treatment is determined. Petitioners expressly exclude the other features of the lower court's procedural ruling from their second question. Their requested alternative is reversal only as applied to private insurers and employers if they win state action and the Court does not reach due process. Their broader requested reversal depends on also prevailing on the withholding question. ## Validated record and attributed disputes

The [complete primary lower opinion](../sources/chunk5_b/sullivan_below.txt), including its footnotes, supplies the following system and procedural facts. Pennsylvania requires employers to secure compensation through private insurance, the state fund, or self-insurance. Section 531(5) requires payment within thirty days of receipt of bills and records unless the employer or insurer disputes reasonableness or necessity through paragraph (6). Paragraph (6) permits prospective, concurrent, and retrospective review. A reviewer in the same profession and same or similar specialty determines reasonableness or necessity using the provider's medical materials and generally accepted protocols; the employer or insurer pays the initial review cost. The statute's thirty-day report command and the regulatory intervals described in the opinion remain distinct. An aggrieved provider, employer, employee, or insurer has thirty days after receipt of the report to seek departmental review; the compensation judge considers the report but is not bound by it.

An insurer independently initiates review. The Bureau checks completeness and eligibility of the request, not its substantive medical legitimacy, and assigns a review organization. A properly invoked review permits withholding; it does not require it. Screening includes the accepted work-related injury, treatment identity, dates, signature/service and necessary information, and related procedural conditions. The parties disagree whether this administrative involvement amounts to joint participation in deprivation. Labeling it ministerial or a required gatekeeping act is advocacy about its constitutional significance. The Bureau's enforcement of statutory obligations and later provision of adjudication do not establish substantive approval of a particular initial medical objection.

Review assumes a work-related condition and does not adjudicate continuing disability, maximum improvement, or fee reasonableness. Providers may continue treatment while bearing payment risk. The respondents allege that some plaintiffs lost ongoing care when payment stopped; petitioners identify others who continued care. Neither account establishes the experience of every plaintiff. Retroactive bills, threatened future treatment, and interruption of existing treatment are different applications. Payment and interest can address an unpaid bill but cannot necessarily replace treatment never received. No new factual finding of interruption, availability of an alternative provider, bad faith, or the adequacy of compensation is made.

Act 57, effective August 23, 1996 as reported by the lower court, removed intermediate reconsideration. Under the revised arrangement a worker-favorable initial review lifts withholding during compensation-judge review; an adverse initial result permits continued withholding pending that review. The earlier two-review procedure and its fee allocation must not be merged with this revised one. The opinion identifies no required completion time for compensation-judge adjudication. Its descriptions of possible delays are not new findings that every review lasts months or years.

The named workers' preexisting general eligibility is reported to rest on agreements, acknowledgments, or compensation judgments. This does not establish prior adjudication of each specific treatment's reasonableness and necessity. Respondents additionally allege that four workers faced utilization-review withholding after an earlier compensation judge refused suspension and ordered benefits continued. The identity of the treatment, grounds, and operative scope of those earlier orders is not established by the recovered supplement. A ruling dependent on their preclusive effect or identical subject would require the cited pleading/order materials, not an assumption either way.

The [petitioners' source facts](../sources/chunk5_b/SULLIVAN_PETITIONERS_SOURCE_FACTS.md) and [respondents' source facts](../sources/chunk5_b/SULLIVAN_RESPONDENTS_SOURCE_FACTS.md) preserve complete-reader accounts of the original briefs without exposing present-case Supreme Court procedural recitations. Petitioners cite balance-billing protections, one example of out-of-pocket care, recovery with interest, an insurer proof burden, and assessment-funded reimbursement. Respondents contend that section 531(3)(i) and reported Bureau testimony restrict workers' self-payment. The distinct provisions, voluntary purchase, balance billing, and allegedly unlawful collection are not merged into a universal self-payment finding. No selected disposition may depend on resolving their precise statutory scope without the period text and cited testimony.

The carrier complaint was dismissed January 24, 1996 for lack of state action. The lower opinion reports the amended complaint and later public-defendant dismissals as May 21, November 7, and December 13, 1996. Respondents' brief supplies differing dates of May 29, November 8, and December 16; no exact-date-dependent issue is decided by choosing silently between them. No plaintiff class was certified. Requested relief included declarations, injunctions, restoration, compensatory damages, and punitive damages; requests establish no entitlement or award.

The Third Circuit reversed the private-actor and process dismissals. It severed the disputed-treatment exception from the payment command and separately required timely notice, reasons, an opportunity for a personal written statement, and appeal information. It rejected the inadequate-review-standards claim. It remanded reconsideration fees because payment of them was unestablished, and left capacities, immunity, and class certification for further consideration. Its footnote 24 reports that the property-interest determination was undisputed below. That presentation fact is distinct from petitioners' preserved appellate contention that general compensation eligibility does not confer unconditional payment for every disputed service.

The revised employee notice at JA 50 warns of possible nonpayment after an adverse determination, permits a written statement within twenty days, and describes appeal instructions. Respondents argue the revisions make prospective process review advisory, but separately identify earlier liability. Petitioners contest mootness of withholding and seek to distinguish their limited second issue from the unchallenged notice/personal-statement features. The revisions neither prove complete mootness nor establish a universal live prospective claim. Any remaining case must involve the actual plaintiffs, defendants, relief, and operative procedures.

Respondents report that all counsel settled the systemic reconsideration-fee claim against Commonwealth defendants at an August 19, 1998 meeting. No settlement instrument, terms, filed stipulation, dismissal order, or treatment of individual damages has been recovered. This is a reported party act, not an authorized complete terminal event or a basis to announce that all fee litigation survives. If the fee claim remains material on remand, its actual status and settlement effect must be determined under ordinary procedure. The old fee claim is not one of the two petition questions.

## Entering law and competing paths

The cumulative [neutral projection](../workspace/neutral-projection.md) through February 24 and the Sullivan sections in [B focused law](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK5_B_FOCUSED.md) replace the stale opening-only baseline. The exact register **Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review**, with present operation effective Lebron, February 21, 1995 (and X-Citement Video, November 29, 1994, for the favorable-judgment defense), permits included legal arguments in its stated circumstances but does not enlarge distinct claims or relief. No new effective earlier event is assumed.

Lebron treats special governmental creation, governmental objectives, and permanent governmental majority-appointment authority together as constitutional institutional status. Ordinary corporate operating discretion does not require a new command for each act once that institutional test is met. No such institutional facts are supplied about these private carriers. Denver Area's permission judgments lack a controlling sufficient rationale; no categorical permission-immunity rule or rule attributing all private refusals to government resulted. Its published separate positions remain evidence of the individual Justices' legal commitments. McClellan reserves operator state action and decides only the particular implied damages action; it does not decide Sullivan. West Covina distinguishes notice of the operative deprivation from individualized instruction about published procedures, and preserves actually obstructed or inaccessible remedies. It does not eliminate health-care interests or decide pre-withholding process here.

Surviving pre-divergence Lugar distinguishes a state-created rule from fair attribution to the person charged; its attachment involves direct official seizure. Blum and Jackson require a sufficient connection to the challenged private decision; regulation, authorization, and public consequences alone do not supply it. West concerns delegated performance of the State's constitutional duty to prisoners. Edmonson concerns private participation in constituting an actual governmental jury, including judicial implementation of exclusions. These settings must be compared to the actual challenged payment choice, not reduced to a label. Loudermill separates substantive entitlement from procedures governing its deprivation; Mathews weighs the actual private interest, risk and value of safeguards, and governmental burden. They do not give an unconditional right to every claimed treatment or permit elimination of a substantive entitlement by defining all process as eligibility.

**Private attribution alternatives.** Private independently chosen withholding can remain private despite statutory authorization and administrative processing, requiring reversal of the private-carrier holding. Alternatively, required Bureau involvement plus a delegated power to interrupt an established statutory benefit could constitute joint governmental action. The latter path must answer the absence of substantive official approval, the independent decision, private compensation history, and the difference from an official seizure or jury. The former must address real screening, enforcement, exclusive compensation coverage, and direct review of the State's own law; merely saying that every actor is private is insufficient.

**Scope/entitlement alternatives.** A decision could reach the included withholding question and distinguish an unestablished right to a particular service from general eligibility, while confronting care-interruption allegations and Loudermill. An independent procedural-sufficiency path would have to consider actual care interests and process, rather than assume a nominally short delay. Alternatively, once private attribution fails, the Court can grant the private petitioners' requested confined relief without reaching constitutional property/process questions unnecessary to that result or altering unreviewed public-defendant claims. Petitioners' systemwide cost and assessment arguments are the strongest answer to that limit; they establish neither the public defendants' own entitlement to relief nor every underlying fact. No automatic destruction of all workers' compensation property interests is a permissible inference.

**Remedy alternatives.** The statutory private-carrier attribution theory may be rejected without an immediate award or a universal validation of every state procedure. The separate public-defendant claims, prior injuries, capacities, immunity, revised process, fee-settlement status, and uncertified class must retain their actual procedural positions. Conversely, relief extending to the complete severance judgment requires a sufficient reached ground and proper scope; it cannot arise merely from treating public and private defendants as interchangeable. No missing research source establishes litigation default.
