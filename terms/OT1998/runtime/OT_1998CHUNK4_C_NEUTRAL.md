## Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala

### Record and questions requiring distinct treatment

Four constituent agencies' 1989 cost reports are at issue. The provider disputed $48,890 in owners' compensation and included a protested-items schedule after following the intermediary's earlier treatment of the costs. The claimed error and entitlement to additional reimbursement remain claims. The intermediary issued the notices of program reimbursement on March 29, 1991. No appeal was taken within the statutory 180 days. On March 28, 1994, the provider requested reopening, relying on salary-comparison material it characterized as newly discovered. The timely reopening request did not make an original reimbursement appeal timely.

The intermediary refused reopening on April 21, 1994, concluding that the request showed neither clear and obvious error, inconsistency with governing requirements, nor new material evidence establishing that the compensation determination was inappropriate. The provider sought Board review on October 14, 1994; the Board dismissed on January 10, 1995 because reopening authority belonged exclusively to the administrative body that made the last determination. The district court dismissed and the Sixth Circuit affirmed. The Sixth Circuit's published discussion is preserved in the [source passages](../sources/chunk4_c/C_VALIDATED_SOURCE_PASSAGES.md), original petition appendix lines 1375–1940.

The Sixth Circuit expressly distinguished two kinds of exhaustion: the missed original appeal prevents review of the original reimbursement determination through that appeal, but the provider had exhausted the available avenues concerning the refusal to reopen. Do not recast the latter claim as simply an unexhausted original appeal. The court also noted a possible necessary-party problem because the intermediary was not joined; it did not rest its judgment on that ground, and possible joinder was distinct from automatic dismissal. Letters concerning other cost years that were not before the district court did not establish a different result for 1989.

The presented paths are: (1) Board review under §1395oo of the intermediary's refusal and judicial review of the Board's jurisdictional ruling; (2) federal-question jurisdiction; (3) mandamus under 28 U.S.C. §1361; and (4) APA review, including the provider's express alternative request to reconsider the APA as an independent jurisdictional grant if the other channels fail. The APA alternative appears in the petitioner's merits brief, source lines 1520–1588, and reply, lines 990–1082, copied in the source passages. The raw packet's narrower enumeration must not silently omit it. Whether an asserted route supplies jurisdiction, whether review is available, whether a refusal was unlawful, and whether reimbursement is owed are different questions.

### Governing text and operative limits

Read the complete official [§1395oo text](../sources/chunk4_c/usc_1997_42_1395oo.plain.txt), with subsections (a)–(g) also copied in the source passages. Ordinary Board review requires the statutory cost report, dissatisfaction with an intermediary's final determination **as to the amount of total program reimbursement**, at least $10,000 in controversy, and a request within 180 days of notice. Subsection (a)'s delayed-determination alternatives retain their own conditions. Subsection (b)'s group route requires otherwise eligible providers **without regard to the individual $10,000 limitation**, a common question, and at least $50,000 in the aggregate. Subsection (f)(1) requires a group appeal or action for common issues of providers under common ownership or control, or providers that obtained a subsection (b) hearing. Four reports do not alone establish the applicable grouping facts; the lower opinion treats statutory requirements other than the disputed final-determination issue as not at issue. Do not infer a new amount-in-controversy defect from this summary. Subsection (d)'s authority to revise matters covered by a cost report does not eliminate the threshold conditions for a Board hearing.

Subsection (f)(1) supplies judicial review of a final Board decision, subject to the Secretary's 60-day review authority and the 60-day civil-action period. Its expedited legal-question route permits an action within 60 days after notice that the Board lacks authority to decide the relevant legal or regulatory question. A provider entitled to a subsection (a) hearing and having requested it may seek that authority determination with the required materials; the Board must respond in writing within 30 days, its determination is final without Secretary review, and failure to respond permits an action within 60 days after that period ends. Review of a Board decision that it lacks jurisdiction does not itself confer jurisdiction on the Board over the underlying refusal or authorize the court to determine reimbursement directly.

Section 405(h), incorporated through §1395ii, bars the specified §1331 and §1346 routes for claims arising under the Medicare scheme. The provider's procedural description does not alone decide whether the claim arises under it. Mandamus is a distinct asserted route; do not convert the incorporated text into an already resolved universal bar on §1361.

The complete official reopening provisions are copied in the source passages:

- **42 C.F.R. §405.1885(a):** the identified administrative decision may be reopened concerning matters at issue, on the administrative body's or affected provider's motion. A request ordinarily must be made within three years of notice; reopening after that period is restricted subject to paragraphs (d) and (e).
- **Paragraph (b):** an intermediary determination or hearing decision **shall** be reopened and revised when HCFA, within that three-year period, notifies the intermediary that it is inconsistent with applicable law, regulations, or general instructions issued under the Secretary's agreement. A provider's asserted inconsistency is not itself the specified HCFA notification.
- **Paragraph (c):** jurisdiction for reopening rests exclusively with the administrative body that rendered the last determination or decision. Whether this identifies the initial decisionmaker alone or also excludes the proposed review is disputed.
- **Paragraph (d):** reopening and revision are mandatory at any time if procurement by fraud or similar fault of a party is established. No such finding is supplied here.
- **Paragraph (e):** paragraphs (a) and (b) apply to periods ending on or after December 31, 1971; the three-year period also applies to earlier periods if reopening was undertaken after May 27, 1972. These historical application terms do not describe the 1989 dispute but remain part of the regulation.
- **Section 405.1887:** reopening requires written notice; a resulting revision requires notice explaining its basis, and parties receive a reasonable time to present additional evidence or argument in a reopening. These provisions do not say that every request must be granted.
- **Section 405.1889:** an actual revision in the amount of program reimbursement **after reopening** is a separate and distinct determination reviewable under the listed provisions. A refusal to reopen and a revision after reopening must not be conflated.

The manual's considerations for reopening—new and material evidence, clear and obvious error, or inconsistency with governing law and instructions—and its stated exclusion of refusal-to-reopen hearings must be identified as manual instructions. They cannot be substituted for the statute or binding regulation. Neither an assertion of general discretion nor a general statement that the agency must follow its regulations eliminates the regulation's distinct mandatory triggers.

### Competing legal paths, without a resolution

The provider argues that a refusal finally determines whether reimbursement will remain unchanged, that the statutory review presumption and Board authority permit review of an abuse of discretion even if reopening begins with the intermediary, and that otherwise unlawful refusals would be insulated. The Secretary distinguishes the final original reimbursement amount from a regulatory reopening decision, invokes the 180-day statutory route, and reads the regulation and manual as providing a discretionary additional opportunity rather than a renewed appeal. A decision must confront both text and structure; neither the word “final” alone nor the missed original appeal alone resolves every alternative.

The mandamus claim has two asserted mandatory predicates: the reasonable-cost and owner-compensation provisions, including the rule measuring comparable services at comparable institutions; and the statutory command for suitable retroactive corrective adjustments in §1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii). The provider also invokes the duty to obey binding agency regulations. The competing inquiry is whether any of these establishes a **clear duty to reopen these final cost determinations**, as distinct from a duty governing reimbursement when timely review is available. Existing Ringer requires exhaustion of other avenues and a clear nondiscretionary duty; its reservation about whether §405(h) universally forecloses mandamus must remain a reservation. The factual correctness of the compensation claim does not itself establish the required reopening duty.

Califano v. Sanders supplies the predivergence rule that the APA is not an independent grant of subject-matter jurisdiction and its analysis of reopening under the Social Security statute. The provider expressly seeks a different result based on the Medicare corrective-adjustment command. Distinguish the APA's standards for reviewing action from an independent jurisdictional grant. Sanders's treatment of genuine constitutional claims is not an automatic reopening exception for this reimbursement dispute; no distinct supported constitutional claim is supplied here.

Michigan Academy supplies a presumption of review in its different Part B setting, not automatic jurisdiction over every reimbursement reopening request. Bethesda identifies the ordinary Board route for protested or omitted costs; it does not erase the original appeal deadline. The applicable public versions of Good Samaritan, Thomas Jefferson University, Guernsey, Auer, and Regions Hospital must control over any different account in a party filing:

- Good Samaritan preserves lawful reimbursement methods and ceilings and does not command payment of every asserted expense. A changed agency interpretation requires the consideration the actual public holding specifies.
- Thomas Jefferson and Auer address permissible interpretation of an agency's own regulation within the text. Auer requires the responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation, not a plainly erroneous or inconsistent one; an amicus presentation alone neither disqualifies the interpretation nor creates authority or missing facts.
- Guernsey permits a lawful interpretive explanation of existing requirements, not contradiction of binding law.
- Regions distinguishes correction of an input used for future payments from reopening the final reimbursement paid for an earlier year. It supplies no automatic symmetry requiring reopening here.
- Thunder Basin requires assessment of the particular statutory channel and the review it actually permits. Its application is not a universal mandatory three-factor test.

If a statutory or alternative review channel is established, the remedy must identify whether the Board must hear the reopening challenge or the district court may review a specified decision; that does not establish the compensation claim or direct a payment award. If the asserted channels fail, the disposition must preserve the distinction between review of the jurisdictional refusal, direct review of reopening discretion, and the expired original reimbursement appeal. No missing source presently prevents neutral modeling of those threshold alternatives. A substantive reimbursement award would require findings this preparation does not establish.

## Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee

### Parties, posture, and preliminary record

The operative individual claims concern eight persons. Hamide and Shehadeh are permanent residents; the other six originally held temporary visas. The original advocacy-of-world-communism charges were dropped in April 1987. The six faced status-based grounds; the two permanent residents faced different amended charges, including later terrorist-activity/material-support grounds. Describing all eight as having only routine visa violations, all as unlawfully present, or all as personally adjudicated terrorists would misstate the record.

Barakat and Sharif later obtained lawful status, and the Government says the original status grounds no longer make them deportable. Respondents assert a continuing dispute concerning possible new charges and the injunction's protection. These propositions require individual treatment; neither universal mootness nor a demonstrated current removal threat for each person follows automatically. The organization's standing had been rejected earlier, and other organizational parties are no longer operative plaintiffs. The caption does not establish organizational standing.

No final deportation order is presented. This is the Government's appeal from collateral district-court preliminary relief, affirmed by the Ninth Circuit, rather than a petition to review a completed removal order. There are two procedural strands: the six persons' earlier preliminary injunction, as to which dissolution was denied, and the permanent residents' later preliminary injunction. The district court rejected the Government's renewed presentation of already available material as an adequate changed-circumstances basis for dissolving the first injunction. That ruling is distinct from the showing supporting the second injunction.

The lower courts made preliminary findings of purposeful association-based selection, disparate treatment, and a present chilling injury. Those are more than untested pleading allegations but are not final adjudications of deportability or all factual merits. The Ninth Circuit describes an absence of evidence supporting an inference that these individuals specifically intended to further the PFLP's unlawful ends; it also describes findings the Government did not challenge. The PFLP's lawful and unlawful activities, the Government's fundraising/investigative evidence, and the individuals' own conduct must remain separate. The Government contests the legal requirement for specific intent and the legal treatment of the conduct; a preliminary evidentiary conclusion under the district court's standard does not resolve those arguments. The lower record's comparator discussion is concrete; it is not a rule that all selective-enforcement cases require the same named comparator.

### Existing §1252(g) law and statutory sequence

The raw statement that no supplied public holding construes §1252(g) is incorrect. Read the complete Yang, Reyes-Hernandez, Hincapie-Nieto, Salazar-Haro, Kolster, and transition-standard entries in the [supplemental selections](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK4_C_SUPPLEMENTAL_SELECTIONS.md). The official corrected [§306(c) note and §1252 text](../sources/chunk4_c/usc_1997_8_1252.plain.txt) and [§309 text](../sources/chunk4_c/usc_1997_8_1101_notes.plain.txt) are copied in the source passages. The Ninth Circuit appendix quotes an earlier version of §306(c); it must not replace the corrected enacted text or the existing public holdings.

Yang establishes commencement on **April 1, 1997**, with corrected §306(c)(1)'s reach across past, pending, and future proceedings when the new provision commences. Reyes-Hernandez establishes that §242(g) withdraws review of the identified executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases, or execute removal orders; it does not withdraw every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Its saved review of temporal eligibility, a completed waiver denial, and treatment of a supplemental brief did not create a general constitutional-review exemption. This case's prospective challenge to selective initiation/adjudication must be compared to that actual boundary, not treated as an issue with no existing construction.

Section 1252(g)'s introductory exception for what is provided in the section and its notwithstanding clause are operative parts of the text. “Continue proceedings” is not a fourth enumerated act; identify which actual challenged decision or action is said to fall within commencement, adjudication, or execution. A collateral label alone neither establishes nor defeats the required connection.

Section 309(c)(1) generally preserves the old procedure for old proceedings, subject to its express exceptions; it does not exempt such proceedings from §242(g)'s cross-temporal operation. Paragraph (2)'s election requires that the evidentiary hearing not yet have begun and at least thirty days' notice; paragraph (3)'s termination/reinitiation option requires no final administrative decision. Neither election is established here. Paragraph (4)'s transitional judicial-review rules depend on the specified final-order date; there is no final deportation order in the presented case. The new permanent review scheme, including §1252(b)(9), cannot be imported wholesale merely because this event occurs after April 1, 1997.

Section 306(c)(2) protects actual judgments or orders entered under former §106, as amended, against invalidation or compelled reconsideration under paragraph (1). It does not itself create jurisdiction or bar ordinary review of the judgment's correctness. A collateral district-court injunction is not automatically a qualifying former-§106 judgment.

Section 1252(f)(1) restricts lower-court injunctions against operation of the designated provisions, with its exception for applying them to an individual alien against whom proceedings have been initiated. Whether the exception preserves relief within otherwise existing jurisdiction or supplies an answer to the asserted withdrawal must be reasoned from the text; do not assume it affirmatively grants subject-matter jurisdiction. Section (f)(2)'s distinct clear-and-convincing condition concerns an injunction against removal pursuant to a final order; do not substitute that condition for (f)(1) or treat a final order as already present.

Kolster preserves §2241 capability against AEDPA §440(a) for substantial legal and constitutional objections, including legal pretermission of eligibility, subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion, and the other actual habeas predicates. It does **not** already determine §1252(g)'s effect on the present claims. No unfiled habeas petition, qualifying custody, adequate substitute, transfer, or habeas relief is supplied by inference.

### Review-channel and constitutional alternatives

The Government contends that the suits attack the named enforcement decisions and that review following a final order, including the asserted use of 28 U.S.C. §2347(b)(3) for necessary factfinding, provides the proper route. Respondents contend that the immigration adjudicators cannot decide discriminatory selection, that the review court's administrative-record restriction prevents creation of the necessary selection evidence, and that delayed review cannot remedy the present chilling of association. These are competing legal and practical accounts, not agreed facts about the adequacy of the later channel. A governmental stipulation cannot confer jurisdiction.

McNary's systemic challenge and meaningful-review reasoning and the public Catholic Social Services holding concerning an actually blocked individual-review route are relevant analogies. Neither makes every claim labeled collateral reviewable. Thunder Basin requires examining what review the actual statutory system offers. The raw packet's proposed distinction of McNary must remain an argument, not an established answer. If constitutional avoidance is used, identify the serious constitutional question, the actual channel problem, and the permissible statutory construction; neither “constitutional claim” nor “immigration discretion” supplies the entire analysis.

If the threshold permits consideration of the selective-enforcement question, distinguish First Amendment association and retaliation, discriminatory administration, immigration enforcement discretion, the Government's allegations of conduct supporting unlawful activities, and the procedural requirements for each injunction. Healy and Claiborne address the conditions for attributing unlawful organizational conduct to an individual; the asserted immigration and conduct distinctions remain to be considered. Harisiades, Mandel, Fiallo, Wayte, and Yick Wo must be used at their actual subjects and scope, not as a universal statement that all aliens either possess identical rights or possess none.

The following complete public-law selections were added because the raw packet did not supply their material limits:

- **Armstrong:** Rule 16's defense-materiality branch does not authorize discovery of the selective-prosecution claim addressed there. Its discovery showing concerns an indirect racial-selection theory and requires credible evidence of discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable similarly-situated inference. It is less than a prima facie merits showing; reliable statistical or circumstantial support and a justified alternative to named comparators remain possible. The decision does not supply a complete merits test, resolve the direct-admission alternative, or decide every other source of discovery. It cannot silently become the test for a developed First Amendment preliminary-injunction record.
- **Whren:** objective cause for the traffic stop does not resolve an independently prohibited purposefully discriminatory selection. Its scope does not itself decide immigration selection.
- **Dawson:** protected association alone cannot support capital aggravation without the required relevant connection; relevant association evidence remains permissible. The sentencing setting is not a wholesale immigration rule.
- **R.A.V.:** the effective controlling public rationale is overbreadth. The content-selection rationale identified in the public entry is a separate, noncontrolling writing. A lower-court account of a different majority rationale cannot make it controlling here.
- **Purposefully arbitrary group-based selective enforcement:** the current standard preserves purposeful differential treatment, relevant comparisons, personal involvement, injury, and legitimate distinctions. Its police-enforcement setting neither establishes a general defense to every lawful prosecution nor decides the immigration question.

### Disposition paths and exact limits

A determination that the challenged acts fall within a controlling withdrawal of the district court's jurisdiction requires a disposition addressing the injunctions and the proper extent of dismissal or remand; it does not adjudicate discrimination, individual deportability, or immediate removal. A supported retained-channel construction requires explanation of its text and review rationale and then separate treatment of the two injunction strands and any individual live-dispute limits. A jurisdictional or live-controversy disposition may leave the broader constitutional question unresolved. No broad constitutional immunity from review, new terrorism exception, or newly invented exception for unusually severe discrimination is an established premise of this handoff.

The selected sources sufficiently establish the pending proceedings, two injunction strands, and jurisdictional alternatives for neutral modeling. **Conditional source limit:** if the proposed disposition turns on injunction protection against particular new charges, or on individualized mootness for Barakat and Sharif, verify the actual operative injunction language and current charge/status record rather than treating a party's assertion of that scope as an adjudicated fact. The present copies do not establish those details conclusively. Likewise, any final merits or individualized removal direction would exceed the preliminary posture and findings supplied here.
