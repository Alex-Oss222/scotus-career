# Holloway v. United States

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Second Circuit’s judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No separate remedial instruction is required.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Conditional intent under § 2119

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads the intent element to require a real decision to inflict the specified harm if the condition occurs. A mere threat, bluff, recklessness, or later-formed intent is insufficient, but the law does not require a pointless desire to harm a compliant victim.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2119 is satisfied when, at the moment the defendant demands or takes the vehicle, the defendant possesses the conditional intent to kill or cause serious bodily harm if necessary to complete the taking.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The jury was required to find that Holloway would use the gun and inflict serious harm if a driver resisted. That is an intent concerning the prohibited result, not negligence or a threat divorced from purpose. Requiring unconditional intent would exclude the paradigm armed carjacking in which violence is contingent on resistance.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit conviction on proof of intimidation alone, an empty threat, reckless risk, or an intent formed only after the taking.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Eighth Amendment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Distinguish waiver of an underlying constitutional guarantee from independently dispositive, unexcused procedural default.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Choosing among methods of execution authorized by law is not an affirmative knowledgeable relinquishment of all objections to the chosen method. But that correction does not reopen a procedurally defaulted habeas claim absent a recognized excuse. Neither the severity of a death sentence nor the incorrect waiver rationale manufactures habeas jurisdiction or automatically excuses default.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** This election among statutory execution methods does not establish knowing waiver of an Eighth Amendment objection; the preserved claim nonetheless fails at the independent procedural-default threshold on this record.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Does not decide categorical constitutionality of gas, foreclose claims with adequately preserved objections, or adjudicate the Germany original-action request.

# Jackson v. Benson

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Wisconsin Supreme Court's judgment insofar as it rejects the federal Establishment Clause claim, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Wisconsin courts shall enter relief preventing public payment for religious-school tuition or integrated devotional and inseparably sectarian instruction, including payment through checks, vouchers, tax credits, reimbursements, provider-redemption instruments, or another third-party route. They shall apply state severability law to the secular-school portion and may preserve genuinely severable, publicly controlled secular services. State-law judgments outside the grant remain untouched.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Religious-school payments under the choice program

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the federal Establishment Clause questions within the simulated grant and leaves the Wisconsin-law claims outside it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Neutral eligibility and parental selection do not cure a program that keeps public value restricted to approved educational providers and permits its use for devotional or inseparably sectarian instruction. The parent's designation routes the payment but does not convert public money into private money.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State violates the Establishment Clause when it uses public money or a public monetary equivalent to pay for religious-school tuition, worship, devotional instruction, theological formation, or inseparably sectarian education, even when neutral eligibility and an intervening private selection determine the provider.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wisconsin earmarks the benefit for approved educational providers and uses the payment to satisfy tuition rather than giving recipients unrestricted private income. Stone’s objection is to purchasing the religious formation integrated into the instruction described here. Agostini’s Title I service remained under public secular control and separate from devotional teaching; Rosenberger funded an independently edited publication within a student-expression program. Those permissible relationships do not establish that the tuition arrangement has the same function. Conversely, the school’s religious identity cannot alone prove what the payment buys. Reversal reaches the legal approval of financing inseparable devotional instruction; on remand the operative payment terms, instructional program and severability determine the lawful reach of relief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not exclude religious institutions from a genuine neutral forum or from a severable, publicly controlled secular service supplied on the same civil terms to all eligible participants.

### Issue 2: Neutral forum access, secular rules, and anti-circumvention

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the attribution boundary necessary to decide the payment program.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A neutral printing benefit for independently attributed private expression can be permissible even though it costs public money. Wisconsin’s restricted tuition instrument differs if it pays for an inseparable instructional program that includes devotional formation. The constitutional distinction turns on the purchased function, administration and attribution, not simply whether money changes hands.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Neutral eligibility and parental selection do not alone validate public purchase of integrated devotional instruction; neither do public payment and a religious provider alone establish invalidity. Apply Agostini’s attribution-and-function inquiry to the tuition actually purchased.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The neutral publication subsidy in Rosenberger defeats a blanket equation of financial aid with establishment. Stone instead examines why the parent’s choice does or does not alter attribution of the purchased service. An instrument usable only to discharge tuition remains a state-defined purchase of that educational program. That is material where the program integrates worship and religious formation, but supplies no categorical prohibition of separate secular services or the distinct arrangements preserved in Witters and Mueller.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide every neutral transportation, health, meal, safety, or other genuinely severable public service, but no form of public money may finance religious exercise or instruction through an intermediary.

### Issue 1: Presidential public finance equalization

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Objective eligibility and historical support do not create a constitutional duty of parity.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Federal subsidies are government spending rules and cannot make exercise of campaign speech contingent on surrendering constitutional rights; here no ceiling restrains nonrecipient political expression and the statutory support gradient is tied to ascertainable past vote totals. There is no constitutional command to fund competing parties equally, although administration must not discriminate by viewpoint. General dissatisfaction with commission partisanship does not establish personal redressable injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Objective eligibility and historical support do not create a constitutional duty of parity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Buckley v. Valeo upheld public-financing distinctions based on prior electoral support while reserving a properly supported discriminatory application. The Party received a substantial subsidy and argues that the contribution cap prevents matching the major parties’ larger subsidy; inequality alone repeats the feature Buckley sustained. It does not establish that the rules actually suppress its candidacies below the position available without public financing. The contribution limit and the voluntary spending condition are distinct: nonrecipients do not accept a subsidy-based spending ceiling, but generally applicable contribution restrictions remain. No viewpoint discrimination or concrete exclusionary administration is demonstrated here. Stone would sustain the formula on the presented record while leaving a factually different discriminatory-application challenge available.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No declaration that all major-party advantages or commission structures are immune from evidence of deliberate exclusion or unlawful source-based restrictions.

### Issue 2: FEC composition claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Standing and authorized enforcement must be demonstrated independently.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Federal subsidies are government spending rules and cannot make exercise of campaign speech contingent on surrendering constitutional rights; here no ceiling restrains nonrecipient political expression and the statutory support gradient is tied to ascertainable past vote totals. There is no constitutional command to fund competing parties equally, although administration must not discriminate by viewpoint. General dissatisfaction with commission partisanship does not establish personal redressable injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Standing and authorized enforcement must be demonstrated independently.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute prevents a single party from occupying more than three of six seats; it does not reserve seats by name to Democrats and Republicans or prohibit appointment of Reform Party members. Striking that cap would allow the same six appointees and would not itself increase the prospect of a Reform appointment. Lujan therefore defeats the asserted causal and redressability chain on these allegations. The proper consequence is dismissal of this composition claim without deciding that every partisan practice or appointment arrangement is constitutional. The lower judgment’s separate damages and certification holdings remain outside the specified merits grant.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No declaration that all major-party advantages or commission structures are immune from evidence of deliberate exclusion or unlawful source-based restrictions.
