## Components and supported conclusions

**C1 — Franklyn's machinegun commerce challenge.** All eight reject the objection and affirm the section 922(o) conviction against it. The admitted category and actual fully automatic .45-caliber weapon fall within the national transfer-and-stock mechanism sustained in Hale, Rybar, Kirk and now Wright. Possession permits retention and circulation of stock that would defeat the valid supply restriction if omitted; no individual sale or interstate journey is constitutionally required by that rationale. The alternative — a local possession offense beyond Lopez — correctly rejects dangerousness, remote manufacture and generalized crime costs as sufficient reasons, but does not defeat this identified anti-evasion connection. Neither a machinegun's actual interstate journey nor a new jurisdictional element is added. No grandfather exception or its burden is newly adjudicated, and no Second Amendment claim is presented.

**C2 — Gonzalez's independent section 922(g) commerce objection.** All eight reject the objection and affirm his separate ammunition conviction, in light of Hiley's effective method at its actual scope. Unlike section 922(o), the prohibited-person statute regulates a disqualified recipient's acquisition and retention in connection with the express prohibition on supplying that recipient under section 922(d)(1). The counterpart recipient restriction closes that route of evasion within the national supply regime. The stipulated interstate movement of the ammunition satisfies the statute's separate nexus on this record; the constitutional mechanism is not the mere fact that any object once crossed a boundary. This preserves Lopez and the distinction between the two provisions. No unraised arms theory, new convicted-person classification, or guilt determination for a different count is supplied.

**C3 — Batson.** All eight affirm rejection of the presented discriminatory-strike claim on the actual alternative merits determination. Three of the government's six strikes removed three of four Black panel members; those figures properly call for examination and are not dismissed as legally irrelevant. The trial court heard the prosecutor's explanations and separately found no purposeful discrimination; the appellate court found no clear error in that ultimate determination. Batson and Hernandez preserve the difference between production of a neutral explanation and the court's ultimate assessment of discriminatory purpose, including credibility. Trevino and McCollum preserve ordinary staged burdens; J.E.B. reinforces that a neutral label cannot defeat supported pretext, without changing the racial inquiry into a cause-challenge requirement.

The strongest contrary path is a remand if the trial court merely accepted facial neutrality without deciding genuineness. The published source expressly reports the additional ultimate finding and appellate examination, so that defect is not established here. The unreproduced individual explanations must not be invented, assumed inherently adequate, or characterized as a detailed comparator finding. On the supported published recitation there is no identified clear error requiring displacement of the actual finding. Missing independent transcript access alone is neither litigant default nor a reason to vacate it. The opinion must not import historical Purkett's post-divergence step-two formulation; the actual step-three determination supplies the ground.

Counsel objected only after voir dire, release of the challenged jurors and the lunch recess. Timely objection serves corrective adjudication; Trevino's preserved pre-Batson objections do not make this objection timely. The independent merits ground makes it unnecessary to announce a universal forfeiture time or rule for all circumstances. No new trial or corrective hearing follows the rejected claim.

**C4 — Ineffective assistance.** All eight reject the Sixth Amendment claim under Strickland's prejudice requirement, assuming without deciding deficient lateness. The supported alternative Batson merits determination defeats a reasonable probability that timely objection would have produced relief on this record. Lateness itself does not prove absence of prejudice; an invalid merits inquiry could not supply this ground. No general professional approval of delayed objection is adopted.

**C5 — Gonzalez's base offense level.** All eight sustain level 20 under section 2K2.1(a)(4)(B), rather than the proposed level 14 under (a)(6). His matching magazine and ammunition, the group's circumstances and the district court's supported finding of assistance establish attribution of the machinegun conduct. Section 1B1.3 separately permits another participant's acts only within jointly undertaken criminal activity, in furtherance of it and reasonably foreseeable in connection with it. Foreseeability alone is not the rule. The court found both aiding/abetting assistance and the relevant joint activity; the known gun-and-matching-ammunition evidence supplies a supported ground rather than presence alone. Neither the prosecutor's recommendation nor a plea sentencing estimate binds the sentencing judge to the lesser level.

**C6 — Discharge departure.** All eight sustain the three-level section 5K2.6 departure on the supported recent discharge, not on an unreviewed general high-crime-neighborhood ground. The gunshot report, bystanders' direction toward the group, spent shell jammed in the weapon and bore carbon support the discharge finding and its attribution in the joint activity. Mere possession underlying the firearm level does not already account for discharge; the additional departure addresses a materially different risk. Koon requires legal classification of the permissible factor and deferential review of its supported application and extent. Three levels fall within that supported discretionary judgment here. The alternative claim of double counting fails at that distinct-conduct level, not because every weapon factor can always be counted twice. The lower court's alternative neighborhood rationale is unnecessary and not approved.

**C7 — Criminal history departure.** All eight reject the presented objection to the section 4A1.3 increase from category II to IV: that the judge mechanically moved beyond category III solely by adding the stale misdemeanor, which was neither serious nor similar. Two old serious juvenile felonies permissibly inform the inadequacy inquiry under section 4A1.2 application note 8; the documented repeated offending and response to prior dispositions permit a separate recidivism assessment. Category IV was the judge's assessment of the whole supported history, not invented points for that misdemeanor. The judge explained the inadequacy and selected the reference category; no empty recital of every intervening category is required when the actual criminal pattern supports the selected extent.

The strongest broader alternative is Koon's remand where an impermissible consideration materially drove a departure. The judge did mention education, employment and drug use; the Court may not erase those words or declare them categorically permissible predictors. The 1995 section 5H1.2 and 5H1.5 policy statements make education/vocational skills and employment record ordinarily irrelevant to a departure; section 5H1.4's distinct drug provision is not an unconditional upward-departure authorization. The published appeal identifies the mechanical-misdemeanor and extent objection, not a separate preserved challenge under these offender-characteristic provisions. These commitments decide that presented objection only, not a newly supplied section 5H ground. They do not find harmlessness of an established invalid factor, infer that the same sentence necessarily would recur, or supply an appellate replacement sentence. An actual preserved offender-characteristic challenge would require its own Koon classification and mixed-factor remedy analysis.

**C8 — Other-trial evidence and final range selection.** All eight reject the assertion that evidence from Franklyn's trial is categorically unavailable at Gonzalez's sentencing. Ordinary relevant-conduct findings may use reliable evidence under the applicable burden, subject to Rule 32, an opportunity to address material factual assertions and all independent Guidelines limits. The source supplies no identified denied opportunity or particular unreliability requiring a new hearing; it does not establish that every fact in another person's trial is conclusively true. Watts and Witte illustrate the limited distinction between sentencing consideration and a new conviction, without importing acquitted-conduct permission where none is needed. Stevens and Kennedy need not surrender their Watts objections to accept this narrower use of unacquitted joint-activity evidence.

The stated 63-month sentence is the top of the resulting 51–63-month range. Flight is the reported reason for that within-range selection, not a separately imposed obstruction adjustment. No new Guidelines enhancement or categorical flight rule is created. After sustaining the base level and two departures, the presented objections supply no ground to disturb the sentence.
