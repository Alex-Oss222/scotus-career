## Competing statutory paths

**Individual-violation elements:** The statute requires a felony violation that belongs to a continuing series of violations. Each violation is independently unlawful conduct, rather than merely a piece of evidence explaining how an agreed single act occurred. Reading the series to require agreement on its constituent offenses preserves the ordinary jury protection of elements and avoids allowing different jurors to convict on materially different criminal episodes. The enterprise features add requirements; they do not make the unlawful episodes interchangeable evidence. No agreement on every evidentiary detail of each agreed violation follows.

The strongest contrary argument is that Congress punished the continuing drug enterprise, not a specified collection of separately charged transactions, and the supervisory and substantial-income requirements already distinguish it from isolated offending. That argument explains the statute's target but does not establish that the independently criminal violations disappear into one undifferentiated means element. It has particular force against requiring a detailed organizational chart or agreement about every person, but those questions are not presented.

**Series as one course-of-conduct element:** Congress can make a continuing criminal business the object of an offense while permitting transactions to prove its existence. A unanimous finding that the defendant committed the required continuing series, with the separate management, concert and resources elements, may therefore be enough without a unanimous selection of transactions. Schad permits genuine alternative means, and Gaudin governs actual elements rather than deciding the antecedent classification.

The strongest objection is that this aggregation allows the jurors to disagree about every concrete offense. Schad's historically grounded single-homicide alternatives supply no automatic authorization for modern, separate crimes on different occasions. The Associates taking the course-of-conduct path nevertheless regard the substantive, temporal and enterprise connections as a sufficient legislative definition of the series here; they do not endorse unlimited legislative relabeling or a generic offense embracing unrelated crimes.

## Error classification, application and mandate remain separate

Griffin's supported-lawful-alternative rule expressly leaves an independent unanimity defect outside its operation.

Two supported remedial paths must be distinguished. Richardson proposes reversal for a new Count Two trial because express permission to disagree leaves no valid unanimous series finding; the absence of an equivalent finding is his additional argument. Evidence of many drug sales is not a finding that the jury unanimously chose the same sales, and the section 846 agreement is not three completed section 841 transactions.

The remand path survives because the Seventh Circuit decided no harmlessness issue, the governing decisions do not automatically classify this precise defect, and the positive materials neither identify a unanimous triplet nor establish every possible implication of the complete charge and verdicts. The mandate can permit first consideration of availability and, if available, application of harmlessness without directing that the defect is harmless-reviewable. ## Shared legal conclusions

**Independent constitutional claim:** Title IX does not displace section 1983 enforcement of an independently established Equal Protection right merely because the constitutional claim and a Title IX action arise from the same harassment. The constitutional claim retains its own intentional discrimination, state action, personal responsibility or municipal attribution, causation and remedial requirements. Overlapping evidence is not a comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme.

**Statutory claim:** The individual right not to be subjected to sex discrimination under a covered educational program satisfies the particular-right inquiry for the claim presented. Title IX's administrative funding process and established implied private action do not expressly exclude section 1983 and are not incompatible with section 1983 enforcement of that same right. This conclusion does not create an action for aggregate regulatory compliance or supply a Title IX duty for a defendant whom the statute does not bind. Any statutory section 1983 claim must establish the actual Title IX violation, including the recipient-responsibility conditions applicable to it; changing the enforcement label does not expand the underlying right.

Blessing unanimously separates individual rights from general program duties and treats an actual individual right as presumptively enforceable absent exclusion or incompatibility. Cannon establishes the private Title IX action, and Franklin recognizes appropriate compensatory relief within it. The administrative funding process does not give this student an exclusive personal remedial forum, and the implied private action has no elaborate claim-processing structure that a parallel constitutional action would evade. Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson preserve their actual incompatible-scheme holdings; neither establishes that any private action automatically erases section 1983.

Kinman independently adjudicated Title IX and constitutional supervisory/municipal theories, without deciding the present preclusion issue. Gebser and Davis refine recipient attribution, not constitutional extinction. Davis's express distinction between peer conduct and an official's own discriminatory enforcement is particularly important to the scope of the constitutional claim. Those decisions neither establish liability here nor change what was clearly established for the individuals in 1993.

## Adversarial paths

**Displacement path:** The combination of agency oversight, termination of funds and a direct damages action may be read as Congress's selected enforcement arrangement. Allowing section 1983 suits could permit defendants or substantive conditions omitted from the Title IX action to reenter through a different caption, undermining the funding bargain. Its strongest application concerns a nominal statutory claim against a nonrecipient.

**Non-displacement path:** Congressional foreclosure requires actual remedial incompatibility. Different defendants and constitutional duties demonstrate that the claims are not interchangeable; the absence of an individual administrative adjudication or obligatory exhaustion path distinguishes Smith and Sea Clammers. Statutory section 1983 enforcement need not evade Title IX limits because the claimed statutory right retains them.

All eight Associates reject categorical displacement on the second path. The strong evasion objection is answered by enforcing the underlying right and defendant-specific duties, not by extinguishing a separately protected constitutional right or declaring the administrative process comprehensive. Conversely, the non-displacement position cannot treat the general jury verdict as establishing an official's discriminatory intent or a municipal policy. A verdict for the recipient may have a properly demonstrated issue-preclusive effect, but its general form does not identify an issue necessarily decided.

## Jurisdiction: shared result, actual rather than hypothetical route

**Commitment of all eight Associates:** The district court has section 2241 jurisdiction to decide this substantial legal claim that an unlawful temporal eligibility rule prevented the agency from considering discretionary relief. This is not a request to reweigh the agency's discretionary equities or retry disputed deportability facts.

Kolster already holds that withdrawal of the ordinary INA review channel does not clearly repeal the independent section 2241 grant and that legal pretermission of discretionary-relief eligibility falls within its conditional scope. Its three nonjoining Associates reserved an unfiled route; their reservation was not a holding against the actual collateral jurisdiction now presented. The ordinary statute reaches custody contrary to federal law as well as the Constitution. No constitutional-label requirement may be inserted into the recognized substantial statutory-authority category.

The additional enactments do not supply a sufficient distinction. Section 401(e) deletes the specialized INA habeas provision, not section 2241. Section 440(a) restricts ordinary direct review. Section 309(c)(4)(G) bars the covered appeal in a transitional case whose final order falls after the statutory date; its appeal language does not silently delete the independent writ. Section 242(g), specially applicable to older proceedings, concerns the three named executive acts under Reyes-Hernandez and AADC, not this legal challenge to completed adjudication. The permanent section 242(b)(9) regime cannot be imported wholesale. These provisions perform different work even when read together.

**Strongest contrary route:** The combined repeal of the express INA habeas provision and broad criminal-alien review restrictions could reflect a complete congressional channel choice; retaining a general writ might defeat it. The obstacle is the independent surviving grant and the already controlling channel-specific construction. AADC's confined constitutional section 1331 route is neither needed nor extended here. Felker, McCardle and Yerger support identifying the particular channel withdrawn. The Court need not decide whether the Suspension Clause independently compels this exact statutory scope.

## Section 440(d): two genuinely different paths

**Pending-application construction:** Ordinary text and structure establish that section 440(d)'s new eligibility restriction does not govern an application filed before April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action that day. Sections 413(g) and 421(b) expressly reach pending applications, including discretionary relief, while section 440(d) does not. Section 440(f) expressly governs subsection (e); it supplies no borrowed timing clause for (d). Lindh permits this contextual inference at Landgraf's initial statutory-direction stage. The narrower holding concerns Goncalves's September 1994 application and pending administrative appeal, not every earlier crime, plea or later application.

Its strongest objection is that the neighboring provisions have different subjects and that the agency is making a present discretionary decision, so the omission does not necessarily protect pending applications. The affirmative response is that Congress expressly addressed pending discretionary relief in the same statutory undertaking; treating all discretionary restrictions as automatically applicable deprives that deliberate distinction of force. The conclusion does not depend on an invented plea bargain, subjective reliance, a vested right to a waiver, or the mere proposition that pendency always preserves old law.

**Present-decision construction:** Section 440(d) limits the Attorney General's present power to waive deportation; the pending applicant has not received a waiver and no specific completed choice shown here requires preserving that future discretion. Sections 413 and 421 address distinct terrorist/asylum restrictions and do not affirmatively create a saving clause for section 440(d). On this reading the new eligibility restriction governs the unresolved agency decision. The strongest objection is the express treatment of pending discretionary applications elsewhere; the three Associates taking this path conclude that the differently targeted provisions are not close enough to dictate a contrary instruction.

The pending-application majority resolves this case through statutory direction, not a universal completed-event rule. The alternative relevant-activity approach remains the three Associates' position, not current Landgraf law. Earlier section 440(a) decisions rejecting pendency or old convictions alone as sufficient are distinguished because the present amendment changes legal eligibility, and its own contextual timing inference must be addressed. Reyes-Hernandez supplies no automatic protection absent its proved surrendered-defense premise, and no such premise is invented here.

## Shared sufficient rule

The 1909 and 1910 reservations of coal do not reserve the naturally occurring, chemically distinct methane held by physical adsorption in the coal or in its fractures. The statutory estate is the coal identified by the ordinary meaning of the enacted term in its historical setting, with the expressly accompanying mining and removal rights. The methane at issue does not become reserved coal merely because it formed alongside coal, is physically associated with it, or now can be profitably extracted from the reservoir.

This construction does not hold that later-discovered value can never belong to an owner. Coal that was unmarketable at enactment remains coal, and later profitable uses of the reserved substance do not change title. The point is that a different substance was not included in the term Congress selected. Nor does separate gas ownership authorize destruction of the coal estate, dispense with lawful access, or answer compensation for actual interference.

## Adversarial alternatives and resolution

**Reservoir/sovereign path:** Coal is a heterogeneous deposit containing hydrocarbons; contemporaneous descriptions acknowledged occluded gases, and extraction formerly entailed disturbing the coal. Congress preserved all coal and its future energy value while enabling agricultural settlement. If that creates unresolved ambiguity, the sovereign-grant canon favors the retained United States estate, to which the Tribe succeeded. This is the strongest route to affirmance and does not depend solely on a tribal canon.

**Ordinary-substance path:** Coal and methane were separately understood substances at enactment; known hazardous gas was vented during mining while coal retained its recognized solid-fuel identity. Physical attachment does not answer legal classification. The Acts conveyed the remaining estate and reserved a particular resource, unlike the later reservation of coal and other minerals. Ownership and lawful extraction were already distinct problems in split estates. The term's meaning in this statutory setting therefore resolves the question without using a canon to add a gas reservation.

All eight Associates take the second path. Its strongest objection is that a simple solid/gas contrast may ignore the complex composition of an actual deposit and treat later technology as retroactively dividing it. The response is narrower: the record describes the methane at issue as a separately recognized substance retained through physical association; nothing about improved recovery changes either its identity or the enacted coal reservation. The broader contemporary descriptions of coal's hydrocarbon content do not make every adjacent or physically held hydrocarbon the reserved substance. No universal scientific taxonomy of all coal constituents is adjudicated.

Western Nuclear construed the broader coal-and-other-minerals reservation in the 1916 Act; its sovereign canon operates within that actual text. Leo Sheep prevents a land-grant canon from defeating the enacted conveyance. Neither canon supplies an automatic winner before reading the statute. The Tribe's successor status does not convert the original United States reservation into a new enlargement of an existing tribal estate. Yankton's attention to the actual transaction and preservation of unaddressed parcels is consistent with this bounded construction; Bourland's regulation question does not decide mineral title.

## Competing constructions

**Complete ordinary review:** A prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate review, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that step remains available. The right to submit a claim differs from a right to secure a merits decision. Selective grants do not make the petition unavailable, and ordinary error correction can present a federal question even if leave is unlikely. This rule does not require repeated presentation through collateral procedures after an adequate complete round or automatically require extraordinary remedies.

The strongest objection is that Illinois uses selective review to manage institutional questions and does not demand presentation of every error, so a federal requirement burdens the system the State designed. The five Associates adopting this rule answer that selection belongs to the state court: an available ordinary petition permits that court to decide whether to hear the claim. A genuinely applicable state rule removing the step from ordinary required review must be respected, but selective criteria and low acceptance alone do not establish such a rule here. A petition to the United States Supreme Court does not provide a State's own highest court that opportunity and is not an equivalent exhaustion requirement.

**Full fair presentation as of right suffices here:** Boerckel gave Illinois courts a complete opportunity on the three claims through the merits appeal the State guaranteed. Rule 315's selective character does not require multiplying every rejected error into a leave petition to preserve federal review. The strongest objection is the text's reference to any available procedure; the three Associates taking this path read availability in light of the settled limits on endless exhaustion and the actual purposes of the State's discretionary system. They do not excuse a failure to present a claim at any proper merits stage, or deny force to an actual independent adequate state bar.

Castille concerns claims raised for the first time in a discretionary posture, not the effect of omitting an already presented claim at the next ordinary stage. It therefore does not decide this case mechanically in either direction. Picard's fair-presentation requirement remains. Coleman preserves the distinction between an unexhausted available route and procedural default after a required route has closed; determining what was required is the present antecedent question. Lonchar forbids replacing the applicable rules with generalized disapproval or delay, but does not itself resolve Illinois's ordinary review sequence.
