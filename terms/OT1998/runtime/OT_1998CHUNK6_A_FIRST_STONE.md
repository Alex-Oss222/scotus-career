# Central State University v. American Association of University Professors, Central State University Chapter

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse federal equal-protection judgment and remand for surviving independent state-law questions, if any.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reverse federal equal-protection judgment and remand for surviving independent state-law questions, if any.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equal Protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Rational-basis scrutiny; challenger bears burden to negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate fit. Separate any Ohio Constitution state-law ground.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Legislature may rationally find uniform faculty teaching standards a legitimate management goal and exemptions from contractual veto an effective way to further it. No record evidence that bargaining previously caused every faculty workload change is constitutionally demanded under ordinary rational basis. Nothing suggests forbidden animus or a deliberate proxy for a suspect classification on the presented record.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ohio may exempt university workload standards from collective bargaining while retaining bargaining for other public employees because improving classroom instruction is a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis for the occupational line.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Legislature may rationally find uniform faculty teaching standards a legitimate management goal and exemptions from contractual veto an effective way to further it. No record evidence that bargaining previously caused every faculty workload change is constitutionally demanded under ordinary rational basis. Nothing suggests forbidden animus or a deliberate proxy for a suspect classification on the presented record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Leaves independent state constitutional limitations and bargaining rights created elsewhere untouched; this does not establish an employer’s power to discriminate against protected union activity outside the statute.

# Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Eleventh Circuit shall review the district court’s reliability ruling for abuse of discretion under the complete Rule 702 framework and reinstate summary judgment if no admissible evidence supports defect and causation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Rule 702 gatekeeping beyond scientific testimony

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 702 to the knowledge offered, not the expert’s professional label. The trial judge selects the reliability considerations that perform useful work for the particular method and explains the connection between method and conclusion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical, and other specialized expert testimony; Daubert’s considerations are flexible, nonexclusive tools rather than mandatory elements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The tire expert’s experience was relevant, but he did not reliably explain how his inspection method distinguished defect from abuse or why his observations supported the asserted cause. The district court was entitled to reject the unsupported analytical bridge.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require peer review or testing in every technical field, replace the jury on disputed but reliable evidence, or authorize exclusion because the judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

# South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject Alabama's Eleventh Amendment objection, reverse the preclusion and Commerce Clause judgments, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Alabama courts shall provide a constitutionally adequate remedy for the discriminatory tax, choosing among lawful refund, corrective assessment, or other state-law mechanisms consistent with federal law. The Court does not select the remedial formula.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Supreme Court review and nonparty preclusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves reviewability and preclusion before the tax merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The Eleventh Amendment does not prevent Supreme Court review of the federal questions decided in Alabama's own courts. *Richards* forecloses binding these taxpayers through litigation they neither controlled nor were adequately represented in.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** These taxpayers are not bound by litigation in which they were neither parties nor adequately represented, and the Court may review the federal judgment entered by Alabama's courts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The earlier action involved different taxpayers and tax years. Shared taxpayer status and a common public issue supplied neither privity nor legally accountable representation under *Richards*.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding leaves ordinary party preclusion, actual control, adequate class representation, fiduciary representation, and authorized public representation intact.

### Issue 2: Discrimination against foreign corporations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Commerce Clause merits after rejecting preclusion.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Alabama's franchise-tax structure burdens foreign corporations on actual capital employed while permitting domestic corporations to set par value well below actual value. The domestic shares tax does not compensate because it reaches a different event and is not a substantially equivalent intrastate burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Alabama's tax structure discriminates against foreign corporations, and the domestic shares tax does not qualify as a compensatory burden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The practical tax bases favor domestic corporations competing with foreign corporations. Under *Oregon Waste* and *West Lynn Creamery*, labels cannot substitute for equivalence in event, purpose, and burden, which Alabama failed to establish.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding permits evenhanded franchise taxation and genuinely equivalent compensatory charges.

### Issue 3: Remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the need for a constitutionally adequate state remedy and leaves its precise form to Alabama in the first instance.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The remedy must cure the discriminatory exaction consistently with *McKesson*, but federal law does not require the Court to choose among all lawful state remedial mechanisms on this record.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The judgment is reversed and the refund and corrective-tax details are remanded to the Alabama courts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** State law may supply a refund, retrospective equalization, prospective correction, or another adequate mechanism, provided it does not preserve the unconstitutional competitive disadvantage.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No particular refund amount or corrective assessment is ordered by this Court.
