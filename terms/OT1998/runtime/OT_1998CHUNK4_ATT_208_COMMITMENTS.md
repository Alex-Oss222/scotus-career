# AT&T — section 208 independent commitments

**Event and research cutoff:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board and consolidated proceedings; January 25, 1999. This handoff concerns only review of the announced section 208 enforcement position. It assigns no opinion and supplies no position for the Chief Justice or any other AT&T component.

**Stage:** Independent non-Stone modeling, frozen before historical reconciliation. No current-matter comparator, earlier commitments, reconciled handoff, private Stone material or Record core was supplied or opened. General model knowledge is not represented as erasable. The conclusions below rest on the identified authorized materials.

**Provisional disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the portion of the appellate judgment adjudicating the section 208 announcement's legality and remand for the omitted, claim-specific threshold inquiry. They do not affirm that the challenge is ripe, direct dismissal as conclusively unripe, or reach the enforcement merits. This agreement follows the incomplete legal standard and the bounded source account, not a presumption that every uncertain jurisdictional question requires remand. Each Justice's position and barriers appear below.

## What the present record does and does not establish

The FCC has completed its general interpretation. Paragraphs 126–127 maintain existing complaint authority, and the intended later rulemaking concerns expedited procedures rather than a condition precedent to that authority. Paragraph 127 includes a complaint alleging statutory or implementing-rule violations despite compliance with a state-approved agreement and, separately, a complaint alleging breach of the agreement itself. Paragraph 128 confines the stated complaint inquiry to the carrier's acts or omissions under the Act, while presenting that complaint as an alternative for someone aggrieved by a state determination. Its statement about not revisiting judicially decided issues is qualified, not absolute. Footnote 236 permits declining financial penalties in some instances of state authorization; it supplies neither immunity nor a command to impose a penalty. Paragraph 129 identifies additional possible enforcement mechanisms without commencing any particular case.

These assertions make present review a substantial alternative. A carrier actually caught between its approved agreement and a credibly asserted federal obligation may face a present burden before any sanction. The court cannot dismiss that possibility merely because the FCC used explanatory text, because compliance duties also have a statutory source, or because judicial review could eventually follow enforcement.

The available material nevertheless identifies no particular pending section 208 complaint, current carrier-specific conflict between an agreement and a federal requirement, statement-caused change of conduct or immediate cost, or loss of meaningful later review. That is a limit of the supplied source account, not a finding that the entire record lacks such matters or that a litigant failed a production obligation. The Eighth Circuit's reported ripeness reasoning treats completion and legal disagreement as sufficient without resolving the distinct present-effect inquiry. The appropriate corrective disposition can rest on that legal omission without settling the unknown facts.

## Common propositions available for a full join

All eight Associates commit to the following confined propositions, subject to the Justice-specific limits below:

1. Completion of an agency interpretation, the statutory final-order review channel, a concrete Article III controversy, and fitness and hardship are distinct questions. The FCC's settled view and a predominantly legal dispute do not alone resolve them. The policy-statement label does not independently defeat review either.
2. Ohio Forestry preserves review of an immediately binding provision or other supported present constraint but permits deferral when the challenged action imposes no sufficient present legal hardship and leaves meaningful review before implementation. Lee permits a presently applicable duty or supported civil-enforcement threat to supply a burden without a completed prosecution or an express criminal penalty. Bennett supplies a concrete legal-consequence comparison, not an inference that every announced forum creates such consequences.
3. The section 208 inquiry must identify the actual challenged use of authority and its effect on the actual challengers. A present conflict or credible exposure cannot be dismissed simply as a future complaint; an abstract possibility of future complaint and general disagreement about jurisdiction cannot substitute for supported present effect. The applicable litigation stage governs support; this handoff supplies no new evidence threshold.
4. The appellate section 208 merits determination must be vacated and the threshold inquiry returned under these principles. The lower court may resolve that inquiry on its proper existing record and through ordinary further proceedings as appropriate. No discovery, hearing, amended pleading, production order, new claim or additional evidence entitlement is commanded.
5. The mandate neither validates nor prohibits the asserted FCC power. It does not disturb another AT&T component, including the distinct numbering-cost ripeness question. It decides no particular complaint, agreement, penalty or state commission determination.

## Justice-specific two-path assessment and commitments

These are decisional conclusions and compatibility limits, not a reconstruction of deliberations. A common remedy does not erase the different precedents each Justice authored or joined.

### Stevens

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. His authored Lee Part III-A rejects both legal-question-only ripeness and a categorical demand to await civil enforcement. His Part III-B returns the actual-duty inquiry when the lower analysis has not adequately resolved it. His Franklin Part IV join and his Bennett join recognize completed action with present consequences; his Ohio Forestry join requires an actual present-burden application. His NAACP and WRTL joins support correction without inventing missing allegations.

**Present-review path and objection:** The strongest present-review case is the settled threat of carrier proceedings despite state-approved compliance, with no promised penalty immunity. Stevens would not require the carrier first to violate an asserted obligation. But these passages do not establish which challenger presently confronts which incompatible obligation or concrete exposure; legal availability of a complaint does not by itself complete Lee's application.

**Deferral path and objection:** A general forum announcement with meaningful later review can be unripe under Ohio Forestry. The strongest objection is that the announcement may already burden present compliance, just as a civil duty can burden a provider before suit. Because the appellate explanation does not decide that question, Stevens selects corrective remand rather than conclusive deferral.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** No join in a finding that all commentary is nonbinding, that only criminal penalties matter, or that ordinary later review always cures a current burden. He accepts dismissal without prejudice on remand if the proper inquiry establishes no sufficient present effect; he does not require repeated proceedings after an actual threshold resolution.

### O'Connor

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. Her full Ohio Forestry join supplies the present-hardship limit; her Lee III-A and III-B joins preserve both civil-obligation injury and a corrective inquiry into actual application. Her Bennett join distinguishes an instrument with concrete legal consequences from tentative advice. Her disagreement with Franklin's completed-census reviewability holding does not license disregarding that controlling holding or treating APA finality as the answer to FCC ripeness. Her NAACP and WRTL joins support this bounded remedy.

**Present-review path and objection:** A definite competing federal enforcement route may matter now to a carrier governed by an approved agreement or to a state commission whose operative determination is affected. The strongest objection is the missing identification of a presently affected determination or conduct; institutional disagreement by itself does not establish a presently consequential allocation dispute.

**Deferral path and objection:** Future carrier proceedings could supply a concrete dispute without present hardship, as future project decisions did in Ohio Forestry. But paragraph 127 is a present assertion of authority, not a promise to consider adopting authority later, and footnote 236 leaves penalties possible. She would require the actual effects to be considered before treating the future-proceeding characterization as dispositive.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** No categorical immunity for policy text and no assumption that state approval supplies a federal safe harbor. She leaves both the legality of concurrent carrier enforcement and any interference with the statutory state-review arrangement open; the remand creates neither a new exhaustion rule nor a required delay until sanctions.

### Scalia

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. His Ohio Forestry opinion distinguishes completion from ripeness and preserves immediately binding provisions. His Bennett opinion identifies an actual conditional change in legal exposure, rather than practical influence alone. His Lee separate writing and WRTL dissent insist that an untriggered duty or inadequately connected future enforcement cannot establish current injury. His authored NAACP opinion also rejects treating an allegation omitted from the available account as absent from the actual pleadings and supports correcting a threshold inquiry the lower court bypassed.

**Present-review path and objection:** The most substantial Bennett analogy is exposure to FCC complaints and possible financial penalties despite state-approved compliance. Scalia would not dismiss that analogy merely because the agency has yet to apply its position to a named carrier. Its weakness is that the record supplied here has not established the equivalent of Bennett's operative change in protection or a presently applicable adverse constraint; the statute's existence and a legal interpretation cannot alone establish that effect.

**Deferral path and objection:** He would favor dismissal without prejudice if the actual challenge concerns only a possible future complaint and no current constraint, with meaningful later review. Lee and WRTL supply that narrower dismissal position. Unlike their supported defect, the reported appellate analysis here does not establish the relevant negative premise; NAACP makes a bounded remand appropriate instead of converting a source omission into a fact about the record.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** He joins no presumed injury, hardship or final-order holding. He will not join an automatic hearing or an invitation to manufacture a new dispute. If the record independently establishes that the statement has no present legal effect on these challengers, he would direct dismissal rather than remand simply because the appellate opinion omitted the word hardship. The current handoff does not establish that additional premise.

### Kennedy

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. His Pic-A-State opinion permits review when a federal rule actually stops an existing business under a real enforcement risk, while refusing to make nondisavowal sufficient in every case. His Franklin, Bennett, Lee III-B, Ohio Forestry, NAACP and WRTL joins preserve the distinction between present federal constraint and incomplete threshold analysis.

**Present-review path and objection:** The policy's explicit coverage of state-compliant carriers and possible penalties can supply credible federal exposure without a completed complaint. The strongest objection is that the packet supplies no Pic-A-State analogue of an existing activity actually constrained by that asserted authority. His own rule requires that connection rather than a general apprehension about an additional forum.

**Deferral path and objection:** If nothing yet constrains conduct beyond independently applicable law, later complaint review can be adequate. The objection is that imposing an incompatible current compliance choice can create hardship before such review. Kennedy would return the actual-effect inquiry because neither condition is established by the lower court's completion rationale.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** No requirement that the requested relief eliminate every other regulatory burden; relief from a real federal constraint can suffice. Conversely, no finding that invalidating this statement would remove an established burden absent the causal connection. His commitment does not resolve whether section 252 district-court review is exclusive for any particular claim.

### Souter

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. His Franklin Part IV and Bennett joins support review of completed, legally consequential action notwithstanding a later governmental step. His Lee III-B, NAACP and WRTL joins support applying the correct claimant-specific threshold rule. His Ohio Forestry join prevents substituting finality for hardship.

**Present-review path and objection:** The agency has definitively asserted a complaint route despite state-approved compliance; treating planned expedited procedures as proof of tentativeness would contradict the actual text. Yet the completed interpretation does not itself establish the presently consequential conflict that his Franklin join involved. He would not infer that conflict from the mere legal disagreement.

**Deferral path and objection:** The availability of a future complaint with ordinary review supports deferral when there is no current burden. Its strongest weakness is that it can overlook an already operative compliance conflict or liability risk. Souter selects remand to apply both parts of the inquiry rather than resolve that unanswered premise in either direction.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** No rule making agency finality sufficient or making future discretion invariably fatal. He would join a narrow determination that an identified claim is fit, or unfit, only on its actual consequences; the remand does not grant present review of every jurisdictional allocation question.

### Thomas

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. His joins in Scalia's Lee separate writing and WRTL dissent supply a demanding requirement of actual application and a supported defendant-linked threat. His full joins in Bennett, Ohio Forestry and NAACP preserve the separate importance of concrete legal consequences and correction of a bypassed threshold inquiry. His earlier disagreement with Franklin's census finality holding neither displaces that governing rule nor decides this different statutory review channel.

**Present-review path and objection:** A carrier-specific current legal exposure could make this dispute reviewable before enforcement; the conditional penalty language prevents treating state approval as guaranteed protection. The objection is that a statutory power to accept a complaint and a possible future invocation do not alone identify the present personal constraint his Lee position requires.

**Deferral path and objection:** If the complained-of harm is solely a future carrier proceeding with no presently operative burden, he would order dismissal without prejudice. But the Court cannot infer that complete factual premise from a limited source account, and the appellate decision has not performed the required present-effect inquiry. His NAACP join supports the selected remand on that distinction.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** No presumed threat merely from a failure to disavow enforcement, no entitlement to an evidentiary hearing, and no legal presumption against every pre-enforcement challenge. He would refuse further remand if an independently sufficient and supported threshold defect were established; none is supplied here beyond the incomplete standard.

### Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. Her Bennett join recognizes conditional legal exposure; her Lee III-A and III-B joins distinguish actual civil duties from speculative future triggers. Her Ohio Forestry, NAACP and WRTL joins support a claim-specific inquiry and a remedy that removes premature merits adjudication without resolving the omitted jurisdictional facts.

**Present-review path and objection:** Definitive authority to proceed despite compliance with an approved agreement, coupled with possible financial penalties, may leave a carrier with a present choice requiring judicial review. The strongest objection is that no identified carrier's current agreement, conduct or federal-duty conflict is established in these sources. She would not equate a potentially burdensome policy with a proved personal burden.

**Deferral path and objection:** A genuinely contingent complaint can be reviewed when sufficiently concrete. But making later litigation the universal answer would disregard Lee's civil-obligation holding and the announced lack of an absolute state-authorization defense. The omitted present-effect inquiry warrants remand under the correct rule.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** The mandate must not extinguish a supported existing challenge, restart statutory review periods, promise later timeliness, or establish that every future remedy is adequate. It also must not turn the possibility of a present effect into a standing or ripeness finding. No section 208 merits remedy follows from her vote.

### Breyer

**Commitment:** Join the confined vacatur and remand and all five common propositions. His Ohio Forestry join requires attention to what further implementation would clarify and whether delay actually harms the challengers. His Bennett join preserves review when present legal consequences already exist. His Lee III-B, NAACP and WRTL joins favor correcting an incomplete threshold analysis without prescribing unnecessary additional proceedings.

**Present-review path and objection:** The statutory allocation question is largely legal, and further agency repetition would add little; a real present compliance burden might make immediate review appropriate. But complaint particulars can show whether the agency is addressing carrier statutory misconduct, contract enforcement, or an indirect attack on a state decision. Abstract legal fitness therefore does not establish that all these uses present the same ripe dispute.

**Deferral path and objection:** Concrete enforcement could clarify the authority actually used and permit review before an irreversible injury. The strongest objection is that current compliance or exposure costs might already make delay harmful, particularly because footnote 236 gives only discretionary penalty relief. Breyer would return that inquiry rather than presume either practical harmlessness or present coercion.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** No universal need to await enforcement, no assumption that regulatory uncertainty alone proves hardship, and no mandatory factual hearing where the existing record resolves the issue. The remand must leave the court free to distinguish ripe and unripe uses if the preserved claims and record support that distinction.

## Merits boundary if threshold review later becomes available

No Associate's present commitment reaches the enforcement merits. These are compatibility boundaries for the ripeness rationale and any later lawful merits stage, not alternative merits holdings or additional votes:

- **Carrier violations of the Act:** Section 208(a) concerns acts or omissions of a common carrier subject to the Act in contravention of the Act. A complaint alleging such a violation cannot simply be renamed appellate review of a state commission because an approved agreement is involved. Conversely, the existence of that general complaint language does not itself decide its relationship to every section 252 limitation. The paragraph 127 claim of exposure despite agreement compliance must be assessed in its actual statutory and factual setting.
- **Review of state determinations:** Section 252(e)(6)'s district-court review of a state determination presents a distinct allocation question. The FCC's carrier-focused description in paragraph 128 is material but does not itself establish that every nominal carrier complaint avoids that allocation. The challenged action and requested remedy matter. Section 252(e)(5)'s state-failure mechanism must remain distinct from review of a state determination already made.
- **Enforcement of agreement terms:** Paragraph 127 separately contemplates agreement enforcement. The Act-violation predicate of section 208 and the state approval/enforcement structure require independent treatment; breach of a contractual term is not silently equated with a statutory violation. A broad FCC rulemaking conclusion on another component supplies no automatic answer.
- **Penalty and review qualifications:** State authorization is not immunity under footnote 236, but neither does the footnote require a financial penalty. Paragraph 128's stated reluctance to revisit judicially resolved issues is not an absolute preclusion rule. Section 208(b)(3) makes orders concluding its covered investigations final and appealable under section 402(a); it does not establish that this policy announcement is such an order, that a complaint has occurred, or that every later challenge will satisfy its own requirements.

All eight would withhold assent from a present opinion that resolves any of those merits questions through the ripeness disposition. No unprovided merits vote should be inferred from their threshold agreement. A later stage requiring a merits commitment must supply the applicable complete statutory and entering-law merits materials through the authorized neutral process.

## Remedy, source limits and freeze validation

The selected mandate vacates only the appellate section 208 merits adjudication and returns the threshold inquiry; it does not reinstate a merits approval of the policy or remove another component's independently supported judgment. If the proper inquiry establishes the absence of sufficient present hardship, dismissal without prejudice is available. If a concrete, fit dispute with sufficient hardship and a lawful review channel is established, appropriate merits review remains available. Those possible outcomes are remand instructions, not findings made now.

The material unresolved propositions are the actual challengers' current exposure, any present conflict between an operative state-approved agreement and the asserted federal obligation, any statement-caused compliance burden, and the adequacy of review in the particular challenged use. The exact needed support would be the relevant preserved claim and agency/judicial record identifying that effect, or the record establishing its absence. No hypothetical allegation or new enforcement event is adopted. No in-world production obligation is invented to fill a research gap. The bounded remand is complete without selecting either factual premise.
