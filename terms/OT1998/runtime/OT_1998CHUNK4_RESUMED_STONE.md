# Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives; Clinton v. Glavin

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved. The revised disposition stated below was user-approved 2026-10-27.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In Clinton v. Glavin, REVERSE the judgment prohibiting the proposed sampling methods and vacate the injunction: §195 does not forbid sampling for apportionment, and the Enumeration Clause does not require a headcount-only method. In the House matter, VACATE the injunction and REMAND with instructions to dismiss for lack of standing: the House identifies no institutional injury of its own, and its standing may not be inferred from the individual plaintiffs' representation claim or from consolidation. Stone joins the Justices who would so dispose of the House matter.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Glavin is remanded for proceedings consistent with §§141 and 195 permitting sampling; no injunction survives on the rejected categorical statutory or constitutional prohibitions. The House action is dismissed for want of standing, and no merits ruling is entered in it.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Standing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which action permits merits review?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reach the individual plaintiffs’ adequately supported representation claim; do not infer the House’s standing from that claim.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reach the individual plaintiffs’ adequately supported representation claim; do not infer the House’s standing from that claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The projected allocation consequences give the individual plaintiffs a concrete stake in the method. The merits can be decided in their action. The House’s suit requires its own institutional-injury analysis; after the operative injunction is vacated, its remaining demand cannot preserve relief on a statutory or constitutional theory rejected in the live companion case.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general legislative standing rule.

### Issue 2: Statutory sampling authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does §195 withdraw authority supplied by §141?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 195 removes the obligation to use sampling for apportionment; it does not forbid its use.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 195 removes the obligation to use sampling for apportionment; it does not forbid its use.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 141(a) expressly permits sampling in the decennial census. Section 195 speaks in mandatory terms about when sampling shall be used. Its exception makes that mandate inapplicable to apportionment. Reading the exception as a prohibition inserts a restriction Congress did not express and unnecessarily defeats the affirmative authority in §141. Both provisions retain work: sampling is discretionary for apportionment and required where feasible for other authorized uses. Wisconsin reserved this construction, so its constitutional deference does not supply the statutory answer.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Secretary must remain within the actual statutory design.

### Issue 3: Enumeration Clause

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the proposed statistical methods be used?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A reasonable population-counting method may include sampling; a categorical headcount-only rule is unwarranted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A reasonable population-counting method may include sampling; a categorical headcount-only rule is unwarranted.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An actual enumeration requires a genuine effort to determine state populations, not adherence to a counting technique known to omit people. Conventional enumeration itself involves imputation, correction and uncertainty. Under Wisconsin, the constitutional inquiry is whether the selected method reasonably serves accurate representation, not whether it is statistically flawless. The categorical objections here do not justify the injunctions. Franklin preserves review of the completed determination and its explanation, so recognition of methodological discretion does not immunize arbitrary implementation. Wisconsin does not compel adjustment or sampling: the geographic reliability explanation justified nonadjustment there. Here Stone’s statutory construction permits the Secretary to select a reasonably supported method; it does not turn permission into a constitutional duty or resolve later implementation disputes by national accuracy alone.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No advance approval of unexplained future methodological changes.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Judicial campaign spending cap

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Under Buckley the direct total-spending cap remains unconstitutional.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A blanket $75,000 ceiling throttles campaign discourse regardless of corruption source and cannot be sustained solely because the candidate seeks judicial office. Prior fundraising cash is not itself unlawful expression, but moving donor money between electoral accounts can implicate legitimate contribution limits if a distinct source-rule supplies them. Both questions must be evaluated on the actual texts, not collapsed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under Buckley the direct total-spending cap remains unconstitutional.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A blanket $75,000 ceiling throttles campaign discourse regardless of corruption source and cannot be sustained solely because the candidate seeks judicial office. Prior fundraising cash is not itself unlawful expression, but moving donor money between electoral accounts can implicate legitimate contribution limits if a distinct source-rule supplies them. Both questions must be evaluated on the actual texts, not collapsed. The appeal concerns preliminary relief. The expenditure ceiling’s constitutional defect supports the existing plaintiff-specific injunction; it does not convert this interlocutory record into a final statewide decree. The transfer component must be reconsidered separately under the ordinary requirements for preliminary relief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Leaves ordinary judicial recusal/disqualification, ethics restrictions and lawful donor contribution limits untouched. Repealed primary limit is not a live merits target.

### Issue 2: Transfers from prior nonjudicial campaigns

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Source and contribution rules must be tested separately; remand any undeveloped predicates.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A blanket $75,000 ceiling throttles campaign discourse regardless of corruption source and cannot be sustained solely because the candidate seeks judicial office. Prior fundraising cash is not itself unlawful expression, but moving donor money between electoral accounts can implicate legitimate contribution limits if a distinct source-rule supplies them. Both questions must be evaluated on the actual texts, not collapsed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Source and contribution rules must be tested separately; remand any undeveloped predicates.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Funds lawfully raised for a legislative race remain different from a judicial candidate’s fresh personal expenditures and from new donor contributions. ### Issue 2: Ballot remedy and invalid signatures

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Not reached: the federal claim is moot after the December 23, 1998 qualification; the views stated in this issue are Stone's own and are not adjudicated.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Correctly count only valid signatures; do not assume enough remain to qualify.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Correctly count only valid signatures; do not assume enough remain to qualify.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Removing an unconstitutional residency exclusion would require reassessing the affected signatures under the surviving petition rules.
