## 1. Operative components and votes

### A. Attribution of these private carriers' initial withholding

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer join the following ground and the corresponding reversal: these private carriers do not become state actors merely by independently choosing withholding and invoking the described utilization-review process. Compulsory coverage, statutory permission, Bureau screening for proper invocation, assignment to a reviewer, enforcement of compensation obligations, and later adjudication do not establish governmental responsibility for the carriers' initial medical objection and payment choice. No official substantively approves that objection or orders the initial suspension on the established record. Private employment-injury obligations are not the State's exclusively governmental medical-care function, and the carriers do not satisfy Lebron's institutional-status conditions.

This ground requires reversal of the private-carrier attribution ruling and rejection on remand of that particular section 1983 theory against those carriers. It is not general immunity for private entities, a finding that all public participants are private, or an exemption of the legislature's own action from constitutional review. It does not deny that an actual review decision or other properly identified governmental conduct must satisfy applicable constitutional requirements.

Ginsburg does not reach A because the narrow resolution of the second presented question supplies the sufficient ground she selects. Stevens does not adopt A as the controlling ground; he addresses the fairness of the actual governmental dispute-resolution procedure and regards private-party attribution as unnecessary to that inquiry. Neither is a merits vote that the private carriers are state actors. Neither is counted as joining A's carrier-specific reversal or its rationale. Their alternative disposition positions appear under C.

**Associate support:** six for A's reversal and rationale; two withholding assent to that additional adjudication. Do not convert this into eight merits votes on private attribution.

### B. The limited advance-payment demand

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer join this limited negative rule: general eligibility for workers' compensation does not, by itself, establish an unconditional constitutional entitlement to constant payment of each disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character has been established. The categorical payment/severance judgment cannot stand insofar as it rests on that asserted unconditional right.

The rule does not declare every claim for compensation unprotected, require a new adjudication before every payment, make an insurer's objection conclusive, or find that all named workers lack any previously established entitlement. State law's substantive entitlement to reasonable and necessary care remains distinct from a demand for payment of every disputed bill without the relevant substantive condition. Whether a particular service is already protected by an operative agreement, adjudication, or a legally sufficient basis for continued-payment reliance is not resolved against a claimant merely because utilization review was requested. The identity and effect of alleged earlier orders remain unestablished. No new exhaustion rule or worker burden of proof is imposed.

Ginsburg's full join in this confined rule retains her affirmative position that claims for benefits, including medical care, require fair adjudication. Souter and Breyer retain the further reservation that prior payments may, in individual circumstances, support a reasonable expectation of continuation protected as property. The present rule neither establishes such a case nor forecloses it. Their joins do not support a universal absence of property or an unconditional entitlement to every bill.

Stevens agrees with rejecting a categorical obligation to advance every disputed payment, but does not join this entitlement rationale. His independent ground is that permitting withholding pending a fair determination of the amount or scope of a state-created entitlement is not inherently unfair. He treats the claim to reasonable and necessary care as protected property and requires fair governmental adjudication of it. His ground supplies a judgment vote, not an eighth join to B's entitlement explanation.

**Associate support:** seven for the stated B rule; all eight for rejecting the categorical advance-payment requirement, with Stevens on his distinct procedural ground. No aggregation of the grounds is needed to establish the seven actual joins, and no Marks claim is made.

### C. The disposition and what remains

All eight support setting aside the categorical advance-payment/severance requirement to the extent stated in B. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer support that correction on the limited entitlement ground. Stevens supports it because temporary withholding is not inherently unfair under the revised procedure's notice and opportunity for input, rather than because the underlying claim is not property.

The six joining A additionally require rejection of the supported private-carrier state-action theory. Ginsburg instead rests the private petitioners' relief on B and does not add an attribution holding. Stevens would retain the notice and personal-statement protections required below, reject further categorical advance-payment requirements, and leave the carrier-attribution question outside his chosen decision. His position does not supply an affirmative ruling that the carriers are governmental, or a damages award against them.

The independent notice/personal-statement features that petitioners expressly excluded from review remain undisturbed. Stevens supports their retention because the original procedure lacked warning of payment suspension and a meaningful opportunity for employee input into the initial governmental determination. Ginsburg agrees with that assessment. Both regard the revised notice/input procedure as meeting the procedural objection they address, while not adjudicating every possible delay, actual obstruction, prior award, or interruption-of-established-care claim. This is not a factual finding that all treatment continued or that money necessarily replaces care lost.

The proper remand implements the reached grounds in the existing action. It does not order wholesale dismissal of every public-defendant claim. Public officials, the State Workmen's Insurance Fund, and the School District remain distinct parties with their own actual procedural positions. Their capacities, immunity, actor issues left unresolved, surviving claims, past injury, revised procedures, and available remedies must be handled under ordinary preserved grounds. No new claim, compulsory discovery, additional process design, class certification, damages award, equal-protection decision, or global validation of the old system follows.

The old reconsideration-fee issue is outside the two questions. Respondents' report of settlement remains a report without recovered terms or a terminal instrument. Neither its universal survival nor complete termination is adjudicated. The remand cannot manufacture a settlement term or use the missing instrument as litigant default.

It is not simply an opinion refusing all further merits review after private attribution.

The present limited rule and remand do not silently import the historical opinion's statement that every respondent had failed the service-specific entitlement condition. They reject the facial unconditional-payment demand while preserving the neutral packet's unresolved identity and scope of earlier orders and particular treatment claims. This is a material boundary of the proposition being joined, not a finding resolving those allegations.

## 4. Alternatives, compatibility, and the boundaries of assembly

The strongest attribution alternative combines mandatory coverage, Bureau gatekeeping, alleged interruption of established benefits, and state enforcement as joint governmental action. The six A joiners retain the independent commitments' functional response: these acts do not supply substantive official approval or execution of the particular initial withholding, and later adjudication is distinct. Genuine official execution or a materially different governmental institution could present a different case. Ginsburg and Stevens's nonreach choices do not resolve that alternative against the six.

The strongest objection to B is that a State cannot erase substantive property merely by making a dispute trigger a new eligibility procedure, particularly where an existing award or continuing-care expectation may already protect the service. B answers that objection only by its actual narrow scope: no insurer-controlled destruction of property, no finding about the cited prior orders, and no conflation of a right to a fair determination with unconditional advance payment. Loudermill's substantive/procedural distinction remains. The seven do not rely on reimbursement and interest as a universal substitute for care or decide all procedures adequate under Mathews.

The alternative of declining B after A is lawful but not the supported choice for these seven once the historical evidence is considered at the correct limited level. The second question is actually presented and included, and its categorical payment premise can be rejected without resolving the individualized allegations. Lebron permits that legal review; it does not require resolution of missing facts or enlarge the unchallenged notice features. Ginsburg prefers B as sufficient and declines A instead. Stevens adopts the existing, narrower procedural-sufficiency route without treating the underlying claim as nonproperty.

Assembly must preserve the precise coalitions: six affirmative A joins; seven affirmative B joins; Stevens's separate process ground and partial remedial position; Ginsburg's refusal of A; and Souter/Breyer's continued-payment reliance reservation. A shared disposition on categorical advance payment does not mean a shared theory of property or private attribution. No author is selected here.

If an assembled opinion proposes complete dismissal of public claims, a finding that an earlier order concerns a different service, a universal cure of past injuries by revised notices, or an unconditional rule that an insurer's dispute defeats property, it exceeds this handoff. A material new fact requires the affected neutral stage; a genuinely new circulation proposition requires the Engine's scoped reconciliation check. The neutral packet's treatment, self-payment, delay, settlement, and old-order gaps remain unresolved at their stated scope.
