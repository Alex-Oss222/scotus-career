# NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved. The revised disposition stated below was user-approved 2026-10-27.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE AND REMAND. The court below assumed the plaintiffs' disputed Article III standing and proceeded to the merits; the Court may not decide the constitutional question on assumed jurisdiction. Stone joins the Justices who would vacate the judgment and remand for an actual determination of standing under Lujan and Steel Co. before any merits ruling.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts determine standing on the actual record. If jurisdiction is established, the merits are open on remand; the Court expresses no view on them. Stone's Issue 1 and Issue 2 positions below are his own views of the merits, not reached by this disposition, and do not control the remand.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Subsidized political opportunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Not reached. Standing was assumed below and is disputed; the Court vacates for its determination first, so the views stated in this issue are Stone's own and are not adjudicated.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** No general affirmative duty to fund campaigns follows from voting equality or free-speech law.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The scheme does not levy a price on voting, candidacy, or appearance on the ballot. A self-financed campaign’s competitive disadvantage, standing alone, is not a governmental classification denying political membership. Limiting this decision to these nonindigent plaintiffs and cost reimbursement is essential; source-respecting speech protection does not create spending authority or compulsory subsidy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No general affirmative duty to fund campaigns follows from voting equality or free-speech law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The scheme does not levy a price on voting, candidacy, or appearance on the ballot. A self-financed campaign’s competitive disadvantage, standing alone, is not a governmental classification denying political membership. Limiting this decision to these nonindigent plaintiffs and cost reimbursement is essential; source-respecting speech protection does not create spending authority or compulsory subsidy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An unavoidable ballot-access fee, price discriminator, deliberate viewpoint censorship, indigent-candidate rule, or proof that free distribution is an exclusive electoral gateway could present another case.

### Issue 2: Reimbursement for optional statement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Not reached, for the same jurisdictional reason; the views stated in this issue are Stone's own and are not adjudicated.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Need distinguish optional printing fee from filing fee or viewpoint-based electoral exclusion.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The scheme does not levy a price on voting, candidacy, or appearance on the ballot. A self-financed campaign’s competitive disadvantage, standing alone, is not a governmental classification denying political membership. Limiting this decision to these nonindigent plaintiffs and cost reimbursement is essential; source-respecting speech protection does not create spending authority or compulsory subsidy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Need distinguish optional printing fee from filing fee or viewpoint-based electoral exclusion.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The optional printed statement is a distinct state service. For these nonindigent plaintiffs, a cost-reimbursement charge does not itself exclude a voter or candidate from the ballot. The State must still administer the service without viewpoint discrimination, and an actually preserved indigence claim could present different access concerns. Neither is established by the preserved complaint; ordinary competitive disadvantage does not create a judicial appropriation for free printing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An unavoidable ballot-access fee, price discriminator, deliberate viewpoint censorship, indigent-candidate rule, or proof that free distribution is an exclusive electoral gateway could present another case.
