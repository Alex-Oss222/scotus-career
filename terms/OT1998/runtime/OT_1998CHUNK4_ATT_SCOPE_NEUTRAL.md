# AT&T — residual-rule scope and remedy supplement

**Matter and cutoff:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board and consolidated proceedings; supplied January 25, 1999 merits event. This neutral supplement concerns the residual requests described below. It supplies no vote, assignment, preferred disposition or historical Court handling.

## The three residual components

### Technical-feasibility presumption — Rule 51.317

Rule 51.317 applies to a state commission's identification of elements **beyond the Rule 51.319 list**. It first asks whether unbundled access is technically feasible. Following an affirmative determination, subsection (b) permits declining to require access only under its specified proprietary-element alternative or its quality/cost impairment condition. The proprietary alternative asks whether a carrier could offer the same service using other nonproprietary unbundled elements within the incumbent's network. The other condition examines whether denial would decrease quality or increase financial or administrative cost compared with using other unbundled elements in that network. These qualifications are part of the presumption's actual operation; the rule does not make feasibility the only textually stated inquiry.

The lower court, appendix 46a–47a and note 32, distinguished **where** access must be technically feasible under §251(c)(3) from **which** elements should be provided under §251(d)(2). It vacated only the portion of Rule 51.317 and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 creating the presumption that a technically feasible element must be unbundled. It separately sustained the definition of technical feasibility. Describing the lower judgment as striking all of Rule 51.317, or as holding that technical feasibility can never be relevant, would overstate it.

The agency/entrant path invokes §251(d)(2)'s “at a minimum” language and broader implementation authority: a reasonable additional consideration need not appear expressly among the two statutory criteria. The contrary path accepts that feasibility may matter but asks whether this burden structure and its actual exceptions preserve the independently required necessary/impair limits. A ruling about Rule 51.319 or its governing interpretation may affect that inquiry, but it does not mechanically decide the validity or remedy for every part of Rule 51.317.

### Superior quality — Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c)

Rule 51.305(a)(4) requires superior interconnection **upon request and to the extent technically feasible**, measured against provision to the incumbent, its subsidiary, affiliate or another interconnection recipient. Rule 51.311(c) separately concerns the quality of an unbundled element **and access to it**; upon request, to the extent feasible, it requires quality superior to the incumbent's own and places the burden of proving technical infeasibility on the incumbent before the state commission. The provisions also preserve lesser-quality interconnection at the requesting carrier's sole request. The surrounding equal-quality and nondiscrimination duties remain distinct.

The lower court, appendix 50a–52a, treated §251(c)(2)(C)'s “at least equal” requirement as a statutory floor that permitted voluntary agreement to more but did not itself compel superior performance on demand. It also read §251(c)(3) as access to the existing network, rather than an unbuilt superior one, and rejected compensation and nondiscrimination as sufficient answers. Its note 33 nevertheless preserved modifications necessary to accommodate interconnection or access. It did **not** hold that every necessary facility modification was forbidden.

The supported agency/entrant response is that a statutory minimum need not be a ceiling on lawful implementing regulations, and that feasible improvements, paid for by the requester, may reasonably implement the duties within delegated authority. The supported challenge is that broad rulemaking power cannot impose a substantive obligation inconsistent with Congress's actual allocation, and payment alone supplies no missing authority. A corrected-standard remand can leave the application and agency justification open; a substantive decision requires addressing these actual limits rather than announcing that the words “at least” alone resolve every question.

### Affirmative combination duties — Rule 51.315(c)–(f)

These are distinct from subsection (b)'s prohibition on separating already combined elements. Subsection (c) requires the incumbent, upon request, to perform functions necessary to combine unbundled elements even in configurations not ordinarily used in its network, provided the combination is technically feasible **and does not impair other carriers' ability to obtain unbundled access or interconnect**. Subsection (d) addresses combination with elements possessed by the requesting carrier in any technically feasible manner. Subsections (e) and (f) place the respective infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showings before the state commission on the incumbent denying the request. The other-carrier condition belongs expressly to subsection (c); it must not silently be transposed into (d).

The lower court's amended combination discussion, appendix 70a–72a, retained its conclusion that §251(c)(3)'s direction to furnish elements in a manner allowing requesting carriers to combine them did not impose the actual combining task on incumbents. It separately added invalidation of subsection (b), thus extending the vacatur to **(b)–(f)**. The current review must preserve that difference in grounds and in the duties involved.

AT&T requests corrected-standard reconsideration of (c)–(f), arguing that the incumbent's network knowledge and control, feasibility restrictions and compensation support these implementation duties. The contrary reading treats Congress's designation of the requesting carrier's combination role as a substantive limit that broad rulemaking authority cannot displace. Upholding the protection of an existing combination does not automatically uphold the affirmative creation of a new combination, its proof burdens or every demand involving an entrant's own facilities.

## Minimal supported disposition paths

The [operative MCI holding](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK4_A_FOCUSED.md#mci-telecommunications-corp-v-american-telephone--telegraph-co-512-us-218) requires identifying the enacted delegation and preserving substantive statutory requirements before deference. It neither categorically forbids consequential implementation rules nor makes general authority sufficient to sustain each rule. These principles apply without predicting any Justice's position.

- If the jurisdictional/limited-authority premise is sustained, explain its actual consequence for each challenged component. Do not assign to an independently reasoned rule invalidation a rationale the lower court did not use.
- If that premise is rejected and materially shaped the lower court's residual analysis, the Court can vacate the affected portions of the lower judgment and remand for review under the correct authority and interpretive standard. Such a mandate must identify the relevant portions of Rule 51.317, Rules 51.305(a)(4)/51.311(c), and Rule 51.315(c)–(f), as appropriate. It should state whether their substantive validity is left undecided and should not describe reconsideration as unconditional merits approval.
- If a residual statutory issue is adequately presented and resolved directly, the judgment and reasoning must address that rule's actual conditions and burdens. It may be severable from the result on another residual component, Rule 51.319, Rule 51.315(b), pricing authority or pick-and-choose. A substantive invalidation requires its own legal ground; correction of general authority cannot alone erase it.

No actual narrower grant limitation is established in the clean inputs. If an operator later asserts one, the exact controlling grant text is the needed source; it cannot be invented from the arrangement of questions in an advocate's brief. Conversely, the broad supplied question and express corrected-standard request do not require deciding every residual rule's ultimate merits. The sources now support inclusion and independent modeling of these requested remedial components without additional general research.
