## El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng - January 12, 1999

### Components and common boundaries

The reviewed component is the personal-injury branch. The separate $1,034.90 baggage award is outside this review and remains undisturbed under either judgment position. The accepted record supplies no Article 17 bodily injury; this does not decide every possible physical manifestation of emotional harm. The search occurred in the operations of embarking for the covered international flight. Its location at an airport alone is not the reason for treaty coverage.

**Exclusivity proposition:** A personal-injury damages action against the carrier arising within Article 17's substantive and temporal field is subject to the Convention's conditions and limits whatever its local-law label. Failure to satisfy the bodily-injury or accident condition does not itself put the event outside that field or create an independent local damages route. This does not displace claims arising outside the Convention's actual field.

**Application and Article 25 limit:** On that proposition, the accepted absence of qualifying bodily injury defeats this personal-injury claim without deciding whether this security search was an accident. The willful-misconduct argument is considered assuming, without deciding, that it is available notwithstanding the disputed preservation objection. The trial found no treaty willful misconduct, and the supplied record supports no reversal of that finding. Intentional touching does not itself establish treaty willfulness. Consequently Article 25 provides no different result on this record. No conclusion is needed about the full reach of Article 25 where its misconduct predicate is established; in particular this handoff does not reduce its language about exclusion and limitation to monetary caps alone.

**Alternative nonexclusivity proposition:** Article 24 applies its conditions to cases meeting Article 17's stated injury predicates. Where no qualifying bodily injury exists, the Convention supplies no recovery but does not itself extinguish an otherwise available local tort action. This position permits local adjudication, without establishing battery, false imprisonment, compensable loss or the absence of a local defense.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

The strongest objection to exclusivity is the actual limiting language about cases covered by Article 17, reinforced by Floyd's reservation and Zicherman's refusal to invent a comprehensive treaty damages code. The answer supporting exclusivity is that Article 24 subjects actions however founded to the liability conditions: making a failed condition the route around that condition would deprive the restriction of its work within the covered field. Zicherman allows domestic identification of losses and beneficiaries subject to treaty conditions; it does not authorize liability despite their failure. Uniformity supports this reading of the text but does not enlarge the field beyond the treaty.

The strongest objection to nonexclusivity is that it leaves greater local recovery possible precisely where the treaty denies recovery. Its answer is that this consequence cannot enlarge the cases Article 24 describes; the treaty can impose common terms on the injury cases it covers while leaving other local liabilities untouched. Neither Saks nor Floyd decided the reserved exclusivity question. These competing constructions, not a new factual inference about the search, produce the disagreement.

## State Street Bank & Trust Co. v. Signature Financial Group, Inc. - January 14, 1999

### Components, claim scope and proposed sufficient rules

**Construction:** The issued claims must be read as the actual combinations defined by their language and the corresponding disclosed structure and equivalents under section 112, paragraph 6. The supported construction includes the programmed personal computer and storage implementing the specified routines; it does not cover every imaginable means of obtaining the stated result. Markman and Vitronics allocate construction to the judge, including technical usage embedded in that task, and preserve external factual work. They establish no universal de novo rule for every subsidiary finding. Warner-Jenkinson prevents disregarding a material boundary. Neither a machine label nor cancellation of separately drafted method claims decides eligibility or establishes an undisclosed disclaimer.

**Section 101:** Mathematical calculation does not become eligible subject matter solely because it produces a useful financial result, is confined to a named financial arrangement, or is expressed as a programmed machine. The whole claimed combination must supply a patentable application of the calculation rather than the calculation with its ordinary implementation and handling of numerical inputs and outputs. Conversely, a claim is not ineligible merely because it employs mathematics or a computer. This is the distinction in Benson, Flook and Diehr, not a later two-stage test. No universal physical-transformation requirement, independent noncomputer-contribution requirement, or ban on all software is adopted as the common rule.

**Business use:** Section 101 supplies no separate exclusion for an otherwise eligible invention merely because it is used in business. Rejecting that categorical exclusion does not establish that these claims are eligible. Chakrabarty's broad statutory categories preserve both that distinction and the established exclusion of abstract principles.

**Application:** Reading every claim with its disclosed programmed implementation and all incorporated limitations, the actual work claimed here is the specified calculation and accounting of numerical financial allocations. The personal computer, storage, input, retrieval, disk/date checks and output implement and protect accuracy of that calculation; the claims do not require an additional operation applying its result comparable to Diehr's actual rubber-curing process. A numerical allocation's importance to ownership accounts, tax reporting or daily valuation does not by itself alter that character. This conclusion accepts the described implementation; it does not delete the machine limitations, assume them absent, compare them with undisclosed prior art, or reject a genuine external technical fact against the nonmovant. Even assuming the claimed combination novel and nonobvious, its described subject remains the calculation and its bookkeeping implementation.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison and remedy

The strongest eligibility path treats the programmed, limited combination as a specific practical machine under Diehr and Chakrabarty. Its best objection to invalidity is that real implementation limitations cannot be stripped away simply because they use familiar components or yield numbers. That objection is accepted as a limit on the analysis. It does not change the conclusion here: with the limitations retained, the combination's disclosed operation still computes, checks and records the financial values themselves. Flook denies that a restricted useful numerical output automatically establishes an application; Diehr expressly preserves that limit while prohibiting dissection by novelty.

The strongest objection to this ineligibility path is that it may covertly require a physical product or refuse protection because the implementation is conventional. The commitments reject both propositions. The result is confined to these actual claims and the disclosed function of their limitations; no class-wide answer follows for every computer system, commercial application, data structure or program. The hardware and routines are considered together, not removed as prior art. Nor is the utility of the values disputed.

A remand to apply the corrected legal standard was considered. The identified patent and lower construction supply the relevant operation of every issued claim, and the outcome does not depend on a missing prosecution reason, a new construction hearing, a disputed external technical proposition or subsidiary-review standard. No such missing task is identified that would justify withholding the present eligibility answer. The lawful remaining remand concerns disposition of the other claims and counterclaims in light of the eligibility ruling, not a mandatory new evidentiary inquiry into section 101.

**Judgment commitment:** Reverse the Federal Circuit's eligibility-based reversal of the section 101 invalidity judgment for issued claims 1-6 and remand for proceedings consistent with that determination. The district court's ineligibility result is sustained on the mathematical-calculation ground, without adopting its categorical business-method rationale. ## Department of the Army v. Blue Fox, Inc. - January 20, 1999

### Competing paths and sufficient common ground

APA section 702's waiver for relief other than money damages does not cover a demand for substitute payment of the subcontractor's unpaid private debt merely because the requested means is an equitable lien. Bowen preserves specific relief enforcing an actual entitlement, including an entitlement to payment, and therefore prevents treating every request involving money as damages. The inquiry concerns what Blue Fox is entitled to obtain and what the requested order would accomplish.

Blue Fox expressly limits its asserted lien to contract funds and argues that notice and the filed injunction action prevented later disbursements from defeating it. The theory is not disregarded as an undisguised demand for a damages award simply because it would recover money. Its decisive deficiency is that neither identification of the project account nor the Army's bond failure supplies Blue Fox an independently established right to the Government's completion funds. No earned Verdan balance remains withheld. The $46,586.14 private unpaid debt, $86,132.33 paid to Verdan after notice, $84,910.52 completion balance, and $126,772.78 replacement cost are distinct. No part is silently reclassified as property belonging to Blue Fox.

Henningsen and Pearlman concern rights arising in surety/subrogation settings; they do not give this unpaid subcontractor a direct enforceable right to attach Army funds free of immunity. Munsey Trust does not establish a general direct subcontractor right either. The Miller Act's required bond protects payment through a bond; the Government's failure to obtain one does not itself enact Treasury consent or an equivalent statutory payment obligation. Bowen's specific statutory entitlement is absent. Nordic Village and Lane require remedy-specific consent and prevent using jurisdiction or an equitable description to supply it.

The strongest contrary route treats the asserted equitable charge as already attached at notice, so that later disbursement is a stakeholder's wrongful payment and cannot destroy the right. That route would be material if an independent entitlement to an identified fund were established. But the supposed attachment is the legal conclusion at issue, not an accepted ownership finding. It cannot itself establish the waiver needed to impose the substitute payment obligation after the funds have been used. Describing the Government as a stakeholder supplies neither the underlying right nor its consent to the actual recovery.

The competing narrow-remand route would require a legally material unresolved fund or entitlement. The packet supplies no retained earned Verdan sum, and the argument for a lien on completion funding fails as a matter of the identified authority. A remand to search for or reconstruct a new fund is not justified. A true proceeding for particular claimant property under an independently applicable waiver remains outside this holding; no blanket rule declares all equitable liens damages or denies every specific money remedy.

**Judgment and remedy:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit insofar as it permitted the lien claim against the Army, and remand for dismissal of that claim for lack of an applicable waiver. No payment, recreated project balance, replacement lien or new tracing hearing is ordered. The rejected SBA branch remains outside review. The independent section 702/704 agency-action, reviewability and adequate-remedy contentions, together with Blue Fox's preservation objection, are not decided as conceded or as alternative holdings. No independent direct Tucker Act entitlement is created or denied on an unpresented theory.

## Humana Inc. v. Forsyth - January 20, 1999

### Scope, competing paths and sufficient common rule

The reviewed RICO controversy is the Co-Payors' alleged overpayments under the represented allocation of hospital charges. The Premium Payors' rejected RICO branch is not restored. The accepted limitation to proved overpayment injury is not enlarged into every asserted financial loss; a lawful statutory multiplier is a separate remedial question. The McCarran-Ferguson question does not reopen ERISA, antitrust or other RICO requirements. RICO's nonspecific relationship to insurance and the relevant state law's insurance-regulatory character are outside the granted dispute, not new contested holdings here.

**Rule:** A generally applicable federal law impairs protected state insurance law when its operation contradicts the state law or frustrates an actual state policy or the administration of the state scheme. Literal contradiction is not the only possible impairment. But overlap in the regulated conduct, an additional private action, or a greater federal remedy does not by itself establish impairment. The protected state choice must be identified and the actual operation of the federal claim compared with it.

**Application:** Nevada prohibits the deceptive insurance conduct alleged, and its regulatory jurisdiction and remedies are not established as an exclusive civil-enforcement arrangement that this Co-Payor action displaces. On the supported relationship, RICO can enforce an independent prohibition of the same fraud without invalidating Nevada's command, replacing its regulator, or frustrating an identified choice to forbid the action or remedy. Greater federal recovery alone does not prove that Nevada chose to protect the alleged fraud from that federal consequence. The record does not establish a conflicting allocation of a finite insolvency estate or displacement of an administrative judgment.

The conduct is alleged for 1985-1988. The 1987 addition of an express private remedy in section 686A.310 is not applied retrospectively. The parties dispute whether a private statutory remedy was already implied; that dispute is unnecessary here. Even assuming for purposes of this federal question that the express remedy was unavailable for earlier conduct and that no earlier implied statutory action existed, that absence does not itself establish a state policy making administrative sanctions the exclusive means of redress or immunizing the conduct from independent federal law. The neutral packet's nonexclusivity premise, rather than an invented pre-1987 private cause, supports this limited application. No conclusive inventory of Nevada common-law punitive relief, state RICO or an unverified state enforcement action is adopted from advocacy.

**Adversarial comparison:** The strongest impairment argument is that the State's choice of enforcement machinery and calibrated sanctions may be part of its protected insurance policy. The commitments accept that proposition in principle and reject a rule limited to direct textual contradiction. It fails here because the actual exclusivity or other obstructed state policy has not been established; different machinery and stronger federal relief are not alone the missing conflict. The strongest objection to the complementary-remedy route is that a shared substantive prohibition cannot settle every remedial conflict. The answer is the additional examination of the actual state enforcement arrangement and the bounded conclusion here, not a general declaration that parallel prohibitions always harmonize. Fabe's protection of policy performance and necessary administration remains fully operative. Barnett's insurance-specific federal exception is distinct and does not exempt RICO by analogy.

**Judgment and remedy:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit on the McCarran-Ferguson question insofar as it allows the Co-Payors' surviving RICO overpayment claim to proceed. The case returns to the lower proceedings contemplated by that judgment with ordinary remaining RICO elements and defenses intact. The related conspiracy-amendment instruction follows that surviving branch without a finding of conspiracy liability. No liability, treble-damages amount, premium recovery, automatic amendment success or new trial is directed by this ruling. A genuinely exclusive state scheme, a conflict with actual state administration, unrelated insurance conduct, criminal RICO and other federal laws remain undecided.
