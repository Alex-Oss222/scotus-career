# El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit's judgment as to the personal-injury claim and reinstate dismissal of that claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The New York personal-injury claim shall be dismissed. The separate baggage judgment and questions concerning occurrences outside the Convention or Article 25 remain untouched.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Exclusivity of the Warsaw Convention remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Convention exclusivity for the personal-injury claim. He does not revisit whether the search was an Article 17 accident or decide the baggage and Article 25 matters outside the grant.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Article 24's reference to actions 'however founded,' read with Article 17 and the Convention's structure, makes Article 17's conditions limits on recovery rather than invitations to replace an unsuccessful Convention claim with local tort law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a passenger-injury occurrence falls within Article 17's temporal and substantive field, Article 24 permits an action, however founded, only subject to the Convention's conditions and limits. Local law cannot supply recovery solely because the claimant fails one of those conditions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Tseng's claim arose during the international carriage setting governed by Article 17, but she alleged no qualifying bodily injury. *Saks* and *Floyd* support enforcing that boundary, while *Zicherman* does not authorize local law to create liability when the Convention's liability conditions are absent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern occurrences outside embarking, carriage, or disembarking; baggage matters outside the grant; or the operation of Article 25 and other treaty-preserved consequences.

# Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit's invalidation of the registered-voter rule, personal-name badge, and challenged individual reporting requirements.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The injunction against those three provisions remains in force. Colorado may continue to enforce the age rule, circulation period, affidavits, fraud and signature-verification provisions, paid-or-volunteer notation, sponsor attribution, aggregate expenditure disclosure, and any narrower reporting rule supported under the governing scrutiny.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Registered-voter qualification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the registration requirement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Meyer*. Excluding otherwise qualified resident adults because they are unregistered materially reduces the available speakers, while retained identification and service mechanisms address accountability more directly.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Colorado may not exclude otherwise qualified resident-adult circulators solely because they are unregistered when retained safeguards address identification and amenability to process.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Registration does not itself measure honesty, residence-based service, or compliance with petition rules. Affidavits, address information, signature verification, subpoena authority, and fraud sanctions provide materially less speech-restrictive enforcement tools.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide a residence requirement, age rule, circulation period, affidavit requirement, or fraud and signature-verification rule.

### Issue 2: Personal-name badge during solicitation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the personal-name component of the badge requirement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *McIntyre*'s exacting review and narrowly extends it to live petition advocacy. Post-circulation identification serves enforcement with materially less burden than compelled personal attribution at the moment of persuasion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Colorado may not compel a circulator's personal name during solicitation where post-circulation identification serves enforcement with materially less burden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The badge exposes the natural-person advocate during the expressive encounter. Colorado can identify and subpoena circulators through retained records after circulation, so the real-time name requirement is not narrowly fitted to fraud prevention or administrative accountability.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate a paid-or-volunteer notation, sponsor identification, or post-circulation confidential identification used for enforcement.

### Issue 3: Public reporting of individual circulators

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the challenged public reporting of each paid circulator's identity, address, and individual compensation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Buckley v. Valeo* exacting scrutiny. Sponsor identity and aggregate expenditure disclosure reveal who finances the initiative, and Colorado has not established a substantial incremental relation between its interests and publication of every circulator's address and earnings.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Sponsor and aggregate spending disclosure remain permissible, but the challenged individual reports fail because the record does not substantially connect each circulator's public address and earnings to an additional governmental need.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The record supports election-integrity enforcement through confidential affidavits, sponsor disclosure, aggregate reporting, subpoenas, and fraud sanctions. It does not show why public disclosure of each circulator's address and individual pay adds a sufficiently related benefit.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not create categorical immunity from election disclosure or foreclose a narrower individual-reporting rule supported under the governing scrutiny.

### Issue 1: Content of post-seizure notice

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit’s categorical eligibility determination and remand under the claim-specific §101 standard stated here.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the categorical business-method exception and the useful-result sufficiency rule; decide the actual machine claims under the inherited abstract-idea cases and §112 construction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Business methods

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the field of commercial use exclude the claim?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reject a categorical business-method exclusion unsupported by the statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reject a categorical business-method exclusion unsupported by the statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 101 identifies processes, machines, manufactures and compositions without differentiating favored industries. A genuine invention does not become ineligible because its useful application concerns financial administration. That conclusion does not authorize patents on bookkeeping ideas; the ordinary abstract-idea limit and every other statutory requirement still apply.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No eligibility rule based solely on commercial value or successful regulatory reporting.

### Issue 2: Abstract calculation and claimed implementation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Is a useful share-price result enough?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A useful, concrete and tangible result alone is insufficient; vacate the categorical eligibility ruling and require a claim-specific application of the inherited cases.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A useful, concrete and tangible result alone is insufficient; vacate the categorical eligibility ruling and require a claim-specific application of the inherited cases.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Benson concerned a useful computation and Flook a useful numerical update, so usefulness cannot do all of the work. Diehr protects an application considered as a whole, not a formula rescued merely by a field-of-use designation or routine output. Here the calculations allocate financial values; producing an accurate share price does not by itself identify the additional statutory application. Conversely, the claims cannot simply be treated as the cancelled methods: §112 limits their means elements to corresponding structure and equivalents. The court must determine that actual scope and whether the integrated system claims a practical machine implementation or effectively the accounting mathematics itself. The Federal Circuit’s sufficiency rule bypassed that decisive distinction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No later two-step patent test, categorical physical-transformation requirement or finding that all software is abstract.

### Issue 3: Further proceedings

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What should be decided now?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Remand for the proper §101 inquiry without automatically reinstating the district court’s categorical reasoning.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Remand for the proper §101 inquiry without automatically reinstating the district court’s categorical reasoning.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The district court’s business-method exclusion was too broad; the appellate court’s useful-result criterion was also too broad. Correcting both errors requires examination of the actual claims, specification and undisputed implementation. Markman governs that task without converting every technical finding into a universally de novo issue. Questions of novelty, obviousness, disclosure and infringement retain their separate elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding of ultimate validity or infringement.

# Department of the Army v. Blue Fox, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the lien claim against the Army.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lien claim against the Army shall be dismissed. The judgment does not foreclose a properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against a Miller Act surety, or relief supported by another unequivocal waiver. No Miller Act payment bond was obtained for this contract, so the general reservation of a properly founded alternative remedy does not establish an available surety recovery here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: APA waiver and equitable lien against federal funds

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves sovereign immunity before the equitable merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 702 does not authorize substitute monetary recovery from general federal funds. No identifiable retained fund belonging to Blue Fox or specific statutory payment obligation remains for the court to restore; the requested lien would instead attach federal property to replace the prime contractor's default and alter appropriations and creditor priorities without an unequivocal waiver.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When no identifiable retained fund or specific payment entitlement belonging to the claimant remains, § 702 does not waive federal sovereign immunity for an equitable lien whose function is substitute compensation from Government property for a private debtor's default.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Blue Fox seeks payment from Government-controlled property to make good the prime contractor's unpaid debt, not return of a segregated res or enforcement of an enacted payment obligation owed directly by the United States. *Bowen* is therefore materially different, and the Miller Act's remedial structure cannot be supplemented by an implied lien against general federal funds. The absence of a waiver controls even though the Government's administration contributed to the remedial gap. Nordic Village confirms why describing this claim as a lien cannot itself establish consent. The Army’s failure to require any bond explains the lack of a surety remedy here; no existing Miller Act surety is assumed. The actual disbursements and the absence of retained earned payments distinguish restitution of property belonging to Blue Fox from compensation for Verdan’s unpaid debt.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve the Army's conduct or hold that governmental wrongdoing is immaterial. It leaves specific relief concerning an identifiable retained fund, an enacted payment mandate, a properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against a Miller Act surety, and relief supported by another unequivocal waiver to their own governing law.

# Humana Inc. v. Forsyth

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit’s judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The RICO action may proceed subject to proof of every statutory element, causation, injury, and authorized remedy.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: McCarran-Ferguson reverse preemption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads impairment as conflict with the State’s chosen operation, not every addition of federal liability. Parallel enforcement is permissible when state law remains fully operative and no exclusive remedial bargain is displaced.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal RICO claim does not “invalidate, impair, or supersede” state insurance law when the federal claim targets conduct the State also forbids, leaves the state scheme intact, and adds compatible remedies rather than frustrating an exclusive state mechanism.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Nevada prohibited the alleged deception and did not grant insurers immunity or make administrative remedies exclusive. RICO therefore reinforces the same substantive policy. Greater damages alone do not establish statutory impairment. Barnett Bank’s insurance-specific exception cannot do the work here because RICO has no comparable insurance-specific subject matter. Applying Fabe therefore requires examination of Nevada’s actual policy. Nevada condemns the alleged concealed discount arrangement and does not make its administrative sanctions an exclusive substitute for private redress; the additional federal remedy on these allegations reinforces that policy rather than disabling it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A federal claim would be barred if it required conduct state law forbids, nullified a state-approved practice, or displaced an exclusive remedial structure demonstrated by enacted law.
