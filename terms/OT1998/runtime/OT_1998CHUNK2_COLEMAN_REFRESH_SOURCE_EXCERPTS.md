B. Applicable law 


The Eighth Amendment imposes a heightened 
standard of reliability on the procedures by which a capital 
sentencing decision is made. See Gilmore, 113 S. Ct. at 
2117; Hamilton, 17 F.3d at 1159. To this end, due process 
and the prohibition against crue] and unusual punishment 
require that the discretion of the capital sentencer be 
"suitably directed and limited so as to minimize the risk of 
wholly arbitrary and capricious action." Hamilton, 17 F.3d 
at 1159-60 (quoting Gregg v. Georgia, 428 U.S. 153, 189 
(1976)). A sentencer must thus make "an individualized 
determination on the basis of the character of the 
individual and the circumstances of the crime." Tuilaepa, 
114 S. Ct. at 2635 (quoting Zant, 462 U.S. at 878). This 
individualized review is a "constitutionally indispensable 
part of the process of inflicting the penalty of death." 
Woodson v. North Carolina, 428 U.S. 280, 304 (1976) 
(opinion of Stevens, Stewart and Powell, JJ.); see also 
Lockett _v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, 604 (1978) (opinion of 
Burger, C.J., Stewart, Powell and Stevens, JJ.). To satisfy 
this requirement, a capital sentencing procedure must 
allow the jury to consider all relevant mitigating evidence 
offered by the defendant. See Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 
302, 317 (1989); Lockett, 438 U.S. at 604; Woodson, 428 
US. at 304. 

A penalty-phase instruction violates the Eighth and 
Fourteenth Amendments if there is a reasonable 
likelihood that the jury applied the instruction in such a 
way that prevented consideration of relevant mitigating 


145 


evidence. See Boyde, 494 U.S. at 380; Hamilton, 17 F.3d 
at 1160; see also Carriger v. Lewis, 971 F.2d 329, 334 (9th 


Cir. 1992) (due process error if it is reasonably likely that 
the jury applied an instruction in a way that violates a 
constitutional right). An instruction may not "prevent[] the 
jury from giving a reasoned moral response to the 
petitioner’s mitigating evidence . . . or create[{] the risk 
that the death penalty would be imposed despite evidence

2118.
