## Reconciled result and scope

The five statutory reversal commitments survive: Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg construe the applicable section 2119 as three graded offenses. O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer would affirm on the penalty construction. All eight reach the presented statutory and associated constitutional questions; no participation, jurisdictional, preservation or vehicle defect is established.

Four Associates support a definitive constitutional proposition concerning the present offense: Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. Thomas joins statutory classification and the ordinary constitutional protections of the resulting elements, and accepts constitutional avoidance, but does not join that independent constitutional classification proposition. Their agreement on the judgment does not supply a fifth Associate constitutional join.

The fresh model's treatment of Stevens and Scalia beyond that common proposition is refined. Their historically explicit broader constitutional positions remain strong evidence where their premises survive. A narrower earlier published position is not, by itself, a rejection of a broader view later expressed. Stevens supports protection for facts increasing minimum as well as maximum punishment and facts necessary for death eligibility. Scalia supports protection for facts altering the legislatively prescribed penalty range, a formulation that also implicates mandatory minima; it cannot honestly be rendered as a maximum-only personal position. Neither Justice adopts a prior-conviction exception to his broader principle. Those broader positions do not acquire the joins of Souter, Ginsburg or Thomas, do not themselves overrule McMillan or Walton, and do not change the disposition of an unpresented case.

## Fixed record and remedy boundaries

The December 1992 offense is governed by the original firearm-possession enactment: fifteen years for the basic offense, twenty-five years if serious bodily injury results, and up to life if death results. The injury allegation was omitted from the indictment and not established by the jury's verdict; judicial injury factfinding supplied the asserted aggravated sentencing authority. The Ninth Circuit's 1997 judgment is reviewed. Oliver supplies the earlier record recitation; its earlier judgment and sentence are not substituted for the judgment presently reviewed. The neutral supplement resolves the stale court-identity warning.

Holloway supplies recent statutory methodology but concerns the later serious-harm-intent amendment. It does not insert that amendment into Jones. Death is part of the original statute's grading structure, not a factual finding that Jones caused death; the applicable death-result grade carries life, not the later death-penalty amendment. Neither a new jury waiver, admission, acquittal, harmless-error finding nor a new-prosecution entitlement is assumed.

For the five reversal proponents, reverse the Ninth Circuit's authorization of punishment under the injury grade, vacate the aggravated sentence and remand for lawful proceedings. On the existing indictment and verdict this carjacking conviction authorizes no more than the fifteen-year basic maximum. That ceiling is not an order to impose fifteen years, an aggregate-sentence calculation, an acquittal, or a ruling on every question concerning a properly charged further proceeding. For the three affirmance proponents, affirm the challenged injury-based sentencing authority against the presented classification objection, without automatically reinstating an earlier term or deciding unrelated errors. Monge's completed-insufficiency and retrial problem is absent here and supplies no independent remedy.

## Propositions kept separate

- **S — statutory grades:** Original section 2119 defines the basic offense and the aggravated injury and death offenses. The additional condition of each aggravated grade is an element.
- **A — consequences of statutory classification:** The federal indictment must allege the aggravated element; absent valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary admission to a fact does not by itself cure omission of a required element from the indictment. No such waiver or admission supplies the missing allegation or verdict here.
- **C — definitive present-offense maximum rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the charged offense that is necessary to authorize a greater statutory maximum than the charged elements and verdict otherwise permit performs the constitutional function of an element. Federal charging, jury and reasonable-doubt safeguards therefore apply, subject to valid admission and waiver rules; those rules do not cure this omitted charge by implication. Ordinary factfinding to select punishment within authority already supplied by the conviction remains distinct. This proposition does not determine bare prior-conviction status, unchanged-maximum minima, every Guidelines classification, or capital sentencing as a separate field.
- **U — broader maximum principle:** The same constitutional protection should apply to any fact necessary to enlarge the legislatively authorized maximum, without an exception merely because the fact is a prior conviction. This is a broader position of Stevens and Scalia; Souter and Ginsburg do not commit to its distinct prior-status application here. Adequate admission or other lawful proof can establish the charged status element without relitigating the validity of an existing conviction. Custis's collateral-review limits are not displaced.
- **M — broader penalty-range principle and existing minima law:** Stevens's position expressly includes minimum-increasing facts. Scalia's broader range-alteration position also reaches their constitutional significance; it is not confined to maxima. Neither position means that this case decides a mandatory-minimum application or overrules McMillan. Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg leave that separate question unresolved here; O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer support the existing distinction and reject the proposed categorical extension.
- **D — capital reach:** Stevens separately regards death-eligibility facts as within his constitutional principle and favors reconsidering contrary reasoning in Walton in an appropriate case. No other Justice's join to that distinct recommendation is supplied. No capital disposition or general capital rule is entered here.

These are direct proposition-level commitments, not an instruction to print all propositions as holdings. Assembly must distinguish a joined controlling proposition from a broader separate position, and a choice to leave a question open from agreement that the opposing answer is correct.

## Entering law and strongest competing paths

Almendarez-Torres in this timeline made the aggravated prior conviction a statutory reentry element, required a proper federal allegation, and confined the existing conviction to the basic maximum. Its avoidance explanation did not establish a general constitutional maximum-fact rule or a constitutional recidivism exception. The historical Jones majority's effort to distinguish a binding contrary recidivism holding, and the historical dissent's claim that such a holding controlled, cannot be transplanted.

Monge adds a genuine, later constitutional classification. Its Court opinion permits renewed lawful proof of this noncapital serious-prior qualification for double-jeopardy purposes despite an increased ceiling and despite the generic conviction's failure to establish the needed personal weapon use or infliction of great bodily injury. It is therefore not an already-proved-fact case. It expressly reserves independent jury, indictment and due-process claims and does not authorize all legislative relabeling. The four dissenters definitively treated that newly established personal-conduct qualification as an element for the finality question. Their actual public ground was confined to that qualification and completed failure of proof, not a universal maximum rule. Stevens added a distinct finality ground for genuine sentencing; the others did not join it.

**Element construction, tested against the penalty construction.** The common opening paragraph and dependent clauses are substantial evidence for one offense followed by punishment provisions. But their grammatical dependence does not decide whether the additional facts define aggravated grades; shared basic elements can serve each grade. The sharp increases in authorized punishment and traditional use of injury to distinguish aggravated robbery support the element reading. Section placement and the introductory command do not sufficiently displace that reading. On a fairly available element construction, serious constitutional doubt supports the statutory choice; Gaudin and Hamling then give the resulting elements their jury, proof and federal charging consequences.

**Penalty construction, tested against the element construction.** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer regard the opening firearm-taking definition as the complete crime and the result-based clauses as graded punishment. Victim harm has both offense-defining and sentencing uses; its importance and a higher maximum do not, in their view, by themselves resolve the classification. Their strongest objection is that this is current-offense injury, not recidivist character, and that no jury established the fact needed for the higher ceiling. They answer through their independent judgment about this complete offense and its consequence-based punishment scheme, informed by Patterson's room for legislative definition and Monge's actual rejection of maximum effect as conclusive for its classification. They do not say Monge decided the jury claim, that McMillan decided a higher-maximum case, or that Witte and Watts permit punishment beyond conviction-authorized authority. This is a defended present-injury classification, not transfer of a recidivism exception or deference to a label alone.

**Definitive C, tested against avoidance.** A fairly available statutory answer can dispose of the case, as Thomas maintains. But the four Monge dissenters already adopted a definitive constitutional element-function position for a previously unestablished conduct fact needed for greater punishment authority. Current-offense injury presents that same function without the disputed classification of an old conviction. Their support for C is an application of that published functional judgment together with Gaudin, not an assertion that Monge itself adjudicated a jury claim. Monge's binding double-jeopardy holding remains intact. The absence of the historical Almendarez-Torres constitutional recidivism holding also removes a specific intervening constitutional obstacle; it does not turn avoidance into a compulsory definitive decision.

**Breadth, tested against the actual earlier positions.** Neither the actual statutory Almendarez-Torres result nor Monge's bounded holdings negate Stevens's independent Winship, Mullaney, Patterson and Duncan rationale concerning facts that determine the permissible penalty range. His longstanding McMillan and Walton objections are not displaced. Scalia's historical Jones statement explicitly connected his broader range principle to his earlier opinions. He cannot now describe the actual Monge dissent as already announcing that universal principle; its exact scope was narrower. That requires an accurate explanation of development, not a fabricated repudiation of broader range protection. His actual element-function ground, the statutory protection already afforded prior status in Almendarez-Torres, and the absence of a general constitutional exception remain consistent with the broader personal view. Ordinary sentencing under Witte and Watts is not equivalent to the legislature making a new fact a legal condition for a different statutory range. Neither Justice's broader view supplies the other Justices' assent or silently displaces a still-operative precedent.

## Historical comparison and departure audit

The complete historical sources establish a statutory/avoidance reversal by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, and a penalty-construction dissent by O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer among the eight Associates. They also establish that Stevens and Scalia separately expressed definitive constitutional views broader than the historical Court's holding. The historical Court's notes 6 and 11 expressly limited its own decision to statutory construction despite discussing a possible maximum-fact rule; the historical Court's discussion distinguished recidivism and capital authorities. Authorship and historical joins are not copied into this Court's assignment or topology.

All eight reconciled judgment directions match their historical directions for independently reviewed reasons. Material differences in grounds and scope remain and must not disappear merely because judgment directions match:

**Historical departure:** Souter supports definitive C at the present-offense level, beyond the historical Court-level avoidance disposition. In this timeline his own Almendarez-Torres join helped establish statutory element protection instead of a contrary constitutional permission for recidivism, and his actual Monge join definitively treated a previously unestablished personal-conduct condition needed for a greater maximum as an element for finality. The absence of the historical binding constitutional recidivism premise permits that functional judgment to be applied directly to current-offense injury with Gaudin's jury consequence. It supports C, not U, minima or capital classification; statutory avoidance remains an acceptable sufficient opinion for him.

**Historical departure:** Ginsburg supports the same confined definitive C rather than only the historical Court-level avoidance account. Her actual Almendarez-Torres statutory/avoidance joins and later full Monge element-function join give a specific constitutional-function basis under a legal landscape lacking the historical recidivism permission. Her recent Holloway opinion also protects the enacted current-offense conditions without substituting general dangerousness. Those premises support the injury application; they do not justify assigning her every broader proposition Stevens or Scalia favors.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor retains affirmance but cannot rely on historical Almendarez-Torres as binding constitutional permission or on a wholly proved prior fact in Monge. Her actual reentry dissent remained noncontrolling, and her later full Monge join accepted an unproved qualifying-conduct fact and an enlarged ceiling while reserving jury claims. Her Jones ground therefore independently defends the complete current offense and its consequence-based penalty allocation. That is a changed legal basis, not a statement that the reserved claim was already decided.

**Historical departure:** Kennedy retains affirmance but his historical assertion that Almendarez-Torres foreclosed constitutional doubt is unavailable: its actual statutory judgment went the other way and created no general constitutional exception. His own actual reentry dissent, subsequent Monge join and preserved legislative-definition reasoning support the independent current-injury defense stated above, with no claim that recidivist classification automatically resolves victim injury.

**Historical departure:** Breyer retains affirmance while replacing the historical binding-Almendarez-Torres premise with his actual prior dissent, the statute's complete-offense structure and his later Monge join. The latter accepted a real missing personal-conduct qualification and a greater ceiling, so his constitutional analysis cannot rest on prior proof alone. The independently defended present-injury classification preserves his historical judgment without importing displaced law.

Stevens's and Scalia's personal breadth is preserved after testing the actual changed Monge premise; the provisional maximum-only treatment is not frozen. For Stevens, the historical minimum and death positions rested expressly on his independent due-process and jury authorities and longstanding objections, which the actual Monge finality decision did not displace. For Scalia, the historical assertion that Monge had already announced a universal range rule is unavailable, but the underlying functional reasoning and the broader view's consistency with his actual reentry position remain. He may express the broader conclusion now without falsely backdating it. No supported changed premise warrants treating either Justice as endorsing the constitutional validity of all unchanged-maximum minima. Conversely, their broader language does not establish Souter's, Ginsburg's or Thomas's assent, or automatically decide an unpresented application.

Thomas's statutory/avoidance support matches history. His actual Monge opinion provides an additional concrete reason to keep constitutional classification separate from the fairly available statutory resolution. There is no supported basis for turning his fifth statutory vote into a fifth definitive C vote.
