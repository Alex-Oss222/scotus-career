# Jones — form and proposition determination before assignment count

The event is the March 24, 1999 plenary merits disposition of No. 97-6203. The supported form is one signed lead opinion resolving the original section 2119's graded offenses, the ordinary indictment and jury consequences of those statutory elements, and the bounded sentencing remand. These are one statutory rationale, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. No division of that lead is necessary.

The distinct definitive constitutional current-offense maximum proposition receives four decisional joins: Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. A common concurrence states that independent ground and its limits. Thomas does not adopt it. Stone's approved fixed core expressly chooses the sufficient statutory ground; his maintained broader constitutional position is preserved as his personal position without adding an alternative constitutional ground to his disposition or joining that concurrence. His supplement's explicit statutory-analogue and noninherited-majority statements control its stale description of Almendarez-Torres as already supplying a general constitutional rule. This does not change his chosen ground, remedy or maintained position.

The broader range principle of Stevens and Scalia, including minima and no categorical prior-conviction exception, requires separate substantive coverage. A shared broader concurrence can state that common ground, with a severable Stevens-only discussion of death eligibility and reconsideration of Walton. Their broader views do not acquire the common concurrence's other joins. Stone's short concurrence preserves his maintained constitutional position while relying on the statutory disposition; it supplies no new ruling on prior status, minima or capital questions. One dissent states the independent statute-specific penalty construction and constitutional allocation accepted by O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.

The judgment is reversal of the reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it authorizes the aggravated injury sentence, vacatur of that sentence and remand, six to three. The existing indictment and verdict authorize at most the fifteen-year basic carjacking maximum; this is not a fixed substitute sentence, aggregate calculation, acquittal or automatic new-prosecution entitlement. The Court does not reinstate an earlier sentence or change the statutory version.

These choices precede consideration of author counts and do not depend on the identity of an author. No new Associate commitment, fictional circulation, broadened join or historical authorship is assumed.
