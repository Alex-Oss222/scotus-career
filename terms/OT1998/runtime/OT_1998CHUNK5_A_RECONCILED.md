## Minns v. United States — No. 98-903

**Reconciled disposition:** Preserve the frozen four/four division on the children's execution allegations. Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg sustain dismissal to the extent adjudication would select protected military policy, but vacate categorical dismissal of separable storage, administration and warning-execution allegations and remand for the actual Berkovitz/Gaubert inquiry. O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer affirm the children's dismissal under section 2680(a) on the pleaded policy-dependent claims. These are eight Associate positions, not a complete-Court tie or majority.

**Historical comparison:** There is no Supreme Court merits comparator. The Fourth Circuit's affirmance is a reviewed lower judgment, not an eight-Justice historical poll. Its separate Feres and discretionary-function grounds were already supplied to the neutral/model stage and have each been answered. No historical merits departure is asserted or invented.

**Proposition-level positions:** Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg reject a Feres bar resting merely on the military causal genesis of a child's own bodily injury. O'Connor applies Feres to these challenges necessarily implicating service preparation; Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer reserve it. All eight recognize immunity for actual protected discretionary policy selection. The four remand proponents require identification of the conduct, any actual mandatory direction, permitted discretion and protected policy character; they establish no binding directive, policy-responsibility fact, independent state duty, negligence, causation or compensation. Scalia's officer-responsibility qualification rests on his Gaubert concurrence and does not become an additional majority element through a common remand. His Johnson dissent and Stevens's join resist expansion of the nontextual bar; Ginsburg's Lombard dissent supplies her personal-injury/derivative-loss distinction without importing its elapsed time or new-knowledge facts. The respective Aktepe positions supply the current, claim-specific execution/policy distinction, not a Feres holding.

**Remedy and barriers:** Preserve every frozen limitation: no guaranteed discovery, compulsory officer testimony, adjudication of sections 2680(j) or (k), Walsh venue decision, enlargement to wives' claims, or revision of the MCA branch. An opinion making military subject matter alone decisive would not carry these commitments. No author is selected.

## Arizona Department of Revenue v. Blaze Construction — No. 97-1536

**Reconciled disposition:** All eight Associates reverse the blanket preemption judgment and remand. No judgment change.

**Comparator read:** The complete Cornell syllabus and Court opinion, including all three notes, establish unanimous historical reversal/remand under New Mexico's independent-federal-contract rule. The premise was that the transaction was between the Federal Government and its independent nonmember contractor, with legal incidence on that contractor, no constitutional immunity and no congressional exemption. The opinion declined Bracker/Ramah interest balancing for that transaction. It separately retained the different setting of tribal contracting and direct tribal administration. Historical authorship is not imported.

**Rationale reconciliation:** The frozen hypothetical particularized-interest discussion is adversarial testing, not a second operative test or an independently adopted alternative holding. No changed premise supports converting these ordinary federal contracts into a Bracker/Ramah balancing case. All eight adopt the federal-contract rule: reservation location, federal supervision, reduced federal purchasing power and tribal benefit do not exempt an independent federal contractor from this nondiscriminatory receipts tax absent the applicable constitutional immunity or congressional exemption. Genuine federal incorporation, discriminatory taxation, direct federal or tribal incidence, and contracts with tribes remain separate questions. A controlling congressional provision must of course be honored; that reservation does not authorize fresh interest balancing in these same ordinary federal contracts. No finding of an exact road-mile loss or a project-specific state-service equivalence is necessary.

**Individual reconciliation:** Stevens's Bracker/Ramah dissents and Cotton opinion support the rule and rejection of indirect burden alone. O'Connor's Ramah majority join addresses the distinct tribal undertaking; her Cotton join and California opinion support the actual federal-contract/incidence distinction. Scalia and Kennedy's Cotton joins supply the same distinction. Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer's Chickasaw positions protect actual tribal incidence while preserving examination of a different nonmember tax; they do not prescribe balancing for every such tax. Current California and Chickasaw law does not displace the historical New Mexico premise for any Associate. The eight historical joins confirm, rather than replace, this supported application.

**Specific-policy arguments and remedy retained:** The transaction-period regulations constrain BIA sanctions against tribal contracting choices; they do not assure identical state-tax consequences for all contracting arrangements. The Buy Indian preference does not itself exempt the winning contractor or require universal state-tax equivalence between affiliated and nonaffiliated firms. These textual answers identify the absence of the claimed exemption, not an alternative balancing route. Credits for subcontractor and supplier taxes remain undecided on remand; excluded tribal-housing contracts stay excluded. No net assessment, tax-discrimination holding or other tribal-status classification is entered. All eight can join this formulation in full. No separate writing is needed merely to repeat it; no author is selected.

**Departure assessment:** None on judgment or the governing federal-contract rule. The fuller transaction-period text and credit reservation answer record issues the shorter historical opinion did not adjudicate; they do not supply a contrary legal result.

## Peguero v. United States — No. 97-9217

**Reconciled disposition:** All eight Associates reach the certified Rule 32 claim and affirm. The frozen issued-certificate/eligibility distinction and its limitations remain. The actual judicial certificate admitted this identified question; the government's challenge to the substantial-constitutional-showing assessment does not establish that no judicial permission existed. Government concurrence or waiver is not the source of jurisdiction. Neither Hohn nor this ruling authorizes future bare-rule certificates without section 2253(c)(2)'s showing, reopens the rejected counsel claim, or establishes that Peguero made that showing.

**Comparator read:** The complete Cornell syllabus, Court opinion and O'Connor concurrence were read. The historical Court unanimously denied collateral relief because independent actual knowledge meant the omission caused no loss of an appeal. It retained the judge's mandatory advice duty, distinguished Hill/Timmreck's consequential-defect requirement from technical noncompliance, and distinguished Rodriquez's actual lost appeal. The historical opinion did not decide the government's issued-certificate challenge expressly. Silence is neither a contrary jurisdiction holding nor authority to skip the threshold here. The unchanged knowledge/cooperation findings strongly confirm the frozen merits position of each Associate.

**Individual reconciliation:** Stevens's Hohn join and Bousley separate writing preserve review of judicial business and the distinction between constitutional injury and formal rule error. O'Connor's Hohn dissent requires permission before an appeal; that permission actually issued here. Scalia and Thomas's Hohn dissents likewise distinguish permission from the merits appeal, and their Bousley positions resist automatic collateral relief. Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Hohn/Bousley positions preserve the distinct channel, claim, default and remedy inquiries. No opposite historical threshold conclusion or changed factual premise is supplied.

**Prejudice and writings:** Actual knowledge and the unappealed findings defeat this claim; no universal clear-and-convincing burden, presumption about cooperators, or resolution of future disputed-knowledge procedure is adopted. Causal loss of the opportunity to appeal must not be confused with an appeal's likely success. All eight can join that distinction and the confined disposition. The historical concurrence of O'Connor, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer adds a more explicit future-case point: a defendant who actually loses the appeal because of the judge's omission should not have to identify meritorious grounds before obtaining restoration, since that would deny the lawyer-assisted appellate process the omission prevented. Their frozen concerns are satisfied if the lead formulation expressly preserves that distinction; a substantive clarification remains available if the lead leaves its future application reserved. Historical publication does not require duplicating a separate writing, nor does the historical lead's narrower formulation establish an objection by its other joiners. Do not decide all restoration conditions in a case where no appeal is restored.

**Remedy:** No resentencing, renewed appeal or new hearing; the sentence remains. Whether a hypothetical restored appeal could extend beyond sentencing is expressly undecided. The advice remains mandatory. No author is assigned and no current-case historical opinion becomes authority in the new decision.

**Departure assessment:** No judgment departure. The express threshold treatment answers the existing supported government objection under current Hohn; it is not an invented historical jurisdictional position or later-law import.

## Cedar Rapids Community School District v. Garret F. — No. 96-1793

**Reconciled disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm on the federal physician/nonphysician rule. Kennedy and Thomas reject that federal coverage construction and would vacate the federal affirmance and remand for the undecided Iowa-law basis, including the claimed higher state standard through IDEA. Scalia and Kennedy's provisional rows are revised after substantive historical review. All exact-award, agency, staffing and state-ground limitations of the frozen handoff remain.

**Comparator read in full:** The Cornell syllabus, Court opinion with all ten notes, and Thomas dissent with all three notes establish that Scalia historically joined the Court, whereas Kennedy joined Thomas's dissent. The majority applied Tatro's settled construction, refused the proposed cost/continuity/staffing exception and treated access services as related services. The dissent attacked Tatro's provider-specific reading, its inference from the regulation defining included physician services, and lack of clear funding notice. Its alternative would confine nursing obligations to services nurses could perform as part of normal duties. The majority's notes addressed the agency uncertainty, alternative ALJ findings, Iowa law and disputed future staffing expense. These were not newly discovered neutral facts; the frozen neutral packet already presented each material point.

### Why the provisional swap is not supportable

For **Scalia**, the provisional statutory/notice dissent drew on Suter, his narrower Franklin ground, Carter and Auer. None changes the historical Tatro proposition or the text of the medical-services exception. Suter concerns whether a particular federal right or implied action exists; Carter preserves actual IDEA predicates and equitable reimbursement; neither converts included school-health services into excluded physician services. Franklin's narrower remedial ground is consistent with requiring notice of a condition, but it does not decide this particular service obligation. Scalia's Auer join allows text-consistent fair agency interpretation and does not imply rejection of the existing Tatro construction. The historical join directly answers the same included-only regulatory argument and continuity/cost objection on unchanged premises. After comparing those grounds, Scalia joins the six-Associate physician-boundary analysis and affirms. No newly discovered source fact or remodeled neutral premise is necessary for this correction of an unsupported provisional inference.

For **Kennedy**, the provisional affirmance relied on the same Carter, Suter, New York and Auer distinctions and on the specified nature of the IDEA duty. Those sources allow his statutory/clear-notice disagreement; they do not establish that he accepts Tatro's inference as exhaustive for continuing medical care. Kennedy's historical join in the full dissent is strong issue-specific evidence because that dissent already confronted the statutory wording, regulatory inference, educational setting and funding concern. Current Gebser differs from historical recipient-attribution doctrine, but preserves Franklin's intentional-discrimination remedy and does not remove Pennhurst notice or decide IDEA's medical exclusion. It therefore supplies no concrete reason for Kennedy to abandon the medical-service and funding-notice objections. Kennedy joins Thomas's adverse federal construction and the exact, bounded Iowa-remand position set out below. The mere availability of an arguable majority rationale is not a changed premise.
