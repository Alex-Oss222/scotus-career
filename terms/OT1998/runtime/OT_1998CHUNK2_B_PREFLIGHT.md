## AT&T Universal Card Services v. Rembert, No. 98-352

### Verified neutral corrections and additions

The judgment below is the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of the district court's reversal of the bankruptcy court. The bankruptcy court found the card debts nondischargeable; the district court reversed, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed dischargeability. The packet's compressed wording suggesting a bankruptcy judgment rejecting nondischargeability must not be carried forward. The appellate opinion was decided April 9, 1998; May 29 was rehearing denial. The cited simulated Field decision is November 28, 1995, not November 6.

The published appellate opinion, including Judge Krupansky's concurrence and all three footnotes, was read. Rembert's reduced income followed an accident and lost overtime. She used casino cash advances, obtained a $28,000 second mortgage, paid off identified balances in November 1994, resumed borrowing, and made further substantial payments. Her Chapter 7 petition was filed April 20, 1995. The opinion recites her testimony that she intended to pay with winnings and states that the creditors supplied no evidence of contemporaneous intent not to repay. That is the appellate court's account of the tried record; it is not a new Supreme Court credibility finding.

The bankruptcy judge's words quoted at J.A. 673–74 tie inability and intent to the objective unreasonableness of gambling expectations. The published recitation supports examination of that legal premise without a claim to have recovered the complete trial transcript. Appellate clear-error review of factual findings remains distinct from independent review of the governing fraud standard. The opinion's conclusion that the stated finding was clearly erroneous is not permission to disregard supported credibility findings in another case.

Citibank participated in the consolidated lower proceedings, and its repayment history is part of the recited evidence. The supplied Supreme Court matter names AT&T only and establishes no additional companion grant. Do not create a separate Citibank petition or adjudicate an independently unreviewed judgment merely because the lower opinion discussed both creditors.

The creditor's strongest available argument is that repetitive advances despite manifest insolvency and gambling losses permit an inference that an apparent repayment promise was knowingly false. The debtor's answer is that subjective intent remains the element, and her substantial actual repayments corroborate testimony rather than leaving a bare self-serving denial. These positions do not establish a gambling exemption or immunize testimony from ordinary credibility assessment.

Krupansky's concurrence raises a separate reliance question: whether Ward imposes routine or periodic issuer investigation. That is not a finding that these creditors did or did not investigate, nor was reliance decided by the panel after failure of proof on fraudulent intent. Field supplies no universal duty to investigate. Recipient-specific knowledge, obvious falsity and actually encountered warnings remain relevant; automatic reliance or automatic unjustifiability cannot be inferred from issuance or nonrevocation of a credit card.

### Law entering and lawful paths

The governing register is **Actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud discharge exception**. Present operation effective: In re Cohen, June 30, 1997, for debt extent; Field's reliance standard remains effective November 28, 1995. Its current components separate fraud, actual reliance, recipient-specific justifiability, obtained-by causation and connected debt extent. Field Part I is unanimous; its seven-Justice Part II requires correct-standard lower-court application where the record does not resolve it. Scalia and Breyer's published Field dissent accepts the rule but finds that record already sufficient under it.

The official 1994 §523 text verifies (a)(2)(A)'s exception for fraud and exclusion of statements respecting financial condition, and (B)'s separate materially false writing, reasonable reliance and deceptive-publication conditions. Grogan supplies preponderance, not a heightened burden. In re Cohen preserves the necessary fraud predicate before debt extent. No supplied current-term holding changes those rules.

Supported paths are affirmance of the dischargeability judgment for failed proof of actual intent; or correct-standard remand if supported independent findings leave a genuine unresolved application. Objective hopelessness alone cannot replace actual fraudulent intent. Card use can communicate present intent to repay without warranting solvency. Circumstantial evidence remains admissible; no twelve-factor counting rule, confession requirement, or additional requirement of a plan to file bankruptcy follows. A broader theory of representation-only or false-pretense liability is outside the presented actual-fraud question.

The 1994 version of §523(a)(2)(C) separately supplies specified recent-debt presumptions. The reported last advances were in January and the petition in April; no presented claim establishes the then-applicable sixty-day condition. This case does not rewrite that statutory presumption or decide a new fee claim under §523(d). Failure on intent makes reliance, causation and amount unnecessary here without abolishing them elsewhere.
