### Issue-specific legal conclusions and limits

**Review channel and successive classification.** All eight adopt Martinez-Villareal Part I: a distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary is reviewable; an actual §2244(b)(3) authorization grant or denial is not ordinarily reviewable through certiorari. The packet does not identify the later panel's exact classification, so no Justice asserts that a particular authorization grant has been reversed. The State's application and challenge to the separate injunction are considered within their actual channel; a certiorari grant cannot evade the authorization-review bar. Any final disposition resting on review of a particular classification must first identify that actual classification.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would extend Martinez-Villareal's first-merits principle to the particular previously presented method claim that the appellate court declined to consider as unripe until the prisoner chose the method. This is a proposed extension beyond the existing confined Ford holding, not a claim that controlling law already covers every late-arising claim. The relevant reason is judicial refusal of earlier consideration for prematurity, not the subject matter's capital character, a new constitutional rule, or a general exemption for nonmerits denials. The absence of the literal express-preservation formulation is not decisive for their proposed extension when the appellate ruling itself withheld consideration for the identified impediment. They do not find that the earlier default was correct, incorrect or finally erased; that separate issue remains available on its actual predicates.

Scalia and Thomas oppose that extension and regard this renewed gas application as subject to §2244(b), assuming it is the later application described rather than an actually continuing initial proceeding. Their Martinez-Villareal dissents rejected even its confined non-successive conclusion; that precedent binds them in its stated Ford/presentation/preservation category but supplies no broader method exception. If this is the already-presented claim in a new successive application, §2244(b)(1) requires dismissal regardless of new-claim exceptions; they do not treat §2244(b)(2)'s exceptions as relief from paragraph (1). A genuinely new claim would require paragraph (2)'s separate prerequisites. No reliable new guilt-innocence evidence or existing new rule made retroactive is established by a method objection alone. Their final classification-dependent disposition remains conditional on the actual reviewable channel and application character, not on an invented missing authorization filing.

O'Connor and Kennedy reserve a final extension or classification decision. Their joins in the confined Martinez-Villareal rule make the prior prematurity ruling a substantial distinction, but they need not decide its extension to resolve the independent equitable request on the favorable assumption that a procedural route remains open. The controlling non-successive result is not automatically enlarged; the restrictive text is not automatically applied solely because more than one filing exists.

**Default and independent ineffective assistance.** All eight retain Coleman, Carrier and McCleskey's actual adequate-independent-default, external-cause, prejudice and exception inquiries. All joined Trest, which rejects default as a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect and a mandatory duty to invent an omitted defense. The gas district default and March 2 state bar on the distinct ineffective-assistance claim are reported rulings. Neither possession of those citations nor the certificate denial establishes every adequacy, independence or cause predicate. The prior appellate gas-prematurity ruling supplies a concrete competing procedural consideration, but not automatic cause for an earlier state default and not a final adjudication nullifying it. An unexcused barred ineffective-assistance claim cannot be treated as an established external cause merely because it alleges lawyer error; the cause route and any exception require their own predicates. No Justice's present action depends on finding counsel constitutionally ineffective, finding actual cause absent, using a guilt gateway to decide method legality, or adding a final default ruling not supported by the supplied record.

The added source identifies a concrete error in the actual lower rationale, rather than an unknown reason: the Walter panel adopted the conclusion that there had been no legal or factual basis for a gas claim during original review, with prejudice drawn from a later circuit gas ruling. **O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas** reject the general no-basis premise as a sufficient cause ground. Reed requires a basis not reasonably available; Engle rejects mere futility; Carrier distinguishes a genuine external impediment from counsel's failure to use an available claim. Gray v. Lucas in 1983 actually litigated the general cyanide-gas theory with eyewitness and medical materials. That establishes availability of the generic theory before 1987 without transferring Mississippi protocol findings to Arizona or excluding a genuinely different later factual predicate. A favorable later circuit decision is not necessary to raise the available theory. Because gas was the sole method during the earlier state proceedings, the later election-based ripeness ruling does not excuse omission then. Prejudice from a subsequently recognized violation cannot replace cause.

Those five would vacate the gas injunction and remand for a lawful, claim-specific disposition free of that cause error. This is a sufficient correction of the identified ground for present relief, not a final judgment that every state bar is adequate or every possible external-cause allegation fails. Any actually presented independent ineffective-assistance route must be tested separately, including its state procedural bar and any supported exception; it is neither automatically established cause nor automatically an unexhausted claim. The remand permits any independently justified lawful interim order under Bowersox and Lonchar, but grants no substitute stay or automatic interval. The Court's correction does not itself require an evidentiary hearing.

**Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer** agree that unfavorable precedent and later prematurity alone do not establish cause, but oppose removing protection on this emergency record. Their strongest objection is that a general earlier gas challenge does not prove the availability of every materially developed factual basis of Walter's claim or settle the independent lawyer-error route. They would permit prompt reconsideration of cause under the correct available-basis rule while retaining the existing gas-only injunction on its actual terms. They do not endorse the appellate court's categorical no-basis conclusion merely because it supports protection; their disagreement concerns the remedy and the unresolved claim-specific inquiry. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas answer that the existing injunction's identified cause rationale cannot remain in force merely because another lawful ground might ultimately be established; reentry of properly justified relief remains possible.

**Teague.** No final nonretroactivity ruling is adopted. Direct review was completed in 1987; the later federal habeas-review date does not reset finality. Lambrix permits an accepted Teague issue to precede unresolved default; Schiro permits declining a late defense; Caspari separately addresses a preserved fairly included subsidiary defense. The requested prohibition of gas and the lower court's categorical capital-nonwaivability rationale are identifiable, but the exact operative constitutional formulation, preservation of a Teague defense and application of the contemporary exceptions are not settled by those descriptions. Current Gray v. Netherland requires a dictated-at-finality inquiry into the actual protected function and preserves substantive restrictions and watershed-procedure exceptions; its different preparation-opportunity holding is not a universal old-rule exemption. A pre-1987 gas claim's reasonable availability for cause is different from whether the particular rule granting relief was dictated at finality. All eight reserve that application because their present dispositions do not require it, not because finality is unknown or the State is deemed to have forfeited the defense. No later retroactivity doctrine is imported.

**AEDPA timing, factual restrictions and certificate.** All eight apply the provision-specific Lindh/Carriger principle, as they did in current Coleman IV. The initial preenactment case does not exempt a genuinely new application from every AEDPA provision, and capital status does not establish chapter 154 qualification. A premature-claim classification does not silently decide the temporal treatment of every certificate or factual-development provision. The later circuit certificate is an actual fact; it is neither a merits writ nor a substitute for authorization. No State certificate is required. Hohn supplies review of judicial certificate decisions without itself granting a certificate. The March 2 procedural ruling is not transformed into a state merits adjudication under §2254(d). No hearing entitlement or §2254(e)(2) satisfaction is found. No Justice rescinds the certificate as a necessary consequence of the present application decision.

**Election, equity and waiver.** The repeated gas election after an opportunity to reconsider is relevant to equity independently of a definitive constitutional waiver. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate on a confined application of Gomez and Lonchar's preserved independent equity: after the renewed opportunity to avoid the selected method, the present gas-only restraint is not justified by the election itself and the need to litigate its consequences. They assume a procedurally open claim favorably, recognize the prior prematurity ruling as a reason the renewed claim arrived later, and do not characterize the entire delay as unexplained. Their decisive equitable circumstance is the renewed choice when avoidance of the particular challenged method was offered, rather than the mere age of the claim or a finding of abusive motive. They treat this as sufficient to remove the particular interim restraint under the application review standard, without permanently extinguishing the constitutional claim. The choice is not pronounced a knowing waiver of every Eighth Amendment objection; no advice, exact waiver wording, or universal substitute-method requirement is supplied.

The quoted Arizona provision applies to a death-sentenced defendant whose offense preceded November 23, 1992, requires selection of injection or gas at least twenty days before execution, and makes injection the default if no choice is made. The majority establishes that Walter belongs to the optional class. Neither the reported election nor the Governor's later offer establishes every original deadline or election-condition fact; no untimeliness, compliance finding or construction of a reconsideration entitlement is added. The narrower method option is relevant to equity, not a holding that the statute authorizes an unconstitutional punishment or that any available alternative is categorically lawful.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject that independent equitable application. The appellate court previously made election the condition for considering gas. It is therefore a substantial objection to using the election and resulting late posture as an independent reason to remove method-specific protection. Their proposed narrow Martinez-Villareal extension identifies a first merits opportunity on this claim, not a stay from filing alone. Souter nevertheless votes for vacatur on the separate demonstrated cause error: reachability under §2244 is not excuse from a real state default, and an initial certified claim still requires a legally available ground. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would retain gas-only protection during prompt proper consideration of the specific cause issues. Certification alone would not suffice, a clearly applicable default or successive barrier could dispose of the matter, and no new hearing or merits step is demanded beyond what law actually requires.

The current panel's categorical capital-Eighth-nonwaivability rationale does not become this Court's rule. The claim concerns whether the challenged punishment may be imposed after this election, not merely whether a prisoner can waive appellate process. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reject the categorical rule as an obstacle to their independent equitable assessment without deciding that these facts establish a knowing constitutional waiver. Souter expressly withholds a final nonwaivability or waiver judgment because the default-ground correction suffices. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer preserve the contention that choice cannot itself authorize an unconstitutional punishment, but do not declare that every Eighth Amendment protection is incapable of waiver. No legal rule is inferred solely from a failure to recover the election form or advice record.
