## Additional entering law: Monge

The [mechanically copied published Monge entry](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK6_MONGE_PUBLIC.md), effective June 26, 1998, is material entering law. Its counterpart in the operative register is **Renewed proof of noncapital recidivist status**, present operation effective June 26, 1998. The [compact refreshed Standards slice](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK6_JONES_REFRESH_STANDARDS.md) mechanically copies that rule and the existing Almendarez-Torres, Gaudin and Watts/Witte rules, without altering their text.

Monge's five-Justice Court opinion by Thomas, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, classifies the particular inquiry into the serious character of a prior conviction as a noncapital recidivist-status determination for double-jeopardy purposes. It permits another lawful attempt to prove the required personal-conduct qualification after insufficient proof; it does not reinstate the unsupported finding or require an enhancement. The Court expressly accepts that the generic old conviction did not necessarily establish the required personal weapon use or infliction of great bodily injury and that the qualification increased the available maximum. Its classification therefore cannot be reduced to a proposition that only facts conclusively established in the old conviction were at issue.

That holding does not decide an independent indictment, jury or due-process claim. It adopts neither a general constitutional prior-conviction exception nor a rule for all facts that enlarge a maximum. Nor does it make a legislative sentencing label dispositive. It preserves offense-acquittal protection, the capital acquittal-like exception, ordinary safeguards in any renewed proceeding, and the separate convictions and prison-term enhancement.

## Relationship to the other entering authorities

- **Almendarez-Torres — Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum, effective March 24, 1998.** Its statutory construction, charging rule and bounded remedy remain controlling. Monge expressly preserves them and explains that construction of federal reentry law does not decide California's different constitutional classification. The four-Justice avoidance rationale and the Chief's definitive constitutional ground still do not combine into a general constitutional Court holding.
- **Gaudin — Jury determination of criminal materiality, effective June 19, 1995.** All nine adopted the jury's reasonable-doubt determination of an actual criminal element. It controls the consequences of classification without itself classifying every penalty condition. Monge reserves the independent jury question rather than modifying Gaudin.
- **Watts / Putra and Witte — Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution.** The ordinary acquitted-conduct component is effective January 6, 1997; Witte's uncharged-conduct and successive-prosecution boundaries survive. Both concern punishment within conviction-authorized authority. Monge expressly rejects treating them as an independent answer to its increased-ceiling classification and permits no proof repair after an actual protected offense acquittal.
- **McMillan.** Its surviving mandatory-minimum holding leaves the statutory maximum unchanged. Monge and Almendarez-Torres preserve that limited scope rather than treating it as controlling every increased maximum.

The neutral inquiry must therefore distinguish statutory offense definition, constitutional classification for the protection actually asserted, allocation of an established element, and the consequences of a completed acquittal or insufficient-proof adjudication. The present Jones record supplies no new Monge-style completed failure of proof or double-jeopardy claim merely because Monge is relevant authority.

## Neutral paths and limits

The original statutory alternatives remain: a graded-offense construction with indictment and jury consequences, or a penalty construction supported by the opening offense paragraph and dependent sentencing structure. Monge adds a necessary comparison: a qualification concerning the serious character of an earlier conviction differs in subject from victim injury during the current offense, but the legal significance of that difference must be assessed under the actual statutory wording and constitutional claim. The mere presence of a higher maximum supplies neither an already controlling universal element rule nor automatic permission for judicial factfinding.

A statutory element construction may resolve the case without a general constitutional ruling. A constitutional conclusion, if necessary and supported, must identify its actual scope and the preserved constitutional question rather than transplanting double-jeopardy finality wholesale into an indictment or jury claim. A penalty construction must address the relevant doubts and the date-eligible public element-function positions; Monge's reserved jury and indictment questions cannot be described as already resolved. No direction on the eventual result is supplied here.
