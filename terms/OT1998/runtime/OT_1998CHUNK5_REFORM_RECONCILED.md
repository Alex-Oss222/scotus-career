## Substantive reconciliation and alternatives

**Funding and the actual contribution claim.** The strongest opposing path would treat the pleaded combined system as a substantial impairment of effective advocacy despite formal permission to supplement. That path is legally available when the complaint supplies a distinguishing practical burden; it is not defeated by the word subsidy, voluntary enrollment or the existence of a private-contribution route. Here the supported account reports the inability to close the subsidy gap and does not supply that different impairment. The independent commitments accept the alleged amounts and inability, then apply Buckley's directly pertinent treatment. They do not infer that the program can never suppress competition or demand summary-judgment evidence at dismissal.

The actual statute is essential. Major-party status begins at 25 percent of the prior presidential vote; minor-party status begins at five percent and remains below 25 percent. The returning-candidate route in section 9004(a)(2)(B) and five-percent current-election route in section 9004(a)(3) retain their eligibility, ratio, offset and credit conditions. New parties are not permanently excluded. Section 9003(c) permits minor/new-party qualified expenses up to the **major-party entitlement**, with private supplementation within its separate certifications. It does not cap total expenses at the smaller subsidy. Section 9004(b)'s net-payment limits, subsection (c)'s qualified-use and specified restoration/repayment limits, and subsection (d)'s combined candidate/immediate-family certification remain distinct. None proves fundraising success, supplies an unreported eligibility finding or disposes automatically of F3.

Florida establishes that each Associate accepts objective demonstrated-support benefits with burden-sensitive limits; its unrestricted-spending facts are not transplanted here. Day's intermediary rule and Kentucky's annual all-intermediary remand preserve meaningful pooling and fit, but concern a different recipient class and aggregate mechanism. Kentucky's direct-$1,000 holding confirms adopted contribution doctrine at its preserved facial scope, not a permanent amount or a concession by Reform. Its sourcewide candidate-receipt disagreement concerns the additional exclusion of later donors when a class allowance is exhausted. Colorado's independent-advertisement holding and broader three-Justice party-expression proposal preserve actual transfers and genuine conduits. None erases the separate analysis of an individual's direct payment to a candidate.

**Affiliation and standing.** The strongest opposing path is injury to equal appointment opportunity under Northeastern Florida. It does not require certainty of ultimate selection. Yet section 437c(a)(1) neither names the two major parties nor reserves them seats, and no concrete opportunity barred by this maximum is supplied. The reported appointment practice is not the statute's command. Dismissal therefore rests on missing likely redress at this scope, not on a universal proposition that retaining the same officials defeats every structural challenge. Legi-Tech requires actual lawful reconsideration after unconstitutional participation; it is preserved because this case supplies no particular defective enforcement adjudication. Printed ex-officio language does not restore participation already displaced by that law.

**Channels.** A genuine disagreement finding F2 or F3 substantial would change the remedy to the correct statutory tribunal; it would not warrant selecting a replacement subsidy or contribution amount. The present commitments instead identify the particular theories as foreclosed, without factual weighing. No new damages claim, appointment order, payment award, election replay, independent-advertisement controversy, mandatory discovery or new evidentiary hearing enters the case. Ordinary mandate procedure follows.
