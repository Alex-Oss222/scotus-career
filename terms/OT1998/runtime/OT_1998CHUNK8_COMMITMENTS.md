## Clinton v. Goldsmith — No. 98-347

### Reach and decisional alternatives

The review concerns CAAF's injunction against the administrative drop from the rolls. The final court-martial imposed six years' confinement and monthly forfeiture, **not dismissal**; the military appellate affirmance became final without Goldsmith seeking CAAF review. The later administrative act is distinct from those findings and sentence. The original HIV-medication petition does not turn the subsequent personnel claim into a direct appeal of a finding or sentence.

**Path G1:** Reverse the challenged CAAF judgment and vacate the drop injunction because section 1651 aids conferred jurisdiction but does not extend CAAF's findings-and-sentence jurisdiction to this separate executive personnel act. Preserve constitutional merits and independently authorized other forums. The strongest contrary argument is that changing military status because of the conviction impairs the benefit of a judgment that did not dismiss the officer and therefore warrants ancillary protection. It fails because a sentence's omission of dismissal does not itself adjudicate an entitlement against a different statutory personnel action; finality and CAAF's defined review subject remain material.

**Path G2:** Sustain the injunction as protection of an existing or potential military-appellate judgment. Its strongest support is the writ's established capacity to protect jurisdiction against indirect frustration rather than merely correct a document on appeal. Its decisive difficulty is the absence of a threatened matter within CAAF's actual statutory review jurisdiction: the writ would supply a new administrative subject, not preserve the existing one. McBryde permitted correction of district orders implementing transfers in cases within potential appellate review; that nexus is absent here. The existence or adequacy of another remedy cannot repair it.

## Hunt v. Cromartie — No. 98-85

### Reach, controlling baseline and alternatives

Direct appeal lies from the three-judge court's merits judgment; the review is not restricted by a nonexistent certiorari question. The challenged district's residents have the personal standing established in the neutral packet. Standing does not decide whether their merits theory succeeds. The district court's findings on predominance, political explanation and traditional criteria were entered on cross-motions before discovery or an evidentiary hearing; they receive summary-judgment treatment, not deference as trial findings.

**Path H1 — enforce current law:** Vacate the summary judgment and its dependent districting relief. Under Shaw and Johnson, racial predominance in the common territorial electorate, even with subordinated ordinary criteria, is insufficient by itself; a trial solely to choose race or party cannot cure that legal deficiency. Remand for a conforming disposition rejecting the design-only theory and ordinary treatment of any genuinely presented independently cognizable claim. Such a claim does not require a second act, later electoral loss or financial harm, but it must establish its own unequal-treatment elements. The strongest objection is that official racial assignment is itself the personal classification; Johnson expressly considered and rejected that equivalence for composition alone. Repeating it as a status label does not distinguish the case. Conversely, the absence of original pleadings in this research packet does not justify dismissing every conceivable independent claim with prejudice or declaring that no such claim was preserved.

**Path H2 — retain the Johnson dissent's proposed rule:** Racial predominance subordinating ordinary criteria should trigger strict scrutiny, but the supported political explanation and disputed motive evidence prevent summary judgment against the State. Vacate and remand for ordinary factfinding on that asserted claim, with justification and fit reached only if the trigger is proved. The strongest objection is that this invites trial of a claim current law rejects. A Justice adopting H2 must candidly maintain the proposed replacement of Shaw and Johnson; the factual dispute cannot covertly reestablish the displaced rule. Even on H2, shape and race-party correlation are evidence, not automatic proof; predominance is a trigger for justification, not automatic invalidity. No narrow-tailoring ruling can be imported from the decision below because that issue was not litigated there.

An outright affirmance of the existing invalidation is not supported on either path: H1 rejects its substantive foundation; H2 does not permit the court to choose the adverse motive inference on this disputed summary record. An unconditional decree validating the district against all constitutional and statutory claims is equally unsupported.

## Florida v. White — No. 98-223

### Reach and alternatives

The vehicle was seized in a public workplace lot on accepted probable cause of its use in three earlier cocaine deliveries. There is no stated present probable cause of drugs inside it. The challenged constitutional premise is the seizure for forfeiture; the later inventory cannot retroactively supply seizure authority. The state possession prosecution, not a final civil forfeiture award, is before the Court. The statute's authority to seize and consequent forfeiture command remain distinct from the Fourth Amendment question.

**Path W1 — extend the vehicle rationale at the identified object:** Probable cause that this readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable permits seizure from a public place without a warrant or an additional emergency. Labron supplies mobility and reduced regulated-vehicle privacy without case-specific warrant impracticability; its search holding does not mechanically decide the issue, but its justifications reach custody of the very vehicle for which probable cause exists. Good expressly preserves movable-property distinctions and does not transform a civil real-property hearing rule into a vehicle warrant rule. The strongest objection is that an investigative search for present evidence and a planned retrospective forfeiture seizure burden different interests: a car's mobility alone does not generally excuse taking any property without judicial process. W1 answers at the limited vehicle/public-place/probable-cause setting, not by treating every forfeitable item as outside the Fourth Amendment.

**Path W2 — require prior judicial process for this seizure:** The automobile-search exception does not extend to a planned seizure based only on past facilitating use where officers identify no present evidentiary search object or particular need to proceed without a warrant. The strongest objection is Labron's rejection of extra urgency despite prior opportunity and custody, and Good's express distinction of movable property. W2 accepts those rules in their actual domains but treats retrospective forfeiture custody as a different intrusion; it does not restore urgency as a prerequisite to an evidentiary automobile search. Good supports separate examination of the deprivation, not automatic application of its land-hearing rule. Neither path makes absence of a warrant term in Florida's statute conclusive.

## Statutory coverage: shared operative proposition

Section 4's corporation definition reaches a nonprofit professional association organized to conduct substantial activities that serve its members' commercial profit, even when the association distributes no profits to itself. CDA's substantial business assistance and commercially directed membership activities place it within that definition; a nonprofit or tax designation does not remove the express members'-profit branch.

The proposition is not that every incidental financial benefit to a charitable or educational organization's participants establishes FTC coverage. The association's organization and substantial activities must satisfy the statutory business-and-profit terms. No asserted sector exception removes CDA, and no state-action immunity follows from a guideline's account of California advertising law. Coverage permits the Commission's competition inquiry; it does not establish a restraint's illegality.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** CDA's separate nonprofit identity and the outside suppliers' role could support excluding an association whose own activity is genuinely charitable or educational, with only incidental member benefit. The stronger textual objection to exclusion here is Congress's express inclusion of business for members' profit, which would lose substantial work if only the association's own earnings counted. ## Competing rule-of-reason paths

### Path R: further inquiry before either condemnation

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter would hold that the abbreviated inquiry used below is inadequate for both challenged categories. The defect is the sufficiency of the competitive analysis, not a judicial finding that every advertisement was deceptive or every CDA condition useful.

**Quality:** Suppression of quality claims without examining their truth is likewise substantial adverse evidence.
