# Kravitz — neutral supplemental source receipt

**Event:** Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Kravitz, No. 97-1768; November 12, 1998, user-supplied merits scheduling. No argument date added.

**Source read:** Complete en banc opinion and all six footnotes, Newton/Kravitz, 135 F.3d 266, January 30, 1998: https://openjurist.org/135/f3d/266 . The website's later citation links are not inputs. The lower appeal was No. 96-5045. No separate Supreme Court companion grant is inferred.

The class period alleged is November 4, 1992 through November 4, 1994. The District Court converted the motion to dismiss into summary judgment and decided that the best-execution duty was too indeterminate to support misrepresentation and that scienter could not be shown as a matter of law. The parties agreed that accepting an order without price instructions implied an undertaking consistent with best execution and that accepting while intending to breach that duty would be a material misrepresentation. They disputed the undertaking's actual scope, reasonable availability of better prices and contemporaneous intent. This is an express lower-opinion account of the parties' agreement, not an invented concession about liability or every element.

The three asserted sources of better execution are SelectNet/Instinet quotations, crossing simultaneously held opposing market orders, and matching a customer order against an in-house limit order. The record contains expert and industry declarations, evidence of defendants' own trading through alternative systems, and evidence of particular customer trades with contemporaneous better quotations. Defendants present evidence of added expense, delay and ordinary NBBO practices. All are summary-judgment materials, not findings that every trade could have been better executed.

Footnote 2 makes 'best price' shorthand for best reasonably available terms, including order size, the security's trading characteristics, execution speed, clearing costs, and the cost/difficulty of executing in a market. Footnote 5 rejects an automatic SOES safe harbor on this disputed record, while retaining small-order size as relevant; not all orders used SOES. Footnote 6 applies the same disputed-fact analysis to crossing and in-house limit orders. No universally mandatory venue or order-by-order search is established merely by the duty of periodic assessment discussed in the 1994 SEC report.

Footnote 4 records particular trades evidencing possible economic injury and a Rule 56(f) affidavit seeking information on others. This supports a real damages controversy without a finding of injury in every transaction or automatic class certification. The record does not establish reliance, actual loss or recoverable amounts for every claimant.

**Temporal and doctrinal limits:** Later SEC order-execution regulations do not govern the 1992–1994 transactions retroactively. Their retrospective study may describe those earlier markets, but neither later adoption nor present technology supplies earlier notice or practical access. Effective simulated Central Bank, O'Hagan and First Jersey are read through OTHER, not replaced by historical versions. Central Bank's severe-recklessness holding is confined to affirmative assistance; First Jersey found knowing primary deception. Hochfelder excludes negligence and does not itself resolve every primary-liability recklessness question.
