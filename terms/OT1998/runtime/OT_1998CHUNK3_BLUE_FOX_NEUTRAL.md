### Issue 1: APA waiver and equitable lien against federal funds

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Lane v. Peña* requires an unequivocal waiver for the particular Treasury remedy sought and separates a substantive federal duty from consent to monetary relief. Nordic Village separates jurisdiction, equitable terminology and an asserted trust interest from an unequivocal waiver for the particular recovery. Its reservation for a genuine identified-property proceeding does not establish that Blue Fox owns a retained res or has consent to attach Government completion funds.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Remedy-specific sovereign-immunity waiver.
* **Other Governing Law:** 5 U.S.C. § 702; Miller Act; Tucker Act; sovereign-immunity and Appropriations Clause principles.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Bowen v. Massachusetts* enforced a specific statutory payment obligation and does not authorize substitute compensation for a private debtor's default.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit allowed Blue Fox to pursue an equitable lien against funds in the Government’s possession.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Blue Fox characterizes its requested lien as specific equitable relief available under §702, particularly after the Government failed to obtain a payment bond. The Army treats it as substitute compensation for the prime contractor’s unpaid debt without a remedy-specific waiver. Identification of a retained fund belonging to the claimant would materially affect the characterization.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

An actual specific-property entitlement and applicable waiver could permit a bounded remedy. A claim to payment from general government property fails without clear consent even if described as equitable. Contract remedies against other parties do not create a federal Treasury waiver.
