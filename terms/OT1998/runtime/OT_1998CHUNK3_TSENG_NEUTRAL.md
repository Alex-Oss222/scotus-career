### Issue 1: Exclusivity of the Warsaw Convention remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines (January 16, 1996) allows domestic law to identify compensable losses and beneficiaries subject to the Convention’s own liability conditions and limits, but expressly leaves general exclusivity unresolved. Tseng’s lack of qualifying bodily injury raises that reserved question: domestic damages rules may fill a remedial gap within liability, or local tort law may be argued to create an independent claim. The text of Articles 17 and 24 must resolve that distinction.
* **Other Governing Law:** Warsaw Convention Articles 17, 24, and 25.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Air France v. Saks*, *Eastern Airlines, Inc. v. Floyd*, and *Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co.* supply the real-law interpretive context; *Zicherman* concerns measurement of rights after Convention liability, not creation of liability when Article 17 is unsatisfied.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Second Circuit allowed Tseng’s New York tort claim after concluding that her airport security search did not satisfy the Convention’s accident requirement.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Tseng argues that failure to satisfy Article 17 leaves a local tort remedy rather than complete remedial exclusion. El Al argues that Article 24 makes the Convention’s limits exclusive within its covered carriage field. Zicherman’s use of domestic damages law raises a distinction between supplementing recoverable losses and creating liability when a treaty condition fails.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Exclusivity within Article 17’s field defeats this local personal-injury claim on the stated absence of bodily injury; a nonexclusive construction permits further local-law proceedings. Events outside the treaty’s substantive or temporal field remain a separate question.
