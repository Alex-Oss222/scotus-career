## Seven Associates: shared sufficient rule

The 1909 and 1910 reservations of coal do not reserve the naturally occurring, chemically distinct methane held by physical adsorption in the coal or in its fractures. The statutory estate is the coal identified by the ordinary meaning of the enacted term in its historical setting, with the expressly accompanying mining and removal rights. The methane at issue does not become reserved coal merely because it formed alongside coal, is physically associated with it, or now can be profitably extracted from the reservoir.

This construction does not hold that later-discovered value can never belong to an owner. Coal that was unmarketable at enactment remains coal, and later profitable uses of the reserved substance do not change title. The point is that a different substance was not included in the term Congress selected. Nor does separate gas ownership authorize destruction of the coal estate, dispense with lawful access, or answer compensation for actual interference.

## Adversarial alternatives and resolution

**Reservoir/sovereign path:** Coal is a heterogeneous deposit containing hydrocarbons; contemporaneous descriptions acknowledged occluded gases, and extraction formerly entailed disturbing the coal. Congress preserved all coal and its future energy value while enabling agricultural settlement. If that creates unresolved ambiguity, the sovereign-grant canon favors the retained United States estate, to which the Tribe succeeded. This is the strongest route to affirmance and does not depend solely on a tribal canon.

**Ordinary-substance path:** Coal and methane were separately understood substances at enactment; known hazardous gas was vented during mining while coal retained its recognized solid-fuel identity. Physical attachment does not answer legal classification. The Acts conveyed the remaining estate and reserved a particular resource, unlike the later reservation of coal and other minerals. Ownership and lawful extraction were already distinct problems in split estates. The term's meaning in this statutory setting therefore resolves the question without using a canon to add a gas reservation.

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer take the second path. Ginsburg takes the reservoir/sovereign path for the specific ambiguity and dominion grounds stated below. Its strongest objection is that a simple solid/gas contrast may ignore the complex composition of an actual deposit and treat later technology as retroactively dividing it. The response is narrower: the record describes the methane at issue as a separately recognized substance retained through physical association; nothing about improved recovery changes either its identity or the enacted coal reservation. The broader contemporary descriptions of coal's hydrocarbon content do not make every adjacent or physically held hydrocarbon the reserved substance. No universal scientific taxonomy of all coal constituents is adjudicated.

Western Nuclear construed the broader coal-and-other-minerals reservation in the 1916 Act; its sovereign canon operates within that actual text. Leo Sheep prevents a land-grant canon from defeating the enacted conveyance. Neither canon supplies an automatic winner before reading the statute. The Tribe's successor status does not convert the original United States reservation into a new enlargement of an existing tribal estate. Yankton's attention to the actual transaction and preservation of unaddressed parcels is consistent with this bounded construction; Bourland's regulation question does not decide mineral title.

## Exact remedies and retained questions

For the seven reversing Associates, reverse the en banc judgment insofar as it holds that the coal reservations themselves vested title to the covered methane in the United States and then the Tribe; remand for proceedings consistent with the correct estate construction. Do not direct wholesale judgment on every pleaded claim or treat this construction as a blanket license to extract gas.

For Ginsburg, affirm the en banc ownership construction for methane trapped in and produced from the coal reservoir, with ordinary further proceedings on the matters the lower court preserved. Her result does not award damages, eliminate defenses, resolve migrated-gas ownership or source allocation, or license extraction without applicable property protections.

For all eight Associates, the Acts' associated access provisions remain operative in their own terms. The 1909 Act conditions nonconsensual entry to prospect, mine or remove coal on court-determined security and payment for damage; it preserves domestic coal use before federal disposition and the classification-hearing/right-to-an-unreserved-patent qualifications. The 1910 provisions distinguish prospecting upon a Secretary-approved bond for crop/improvement damage from an acquired coal interest's reasonable surface occupation for mining/removal upon payment of damage or a sufficient bond in the prescribed court proceeding. Domestic-use and classification-contest protections also remain. These coal-access provisions are not adjudicated as a new gas-access code.

Unadjudicated limitations and other common defenses, actual coal-estate damage or trespass, lawful methods of extraction, surface access, tax and collusion theories, facilities, other trust issues and compensation remain subject to their own law and posture. The appellate reservation concerning methane migrated into non-coal strata is not converted into a new parcel-title finding or quantitative allocation. Separate instruments reserving gas are outside this construction. The federal defendants' proven position below favored the producers and defended the 1981 Solicitor interpretation; a later reversal of that position is unverified and not invented.
