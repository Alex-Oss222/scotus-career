## A1–A3 retained operative commitments

### A1. Actual delegation and deference to the Board's construction

**Selected rule:** Sections 1103(a)(1) and 1253(h), implemented through the Board's assigned adjudicative authority under 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), require respect for a permissible Board construction of serious nonpolitical crime after the court independently identifies the enacted boundaries. The phrase leaves evaluative interpretive work; no statutory text supplies the Ninth Circuit's exclusive factor code. Agency application and supported factual findings retain their distinct review standards.

Haggar's unanimous Parts I–III apply authority-before-deference, not automatic agency supremacy. Its tariff regulation setting does not make notice-and-comment rulemaking a universal prerequisite to every agency interpretation; the present statute and Board adjudicative assignment independently provide the relevant authority. NationsBank supports reasonable classification within genuine ambiguity. MCI and NCUA prohibit removing operative conditions; none is removed merely by recognizing discretion to evaluate seriousness and political character.

The strongest competing path reads the exception narrowly through independent judicial construction because withholding protects against persecution and the phrase has a legal political-offense meaning. Independent enforcement of fixed meaning is required, as Cardoza-Fonseca and Stevic illustrate. It does not establish that every evaluative application is reserved exclusively to courts or that the Board must adopt all judicially preferred considerations. A label of foreign-affairs expertise is unnecessary to this conclusion and cannot replace statutory authorization.

### A2. Persecution-risk balancing and the actual exception

**Selected rule:** Once the statutory serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly found, the degree of feared persecution need not be weighed against the seriousness of the offense as an additional eligibility requirement. Paragraph (2) makes paragraph (1) inapplicable on its own stated conditions. A persecution showing therefore does not dissolve the separate exception. The Board could decide the exception while assuming the possibility of the paragraph (1) showing.

The strongest competing path requires humanitarian balancing because return may entail grave persecution, and seriousness might be evaluated relative to that consequence. That is an available policy argument, not a claimed recovered filing. The text separates the persecutory threat from the crime exclusion and supplies no balancing override. The selected construction preserves both the mandatory protection and its actual exception; it does not authorize discretionary denial to every otherwise eligible withholding applicant. Asylum remains a separate discretionary question.

### A3. Political character, proportionality, atrociousness and political necessity

**Selected rule:** Comparing the political character of the offense against its ordinary criminal character is a permissible construction. The Board may consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct as circumstances making criminal character predominant; the statute does not require a separate atrocity finding in every case, an exclusively prescribed sequence of subsidiary labels, or findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving a political objective. The agency must nevertheless apply its actual announced standard to the actual conduct and political setting. Ordinary criminal conduct is not automatically nonpolitical merely because it is unlawful, and political motive is not automatically sufficient to defeat the exception.

The strongest contrary path remands because genuine protest activity cannot be evaluated without its political purpose, proportionality and circumstances. Those matters are material to the actual inquiry. The supplied Board reasoning already compared political dissatisfaction with the destruction and civilian assaults and found the latter disproportionate. It did not omit political character entirely. The Ninth Circuit cannot replace that permissible framework with a mandatory additional factor set. Nor does refusal to require political necessity or success prohibit the agency from considering genuinely relevant circumstances under its lawful standard.

This selected rule recognizes the adequacy of the Board's stated legal framework and rejects the circuit's identified legal objections. It does not announce that every bus burning, every assault or every unsuccessful protest categorically establishes the exception. No universal quantum of injury or atrocity threshold is added.
