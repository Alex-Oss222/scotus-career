## McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc.

### Final non-Stone disposition and grounds

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer retain full agreement with M1–M3 of the frozen commitments, at the following exact scopes.

**M1 — The asserted damages action.** Section 531(e), considered with the Cable Act's differentiated enforcement provisions and amendment history, does not establish congressional intent to authorize the asserted implied private damages action against this operator. Protecting public-access speakers and prohibiting specified editorial control do not by themselves supply this judicial damages action. Cort, Touche Ross and Transamerica require the separate intent inquiry; Suter preserves it. Franklin supplies appropriate relief only after a cognizable action exists. Central Bank and Musick, Peeler develop incidents of a received securities action; neither supplies the missing premise for a new Cable Act action.

**M2 — Remedy.** Reverse the Second Circuit only insofar as its judgment permits the asserted damages action and remand. The question supplied for this grant concerns damages, while the complaint sought damages, prospective relief and CUTPA relief. The disposition neither recognizes nor extinguishes an independently sourced prospective, franchise, constitutional or state-law route. Any such remaining route requires its actual source and ordinary preservation, jurisdictional and remedial requirements. It neither creates an equitable action under §531(e) nor commands dismissal of the entire complaint. The lower courts may address remaining source-supported matters and supplemental jurisdiction lawfully; no route is guaranteed.

**M3 — Boundaries.** The decision does not determine whether the disputed program or blanket future exclusion complied with law, whether the viewers have a separate cognizable claim, whether Cablevision acted as the State, or whether the channel is a constitutional public forum. It creates no unpleaded §1983 claim. Section 531(e) remains subject to §544(d) and its separate permission to refuse public-access programs or portions containing obscenity, indecency or nudity. That permission does not require refusal and does not automatically extend public-access wording to every educational or governmental use. Denver Area's limited judgment concerning 1992 §10(c) does not extinguish the independent 1996 permission or decide its constitutionality and application.

## City of West Covina v. Perkins

### Final common disposition and limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support reversal of the Ninth Circuit's individualized legal-instruction ruling and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground, with remand for proceedings consistent with the decision.

**W1 — Common negative rule.** When the owner has notice of the seizure and the responsible agency and adequate return procedures are reasonably ascertainable in published statutes and judicial decisions, the Due Process Clause does not additionally require an individualized explanation of those state-law remedies. This does not decide inaccessible procedures or a proved withholding of case-specific information necessary to invoke a remedy.

**W2 — Application and remedy.** The receipt identified the agency, search date, warrant date, issuing judge and court, contacts and inventory. The District Court expressly found that respondents failed to establish the omitted warrant number was necessary to seek return. The claimed unsuccessful efforts and disputed statements do not replace that finding with a proved obstruction. The Ninth Circuit's conclusion that California remedies were adequate is the premise for the notice issue; it is not a new Supreme Court finding that every implementation is adequate. Reversal removes the rejected legal-guide requirement and the corresponding judgment direction. It orders no fresh evidentiary hearing, discovery, reopened claim or award.

**W3 — Preserved issues.** Return of the property does not by itself extinguish a supported claim about an earlier deprivation. Individual qualified immunity, the warrant's validity and the reported-stolen pistol are not reopened or adjudicated. Section 1536 custody subject to judicial orders and the judicially recognized return motion remain distinct from §1540's grounds requiring restoration for a mismatch with the warrant or absence of its specified probable-cause basis. The decision neither merges their conditions nor approves all state recovery procedures. Good's civil real-property forfeiture holding and Mullane's general civil notice principle remain intact.

## Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc.

### Final non-Stone disposition and grounds

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer retain full R1–R3 joins, including the following qualifications.

**R1 — No additional motive element.** Section 1395dd(b)'s stabilization duty does not require proof of an improper financial or other invidious motive. The hospital's determination of an emergency medical condition triggers its own enacted examination/treatment or lawful-transfer requirements. Subsection (b) does not carry subsection (a)'s screening language, and neither the anti-dumping purpose nor the concern about general malpractice authorizes an extra motive element.

**R2 — Exact disposition.** Reverse the Sixth Circuit insofar as it affirmed summary judgment on the appealed stabilization claim for failure to prove improper motive, and remand. The recognized genuine stabilization dispute remains. The hospital's asserted absence of actual knowledge and its contention that proper screening ended its statutory duty remain for lawful consideration; this Court decides neither factual correctness nor legal dispositiveness. They do not justify affirmance on an unestablished factual premise. The separate state ostensible-agency ruling and other claims are not reopened. The Court orders no liability judgment, damages or mandatory trial.

**R3 — Remaining predicates.** The decision does not determine emergency condition, hospital determination or knowledge, duration of duty, stabilization, authorized transfer, causal injury or ultimate liability. Subsection (b)(1) requires examination and treatment within available staff and facilities to stabilize or transfer consistent with subsection (c). Subsections (b)(2) and (b)(3) separately address informed refusals of treatment and transfer, with risk/benefit information and reasonable efforts to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (c)(1) separately requires the applicable informed written request or medical certification, together with an appropriate transfer. The applicable certification, including consultation and countersignature when a physician is absent; risk-minimizing care within capacity; receiving resources and acceptance; medical records; and qualified transport requirements are not erased by removing motive. No refusal, certification or transfer exception is found satisfied or violated here. Subsection (d)(2)(A)'s express hospital action retains direct personal harm, state-law personal-injury damages and appropriate equitable relief, and the distinct two-year limit. No personal action or general federal malpractice remedy follows.
