### F1–F3: precise common ground

The Fund Act allocates benefits by demonstrated presidential support rather than party name. A major party has at least 25 percent of the preceding presidential vote; a minor party has at least five but less than 25 percent. Section 9004(a)(2)(B)'s returning-candidate route and section 9004(a)(3)'s current-election, five-percent post-election route prevent the inaccurate conclusion that every new party is permanently excluded. Those provisions carry their separate eligibility, ratio and credit conditions. The present case supplies no new payment calculation or finding that any unreported eligibility condition was met.

The approximately $29 million and $62 million figures describe public receipts. Under section 9003(c), the minor/new-party qualified-expense ceiling is the major-party entitlement, with private supplementation for the excess of qualified expenses over public receipts. Section 9004(b) separately limits aggregate payments to the lower of qualified expenses less received-and-expended-or-retained contributions, or the major entitlement less those contributions. Section 9004(c) restricts use to qualified expenses and its specified loan repayment or restoration; it does not permit restoration of contributions already received and expended for qualified expenses. Section 9004(d)'s $50,000 personal/immediate-family certification includes both members of the ticket. These qualifications defeat the asserted shorthand that total spending must stop at the smaller subsidy; they do not prove successful fundraising or automatically validate every funding condition.

For **F1**, Buckley supplies the directly applicable rule for this federal support-based allocation. Florida confirms that a subsidy classification must be examined at its actual burden and that ballot qualification does not itself create equal financial entitlement. Florida involved a different benefit and unrestricted private spending; its factual premise is not transplanted into this program. There is no holding that any objectively phrased funding criterion necessarily survives.

For **F2**, the reported as-applied claim is considered with its financial allegations accepted, not rejected for want of trial evidence. The source account says that the plaintiffs complain of inability to bridge the subsidy gap and do not allege the different suppression identified below. The defect is legal: resource parity is not itself the constitutional entitlement Buckley reserved. The decision adopts no exhaustive requirement that a party actually disappear, lose a particular election or stop fielding candidates before challenging a burden. Nor does it make subjective intent to harm the exclusive constitutional test. Adequately alleged substantial practical suppression, viewpoint favoritism or discriminatory administration would be different claims.

For **F3**, an individual's payment to the candidate supplies the candidate's resources. It is distinct from a party's own independent advertisement and from an aggregate allowance spanning unrelated intermediary associations. The direct-contribution distinction and fitted prevention of candidate dependency remain available without proof of a completed corrupt bargain. Buckley expressly considered minor-party financing and rejected a categorical absence of corruption concerns merely because a candidate belongs to a minor party: that candidate may be elected. The reported inability to receive and spend as much as the major parties does not, by itself, distinguish that conclusion. The contribution claim is therefore rejected on its own terms, not by consent to public funding or a proposition that formal supplementation always supplies meaningful practical opportunity.

Day and Kentucky preserve review for a demonstrated associational, cumulative or prohibitory burden. Kentucky's unanimous direct-$1,000 holding is useful evidence of each Associate's continuing adoption of the direct-contribution distinction, but its concession and particular facial record do not dispose of Reform's different claim. No Kentucky concession, knowledge term or state-law independent-expenditure exception is imputed here. Colorado Republican preserves direct candidate cash and actual conduits even within its three-Justice broader party-expression proposal. No current-law proposition has converted all contributions into independent expenditures. The present holding neither fixes a constitutionally permanent $1,000 amount nor decides a newly supported inflation, donor-pool, campaign-service or cumulative-effects claim.

### A: precise common ground

Section 437c(a)(1) limits the number affiliated with any one party; it names no party and does not reserve three Democratic and three Republican seats. Reform affiliates are not legally ineligible under that rule. The requested removal of the cap leaves the alleged major-party appointment choice unexplained and supplies no likelihood of improving Reform's representation. That is the decisive redressability failure on this pleaded theory.

Northeastern Florida rejects a demand to prove that a claimant would ultimately win a position when the injury is an actual barrier to equal competition. All eight accept that controlling distinction here. They do not reason that the possibility of appointing the same commissioners would defeat standing even if a concrete exclusionary rule had been identified. The defect is that this cap has not been connected to such an exclusion or other redressable injury. A concrete appointment opportunity impaired by a legal barrier, or a properly presented defective enforcement adjudication, could require different analysis; neither is supplied. The holding need not decide whether every asserted representation interest could constitute injury in other circumstances.

Legi-Tech requires actual lawful agency performance after the earlier structural defect; a prediction that the same individuals would decide the same way is insufficient to cure unconstitutional participation. Nothing here reverses that rule. This case alleges no particular failure of lawful reconsideration. The Code's printed ex-officio language cannot restore the unconstitutional participation already displaced by current law.

### C: threshold and remedial precision

The Court reviews whether the single judge properly declined the special tribunals. It does not first find a claim substantial and then decide it to save a remand. California Medical's threshold exception, as supplied in the neutral packet and the lower opinion, permits dismissal where Article III is absent or the constitutional question is frivolous/foreclosed. Failure to establish ultimate entitlement to relief is not, without more, insubstantiality. Here F1 presents a constitutional question already answered; F2 and F3 identify no legally distinguishing alleged burden beyond the unequal resources that the directly pertinent Buckley analysis addressed. No weighing of affidavits or demand for summary-judgment proof supports that conclusion. A matter-specific disagreement with this substantiality conclusion would require the appropriate remand, not automatic victory on the constitutional merits.

F1–F2 fall under section 9011(b), which provides implementation/construction jurisdiction without administrative or other exhaustion and a three-judge district court with direct Supreme Court appeal. F3 and A concern FECA and fall under section 437h, which requires immediate circuit en banc certification when a live substantial constitutional question is presented by an authorized plaintiff. Organizing F3 under a Fund Act heading does not change its statutory channel. Section 9011(a)'s D.C. Circuit review of an actual Commission action, with its thirty-day petition condition, is separate; no such administrative-review claim or compliance finding is invented.

The affiliation dismissal remains nonmerits and without prejudice. There is no appointment order, judicial selection of commissioners, award of a funding difference, replacement contribution limit, damages award, election replay, new cause of action, or decision of the ungranted issue-advertisement/common-law controversies. No mandatory discovery or new evidentiary hearing is ordered. Ordinary mandate consequences suffice.
