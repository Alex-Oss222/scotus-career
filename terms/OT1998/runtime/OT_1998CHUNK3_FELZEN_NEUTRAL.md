### Issue 1: Party status for settlement appeal

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Izumi (November 30, 1993) distinguishes a sufficient personal interest from party status and permits review of an intervention denial without automatically opening review of the underlying judgment. It addresses §1254 and a particular unreviewed intervention denial, not every shareholder’s §1291 appeal. Felzen requires examining whether Rule 23.1 itself gives the objector party-equivalent appeal rights or whether intervention remains necessary.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1291, 1254; Fed. R. Civ. P. 23.1 and 24; the actual derivative settlement and intervention record.

### Issue 2: Derivative representation and available review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Richards (June 10, 1996) protects unrepresented nonparties from preclusion; it does not make every person affected by representative litigation a party entitled to appeal. Rule 23.1 notice and objection, Rule 24 intervention and adequate representation perform distinct functions. The consequence of insisting on intervention must be evaluated without presuming that a right to object alone resolves appellate status.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1291, 1254; Fed. R. Civ. P. 23.1 and 24; the actual derivative settlement and intervention record.
