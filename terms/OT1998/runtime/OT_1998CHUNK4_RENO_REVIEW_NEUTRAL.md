# Reno — meaningful review and the timing of constitutional access

**Matter and cutoff:** Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee, No. 97-1252; supplied February 24, 1999 merits event. This neutral supplement concerns jurisdiction and the adequacy and timing of review. It chooses no vote, holds no constitutional claim proved, and adds no charge, filing, fact, reconstruction premise or Court action. Read it with [C_NEUTRAL](OT_1998CHUNK4_C_NEUTRAL.md#reno-v-american-arab-anti-discrimination-committee) and the [order/status validation](OT_1998CHUNK4_RENO_SOURCE_VALIDATION.md).

## Actual posture and distinct injuries

The Government seeks review of collateral preliminary relief before any final deportation order. The Six's January 1994 injunction bars conducting further deportation proceedings against them; the later order denies dissolution of that injunction and separately grants preliminary protection to the Two, Hamide and Shehadeh. The two strands have different procedural origins. Existing preliminary findings concerning association-based selection and present chill are judicial findings at that stage, not mere pleading assertions, but are not final determinations of unlawful discrimination, statutory deportability or every injunction condition.

The Six's original status charges and the Two's association/material-support charges cannot be treated as one admitted violation. Barakat and Sharif obtained temporary resident status and are no longer deportable on the original status grounds; that agreed consequence is neither permanent residence nor universal immunity. Respondents' assertion that the injunction prevents new charges is their construction of its wording. No new charge or specific imminent threat is established by that assertion or by the alleged absence of a disavowal.

Respondents identify an injury during the proceedings: coercion to abandon protected association because that association caused their selection. They also identify a different barrier: inability to make and present the selection record in the later statutory review channel. The Government disputes the legal significance and extent of the claimed present chill and proposes eventual judicial consideration. A court can distinguish these issues. A later forum capable of reversing an unlawful order may still leave a distinct interim injury; conversely, an asserted present chill does not prove an entitlement to immediate judicial intervention.

## What the proposed later channel can actually do

The [predecision party extracts](../sources/chunk4_c/RENO_MEANINGFUL_REVIEW_PARTY_EXACT.md), government opening brief printed pages 34–49, respondents' pages 18–37 and 42–46, and government reply pages 12–18, supply the competing arguments. They are advocacy, not findings. The Ninth Circuit's reasons appear in the [validated lower-opinion passages](../sources/chunk4_c/C_VALIDATED_SOURCE_PASSAGES.md), appendix 12a–15a.

The parties agree that the immigration judge and Board cannot adjudicate the selective-enforcement claim. That does not alone defeat channeling: Thunder Basin permits an agency process followed by judicial constitutional review. The material further question is whether the court can receive the selection evidence, decide the claim and give effective relief.

**Administrative-record limitation.** The official [1994 section 1105a copy](../sources/chunk4_c/usc_1994_8_1105a.plain.txt) states the former channel's incorporation of chapter 158 and its requirement that the petition be determined solely on the administrative record. Paragraph (4) expressly excepts the nationality transfer in (5)(B). This historical edition is used for those identified provisions only; its other provisions are not a substitute for the later amendments and transitions in the entering-law holdings. Respondents and the lower court argue that evidence developed in a district court cannot satisfy that limitation. The Government reads it as governing matters resolved, or capable of resolution, in the agency while permitting a separate factual mechanism for a substantial issue the agency could not decide. Neither reading is an agreed operative rule here.

**The Hobbs Act hearing condition.** The complete [section 2347 text](../sources/chunk4_c/RENO_2347_EXACT.md) is mechanically copied from the petitioners' statutory appendix. Subsection (a) uses the agency record when the agency held a hearing. Subsection (b) first requires that the agency held no hearing before the action under review and then distinguishes: a hearing required by law, producing agency remand; no required hearing and no genuine material factual dispute, producing decision on pleadings and affidavits; and no required hearing with a genuine material factual dispute, producing district-court transfer under (b)(3). These are actual statutory conditions, not a general power to develop any missing record.

Respondents identify the reviewable action as the final deportation order, for which a hearing is required. The Government identifies the challenged action as the earlier charging decision, for which no hearing was required or held. That disagreement over the relevant action is independent of the record-only objection. The Government's proposed transfer is also limited: it would screen a specific supported constitutional claim, allow the Government's explanation, and transfer only a material factual dispute that the agency could not resolve. That proposed screening procedure is not already an adopted immigration rule. Armstrong's actual discovery holding does not itself enact it.

**Transfer is distinct from additional agency evidence.** Section 2347(c) permits additional agency evidence on materiality and reasonable grounds for the earlier omission. It is not (b)(3). The particular permanent and transitional prohibitions on subsection (c) must be applied only where their own temporal conditions operate. The Government draws an inference that expressly excluding (c) leaves (b)(3); respondents answer that the latter was already unavailable under the record limitation and hearing conditions. Their competing accounts of the deleted Senate transfer proposal remain legislative-history arguments. No legislative motive is adopted as fact, and a rationale dependent on the exact proposal or its history should use the underlying legislative source.

**No conclusive concession or invented fallback.** The government reply expressly denies conceding either that every such selection claim constitutionally requires a judicial forum or that this record necessarily requires district-court factfinding. Its suggestion that a later court could use Rule 48, devise a comparable procedure, or decide the constitutional problem if transfer proves unavailable is a legal proposal, not an established adequate substitute. The Government's commitment to a legal position cannot create jurisdiction. The same is true of respondents' assertion that all such claims necessarily lack later review.

## Authorities bearing on delay and access

The [older-authority note](../sources/chunk4_c/RENO_REVIEW_OLDER_AUTHORITY_NOTES.md) records full-reading verification of the official opinions, separate writings and footnotes of Younger, Dombrowski, Oestereich, Webster and McNary, with complete mechanical source copies. Their actual limits matter:

- **McNary:** The Court construed language concerning individual application determinations and denials, not an express reference to every enforcement decision. Its record barriers involved systemic proof outside individual applications and actual procedural defects; many applicants also would have had to surrender for deportation merely to obtain review. Pending proceedings here distinguish that access price. They do not establish that selection evidence can be developed or that an interim injury is remediable. Catholic Social Services preserves the concern where an actual government-created barrier prevents the ordinary review route, while rejecting a mere collateral label.
- **Thunder Basin:** The statutory channel was adequate on the actual record, with meaningful judicial review and identified opportunities for contest and protection. Agency inability finally to decide constitutional law was not enough to escape it. The case does not make an unidentified future factfinding procedure adequate or make its Mine Act features a universal mandatory test.
- **Younger and Dombrowski:** Ordinary defense costs, anxiety and undifferentiated chill do not justify interference with a good-faith prosecution; serious continuing injury from abusive process can be different. Dombrowski involved concrete harassment and suppression circumstances, and Younger confines its broader language. Their federal-state comity rationale does not automatically apply to federal immigration administration, and neither independently supplies jurisdiction against a contrary statute. The competing arguments must address both the supported present injury and the availability of an actual defense or review forum.
- **Oestereich:** The Court reconciled pre-induction review restrictions with a concededly satisfied, unequivocal statutory exemption withdrawn for unrelated conduct. It did not create a general exception for every alleged retaliatory act. Harlan's broader treatment of agency inability, hearing timing and interim liberty injury is a separate concurrence. The current record supplies no comparable universal exemption for all eight respondents.
- **Webster and Lincoln:** Separate colorable constitutional review survives the particular statutory-discretion arrangements they address. Webster's concern about eliminating every judicial forum does not guarantee a preferred timing or remedy; avoidance requires a permissible interpretation. Lincoln's unanimous preservation of constitutional review prevents using an older separate view as a later categorical rule of executive immunity.
- **Hollywood Motor Car:** The verified [official opinion](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep458/usrep458263/usrep458263.pdf), [complete text](../sources/chunk4_c/reno_review_hollywood_official.txt), rejects an interlocutory appeal from denial of a vindictive-prosecution motion. A right whose remedy is dismissal is not necessarily a right against undergoing proceedings at all. Its important factual limit is that a district court had already heard the constitutional motion; it does not decide the adequacy of withholding every competent initial forum until the end. The Court opinion, dissent and footnotes were read in full. No identified entering-law entry displaces this predivergence holding.

## Both lawful paths require an answer to the opposing case

**Channel to later review.** The strongest path identifies the actual challenged action within section 1252(g), respects the saved review scheme, and explains why the substantial objection can lawfully receive effective judicial consideration after the agency finishes. Administrative consideration can resolve genuine statutory defenses, develop facts relevant to the actual deportation grounds, and avoid unnecessary constitutional adjudication and intrusion into enforcement decisions. Ordinary process burdens do not establish a separate right to immediate review. If the model adopts a permissible construction enabling factual review, it must state that construction and its conditions rather than promise an unspecified later opportunity.

The strongest objection is that a petition confined to an administrative record the agency cannot build may be nominal review only. A defense defeating deportability does not necessarily decide retaliation, while a favorable statutory result could leave no adverse final order to review. Nor does eventual reversal remedy every supported ongoing suppression injury. The later-review path must address those points, distinguish the preliminary findings without erasing them, and explain why the actual timing remains adequate. It may reserve a genuinely different future failure of review, but cannot make that reservation the unsupported factual premise that today's review is adequate.

**Retain timely constitutional access.** The strongest path identifies a substantial constitutional objection and a concrete defect in later access: unavailable selection factfinding, inability to present the actual objection, or a sufficiently established continuing injury that final-order review cannot repair. It then identifies a permissible construction of the enacted scheme preserving an existing district-court basis for that confined claim. Respondents propose reading subsection (g) not to eliminate constitutional claims otherwise lacking meaningful review and using (f)'s individual-relief language as contextual support while relying on an independent jurisdictional source. That is a proposed construction requiring resolution, not existing law or a categorical exception. The binding Reyes-Hernandez boundary must be confronted.

The strongest objection is that the challenge attacks the very executive action Congress named and that avoidance cannot rewrite clear text. The factual and equitable grounds for initial constitutional review must be distinguished from an entitlement to stop an otherwise lawful adjudication. Existing association findings do not automatically supply every element of a constitutional selection claim or justify the full scope of both injunctions. A retained-jurisdiction disposition can return the case for separate injunction analysis without deciding final discrimination, deportability, new-charge coverage or permanent relief.

**If construction cannot resolve the problem.** Constitutional doubt, a constitutional holding about the review restriction, and the underlying First Amendment merits are distinct. A conclusion that no permissible review-preserving construction exists would require addressing the actual constitutional objection to exclusion, if necessary and properly presented, rather than silently converting doubt into jurisdiction. Scalia and Thomas's public Thunder Basin writing supplies a distinct proposal for limited section 1331 consideration of the constitutionality of excluding original review; it is not the Court's controlling formulation. No wholesale invalidation, hypothetical future failure, or categorical constitutional entitlement is presumed here.

## Preserve substantive law and remedial limits

Statutory defenses are relevant to the usefulness of completing administrative adjudication, not a guaranteed cure for selection injury. The actual charge and defense matter. The Two dispute grounds connected to association and material support; the Six's original status issues differ; Barakat and Sharif's agreed change addresses only their original grounds. No defense, exemption, successful factual showing, future charge or continuing threat is invented.

Actual First Amendment merits remain separate from whether the jurisdictional claim is sufficiently substantial and whether timely review is required. The entering Armstrong rule concerns authority and a preliminary showing for indirect racial selective-prosecution discovery; it does not impose a complete merits test on this developed association record. Whren keeps lawful objective grounds separate from independently forbidden discriminatory selection. Dawson permits relevant association evidence while rejecting abstract belief as an aggravating ground. R.A.V.'s controlling rationale here is overbreadth; its content-selection proposal is noncontrolling. None creates a universal immigration immunity or establishes the present claim's success. The lower court's specific-intent reasoning and the Government's conduct/immigration objections retain their actual preliminary and disputed status.

Kolster preserves conditional section 2241 capability against section 440(a), including substantial legal and constitutional authority objections. It does not decide subsection (g)'s operation here. No custody predicate, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion, filing, transfer, factual-review scope, stay or habeas remedy is assumed. An as-yet-unfiled collateral route cannot be pronounced adequate merely by naming it.

A jurisdictional disposition must state what happens to the existing injunctions and the collateral action; it neither orders removal nor adjudicates guilt, support for terrorism or ultimate deportability. A retained-channel ruling must address the distinct Six/Two injunction postures and individual live-dispute limits before preserving or modifying relief. No new blanket injunction or automatic continuation follows. A narrow resolution may leave individualized mootness and hypothetical-new-charge coverage unresolved where their determination is unnecessary.
