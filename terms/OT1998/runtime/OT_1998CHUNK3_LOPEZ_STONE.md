# Lopez v. Monterey County

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall prevent prospective use of the unprecleared county changes unless and until Monterey County obtains preclearance through a prescribed route. A genuinely independent and necessary federal-court interim plan remains available only within the existing confined judicial-plan boundary.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Administration of state-mandated changes

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the controlled coverage question and leaves discriminatory purpose, retrogressive effect, and entitlement to preclearance to the designated forums.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Lopez* unchanged. The County is the covered jurisdiction administering the change, and absence of local authorship or discretion does not create a state-command escape from § 5.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A covered political subdivision must obtain § 5 preclearance before it administers a postbaseline voting change within the subdivision, even when a noncovered State selected and mandated the policy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The identified post-remand dismissal again treats County compliance with compulsory state law as exempt from §5. The November 1996 judgment already forecloses that reasoning, and Young confirms that practical administration matters. The 1979 state law and 1995 constitutional change cannot authorize prospective implementation without the required clearance. Reversing this later dismissal enforces the mandate; it neither retries the earlier case nor imports a historical reservation inconsistent with the completed holding. The exact reach of the 1983 submission remains the specific factual task left open.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding imposes no statewide preclearance obligation on California and decides neither discriminatory purpose, retrogressive effect, nor entitlement to preclearance.

### Issue 2: Prospective nonuse and a genuinely judicial interim plan

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the remedy necessary to enforce the coverage holding.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An unprecleared governmental change may not be used while preclearance is absent. A wholly court-formulated plan adopted because a lawful election otherwise cannot occur remains distinct when it is genuinely independent, necessary, and no broader than the judicial need.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The unprecleared change may not be used pending lawful preclearance; a genuinely independent and necessary federal-court interim plan is not treated as the governmental plan merely because it operates temporarily in the same jurisdiction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The order below cannot preserve the County's unprecleared arrangement by relabeling its use as judicial. The lawful routes are the precleared baseline, completed preclearance, or a confined plan actually formulated by the court because necessity requires one.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not predetermine the content of any interim plan or authorize a court to adopt the County's unprecleared policy as its own.
