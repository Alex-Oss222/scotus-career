## American Airlines, Inc. v. Tice — No. 98-1704 — June 10, 1999

**Threshold and scope.** All eight reach the already-granted nonparty-preclusion question on the neutral submission premise. The Seventh Circuit's separate venue determination is outside the supplied grant and remains undisturbed. ADEA merits, BFOQ defenses, the validity of the FAA age-60 pilot/copilot rule and potential flight-officer service are not independently adjudicated.

**Common sufficient rule.** A judgment does not extinguish a nonparty pilot's individual ADEA claim merely because earlier plaintiffs challenged the same employer policy, had broadly similar interests, and employed counsel later retained by the pilot. Binding requires consent, control, genuine privity, adequate authorized representation or another recognized arrangement consistent with due process. Both claim and issue preclusion need that relationship. The actual earlier employment interests could differ, the plaintiffs were excluded from Johnson's group, the EEOC action represented 57 identified persons excluding these pilots, and the record establishes no written consent to an earlier ADEA collective action. Section 626(b)'s incorporation of §216(b) requires that written consent before an employee becomes a party plaintiff; an ordinary opt-out class analogy does not replace it.

**Current authority.** Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants, effective Richards, June 10, 1996, including the retained Matsushita representation component, supplies the prerequisite. All eight joined Richards Parts II–IV. All eight also joined South Central Bell, March 23, 1999, applying that prerequisite to claim and issue preclusion, and Dodd, November 19, 1998, giving a necessary fairly litigated finding its effect against actual parties. Hansberry, Martin v. Wilks and Shutts retain their distinct representation, hearing and class-procedure limits. None creates a duty to intervene merely on awareness of another suit.

**Alternative-path check.** American's strongest lawful alternative would establish an actual consent, control or authorized representative relationship; if proven under its own procedural requirements it could support binding even without personal appearance. Shared interests, policy, employment and later counsel do not establish one on this record. The earlier EEOC case's hiring focus and identified representation do not authorize binding these incumbent pilots. A narrower issue-preclusion label fails for the same prior prerequisite, without deciding whether a particular earlier issue was actually necessary. No Justice supports an exploratory remand to construct a new relationship. This does not declare that every imaginable relationship has affirmatively been disproved or prohibit an otherwise lawfully available defense.

**Joins and remedy.** All eight directly join the common rule, its application and affirmance of the Seventh Circuit's reversal of dismissal. Their common mandate leaves its venue affirmance undisturbed and returns the claims for ordinary proceedings on legally available issues. There is no ADEA liability finding, BFOQ rejection, damages award, guaranteed trial, compelled transfer, new class certification or reopened actual-party judgment. Earlier decisions retain precedential force and lawful force among persons actually bound.
