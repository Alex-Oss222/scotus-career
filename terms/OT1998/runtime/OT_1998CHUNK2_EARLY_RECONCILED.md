The complete lower majority, dissent and footnotes were checked in the saved [primary opinion text](../runtime/sources_a/paradise_lower.txt), reproduced by [Public.Resource.Org](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/138/138.F3d.1183.97-3668.html). The majority acknowledged a facial invalidity request but predominantly evaluated an instruction controlling future construction. Flaum disputed that characterization. His further suggestion that facial invalidation would effectively end private suits is **not** adopted by the six remand commitments. The strongest objection to remand remains the actual official disclaimer and absence of a WRTL-directed threat; the frozen six preserve that issue for the proper claim-specific inquiry rather than find a threat themselves.

The current-law test supports the frozen division. Maine Right to Life's unanimous Part I recognizes a supported, ceased-speech controversy under an effective regulation, but retains dismissal of an unthreatening Attorney General. Lee v. Harcleroad's Part III-A distinguishes an actually applicable civil duty from conjecture, while its Part III-B records these same six Justices' willingness to return for an incorrectly performed present-duty inquiry and Scalia and Thomas's refusal to order that return on the supplied showing. NAACP requires actual standing adjudication before contested election-law merits; it neither conclusively establishes standing here nor automatically compels every remand. Lujan's general elements, Bennett's supported causal link, Pic-A-State's meaningful partial relief and Baker's lawful nonparty limits all remain intact. This combination supports both the six-Justice confined return and the two-Justice conclusion that the actual official/nonparty disconnect is already dispositive. No historical magic-words premise replaces Maine's current substantive law.

## AT&T Universal Card Services v. Rembert, No. 98-352 — December 1, 1998

### Comparator and reconciliation conclusion

The complete [Sixth Circuit majority, Krupansky concurrence and footnotes](https://openjurist.org/141/f3d/277) were checked. They support the frozen distinction between the bankruptcy court's adverse finding and its expressly stated objective-impracticality premise. The lower account also reports substantial mortgage-funded and later payments. The eight commitments sustain the intermediate courts' correction on that account, not a new Supreme Court witness-credibility finding. They do not adopt the lower majority's quoted suggestion that a plan to seek bankruptcy is an additional fraud requirement, its factor list as a mandatory test, or Krupansky's proposed routine-investigation requirement. The applicable statutory text and Grogan remain the authority for enacted elements and burden; a lower court's use of Field does not establish that Field previously decided every credit-card-intent issue.

The most substantial compatibility question is Field's remedial division. Souter and its majority required correct-standard application where the findings did not resolve justifiability; Breyer and Scalia concluded that that record already satisfied the correct standard notwithstanding the label. Here all eight can accept affirmance without abandoning either position: the frozen record expressly ties the adverse intent finding to objective inability, identifies corroborating repayment conduct and supplies no unresolved independent contrary intent finding. A remand is not compulsory whenever a lower trial court used an incorrect standard. Conversely, a bare assertion of hope cannot compel discharge where a supported credibility determination or other evidence proves deceit. Those distinctions are already in the frozen commitments; reconciliation adds no new ground or changed fact.

### Reconciled shared propositions

All eight Associates affirm the Sixth Circuit judgment rejecting the presented actual-fraud exception for AT&T's debt and join R1–R3 of the frozen B commitments at their stated scope:

- **R1:** Use of a card to obtain an advance communicates a present intention to repay, not a warranty of current solvency or an objectively reasonable prospect of repayment. Actual fraud requires proof that the professed contemporaneous intent was false and deceptive. Financial condition and other objective circumstances remain evidence of state of mind; inability or unreasonable optimism alone is not a substitute for the element, and the debtor's denial is not conclusive.
- **R2:** The reported payment conduct and quoted objective-unreasonableness inference support affirmance under ordinary factual clear-error review and independent legal review. No new credibility assessment or automatic right to another hearing follows. The creditor's failed proof on this record is not converted into a chance to try a different case.
- **R3:** Actual reliance, recipient-specific justifiability, obtained-by causation and debt extent remain separate. No universal credit investigation is required, but known or personally obvious falsity and actually encountered warnings retain their consequences. Issuance, continued authorization and nonrevocation establish no automatic reliance. Section 523(a)(2)(A)'s financial-condition exclusion remains distinct from (B)'s materially false writing, reasonable reliance and debtor-caused making or publication with intent to deceive. Grogan's preponderance burden controls. No confession, heightened burden, mandatory factor count or additional bankruptcy plan is required. Distinct false-pretense or representation-only theories, (C)'s recent-debt presumption and a (d) fee claim are not decided.
