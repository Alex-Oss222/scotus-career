## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Elections and Voting

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Distinguish source of election expenditures, objective time to organize, ballot access, actual suppression and state control over election administration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A private payment to reimburse publicly administered election costs does not, standing alone, convert an election into private government or change who counts votes. A short schedule can matter if challengers prove genuine inability to campaign or an intent to disable the opposition, but the record supplies no finding of such effects. Remedy cannot be based simply on one side having greater money.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may schedule and publicly administer a referendum while accepting statutorily authorized private reimbursement for election costs if franchise equality, viewpoint neutrality, and meaningful legal participation remain intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A private payment to reimburse publicly administered election costs does not, standing alone, convert an election into private government or change who counts votes. A short schedule can matter if challengers prove genuine inability to campaign or an intent to disable the opposition, but the record supplies no finding of such effects. Remedy cannot be based simply on one side having greater money. The summary-judgment record identifies the challenged reimbursement condition and compressed campaign period, but no fee on an individual voter and no supported discriminatory purpose. Alternative petition routes also remained available. Those features support rejection of the federal challenge without endorsing every privately reimbursed election or revisiting the State’s separate constitutional holdings. Burdick’s calibrated rule applies despite Stone’s disagreement with its particular write-in result. On this summary-judgment record, reimbursement does not impose a voter qualification, and the asserted campaign disadvantage lacks evidence of exclusion or a deliberately disabling schedule. The State’s administration and financing interests consequently support this arrangement without establishing that any schedule, however restrictive, would survive.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No license for secret election rules, selective voter exclusions, private vote tabulation, intimidation or proven deliberately prohibitive campaign timing.
