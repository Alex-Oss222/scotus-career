## Threshold disposition and its exact scope

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas support vacating the appellate judgment only within the two granted components and remanding for determination of the supervening continuing-controversy question. Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer would find the supported personal-deterrence and recurrence account sufficient and reach those two merits questions. This is five Associates for the confined threshold remand and three opposed to that remand; the latter opposition is not a vote for jurisdictional dismissal.

The federal appellate-review channel and the post-grant assignment permit consideration of the two dockets; neither the grant nor 28 U.S.C. §1254 dispenses with a live Article III controversy at the time of decision. Initial standing in the 1995 litigation and continuing exposure in March 1999 remain distinct questions. Gable's past candidacy is established. The Registry's claim that he did not file by January 26, 1999 is a party assertion arising after the April 1998 appellate judgment, not a lower-court finding or a fact found here. The February 5 reply does not assert that he filed; it argues personal deterrence and recurrence. It is not evidence of an invented 1999 candidacy, withdrawal, election result, or continuing enforcement event.

The five-Justice remand asks whether Gable retains a concrete personal connection to each challenged provision through supported present deterrence or a reasonable expectation of renewed candidacy, and whether the requested relief remains capable of redressing that exposure. Formal filing is not a universal prerequisite. Nonfiling does not by itself defeat a real deterrence claim, and short election cycles cannot by themselves supply the same plaintiff's missing continuing interest. The lower courts may resolve the question on existing supported materials and ordinary properly received jurisdictional submissions. No particular document, new trial, special burden of proof, merits discovery, or deadline is ordered.

If the controversy remains live, the lower courts may proceed on the two claims under effective law. If it does not, they must determine the equitable treatment of the respective lower judgments in light of the actual cause and timing of mootness and the cross-petition's requested correction. The present limited appellate vacatur is not a factual finding of mootness, an automatic vacatur of every district-court ruling, or a directive to preserve every lower judgment regardless of the cause of mootness.

Both dockets remain identified. No. 98-897 concerns approval of the responsive-financing trigger; No. 98-1072 concerns invalidation of the own-funds branch of the late-receipt prohibition. The State's cross-petition does not manufacture a continuing controversy if the underlying requirements fail. Nor does the nonfiling assertion automatically defeat the State's requested review or determine the equitable judgment remedy. Those questions must be resolved at their actual procedural scope.

The ungranted sponsor-identification, compulsory-slating, and outside-donor merits receive no new Supreme Court disposition. In particular, the twenty-eight-day question is limited to a nonparticipating candidate's own funds. The outside-donor ruling remains outside this review without a new constitutional endorsement.

The conditional trigger division is four to reverse approval and four to affirm it; this is not an operative equal-division disposition. The actual financing mechanism remains material to both sides: ordinary participants raise qualifying contributions from $300,000 to $600,000 and receive a two-for-one match within a $1.8 million primary or regular-election ceiling, subject to the distinct opposing-slate eligibility threshold. The trigger is the required Registry certification of a nonparticipant's contributions received **or expenditures made** above the ceiling. The resulting further public funds match the participant's additional qualifying fundraising at the same ratio; they are not an automatic dollar-for-dollar payment matching the nonparticipant's expenditures. The hearing/finding and written-notification routes remain distinct. The separate runoff rule is not silently substituted.

The lower court's all-slates construction of the late-window release remains the operation reviewed, while Gable's contrary construction is preserved as a dispute. That distinction is acknowledged rather than converted into either an unlimited contemporaneous-funding right or a mere reporting rule.
