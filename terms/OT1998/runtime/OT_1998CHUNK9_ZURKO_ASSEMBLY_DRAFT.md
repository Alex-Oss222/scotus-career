## Law of the decision
**APA review of PTO factual findings.** Direct Federal Circuit review of PTO factual findings is governed by the applicable APA court-agency standard, not the clearly-erroneous standard used to review district-court findings. Section 559 preserves additional requirements imposed by statute or otherwise recognized by law, but the asserted pre-APA direct-review requirement has not been sufficiently established; the Federal Circuit must identify and apply the proper §706 category on the whole record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The PTO is an agency subject to the APA's review structure. Section 559 does preserve antecedent legal requirements; its expressness rule for later statutes is not a command that every pre-APA rule must have appeared in an express statute. The asserted exception nevertheless needs an identifiable legal foundation. The earlier decisions use varied standards in different settings, including interference suits, equity proceedings and direct appeals. Morgan v. Daniels addressed priority between contestants in a subsequent equity proceeding. Its requirement of a thorough conviction does not establish a general direct-appeal relationship equivalent to review of district-court facts. Neither the later specialized court's practice nor patent uniformity supplies the missing preserved requirement.

Universal Camera demonstrates that agency review remains meaningful on the whole record, including contrary evidence, while preserving agency choice between fairly conflicting views. Allentown Mack requires application of the actual governing standard; it does not authorize substitution of judicial factfinding. Haggar concerns statutory interpretation and does not determine the standard for this factual appeal. The Federal Circuit expressly regarded the standard as outcome-determinative, so the difference cannot be dismissed as wording. The Court remands without choosing between §706(2)(A) and (E), deciding the prior-art fact or ultimate obviousness, ordering a new agency hearing, or governing district-court findings on a new evidentiary record. Section 706's whole-record and prejudicial-error instructions remain applicable on their terms.
