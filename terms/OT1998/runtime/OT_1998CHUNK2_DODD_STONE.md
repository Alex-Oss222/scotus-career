## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Required state litigation and reservation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** The original *England* exception arises from federal abstention; direct state litigation is governed by §1738.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An *England* reservation preserves federal questions after federal-court abstention; it is not a unilateral option to defeat §1738 when a party voluntarily litigates dispositive matters in state court. The result is difficult because then-current ripeness can channel owners to state court, but hardship cannot rewrite preclusion law. ### Issue 2: Issue-preclusion proof and consequence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Only facts/issues actually, necessarily and fairly decided can bind the litigants.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An *England* reservation preserves federal questions after federal-court abstention; it is not a unilateral option to defeat §1738 when a party voluntarily litigates dispositive matters in state court. The result is difficult because then-current ripeness can channel owners to state court, but hardship cannot rewrite preclusion law. If the record shows a federal element not actually litigated, that element survives on remand under ordinary rules.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Only facts/issues actually, necessarily and fairly decided can bind the litigants.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Preclusion extends only to the identical issue actually litigated, necessarily decided and subject to a full and fair opportunity under the governing Oregon rules. A reservation of a federal legal claim does not erase findings meeting those requirements, but a broad label of takings litigation cannot establish that every federal issue was resolved. The court must identify the particular state findings and explain their necessity and effect; any materially undecided federal element remains for adjudication.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No holding that every state takings adjudication categorically bars every federal claim, and no application of later Supreme Court takings-ripeness decisions.
