## Disposition, rule and exact scope

All eight Associates support reversal of the en banc Fifth Circuit judgment and remand for further appellate proceedings. They join the complete following rule, application and reservations. No separate writing is required.

There is no unyielding requirement that a federal court resolve subject-matter jurisdiction before dismissing for lack of personal jurisdiction, including in a removed action. Ordinarily the court should address a readily resolved subject-matter question first. When personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law issue while the asserted subject-matter defect is difficult and novel, the court may choose the personal-jurisdiction ground without first deciding subject-matter jurisdiction. That choice denies adjudicatory authority over the defendant; it does not assume power to decide contested liability. State-law difficulty and the State's forum interest bear on the choice. Where subject-matter jurisdiction is as readily resolved and personal jurisdiction presents difficult state law, those concerns ordinarily favor deciding remand first. This is a bounded permission, not a direction invariably to choose the easier issue or a categorical prohibition whenever some state law is involved.

The District Court was permitted to choose the personal-jurisdiction inquiry here. The reversal does **not** independently affirm the correctness of its contact findings or ultimate lack-of-personal-jurisdiction conclusion. Those matters remain for the Fifth Circuit under the proper sequence. Historical footnote 8 expressly confirms that the correctness of the dismissal falls outside the question presented; this limit accords with the clean model's own bounded review.

### Sufficient peer rule retained by the reversal supporters

These five directly support a recipient's damages responsibility when the following independent conditions are established: receipt of federal assistance; harassment because of sex; actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger; substantial school control over both the harasser and the relevant educational context; deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that known danger; and a causal contribution to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. Actual corrective-official notice remains necessary for this sufficient route; generic constructive notice, technical regulatory noncompliance and the harasser's own knowledge do not establish it. Culpability must be more than negligent or unsuccessful correction. A reasonable response is not unlawful merely because harassment continues, and courts do not prescribe a chosen sanction or the victim's preferred remedy. Constitutional and statutory limits on discipline inform the control and response inquiries; substantial control is not assumed over every location, official or student interaction. ## Path R — O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas

### Rule, price application and quality application

An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry can suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make the likely anticompetitive tendency clear enough to assess it and the material competing explanation. Here the professional market's information disparities make the effects of the challenged restrictions insufficiently obvious for the analysis employed below. More examination of the actual price and quality restraints is required. No fixed sequence of labels or mandatory full industry study replaces the rule of reason; the inquiry must fit the circumstances, details and logic of each restraint.

**Price advertising:** Price communication ordinarily supports comparison and rivalry, and the reported enforcement impedes fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements. But an across-the-board discount without an intelligible baseline or scope may convey less reliable information than accurately specified services and prices. Requiring particular disclosure might improve comparison, harm it or do both. Treating the practical restriction on discount advertising as sufficient proof of harmful net competitive tendency, and demanding empirical justification from CDA on that basis alone, does not adequately examine the professional-information explanation. The point is the legal adequacy of the analysis, not a finding that every advertisement is deceptive, every disclosure condition useful, or CDA's justification ultimately correct.

**Quality advertising:** Restrictions can hinder useful comparison and service rivalry, but professional quality can be difficult for patients to verify. Restrictions on unverifiable or misleading claims may improve information. The court below could not resolve that competing account merely by equating fewer advertisements with a supply limitation in dental services. A possible effect on demand is not itself proof of producer-imposed restriction of service output. This does not deny that quality-advertising restrictions may harm competition, require proof of a measured output decline in every antitrust case, or approve a blanket ban. The actual effect and justification need the fuller analysis missing here.

NCAA supplies flexible analysis where competitive tendencies are established. Professional Engineers rejects a defense that competition itself is undesirable.
