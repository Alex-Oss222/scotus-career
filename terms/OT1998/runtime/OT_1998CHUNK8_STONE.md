# Clinton v. Goldsmith

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment of the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces and vacate its injunction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The injunction shall be dissolved. Goldsmith remains free to pursue any independently available administrative or judicial route challenging the removal, and no view is expressed on the merits of such a claim.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: All Writs authority of a military appellate court

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the received jurisdictional limit. The military appellate court may protect review of findings and sentences lawfully before it, but it may not supervise a separate executive personnel action merely because the same officer was court-martialed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The All Writs Act permits a military appellate court to protect only jurisdiction independently conferred over court-martial findings and sentences; it does not authorize an injunction against a separate administrative personnel action outside that statutory review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Dropping Goldsmith from the rolls was not imposed by the court-martial and would not alter the findings or sentence then within the military appellate process. The asserted constitutional importance of the claim cannot supply the missing jurisdiction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not determine the validity of the removal statute, foreclose an Article III action in a proper forum, or restrict a writ genuinely necessary to preserve lawful military appellate review.

# Hunt v. Cromartie

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the district court’s injunction and remand under Johnson v. Miller.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject racial predominance alone as a sufficient cause of action. Address only actually preserved independent constitutional claims under their own elements and ordinary procedural rules; do not require an otherwise unnecessary motive trial.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Summary judgment on racial predominance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only whether the disputed predominance question could be resolved against the State on summary judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Vacate the injunction because racial predominance alone is not an actionable constitutional deprivation under Johnson v. Miller. A trial over racial versus partisan motive is required only if that dispute is material to an independently cognizable and preserved claim.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A districting injunction cannot rest solely on a finding that race predominated. Remand must apply Johnson v. Miller to the actual asserted injury and may resolve factual disputes only when they matter to a viable claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The competing explanations of the boundary lines may create factual disagreement, but materiality depends on the legal elements. Johnson v. Miller accepts that race may predominate without imposing inferior status, racial eligibility or a representative legally answerable to only one race. The summary judgment cannot stand on the predominance inference alone. If the plaintiffs identify a separately cognizable denial of equal participation or treatment, the court must evaluate its pleaded facts and evidentiary support, including motive when legally relevant. Remand does not require trial merely to determine which label best describes the same shared constituency.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide whether race predominated, whether the district is constitutional, or which scrutiny applies after a supported predominance finding.

# Florida v. White

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Supreme Court of Florida's judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The warrantless seizure and its direct fruits remain suppressed. Florida may continue any prosecution supported by untainted evidence and may pursue forfeiture through a warrant or another lawful process.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Planned warrantless forfeiture seizure of a parked automobile

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone distinguishes *Labron*. Its automobile exception concerns a contemporaneous evidentiary search of a readily mobile vehicle believed to contain contraband or evidence; it does not create a standing warrant exception for a planned civil-forfeiture seizure based on historical use. Probable cause establishes the basis for a warrant, but where officers have a reasonable opportunity to obtain one and identify no concrete urgency, it does not itself excuse neutral judicial process.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Police may not make a planned warrantless seizure of a parked automobile for civil forfeiture solely because probable cause exists and the vehicle is inherently mobile. Absent consent or another exception, a warrant is required when officers had a reasonable opportunity to obtain one and no concrete urgency attended the seizure.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The investigation matured over months, White was arrested away from the car, and officers controlled the scene. They identified no attempted flight, immediate transfer, evidence-destruction risk, or other reason process could not be obtained. *Labron* therefore does not govern, and the later inventory search cannot validate the antecedent unlawful seizure.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require a warrant for an exigent roadside seizure, a contemporaneous evidence search within *Labron*, contraband whose possession itself is unlawful, or a vehicle secured briefly while a warrant is diligently sought.

# Maine Green Party v. Maine

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm First Circuit’s rejection of the facial challenge to the five-percent statewide party-retention rule, leaving concrete as-applied exclusion claims open.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm First Circuit’s rejection of the facial challenge to the five-percent statewide party-retention rule, leaving concrete as-applied exclusion claims open.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Elections and Voting

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Weigh actual access and requalification costs, alternative candidacy methods and relation of 5% threshold to legitimate state-recognition roles; do not assume a statewide party is entitled to recognition on city-only vote.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Statewide privileges can rationally depend on demonstrated statewide electoral support. The law does not itself ban political association or nonqualifying candidates from speaking; though local electoral organizing has constitutional significance, local success alone does not compel all statewide party administrative entitlements. This holding depends on the actual existence of feasible alternative ballot paths; a prohibitive requalification record could change the burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A five-percent statewide presidential-vote criterion for retaining statewide party recognition is not facially unconstitutional when supported by legitimate statewide administrative interests and workable alternative ballot access remains available.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The initial papers invoked the right to organize as members saw fit, cited Eu and Tashjian, and challenged the same presidential-vote condition. Sharpening that associational argument does not automatically create a new claim; the district court did not expressly reject it as late, and Maine did not seek that result. Stone would reach the legal argument on the established record, while refusing to find an exclusive philosophical commitment contradicted or left uncertain by the national-organizing affidavit. On that record, Timmons and Burdick require examination of actual access, not automatic deference to a statewide label. Maine’s support measure is tied to the privileges of statewide recognition; the party remains able to organize, endorse, campaign and use the alternative candidate routes. Its 1994 qualification through an independent gubernatorial candidate demonstrates the operation of an available route. The record does not establish that requalification and alternative access are practically prohibitive. Those features support affirmance without approving a categorical requirement that every local political association nominate a presidential candidate. A developed record of compelled abandonment of a genuinely local organizing choice, with no workable ballot alternative, would require a different burden-and-fit inquiry.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No declaration that a party must compete nationally to engage in local politics, no blanket validation of any requalification procedure, and no importation of later party status precedents.

# Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Fifth Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts shall allow Cleveland to offer a legally and factually sufficient explanation of the prior statements, then decide qualification, accommodation, and any remaining defenses under the ADA. No liability finding is made.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Effect of SSDI representations on an ADA claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone rejects automatic conversion of a representation under one statutory scheme into a dispositive element under another. Candor still requires the claimant to reconcile materially inconsistent factual positions rather than ignore them.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A claim or award of SSDI does not automatically estop an ADA plaintiff from proving qualification, because the schemes ask materially different questions; the plaintiff must, however, explain any apparent factual inconsistency sufficiently to permit a reasonable finding that she could perform the essential job functions with reasonable accommodation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** SSDI may treat a person as disabled without considering a specific employer accommodation. Cleveland can therefore reconcile the positions by identifying the relevant time, job, and accommodation, but a bare contradiction would not survive summary judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require an employer to provide an unreasonable accommodation, prevent use of prior sworn statements as evidence, or guarantee that Cleveland’s explanation will be sufficient.

# Wilson v. Layne

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's qualified-immunity judgment, hold that the media entry violated the Fourth Amendment, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts shall proceed on the damages claims against the participating officers without qualified immunity for the warrant-unrelated media intrusion, subject to ordinary causation, damages, personal-participation, and other preserved defenses.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Unrelated media presence during home entry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Fourth Amendment merits before immunity.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A warrant permits the intrusion reasonably necessary to locate persons or evidence, preserve safety, and perform other lawful execution functions. Bringing private media representatives into the home for a purpose unrelated to execution exceeds that authority and is unreasonable.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Officers violate the Fourth Amendment when they bring private observers into a home under a warrant although those persons serve no function reasonably related to lawful execution.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The media personnel did not assist the search, identify persons, preserve evidence, protect safety, or perform a function required by law. Their presence enlarged the residential intrusion beyond the warrant's law-enforcement justification.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar interpreters, medical personnel, technical specialists, witnesses, or other persons whose presence is reasonably related to lawful execution.

### Issue 2: Qualified immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches immunity after identifying the complete merits rule.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing clearly-established-law inquiry and deny immunity because the asserted entry was an obvious enlargement of a home warrant for a wholly unrelated private purpose. The absence of a case naming media ride-alongs is not by itself decisive, but neither does the merits conclusion automatically defeat immunity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The obvious mismatch between the warrant's authority and the media's unrelated presence defeats qualified immunity even without a prior media-ride-along case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant notice comes from law existing when officers acted, including the protection of the home and the purpose-bound scope of warrant execution. Ramirez may inform present understanding but its later date cannot supply earlier notice. Stone finds that inviting spectators who identify no suspect, preserve no evidence and assist no lawful task crosses the established boundary in an obvious way; this is his application of the fair-notice rule, not a replacement of it with automatic damages liability. The court must still resolve individual responsibility, causation, injury and other preserved defenses.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not deny immunity for an ordinary reasonable mistake about execution details or for a private person's presence reasonably connected to the warrant's lawful purposes.

# Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall allow the Title IX claim to proceed and determine recipient fault, severity, educational denial, causation, and damages. The Court makes no final liability finding and prescribes no particular disciplinary measure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Recipient liability for peer sexual harassment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Gebser’s recipient-fault rule to peer conduct under school control. Liability rests on the recipient’s own deliberate choice after reliable notice or an institutional reporting failure amounting to willful blindness, not on automatic responsibility for a student’s act.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable in damages when severe, pervasive, and objectively offensive student-on-student sexual harassment occurs in a context the recipient substantially controls, deprives the victim of equal educational access, and the recipient’s actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes the harassment to continue.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged reports to teachers and the principal, repeated sexual touching and comments during school, ineffective responses and resulting educational disruption concern the district’s own response to a known danger within its control. Kinman’s actual-notice and deliberate-disregard route remains available after Gebser. The additional authority-based attribution route in Gebser concerned teachers and cannot simply attribute a classmate’s misconduct to the district. Stone would reach peer harassment because the alleged actionable discrimination is the recipient’s own deliberately indifferent failure to use available authority to protect equal educational access. The plaintiff must prove knowledge, clearly inadequate response, meaningful control, sex-based educational deprivation and causal injury; neither peer misconduct nor negligent supervision alone establishes liability.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not impose strict liability, federalize ordinary teasing, require a particular punishment, or reach conduct outside the recipient’s substantial control. Officials retain disciplinary discretion so long as the response is not deliberately indifferent.

# California Dental Association v. Federal Trade Commission

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm FTC jurisdiction, vacate the liability determination, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Commission shall conduct a fuller, restraint-sensitive competitive inquiry sufficient to test the plausible anticompetitive effects and asserted consumer-information justifications. The Court does not require a full market-definition trial in every case or immunize the challenged rules.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: FTC jurisdiction over the nonprofit association

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory coverage before liability.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** An association carries on business for its members' profit when its activities materially advance their commercial operations, even if the association itself is organized as a nonprofit.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nonprofit association carries on business for its members' profit when its activities materially advance their commercial operations.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Association provides insurance, financing, marketing, lobbying, and other economically valuable services to profit-seeking dentists. Sections 4 and 5 therefore reach its commercial regulatory conduct despite its nonprofit form.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make every nonprofit organization subject to FTC jurisdiction without a material connection to members' profit-making business.

### Issue 2: Depth of antitrust analysis

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether the abbreviated analysis used below was sufficient, not final liability.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Abbreviated antitrust review is sufficient only when anticompetitive tendency is apparent without substantial market study. This mixed record requires enough analysis to test both suppression of truthful competition and plausible information-quality justifications.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Abbreviated antitrust review is sufficient only when anticompetitive tendency is apparent without substantial market study; this mixed record requires a fuller, restraint-sensitive inquiry.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The price and quality restrictions may impede useful consumer information, but the record also raises claims about unverifiable quality statements and misleading discount formats. *Khan* requires an inquiry proportionate to those plausible effects rather than a conclusory quick look.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates neither immunity for restrictions on truthful, nonmisleading advertising nor a full market-definition requirement in every case escaping abbreviated review.

# Hanlon v. Berger

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm Ninth Circuit’s denial of qualified immunity and remand for proceedings on the supported constitutional injury and available remedies.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm Ninth Circuit’s denial of qualified immunity and remand for proceedings on the supported constitutional injury and available remedies.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fourth Amendment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Separate constitutional intrusion and personal immunity. A warrant does not grant a media license; objective notice can exist without an identical factual precedent.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Invited filming by uninvolved reporters served no valid warrant task; state entry authority remains purpose-limited. The private ranch context differs from entry into a dwelling but private-property intrusion under the warrant is not converted into authorized broadcast production. The obvious-purpose defect supplies adequate notice on the facts identified; merely novel camera technology does not create qualified immunity for official invitees.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Official invitation of a media crew to a warrant search for independent filming, when not reasonably needed to execute the warrant, exceeds that warrant and is objectively unlawful; factually identical precedent is unnecessary for obvious violation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A warrant authorizes entry for its legal execution, not use of the search target’s protected property as a setting for private broadcast production. Stone would find an invasion where the camera crew entered space protected by the plaintiffs’ own Fourth Amendment interest and served no warrant-related purpose; open fields cannot simply be presumed protected because they lie on a ranch. Immunity then requires separate examination of clearly established law at the time. Stone regards admitting unrelated private spectators under official search authority as an obvious purpose-and-scope violation, but does not use a later Wilson disposition or Ramirez decision to supply earlier notice. Each officer’s responsibility and any distinct private-actor claim retain their own elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding on disputed damages, camera crew’s independent torts, admissible proof or police documentation genuinely essential to lawful execution.
