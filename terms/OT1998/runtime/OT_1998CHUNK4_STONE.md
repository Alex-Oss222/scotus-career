# Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives; Clinton v. Glavin

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgments prohibiting the proposed sampling methods and vacate the injunctions; resolve any remaining House-specific jurisdictional question without treating consolidation as standing.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for proceedings consistent with §§141 and 195 permitting sampling. No injunction survives solely on the rejected categorical statutory or constitutional prohibitions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Standing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which action permits merits review?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reach the individual plaintiffs’ adequately supported representation claim; do not infer the House’s standing from that claim.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reach the individual plaintiffs’ adequately supported representation claim; do not infer the House’s standing from that claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The projected allocation consequences give the individual plaintiffs a concrete stake in the method. The merits can be decided in their action. The House’s suit requires its own institutional-injury analysis; after the operative injunction is vacated, its remaining demand cannot preserve relief on a statutory or constitutional theory rejected in the live companion case.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general legislative standing rule.

### Issue 2: Statutory sampling authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does §195 withdraw authority supplied by §141?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 195 removes the obligation to use sampling for apportionment; it does not forbid its use.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 195 removes the obligation to use sampling for apportionment; it does not forbid its use.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 141(a) expressly permits sampling in the decennial census. Section 195 speaks in mandatory terms about when sampling shall be used. Its exception makes that mandate inapplicable to apportionment. Reading the exception as a prohibition inserts a restriction Congress did not express and unnecessarily defeats the affirmative authority in §141. Both provisions retain work: sampling is discretionary for apportionment and required where feasible for other authorized uses. Wisconsin reserved this construction, so its constitutional deference does not supply the statutory answer.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Secretary must remain within the actual statutory design.

### Issue 3: Enumeration Clause

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the proposed statistical methods be used?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A reasonable population-counting method may include sampling; a categorical headcount-only rule is unwarranted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A reasonable population-counting method may include sampling; a categorical headcount-only rule is unwarranted.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An actual enumeration requires a genuine effort to determine state populations, not adherence to a counting technique known to omit people. Conventional enumeration itself involves imputation, correction and uncertainty. Under Wisconsin, the constitutional inquiry is whether the selected method reasonably serves accurate representation, not whether it is statistically flawless. The categorical objections here do not justify the injunctions. Franklin preserves review of the completed determination and its explanation, so recognition of methodological discretion does not immunize arbitrary implementation. Wisconsin does not compel adjustment or sampling: the geographic reliability explanation justified nonadjustment there. Here Stone’s statutory construction permits the Secretary to select a reasonably supported method; it does not turn permission into a constitutional duty or resolve later implementation disputes by national accuracy alone.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No advance approval of unexplained future methodological changes.

# AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit on FCC implementation authority, Rule 315(b), and the § 252(i) rule; reverse its sustaining judgment on Rule 319, vacate Rule 319, and remand to the Commission.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The FCC retains national implementation authority without displacing functions Congress assigned to state commissions. Rule 319 is vacated and returned to the Commission for a lawful element-specific application of 'necessary' and 'impair.' Rules 315(b) and 252(i) remain in force within the limits stated below.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: FCC authority to implement §§ 251 and 252

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the delegation question before reviewing individual rules.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Sections 201(b), 251, and 252 authorize national FCC implementation of the federal local-competition duties while preserving the agreement, arbitration, approval, and rate functions Congress specifically assigned to state commissions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The FCC has authority to implement §§ 251 and 252 without displacing functions Congress specifically assigned to state commissions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory scheme combines national duties and rulemaking with state implementation responsibilities. Treating the state assignments as exclusive federal-rulemaking bars would frustrate the enacted structure, while recognizing FCC authority does not erase the state functions Congress named.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize the FCC to displace an adjudicative or implementation function Congress assigned specifically to a state commission.

### Issue 2: Rule 319 and the 'necessary' and 'impair' limits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the validity of Rule 319.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Under *MCI*, the Commission must give 'necessary' and 'impair' independent constraining effect. Rule 319 is invalid because it does not make the element-specific statutory judgments those terms require.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Rule 319 is invalid because it fails to give independent effect to 'necessary' and 'impair,' and it must be remanded.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule effectively treated broad competitive usefulness as enough to compel access. That approach eliminates rather than administers the statutory predicates. The proper remedy is vacatur and agency reconsideration, not judicial selection of a replacement list.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not prescribe the final unbundling list or foreclose a different rule supported by the enacted criteria.

### Issue 3: Separation of already combined elements

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Rule 315(b).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The statute requires access to unbundled elements but does not require incumbents to separate combinations they already maintain before leasing them. Rule 315(b) therefore remains within the delegation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An incumbent may not separate already combined network elements solely because a requesting carrier seeks them, although the rule does not require creation of combinations the incumbent does not maintain.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted term describes the carrier's access rights and pricing treatment, not a mandatory act of physical disassembly. The rule preserves existing combinations without commanding incumbents to create new ones.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require an incumbent to assemble a combination it does not already maintain.

### Issue 4: Section 252(i) pick-and-choose rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the § 252(i) rule.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 252(i)'s services-and-elements language permits a requesting carrier to adopt individual qualifying terms from an approved agreement on equal conditions, subject to material cost and comparability distinctions allowed by the statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The § 252(i) rule permitting adoption of individual qualifying services or network-element terms remains within the enacted delegation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress required approved terms to be available to similarly situated carriers. A rule limited to qualifying terms and equal conditions implements that command rather than rewriting negotiated agreements wholesale.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not foreclose material cost, technical-feasibility, or comparability distinctions that the statute permits.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fiduciary status when amending the plan

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether adoption of the amendments was fiduciary conduct.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Lockheed*: plan design and amendment are settlor functions. *Varity* preserves fiduciary responsibility for administration and communications but does not convert the design decision itself into fiduciary conduct.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Adopting these plan amendments was nonfiduciary settlor conduct.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hughes acted in its plan-sponsor capacity when it changed contribution structures and benefit options. No allegation about that design choice alone states a fiduciary breach.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not immunize fiduciary misrepresentation, discriminatory administration, investment misconduct, or diversion of plan assets.

### Issue 2: Surplus ownership and anti-cutback

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the surplus and accrued-benefit theories.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Defined-benefit participants are entitled to the benefits fixed by the plan, not to ownership of actuarial surplus. An amendment that leaves those accrued benefits intact does not violate the anti-cutback rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Defined-benefit participants possess enforceable rights to their accrued benefits, not ownership of actuarial surplus, and no accrued benefit was reduced here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The amendments changed plan financing and options but did not reduce any fixed accrued benefit. *John Hancock* does not convert overfunding into participant-owned accounts.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve underfunding, nonpayment of promised benefits, an actual cutback, or misuse of plan assets.

### Issue 3: Partial termination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the partial-termination theory on the undisputed continuing-plan facts.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A continuing plan is not partially terminated merely because contribution structures and benefit options change while the plan continues to accept participants and pay benefits.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The continuing plan was not terminated merely because contribution structures and benefit options changed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hughes did not terminate the plan or a qualifying severable segment; it continued the plan under amended terms. The asserted changes therefore do not establish the statutory termination predicate.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern a genuine plan termination, mass exclusion, or other event satisfying the enacted termination provisions.

# Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Status of assigned curricular expression

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The claim was finally dismissed, and no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle defect prevents review of the granted question.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies current simulated law. Selection, production, and direction of a play through an assigned public-school drama course and competition are acts of curricular administration performed within the teacher's official responsibilities. They are not transformed into the teacher's personal First Amendment speech because artistic judgment is involved or because the curricular choice provokes public controversy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public-school teacher's selection, production, and direction of material pursuant to assigned teaching responsibilities constitute institutional curricular expression subject to the school system's lawful control, not the teacher's personal speech protected from that control by the First Amendment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The complaint identifies an assigned class, school-sponsored production and interscholastic activity for which the school bears institutional responsibility. Stone would treat this particular content-selection decision as curricular work entrusted to school management, rather than an off-duty address like Jeffries. Settle prevents equating the word curriculum with constitutional immunity: genuine pedagogical grounds cannot conceal a separately actionable forbidden classification or exclusion. But a student’s limited right to submit an otherwise permissible research perspective does not establish a teacher’s personal right to insist that the institution produce her selected play. On the claim presented, the alleged curricular controversy does not convert that assigned function into the personal speech interest asserted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize retaliation for a teacher's separate citizen speech, permit religious or other independently unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination, or decide statutory, contractual, tenure, or state-law limits on curricular and employment authority.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** No additional constitutional scrutiny is required for the curricular and related employment decisions challenged on this theory. A distinct adverse action directed at separate citizen speech would be governed by the public-employee doctrine, but that is not the granted claim.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No holding is entered on the constitutional standard applicable to a teacher who establishes a distinct personal speech interest outside assigned curricular responsibilities.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The conditional premise of Question 2 is absent. Reaching a balancing standard after rejecting the asserted personal speech interest would produce an advisory merits ruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A later case presenting separate citizen speech, pretext for an independently forbidden purpose, or unequal treatment outside curricular control remains governed by its own source and record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Health-Care Programs

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Board statutory jurisdiction and ordinary reviewability are separate; a discretion-to-reopen decision does not reopen original finality absent actual reconsideration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1395oo defines appealable final reimbursement determinations and a filing period, whereas a regulation allows limited discretionary reopening. Reading a refusal as an independent appealable determination would erase the text’s timing choice and let any provider manufacture federal review by filing for reopening. The Board does not acquire authority merely because the matter is important or the claimed cost error credible.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A fiscal intermediary’s discretionary refusal to reopen an already-final reimbursement determination is not itself a reviewable final determination within §1395oo(a).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1395oo defines appealable final reimbursement determinations and a filing period, whereas a regulation allows limited discretionary reopening. Reading a refusal as an independent appealable determination would erase the text’s timing choice and let any provider manufacture federal review by filing for reopening. The Board does not acquire authority merely because the matter is important or the claimed cost error credible.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual reopening followed by a new determination, timely initial appeals, regulatory procedural deviations, and any independently authorized constitutional channel remain unresolved.

# Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the injunction and remand with instructions to dismiss the collateral action for want of jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district-court injunction shall dissolve and the separate action shall be dismissed. The judgment does not decide whether the constitutional objection may later be heard on final-order review or through another channel that is constitutionally indispensable on materially different facts.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Scope and temporal application of § 1252(g)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves the jurisdictional channel before any selective-enforcement merits question.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1252(g), as applied to pending matters by the transition provision, withdraws separate district-court jurisdiction over claims arising from the decisions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases, or execute orders. The provision is confined to those three named acts.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1252(g) bars this separate pending district-court action because it seeks to restrain the Attorney General's decision to commence removal proceedings, one of the statute's three named actions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The requested injunction directly stops commencement and continuation of the removal proceedings. Applying the channel to the pending suit changes forum and timing rather than imposing new liability, while the statute is not expanded to every event occurring during removal.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not cover detention, evidence gathering, adjudicative procedure, or legal errors merely because they arise during removal.

### Issue 2: Immediate constitutional review and selective-enforcement merits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not reach whether retaliation occurred. He decides only whether this record requires immediate freestanding review despite § 1252(g).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Because the record shows neither permanent loss of review nor an independently irremediable injury from allowing proceedings to continue, immediate collateral constitutional adjudication is not required. Later final-order review and a genuinely now-or-never constitutional channel remain unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** This record does not require immediate freestanding constitutional review, and later or otherwise constitutionally necessary review remains unresolved.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional theory can be preserved while the statutory proceedings continue, and the present record does not establish that all meaningful review will disappear. A merits ruling would therefore be advisory after the jurisdictional disposition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve retaliation, decide the selective-enforcement merits, or hold that Congress may eliminate every constitutionally adequate review route.
