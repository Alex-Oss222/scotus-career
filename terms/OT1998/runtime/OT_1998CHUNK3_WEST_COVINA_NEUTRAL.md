### Issue 1: Content of post-seizure notice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property* requires pre-seizure process for civil forfeiture of real property absent proved exigency and expressly leaves readily movable personal property unresolved; it does not itself establish a post-seizure legal-instruction rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Pre-seizure process for civil forfeiture of real property, distinguished.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; applicable California return-of-property procedures.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.* requires notice of the governmental deprivation sufficient to permit response; *Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft* requires procedural instructions when the operative route is unpublished or otherwise inaccessible.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit held that the City’s seizure notice was constitutionally inadequate because it did not explain California’s remedies for return of property.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The owners seek notice explaining how to recover seized property. The City argues that notice of the seizure and custodian, together with generally published state procedures, satisfies due process. A personalized explanation requirement differs from a challenge to the accessibility or adequacy of the return procedure itself.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The court may reject an individualized legal-guide duty while leaving a properly presented inadequate-procedure claim open. If existing information prevents a meaningful opportunity to seek return, remedy must address that identified defect rather than all property seizures.
