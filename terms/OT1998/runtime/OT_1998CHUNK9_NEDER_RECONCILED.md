## 1. Verified comparator and the decisive disagreement

The abbreviated [comparator](../runtime/chunk9-scoped/NEDER_COMPARATOR.md) says simply affirmed, 6–3. The complete historical judgment was **affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded**: the tax-count judgment was affirmed; the remaining-count judgment was reversed for proceedings consistent with the fraud-materiality ruling and initial harmlessness review. The historical tax affirmance had Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer. Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg opposed tax affirmance. All nine joined Parts I and III, including statutory materiality and the first-instance fraud harmlessness remand. Only Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer joined Parts II and IV. Stevens concurred in the judgment on a different tax rationale. This historical authorship supplies no assignment here.

The full [Court opinion](../runtime/chunk9-control/neder-court.txt), [Stevens opinion](../runtime/chunk9-control/neder-stevens.txt), and [Scalia opinion, joined by Souter and Ginsburg](../runtime/chunk9-control/neder-scalia.txt) were read, including every footnote. The [official report](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep527/usrep527001/usrep527001.pdf), 527 U.S. 1, independently confirms the mixed judgment, the writing structure, Stevens's precise annual-income reasoning at pages 26–27, and Scalia's express rejection of it at pages 35–36, note 2. Cornell's Scalia writing uses suffix `ZX`, not `ZC1`.

Three propositions must remain distinct:

1. The historical Court's Part II permitted affirmance when uncontested, overwhelming proof established beyond a reasonable doubt that a rational jury would have convicted absent the omission. Its footnote 1 denied that this jury actually made a materiality finding after being told not to do so.
2. Stevens instead relied on the actual instruction requiring knowing falsity of the particular annual-income totals. He understood those findings to include the information's material relation to determining income-tax liability. He rejected appellate supplementation based on what another jury would have found. That is substantially the same tax ground identified independently in the frozen commitments.
3. Scalia's note 2 expressly rejected Stevens's precise ground. A knowing understatement of total income did not, for him, become the jury's own application of materiality merely because a judge could logically derive its importance. His examples of a very small understatement or offsetting deductions illustrated the allocation objection; they were not findings that those circumstances existed here. Ginsburg and Souter joined that objection historically. Scalia nevertheless accepted genuine equivalent findings elsewhere in his opinion. It is therefore inaccurate both to call his view automatic reversal for every omission and to infer that his acceptance of functional equivalence resolves this particular tax application in favor of affirmance.

The new trial extracts confirm the same historical charge that Stevens cited. Better verification of an unchanged historical fact is not a changed in-world record. A departure cannot be justified by saying the Associates now have an annual-total instruction that the historical dissent lacked.

## 4. Common statutory and procedural commitments

All eight retain the independent commitments' provision-specific construction. Materiality of the deceptive representation is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under sections 1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under section 1344. The settled legal content of fraud supplies that limitation. Morissette's borrowed-term principle supports it; Durland includes fraudulent future promises without eliminating materiality. The term scheme does not import completed common-law reliance or damage. Neither successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, nor status as the sole reason for the transaction is required. Mail and wire use remain distinct requirements, and the communication itself need not contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Materiality here concerns a representation's natural tendency or capacity, in its transaction context, to influence the decision it targets. The independent commitments do not purport to exhaust every common-law materiality formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. In particular, the historical Court's quotation of Restatement section 538's reasonable-person and known-recipient alternatives is not silently converted into a new universal test beyond the frozen commitments' present financial-deception holding. Neither a new exception nor rejection of an unpresented alternative follows from that reservation.

Section 1344's two routes remain distinct: a scheme to defraud a covered institution and a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. The present construction does not resolve every difference between their mental-state requirements or other applications. Wells's different section 1014 holding remains intact. Purpose to influence under that section does not automatically establish objective materiality in these fraud counts. Stevens's continued disagreement with Wells does not overrule it or reopen section 1014 convictions.

Section 7206(1)'s materiality requirement rests independently on its express text. Gaudin requires the jury to apply an actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; the judge may define the law but cannot withdraw its application. The six tax-affirming Associates do not legalize the trial instruction. Scalia and Ginsburg do not dispute that a genuine equivalent finding can make a particular omission harmless. They disagree with the wider method and with its use to sustain these tax verdicts.

Sullivan retains its rule for a defective reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all findings. Johnson's forfeited-error fourth-condition judgment does not supply the disposition of these preserved objections. The [Richardson decision](../entering-law/richardson_v_united_states_merits_1999-06-01.md) reserves availability and application of harmless-error review for its distinct predicate-unanimity defect. Nothing here backfills that reservation. Jones protects charged statutory elements but does not itself classify this omission; Mitchell rejects substituting sufficient other evidence for a showing that the error did not contribute.

## 5. Six-Associate method and tax application

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer support applying Chapman to these materiality errors as trial errors. The Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Under the [prior Roy public decision](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK9_ROY_PUBLIC_POSITIONS.md), supported whole-record inferences are permissible in addition to necessary equivalent findings, but only after examining the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts. If the omission prevents a reliable connection to this jury's verdict, strong evidence alone does not suffice. Neither an uncontested-element label nor likely conviction by a properly instructed jury is a substitute for this inquiry.

For the two tax counts, the sufficient common ground is the connection between the actual annual-income findings and the information's role in assessing those returns. The charge positively required the jury to resolve whether these identified totals were knowingly and purposely false. The loan-character and advice defenses were presented to that task. The verdict therefore establishes the particular falsity and culpability, not merely that the prosecution offered persuasive evidence. Its subject is the annual income the return reports for tax administration, rather than an unidentified collateral statement. On this charge and record, the six conclude beyond a reasonable doubt that removing a separate materiality determination did not contribute to the tax verdicts.

Their conclusion does not require a finding that every juror accepted the alleged multimillion-dollar amounts, an unpaid-tax calculation, an actual IRS response, or successful deception. It does not infer that jurors disobeyed the express materiality withdrawal. It does not relieve future courts of submitting materiality, even when a judge regards a reported income statement as plainly significant. The reviewing court must still perform the supported actual-verdict inquiry; a generic false-return verdict is insufficient by itself.

The strongest objection is that the last step still assigns significance to findings whose materiality the jury was forbidden to assess. Scalia and Ginsburg accept the annual-income findings as facts but regard the added significance judgment as an appellate substitute for the missing jury determination. The six answer through Roy's permitted actual-effect inquiry: an otherwise valid verdict and its specifically resolved subject allow review of contribution even though the charge failed to submit one distinct application. This is the contested boundary; it is not erased by calling materiality a pure question of law.

Stevens also retains his more specific view that the finding of knowing false annual total income embraces the materiality issue under the applicable tax-information formulation. His historical concurrence confirms that ground. Souter's assent is instead the qualified actual-verdict conclusion authorized by his own Roy opinion, without the claim that the jury necessarily made the omitted evaluation. O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer can join the sufficient common actual-verdict explanation without asserting that the historical Court's rejected literal-materiality-finding theory was correct. Accordingly, **do not manufacture an eight-Justice, six-Justice, or five-Justice holding that the jury necessarily found materiality merely from the common tax judgment**. The six directly support the complete noncontribution rule and application stated above. Stevens's additional formulation is not needed to create a majority and supplies no untested additional joins.

## 6. Scalia and Ginsburg: corrected tax result and preserved narrow method

Both accept the existing jury findings of knowing, purposeful false annual income. Their disagreement is not a claim that the verdict might concern an unspecified entry. It concerns whether those findings amount to the jury's own application of the omitted materiality requirement.

Their prior Roy separate position allows review to identify what actual findings effectively embrace. It forbids replacing an omitted factual application with appellate certainty. The historical Neder dissent makes the application decisive: its note 2 rejects the exact annual-income reasoning now proposed, despite accepting genuine equivalent findings in the main text. The trial charge, preservation, and tax rule have not materially changed. Simulated Roy preserves their constraint rather than supplying a reason to abandon it. Richardson decides a different statutory unanimity question and supplies no changed materiality premise. Jones and Gaudin reinforce actual jury allocation; neither establishes a tax exception.

The independent model's proposed affirmative path was therefore tested and rejected for these two Justices. Its premise that the annual-total subject has an inherent relationship to tax assessment gives a reviewing judge a logical basis for materiality, but does not identify a jury evaluation of that relationship within the instructions. Rephrasing that logic as legal significance, rather than factfinding, does not answer their published objection. No separately established finding in the supplied packet supplies the omitted application. The Government consequently fails their necessary-equivalence requirement on counts 86 and 87.

They would reverse the judgment sustaining those convictions and remand for proceedings permitting a new trial with every element submitted. Their ground is preserved instructional error, not evidentiary insufficiency; there is no acquittal, unconditional-release order, or determination of the eventual sentence. They need not demand a second tax harmlessness inquiry merely to repeat the inquiry already capable of resolution on the established charge and findings.

They withhold assent from the six-Associate general permission to infer harmlessness beyond equivalent findings and from calling every preserved element omission categorically nonstructural. Their own Roy join classified its defined personal-purpose omission as trial error and remains binding within that category; it did not abandon their actual-findings rule or decide this express materiality withdrawal. Their present position permits a court to inspect the verdict for equivalence, but when no equivalent determination exists, the missing jury decision cannot be supplied by judges. A concise common statement may say that omission alone does not prevent examination for equivalent findings. It may not state that all eight adopt the six-Associate classification and wider method.

No separate-writing author is assigned. The substantive function needed for assembly is a partial concurrence in the fraud construction and limited remand, with dissent from tax affirmance and a clear explanation of the narrower review rule.

## 7. Fraud remand and remedial boundaries

All eight reverse the court of appeals' holding that materiality is absent from sections 1341, 1343, and 1344, vacate its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions, and remand for the first application of the proper harmlessness inquiry. They make no present determination that any particular fraud-count error was harmless or necessarily harmful.

The immediate-affirmance path has substantial grounds: the charges concern inflated values and prices, concealed relationships and down payments, presales, and advance requests that bear on lending decisions. Actual findings may establish a material scheme. But the charge permits less than proof of every alleged detail, expressly removes bank materiality, and uses cross-references in the mail and wire portions. A generic finding of intent to deceive does not necessarily resolve capacity to influence; the section 1014 counts cannot automatically cure a different element. The defense's dispute over lending reasons and the significance of particular facts must be addressed in context. These are grounds for the first proper appellate inquiry, not a finding that the Government has failed on every count.

The six apply Roy's qualified actual-verdict method. Scalia and Ginsburg allow the remand to identify genuine equivalent findings, consistent with their historical join in Part III and their present narrower method. The court below rejected the antecedent statutory element and did not undertake this task; that is a genuine unfinished adjudicative issue. They do not thereby accept a remand instruction authorizing appellate supplementation where equivalent findings are absent.

Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts. The tax majority's result does not decide fraud harmlessness. If a conviction cannot lawfully be sustained under the governing standard, it must be set aside and retrial under correct instructions remains permissible. No automatic new trial on every fraud count, reinstatement of every count, sentence calculation, restitution amount, or release follows.

The limited mandate does not independently adjudicate section 1014, conspiracy, or racketeering claims. A properly available dependent consequence follows its actual predicate and ordinary mandate proceedings, not an invented global count disposition. Acquittals are not reopened. The judgment extract's validated tax linkage does not authorize resolving peripheral OCR defects or manufacturing an individual fraud-count result. The historical phrase remaining counts is comparator language; it is not authority for an enlarged in-world disposition beyond the granted questions and demonstrated dependencies.
