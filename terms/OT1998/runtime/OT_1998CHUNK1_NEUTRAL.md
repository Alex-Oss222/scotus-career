# United States v. Wright

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Wright
* **Citation or Docket:** 117 F.3d 1265 (11th Cir. 1997), sentencing portion replaced on rehearing January 28, 1998; No. 95-8397
* **October Term:** 1998
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Simulated post-grant merits consideration. This brief enters no Court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1998-10-05 — user-authorized simulation scheduling assignment.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the personal arms guarantee permits the machinegun-possession and destructive-device registration convictions; whether §922(o) is within Congress’s commerce power; whether the Ninth Amendment supplies an independent defense; and whether denial of acceptance-of-responsibility credit rests on permissible sentencing grounds.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the personal arms guarantee permits the machinegun-possession and destructive-device registration convictions; whether §922(o) is within Congress’s commerce power; whether the Ninth Amendment supplies an independent defense; and whether denial of acceptance-of-responsibility credit rests on permissible sentencing grounds. (User-authorized simulated grant; no historical Supreme Court grant is asserted.)
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit affirmance of the Northern District of Georgia convictions and, after the January 28, 1998 limited rehearing, the sentence. The original July 24, 1997 sentencing vacatur is no longer the operative historical disposition.
* **Relief Requested:** Wright seeks relief from the convictions on preserved constitutional grounds and correction of the denial of the §3E1.1 adjustment.
* **Material Record:** In June 1994 Wright sought help assembling a .50-caliber machinegun and told undercover officers he intended to shoot, grease and bury it. Officers arrested him with that disassembled weapon. A consensual home search uncovered a .223-caliber Olympic Arms automatic machinegun and three pipe bombs in a shed. He pleaded guilty, reserving constitutional challenges, to one §922(o) machinegun count and one §5861(d) unregistered-destructive-device count. He admitted possession and described a private group preparing for perceived threats. The government recommended acceptance credit, but the court imposed 41 months without it. The rehearing majority read the judge’s remarks as lack of remorse; the dissent read them as punishment for a legal argument.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The record distinguishes a completed automatic rifle, a disassembled .50-caliber weapon and three destructive devices. Do not treat all as ordinary semiautomatic firearms or infer a registration-impossibility defense concerning machineguns where the registration count concerns pipe bombs. Constitutional reservation does not preserve every unraised statutory theory.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Stinson gives authoritative guideline commentary its appropriate force; Koon preserves discretion within lawful sentencing rules, not discretion to apply an erroneous legal premise.

* **Material Positions and Limits:** Wright invokes personal possession, militia utility and lack of an interstate element; the government invokes regulation of automatic weapons, destructive-device registration and the transfer/stock regime. The sentencing dispute concerns whether factual remorse was assessed or a legal objection was penalized.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Resolve commerce and arms independently; evaluate each count and the sentence separately. An arms error need not invalidate registration or authorize acquittal. A sentencing error may require resentencing without disturbing valid convictions.
