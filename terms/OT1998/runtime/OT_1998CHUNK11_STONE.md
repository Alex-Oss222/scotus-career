# Albertson’s, Inc. v. Kirkingburg

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand with instructions to enter judgment for Albertson's on qualification.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enter judgment for Albertson’s on the federal qualification issue. Do not treat experimental waiver availability as a duty to disregard the operative standard, and do not decide disability categorically.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Individualized disability and natural compensation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the legal error in treating monocular vision as automatically disabling; qualification independently supplies the complete disposition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Require individualized evidence of a substantial limitation in seeing, disregarding the beneficial effect of mitigation or adaptation on the impairment. A diagnosis alone does not establish substantial limitation; qualification remains independently necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Monocular vision requires individualized proof of substantial limitation; beneficial compensating measures should not defeat original-Act coverage. The binding occupational qualification independently resolves this case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Monocular vision is not a categorical disability or categorical absence of disability. Bragdon requires the claimant’s actual impairment and substantial limitation to be identified. Beneficial adaptation should not automatically erase coverage, for the same original-Act reasons that support disregarding beneficial correction in Sutton. Qualification remains a separate inquiry: an employer may insist on the operative federal commercial-driving requirement even where the claimant is disabled. No completed Sutton decision is assumed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Natural adaptation does not automatically negate disability, and an employer may not rely on stereotype or an invented safety criterion.

### Issue 2: Operative federal safety qualification and experimental waiver program

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the employer’s entitlement to insist on the binding DOT standard, including the effect of the later experimental waiver.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Albertson's could insist on compliance with the binding federal driving qualification. An experimental waiver program did not amend the operative rule or make every potentially waivable driver presently qualified.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An experimental waiver program does not amend an operative federal safety standard or make every potentially waivable driver presently qualified; an employer may require the certification the binding rule demands.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The operative DOT vision standard supplied the legal qualification for this commercial-driving job. An experimental waiver program does not compel the employer to participate in experimentation or waive that standard. Kirkingburg ultimately obtained an experimental waiver, but its existence does not make Albertson’s earlier reliance on the binding qualification unlawful or require acceptance of the experimental alternative. This conclusion does not authorize employers to invent safety restrictions unsupported by law or objective evidence.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The experimental program did not compel the employer to waive the basic regulatory standard. The conclusion does not decide a different regulation that makes an exemption legally equivalent to basic qualification or authorize unsupported private safety restrictions.

# Olmstead v. L.C.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand under the community-placement standard stated below.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall determine appropriate placement and the State’s fundamental-alteration defense in light of available resources, the needs of others, and any comprehensive, effectively working plan with a reasonable waiting list. Relief shall be effective for the plaintiffs and no broader than the lawful record supports.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Unnecessary institutionalization and reasonable modification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone treats isolation as a form of unequal administration when it is not medically necessary. The State retains authority to allocate finite services and is not required to dismantle its entire system in one case, but it must justify the actual institutional placement and proposed modification.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Unjustified institutional isolation of a qualified person with a disability is discrimination under Title II when state professionals determine community placement appropriate, the person does not oppose it, and placement can be reasonably accommodated without fundamentally altering the State’s service system.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Both plaintiffs were professionally approved for community care and sought it. Georgia may prove that immediate placement would fundamentally alter services by considering resources and obligations to others, but generalized fiscal preference or an inert waiting list is insufficient.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require community placement contrary to informed choice or medical judgment, guarantee a particular residence, displace neutral eligibility rules, or require relief that fundamentally alters the lawful program.

# Murphy v. United Parcel Service, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand under the original ADA’s proper mitigation rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reassess the actual-disability claim without using beneficial mitigation to defeat coverage; resolve any preserved regarded-as theory, qualification, accommodation and safety defense on its own elements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Coverage without beneficial mitigation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** How should substantial limitation be assessed?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Evaluate the qualifying impairment without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating coverage.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Evaluate the qualifying impairment without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating coverage.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Murphy’s medication controls hypertension that allegedly produces serious limitations when untreated. Treating that success as an automatic loss of coverage would make statutory protection depend on continuing vulnerability rather than the impairment for which accommodation or protection is sought. Arline and Bragdon support an individualized examination of the condition while keeping the safety and qualification inquiries separate. Neither hypertension as a diagnostic label nor use of a prescription alone proves substantial limitation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Beneficial mitigation is disregarded for this coverage inquiry; actual functioning remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and safety.

### Issue 2: Other coverage routes and job qualification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What remains after correcting the coverage rule?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Remand the adverse coverage judgment; require the elements of each actual-disability and regarded-as theory.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Remand the adverse coverage judgment; require the elements of each actual-disability and regarded-as theory.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The employer’s reliance on a driving qualification does not establish what it believed about Murphy’s capacity for other mechanical work. Any regarded-as claim must meet its own broad-class-of-jobs requirement. The court must also determine the job’s essential driving functions and the operative safety qualification rather than presuming either liability or entitlement to a waiver.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic hiring, reinstatement or accommodation award.

# Kolstad v. American Dental Association

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the D.C. Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall determine whether the decisionmaker acted with malice or reckless indifference to Kolstad’s federal rights and whether the conduct is attributable to the association under the bounded good-faith rule. No punitive award is directed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Punitive-damages mental state and employer attribution

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads the enacted words “malice” and “reckless indifference” as directed to the actor’s awareness of federal rights, not an undefined extra level of outrageousness. Employer attribution requires genuine managerial authority and may be defeated when the decision was contrary to an effectively implemented good-faith compliance system, not by a paper policy alone.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VII punitive damages are available when a managerial actor intentionally discriminates with malice or reckless indifference to the claimant’s federally protected rights; no separate egregious-conduct element applies. The employer avoids vicarious punitive liability only by showing that the act was contrary to meaningful, good-faith efforts reasonably designed and actually implemented to comply with Title VII.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Evidence that the official knew the governing prohibition yet denied promotion for a forbidden reason could satisfy reckless indifference. The association’s written policy is relevant but not conclusive without evidence of training, reporting, enforcement, and corrective operation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make punitive damages automatic upon liability, impose respondeat superior for every employee, excuse acts implementing company policy, or eliminate statutory caps and constitutional review.

# Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved. The revised position stated below was user-approved 2026-10-30.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit’s judgment sustaining the denial of sovereign immunity, and remand for ordinary adjudication of the patent claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the sovereign-immunity obstacle to this expressly authorized action. Patent validity, infringement, causation, defenses and entitlement to particular remedies remain for adjudication. Affirming jurisdiction to proceed awards no damages and does not preapprove enhanced damages or fees.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Patent Clause enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Congress authorize this action against a State?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Article I independently sustains the action. Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Article I independently sustains the action. Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Uniform patent rights lose an important part of their content if a State may exploit a patented invention while withholding the ordinary federal remedy. Congress created a national patent system, concentrated adjudication in federal court, and unmistakably included States in §296. Union Gas supplies a retained Article I foundation; extending its principle to the Patent Clause is an express doctrinal extension, not a claim that Seminole already decided it. Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly establish state coverage, federal suit and otherwise available remedies. Extend the retained constitutional conclusion of Union Gas into a general Article I enforcement principle; the simulated Seminole Tribe supplies no contrary categorical prohibition. Adopt the federal-question reading of the Eleventh Amendment and clarify that Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. Reach Article I as a preserved alternative ground for affirmance: the Patent Clause argument is an alternative source of congressional authority supporting the same immunity-denial judgment, without enlarging the respondent’s requested relief. The remedial power remains tied to enforcement of a valid enumerated-power obligation and a clear congressional choice.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding of infringement, patent validity or damages entitlement.

### Issue 2: Section 5

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does Section 5 independently sustain the authorized action?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 5 supplies an independent constitutional basis. Under Fitzpatrick and the simulated Boerne, Congress may prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional; patents constitute protected property.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 5 supplies an independent constitutional basis. Under Fitzpatrick and the simulated Boerne, Congress may prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional; patents constitute protected property.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative state remedies support Congress’s preventive judgment that immunity could leave unconstitutional deprivations without adequate redress. The Act supplies an enforcement mechanism for existing patent obligations; its validity does not depend on treating every infringement as a constitutional violation or requiring each plaintiff to establish one. Neither the Article I ground nor the Section 5 ground depends on commercial participation constituting consent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Fitzpatrick and Boerne retain their independent force. Consent inferred from a State's continued commercial participation is rejected; actual consent through a distinct State choice remains available; whether Florida's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided because the Section 5 and Article I grounds sustain the judgment.

# College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Third Circuit’s immunity dismissal and remand for adjudication of the Lanham Act claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the preserved statutory claims and remedies to be considered on their own elements; neither damages nor prospective relief is awarded at this stage.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Article I remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the Lanham Act claim proceed?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Give effect to the express congressional authorization of suit against States participating in the regulated interstate market.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Give effect to the express congressional authorization of suit against States participating in the regulated interstate market.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged misrepresentations concern competing college-financing products offered in interstate commerce. A federal rule against misleading commercial representations can bind state sellers, and an expressly provided enforcement remedy prevents the regulated State from enjoying an immunity unavailable to private competitors. Union Gas permits this commerce-power starting point; Seminole does not supply the opposite categorical rule. The extension concerns enforcement of this federal commercial obligation, not unlimited power to abolish every state immunity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Falsity, materiality, commercial injury, causation and relief remain unproved.

### Issue 2: Consent and §5

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does market participation waive immunity or create constitutional property?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reject constructive waiver and the proposed automatic constitutional-property theory.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reject constructive waiver and the proposed automatic constitutional-property theory.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Entering a commercial market is conduct regulated by Congress, not an unequivocal election to be sued. The action therefore rests on valid congressional power and unmistakable legislation. Nor does every injury caused by false advertising deprive the competitor of property without due process. Boerne cannot be satisfied by relabeling the statutory claim as a constitutional tort.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual consent and a properly supported §5 remedy remain distinct routes in other cases.

# Alden v. Maine

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Maine Supreme Judicial Court’s immunity judgment and remand for adjudication of the federal overtime claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Maine court must determine coverage, compensation, defenses and relief. This disposition neither awards wages nor decides the separate federal action.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: State-court enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Maine invoke immunity against the expressly authorized FLSA action?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A competent state court must adjudicate the federal wage action authorized by Congress.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A competent state court must adjudicate the federal wage action authorized by Congress.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The FLSA validly regulates covered state employment under Garcia and clearly provides a private action in competent state courts. The Eleventh Amendment addresses federal judicial power; its text does not itself establish immunity from federal obligations in a State’s own courts. Testa and Howlett prevent a state-law defense from defeating supreme federal law simply because the obligation is federal. Hilton’s actual holding is confined to the FELA railroad setting, but its enforcement principle supports an express extension to these wage obligations. Adjudicating a federal cause is constitutionally different from conscripting state lawmakers or officers to administer a program under New York and Printz.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This state-court holding does not automatically decide federal-court amenability or whether overtime is owed.

### Issue 2: Separate state-court judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the stated question within the verified scope of this proceeding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the earlier federal proceeding eliminate the state-forum controversy?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Review the independently entered Maine immunity judgment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Review the independently entered Maine immunity judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The state action was separately filed and produced its own trial and appellate judgments. A dismissal for lack of federal jurisdiction does not adjudicate overtime liability, and the historical lower court’s use of a different Seminole rationale is not a binding premise of this review. The live state-forum question remains whether Maine may defeat the expressly authorized federal cause in an otherwise competent state court. There is no need to manufacture a new federal dismissal or a different plaintiff to reach it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling here revives or disposes of the separate federal action, finds overtime owed or changes an existing completed judgment.

# Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit’s judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The mandatory class certification and settlement approval shall be vacated. The parties may pursue individual settlements, narrower opt-out classes, separately represented subclasses, bankruptcy, or a genuine limited-fund proceeding satisfying Rule 23 and due process.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Rule 23(b)(1)(B) limited fund and adequate representation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the traditional limited-fund category narrowly because mandatory aggregation eliminates individual control and opt-out. The parties may not manufacture the fund’s limit through negotiation, reserve value outside the class, or use one representation structure for materially opposed claimant groups.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A mandatory settlement class under Rule 23(b)(1)(B) requires a demonstrated fund fixed independently at its maximum practical value, insufficiency to satisfy the aggregate claims, commitment of substantially the whole fund, equitable distribution, and adequate conflict-free representation, including separate subclasses where present and future claimants materially diverge.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The record did not establish Fibreboard’s total available value independently of the agreement, the settlement preserved disputed value and preferential arrangements, and the single class structure did not adequately represent present injuries, future disease, and varying insurance interests. Certification therefore fails.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar mass-tort settlements, bankruptcy trusts, opt-out damages classes that satisfy Rule 23(b)(3), genuine limited funds, or settlements with separately represented subclasses and lawful notice protections.
