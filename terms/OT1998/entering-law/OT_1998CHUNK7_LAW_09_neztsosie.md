#### Strate v. A-1 Contractors

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1872.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

##### This authorized public highway falls within Montana's nonmember framework

**Holding:** For ordinary public motorists using this lawfully open, state-maintained federal right-of-way, the Tribe lacks the exclusion power that would otherwise govern access; Montana's nonmember framework therefore applies to this road-use tort. That consequence lasts within the authorized public use and neither conveys fee title nor removes every compatible tribal power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The May 8, 1970 grant applies to trust lands only, excludes non-trust property, and acknowledges $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. Tenure is unlimited only while used for the specified highway purpose and remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct a breach of grant terms or applicable regulations, two consecutive years of nonuse for the purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the completion affidavit when construction ends. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. The stated conditions bind and benefit the grantee's successors. No termination trigger is established on this record. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres do not identify the collision parcel; missing incorporated maps and a separate consent instrument establish no grant defect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Montana supplies the nonmember framework; Bourland supplies a particular public-use/exclusion consequence with residual authority preserved. Sac and Fox's Indian-country classification does not settle governing power over this claim.

##### A tribal forum requires an adequate source of governing power

**Holding:** Subject to controlling treaties or statutes and Montana's two exceptions, tribal adjudicatory authority over nonmembers does not exceed the corresponding substantive governing authority. A tribal court cannot compel these defendants to litigate this highway claim solely by proposing to apply another sovereign's law; completed exhaustion supplies no independent jurisdictional grant.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual supply review and initial tribal examination, not unlimited ultimate authority. Williams's resident-member trading relationship differs. Merrion preserves sovereign taxation distinct from private exclusion and rejects surrender by silence.

##### The subcontract does not bring this ordinary highway claim within the consensual exception

**Holding:** Montana's first exception requires a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or its members. Even assuming Stockert's trip served A-1's tribal community-center subcontract and Fredericks resided on the reservation, this non-Indian motorist's ordinary highway-negligence claim lacks that relation; contracting with a tribal enterprise does not submit a contractor to every reservation claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Express forum consent and contractual privity are not universally necessary for the exception. Injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a participant in the consensual activity is reserved; the asserted extra-record forum clause supplies no ground.

##### The supported interests do not satisfy the self-government exception

**Holding:** Montana's second exception preserves authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The serious individual injury, family relationships, assumed residence and ordinary road-safety interests shown here do not establish the threat or direct tribal effect necessary to bring this nonmember highway claim within that exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not deny the injury's real familial or community consequences, but they do not establish the required direct tribal effect. A possible state forum is not the independent reason tribal power is absent; no negligence or limitations result is decided.

##### The jurisdictional judgment does not itself establish every predicate for an injunction

**Holding:** Affirmance requires federal relief consistent with the absence of tribal jurisdiction over the reviewed claim, but does not itself issue an injunction or establish entitlement to every coercive remedy. On remand the federal court must respect the actual claims and lawful parties, immunity limits and ordinary equitable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The jurisdiction-only declaratory consent and resolution do not waive immunity for every claim or remedy. The sons' consortium claims, dismissed insurer, personal jurisdiction, tribal/internal roads, member conduct, express consent, superior federal authorization and distinct police/criminal powers remain outside decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of jurisdiction over Fredericks's reviewed claim unanimously, without further exhaustion or factfinding on the assumed work-trip and residence facts. Eight Associates require relief fitted to lawful parties, immunity and equity; no injunction automatically issues.

#### Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706

**Docket or dockets:** 95-244
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does section 1447(d) bar review of a remand entered on Burford abstention grounds?

**Holding:** Section 1447(d), read with section 1447(c), bars appellate review of remands actually resting on the covered subject-matter-jurisdiction grounds or timely raised removal-procedure defects; a procedural-defect motion must be made within thirty days. A remand on Burford abstention grounds is outside that bar, although escaping the bar does not itself establish an affirmative appellate channel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I-II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; unanimous controlling rationale for all questions in this entry.

**Controlling explanation:** The reviewing court identifies the remand's actual stated ground; it does not reconsider the correctness of a covered ground as a way around the bar. Burford abstention does not assert defective federal jurisdiction or removal procedure. The statutory section 1443 exception does not govern, and the reserved Cohill question about discretionary remand of state-law claims is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Thermtron's covered-ground construction remains controlling; Rice and Gravitt retain the bar on review of covered remands even when error is alleged. Cohill's distinct remand-review question remains open.

##### Is this conclusive surrender of the federal forum reviewable under section 1291?

**Holding:** A remand that conclusively surrenders the entire federal action and leaves no further federal adjudicative work is reviewable under section 1291 because it effectively puts the litigant out of federal court. Independently, this order satisfies the collateral-order doctrine: it conclusively determines an important public question of federal jurisdiction, separate from the merits, that would be effectively unreviewable after a later federal judgment that will never occur.

**Controlling explanation:** The first, independently sufficient ground applies Cone's practical treatment of complete federal-forum surrender; it does not pretend that this remand is a conventional Catlin final merits judgment. The second, independently sufficient ground satisfies Cohen and Digital Equipment because it concerns the public allocation of judicial responsibility and forecloses later review. Litigation expense, a private settlement right or a bare wish to avoid trial would not meet that test. Each ground independently supports jurisdiction; the order's particular conclusive effect matters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Not every stay, remand or interlocutory order is appealable. Section 1292(b) certification is unnecessary on these grounds; that distinct route still requires a written finding of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for disagreement and material advancement of termination, a timely application within ten days, appellate permission, and no automatic stay.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Cone supplies the independently sufficient effectively-out-of-court route for complete surrender of the federal forum.
- Catlin retains its conventional final-decision rule, which is not stretched into a fictional final merits disposition here.
- Cohen supplies the independent collateral-order requirements.
- Digital Equipment requires public importance and effective unreviewability; a private right to avoid litigation is insufficient.
- Thermtron's mandamus-only language is disavowed insofar as it conflicts with the later Cone application. Its construction of section 1447(d)'s covered grounds remains intact.

##### May Burford abstention support outright remand of independent legal damages claims?

**Holding:** Burford abstention permits outright dismissal or remand only when the relief sought is equitable or otherwise discretionary; an independent legal damages claim cannot be surrendered on that basis. A properly justified stay may preserve federal jurisdiction over such a claim, but this decision neither requires nor automatically authorizes one.

**Controlling explanation:** These independent contract claims seek legal damages. State liquidation interests, setoff, participation in liquidation, procedural merger of law and equity, and the presence of a genuine declaratory claim do not make all the requested relief discretionary. Wilton's discretion over an action seeking only declaratory relief does not convert this mixed action into such a case. The actual state asset orders permit proceedings elsewhere; the Court does not assume that every insurance action disposes of assets under exclusive state custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether Burford's predicates otherwise exist. They require timely and adequate state review and either difficult state-law questions bearing on public policy transcending the case's result, or disruption of coherent state policy on a matter of substantial public concern; those alternatives are not cumulative, and complexity or potential conflict alone is insufficient. The Court rejects neither all abstention-related stays in damages actions nor the distinct doctrines involving state-tax comity, Thibodaux, Colorado River or foreign forum non conveniens.

The Court decides no arbitration entitlement or sequencing priority. Any Federal Arbitration Act section 3 application still requires a written covered agreement and referable issue, the court's satisfaction, an application by a party, a stay according to the agreement, and absence of applicant default. Ordinary evenhanded formation, fraud, duress, unconscionability, assent and preservation rules remain; coverage, scope, delegation, nonsignatory enforcement and McCarran-Ferguson issues are undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment vacating the abstention remand is affirmed, 9-0. Federal proceedings resume without an ordered stay, arbitration, damages award, distribution of assets or new evidentiary hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Burford is confined as to outright dismissal or remand to equitable or otherwise discretionary relief, while leaving a properly justified stay available for damages claims.
- Wilton's discretion over solely declaratory relief does not authorize surrender of an independent legal damages action.
- Fair Assessment's state-tax comity doctrine remains distinct.
- Thibodaux and Colorado River retain their separate grounds and limits; neither supports a universal ban on stays in legal actions.
- The separate law of foreign forum non conveniens remains undisturbed.

### Appeal of an abstention order surrendering the federal forum

**Governing question:** A remand that completely disassociates the district court from the action.

**Current rule:** Absent a statutory review bar, an abstention remand conclusively surrendering federal adjudication is appealable under §1291 on two independently sufficient grounds: Moses H. Cone's limited effectively-out-of-court finality rule and Cohen's collateral-order rule. The latter requires a conclusive, important federal-forum question separate from the merits and effectively unreviewable after a later federal final judgment; this surrender satisfies each condition. Mere delay, litigation expense or an asserted right to avoid trial does not suffice. Thermtron's mandamus-only language yields to this application of Cone, while its construction of the covered-remand bar survives.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent finality and collateral-order grounds, with limited disavowal: Quackenbush — Complete surrender of the federal forum supplies an appealable decision; The collateral-order doctrine independently permits this appeal, June 3, 1996; unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Quackenbush, June 3, 1996.

**Holdings navigation:** Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706.

### Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band of Chippewa Indians; No. 97-1337. — 1999-03-24

#### Holdings

##### I. The combined removal order did not terminate the reserved use rights

**Holding and operative rule.** An unauthorized compelled-removal order does not terminate reserved treaty use rights when its revocation and removal commands are inseparable parts of the same purpose and operation. The 1850 order therefore did not extinguish these rights; the Court does not decide the full extent of an independently exercised presidential power under the treaty's pleasure clause.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, counting the author within that six-Justice coalition. All six join the bounded combined-order rationale and its reservation of independent presidential revocation authority.

**Controlling explanation.** The Removal Act's consent structure did not authorize the compelled removal described by this record. That unlawful removal command could not cancel the separately reserved use rights. The order connected its revocation command to that same removal purpose and operation; treating revocation as a standalone measure would create a materially different executive action rather than preserve an independently chosen lawful termination. Minnesota's strongest argument is that the treaty separately permitted revocation during the President's pleasure. Even assuming an independently lawful revocation could occur, that possibility does not make this combined order severable or validate its unauthorized removal command. The Court decides this order's supported relationship and operation. It adopts neither a prohibition of every independent presidential revocation nor a rule that subsequent nonenforcement restores rights already lawfully extinguished.

##### II. The 1855 land cession did not surrender these independent use rights

**Holding and operative rule.** A later land cession does not extinguish separately retained treaty use rights unless the agreement, read in its setting and as the tribal parties reasonably understood it, clearly relinquishes those rights. The 1855 agreement did not surrender the Band's retained hunting, fishing and gathering privileges.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part II has the six-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** Dion requires clear evidence that the disputed right was considered and relinquished, without imposing an invariable formula of express words. Menominee and Fishing Vessel protect retained rights against casual implication. Here the supported negotiations concerned a land purchase and did not address surrender of the independent use privileges. The broad cession language must be read in that setting. Klamath's reservation-dependent exclusive rights do not make every land transfer a surrender of a separately retained use right. Bourland likewise distinguishes regulation based on a power to exclude from personal treaty uses that remain after the relevant land change. The Court therefore rejects Minnesota's claim that comprehensive conveyancing words alone settle this distinct right. No cession by a nonsignatory Band or surrender under another agreement is inferred.

##### III. Statehood did not itself extinguish the treaty rights

**Holding and operative rule.** Minnesota's admission to the Union did not itself terminate otherwise surviving federal treaty use rights. Those rights can coexist with lawful, necessary nondiscriminatory conservation regulation; the treaty's presidential-pleasure condition does not make state admission the terminating event.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part III has the same six-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** The Supremacy Clause preserves enforceable federal treaty rights after state admission. Winans, Tulee and Fishing Vessel demonstrate the compatibility of reserved uses with lawful conservation authority. Race Horse addressed a differently conditioned right on unoccupied lands. Its treaty-specific construction does not establish that this pleasure-qualified reservation ended at statehood. To the extent Race Horse treats equal footing alone as incompatible with surviving treaty use rights, the Court rejects that proposition. Minnesota's resource-management interest remains substantial, but it can operate through the governing conservation standards rather than an inference of wholesale extinction. The Court does not decide every concrete regulation or displace Race Horse's separate construction of its own treaty condition.

##### IV. The affirmance preserves the rights at their actual scope

**Holding and operative rule.** Recognition of the surviving rights within the ceded territory remains in force against the presented extinction claims, subject to the applicable treaty and conservation standards. Survival does not itself decide private access, unrelated criminal conduct, numerical allocations or rights beyond the treaty's territory.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** None of the presented terminating acts extinguished the rights, and no supported later event supplies another termination premise. That resolves the reviewed judgment. It does not require reopening every resource-management ruling in the larger proceedings, granting an unrestricted harvest or conferring a veto over every state rule. The declaration and related injunctive framework therefore remain in force within their lawful scope, without a new allocation, title transfer or retrospective award.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Dion is applied: clear abrogation is required, but no mandatory form of express words is imposed.
- Menominee and Fishing Vessel are applied: independently reserved rights are not extinguished by casual implication.
- Klamath is distinguished: reservation-dependent exclusive rights do not determine the fate of these retained use privileges.
- Bourland is distinguished and preserved: exclusion-derived nonmember regulation and retained personal uses remain different rights.
- Hagen and Yankton retain their instrument-specific inquiry into actual federal displacement; land or ownership change alone does not decide every retained right.
- Race Horse is limited: its broader equal-footing incompatibility premise cannot terminate otherwise surviving rights merely at statehood; its distinct treaty-condition construction is not decided here.
- Winans and Tulee are applied with Fishing Vessel: treaty uses and lawful conservation regulation can coexist.

#### Law After Decision

The Band's reserved uses survive the three asserted extinction grounds. The 1850-order, 1855, statehood and bounded-remedy holdings each control through the same six direct joins. No combination of separate opinions is needed to identify the governing rules. Race Horse no longer supports extinction solely from equal-footing incompatibility at statehood.

### Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc., No. 97-1909; judgment below, 125 F.3d 1396. — 1999-04-05

#### Holdings

##### Formal process and the statutory pleading events govern the original removal period

**Holding and operative rule:** Informal receipt of a courtesy copy before formal service, waiver or legally equivalent process has subjected the defendant to the court's authority does not by itself begin §1446(b)'s original-pleading removal period. Under its first branch, thirty days run when that process requirement and receipt, through service or otherwise, of the initial pleading setting forth the claim for relief have both occurred; the separate branch runs thirty days after service of summons if the initial pleading has then been filed in court and is not required to be served on the defendant, whichever period is shorter.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has the complete seven-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All seven directly adopt both statutory branches, their limits and the application to Murphy's removal.

**Controlling explanation:** Receipt of useful information and an obligation imposed by court process are different events. Section 1446(b) operates within the system in which formal service, waiver or a legally equivalent process subjects the defendant to judicial authority. The phrase permitting receipt otherwise than through service has work to do: pleading information may arrive separately from formal process. If the complaint arrives first, later process starts the first branch; if process precedes the required pleading receipt, that branch awaits receipt. The separate summons branch applies on its own stated conditions and does not invariably require actual receipt or service of the complaint. The dissent correctly observes that Congress expressly referred to receipt in the first branch and summons service in the second. Reading the paragraph as a whole, however, those different information events operate within the formal-process framework, rather than making an unsolicited advance copy a compulsory forum-election deadline. Henderson supports attention to service's actual procedural role without answering this statute or erasing its commands. Caterpillar preserves prospective removal requirements; no completed-trial cure is invoked here. Murphy had no obligation to appear or face default when the fax arrived, and its removal was within thirty days after the subsequent formal service. The notice was therefore timely under the statutory rule, not an equitable extension. This construction does not hold that Congress could never enact an earlier deadline or impose a universal clear-statement requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both original thirty-day periods and the shorter-period direction remain operative. A validly begun period is not extended, and service-evasion gamesmanship is not excused. The Court decides no unpresented waiver's precise timing or other process sequence, does not prohibit removal before service, and does not apply the separate later-removability paragraph. Timeliness supplies no subject-matter jurisdiction. Other preserved remand grounds, contract merits and fees are not decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Henderson v. United States, decided May 20, 1996, supplies source-specific attention to service's procedural function. Its Suits in Admiralty Act holding does not itself define this removal trigger, repeal other service statutes or authorize a discretionary extension here.
- Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis, decided December 10, 1996, preserves the prospective duty to comply with removal requirements. Its bounded protection of an actually jurisdictionally sound completed judgment is distinguished; this case has no such judgment and applies no cure.
- Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, decided December 5, 1995, and Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., decided June 3, 1996, retain their covered-remand-review boundaries. Review here arose from the certified denial of remand, not appellate reconsideration of a district-court remand order protected by §1447(d).
- Omni Capital International, Ltd. v. Rudolf Wolff & Co. preserves the distinct role of authorized service in establishing personal jurisdiction. It does not independently fix the statutory time to elect removal.

#### Law After Decision

A courtesy complaint copy received before process does not itself start the original removal clock. The first branch requires both the legally relevant process status and pleading receipt; the summons branch operates independently when its filed-pleading and no-service-required conditions are met, with the shorter statutory period controlling. The rule preserves timely statutory compliance without confusing the removal opportunity, duty to appear and subject-matter jurisdiction. No precedent is overruled.

### Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc., No. 97-1909; judgment below, 125 F.3d 1396. — 1999-04-05

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, would affirm the appellate remand direction. The first statutory branch expressly makes receipt of the filed initial pleading the triggering event and permits receipt otherwise than by service. Formal service is a means of receipt under that language, not an additional condition. The second branch expressly requires summons service when the pleading has been filed and need not be served, and the shorter period remains controlling. General service principles cannot add the second branch's process condition to the first. Congress may require an election of a federal forum before state process imposes a duty to answer; an expired removal period neither authorizes default without service nor establishes personal jurisdiction. Because Murphy removed more than thirty days after receipt of the fax, the dissent finds the removal untimely. It adopts no waiver, bad-faith or equitable-extension finding.
