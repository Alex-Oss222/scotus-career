#### United States v. Fields / Crawley

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit Nos. 96-1168 and 96-1257; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-02-09.

##### Each defendant has his own protected relationship to the room

**Holding:** Fields's sustained paid access, key, independent entry and permission to bring guests establish his own legitimate privacy interest in the apartment and private room. Crawley's repeated, expressly permitted sharing of the closed room's privacy also establishes his own interest on these facts, although he had no established overnight plan that evening; neither mere presence nor another person's rights would suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The other four assume Crawley's interest to decide the observation question and do not join that extension.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Fields’s control and Crawley’s recurring permitted closed-room sharing are separate personal grounds; no protection for mere presence or every commercial visitor is established. Predominant cocaine activity does not itself destroy an otherwise legitimate privacy interest.

##### Limited residential sharing is not itself general publication

**Holding:** Limited visibility of bedroom activity to cotenants using shared residential space is not, by that fact alone, general publication. An incompletely closed blind cannot establish absence of subjective privacy independently of the actual vantage from which the challenged police observation was obtained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The Court does not adopt Part II-B's four-Justice application as a majority search determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no majority finding that the shared yard was curtilage or observation was a search. The street and sidewalk gave no view; the gap, shared access and ordinary viewing measures matter, but neither fencing nor imperfect screening alone determines the result.

##### Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine whether the established circumstances supplied a constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences retain clear-error protection, and grounded contextual experience receives genuine due weight without controlling the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A has O'Connor, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia withholds this extension; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

##### Entry authority and announcement require separate contemporaneous justification

**Holding:** Probable cause alone does not authorize warrantless home entry; an applicable consent or exigency justification is required. Separately, an announcement exception requires particular facts and rational inferences known when force begins supporting reasonable suspicion of danger, futility or threatened evidence loss from announcement or delay; later discoveries cannot supply earlier justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion: Part III-B has all eight Associate Justices. Four reserve the ultimate applications and four would sustain them; neither application has a controlling majority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Announcement requires more than a hunch but less than probable cause. The porch warning, known history and disposability are contemporaneous facts; later weapon discovery and flight are not. Forced common-door entry counts despite the open apartment door. No majority adopts actual entry legality or a general police-created-exigency test.

##### A later warrant or consent does not automatically decide suppression

**Holding:** If an antecedent intrusion was unlawful, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. Consent must satisfy its own authority, scope and voluntariness requirements and any applicable taint analysis; neither a warrant's arrival nor a rights explanation and signature automatically validates an earlier intrusion or decides exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion: Part IV has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally agrees in returning suppression, consent, independence, attenuation and later-warrant consequences to the lower courts without final suppression, but joins no additional remedial rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Leon excludes knowing/reckless deception, abandoned neutrality, affidavits wholly lacking probable-cause indicia and facially deficient warrants; obtaining/supplying officers and scope remain relevant. Evans remains confined to qualifying court-clerical error. Inevitable discovery and any Rule 52(b) claim require their actual predicates; no waiver or forfeiture is established here.

##### The judgment does not establish a unified search or entry application

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices vacate the reviewed suppression determination and dependent appellate affirmances and remand. O'Connor, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would require a renewed actual-vantage application while reserving search and entry legality. Stone-Zsela finds the observation a search and the planned exigency insufficient, leaving remedies for remand. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer would affirm because they find the actual shared view and specific pre-force exceptions sufficient.

The directly supported propositions above control their subjects. The two judgment-supporting application rationales are not treated as a single Marks holding: the plurality leaves open a lawful observation and entry that the concurrence rejects on this record, while the concurrence adds a police-created-exigency ground the plurality does not adopt. Neither full rationale is a logical subset supplying a common answer. The binding judgment therefore requires lawful further proceedings under the actual majority rules, without imposing either noncontrolling application as the Court's final answer. No automatic exclusion, plea withdrawal, verdict vacatur or unrelated appellate reopening follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** By Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, the reviewed suppression determinations and dependent appellate conviction affirmances are vacated to that extent and remanded. No evidence is ordered excluded or admitted; Fields’s jury verdict is not vacated and Crawley’s conditional plea is not automatically withdrawn. Unreviewed appellate branches remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rakas and Jones supply the personal-interest inquiry; Olson's protection of shared residential privacy is extended to Crawley's specific recurring, permitted closed-room use without an all-visitors rule.
- Katz, Pinson and Myers supply protection of private domestic information and the distinction between a protected relationship, exposure, search justification and remedy. Their thermal-search holdings are not converted into a rule about every naked-eye view or every device.
- Ciraolo and Riley retain their actual public-vantage settings. Dunn supplies contextual proximity, enclosure, use and screening considerations; no first-fence rule or classification of every shared yard follows.
- Ornelas is extended to ultimate home-entry exigency, preserving factual protection and genuine due weight. Its limits on hearings, remedies and other mixed questions remain intact; Gates's warrant-issuance review is unchanged.
- Payton and Hayden retain the need for an actual home-entry exception. Wilson and Richards retain separate, particularized announcement rules and Richards's already governing independent ultimate review. No general police-created-exigency doctrine or announcement-exclusion rule is adopted.
- Murray and Karo retain genuine independence and supported untainted-warrant possibilities. Leon, Evans and Olano retain their stated institutional, factual and procedural limits; no later warrant or consent is treated as an automatic cure.

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.

### Personal residential sharing and limited exposure

**Governing question:** Fourth Amendment privacy in permitted residential use and visibility to cotenants.

**Current rule:** Sustained paid access, a key, independent entry and permission to bring guests can establish a personal residential privacy interest without formal residence or stored belongings. Repeated expressly permitted sharing of a closed room may establish that interest without an overnight plan; mere presence or borrowed rights is insufficient. Limited visibility to cotenants in shared residential space is not by itself general publication, and imperfect screening does not negate subjective privacy independently of the actual police vantage.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal room relationships; limited residential sharing: United States v. Fields, 1998-02-09; O’Connor Part I-A for all nine; Parts I-B and II-A with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-02-09.

**Exceptions and limits:** Commercial or unlawful activity does not automatically erase otherwise legitimate personal privacy, but every visitor is not protected. These rules do not classify every shared yard, resolve the particular observation as a search or establish a general police-created-exigency doctrine. Justification and exclusion remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Fields / Crawley — Residential privacy, warrantless entry and suppression.

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion, nonwarrant probable cause and warrantless-home-entry exigency.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Appellate courts likewise independently decide whether established circumstances supply constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry, with the same protection for historical and genuinely factual subsidiary findings and genuine due weight for grounded experience. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Review allocation, objective totality and due weight: II. Ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently reviewed, with factual protection and genuine due weight, 1996-05-28; O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

- Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review: United States v. Fields, 1998-02-09; O’Connor Part III-A with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Fields, 1998-02-09, for home-entry exigency; Ornelas, 1996-05-28, for reasonable suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

### Wyoming v. Houghton; No. 98-184. — 1999-04-05

#### Holdings

##### Vehicle probable cause reaches this detached passenger-owned purse

**Holding and operative rule.** When police have probable cause to search an automobile for drugs, the automobile exception permits searching a detached passenger-owned purse found inside the car if it can conceal the drugs. Known passenger ownership does not alone remove that container from the object-based search; this holding does not authorize searching the passenger's person or decide the treatment of an effect worn or carried on the person.

**Authority.** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, counting the author within that five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** Labron applies the automobile exception without a separate exigency requirement and measures its container scope by the object sought. Ross and Acevedo likewise permit a properly supported search of places and containers capable of concealing that object. Wellons recognizes a personal privacy interest in luggage but preserves otherwise valid search exceptions. Those propositions coexist: a protected interest does not defeat an established justification for searching it. Houghton's purse was a container within the searched vehicle and could conceal the drugs. Its acknowledged ownership does not change that capacity or establish an exception to the rule. Di Re's protection of the passenger's person remains distinct; the Court does not equate a detached purse with the passenger's body. Nor does it presume Houghton guilty or engaged in a common criminal enterprise. Young's syringe and drug-use admission supplied the conceded vehicle probable cause; that existing authority justifies the initial purse intrusion. The false-name discrepancy and later discoveries play no role in justifying the initial intrusion. Searches of clothing or attached personal effects remain outside the decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Labron/Kilgore is applied without modification: actual vehicle probable cause supplies object-based container authority without an additional exigency requirement.
- Ross and Acevedo are applied: a capable container within a justified automobile search is not exempt solely because a passenger owns it.
- Wellons is preserved: separate personal privacy and an applicable search exception are distinct questions.
- Di Re is distinguished: authority over a vehicle does not by itself authorize a bodily search.
- Knowles retains its rule that a citation alone supplies no general search authority; the independent probable cause conceded here presents a different ground.
- Carter/Johns retains its personal-right, justification and causal limits; the Court makes no new independent-source finding.

#### Law After Decision

The existing automobile exception reaches this detached passenger purse. The four dissenting Justices support the same proposed ownership qualification and suppression judgment; that qualification is not adopted. Known ownership neither eliminates personal privacy nor, by itself, defeats the applicable exception. Person searches and attached effects remain separate questions.

### Wyoming v. Houghton; No. 98-184. — 1999-04-05

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would qualify Labron for a closed personal effect known before intrusion to belong to a passenger when vehicle probable cause derives from someone else. Location and capacity alone would not authorize the search. They would require particularized facts connecting the effect or the passenger to the object sought, establishing probable cause reaching the effect, or another independently applicable search ground. Passenger-related facts can supply that connection; the evidence need not originate from the effect itself. Independently supported probable cause reaching the passenger's effect, consent and other established exceptions remain available.

Wellons supplies the distinct privacy interest, and Di Re supports refusing to transfer suspicion merely from the car to the passenger; neither already establishes their proposed exception. They acknowledge Ross, Acevedo and Labron as the principal contrary authority. A uniform container rule, in their view, gives insufficient protection to an identified person's private effects. They answer the concealment and administrability concerns by requiring concrete indicators of transfer, shared ownership or common concealment rather than presuming those circumstances from companionship. The qualification preserves ordinary vehicle compartments, unclaimed or shared containers and independently justified searches. It creates no blanket passenger immunity, universal luggage-warrant rule or separate automobile-exigency test.

Young's syringe and admission supplied vehicle probable cause but no reported fact tied Houghton's effect or her participation to the drugs before entry. Her claimed lack of identification did not by itself create narcotics probable cause; the false identity was discovered through the intrusion requiring justification. The later pouch, track marks and admission about the black container likewise cannot justify the first search retrospectively. All four would adopt the stated limited modification and affirm the reviewed suppression judgment. They make no independent-source finding and propose no acquittal or unqualified disposition of every later intrusion.
