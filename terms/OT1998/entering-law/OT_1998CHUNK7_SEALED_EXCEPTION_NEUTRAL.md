# In re Sealed Case — precise asserted privilege

The lower opinion, 148 F.3d 1073, 1075, describes the Executive's proposed privilege as protecting information obtained by Secret Service personnel performing protective functions in physical proximity to the President. Its proposed exception applies in a federal investigation or prosecution where the observation or statement, **at the time made**, provided reasonable grounds to believe a felony **had been, was being, or would be committed**. The neutral packet's shorter reference to a felony that occurred omits the present/future branches and should not narrow the asserted exception.

The published lower opinion describes affirmative support: Director Merletti's declaration concerning the importance of close physical access and predicted loss of presidential trust, and former President Bush's April 15, 1998 letter making the confidence/proximity argument. These are statements and predictions offered for privilege recognition. They do not constitute an adjudicated finding that testimony has already impaired presidential protection. Conversely, absence of such a finding does not mean that no supporting declarations were submitted.

Source: lower appellate opinion's description of the asserted privilege and supporting materials, Part II; no historical Supreme Court outcome, reasoning or lineup is included.
