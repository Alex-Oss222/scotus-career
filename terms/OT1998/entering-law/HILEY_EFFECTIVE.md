# Hiley — effective entering-law reading copy

**Authority:** [Hiley v. Barrett, merits decision, October 9, 1998](../records/hiley_v_barrett_merits_1998-10-09.md#public-projection).
**Effective date:** 1998-10-09.
**Scope:** Mechanical copy of the bounded Public Projection below; the Record remains authority. The separately identified concurrence is noncontrolling. No private position, commitment, comparator or audit annex is included.

## Event

Hiley v. Barrett; Eleventh Circuit No. 97-9006, 155 F.3d 1276. Merits decision, October 9, 1998, on certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit after merits briefing. No argument or precise submission date is stated. The September 21, 1998 appellate judgment adopted the July 2, 1997 order in National Association of Government Employees, Inc. v. Barrett, 968 F. Supp. 1564, dismissing the surviving constitutional action.

Hiley pleaded no contest to misdemeanor domestic battery in August 1995 and received twelve months of nonreporting probation. After enactment of the federal disability, the sheriff removed him from armed-deputy work in January 1997. Settlement rescinded termination, reassigned him to detention work and provided restoration to the deputy position if he prevailed. Claims against the county and sheriff were dismissed with prejudice. NAGE represents similarly affected members.

The surviving questions concern commerce, three equality theories, substantive employment rights, ex post facto, attainder and the Tenth Amendment. The separate abandonments of strict scrutiny and procedural due process limit review. This is a prospective civil challenge arising from employment injury, not a firearm prosecution or a newly preserved private-possession claim.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. No case-specific nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment dismissing the surviving federal constitutional action, unanimously, on the separate grounds stated below.

## Holdings

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-X, is joined in full by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight Justices adopt every holding below. Stone-Zsela agrees in every component judgment without joining the opinion and states his grounds separately. No fractured-decision inference supplies controlling law.

### I. The continuing employment injury supports standing and present review

**Holding and operative rule:** Hiley's continuing exclusion from armed-deputy work, traceable to application of the federal disability and redressable through the settlement's restoration-if-successful condition, establishes a concrete controversy despite his reassignment to detention work. NAGE may pursue this uniform prospective legal challenge through affected members where the claim is germane to its representative purpose and neither the claim nor the requested relief requires individual participation; the already operative injury makes the controversy ripe.

**Controlling explanation:** The settlement did not end the disadvantage Hiley challenges; it expressly connects success in the federal action with restoration of his deputy assignment. The sheriff's intervening employment choice therefore does not make causation or redress speculative. *Hunt* supplies the separate member-injury, germaneness and participation requirements. *United Food* preserves those constitutional injury requirements and does not dispense with the participation condition outside its statutory exception. Here the uniform legal question and prospective relief require no individual damages determination. The present employment effect is sufficiently concrete for decision; possible future pardon or collateral relief does not eliminate it. No threatened prosecution is alleged or needed for this employment-based standing conclusion.

### II. Personal arms protection does not revive abandoned claims

**Holding and operative rule:** The recorded abandonment of strict-scrutiny review and the separately recorded abandonment of procedural due process limit this review; rejecting the lower court's collective-only arms premise neither withdraws those abandonments nor creates a separately preserved Second Amendment claim. The surviving federal challenges remain for decision, while claims against the county and sheriff settled and dismissed with prejudice remain closed.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love*, *Hale* and *Rybar* recognize personal invocation of the arms guarantee independently of militia affiliation. *Wright* supplies Core-Preservation Review: first determine constitutional coverage, then measure justification against the severity of the restriction's practical burden. *Fraternal Order of Police* applies that rule to a qualifying domestic-violence disability as a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny, and holds that personal protection does not require an official-use exception. A preserved Second Amendment challenge here would be governed by that framework and application.

Hiley abandoned the arms claim, and the incorporated district order's footnotes 11 and 16 separately record the abandonments of strict-scrutiny review and procedural due process. The Court rejects the collective-only premise without withdrawing those abandonments. It decides the surviving equality and employment theories at their preserved scope; it does not apply intermediate scrutiny to an unpreserved arms claim, decide Hiley's private-possession rights, or reopen the local settlement. The reference to *Wright* and *Fraternal Order of Police* identifies governing law rather than an additional holding on the abandoned claim.

### III. The possession restriction supports the distinct prohibited-recipient supply regime

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented facial commerce challenge to section 922(g)(9) fails because possession by the qualifying recipient class is a fitted anti-evasion and anti-impairment component of the valid federal restriction on supplying firearms and ammunition to that class. The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit that inclusion, while section 922(g)'s enacted interstate conditions remain separate requirements rather than an automatic constitutional cure.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 922(d)(9) restricts sale or other disposition to a recipient known, or reasonably believed, to have the qualifying conviction. Allowing that recipient class to acquire and retain covered firearms free of the possession restriction would permit evasion and materially impair that supply rule. *Lopez* requires identification of this mechanism before aggregate consequences can do constitutional work. *Hale* developed necessary inclusion for a different machinegun-stock regime; *Kirk* applied that regime without requiring a defendant-specific transaction. Their method supports this new application, but their results do not dictate it merely because both statutes concern guns. The specific supply-and-recipient relationship supplies the connection here. Congress's authority over the separately enacted interstate shipment, transportation and receipt branches also remains; this facial demand does not remove those direct interstate applications. No individual firearm's interstate facts are found in this civil action, and any prosecution must establish the actual statutory conditions. Remote historical movement, dangerousness or general crime costs alone supplies no universal firearms power.

### IV. Ordinary equality review permits the domestic-violence category

**Holding and operative rule:** On the preserved ordinary-review challenge, Congress may restrict firearm access following a qualifying domestic-violence misdemeanor without extending the restriction to every other violent misdemeanor at the same time. The specified violent-conduct and domestic-relationship predicates rationally connect this classification to preventing further violence.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lee Optical* and *Beach* permit incremental regulation, while *Heller v. Doe* and *Romer* require a rational relation to the actual disadvantage imposed. The category requires force, attempted force or a threatened deadly weapon within the statutory domestic relationships, with conviction safeguards and relief qualifications. That is a specific connection to the risk addressed, rather than mere dislike of a group. The argument that Congress should also address other violent misdemeanants proposes a broader reform; it does not negate the relation supporting this one. The Court does not resolve the abandoned heightened-review request, declare every misdemeanor disqualifying, or dispense with meaningful review whenever public safety is invoked.

### V. Different restoration systems do not invalidate this federal selector

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may recognize the convicting jurisdiction's expungement, set-aside, pardon and qualified civil-rights-restoration arrangements without guaranteeing identical firearm consequences across States or between offenses that do and do not cause loss of civil rights. The presented facial equality challenge based on those differences fails; the Court need not decide whether rights never lost can count as restored in a particular statutory application.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute identifies actual conviction relief under local law and separately conditions the restoration route on that jurisdiction providing loss of civil rights for the offense. The strongest objection is that a less serious offense can therefore produce a more persistent firearm disability than some felonies. But recognizing actual local relief is a rational selector, and expungement, set-aside and pardon remain distinct textual avenues even when restoration is unavailable. Their existence does not establish that Hiley can practically obtain any particular relief. *Caron* distinguishes the jurisdiction supplying restoration status from federal law determining its consequences; it does not decide this never-lost-rights question. Ordinary review does not require uniform state practices or compel a new federal restoration process. The statutory firearms-reservation qualification remains operative, and no individual restoration application is adjudicated.

### VI. The distinct occupational-effects theory lacks a discriminatory-purpose allegation

**Holding and operative rule:** The greater employment effect of a qualifying-conviction firearm restriction on occupations requiring weapons does not itself establish the asserted occupational discrimination where the rule applies beyond those occupations and the complaint alleges no purpose to disadvantage law-enforcement officers. That uneven-effects theory fails without changing the separate analysis of an express statutory classification.

**Controlling explanation:** *Feeney* distinguishes unequal effects of an otherwise rational rule from discrimination attributable to a purpose directed at the disadvantaged group. Hiley's lost armed assignment is substantial, but the conviction-based rule applies to persons outside law enforcement as well. The supported complaint supplies no allegation that Congress chose it to disadvantage officers. This theory is distinct from the express official-use exclusion considered in *Fraternal Order of Police*, which survives for its own fitted-gap and conduct-based reasons. The Court does not substitute an intent requirement for rational review of every express classification or erase the practical employment burden.

### VII. Public employment alone supplies no fundamental substantive entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** A state-created employment interest and a wish to retain an armed public position do not alone establish a fundamental substantive federal right to that position. The qualifying-conduct firearm restriction rationally serves prevention of armed recurrence, so the preserved legislative employment claim fails.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love* distinguishes a state-created interest from the substantive federal guarantee claimed. The serious effect on Hiley's employment does not make the federal rule arbitrary: the qualifying violent conduct and prospective access restriction supply the actual safety connection. Armed employment does not remove that connection. This conclusion does not declare personal arms outside constitutional protection, dispose of the abandoned procedural-due-process claim, invalidate ordinary employment procedures, or adjudicate a breach of the settlement. The settled local claims are not before the Court for renewed relief.

### VIII. The prohibition governs later firearm conduct, not punishment for the earlier battery

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying section 922(g)(9) to firearm conduct after its September 30, 1996 effective date does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause merely because the qualifying conviction occurred earlier. The conviction identifies the regulated class; the statute neither criminalizes completed preenactment firearm possession nor increases the sentence for Hiley's 1995 battery.

**Controlling explanation:** *Calder* and *Weaver* supply the prohibition against retrospective criminal punishment. Here the relevant prohibited conduct occurs after enactment. *Landgraf* helps distinguish use of antecedent facts from attaching new consequences to completed regulated acts; its civil temporal framework does not replace the Ex Post Facto inquiry. The continuing employment burden does not turn the stated prospective conduct rule into an offense committed in 1995. The Court makes no ruling on notice or mental state in a later criminal prosecution, and the existence of an earlier conviction is not a general exemption from retrospective-punishment review. The separate attainder analysis below is not adopted as an interchangeable Ex Post Facto test.

### IX. This restriction is not legislative punishment without trial

**Holding and operative rule:** The general, prospective firearm disability tied to adjudicated qualifying domestic violence is not a bill of attainder under the historical, functional and purpose inquiries. Its fitted prevention of further violence and its serious but incidental employment consequences do not establish legislative occupational punishment or a congressional adjudication of guilt for a future firearm offense.

**Controlling explanation:** *Nixon* and *Selective Service System* require examination of the kind and severity of the burden, its relation to nonpunitive purposes, and evidence of punitive legislative purpose. The restriction regulates access after a judicial conviction; Congress did not adjudicate Hiley's guilt by name. Unlike the occupational proscription in *Brown*, it is a general firearm rule with effects on jobs requiring possession, not legislative exclusion from a profession based on the targeted association there. Prevention of armed recurrence supplies a concrete regulatory purpose. No congressional purpose to punish this claimant or his occupation is established. Any future firearm prosecution requires ordinary adjudication of that offense. The holding does not make every disability based on a past conviction automatically nonpunitive.

### X. The federal rule does not command the sheriff's administration

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged possession-and-supply rule does not violate the Tenth Amendment on the asserted staffing theory because it does not command the sheriff to terminate Hiley, legislate, prosecute federal offenses, administer background checks or appropriate funds. Its consequences for lawful staffing choices do not themselves make it a federal direction to administer a regulatory program.

**Controlling explanation:** *New York* distinguishes direct regulation from compelled state legislation and program responsibility. *Printz* preserves its confined source-and-function rule and unresolved fiscal objections concerning affirmative officer duties. Hiley presents no such statutory command: the employer chose termination and then reassignment, while the federal law regulates the relevant firearm conduct. The Court therefore rejects this particular structural objection independently of the commerce holding. It does not adopt the lower court's broader proposition that every exercise of commerce authority necessarily defeats every Tenth Amendment claim, nor does it turn *Printz*'s fiscal reservation into immunity from this direct rule.

### Statutory qualifications governing these holdings

Section 922(g)(9) separately covers interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of firearms or ammunition previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Section 922(d)(9)'s distinct sale-or-disposition rule requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction; that mental-state condition is not silently imported as the complete culpability rule of section 922(g)(9). The present civil action decides no prosecution's proof or notice defense.

Under section 921(a)(33), the qualifying offense must be a federal or state misdemeanor with an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. The person must have had counsel or knowingly and intelligently waived counsel. If entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, the person must have received a jury trial or knowingly and intelligently waived it by plea or otherwise; this creates no additional jury entitlement. Hiley's predicate is treated as qualifying in this action, without supplying an unproved different conviction or deciding a challenge to one of these conditions.

An expunged or set-aside conviction, a pardon, or restoration of civil rights where the jurisdiction provides for loss of those rights for the offense can remove the conviction from the definition, subject to the express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court neither enlarges that wording nor decides its application to Hiley. No actually available pardon, administrative relief process or individual relief entitlement is found.

Section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception does not extend to sections 922(d)(9) or 922(g)(9); it concerns its defined governmental channels, not every privately held gun of a public employee. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with timely renewal when required, during indictment and until a conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement here. The Court does not decide any of those individual applications.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Hunt** and **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** applied to the uniform prospective representative claim, preserving real member injury and the distinct participation requirement; United Food's WARN exception is not generalized.
- **Love, Hale and Rybar:** applied to reject a collective-only arms premise and distinguish personal invocation from a particular restriction. Love's state-interest/federal-right distinction also governs the employment theory.
- **Wright:** its Core-Preservation Review governs a preserved arms challenge; constitutional coverage precedes justification measured by practical burden. It supplies no decision or automatic remand on Hiley's abandoned claim.
- **Lopez:** applied for the identified interstate mechanism and the boundary against generalized crime costs or jurisdictional words alone.
- **Hale** and **Kirk:** their necessary-inclusion method is extended to the particular prohibited-supply/recipient relationship established here. Their machinegun-stock holdings are not treated as already deciding section 922(g)(9).
- **Fraternal Order of Police (October 7, 1998):** its application of Wright treats the qualifying domestic-violence disability as a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny and rejects an official-use exception. That arms rule governs a preserved challenge, which Hiley does not present. Its resolved express classification is distinct from Hiley's uneven occupational-effects theory; its reservation of the different commerce question is resolved here only for the presented facial challenge.
- **Lee Optical** and **Beach:** applied for rational incremental regulation, without eliminating the need for a relevant justification.
- **Heller v. Doe** and **Romer:** applied for a relation to the actual disadvantage; their distinct operative holdings remain, and no narrower-alternative or mandatory-animus test replaces ordinary review.
- **Caron:** applied only for the jurisdictional selector and federal consequences; its actual residual-firearms restriction holding does not answer whether never-lost rights are restored.
- **Feeney:** applied to the separate occupational-effects claim and its absent discriminatory-purpose allegation, without imposing that requirement on every express classification.
- **Calder** and **Weaver:** applied to retrospective punishment; **Landgraf** supplies the distinction between antecedent facts and completed regulated conduct without becoming the constitutional test. **Lynce** supplies no Ex Post Facto merits holding in the governing law and is not used for one.
- **Nixon** and **Selective Service System:** applied for the distinct attainder inquiries; **Brown** is distinguished in its legislative occupational-proscription setting.
- **New York** and **Printz:** preserved at their actual command, source/function and fiscal boundaries; the absence of an administrative command independently defeats this staffing objection.

No precedent is overruled. The district order's collective-only arms premise and its automatic inference from commerce authority to Tenth Amendment validity are not adopted.

## Law After Decision

Effective October 9, 1998, section 922(g)(9) survives the presented facial commerce challenge through the identified relationship between prohibited supply to qualifying recipients and their possession, with enacted interstate conditions retained. This is a distinct application of necessary inclusion beyond the machinegun-stock cases, not a universal historical-movement rule.

The three preserved equality theories and the substantive employment claim fail on their separate grounds. The prospective conduct rule survives the Ex Post Facto challenge, and the distinct attainder inquiry finds no legislative punishment here. The direct rule commands no state administration on the presented record. Wright's Core-Preservation Review and Fraternal Order of Police's intermediate-scrutiny application remain governing for preserved arms challenges. This decision does not adjudicate Hiley's abandoned arms claim. Other abandoned claims, a genuinely different private-possession application, individual qualification and restoration disputes, and criminal notice or mental-state questions remain unadjudicated. Stone-Zsela's additional personal-right application and never-lost-rights interpretation are noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment, unjoined.** He agrees with the entire affirmance on his stated grounds without joining the Court's opinion. He preserves a distinct personal-right and classification ground. *Love* protects persons but does not make adjudicated violent conduct irrelevant. The force, attempted-force or deadly-weapon predicate within a domestic relationship, together with counsel and jury-where-entitled protections and the statute's relief provisions, connects the disability to preventing armed recurrence. A misdemeanor label and armed employment do not eliminate that connection. Hiley's particular predicate is treated as qualifying here; no new conviction is invented.

He would not rewrite retained civil rights as restored rights merely because state systems differ or some misdemeanors caused no initial rights loss. That statutory position goes beyond the Court's decision to leave the never-lost-rights construction unresolved. His personal-right discussion supplies his alternative ground for rejecting the preserved classification challenge; it does not adjudicate an arms cause or revive procedural due process. He recognizes that a preserved Second Amendment challenge would be governed by Wright's Core-Preservation Review as Fraternal Order of Police applies it; Hiley abandoned that claim, so no further arms question is decided here. He approves no disarmament based on any misdemeanor, an allegation or a nonviolent status, and leaves a genuinely different as-applied record open.

He retains jurisdiction because successful federal relief would restore Hiley's armed-deputy assignment, while respecting the local settlement. His reasoning preserves the identified supply-and-possession mechanism, the distinct rejection of a state-administration command, and the prospective, nonpunitive character of this restriction. He would neither issue a criminal judgment, order reinstatement, reopen settled claims nor decide a future prosecution's notice defense or a claimed breach of the settlement.

## Procedure After Action

The dismissal of the surviving federal action is affirmed. No preliminary or permanent injunction, reinstatement, damages award or merits remand issues. The county and sheriff claims remain dismissed with prejudice under the settlement. No firearm conviction or sentence, later criminal notice defense, settlement-breach claim or new private-arms cause is adjudicated. No further Supreme Court proceeding is directed.
