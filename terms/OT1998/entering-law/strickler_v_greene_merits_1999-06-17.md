## Event

Strickler v. Greene, No. 98-5864. Argued March 3, 1999; decided June 17. Certiorari to the Fourth Circuit's judgment directing dismissal of the federal habeas petition. The questions concern cause for the defaulted suppression claim and cumulative Brady materiality separately for capital guilt and selection of death. The March 5, 1996 application preceded AEDPA; former law governs the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions. No Chapter 154 qualification follows from capital status alone.

Five suppressed documents—Exhibits 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6—qualify Stoltzfus's identification and early recollection and expose reconstruction and prolonged photo-assisted identification. She witnessed the abduction, not the killing. Suppression of three other documents remains disputed. Independent evidence concerns joint active participation, the victim's car and property, physical connections and a contested account of the killing conversation. The instruction permitted capital guilt for active joint participation in the fatal beating, without requiring a sole fatal actor. No separate instruction or recantation claim is decided.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court finds cause but affirms denial of the reviewed conviction and death-sentence relief for lack of materiality.

## Holdings

### Suppression and reasonable disclosure reliance establish cause here

Governmental suppression of the five documents, combined with counsel's reasonable reliance on the prosecution's open-file practice and the Commonwealth's later completeness representation during state collateral proceedings, supplies cause for this omitted claim. General awareness of interviews and changing recollection, and the existence of a discretionary discovery avenue, do not on these facts establish knowledge of the missing factual basis or a duty to distrust the asserted disclosure.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I has eight direct votes; Thomas dissents from this ground.

Murray v. Carrier and Amadeo distinguish an external impediment and unavailable factual basis from ordinary attorney error. The case-team police possessed the missing material, and Kyles does not let the prosecution avoid its disclosure responsibility through personal ignorance. That responsibility is distinct from an entitlement to unrestricted open-file access. Here the actual open-file practice and later response asserting full Brady disclosure reasonably concealed the particular basis of this claim.

The Commonwealth's later response reinforced reliance while state proceedings continued; it is not moved to a time before the state petition was filed. References to several interviews and recollection changes gave general information, not the five documents' specific contents. Nor does a discretionary discovery mechanism prove that a hypothetical request would have produced them. The Court decides this combination, not automatic cause for every Brady allegation or a new right to collateral counsel. Coleman v. Thompson's default rule and recognized excuses remain. Cause permits the materiality inquiry; it does not establish prejudice, a completed Brady violation or entitlement to a writ.

### The cumulative suppression does not undermine confidence in capital guilt

Considering all lawful combined uses of the five established suppressed documents in the whole record, there is no reasonable probability of a different capital-guilt verdict sufficient to undermine confidence in that verdict. This conclusion assesses revised preparation and presentation as well as impeachment; it does not require proof of innocence or rest merely on legally sufficient remaining evidence.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II is joined by all nine, with Thomas adopting it as an independent alternative to his default ground.

Kyles requires the documents to be considered together. Their identification uncertainty, reconstructed timing and photo assistance could support a materially different cross-examination and investigation of how a tentative account became confident. Disclosure could also alter the defense presentation; counsel's existing arguments are not binding concessions that erase those uses. The Court assumes substantial damage to Stoltzfus's confident portrayal and considers the Henderson-related defense evidence and challenges to Tudor.

The independent driving, property, bank-card, blood and forensic evidence and the supported account of the killing conversation nevertheless preserve confidence in Strickler's active joint participation. Stoltzfus did not witness the killing. The actual instruction permitted joint participation in the fatal acts and did not require identifying who alone dropped the rock; evidence of Henderson's participation therefore does not necessarily displace Strickler's active role. The Court does not convert possession into eyewitness proof or add later admissions and an unverified recantation to the jury's evidence.

The five-document assessment needs no suppression finding concerning Exhibits 2, 7 and 8. It adjudicates no freestanding challenge to the instruction's disputed predicate. No second harmlessness inquiry is imposed: the claim fails at Brady's initial confidence inquiry on the guilt record itself.

### The separate death-selection inquiry also retains confidence

After separately considering the five documents' combined lawful effects on the jury's individualized selection of death, the Court finds no reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation sufficient to undermine confidence in that selection. Reliable capital guilt or a surviving aggravator does not by itself answer this question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas's join is an independent alternative. Five other Justices directly adopt the complete rationale without relying on Thomas's default position.

The witness's vivid portrayal of Strickler as the aggressive initiator matters beyond the bare fact of participation. The Court considers the potential for a substantially changed defense account, including greater emphasis on Henderson's role, and the actual jury-closing references to grabbing and abducting a stranger in the dangerousness argument. The longer leadership passage at 20R15 was addressed to the judge, not the penalty jury. The prior-record account describes no convictions involving actual violence; the Court does not invent such convictions or equate that qualification with absence of all dangerousness evidence.

Even with severe impeachment of Stoltzfus's certainty and relative-role portrayal, the independent evidence of active fatal conduct, the brutality of the rock killing and the supported surrounding conduct retain their force in the individualized death choice. The Court evaluates those facts with the revised defense uses; it does not treat argument brevity, lawful eligibility or the existence of an aggravator as conclusive. The complete jury mitigation presentation is not established by the sentencing judge's later account, and no assumed rejection of childhood-abuse testimony supplies this conclusion. The judge's separate leadership comments furnish neither a substitute jury finding nor an independent cure.

Tuggle preserves the distinction between eligibility and selection within its own assistance setting; it does not mandate this Brady result. Kyles governs the present initial materiality inquiry without a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle. No different burden from Coleman, Lilly or Neder is imposed. The claim fails because the collective disclosure counterfactual does not sufficiently change confidence in this actual selection, not because every eligible defendant may receive death.

## Precedent Treatment

- Murray v. Carrier: applies the objective-external-impediment requirement for cause, preserving the distinction from ordinary attorney error.
- Amadeo v. Zant: applies its treatment of an unavailable factual basis concealed by government conduct.
- Coleman v. Thompson: preserves procedural default and its established excuses; this record satisfies cause for the reasons stated.
- Kyles v. Whitley: applies collective lawful uses, case-team disclosure responsibility and the confidence standard; preserves the prohibition on a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle after a material violation is established.
- Tuggle v. Netherland: preserves the distinct inquiry into death selection despite surviving eligibility, without converting its Ake rule into automatic Brady relief.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: apply former-law treatment of this pre-AEDPA capital application for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions; no broader temporal exemption is decided.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel: preserves default, exhaustion and recognized gateways without resolving the suppression-specific cause facts.
- Calderon v. Coleman: distinguishes its established penalty-instruction error and collateral harmlessness inquiry from initial Brady materiality.
- Lilly v. Virginia and Neder v. United States: preserve their distinct error rules; neither adds a harmlessness hurdle or supplies automatic reversal for this nondisclosure.

## Law After Decision

The actual suppression and reasonable-reliance combination establishes cause, while cumulative materiality remains independently necessary. Guilt and death selection require separate confidence assessments, with all supported lawful disclosure uses considered. This record establishes neither phase's materiality. The decision creates no eligibility-only cure, categorical rule about abduction testimony or additional harmlessness burden. The reviewed dismissal stands.

## Separate Writings

Thomas concurs in the judgment and the merits holdings as independent alternatives. He would also enforce default: known repeated interviews and changes in recollection, together with the available state discovery route, warranted further inquiry despite the open-file practice. He does not assume that a request certainly would have succeeded. His diligence conclusion has no other join.

Souter, joined by Kennedy, concurs in cause and conviction denial but dissents from sentence denial. The five early accounts expose uncertainty and reconstruction behind the apparently disinterested witness's vivid allocation of initiative and dominance. That allocation could materially alter the jury's moral choice even where independent proof preserves active joint guilt. The supported jury references to stranger abduction connect the account to dangerousness; the argument is not confined to how often the prosecutor mentioned the witness. The five suffice without finding the other three suppressed, specifying unknown jury mitigation or treating the judge's comments as a separate violation. They would direct conditional sentence-only relief, allowing Virginia within a reasonable district-court period to provide a lawful disposition correcting the jury-selection injury or a lawful nondeath disposition. If it failed to comply, the death sentence would be vacated, with convictions and valid imprisonment intact. They prescribe no particular jury/judge mechanism or automatic life term.

Stone-Zsela concurs in cause and conviction denial and separately dissents on punishment. Integrating the five documents with the defense and independent evidence preserves guilt confidence, but undermining the dominant-aggressor portrayal creates a reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation. The trial narrative and actual jury references support that conclusion without transferring the longer court-directed passage to the jury or assuming violent prior convictions or a particular mitigation presentation. He would require a conditional sentence-only writ unless Virginia conducts a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable period fixed by the district court. The conviction remains intact, and Virginia may impose any lawfully available sentence after that proceeding. He does not decide the three disputed documents or the new proceeding's outcome.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's reviewed dismissal remains effective on the independent no-materiality grounds, with its cause rationale rejected. No new penalty or guilt proceeding is directed. Unrelated claims and independently available procedures are not resolved, and no execution timetable or release order is entered.
