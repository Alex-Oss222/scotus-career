#### Jaffee v. Redmond, 518 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** 95-266
**Decided:** June 13, 1996

##### What confidential psychotherapy communications does federal common law protect?

**Holding:** Federal common law protects confidential communications between a patient and licensed psychotherapist during diagnosis or treatment, including confidential psychotherapy by a licensed clinical social worker and the treatment conversations and notes at issue. Professional title alone does not protect administrative conversations, ordinary workplace counseling or communications outside confidential treatment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined on all controlling grounds by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight. Scalia dissents.

**Material application:** Beyer's license, training and actual psychotherapy place Redmond's treatment within the privilege. Rule 501's reason-and-experience delegation, treatment's reliance on predictable confidentiality, and widespread state recognition justify the defined relationship privilege despite evidentiary cost.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general physician-patient or all-social-worker privilege is created. State recognition does not establish identical definitions or exceptions; proposed Rule 504 was not enacted. Rule 501's state-law sentence continues to govern an element of a civil claim or defense supplied by state law. Choice of privilege law for evidence relevant to both state and federal claims in an actual conflict remains undecided; no general federal-preemption rule is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Trammel supplies development through reason and experience with attention to function and cost. University of Pennsylvania's rejection of an unsupported peer-review privilege is distinguished by this demonstrated relationship and justification.

##### May routine evidentiary balancing defeat the privilege or valid withholding support an adverse inference?

**Holding:** A protected communication does not lose privilege merely because a judge weighs the opposing litigant's evidentiary need more heavily. Valid assertion cannot support the adverse inference and lack-of-legal-justification instruction used here.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same eight-Justice controlling opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A patient may waive protection, but no wholesale waiver is adjudicated from partial answers and three redacted pages. Waiver rules, court-ordered examinations, litigation-use exceptions, criminal constitutional overrides and serious-threat disputes remain open. The seven Associates' serious-threat illustration is nondecisional and establishes no operative exception; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Seventh Circuit affirmed, 8–1; the new trial stands without its general balancing rationale, protecting validly withheld communications. No reinstatement of the two damages verdicts or finding that the shooting was lawful follows; the distinct deadly-force instruction issue is not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Nixon's executive privilege in criminal process supplies no universal balancing requirement. Daubert and Williamson concern admissibility and reliability rather than Rule 501 relationship protection.

#### Swidler & Berlin v. United States, 524 U.S. 399

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1192.

**Decided:** June 25, 1998.

##### Attorney-client confidentiality survives death without a general investigative-need exception

**Holding:** The federal attorney-client privilege ordinarily continues after the client’s death. Substantial importance to a criminal investigation alone does not authorize disclosure of protected communications through a case-specific need balance; waiver, crime-fraud and genuinely distinct recognized exceptions retain their separate predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the rule, its explanation, limits and reversal.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The legal-consultation notes remain protected against the asserted need ground. The Court finds no contents, waiver, crime-fraud predicate or subjective intention of this client. Work product, successor authority or waiver, a presented testamentary dispute and an exceptional criminal defendant’s constitutional need are not decided. Independently discoverable facts remain distinct, and a future source-grounded exception requires its own proper record. Rule 501’s separate state-law sentence remains effective; no comprehensive code of privilege exceptions is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The court of appeals’ privilege-balancing disposition is reversed. Further proceedings must protect the communications against the asserted investigative-need ground. No production order issues, and work product or a genuinely distinct, properly supported exception receives no decision here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Upjohn Co. v. United States:** Predictable confidence at the time legal advice is sought supports survival after death; protection of communications does not conceal independently discoverable facts.
- **Trammel v. United States:** Its reason-and-experience development of privilege is applied with attention to function and evidentiary cost, not an automatic preference for disclosure.
- **Glover v. Patten:** Its client-serving testamentary setting is distinguished from a general criminal-investigative exception; no actual estate dispute is resolved.
- **Jaffee v. Redmond:** Its predictable-relationship principle and rejection of routine need balancing support the reasoning. Its psychotherapy scope and reserved contours remain unchanged.
- **United States v. Nixon:** Its executive-privilege and criminal-trial setting is distinguished; it does not require balancing within every established confidential relationship.

#### Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1853.
**Decided:** 1997-05-27.

For connected appellate review and the invalid categorical trial stay, see Federal Courts — Presidential immunity appeals and trial stays, Clinton v. Jones, May 27, 1997, No. 95-1853.

##### No temporary immunity for the presented pre-presidential conduct

**Holding:** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a properly brought federal civil action seeking damages for alleged unofficial conduct before taking office. The Presidency alone neither requires dismissal without prejudice nor suspends that action until the term ends; concrete demands upon official duties require their own accommodation analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns a properly brought federal damages action for alleged unofficial pre-office conduct. It decides no liability, state-court proceedings, criminal proceedings or compulsory attendance at a particular place or time. Litigation's political motive or the claim's abstract importance supplies no constitutional immunity rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fitzgerald preserves official presidential decisionmaking immunity; Forrester distinguishes function from identity and Antoine supplies a limited functional analogy. United States v. Nixon rejects absolute insulation from process but reserves civil-evidence disputes. Nixon v. United States, Morgan Stanley, Plaut and Reich retain their confined structural/remedial holdings and establish no general civil exemption.

##### Official-act immunity turns on function, not presidential status

**Holding:** Fitzgerald's absolute personal civil-damages immunity continues to protect conduct within the outer perimeter of presidential official responsibility; title alone cannot convert pre-presidential conduct into an official presidential act, and alleged illegality or improper motive alone does not strip an otherwise official act of that protection. The possible official-function defense concerning post-inauguration press-secretary statements remains for initial District Court consideration on a developed record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The post-inauguration press-secretary statements are not classified on this undeveloped record, and not every pleaded allegation is declared unofficial. No new universal classification sequence, reasonable-officeholder test, motive or grave-wrongdoing exception, or qualified-immunity replacement is adopted; state-office defenses and claim elements remain separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fitzgerald retains its outer-perimeter protection and rejection of improper purpose alone as disqualifying. Forrester distinguishes responsibility performed from present title without diluting that presidential immunity.

##### Concrete presidential duties receive case-specific accommodation

**Holding:** The District Court must give substantial respect to the President's constitutional duties throughout this action; a party seeking protection must identify the particular litigation demand and the conflict or hardship, and any relief must respond to its supported need, duration and alternatives while accounting for the plaintiff's legitimate interests. The court may adjust timing, sequence or manner and may grant a justified, bounded stay, but its discretion cannot authorize significant constitutionally forbidden impairment of another branch's assigned functions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Landis's clear-showing burden applies when even a fair possibility of harm from the requested stay exists. Relief must remain within justified need, duration and alternatives and be reconsidered as circumstances change; neither a term-long presumption nor a burden requiring the plaintiff to prove exceptional/irreparable prejudice is adopted. There is no absolute date/place/method entitlement, universal hearing, special proof quantum, fixed discovery limit or new interlocutory appeal. Concrete compulsion, privilege, sanctions, irreconcilable scheduling conflict and its precise constitutional remedy are reserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Proceed under ordinary federal law, determine properly presented official-act and other defenses separately, and assess specifically supported accommodation requests. The denial of immunity and both categorical-stay removals are affirmed unanimously; no liability, damages or immediate fixed-date trial follows. The concurrences supply no additional controlling classification test, scheduling presumption, constitutional exception or attendance standard.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landis governs a supported stay at its stated harm trigger. Fitzgerald explains the institutional importance of presidential duties without immunizing private conduct. United States v. Nixon's civil-process reservation and Reich's presidential-coercion reservation remain intact.

### Confidential psychotherapy communications under Rule 501

**Governing question:** Federal common-law protection for confidential treatment communications with licensed psychotherapists.

**Current rule:** Confidential patient communications to a licensed psychotherapist in diagnosis or treatment, including actual confidential psychotherapy by a licensed clinical social worker, are privileged under federal common law. The protection covers treatment conversations and counseling notes, but title alone does not protect administrative exchanges, ordinary workplace counseling or communications outside confidential treatment. Protection does not turn on routine lawsuit-specific balancing of the opponent’s evidentiary need; a valid invocation cannot justify an adverse inference or instruction treating withholding as legally unjustified. Waiver remains possible, but no particular waiver rule is established.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected relationship and social-worker inclusion: I. Confidential psychotherapy communications are privileged; no routine balancing and no adverse inference: II. Protection does not depend on routine balancing in each lawsuit, 1996-06-13. Stevens with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight) on each holding.

**Present operation effective:** Jaffee, 1996-06-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 501’s Constitution, congressional and authorized-rule boundaries and its separate state-law sentence remain. No general physician-patient privilege follows. Conflict for evidence relevant to federal and state claims, court-ordered examinations, litigation use, constitutional criminal overrides and serious threats remain unadjudicated. The seven-Justice serious-threat illustration is not an operative exception.

### Conn v. Gabbert, No. 97-1802; judgment below, 131 F.3d 793. — 1999-04-05

#### Holdings

##### This interruption does not establish deprivation of occupational liberty

**Holding and operative rule:** Liberty to pursue a profession does not guarantee freedom from government interruption on every occasion for providing professional services. This search-related interruption of consultation, with no prohibition on practice, impairment of professional qualifications or supported consequential interference with Gabbert's legal practice, does not establish deprivation of his Fourteenth Amendment occupational liberty, even assuming the prosecutors deliberately timed the search to obstruct consultation.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this occupational-merits portion of Stone-Zsela's Court opinion and its record-limited application. It is the sufficient ground for reversal; no combination of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Roth recognizes liberty to pursue a vocation, not a personal guarantee of every opportunity to perform a professional service. Collins requires an identified constitutional deprivation; its separate workplace-omission holding does not itself answer affirmative interference with a lawyer. The reported record supplies no evidence of adverse effects on Gabbert's income, reputation, clientele or professional qualifications. It separately supplies no real evidence or allegation of substantial prejudice to the client. Those descriptions do not erase the interrupted consultations or establish lawful search execution. They identify the limited professional interference shown here. Gabbert's strongest argument is that deliberately obstructing consultation at the moment counsel is needed can injure professional activity without lasting financial harm. The Court does not reject that possibility categorically, but the asserted vocation guarantee and this record do not establish the professional deprivation claimed. Duration alone is not decisive, and permanent exclusion, lost fees, reputational harm and client prejudice do not become mandatory elements for every occupational-liberty case. Soldal and James Daniel Good preserve genuinely independent constitutional protections; Albright supplies no controlling broader displacement rule or palpable-consequence threshold. Lewis's emergency-pursuit standard does not govern this planned investigation, and Haddle's statutory employment-injury rule does not create a constitutional guarantee of uninterrupted practice. The Court decides Gabbert's own occupational claim, not his client's constitutional rights.

**Confined source explanation and authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join the explanation that, to the extent Gabbert objects that the search itself was unreasonable because of its execution or timing, the Fourth Amendment supplies the governing standard under Graham. Lanier preserves that actual-coverage rule without excluding every independent due-process injury. This is a distinction of constitutional sources, not a second sufficient ground that absorbs the entire occupational allegation; the occupational interest is resolved by the preceding holding. Stevens does not join this additional case-specific explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not hold the search lawful, extinguish a genuinely independent protected interest, foreclose preserved privilege or attorney-client objections, or decide the separately pleaded Fourth Amendment claim. It creates no immunity based on prosecutorial office or possession of a warrant. Because the asserted occupational deprivation is not established, the Court does not decide whether that asserted right was clearly established when the prosecutors acted. No mandatory merits-first sequence, absolute-immunity ruling or new client-right holding is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Board of Regents v. Roth supplies the recognition of vocational liberty; it does not establish an entitlement to every occasion for professional service.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, decided February 26, 1992, supplies the need for an actual constitutional deprivation. Its workplace-omission rules are not extended automatically to affirmative investigative interference.
- Graham v. Connor supplies the specific-protection inquiry for the search objection. United States v. Lanier, decided March 31, 1997, preserves that inquiry while rejecting categorical exclusion of independent due-process injuries.
- Albright v. Oliver, decided January 24, 1994, retains its fractured judgment without a controlling combined rationale. No broader displacement rule or palpable-consequence requirement is extracted from it.
- Soldal v. Cook County and United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property preserve genuinely independent protections despite possible constitutional overlap. City of West Covina v. Perkins, decided January 13, 1999, preserves that distinction in the criminal-warrant setting without deciding occupational liberty.
- County of Sacramento v. Lewis, decided May 26, 1998, remains confined to its supported emergency-pursuit setting; its culpability rule does not decide this planned investigation.
- Haddle v. Garrison, decided December 14, 1998, remains a statutory witness-conspiracy injury decision, not a constitutional entitlement to uninterrupted employment or professional practice.

#### Law After Decision

The described interruption does not establish Gabbert's claimed occupational deprivation. The ruling creates neither a categorical rule that every temporary interference is harmless nor a requirement of permanent exclusion or financial loss. An objection to this search as unreasonable belongs under its specific constitutional standard, while a genuinely independent professional deprivation requires its own analysis. The search claim and immunity law are otherwise unchanged; no precedent is overruled.
