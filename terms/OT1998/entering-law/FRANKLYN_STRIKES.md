# Entering-Law Reading Slice

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposeful racial peremptory strikes by the criminal defense

### Purposeful racial peremptory strikes by the criminal defense

**Governing question:** State objection to a criminal defendant's purposeful exclusion of prospective jurors by race through peremptory challenges.

**Current rule:** The defense's invocation of state peremptory authority to secure judicial exclusion from a governmental jury is state action for that particular act; purposeful racial exclusion violates the juror's equal-protection right. The State may assert that right in its own criminal trial because its tribunal suffers an integrity injury, it has a direct relationship with summoned jurors and jurors face substantial obstacles to separate litigation. When the State establishes a prima facie inference of purposeful discrimination, the defense must give race-neutral explanations and the judge must decide whether the State has proved purposeful discrimination. The ultimate persuasion burden remains on the State. Effective counsel and impartiality confer no right to racial exclusion; genuine race-neutral challenges, actual-bias inquiry and appropriate protection of concrete confidential matters remain available.

**Authority by component:**
- Attribution and prohibition: Georgia v. McCollum, “1. Purposeful racial defense peremptories are attributable to the State”, June 18, 1992.
- State's assertion of juror rights: McCollum, “2. The State may assert the excluded jurors' rights,” June 18, 1992.
- Ordinary sequence and defenses: McCollum, “3. Batson's ordinary burdens govern, with defense protections preserved,” June 18, 1992.
- Each component: Blackmun, joined by White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter (five); Batson's purpose, production and persuasion requirements remain unchanged within the newly covered defense selection act.

**Present operation effective:** Georgia v. McCollum, June 18, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary defense advice, representation and investigation do not become state action. No explanation is automatically required for every minority-juror strike. Nonracial classifications, unconscious-bias safeguards and particular privilege disputes remain undecided; no general confidential-review procedure is imposed.

**Holdings navigation:** Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992) — Standing to assert third-party rights; Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992) — Racial peremptory challenges.

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

### Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

**Governing question:** How equal protection governs a State’s use of sex in peremptory jury selection.

**Current rule:** The State may not strike a juror because of sex or a stereotype that sex predicts competence or impartiality. Sex classifications must substantially serve an important governmental objective; a generalized prediction of sympathy does not suffice. The objector first must make the required prima facie showing, after which the striking party must produce a genuine sex-neutral explanation, without having to establish cause. The court decides purposeful discrimination with the ultimate persuasion burden on the objector. A correlated individual characteristic may be a genuine reason but cannot serve as pretext or proxy. Established discriminatory selection requires a new trial before a lawfully selected jury, not a proportionally composed jury.

**Authority by component:**

- Prohibition, staged proof and conditional new-trial remedy: J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B — Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes; Staged proof and the remand, 1994-04-19. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the rule and explanation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment and independently supports the operative prohibition on individual-right grounds. Blackmun’s six-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the procedure and conditional remedy; Kennedy independently agrees with the operative inquiry and remand, producing seven votes for reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B, 1994-04-19.

**Open question:** The sex-strike rule for private parties is not decided. The earlier racial-strike rules, including the criminal-defense rule, retain their separate scope.

**Holdings navigation:** Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes; Staged proof and the remand.
