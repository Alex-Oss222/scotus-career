#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.
- Siegert v. Gilley: preserves early examination of a cognizable constitutional deprivation without converting its threshold holding into a special motive burden.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby: applies a valid governing substantive burden at summary judgment, without authorizing courts to invent a new one.
- New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: distinguishes the constitutional speech basis of its defamation burden from this retaliation action against an official.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: applies the claimant's protected-conduct and motivating-factor burden and the defendant's separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board: applies its actual-causation distinction, not school-specific substantive rules; a possible lawful reason is insufficient by itself.
- Pell v. Procunier and Turner v. Safley: preserve prison-access limitations and supply no unrestricted press-conference right.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: preserve the distinction between legal immunity review and disputed factual sufficiency; no new interlocutory-review right is created.

#### Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1440.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Who decides qualified immunity from an arrest-without-probable-cause claim and what record governs at summary judgment

**Holding:** Qualified immunity from a claim of arrest without probable cause is a legal question for the judge under ordinary summary-judgment constraints. The court must determine whether a reasonable officer could believe the arrest lawful on the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; choosing the “more reasonable” interpretation is not the test, and a generalized security concern alone does not establish a reasonable belief that the offense's elements were satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; these five Justices supply the controlling majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** The agents could consider assassination references, a reported gesture, oral statements, the confusing interview, and refusal to elaborate. They also had the letter's repeated attribution of the supposed plot to an outside group, bearing on whether Bryant communicated a serious threat under §871 or reported an imagined danger. The Ninth Circuit's comparison of the relative reasonableness of interpretations and its general assignment of legal immunity to a jury were erroneous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither guilt, innocence, bad faith, nor a constitutional violation. It neither directs immunity nor orders an automatic trial. The separate warrant ruling remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is vacated and the case remanded. The remand court must specify any supported dispute over material historical facts and decide immunity promptly under the correct standard.

**Material judgment:** Vacatur and remand are supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter, 6–2. White would direct immunity; Stevens would affirm, and Scalia would reverse and direct immunity. These differing remedial positions do not enlarge the five-Justice controlling rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow, Anderson v. Creighton, and Malley supply protection for reasonable mistakes and early judicial resolution when supported facts permit it. Watts supplies the contextual distinction between a true threat and protected expression. Mitchell, Beck, and Liberty Lobby are applied and preserved. No qualified-immunity rule is revised.

#### United States v. Ramirez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1469.

**Decided:** 1998-03-04.

##### Property damage does not raise the announcement threshold

**Holding:** Damage during warrant execution does not categorically require a greater exigency than particularized reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including through destruction of evidence. The facts supporting that exception must be known when the challenged force begins, and the means and extent of any destruction remain independently subject to Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Particular facts and rational inferences must support the exception; an offense category, hunch or warrant label is insufficient. There is no universal waiting period or changed review standard.

##### The contemporaneous danger and limited damage justified this execution

**Holding:** The reliable report of a dangerous escapee, corroborating observation and location-specific information about garage weapons supplied reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would create danger. Breaking one garage window to control access to those weapons, with a firearm directed through the opening for that purpose, was reasonable on this record; the justification permits neither gratuitous destruction nor any amount of damage merely because announcement may be excused.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ramirez’s later shot cannot justify the entry retrospectively, and the later failure to locate the escapee does not erase the information reasonably known beforehand. Gratuitous, punitive, disproportionate or objective-unrelated damage remains unapproved.

##### Section 3109 preserves a supported common-law danger exception

**Holding:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking to execute a search warrant after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate the officer or someone assisting execution. Its codification of the common-law announcement rule also preserves a danger exception satisfied by particularized reasonable suspicion that following the ordinary process would expose officers or occupants to danger; the pre-entry escapee and garage-weapons information satisfies that exception here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Material application:** Simultaneous announcement and breaking do not prove prior refusal, and no liberation predicate is established. The supported common-law danger qualification independently sustains the entry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statutory danger exception does not automatically incorporate every constitutional exception; futility, escape and evidence-destruction applications remain open. The Court decides no attenuation, independent-source, inevitable-discovery or exclusionary consequence of an actual violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The suppression judgment is reversed and remanded because the challenged entry and single-window damage violated neither the Fourth Amendment nor §3109. No particular prosecution outcome follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied for the common-law announcement protection and supported exceptions; it does not constitutionalize every federal statutory detail.
- Richards v. Wisconsin is applied for particularized reasonable suspicion and contemporaneous justification; its announcement quantum is not increased because damage occurs, and its separate review-method holding is unchanged.
- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301, supplies the statutory common-law background. Its reservation of a general exigency exception is respected rather than described as an earlier holding.
- Sabbath v. United States, 391 U.S. 585, retains its rule that opening a closed unlocked door is breaking and its rejection of unsupported danger. Its common-law discussion supports the danger qualification adopted here; physical damage creates no new announcement tier.
- Aponte Matos v. Toledo Dávila retains its separate conduct-date qualified-immunity scope and supplies no direct suppression immunity here.

### Announcement before dwelling entry

**Governing question:** The role of knock-and-announce in Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Current rule:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of the reasonableness inquiry when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened evidence destruction, may justify entry without announcement. Justification depends on the actual circumstances; the rule is not an inflexible requirement and does not itself decide exclusion or another remedy. ## Holdings

### I. Each respondent has a personal interest in the shared domestic privacy

**Operative rule:** Invited occupants who use a resident's deliberately screened home for a private activity possess their own Fourth Amendment interest against police acquisition of interior information; brief duration, commercial purpose and illegality, separately or together, do not automatically defeat that interest. Carter and Johns each have that interest on the actual host-authorized joint occupancy here; permission merely to enter, another person's rights or a desire to conceal crime alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The proposition controls despite their disagreement over the ultimate disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Rakas requires a person's own protected interest and rejects a claim derived solely from someone else's rights. Jones, as preserved by Rakas, shows that ownership is unnecessary. Olson recognizes privacy shared with a guest. Fields applies those principles both to independent control and, separately, to expressly permitted sharing of domestic privacy without an overnight arrangement. Its protection of Crawley's recurring closed-room use did not make recurrence indispensable to every shared-privacy claim.

The resident deliberately admitted Carter and Johns to the screened living interior, joined their private undertaking and allowed that use for approximately two and one-half hours. Their interest rests on that actual sharing, not her title alone. The State stresses the brief compensated transaction and absence of an established prior relationship or continuing control. Those facts bear on the relationship, but do not erase the personally shared private occupancy actually shown. Payment informs the arrangement without turning the domestic interior into a public business area. Criminal activity neither supplies the interest nor forfeits one otherwise established.

Neither respondent needs an accepted key finding for this application. Carter's disputed proffer is not adjudicated and is not attributed to Johns. The Court does not protect every casual visitor, unauthorized occupant, person permitted merely to enter, or activity plainly exposed to ordinary public observation. Recognition of personal privacy leaves the distinct question whether the officer's particular observation invaded it.

**Precedent treatment:** Rakas retains the personal-right requirement and exclusion of mere presence. Jones remains relevant to protected use without ownership. Olson's shared-home principle is applied beyond its overnight facts through Fields. Fields's relationship inquiry extends beyond Crawley's recurring use to the particular host-authorized sharing here; its independent-control branch and rejection of automatic protection for every visitor remain intact.

### II. Conditional suppression rules remain distinct from the observation question

**Operative rule:** If an antecedent intrusion is held unlawful in properly available further proceedings, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. A warrant's arrival does not retroactively authorize the earlier intrusion, and consent, attenuation, inevitable discovery and other asserted grounds require their own factual and procedural predicates; neither automatic exclusion nor automatic admission follows.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional remedial rationale. These conditional rules govern the remand if their triggering conditions arise; they do not find that this observation was unlawful or that any particular later item must be excluded.

**Controlling explanation:** Fields and Murray require genuine independence of both the decision to seek a warrant and its issuance. Karo preserves a supported untainted-warrant ground; Pinson and Myers separate correction of an antecedent search premise from final exclusion and from revival of unavailable arguments. The trial memorandum here treated the observation as central to the stop and warrants. Its rejection of the particular Franks attack on descriptions of the investigation and surveillance does not establish an independent source.

The stop, door opening, handgun discovery, arrests, apartment and vehicle warrants, physical evidence and statements require their actual personal-right and causal inquiries. The prosecutor's concession that an invalid arrest would bring down statements and physical evidence retains that condition. It does not concede that every defect in the observation automatically excludes every later item for both respondents. The ninety-minute surveillance gap is not erased.

Consent requires actual authority, scope, voluntariness and any applicable taint analysis. Leon affords no protection for knowing or reckless deception, abandoned judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia or a facially deficient warrant; the conduct of obtaining and supplying officers and proper execution scope matter. Evans remains confined to qualifying court-clerical error with objectively reasonable reliance, not a general exception for deliberate police investigation. Attenuation and inevitable discovery need their actual predicates. Ordinary preservation rules remain operative, and no new statement, Miranda or Franks claim is created.

The Court orders neither an evidence-by-evidence exclusion nor unconditional admission or reinstatement. The remaining claims may be resolved only to the extent properly available under ordinary procedure.

### III. The judgment does not establish a controlling search application

**Judgment without controlling search rationale:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer vacate the reviewed judgments to the extent they dispose of the federal suppression claims through the observation determination and remand for lawful further proceedings. O'Connor would require renewed actual-vantage analysis. The other four accept the judgment only on that point and would conclusively find no search on their qualified actual-view ground. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm because they find a search on the supported minimum.

The five-Justice personal-interest rule and eight-Associate conditional remedial rules control their subjects. No majority decides whether this observation was a search. O'Connor's reservation and the four-Justice conclusive no-search rule are not combined under Marks: leaving the legal application open does not supply a logical subset of a rule conclusively resolving it. The five votes for the operative judgment do not create a fifth vote for no search or for O'Connor's rationale.

The mandate removes the adverse federal suppression judgments and permits ordinary lawful further proceedings. It neither declares the record insufficient nor holds that further actual-vantage analysis is necessary. If a later disposition depends on rejecting an appellate factual characterization, the governing factual-review and preservation objections must be addressed. ## Holdings

### This interruption does not establish deprivation of occupational liberty

**Holding and operative rule:** Liberty to pursue a profession does not guarantee freedom from government interruption on every occasion for providing professional services. This search-related interruption of consultation, with no prohibition on practice, impairment of professional qualifications or supported consequential interference with Gabbert's legal practice, does not establish deprivation of his Fourteenth Amendment occupational liberty, even assuming the prosecutors deliberately timed the search to obstruct consultation.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this occupational-merits portion of Stone-Zsela's Court opinion and its record-limited application. It is the sufficient ground for reversal; no combination of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Roth recognizes liberty to pursue a vocation, not a personal guarantee of every opportunity to perform a professional service. Collins requires an identified constitutional deprivation; its separate workplace-omission holding does not itself answer affirmative interference with a lawyer. The reported record supplies no evidence of adverse effects on Gabbert's income, reputation, clientele or professional qualifications. It separately supplies no real evidence or allegation of substantial prejudice to the client. Those descriptions do not erase the interrupted consultations or establish lawful search execution. They identify the limited professional interference shown here. Gabbert's strongest argument is that deliberately obstructing consultation at the moment counsel is needed can injure professional activity without lasting financial harm. The Court does not reject that possibility categorically, but the asserted vocation guarantee and this record do not establish the professional deprivation claimed. Duration alone is not decisive, and permanent exclusion, lost fees, reputational harm and client prejudice do not become mandatory elements for every occupational-liberty case. Soldal and James Daniel Good preserve genuinely independent constitutional protections; Albright supplies no controlling broader displacement rule or palpable-consequence threshold. Lewis's emergency-pursuit standard does not govern this planned investigation, and Haddle's statutory employment-injury rule does not create a constitutional guarantee of uninterrupted practice. The Court decides Gabbert's own occupational claim, not his client's constitutional rights.

**Confined source explanation and authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join the explanation that, to the extent Gabbert objects that the search itself was unreasonable because of its execution or timing, the Fourth Amendment supplies the governing standard under Graham. Lanier preserves that actual-coverage rule without excluding every independent due-process injury. This is a distinction of constitutional sources, not a second sufficient ground that absorbs the entire occupational allegation; the occupational interest is resolved by the preceding holding. Stevens does not join this additional case-specific explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not hold the search lawful, extinguish a genuinely independent protected interest, foreclose preserved privilege or attorney-client objections, or decide the separately pleaded Fourth Amendment claim. It creates no immunity based on prosecutorial office or possession of a warrant. Because the asserted occupational deprivation is not established, the Court does not decide whether that asserted right was clearly established when the prosecutors acted. No mandatory merits-first sequence, absolute-immunity ruling or new client-right holding is adopted.

## Holdings

### Future discard and ordinary approach access do not themselves authorize inspection in protected residential space

**Holding and operative rule:** Greenwood does not authorize warrantless inspection of closed refuse containers merely because their owner intends later collection or private persons can approach them; courts must determine whether police entered or exploited constitutionally protected residential space and assess the actual location, exposure and intrusion. The lower determination must be set aside because collection-point equivalence and ordinary passage access cannot substitute for that legal inquiry into this home-side physical inspection; the Court leaves its ultimate search classification open.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the complete bounded rule and vacatur/remand. The three concurring Justices' additional search conclusion is not a majority holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenwood concerns refuse exposed for collection outside the home's protected surrounding area, or curtilage. Dunn examines proximity to the home, enclosure, the area's uses and measures shielding it from passersby; no one consideration controls. The layout here is known: the open common driveway supplies the approach to both front doors, and the containers stood near the connected garages about twenty-four feet from the sidewalk for routine collection. The lower court discussed Dunn and announced a fact-specific result. The legal objection is not that it ignored every circumstance. Ordinary passage and future transfer to a collector do not, without the proper residential-space inquiry, establish authority to remove concealed contents. Fields and Carter distinguish actual lawful access and natural observation from access to protected information or effects. The shared approach is substantial contrary evidence, and collection placement also matters; neither becomes a universal investigative license. The three concurring Justices find these facts already establish a search, but five agree that the adverse determination cannot stand on the legal equivalence used below. Reconsideration preserves supported facts and requires no finding that every driveway is curtilage. If no protected space was entered or exploited and the exposure fits Greenwood, the judgment may be reinstated. If a constitutional search occurred, ordinary warrant-fruit, independent-source, attenuation and suppression rules and the actual conditional-plea consequences apply on their established predicates. No exception, exclusion or outcome is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision creates no general privacy right in curbside garbage and does not decide a collector's private inspection, a private search later reported to police, or containers outside protected residential space freely accessible from a public route. It orders no new facts, automatic hearing or sentence and does not treat limited private access as general police permission.
