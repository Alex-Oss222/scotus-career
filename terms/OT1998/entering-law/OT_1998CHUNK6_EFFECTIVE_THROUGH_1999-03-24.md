## Event

Central State University v. American Association of University Professors, Central State University Chapter, No. 98-1071. Summary merits decision, March 22, 1999, following the separate certiorari grant of the same date. The case is decided without oral argument.

The Ohio Supreme Court invalidated Ohio's exclusion of public-university faculty workload standards from collective bargaining on the reviewed federal equal-protection ground. The question is whether that occupational distinction has a rational basis. The university seeks reversal; independent Ohio constitutional protections remain outside the federal holding.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the federal equal-protection judgment and remands in a per curiam opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded on the federal equal-protection question, 8–1.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens, who opposes summary resolution and takes no final position on the federal merits.

The Ohio courts may address surviving independent state-law questions. The Court does not adjudicate existing agreements, other statutory bargaining rights or retaliation for protected association.

## Opinion Topology

The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens dissents from summary disposition.

## Holdings

### Faculty workload administration supplies a rational basis for this distinction

**Holding and operative rule.** Ohio may exclude public-university faculty workload standards from collective bargaining to promote classroom teaching without proving that bargaining caused each earlier reduction in teaching. An ordinary occupational classification must rationally serve a legitimate purpose; a challenger cannot establish irrationality merely by identifying a lack of empirical proof or a different possible means.

**Authority.** The eight Justices joining the per curiam adopt this rule, its application and the stated limits in full.

**Controlling explanation.** Beach supplies ordinary rational-basis review without an empirical-certainty requirement. Nordlinger permits reasonable generalization, while Heller's distinct holdings require a rational connection to the actual disadvantage and preserve legislative choice among rational arrangements. Faculty classroom workloads are the subject Ohio seeks to administer. Removing those workload standards from bargaining can enable universities to increase or standardize classroom teaching, whether or not bargaining caused every past decline. The fact that other public employees retain bargaining rights does not defeat that subject-specific connection. Equality Foundation requires examination of the law's actual reach; it does not equate this defined management measure with a sweeping exclusion from ordinary civic protection. The presented record supplies no forbidden-purpose or independently protected-right ground that defeats the rational connection. The Court decides neither independent Ohio constitutional limits nor other bargaining and association rights.

## Precedent Treatment

- Beach is applied: ordinary economic and occupational classifications do not require empirical proof of the legislature's proposed causal account.
- Nordlinger is applied: reasonable generalization may sustain a classification despite imperfect fit.
- Heller's separate holdings are preserved: review examines the actual disadvantage without converting legislative choice into a necessity or empirical-certainty test.
- Equality Foundation is distinguished: the workload exclusion has a defined management relationship and does not enact the sweeping civic disability addressed there.

## Law After Decision

Existing rational-basis doctrine governs this faculty-workload classification and sustains it against the presented federal challenge. No new scrutiny tier, general immunity for anti-union action or rule overriding independent state protections is adopted.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents from the Court's summary treatment. He would deny review or, after the grant, permit plenary consideration of the occupational distinction and the state court's rationale. His Beach and Nordlinger positions require attention to the actual comparison between faculty and other workers; a general management objective should not obscure that inquiry. He acknowledges the possible force of the teaching-management connection and does not decide that Ohio's exclusion ultimately fails federal equal protection. His objection does not adopt an empirical-proof requirement or establish a separate academic-freedom holding.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court review of the federal question is complete. The cause returns to the Ohio courts for proceedings consistent with that ruling, including any properly surviving independent state-law question. No particular state-law outcome, new hearing or deadline is directed.

## Source Notes

The reviewed decision is reported at 83 Ohio St. 3d 229, 699 N.E.2d 463. The public case record appears in the [official United States Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/526bv.pdf), at 526 U.S. 124–134. The Court's ruling is confined to the federal equality ground and does not determine other bargaining protections or existing agreements.

## Event

Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, No. 97-1709. Merits decision, March 23, 1999. Argued December 7, 1998. ## Public Action

## Holdings

### I. Rule 702 requires reliable specialized knowledge, whatever the field

**Holding and operative rule:** Original Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized expert testimony. Experience may supply relevant knowledge, but the expert's professional label does not excuse a reliable connection between the method used and the proposition offered.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this rule; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Rule addresses knowledge that will assist the factfinder and expressly includes technical and other specialized knowledge. Its reliability requirement cannot turn on whether the witness describes the same practical inference as scientific or experiential. Daubert supplies the preliminary duty to examine foundation and fit; its scientific setting does not establish a categorical exemption for technical testimony. Joiner supplies the requirement that the offered conclusion have an adequately supported inferential connection. Experience can provide that connection, but credentials alone cannot replace it. The Eleventh Circuit therefore erred in excluding experience-based tire analysis from the reliability inquiry. Cross-examination remains available for admissible evidence; it does not eliminate the threshold foundation required by the Rule. This holding does not decide ultimate defect or causation.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's reliability-and-fit duty is extended to the other categories of knowledge expressly covered by original Rule 702; its flexible character remains. Joiner's inferential-connection rule applies to this technical proffer, without converting admissibility into a decision on the underlying claim.

### II. The reliability inquiry must fit the proffer and receives abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding and operative rule:** A trial judge may use Daubert's nonexclusive reliability considerations when they help evaluate the particular expertise, method and application, but need not demand every consideration in every field. Appellate abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable choice of how to assess reliability and the ultimate admission or exclusion, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by the same nine-Justice coalition identified for Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Daubert's considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance. Their usefulness depends on the question and the proposed expertise. A technical practice may yield reliable knowledge without laboratory testing or publication; conversely, the assertion that a method rests on experience does not establish its reliability. The judge must select considerations that examine the method's actual work and explain its connection to the conclusion. Flexibility permits a suitable inquiry, not abandonment of screening. Joiner's allocation of appellate review preserves correction of legal error and deference to permissible evidentiary judgments; an exclusion's effect on summary judgment does not change that allocation. The inquiry concerns preliminary foundation, not proof of the ultimate tort claim. The Court requires no invariant checklist, identical independent study, expert appointment or new hearing, and permits no exclusion merely because the judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's considerations remain useful but nonmandatory tools. Joiner's uniform review rule is applied to technical evidence and to reasonable selection of the reliability inquiry; its protection against unsupported analytical gaps remains intact.

### III-A. The District Court was entitled to reject the unsupported defect inference

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 702 permitted the District Court to reject Carlson's defect inference because he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause. This permissible-exclusion proposition does not itself settle the distinct instruction governing appellate review and summary judgment on remand.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins this bounded application and its unsupported-inference ground; Justice Stevens reserves application for the Court of Appeals. Part III-B's fuller direct-disposition discussion and mandatory conforming remand have a separate coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson's experience was relevant, but experience alone did not establish the connection between his observations and a defect rather than abuse. Assuming that separation caused the blowout did not supply that further inference. Joiner permits the judge to examine the analytical connection without deciding ultimate causation. On this proffer, the District Court was entitled to reject an unsupported bridge between inspection and conclusion. The Court holds rejection permissible, not compulsory for every reasonable judge, and does not discredit visual tire inspection as a category or authorize exclusion merely because a judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's permission to reject an unsupported inference is applied to this technical proffer. Daubert's flexible foundation-and-fit requirement remains distinct from a finding of ultimate causation.

### III-B. The remand must conform to the exclusion and resulting summary judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** On the reconsidered proffer, the case is remanded for a disposition sustaining the exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment, because the record supplies no remaining evidence of defect. The mandate does not require a new hearing, appointed expert or another appellate determination whether to sustain this exclusion.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justice Stevens oppose this conforming mandate and retain their distinct appellate-review remedies.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson proposed to exclude overdeflection unless at least two of four physical signs were significant, then infer a manufacturing or design defect. He acknowledged shoulder wear, a bead groove, discoloration, rim-flange marks and inadequately repaired punctures, and explained why he regarded the symptoms as insufficient. Those explanations did not require acceptance of his significance threshold or the resulting elimination of misuse. After reconsideration, the District Court recognized Daubert's flexibility and possible accepted uses of visual inspection; its remaining objection concerned the analysis connecting this inspection to defect. Thus the initial concern about an inflexible checklist does not describe the ruling under review. Joiner's deferential allocation permits the Court to resolve the supported exclusion now; the Court need not leave that application for another appellate determination. Because no other defect evidence remains on this record, the exclusion carries the stated summary-judgment consequence. This direct disposition does not establish the accident's actual cause.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's abuse-of-discretion allocation is applied to the reconsidered ruling and its supported procedural consequence. Daubert's adaptable inquiry is followed as reflected in reconsideration; neither decision requires rejection of every experience-based method.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Original Rule 702 governs. The Court adopts no later Rule amendment, universal technical checklist or substantive finding that misuse rather than defect caused the accident. The ruling establishes neither automatic admissibility of experiential evidence nor categorical unreliability of visual inspection.

## Precedent Treatment

Daubert supplies flexible foundation and fit and now reaches all knowledge covered by original Rule 702, while its nonexclusive considerations remain adaptable. Joiner supplies uniform abuse-of-discretion review and permission to examine an unsupported analytical connection; both apply to technical expertise. Neither decision becomes a mandatory-factor checklist or a merits causation rule. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Scientific, technical and other specialized testimony receives reliability-and-fit screening suited to the actual proffer. The judge's reasonable screening method and evidentiary ruling receive abuse-of-discretion review, subject to correction of legal error. This particular exclusion and the resulting summary judgment are sustained; experience-based tire analysis as a category remains available when adequately supported.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins Parts I–II and Part III-A's permissible-exclusion proposition and unsupported-inference ground, and concurs in reversal, but dissents from Part III-B's operative remand. Rule 702 addresses the knowledge offered rather than the expert's professional label. Carlson's experience was relevant, but he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause; the District Court was entitled to reject that unsupported analytical bridge. Stone-Zsela nevertheless would direct the Eleventh Circuit to review the reliability ruling for abuse of discretion under the complete Rule 702 framework and reinstate summary judgment if no admissible evidence supports defect and causation. His position preserves flexible reliability screening without mandatory peer review or testing in every technical field, displacing the jury on reliable disputed evidence, or excluding a well-supported conclusion merely because the judge disagrees with it.

Justice Stevens joins Parts I–II but dissents from Part III and the judgment. The common Rule 702 obligation defeats the Eleventh Circuit's categorical exemption, but that court should first apply the correct framework to the complete technical proffer. As in his Joiner position, agreement on appellate review and inferential fit does not settle the actual exercise of discretion. He would vacate and remand for appellate application, without ordering admission, reopening every foundation submission or requiring a new hearing.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit must conform its disposition to the sustained exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment on the record presented. The Court requires no new foundation hearing or expert appointment and makes no finding of the accident's actual cause.

## Event

South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama, No. 97-2045. Merits decision, March 23, 1999. Argued January 19, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Alabama. The judgment under review left franchise-tax assessments in place through its preclusion treatment and disposition of the federal challenge. The questions concern this Court's review of the state judgment, authority to bind taxpayers through earlier litigation, discrimination against foreign corporations, the asserted compensatory tax and the necessary relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Amendment objection is rejected, the preclusion and Commerce Clause judgments are reversed, and the case is remanded for constitutionally adequate state relief.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The same coalition supports each component. Alabama's courts must provide constitutionally adequate relief, choosing among lawful refunds, corrective assessments or other permissible state mechanisms under the conditions stated below. The Court selects no remedial formula, exact refund or assessment against a particular domestic corporation.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–V | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal and remand, 9–0 | Review of the state judgment; nonparty preclusion; discriminatory tax structure; both independent defects in compensation; constitutionally adequate state remedy |

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt each following holding and its qualifications; no separate-rationale aggregation is required.

### I. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar review of Alabama's own judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Amendment does not prevent this Court from reviewing under 28 U.S.C. § 1257 the federal questions decided by Alabama's own courts. Such review is distinct from initiating a new federal damages action against the State.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Opinion of the Court, under the common authority stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Alabama's immunity objection concerns the character of this proceeding. The taxpayers seek review of the federal judgment entered in litigation in Alabama's courts. McKesson recognizes both this Court's authority to review such federal questions and the State's obligation to administer constitutionally adequate relief when required. Treating appellate review as a new action would insulate the state court's decision of federal law from the review Congress authorized. The Court rejects that objection without deciding an unpresented immunity question concerning a separate federal suit or awarding damages against the State.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson supplies the distinction between review of a state judgment and initiation of a federal action; it remains effective on that ground.

### II. Shared taxpayer interests do not authorize binding these nonparties

**Holding and operative rule:** Claim or issue preclusion cannot bind these taxpayers to earlier litigation merely because they share the earlier taxpayers' interests or were aware of the public dispute. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or other legally accountable representation, or another recognized relationship consistent with due process; the asserted common-interest rationale supplies none of those prerequisites.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards requires lawful authority to bind the person before the State may extinguish that person's own claim by another litigant's judgment. The earlier litigation concerned different taxpayers; shared objectives, vigorous advocacy and the public importance of a tax question do not establish representative authority over these taxpayers' personal liabilities. Awareness of another action creates no general obligation to intervene. Describing the proposed bar as issue preclusion rather than claim preclusion does not cure the missing relationship. The Court rejects the relationship asserted below without inventing a factual finding that every conceivable ground of control or representation has been disproved. Ordinary party preclusion, actual control, adequate class representation, fiduciary representation and authorized public representation retain their lawful operation. A prior decision may also supply precedent without conclusively binding a stranger to its judgment. The tax merits therefore require independent consideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Richards's due-process prerequisite is applied to both proposed forms of preclusion; its protection of lawful representative adjudication and ordinary precedent remains intact.

### III. Alabama's franchise-tax bases discriminate against foreign corporations

**Holding and operative rule:** Alabama's franchise-tax structure discriminates against interstate commerce by taxing foreign corporations on actual capital employed in the State while allowing comparable domestic corporations to use a par-value base that may be set well below actual value. The favorable domestic base cannot be justified by comparing nominal rates or tax labels while disregarding the actual competitive burden.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste requires scrutiny of an express geographic tax preference, and West Lynn Creamery requires examination of linked economic operation. Fulton applies those principles to corporate tax classifications. Here, the ability of domestic corporations to employ the favorable par-value base places a materially heavier franchise-tax burden on comparable foreign corporations. Calling both charges franchise taxes does not equalize their bases. Nor does the State's collection of revenue from domestic corporations in other forms establish that the challenged distinction is evenhanded. Tracy's different regulated-service comparison supplies no exemption for this domestic/foreign classification on the comparable-business facts presented. The Court decides this discriminatory structure, not the validity of every regulation with differing economic effects or every tax imposed on corporations with some common product.

**Precedent treatment:** Oregon Waste's origin-discrimination rule, West Lynn Creamery's practical comparison and Fulton's treatment of corporate tax preferences are applied. Tracy's distinct actual-service comparison is distinguished, not displaced.

### IV. The domestic shares tax does not establish a compensatory defense

**Holding and operative rule:** A compensatory-tax defense requires an identified actual intrastate burden, an interstate charge that roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and substantially equivalent taxable events capable of functioning as mutually exclusive proxies. Alabama's domestic shares tax reaches a different event and does not establish the equivalence needed to compensate for the discriminatory foreign-corporation franchise tax.

**Authority:** Part IV of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste and Fulton make these requirements cumulative. A domestic tax does not compensate for a foreign-business disadvantage simply because the two taxes relate economically to corporate activity or raise revenue for the same government. The shares tax concerns a different taxable event from the challenged franchise burden, and Alabama has not established substantially equivalent events serving the claimed compensatory purpose. That failure independently defeats the defense. The Court does not hold that different nominal taxpayers always prevent compensation or prohibit genuinely complementary charges. It requires the substantive equivalence that the asserted defense has not demonstrated here.

**Precedent treatment:** Oregon Waste and Fulton supply the cumulative actual-burden, nonexcess and equivalent-event requirements. Their allowance for genuinely complementary taxes survives; fiscal correlation alone remains insufficient.

**Independently sufficient alternative holding:** Alabama also fails to establish an actual matching domestic burden and a roughly equivalent, nonexcessive interstate burden by invoking other domestic taxes or an aggregate fiscal balance. This separate deficiency defeats the compensatory defense even apart from the absence of equivalent taxable events.

**Authority for the alternative holding:** The same unanimous Part IV coalition.

**Controlling explanation for the alternative holding:** Fulton distinguishes a demonstrated matching burden from a general description of corporate taxation. The present justification does not establish that the domestic burden actually corresponds to the disadvantage imposed on comparable foreign corporations. An assertion that the overall system balances revenue cannot replace that showing. The Court supplies no numerical ratio, new formula or finding that every conceivable tax comparison necessarily fails; it holds the asserted comparison insufficient on this record.

**Precedent treatment for the alternative holding:** Fulton's independently sufficient actual-burden and approximate-equivalence ground is applied without creating a universal calculation.

### V. Alabama must provide adequate relief through lawful state remedial choices

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for constitutionally adequate relief under state law. When payment was required before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, McKesson requires meaningful backward-looking correction; independently, a State may not withdraw a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after payment and substitute an unpursued prepayment route.

**Authority:** Part V of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** McKesson permits a lawful refund, constitutionally permissible retrospective equalization, or a permissible combination when backward-looking relief is required. Reich separately protects the postpayment remedy the State clearly held out, including after voluntary payment; ordinary proof, reasonable previously announced conditions and prospective changes with fair notice remain possible. Fulton preserves those rules while leaving actual payment, preservation, state procedures and severability for lawful state administration. Alabama may choose a remedy within those constraints. Prospective correction may govern future taxation, but cannot by itself replace backward-looking relief when the stated conditions require that relief. A genuinely adequate prepayment system presents a different remedial setting. The Court does not select a replacement tax base, order assessments against particular domestic corporations, fix a refund amount or declare every taxpayer entitled to recovery. Those matters depend on the actual claims and lawful state choices, which must cure rather than preserve the unconstitutional disadvantage.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson's conditional duty of backward-looking correction and Reich's independent no-withdrawal rule govern the remand. Fulton confirms the distinction between invalidating a discriminatory tax and choosing its precise lawful remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exact refund, replacement assessment, severability ruling or particular taxpayer's satisfaction of payment and preservation conditions is decided. Ordinary valid preclusion and genuinely equivalent compensatory taxes remain possible under their own prerequisites. The Court does not reopen otherwise binding judgments or decide a new federal damages action.

## Precedent Treatment

McKesson supplies authority to review the federal questions in a State's own judgment and the conditional duty of backward-looking relief. Richards defeats the asserted nonparty bar while preserving genuine representative and party preclusion. Oregon Waste, West Lynn Creamery and Fulton govern the practical tax comparison; Oregon Waste and Fulton require all three compensatory predicates and preserve genuinely equivalent taxes. Tracy's regulated-service distinction does not control this classification. Reich supplies the independent prohibition against withdrawing a clearly held-out postpayment remedy. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The discriminatory domestic/foreign franchise-tax structure cannot stand on the asserted preclusion or compensation grounds. Federal review and nonparty hearing protection remain separate from tax liability and remedy. Alabama retains lawful remedial choices, but must supply backward-looking relief when its governing conditions apply; prospective correction alone cannot discharge that duty. The Court establishes no automatic refund or replacement tax base.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to Alabama's courts for proceedings consistent with the invalidity of the discriminatory structure and a constitutionally adequate remedy. Payment, preservation, state-law severability where relevant and lawful implementation remain for those proceedings; no particular recovery is fixed here.

## Event

Jones v. United States, No. 97-6203. Merits decision, March 24, 1999. Argued October 5, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's 1997 judgment, 116 F.3d 1487 (table). The question is whether the serious-bodily-injury and death provisions of the governing 18 U.S.C. § 2119 define aggravated offenses or sentencing factors for the judge.

The December 1992 offense is governed by the original firearm-possession enactment: fifteen years for the basic offense, twenty-five years if serious bodily injury results, and up to life if death results. The indictment did not allege serious bodily injury, and the jury's general verdict did not establish it; the judge's injury finding supplied the asserted aggravated sentencing authority. The earlier Oliver appeal supplies the record recitation, not the judgment now reviewed. Death is relevant to the statute's structure, not a finding about this offense's result.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the reviewed judgment insofar as it authorizes the injury-based aggravated sentence, vacates that sentence and remands for lawful proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed in the challenged aggravated-sentencing respect; sentence vacated and remanded, 6–3.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, who would affirm against the presented classification challenge.

On the existing carjacking indictment and verdict, punishment may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum. This is not an order to impose fifteen years, an aggregate-sentence calculation, an acquittal or automatic authorization of a new aggravated prosecution. Any further prosecution must satisfy its own procedural and constitutional requirements. The Court does not reinstate an earlier sentence or decide unrelated sentencing errors.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six Justices directly adopt the statutory construction, its ordinary element protections and the stated remedy. No aggregation of separate constitutional rationales is necessary.

### I. Serious bodily injury and death define aggravated carjacking offenses

**Holding and operative rule:** The version of 18 U.S.C. § 2119 governing the December 1992 offense creates a basic carjacking offense and aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results. Serious bodily injury is an element of the grade carrying a twenty-five-year maximum, and death is an element of the grade carrying a maximum of life imprisonment; the basic offense carries a fifteen-year maximum.

**Authority:** Part I of the six-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the additional injury and death conditions as facts defining aggravated crimes. The numbered clauses prescribe sharply different limits on lawful punishment and use harm to the victim, a traditional distinction among robbery offenses, to select the applicable grade. The Government emphasizes the complete opening description of firearm carjacking, the common punishment command, dependent clauses and penalty headings. Those features support its reading, but they do not establish that the additional facts merely guide sentencing: aggravated offenses may share the basic offense's elements and add the fact that permits greater punishment. Almendarez-Torres supplies a statutory analogue in which placement within a punishment provision did not dispense with an aggravated element. The fairly available graded-offense reading also avoids serious constitutional doubt about assigning to a judge a current-offense fact indispensable to punishment beyond the charged verdict's authority. Gaudin protects the jury's determination of actual elements; neither it nor Almendarez-Torres already establishes a universal rule classifying every sentencing fact. Monge permits renewed proof of its particular noncapital serious-prior qualification for double-jeopardy purposes and expressly reserves independent charging and jury claims. It does not resolve this statute's construction. The Court decides the original enactment, without adding the later intent-to-cause-serious-harm language or death-penalty amendment.

**Precedent treatment:** Almendarez-Torres is applied as a statutory element-and-charging analogue, not as a general constitutional maximum-fact holding. Gaudin supplies the significance of identifying an actual element. Monge's bounded double-jeopardy classification is preserved, including its reservation of independent charging and jury questions. Holloway's attention to enacted offense conditions is consistent with this reading, but its construction of later statutory language is not imported into the earlier offense.

### II. The existing indictment and verdict authorize only the basic carjacking range

**Holding and operative rule:** The aggravated injury or death element must be alleged in the federal indictment; absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission does not itself cure an indictment that omits the aggravated element. Because injury was neither charged nor established by Jones's verdict, the judge's later injury finding cannot authorize the aggravated range; the aggravated sentence must be vacated, and sentencing on the existing carjacking indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum.

**Authority:** Part II of the same six-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Hamling requires the federal charge to contain the offense's elements, and Gaudin places their determination beyond a reasonable doubt with the jury. Almendarez-Torres applies those safeguards to a statutory aggravator and holds that an admission to a fact cannot repair its omission from the charge. Here the indictment and general verdict establish only the basic carjacking offense. No valid admission or waiver supplies the missing aggravated charge and verdict. The judge's injury finding therefore cannot perform their work. Witte and Watts permit relevant-conduct findings to select punishment within authority supplied by the conviction; they do not supply additional statutory authority or remove an actual element from the jury. The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's authorization of the injury-based aggravated sentence, vacates that sentence and remands for lawful proceedings. The fifteen-year ceiling concerns this carjacking count on the existing charge and verdict. It neither fixes the sentence at fifteen years nor determines an aggregate sentence. No acquittal, harmless-error finding or automatic entitlement to another aggravated prosecution is entered; any further prosecution must satisfy its own procedural and constitutional requirements. Monge's completed-insufficiency and renewed-proof question is absent.

**Precedent treatment:** Hamling and Gaudin are applied to the aggravated statutory elements. Almendarez-Torres supplies the distinction between admitting a fact and properly charging the aggravated offense. Witte and Watts retain their ordinary within-authority sentencing operation and provide no basis for this increased statutory range. Monge supplies no independent remedy here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court's ground is statutory construction and the protections of the resulting elements. It does not decide a general constitutional rule for all maximum-increasing facts, bare prior-conviction status, mandatory minima within an unchanged maximum, every Guidelines classification or capital sentencing. McMillan's unchanged-maximum holding and Walton's capital rule are not overruled. The death grade is part of the statute's construction, not a finding that Jones caused death.

## Precedent Treatment

Almendarez-Torres supplies the statutory aggravated-element and charging analogue; its result remains distinct from a universal constitutional rule. Hamling requires a federal indictment stating the offense's elements, and Gaudin requires the jury's determination beyond a reasonable doubt. Monge retains its noncapital serious-prior double-jeopardy classification and its express reservation of independent charging and jury claims; no renewed-proof remedy is drawn from it here. Witte and Watts retain their within-authority sentencing permission and do not authorize punishment beyond the charged conviction's statutory range. Holloway concerns later statutory language and does not amend the law governing this offense. McMillan and Walton are not overruled, and Custis's limits on collateral attack remain undisturbed.

## Law After Decision

The original section 2119 creates aggravated injury and death offenses whose added elements receive ordinary federal charging and jury-proof protections. The existing basic conviction cannot authorize the injury range. The Court adopts no general constitutional maximum-fact rule, prior-conviction exception, new mandatory-minimum rule or capital-sentencing rule. The distinct constitutional positions in the concurrences retain their stated, noncontrolling scope.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, joined by Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg.** These four join the Court's sufficient statutory ground and also conclude that a fact about the commission or consequences of the charged offense, indispensable to a greater statutory maximum than the charged elements and verdict otherwise allow, performs the constitutional function of an element. The federal charging, jury and reasonable-doubt safeguards follow, subject to valid admission and waiver rules; those rules do not repair the omitted charge by implication. Their Monge position treated an additional, previously unestablished personal-conduct qualification as an element for the finality question. They now apply that functional reasoning to injury in the present offense, with Gaudin supplying jury allocation. This is their constitutional conclusion here, not a claim that Monge already decided the jury question. Ordinary factfinding within existing conviction-authorized punishment remains distinct. The concurrence leaves bare prior-conviction status, unchanged-maximum minima, general Guidelines classification and capital sentencing unresolved. Four Justices adopt this independent constitutional ground; it is not the Court's holding.

**Stevens, joined by Scalia in Part I.** Their broader position protects facts that change the legislatively prescribed punishment range, including facts increasing the minimum, and recognizes no categorical exclusion merely because the fact is a prior conviction. Stevens relies on Winship's proof requirement, Mullaney's protection against shifting the burden on a fact determining punishment, his objections to Patterson's treatment of offense definition, and Duncan's protection of jury adjudication. Scalia agrees that the legislature cannot avoid the protection by naming a range-determining fact a sentencing consideration. His narrower Monge element-function ground supports that conclusion without having already established this universal range principle. Neither ordinary relevant-conduct sentencing under Witte and Watts nor an existing conviction's validity is the same question as whether a new fact legally authorizes a different punishment range. A charged prior-status element may be established by adequate admission or other lawful proof without relitigating the conviction's validity; Custis's collateral-review limits remain. These positions do not decide a mandatory-minimum application or overrule McMillan here. In Part II, Stevens alone also places facts necessary for death eligibility within his constitutional principle and favors reconsideration of contrary Walton reasoning in an appropriate case. No capital issue is decided, and Scalia does not join that recommendation.

**Stone-Zsela.** The Chief Justice joins the statutory construction and remedy as sufficient to decide this case. He maintains that a fact increasing authorized punishment must receive the jury protection owed an element, but does not make that broader position an additional constitutional ground of this disposition or join Souter's independent constitutional concurrence. Almendarez-Torres supplies a statutory analogue, not an already controlling universal constitutional rule. His maintained position preserves his view while the Court's decision rests on the enacted aggravated offenses. It supplies no decision of the separate questions concerning prior status, unchanged-maximum minima or capital sentencing.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor and Breyer.** The dissent reads the opening firearm-taking paragraph as a complete offense and the dependent injury and death clauses as graded punishment for its consequences. The sharp increases and the traditional use of injury to grade robbery support the Court's reading, but do not overcome that offense-first structure in the dissent's view. Patterson preserves a bounded legislative role in defining an offense; Gaudin protects the jury's determination once an actual element is identified. The dissent directly defends treating injury during this completed offense as a punishment condition, acknowledging that present victim injury differs from recidivist character. Monge accepted both an unproved personal-conduct qualification and a higher ceiling without treating those features as conclusive of offense status, while leaving the independent jury question open. The dissent therefore does not claim that Monge already resolved this claim or that the qualification was previously proved. Almendarez-Torres's statutory reentry holding remains binding but supplies no general constitutional rule either permitting or forbidding this allocation. McMillan's unchanged-ceiling setting and Witte's and Watts's within-authority sentencing do not themselves resolve the increased maximum. The dissent would sustain the challenged injury-based sentencing authority against this classification objection, without adopting unlimited legislative relabeling, resolving unrelated sentencing errors or directing reinstatement of an earlier sentence.
