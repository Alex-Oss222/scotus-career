#### Henderson v. United States, 517 U.S. 654

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-232.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### The SAA forthwith-service timetable is procedural, not a jurisdictional consent condition

**Holding:** Section 742's direction to serve the filed complaint forthwith regulates procedure in an already authorized Suits in Admiralty Act action; it does not independently condition consent to suit or remove subject-matter jurisdiction. Congress can make procedure a jurisdictional consent condition, but this timetable's placement, function and surrounding provisions establish that it did not do so here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; the same seven Justices adopt all three Henderson holdings. O'Connor and Thomas dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 743's private-party practice direction supports classification without erasing its other prescriptions. Section 745's two-year commencement limit, substantive consent limits and designated service recipients remain. Neither proximity to a waiver nor procedural subject matter alone determines jurisdictional effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Nordic Village retains clear consent to the actual monetary remedy and distinguishes consent from general jurisdiction; it does not classify this service timetable. McNeil's prefiling administrative-exhaustion sequence and Keene's filing-time bar are distinguished from this postfiling event. Carlisle supplies the procedural-authority/jurisdiction distinction without categorically classifying all procedural commands.

##### Congress's later Rule 4 enactment displaces the incompatible service timetable

**Holding:** In a timely commenced domestic SAA action, the applicable Rule 4 timing scheme, including authorized enlargement, governs service; §742 cannot independently require dismissal for failure to serve sooner. Congress's direct enactment of former Rule 4(j) displaces the incompatible procedural timetable without enlarging substantive consent or dispensing with the designated-recipient requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, the same seven Justices.

**Material application:** Former Rule 4(j), directly enacted in Pub. L. 97-462, required dismissal without prejudice for failure to serve within 120 days without good cause, on motion or the court's initiative after notice, except foreign service under former subdivision (i). A separately fatal shorter timetable would deny that scheme's service allowance and authorized enlargement; the possibility of prompt compliance with both texts does not eliminate the conflict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2072 supplies a procedural/substantive framework, not an independent direct-nullification ground through a rule Congress itself enacted. Rule 82's jurisdictional limit is preserved. No deliberate disregard of service responsibilities is licensed; no universal repeal of special service statutes, government/private procedural differences or registered-versus-certified-mail requirement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hanna and Mississippi Publishing Corp. v. Murphree support service's procedural role without replacing the actual-conflict inquiry. Walker and Ragan retain a state-created right's distinct state-law commencement condition; unlike Rule 3 there, the rival provisions here address the same service event.

##### Removing the statutory dismissal does not validate an extension or compel a new service hearing

**Holding:** The statutory dismissal must be reversed as to both designated recipients, with the governing Federal Rules applied to genuinely available, properly presented service questions in further proceedings. Any necessary use of amended Rule 4 must honor its express application to proceedings in cases pending on December 1, 1993 only insofar as just and practicable; this judgment neither validates the existing extension nor orders a new service hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, the same seven Justices.

**Material application:** The action was timely commenced. Attorney General receipt on day 47 and United States Attorney service on day 148 cannot sustain the displaced §742 dismissal. The latter service was outside the original 120 days but within the district court's fifteen-day direction; absence of express good-cause findings neither invalidates that order by itself nor requires renewed factfinding. No distinct Rule 4 ground supported the judgment reviewed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former Rule 4(j) governed the April–September 1993 acts. Amended Rule 4(m), if applicable under the transition, requires dismissal without prejudice or a direction to serve within a specified time after notice when service exceeds 120 days; good cause requires an appropriate extension, while enlargement without good cause is permitted, not compelled. Foreign service under subdivisions (f) and (j)(1) is excepted. A limitations bar may inform discretion but does not require enlargement. No Clarification Act/Public Vessels Act classification is decided. No Rule 12 waiver ground is adopted because the transfer motion's legal effect and appellate preservation are unestablished. Section 745's narrowly dated 1950 savings provision creates no general new refiling opportunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 7–2, for ordinary further proceedings. No new service objection, mandatory good-cause hearing, immediate trial, injury finding, maintenance or cure award, damages judgment or separate due-process ruling follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pioneer preserves flexibility authorized by the particular rule, not general SAA tolling or a substitute good-cause test. Landgraf requires attention to express transition and does not automatically reopen completed acts merely because a case is pending or a provision procedural.


#### Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1263.

**Decided:** December 10, 1996.

##### The erroneous refusal to remand remains error

**Holding:** Complete diversity was absent when Caterpillar removed, and Lewis's timely motion required remand under the applicable removal and remand statutes. The later dismissal of the nondiverse party does not make the earlier ruling correct, waive Lewis's preserved objection, or require him to have sought extraordinary pretrial relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No collateral challenge to an entered covered remand is authorized. No late alternative removal argument or independent fee or sanction entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Things Remembered and Quackenbush retain the mandatory-remand and covered-remand-review rules at their proper stages. This case concerns an erroneous refusal to remand followed by trial, not review of an actually entered remand protected by section 1447(d).

##### Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect

**Holding:** When complete diversity actually arises through the nondiverse party's lawful dismissal before trial and persists through judgment, an earlier erroneous refusal of a timely remand motion does not by itself require vacating the completed federal judgment. The rule depends on actual section 1332 jurisdiction during trial and judgment; consent, finality or efficiency cannot cure a judgment entered without that jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal cure for every removal defect follows. The decision authorizes no collusive manufacture of diversity, finds no manipulation, and does not extinguish a separately authorized fee or sanction request.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Finn's defect persisting through judgment is distinguished from actual diversity before trial; its bar on jurisdiction by consent survives. Grubbs's completed-judgment distinction is expressly extended beyond its unobjected-removal facts to this preserved objection and actual pretrial cure. Newman-Green retains lawful dismissal, dispensability and prejudice limits without authorizing manufactured trial jurisdiction. Willy preserves independently authorized collateral consequences but supplies no merits jurisdiction or cure for an uncured trial.

##### Restoration against jurisdictional vacatur leaves the ordinary appeal for decision

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's jurisdiction-based vacatur and state-court-remand direction are reversed; the district-court judgment is restored against that jurisdictional ground, subject to the unresolved ordinary appeal. The case returns to the Sixth Circuit to decide the five preserved issues it did not reach: prior-accident discovery restrictions, the discovery-sanction request, excluded exhibits, excluded witnesses and the omitted failure-to-warn instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this remand disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not finally affirm the defense verdict against the five remaining challenges or decide any of their merits.


## Standards and Tests selection: Service timing in Suits in Admiralty Act actions

### Service timing in Suits in Admiralty Act actions

**Governing question:** Whether §742 supplies a separate jurisdictional or procedural deadline alongside Rule 4.

**Current rule:** Section 742's forthwith-service timetable is a procedural direction implementing existing consent, not an independent jurisdictional condition. In a timely commenced domestic SAA action, Congress's later enactment of former Rule 4(j) displaces the inconsistent shorter timetable; the applicable Rule 4 timing scheme and authorized enlargement govern without enlarging substantive consent or removing designated-recipient requirements. Former Rule 4(j) required dismissal without prejudice after notice when service exceeded 120 days without good cause, except foreign service under former subdivision (i). Amended Rule 4(m) requires, after notice, dismissal without prejudice or an order for service within a specified time; good cause requires extension, while enlargement without good cause is permitted but not compelled. Foreign service under (f) and (j)(1) is excepted; a limitations bar may support discretion but does not mandate relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Procedural classification, later-enactment displacement and distinct Rule schemes: Henderson — Holdings I–III, May 20, 1996; Scalia for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Henderson, May 20, 1996.

**Effective transition:** Amended Rule 4 took effect December 1, 1993 for new cases and, insofar as just and practicable, proceedings then pending. It does not automatically relabel completed 1993 service acts or validate a particular enlargement.

**Exceptions and limits:** The two-year commencement limit and substantive waiver remain. This is no general rule displacing every service statute and no independently sufficient Court-promulgation ground under §2072; the operative conflict is with Congress's direct enactment.

**Holdings navigation:** Henderson v. United States, 517 U.S. 654.

### Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

**Governing question:** Effect of a preserved removal error when complete diversity actually exists before trial and through judgment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Covered removal grounds and the bar on appellate remand review

### Covered removal grounds and the bar on appellate remand review

**Governing question:** When §§1447(c)–(d) bar review, including a removal also invoking bankruptcy jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** A remand to the originating state court for a timely raised removal-procedure defect or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is not reviewable on appeal or otherwise under §1447(d), even when removal also invokes §1452; a procedural-remand motion must be made within thirty days after removal. The then-applicable exception permits review of a case removed pursuant to §1443. Courts may identify the stated ground but may not review a covered ground's correctness merely because it is alleged erroneous. A Burford abstention remand is outside this bar, but absence of the bar does not itself create an appellate route. Section 1452(b) coexists with the ordinary bar rather than displacing it.

**Authority by component:**

- Covered grounds, §1443 exception and bankruptcy coexistence: Things Remembered — The ordinary covered-remand bar applies in bankruptcy removals, December 5, 1995; Thomas for all nine.
- Abstention-ground classification: Quackenbush — The statutory remand-review bar does not cover this abstention ground, June 3, 1996; unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Things Remembered, December 5, 1995; Quackenbush, June 3, 1996, clarifies the abstention boundary.

**Open question:** No universal rule for all bankruptcy or discretionary state-law remands is adopted; reviewability of Cohill remands and the complete §1452(b) equitable-remand scope remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, 516 U.S. 124; Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706.

#### United States v. Dunnigan, 507 U.S. 87 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1300.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Constitutional permission and the Guideline’s consequence

**Holding:** A sentence increase under U.S.S.G. § 3C1.1 for independently established trial perjury—willfully false testimony under oath or affirmation about a material matter—does not violate the defendant’s right to testify. Once the court properly determines that the defendant committed such perjury, the applicable Guideline requires its two-level obstruction adjustment; neither exercising the trial right nor a guilty verdict alone supplies that predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), applied to the right’s boundary: the right to present testimony includes no entitlement to perjury; the decision does not authorize assuming falsehood from an unsuccessful defense.
- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied: actual willful trial falsehood may inform sentencing; its rehabilitation discussion is not an exclusive justification and its warning against reflex increases remains operative.

##### Perjury predicates, independent findings and application

**Holding:** When a defendant objects to a § 3C1.1 increase based on trial testimony, the court must review the evidence and independently find willfully false sworn testimony concerning a material matter, excluding confusion, mistake and faulty memory. Separate clear findings for each element are preferable, but a single determination encompassing all the factual predicates is sufficient; the district court’s findings here meet that standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied with its safeguard intact: a sentencing response requires an actual assessment of deliberate false testimony, not an automatic consequence of conviction.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), left intact in its factual boundary: absence of a protected right to perjury does not establish that disputed testimony was perjurious.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No separate perjury prosecution or special verbal formula is required. Neither a guilty verdict nor rejection of a truthful defense establishes perjury. No general proof quantum, universal Guidelines-commentary rule or separate actual-obstruction element from summary-contempt law is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical constitutional prohibition and remand; the district court’s independent finding encompasses the required perjury predicates.


#### United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1906.

**Decided:** January 6, 1997.

##### A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings

**Holding:** A general acquittal does not prevent a sentencing court from considering reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance for ordinary Guidelines sentencing within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. That permission does not authorize a new conviction, a separate disguised punishment for the acquitted offense, punishment beyond conviction-based authority, removal of an offense element from the jury, or disregard of independent due-process, evidentiary, Guidelines or Rule 32 requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No special factual acquittal, extraordinary magnification ruling, complete sentencing-element classification, or universal below-statutory-maximum safe harbor is adopted. The Commission's authority to change its policy remains undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dowling and McMillan are applied to the ordinary proof and allocation questions; Witte is extended at its reserved acquitted-conduct boundary only as stated. Gaudin, Roy, Grady and Dixon retain their distinct scopes.

##### Each categorical reversal requires its own lawful sentencing proceedings

**Holding:** Each Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed insofar as it categorically forbids the stated acquitted-conduct consideration, and each case is remanded separately for proceedings under §3661, the applicable Guidelines, Rule 32 and ordinary evidentiary requirements. Permission to consider conduct does not establish the correct sentence, validate an otherwise unsupported finding or require automatic reinstatement of an original term.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The acquittals remain intact. No original sentence, exact range, replacement term, automatic aggregate cap or duplicate-punishment credit is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Koon's distinction between legal permission and lawful sentencing application is preserved; Witte's conditional coordination rule is not enlarged into an automatic remedy.

##### Required clemency procedures do not create an entitlement to mercy or triallike review

**Holding:** Ohio's required investigation, hearing and advisory recommendation do not create a substantive entitlement to clemency because they do not constrain the Governor to grant it on specified facts. The analogy to a first appeal as of right does not itself require appellate representation, triallike evidence procedure or a new determination of guilt in this discretionary executive process.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A further hearing or revised recommendation upon new information is permitted, not mandatory. No right to favorable mercy follows from the required administrative steps.

##### Capital-clemency procedures retain a constitutional minimum, satisfied on these pleadings

**Holding:** An established capital-clemency process must afford minimal safeguards against fundamentally arbitrary procedure, including arbitrary denial of any access or substitution of an entirely arbitrary device such as a coin flip for consideration. The notice and offered interview here, considered with the pleaded restrictions, satisfy that minimum; they establish no entitlement to clemency, trial protections, a favorable recommendation or judicial review of mercy's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion: Part II-A's minimum safeguard has six direct joins: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Limits and questions not reached:** The application accepts the alleged three-day interview notice, ten-day hearing notice, counsel restrictions, preparation difficulty and inability to testify or submit documents at the hearing. It assumes no additional document channel. The rule fixes no universal procedural checklist and leaves independent constitutional wrongs distinct.

##### The optional interview does not establish compelled self-incrimination

**Holding:** An optional clemency interview does not compel self-incrimination merely because the prisoner must choose between providing potentially harmful information and remaining silent; absence of counsel, confidentiality or immunity does not itself convert this invitation into compulsion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part III-A, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Independent alternative holding:** The conclusion also holds if an ordinary civil adverse inference from silence is assumed, but it does not authorize an automatic privilege penalty, compelled waiver or criminal use of compelled answers.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O’Connor’s Part III-B, joined by all seven other Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ohio’s actual use of silence or answers is not found. A genuine threat of an automatic privilege penalty, compulsory waiver or criminal use of compelled answers requires its own analysis.

## Standards and Tests selection: Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

### Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

**Governing question:** Enforceability of a plea-discussion agreement permitting impeachment of the defendant’s own inconsistent testimony.

**Current rule:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that statements may impeach the defendant’s own inconsistent trial testimony. Absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, that limited waiver is enforceable. Supported ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness and agreement-scope objections receive individual review. Inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority by component:**

- A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable: A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable, 1995-01-18. Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-18; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Then-Rule 410 and parallel Rule 11(e)(6) protect against use against the participating defendant in civil or criminal proceedings of withdrawn guilty pleas, no-contest pleas, related Rule 11 or comparable state-proceeding statements, and prosecutor plea discussions resulting in no guilty plea or a withdrawn plea. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion already introduced and fairness requiring contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires oath, an on-record statement and counsel’s presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. The waiver holding decides no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing or other-person-testimony use, no independent perjury-use term and no universal waiver of fundamental protections. Independent constitutional and evidentiary limits remain; the concurrence’s additional Government-proof requirement does not control.

**Holdings navigation:** A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable.

### Accepted guilty pleas and deferred plea-agreement approval

**Governing question:** Presentence withdrawal after acceptance of a plea but before approval of its agreement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Notice of statutory elements and collateral challenges to guilty pleas

### Notice of statutory elements and collateral challenges to guilty pleas

**Governing question:** When may a prisoner challenge a guilty plea based on a mistaken description of the statutory offense?

**Current rule:** A plea requires real notice of the essential charge; a materially mistaken description of an element may make it unintelligent. A statutory construction defining criminal conduct is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague. Failure to raise plea intelligence on direct review defaults the claim. ## Holdings
### 1. Notice of the seizure does not require an individualized guide to published return procedures

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process requires timely notice reasonably calculated to identify the seized property and its responsible custodian so that an owner may seek its return. When the owner receives that notice, due process does not additionally require personalized instructions describing adequate state return procedures reasonably ascertainable from generally available statutes and judicial decisions; publication alone does not establish meaningful access to an inadequate, concealed, misleading, obstructed or practically inaccessible remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other members of the seven-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They adopt both holdings and the procedural-due-process explanation. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment and the negative rule rejecting the additional legal-instruction duty, but withhold assent to deriving notice duties for criminal warrant execution from civil procedural-due-process decisions. The seven complete joins independently establish controlling authority; no fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that notice must inform the owner what the government has done and where to seek the property. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. supplies that practical notice principle. It does not require an official to provide a personal explanation of the law an owner may use. Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft involved an operative utility-dispute procedure that a customer could not discover simply by consulting generally available law. California's judicial return procedures present a different question. Mathews v. Eldridge requires attention to the actual interests and procedures; the modest expense of adding instructions to a receipt does not alone make those instructions constitutionally necessary. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property preserves an independent due-process inquiry despite a warrant, but concerned process before civil seizure of real property. It neither settles notice after criminal seizure of movable evidence nor requires a guide to judicial remedies. The receipt here identified the property, agency, issuing judge and court, relevant dates and officer contacts. The Court rejects the additional legal-guide requirement without independently adjudicating every aspect of California's procedures or their operation. Withholding information actually necessary to invoke a remedy, or misleading or obstructing the owner, would present a different properly preserved claim.

**Limits:** The holding does not validate an unlawful initial seizure, unreasonable retention, a failure to disclose the seizure or custodian, or an inaccessible return route. It adopts no comprehensive notice checklist, universal deadline or rule that a warrant satisfies every later process obligation. California Penal Code §1536's custody subject to court order, the nonstatutory return motion recognized in People v. Icenogle, and §1540's separate restoration conditions remain distinct; this case does not merge them or decide that every owner satisfies any one route.

### 2. The additional-notice judgment must be reversed without deciding an unproved obstruction claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's requirement of individualized instructions about published return procedures, and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground, must be reversed. The omitted warrant number does not sustain that judgment because the District Court found that respondents had not established the number was necessary to bring a return motion; the reported difficulties do not establish a different obstruction ground.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling explanation. Scalia and Thomas also support this application and its reservations through their concurrence in the judgment; all nine support reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit held the available California procedures constitutionally adequate but demanded additional instruction about using them. This Court rejects that legal demand; it does not independently endorse every procedure or its practical administration. Perkins reported difficulty finding the warrant, the issuing judge's absence, a mistaken number and a sealed warrant. Some officer statements were disputed. A public index connected the searched address to the warrant number, and the District Court's unrefuted finding was that necessity of that number had not been established. These circumstances do not permit the Court to replace the lower finding with a determination that officials withheld an indispensable identifier. Nor does rejection of the instruction theory extinguish a distinct, properly preserved challenge to actual obstruction or accessibility. Such a claim must rest on its own record and procedural basis. Return of most property in June 1994 does not itself dispose of a supported claim concerning an earlier deprivation. The judgment on individual qualified immunity is not reopened, and the Court adjudicates neither entitlement to the reportedly stolen starter pistol nor damages.

**Remand limit:** The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any preserved adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation issue remains subject to ordinary procedural and substantive requirements; no new claim, fresh hearing or exploratory discovery is ordered.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306:** Applied to notice of governmental action that permits a meaningful response; it does not impose a general duty to teach published law.
- **Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft, 436 U.S. 1:** Distinguished because notice of an operative utility-dispute procedure not reasonably discoverable in public legal sources differs from instruction in published judicial remedies. Its protection of meaningful access survives.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applied to the actual notice function and available procedures; low administrative cost alone does not establish an added constitutional duty.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property (December 13, 1993):** Its independent due-process protection is preserved. Its civil real-property, pre-seizure rule is distinguished from notice following criminal seizure of movable property; no warrant-based displacement of all due process is adopted.
- **People v. Icenogle, 164 Cal. App. 3d 620:** Its recognition of a nonstatutory return motion identifies an available state-law route. The Court does not convert that route into §1540's different statutory restoration conditions or decide a particular owner's entitlement.

## Law After Decision
An owner with timely notice of the seizure and custodian has no federal entitlement to personalized instruction about adequate, reasonably ascertainable published return procedures. Meaningful notice, actual access and a distinct supported obstruction claim remain separate questions. The ruling changes the Ninth Circuit's instruction requirement while leaving Good's civil real-property protection and California's separate return routes intact. It resolves no independent immunity, warrant-validity, damages or title question.

## Separate Writings
**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** The concurrence agrees that officials need not give personalized instruction about generally available return law and that the missing warrant number does not sustain the judgment on this record. It withholds assent to the Court's use of civil procedural-due-process decisions as the source of notice requirements when officers execute a criminal search warrant. Mullane's notice setting and Good's civil real-property seizure do not, without further analysis, establish the duties attending execution of a criminal warrant. That source objection does not require approval of secrecy or obstruction, a finding that the Fourth Amendment affirmatively commands a particular inventory, or a holding that it displaces every later due-process protection. The concurrence leaves the constitutional source, timing and detailed content of any separate notice obligation unresolved and joins the limited reversal and remand.

## Procedure After Action
The case returns to the lower courts with directions to reject the additional individualized-instruction theory. Any properly preserved distinct claim remains governed by its own procedural posture and proof. The decision does not establish obstruction, entitlement to the reported stolen pistol, a damages award or invalidity of the original seizure.

The reported difficulties remain distinct from the District Court's finding that necessity of the warrant number had not been established. The opinion adjudicates no independent claim that requires resolving those factual disputes.

## Holdings

### Loss of at-will employment may be the injury caused by a forbidden federal-witness conspiracy

**Operative rule:** When two or more persons conspire to deter a party or witness by force, intimidation or threat from attending federal court or testifying there freely, fully and truthfully, or to injure a party or witness for having attended or testified, loss of at-will employment and wages caused by acts furthering that conspiracy may constitute injury to person or property under §1985's civil-remedy clause without a constitutional entitlement to continued employment. Haddle's allegations of concrete employment loss cannot be dismissed merely because the employment was terminable at will; the prohibited conspiracy and object, required federal-witness nexus, acts, causation, recoverable damages and properly presented defenses remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this proposition, its application to the categorical ground of dismissal and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute protects federal judicial proceedings from the specified conspiratorial intimidation and retaliation. Its civil-remedy language distinguishes injury to person or property from deprivation of a federal right or privilege. Requiring a separate constitutional employment entitlement for the injury branch would collapse that distinction. Kush v. Rutledge explains why the federal-process clauses must be read on their own terms rather than importing the equal-protection-purpose conditions of different clauses; Kush did not itself decide this damages question.

At-will status defines the employer's ordinary authority to terminate the relationship. It does not make the employee's existing work and wages valueless against prohibited interference. Truax v. Raich recognizes that distinction. Roth's constitutional-property rule concerns an entitlement protected against deprivation without due process, a different inquiry from injury caused by this statutory conspiracy. Darden's attention to common-law context supports reading the employment interest in its actual legal setting, without importing its separate employee-classification factors.

Respondents' argument correctly denies guaranteed employment but draws the wrong consequence for the alleged economic harm. Bray requires attention to the particular §1985 clause and does not make its distinct private-right requirements govern this federal-witness injury branch. Robinson and Inter-Modal likewise distinguish protection against conduct forbidden by a statute from a permanent right to the underlying job or benefit. Haddle alleges lost employment and wages from the forbidden object, which defeats this categorical pleading objection.

The Court does not find that the alleged conspiracy occurred or that these corporate agents are legally independent third parties capable of conspiring. It decides neither every witness-definition question nor damages amount, reinstatement or punitive relief. The statutory object, causation and other applicable requirements prevent this holding from becoming a federal remedy for every discharge.

### Albright v. Oliver, No. 92-833

Merits decision, January 24, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

510 U.S. 266. Argued October 12, 1993. On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 975 F.2d 343, affirming Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of a §1983 action. The question is whether Albright's asserted freedom from prosecution without probable cause is enforceable as substantive due process on the claim presented. Albright surrendered on a warrant, posted bond and needed permission to leave Illinois. After a preliminary hearing bound him over, the charge was dismissed because it stated no offense under Illinois law. Alleged deliberate misuse and informant unreliability are allegations, not findings.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

#### Judgment

The Seventh Circuit judgment is affirmed, 7–2. The presented substantive-due-process claim remains dismissed.

The state malicious-prosecution count's dismissal without prejudice is not disturbed. No Fourth Amendment claim is reinstated, and no authorization to relitigate a different claim is given.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Presented substantive-due-process claim

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Dismissal of Albright's presented substantive-due-process claim is affirmed, 7–2. O'Connor's specific-source plurality has four votes: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Ginsburg. Kennedy and Thomas adopt a distinct prosecution/due-process analysis; Souter adopts a distinct absence-of-additional-substantial-injury ground. The judgment binds the parties, but neither a general Fourth Amendment displacement rule nor a general state-remedy bar becomes law.

**Explanation of authority and limits:** The Court affirms the dismissal, but no opinion supplies five votes for a sufficient legal explanation. The plurality applies Graham's specific-source approach to the alleged seizure-related liberty burdens while preserving Soldal and James Daniel Good's independent protections. Kennedy and Thomas distinguish arrest from prosecution and consider the nature of the asserted due-process interest, including the conditional significance of Illinois's remedy for unauthorized malicious prosecution. Souter asks whether the complaint supplies a substantial deprivation outside the protection ordinarily afforded by the Fourth Amendment. These inquiries are not interchangeable. A claim involving an additional substantial injury, a properly presented procedural defect, or an established substantive right could divide those approaches differently. Marks does not permit adding their votes to announce the plurality's rule, the state-remedy rationale, or a new synthesized test. The Court does not adopt the Seventh Circuit's requirement of incarceration, employment loss or another palpable consequence. Nor does it decide the abandoned Fourth Amendment theory, its accrual, immunity, municipal attribution or a general fabrication claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Marks v. United States supplies the governing fractured-decision inquiry; no rationale here is established as a controlling logical subset. Graham, Collins, Soldal, James Daniel Good, Williams, Medina, Costello, Gerstein, Paul, Parratt and Hudson retain their existing force; the uses of those decisions in the separate rationales do not establish a new majority construction.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No majority adopts a new construction of the cited constitutional or remedial precedents. Graham's specific-source rule and Soldal and James Daniel Good's independent-protections rules retain their existing scope. Williams's confined federal supervisory duty and dismissal authority remain distinct from this state constitutional damages claim. Medina supplies no exclusive criminal due-process methodology. Parratt and Hudson remain limited procedural-adequacy authorities, not a general exhaustion requirement or defense to an established substantive violation.

The judgment disposes of this substantive-due-process claim without producing a controlling general test for constitutional malicious prosecution. The plurality, Kennedy/Thomas opinion and Souter opinion remain distinct noncontrolling rationales. The Court leaves the abandoned Fourth Amendment claim, procedural-due-process and interstate-travel questions, accrual, immunity, municipal liability and deliberate-fabrication boundaries undecided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

O'Connor's plurality, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Ginsburg, applies Graham's specific-source approach and Collins's requirement of an identified constitutional wrong. The arrest and pretrial restraints belong under the appropriate specific protection; they do not establish the asserted freestanding substantive guarantee. The plurality rejects the lower court's palpable-consequence requirement and preserves independently applicable guarantees under Soldal and James Daniel Good. Gerstein distinguishes restraint from the decision to prosecute. Williams's federal supervisory protection does not establish the state constitutional tort, but prevents a categorical denial of all judicial scrutiny of charging misconduct.

Scalia concurs separately and joins the plurality. He emphasizes the need to identify a constitutional guarantee rather than convert common-law malicious prosecution into a new substantive right. Graham supplies the specific-source approach; his additional reasoning does not displace existing independent protections or decide a procedural claim not presented.

Ginsburg concurs separately and joins the plurality. Release on bond need not end all constitutional restraint: required court appearances and travel permission can continue governmental control. That reservation explains why the Fourth Amendment remains a meaningful source; it does not decide the abandoned claim, accrual, preliminary-hearing liability or immunity.

Kennedy concurs in the judgment with Thomas. They distinguish an arrest claim governed by Graham from initiation of prosecution and find no independent substantive guarantee of the kind asserted here. Gerstein and Costello preserve the distinction between restraint and lawful charging procedures; Williams's actual federal supervisory protection remains separate. Alternatively, assuming an ordinary due-process interest against unauthorized malicious prosecution, Illinois's adequate tort remedy answers the random-unauthorized-deprivation concern under Parratt and Hudson. This is neither exhaustion nor a rule curing an established substantive or Fourth Amendment violation. They leave a materially different absence of adequate redress open and do not join the plurality.

Souter concurs only in the judgment. Soldal and James Daniel Good preserve overlapping constitutional protection, but Collins counsels restraint where no additional substantial deprivation has been alleged. He finds no substantial injury here attributable to mere prosecution outside the protection ordinarily afforded for the ensuing seizure. He does not treat the harms as trivial, decide the Fourth Amendment claim or rule out an otherwise unprotected substantial injury in another case.

Stevens dissents with Blackmun. They would allow development of the asserted deliberate, arbitrary prosecution claim. Hurtado protects substance beyond procedural form; Winship and Mooney show why meaningful proof and freedom from deliberate deception matter. Collins's workplace omission differs from affirmative abuse, and Soldal and James Daniel Good preserve independent rights. Williams reinforces scrutiny of deliberate distortion without itself supplying a state damages cause. They reject the view that a potential Fourth Amendment remedy eliminates an independently protected injury, and regard Parratt as insufficient to defeat an established substantive violation; Zinermon also limits treating delegated official conduct as necessarily random and unauthorized. Their proposed reversal would leave causation, culpability, immunity and municipal attribution unresolved.
