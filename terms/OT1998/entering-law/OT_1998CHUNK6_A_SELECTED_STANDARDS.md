## Standards and Tests selection: Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications

### Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications

**Governing question:** What instructions govern rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications?

**Current rule:** For an economic classification involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right under the question reviewed, a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis suffices and the challenger must negate it. Formal legislative findings are unnecessary. Conceivable differences in bargaining arrangements, regulatory costs or competition can justify a line without proof that every regulated entity shares a monopoly. Moreno and Cleburne retain their prohibition of irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications; this rule supplies no resolution of a distinct heightened-scrutiny or First Amendment claim.

**Authority by component:**

Thomas’s Parts I and II-A; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Competitive entry independently supports the ownership distinction: FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc. — Competitive entry independently supports the ownership distinction, 1993-06-01. Thomas’s Part II-B; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-01; FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., 508 U.S. 307 (1993); FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., 508 U.S. 307 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

### Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern equal protection of commitment proof and family participation?

**Current rule:** Kentucky's clear-and-convincing burden for adult intellectual-disability commitment, compared with reasonable-doubt proof for mental illness, fails rational-basis review because the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not justify the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Lawful equalization and severability must be addressed; Addington's federal minimum is unchanged and no national reasonable-doubt requirement or automatic release follows. Separately, continuing family knowledge rationally supports guardians' and immediate relatives' party participation for lifelong developmental disability. Such participation is not categorically unfair where the adult has counsel and effective opportunity to contest evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives cannot replace statutory grounds, control decision or suppress the adult's position. Heightened scrutiny remains undecided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

### Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

**Governing question:** What instructions govern scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702?

**Current rule:** Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern scientific expert evidence under the federal Rules. Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance a preliminary foundation of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the disputed factual question and assisting the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application flexibly. Relevant nonexclusive considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance; none is an invariant prerequisite. Acceptance alone does not establish admissibility, and its absence alone does not require exclusion. Admissibility does not determine causation or sufficiency. The judge may examine the reliable inferential connection from the scientific materials to the offered conclusion; credentials or the label weight of evidence cannot replace that connection. No identical-study condition, automatic exclusion of animal or nonsignificant studies, or requirement that each study independently prove the conclusion follows; supported cumulative inference remains possible. Admission and exclusion both receive abuse-of-discretion appellate review, with independent correction of legal error and proper protection for historical findings. An exclusion’s decisive effect on summary judgment does not alter that standard. No later Rule text or universal technical-expertise rule is adopted.

**Authority by component:**

- The enacted Rules displace mandatory general acceptance: Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals — The enacted Rules displace mandatory general acceptance, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Reliable scientific foundation and fit require flexible preliminary screening: Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals — Reliable scientific foundation and fit require flexible preliminary screening, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

- Admission and exclusion review; reliable inferential connection: General Electric Co. v. Joiner, 1997-12-15; Kennedy Parts I–II for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Joiner, 1997-12-15, adds uniform appellate review and clarifies inferential fit; Daubert’s foundational components remain effective 1993-06-28.

**Holdings navigation:** Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993). General Electric Co. v. Joiner — Scientific expert evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

## Standards and Tests selection: Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

### Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

**Governing question:** When a state or local economic measure must satisfy the discrimination rule rather than ordinary incidental-burden balancing.

**Current rule:** A measure discriminates when it favors local economic interests by burdening interstate competitors, including a waste charge expressly keyed to out-of-state origin, compulsory routing that reserves a processing service to a designated local private operator, or an integrated assessment-and-distribution scheme that collects from the whole market but returns the proceeds only to local producers. The entire linked operation matters; formally equal collection does not cleanse its discriminatory distribution. The government must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Ordinary incidental-burden balancing does not validate discrimination. Conservation, sanitation, cost recovery and financing a favored private facility do not themselves meet that burden; contracting with a private operator does not make compulsory market regulation governmental market participation. A municipality that actually purchases collection and disposal may specify the destination as part of that purchased performance, including through a paid hauler and privately owned facility. Separate suppliers or prices do not themselves turn purchased disposal into unrelated downstream business. This bounded purchasing authority does not reach independent transactions merely because government has a contract or financial stake. In comparing regulated distribution businesses with independent commodity sellers, actual compulsory rate, reliability and public-service obligations may distinguish the offerings. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence where a separate sales category free of those obligations has not been shown. Neither a utility label nor equal taxation of local outsiders suffices by itself; no utility immunity or new public-service exception to established discrimination follows. Public ownership of a disposal facility alone does not establish governmental purchase of independent generators’ or haulers’ transactions compelled by routing or burdened by a fee. A claimed public-service distinction must therefore be assessed separately from the purchased-performance protection.

- Private processing monopoly and neutral alternatives: C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown — Compulsory use of this local private processor discriminates in the service market; Sanitary service and financing do not justify this discriminatory route, 1994-05-16. Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the discrimination classification and its stated private-service boundary. O'Connor agrees only with the judgment on a distinct Pike ground. Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this application of the discrimination rule. - Integrated levy, local distribution and justification: West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

### Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

**Governing question:** When an otherwise discriminatory tax can compensate for a tax already borne by the corresponding intrastate activity.

**Current rule:** The State must identify the actual intrastate burden for which it compensates, show that the interstate charge roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and establish that the taxed events are substantially equivalent, reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies for one another. General income taxation and waste disposal are not such equivalent events. For a use tax said to equalize local sales taxes, the comparison must use the sales tax actually imposed in the affected locality. Statewide averages and burdens elsewhere cannot cure an excess there. Equal or lower use-tax burdens in other localities are not invalidated merely because an excess exists elsewhere; relief addresses the established inequality through lawful remedial procedures. A resident shareholder’s location and asserted substantial equality with a separate corporate-level tax supply no exception: an issuer-based tax preference favoring local corporate activity must satisfy the same actual-burden and equivalent-event requirements. Typical price/earnings ratios or aggregate corporate contributions do not themselves establish the matching burden.

**Authority by component:**

- Three compensatory-tax predicates: Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality — General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax, 1994-04-04. Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.
- Local comparison, bounded invalidity and relief: Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman — The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality; Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures, 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Blackmun agrees with the judgment without joining this full framework.
- Fulton — holdings 1–4, investment discrimination and rejection of Darnell’s shortcut, 1996-02-21; Thomas for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Fulton, February 21, 1996, removing the contrary Darnell permission; the earlier compensatory predicates survive.

**Holdings navigation:** General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax; The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality; Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures; Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner, 516 U.S. 325.

**Material development:** Darnell is overruled only insofar as it permits discriminatory taxation of residents’ out-of-state shares merely by reference to a different corporate-level local tax and asserted substantial equality. Its independent equal-protection and standing determinations remain.

## Standards and Tests selection: Backward-looking relief for unconstitutional taxes

### Backward-looking relief for unconstitutional taxes

**Governing question:** What instructions govern backward-looking relief for unconstitutional taxes?

**Current rule:** Applying a federal tax rule retroactively does not alone determine remedy. When the State required payment before adequate opportunity to contest, due process requires meaningful backward-looking relief; lawful refunds, retroactive nondiscriminatory assessments or a combination may suffice. Meaningful prepayment review presents a different remedial setting. State law and due process govern the correction; no automatic refund of every payment follows. Even if an adequate prepayment route existed, a State that held out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, may not withdraw that remedy after payment and rely on failure to use the other route. It must afford a clear and certain opportunity for a refund or constitutionally adequate equivalent relief, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount. A prospective adequate prepayment system remains possible with fair notice.

**Authority by component:**

- Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate: Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation — Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate, 1993-06-18. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

- A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment: A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment, 1994-12-06. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct votes for the bounded procedural rule and remedy.

**Present operation effective:** Reich v. Collins, December 6, 1994, for the after-payment withdrawal limit; Harper’s distinct remedial-choice rules remain.

**Holdings navigation:** Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993); A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum

### Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum

**Governing question:** When does 8 U.S.C. §1326(b)(2) authorize its higher unlawful-reentry maximum?

**Current rule:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction is an element of the aggravated offense and must appear in the federal indictment. Unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense, it must be proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. A factual admission to a prior conviction does not cure omission of that element from the indictment; a conviction charged only under subsection (a) authorizes no sentence above its two-year maximum.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory element construction and ordinary indictment/jury consequences; five direct joins: Almendarez-Torres v. United States, No. 96-6839 — Section 1326(b)(2) requires an aggravated-offense allegation before its higher maximum may apply, 1998-03-24.

**Present operation effective:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States, 1998-03-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** No majority adopts a general constitutional rule covering every fact that increases a maximum. Findings selecting punishment within existing conviction-based authority remain a distinct inquiry.

**Holdings navigation:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States — Aggravated reentry and the charged offense.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution

### Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution

**Governing question:** Whether use of uncharged conduct to select a sentence constitutes prior prosecution or punishment for that conduct.

**Current rule:** Considering relevant uncharged conduct to choose a sentence within the authorized statutory range for the offense of conviction is not itself prosecution or independent punishment for the uncharged offense. That sentencing use therefore does not alone bar a later distinct prosecution under the retained Grady rule. A disguised separate penalty, punishment beyond statutory authority and independent sentencing safeguards remain outside this permission. A general acquittal also does not categorically prohibit reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance in ordinary Guidelines sentencing within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. That use supplies no new conviction, disguised separate acquitted-offense punishment, excess punishment or removal of an actual offense element from the jury. Independent due-process, evidentiary, Guidelines and Rule 32 requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here: Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here, 1995-06-14. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, constitutional-characterization discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this rationale. Scalia and Thomas affirm only in the judgment on the narrower distinct-prosecution ground. 
- General acquittal and ordinary conviction-based sentencing: United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra — United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra — I. A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings, January 6, 1997; per curiam opinion joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This resolves Witte’s acquitted-conduct reservation only at that scope.

**Present operation effective:** Watts and Putra, January 6, 1997, for the ordinary acquitted-conduct rule; Witte’s uncharged-conduct and successive-prosecution boundaries survive.

**Related current rules:** Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt retains the governing successive-prosecution law. Conditional coordination of undischarged sentences addresses a later sentence separately; permission to prosecute orders no conviction or punishment.

**Holdings navigation:** Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here. United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra (1997-01-06) — A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings.



**Open question:** Extraordinary sentence magnification remains unresolved. Neither mandatory Guidelines alone nor a below-maximum sentence establishes a universal element-classification rule. The Commission’s power to change the acquitted-conduct policy remains unresolved; separate opinions do not settle it.

## Standards and Tests selection: Regulation following statutory acquisition of treaty lands

### Regulation following statutory acquisition of treaty lands

**Governing question:** What instructions govern regulation following statutory acquisition of treaty lands?

**Current rule:** On the former Cheyenne River trust lands acquired under the Flood Control and Cheyenne River Acts, displacement of exclusive occupation also displaces non-Indian hunting-and-fishing regulation derived from exclusion. Retained tribal access and personal hunting-and-fishing privileges alone do not preserve licensing authority over non-Indian public users. Residual Montana authority remains separately available if established: consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or arrangements with the Tribe or members, or conduct threatening or directly affecting political integrity, economic security, health or welfare, within Montana's limits. Proper federal authorization remains possible; general inherent sovereignty alone does not restore the displaced power. No general state exclusivity or reservation-boundary ruling follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. When the complete access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate interests, including primary finality and prevention of evasive candidacies or party raiding. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**
- Governing inherited framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden”, June 8, 1992; White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas (five), applying Anderson and the earlier simulated Norman rule.
- Limited-burden write-in presumption: Burdick, “2. Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid in its reasonably accessible system,” June 8, 1992; same five Justices.
- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the write-in presumption and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute validation of all write-in bans follows. Materially more restrictive access systems may impose severe burdens. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

**Holdings navigation:** Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

### Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

**Governing question:** Whether a federal tribal-property claim may proceed against responsible state officers despite disputed title or sovereign interests.

**Current rule:** An alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights may support prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officers actually connected to it. A disputed federal title question or important sovereign interest does not alone bar the claim. Relief must govern proved unlawful future official conduct; it may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or order the State itself to convey title. A general obligation to obey law does not supply the required officer connection.

**Authority by component:**
- Officer route and real remedial boundary: Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho — An ongoing federal property-right claim may proceed against responsible officers within real remedial limits, Stone-Zsela's five-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** State-law commands remain barred by Pennhurst. The rule decides neither title nor every requested remedy, and supplies no sovereign-lands exception or general balancing of state interests. The distinct IGRA officer route does not supply procedures or a general State-amenability ruling for this claim.

**Holdings navigation:** Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prospective officer suits and disputed tribal property rights.
