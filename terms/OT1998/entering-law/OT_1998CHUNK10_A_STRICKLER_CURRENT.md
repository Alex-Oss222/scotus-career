## Current-term law

### O'Sullivan v. Boerckel; No. 97-2048; Seventh Circuit judgment, 135 F.3d 1194. — 1999-06-07

#### Holdings

###### I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the pre-AEDPA law governing this application, a prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate process, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. Former section 2254(b) preserves exceptions for absence of a state corrective process or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights; actual state-law unavailability must be respected, and the rule does not require every extraordinary remedy or repetitive collateral presentation after a complete ordinary round.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, commands six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh makes former chapter 153 law govern Boerckel's pre-enactment application. Former section 2254(c) turns on a state-law right to raise the question through an available procedure, not a right to obtain a merits decision. Illinois Rule 315 supplies a regular petition for leave to appeal. Its selective criteria—importance, conflict, supervisory need and finality—allow the Illinois Supreme Court to choose cases; they do not make the ordinary filing unavailable. The State's highest court must have its own opportunity to consider each federal claim. The contrary argument stresses the burden of repeating rejected claims in a selective system. But selection belongs to the state court, and the ordinary petition gives it the choice. Picard supplies fair presentation; it does not permit omission of an otherwise required ordinary step. Castille involved a claim first raised through discretionary review and therefore does not mechanically answer whether an already presented claim must pass through the next ordinary stage. Lonchar requires the governing procedural sources, not a generalized finality preference. Here Illinois supplied the ordinary leave procedure and no established directive excluded it from the process. Mere discretion and unlikely acceptance do not establish unavailability. Petitioning this Court is not a substitute for giving the State's own highest court the opportunity, and no equivalent federal-certiorari exhaustion requirement follows.

###### II. Expiration of the omitted opportunity produces default, with existing gateways preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** When a prisoner omits a claim from an otherwise required ordinary state-review step and the opportunity to use that step has expired, the claim is procedurally defaulted rather than awaiting exhaustion through a still-available remedy. Boerckel's three omitted claims fall within that rule, subject to recognized cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways at their existing scope; further proceedings must respect the actual prior gateway rulings and the governing pre-AEDPA law.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts the classification and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Coleman distinguishes failure to exhaust an available remedy from default when a required remedy has closed. The three claims were omitted from an ordinary petition and their opportunity has expired. The Seventh Circuit's contrary premise therefore cannot support further consideration without addressing default. Gray and Lambrix preserve claim-specific procedural treatment; a ruling on these claims does not decide all claims in the application. Nor does default eliminate the recognized gateways. The district court invited a cause showing, and Boerckel instead pursued actual innocence. It heard evidence in July 1996, incorrectly invoked AEDPA to exclude it, and alternatively concluded that evidence of other participants did not establish Boerckel's absence. Because the court of appeals found no default, it did not resolve any properly preserved challenge to that gateway treatment. Such review must apply pre-AEDPA law and consider the significance of the alternative ground. The Court makes no new finding of innocence, credibility, cause, prejudice or forfeiture, changes no gateway burden and orders no second hearing. The remand permits only further proceedings consistent with these existing procedural conditions; the constitutional merits remain undecided.

###### III. The effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review remains open

**Reservation and operative limit:** The Court does not decide whether a State may expressly identify an occasionally available discretionary procedure as outside its standard review process and state that prisoners need not invoke it before federal habeas. No such Illinois directive is established here. The decision neither requires a State to eliminate every possibility of occasional discretionary relief nor conclusively defines what an effective express opt-out must contain.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It is a five-Justice reservation, not an adopted rule resolving the hypothetical procedure's availability. Stone-Zsela does not join this reservation.

**Explanation:** Ordinary selective review and a State's express definition of its standard process present different questions. The former is established here; the latter is not. Treating actual state-law unavailability as controlling therefore does not require a decision about a procedure that a State retains for occasional use while expressly excluding it from the standard exhaustion route. The Court leaves that question open without displacing the complete-round rule for Illinois's existing ordinary procedure.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lindh supplies pre-AEDPA chapter 153 for this application; the decision does not import amended exhaustion or evidentiary provisions merely because it is delivered after AEDPA.
- Picard's fair-presentation requirement is applied to each claim through the ordinary state-review round.
- Castille is distinguished: first presentation through a discretionary mechanism differs from omission at the next ordinary stage after proper presentation below. Its proper-presentation rule remains intact.
- Coleman supplies the distinction between an available unexhausted remedy and procedural default after an otherwise required route closes; it does not independently identify Illinois's required sequence.
- Lonchar preserves source-governed habeas procedure and does not itself decide which Illinois steps constitute exhaustion.
- Gray preserves separate treatment of distinct claims and defaults; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.
- Lambrix preserves default as an independent procedural issue with its recognized gateway requirements; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.

#### Law After Decision

Illinois's ordinary petition for leave to appeal must include each federal claim sought to be preserved through a complete ordinary review round. Selective acceptance alone does not excuse omission. Expiration of that required opportunity produces default, subject to the existing gateways and actual procedural posture. Actual state-law unavailability and the former statute's absent-process and ineffective-process exceptions remain distinct from mere discretion. The effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review is unresolved. No precedent is overruled, no universal duty to pursue extraordinary remedies is adopted and no new innocence standard is imposed.

## Current-term law

### Lilly v. Virginia, No. 98-5881. — 1999-06-10

#### Holdings

**The asserted firmly rooted exception.** A State cannot establish a firmly rooted federal constitutional hearsay exception merely by placing an unavailable accomplice's custodial, self-exculpatory and blame-shifting accusations within its declaration-against-penal-interest exception. Genuinely disserving admissions and accusations of another cannot automatically be treated as one reliable narrative; genuinely self-inculpatory, equally inculpatory and noncustodial statements remain subject to their actual circumstances.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's exception ruling, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is a direct seven-Justice proposition, not a synthesis of different rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** White preserves accepted, firmly rooted exceptions; it does not turn every police interview admitted under a state label into an established constitutional exception. Lee identifies the distinctive danger that an accomplice will shift or spread blame. Mark's admissions of misconduct do not automatically make his assignment of the killing to Benjamin reliable. Williamson requires context-sensitive attention to individual assertions under federal evidence law and illustrates why a general confession does not carry every accusation with it; it does not itself decide Virginia evidence law or the constitutional question. The narrow custodial, blame-shifting category presented here fails the firmly rooted route. That conclusion alone does not abolish the separate constitutional route of particularized trustworthiness or prohibit all accomplice declarations.

**Particularized guarantees and the challenged accusations.** Under the governing confrontation framework, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from the circumstances surrounding the actual statement when no genuinely applicable firmly rooted exception supports admission. Independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees. Mark's challenged custodial accusations lack them, and their substantive admission without confrontation violated Benjamin's right.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's reliability holding and application, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five supply controlling reasoning at this scope. Scalia and Thomas independently agree that admission violated confrontation on different grounds; neither supplies a reliability-framework join.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Wright distinguish constitutional guarantees arising from the statement's circumstances from evidence elsewhere at trial that might suggest the accusation is true. Mark minimized his violence and denied responsibility for the shooting while accusing Benjamin. Custodial questioning and pressure concerning grave charges created concrete incentives to curry favor and reduce personal exposure; his reported intoxication and inclusion of some self-inculpation do not establish that cross-examination would add little. No express leniency bargain need be found to recognize those incentives. Cross-examination could directly test the allocation of responsibility. Virginia's treatment of blame shifting as weight alone, and its resort to corroboration as a substitute for intrinsic guarantees, did not meet the constitutional requirement. The supported circumstances permit resolution of these challenged accusations without declaring every sentence of the interviews inadmissible under every possible theory. The Court adopts no categorical doctrine replacing the received framework for all testimonial statements, no universal atomized exclusion rule, and no new de novo standard of constitutional review. Genuinely self-inculpatory remarks severable from blame shifting, forfeiture by wrongdoing, dying declarations, and nonhearsay uses with effective limiting safeguards are not barred by this holding.

**Harmlessness and relief.** An established confrontation trial error permits the conviction and sentence to stand only if a properly available harmless-error inquiry establishes beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict; the Commonwealth bears that burden. The present record supports no finding of harmlessness, automatic acquittal or unconditional retrial order.

**Authority:** All nine Justices accept this remedy rule for an established constitutional error. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer find the error now; O'Connor and Kennedy would first have the state court assess particularized trustworthiness under the corrected rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden; Gray keeps the admission error, preservation of a harmlessness contention and ultimate relief distinct. The trial-wide record and properly available arguments must govern that inquiry. The mandate creates no otherwise unavailable harmlessness argument. Because seven Justices find the challenged admission unconstitutional now, that issue is not left open on remand; only the bounded remedial inquiry remains. Unless Virginia satisfies that burden in a properly available inquiry, the conviction and sentence must be vacated. Virginia may retry without the challenged statement or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation. The Court makes no prediction about any retrial or the complete record's harmlessness result.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Roberts remains the received exception-sensitive framework; the Court applies it without replacing it for all hearsay.
- White preserves its accepted exception-specific holdings and does not validate this custodial blame-shifting accusation through a state label.
- Hale preserves White's received framework and its own exception-specific holding; it supplies no validation of this different custodial accusation.
- Lee supplies the special concern about accomplice blame shifting; it supports scrutiny of this accusatory material without banning every self-inculpatory remark.
- Wright supplies the intrinsic-guarantee requirement and bars independent trial corroboration as its substitute; it remains distinct from harmlessness.
- Williamson supplies context-sensitive separation of genuinely disserving assertions under federal Rule 804(b)(3); it does not directly rewrite Virginia evidence law or independently settle the confrontation question.
- Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's burden for constitutional trial error.
- Gray preserves only properly available harmlessness review and separates admission error from ultimate relief.

#### Law After Decision

The narrow exception ruling has seven direct votes. The intrinsic-reliability rule and its application to these challenged accusations have five direct votes and control. Seven Justices find confrontation error, with Scalia and Thomas relying on distinct grounds. No broader categorical accomplice ban, categorical testimonial replacement doctrine, formal-accusation-only limit, or new de novo-review rule is adopted. The state court cannot reinstate admission through the rejected firmly rooted label or reconsider the established violation; the remaining inquiry concerns lawful remedial consequences.

## Current-term law

### Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985. — 1999-06-10

#### Holdings

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes use fraud as a legal term with settled content. Morissette supplies the principle that Congress ordinarily carries that established meaning into the statute; the absence of a separate materiality adjective does not strip fraud of its significance requirement. Durland includes fraudulent future promises, but does not make an inconsequential falsehood a fraudulent scheme or import every element of a completed civil tort. The statutory focus on schemes, and §1344's inclusion of attempts, explains why actual reliance and completed injury are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains a distinct requirement, and the communication need not itself contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Section 1344 separately covers executing or attempting a scheme to defraud a covered institution and executing or attempting a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. Both routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes presented here; their other possible differences, including mental-state requirements in other applications, remain open. Wells construes the different wording of §1014, which does not require objective materiality in addition to its false-statement or overvaluation and purpose-to-influence requirements. It does not turn fraud language into an unrestricted false-statement prohibition. A purpose to influence under §1014 does not automatically establish the separate fraud element.

The natural-tendency formulation, reflected in Kungys, concerns the targeted decision in context. The Court does not exhaust every common-law formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. Nor does it decide materiality under every differently worded offense. The judgment rejecting materiality under the three fraud provisions is reversed.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin assigns the application of an actual element to the jury. Section 7206(1) independently supplies materiality through its express prohibition of willfully subscribing a return declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. Part I establishes the element under the fraud statutes. The express tax and bank directions withheld that application, while the mail and wire charges omitted it. The judge's separate materiality ruling is not a jury finding.

Chapman places the burden on the Government for these preserved constitutional errors. Failure to offer a separately labeled factual materiality defense supplies neither waiver nor forfeiture and does not require Neder to prove innocence. Johnson's refusal to correct forfeited error under the fourth condition of plain-error review does not govern these preserved objections. Genuine findings elsewhere in an actual verdict can establish an equivalent determination; whether they do so here, and whether other supported inferences suffice, are distinct questions addressed in the following parts. Neither the Court's recognition of error nor Gaudin alone decides those remedial questions.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Controlling explanation:** Roy permits examination of a particular omitted element without converting every omission into a structural defect requiring automatic reversal. Its qualified method concerns the error's effect on the jury that returned this verdict. Yates supplies the instructional-focus requirement: a reviewing court must examine whether the instructions directed the jury to evidence supporting the proposed inference, rather than assume the jury decided a question removed from its task. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient, but not invariably indispensable to a reliable noncontribution conclusion.

Sullivan addresses a different defect, an invalid reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all factual findings. These instructions left an otherwise valid reasonable-doubt standard governing the submitted elements. Their express removal of materiality heightens the need to justify the connection; it does not itself destroy every remaining finding or forbid the inquiry Roy allows. Gaudin's allocation rule remains fully operative at trial.

Powerful and unchallenged evidence may support an inference, but neither label creates an automatic safe harbor. A reviewing court must assess defense disputes for what they concern and determine whether the actual findings resolve them. Sufficient evidence for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction is not the required showing. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual contribution from enough other evidence to sustain a result. This holding does not classify every omitted element, constructive amendment, or total denial of jury trial. Richardson's reserved harmlessness questions concerning its distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect remain unresolved.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The instruction required knowing, purposeful falsity of the reported total income: $53,625 for 1985 and minus $980,377 for 1986. It did not permit conviction for an unidentified collateral entry. The jury considered the defenses that the receipts were repayable loans and that professional advice or good faith negated culpability. Its findings reject those defenses insofar as they would make the specified totals truthful or their falsity innocent. The verdict does not resolve every subsidiary advice dispute, but its particular subject and culpability findings furnish the connection required by Roy's actual-contribution inquiry. Annual income is information used to determine income-tax liability and administer the returns.

The objection is substantial: the jury was expressly forbidden to decide materiality, so a court must not supply that missing decision. The Court does not suppose that jurors disobeyed the instruction, treat the judge's ruling as theirs, or declare that they necessarily made the omitted evaluation. It instead asks what effect that withdrawal had on an otherwise valid verdict about these identified income statements. The charge's specific focus and findings establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt under Roy's permitted method.

The conclusion requires no unanimous numerical finding of the alleged additional income, acceptance of every transaction or witness, unpaid-tax calculation, actual IRS response, or successful deception. It does not excuse future courts from submitting materiality. The Government retains the burden in each case; this record-specific conclusion neither creates a tax exception to Gaudin nor decides the fraud counts.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals rejected the antecedent statutory element and therefore did not undertake the required fraud harmlessness analysis. Unlike the tax charge's specified annual-income findings, the fraud instructions and theories require a distinct inquiry into what each verdict resolved. Inflated appraisals, concealed relationships and down payments, presales, and advance requests may bear strongly on lending decisions. Yet the charge did not require proof of every alleged detail, expressly removed bank materiality, and used cross-references for mail and wire fraud. A generic intent-to-deceive finding does not automatically resolve capacity to influence, and Wells prevents treating a §1014 purpose-to-influence finding as an automatic equivalent. The lending-purpose and transaction-significance disputes must be considered in context.

Part V-B applies Roy's qualified method and Chapman's burden to that unfinished appellate task. The tax affirmance proves neither harmlessness nor harmfulness for any fraud count. No present determination requires a new trial on every affected fraud count or sustains any one of them automatically. If the Government fails the governing showing on an affected conviction, instructional-error relief permits retrial; it does not establish evidentiary insufficiency or require acquittal.

The mandate does not independently dispose of §1014, conspiracy, or racketeering claims, reopen acquittals, or calculate a sentence, restitution, or release date. Any properly available dependent consequence must follow the actual predicate and ordinary mandate proceedings. Those boundaries are part of the limited remand, not an advance resolution of unpresented claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

## Current-term published noncontrolling positions

### Calderon v. Coleman — 1998-12-14

**Kennedy, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He would use demanding whole-record substantial-influence review in this category, with genuine grave doubt preventing a finding of harmlessness. His Brecht and O'Neal positions distinguish collateral review while requiring the State to explain why an established violation did not substantially influence the actual decision. That is neither a sufficiency-of-aggravation test nor a rule that the prisoner loses whenever the evidence is evenly balanced. He accepts the binding Chapman categories but would not extend them here. Stringer's distinct weighing-error rule does not make every capital habeas claim identical. The District Court's plausible account of diminished mitigation must be assessed against the full charge and independent institutional-danger case; he supports a return without finding harmlessness or directing denial of relief.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They would require the prisoner to establish substantial injurious influence for this category outside the previously controlling Chapman holdings. Their O'Neal position rests on the limited collateral remedy and §2254(a)'s custody-in-violation requirement, while recognizing that the statute does not expressly state a tie-breaking rule. An evenly balanced showing does not satisfy their proposed burden. They respect Roy's particular personal-intent rule without extending it universally. Potential diversion and instructional misunderstanding do not themselves establish the substantial effect they require. Their objection concerns the unfinished substantive inquiry, not missing words; serious aggravation is not an automatic cure. They rely neither on New's excluded account nor on a universal innocence prerequisite, and they do not require factor-specific capital findings derived from Scalia's different omitted-purpose analysis in Roy.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He would affirm under substantial-influence review with grave doubt preventing harmlessness. The District Court did more than identify inaccurate words: it connected the misleading nondeath alternative to mitigation explaining violent behavior, the prominence of the prior convictions and the prosecution's danger case. The jury's conviction-document request shows salience without proving an actual release discussion. He considers the corrective charge, repeated instructions, extensive violence, prison-safety argument and reported short deliberation, but finds that those matters do not dispel grave doubt about substantial influence. The broader danger language and the instruction itself leave a concrete connection to the sentencing choice even without an express prosecutorial reference to commutation. His Roy position permits affirmance when a proper substantive application already supports relief despite an overbroad lower formulation. Another remand would repeat that task. He relies on the trial record, not excluded deliberation evidence or mitigation developed later in habeas, and would retain sentence-only relief with a reasonable state sentencing opportunity.

**Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** She joins the Chapman rule for this defined capital-selection error but concludes that the State cannot establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt on the existing record. The misleading account could turn explanations of violent behavior into reasons to doubt the nondeath option; the convictions emphasized in aggravation also restricted commutation. She considers the corrective direction and the substantial independent institutional-danger case, but neither establishes that the misinformation made no contribution. The charge first purported to explain life without parole through inaccurate information and then directed the jury to disregard that subject. The District Court's contextual explanation of that conflict supports its judgment. Her conclusion does not depend on a universal rule against corrective instructions, a specific parole question, New's excluded proffer or later habeas mitigation. Her O'Neal reservation of a universal Chapman extension remains, and her Roy position does not require express findings on every capital factor. She would affirm conditional sentence relief rather than require another application of the same substantive inquiry.

## Current-term published noncontrolling positions

### O'Sullivan v. Boerckel; No. 97-2048; Seventh Circuit judgment, 135 F.3d 1194. — 1999-06-07

Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He agrees that Illinois supplied an ordinary available petition and had not told prisoners their federal claims could bypass it. He therefore joins the actual-case exhaustion and default holdings. He would also state affirmatively that a clear state rule making a step unnecessary controls: the inquiry concerns the process the State actually makes ordinarily available. His rule does not demand repetitive extraordinary remedies, applications the State expressly labels unnecessary, or futile petitions after final resolution through another ordinary route. He does not join the Court's reservation of the express-state-opt-out question. His remand preserves cause and prejudice, actual innocence, waiver and any clear applicable Illinois exclusion; it finds none of those predicates satisfied and directs no new hearing or favorable habeas result.

Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, dissents. The three claims were fairly presented for merits decision at trial and through Illinois's appeal as of right. In the dissent's view, that process gave the State a full opportunity; omission from the selectively administered second tier should not create an additional federal default. Technical exhaustion because no remedy remains does not itself establish that the prisoner failed to provide a fair opportunity while remedies existed. Rule 315's institutional criteria matter to what the State's ordinary process requires, and mandatory repetition of every rejected error can burden a selective system without supplying a materially new merits opportunity. The dissent reads the statutory reference to available procedures against established limits on endless exhaustion, rather than treating every opportunity to request extraordinary or selective relief as mandatory. It accepts proper-presentation rules, genuine claim-specific state defaults and existing exceptions. It would affirm the no-default holding and allow ordinary habeas proceedings on these three claims, subject to other actual conditions, without awarding a writ or release.

## Current-term published noncontrolling positions

### Lilly v. Virginia, No. 98-5881. — 1999-06-10

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, agrees that Virginia's penal-interest label cannot establish constitutional firm rootedness for these custodial blame-shifting accusations. She would permit the state court first to identify any genuinely self-inculpatory assertions that independently satisfy the correct trustworthiness standard. Williamson requires context, not wholesale acceptance or automatic exclusion of every collateral assertion. Governing Wright limits that inquiry despite Kennedy's different view of corroboration; a constitutional error established below would then receive only properly available Chapman review. They do not join the Court's present trustworthiness application or a new rule requiring independent appellate factfinding.

Scalia finds the uncross-examined police accusation at the core of the confrontation right. Formal police questioning generated an accusation offered against the accused, who could not test it. A judicial estimate of probable truth is not a complete substitute for that right. He accepts reversal and the constitutional-error remedy while declining to settle every hearsay category or comprehensively replace Roberts.

Thomas rests on the formalized character of the recorded custodial accusation, within the concern he preserved in White and Hale. He agrees that admission violated confrontation and that the case must return for the bounded remedy. He does not endorse the entire reliability analysis, a blanket prohibition on accomplice declarations, or a rule for all informal statements; he also preserves the objection that the lower court had not performed the second Roberts inquiry. His vote for violation supplies no additional vote for the Court's five-Justice reliability rationale.

## Current-term published noncontrolling positions

### Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985. — 1999-06-10

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, II, and III. Fraud's settled meaning supplies materiality, Wells concerns another enactment, and Gaudin requires submission of actual elements. Roy makes these omissions reviewable trial error and permits reliable whole-record inferences beyond necessary equivalent findings. He does not adopt a universal structural-error rule or Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive method.

The Government's case must be weighed: the alleged omissions exceeding $1 million and $4 million plainly concern tax computation, the alleged inflated appraisals and nonexistent down payments concern lending decisions, and Neder did not separately contest materiality as a factual matter. Those considerations are substantial, not disqualified evidence. Nevertheless, the instructions removed tax and bank materiality and never asked the jury to assess that dimension of mail or wire fraud. The findings of knowing falsity and fraudulent intent do not reliably show the omission's lack of contribution to the actual verdict on each affected count. The annual-income findings identify the statements and culpability, but do not close that gap. The Government has shown what a properly instructed jury would almost certainly find, not harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under these instructions and verdicts.

He would reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment, vacate every conviction affected by removal or omission of materiality, and remand for new trials if the United States elects to retry. He therefore dissents both from tax affirmance and from another fraud harmlessness inquiry. Sullivan forbids inventing a verdict, while Roy supplies the governing review; Johnson's forfeited-error disposition does not shift the burden. He would leave unaffected counts to their own preserved issues, disturb no §1014 conviction merely because of this holding, and order no insufficiency acquittal. A valid admission or a record establishing actual-verdict harmlessness could produce a different result.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** Both join Parts I, II, and V-A. They agree on fraud materiality, the jury's role, the preserved claim and Government's burden, and a first appellate inquiry into the affected fraud convictions. Their Roy position permits review for actual findings that genuinely embrace the omitted element; it does not permit appellate certainty about an unresolved element to replace its jury determination. Gaudin and Sullivan protect that division of responsibility. They withhold assent from Part III's broader method and its classification of the express materiality withdrawal, while retaining Roy's classification within that decision's own personal-purpose category.

The tax verdict establishes knowing, purposeful false annual income. It does not establish the jury's own application of the omitted materiality requirement, which the instruction forbade it to consider. A judge's conclusion about the significance of annual income supplies the missing evaluation rather than identifies a genuine equivalent finding. They accordingly reject both Part IV's application and Stevens's additional rationale. Their ground permits new tax trials under correct instructions; it is not evidentiary insufficiency and does not require acquittal or unconditional release.

For fraud, they join the limited remand because the court of appeals' no-element ruling left a real initial inquiry unfinished. They would confine it to genuine equivalent findings on the affected counts, and do not join Part V-B's wider inference instruction. A valid equivalent determination can establish harmlessness, so their position is not automatic reversal for every omitted element. Ginsburg joins this writing throughout.

**Justice Stevens, concurring.** He joins the entire Court opinion, including its qualified actual-verdict method, tax noncontribution conclusion, and limited fraud remand. He adds that the required finding of knowing falsity of these particular annual-income totals embraces their material relationship to determining income-tax liability. His additional ground rests on the actual findings and their specific subject, not on appellate prediction about what another jury would decide. It requires no finding that every alleged dollar was income, that an unpaid-tax amount was established, or that the IRS was successfully deceived. His view supplies no additional holding beyond the Court's sufficient common ground. He continues to reject hypothetical-verdict substitution and does not use his disagreement with Wells to reopen the different §1014 offenses.
