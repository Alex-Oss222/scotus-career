## Authority: Breard v. Greene

Source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Breard v. Greene

**Docket or dockets:** A-732; associated petition No. 97-8214.

**Decided:** 1998-04-14.

##### The stay requires both meaningful review prospects and irreparable harm

**Holding:** A stay of execution pending certiorari requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would accept review, a significant possibility of reversal and likely irreparable harm without protection. Execution's irreversibility supplies the last requirement, but does not dispense with the two review requirements or independently overcome an applicable barrier to relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's signed Circuit Justice explanation, Part I, joined by all eight Associates, states the Court's governing interim standard. These joins reveal no final vote on the pending petition.

##### The present treaty/default showing does not justify a stay

**Holding:** On this submission, the asserted Vienna Convention notification violation does not establish a sufficient prospect of obtaining relief through the surviving default and remedial barriers to justify a stay. The treaty source and asserted novelty of a claim do not alone establish cause, prejudice or an applicable exception; this interim conclusion neither makes every consular claim unenforceable nor decides ultimate prejudice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct joins to this sufficient treaty-ground assessment. Souter supports denial on the distinct causal/remedial assessment in his concurrence and does not join Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The assessment is interim and confined to the submission. Individual treaty enforceability, ultimate cause and prejudice, final default exceptions and hearing entitlement remain undecided. A notification failure is not itself reliable new innocence evidence. Article 36’s receiving-State-law permission remains qualified by full effect to the protected purposes.

##### The separate challenge to Virginia's proportionality-review method does not independently justify a stay

**Holding:** The present claim that Virginia administered its statutory proportionality review through an inadequate comparison pool does not show sufficient reversal prospects to support a stay. State-law error alone is not a federal habeas ground; a genuine arbitrary deprivation of a state-created sentencing entitlement remains a distinct federal question, but this submission does not adequately demonstrate such a deprivation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct joins. Ginsburg and Breyer nevertheless support a stay on the treaty ground. Stevens alone regards the process claim as independently sufficient for protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The challenged proportionality-review method is distinct from a substantive sentence-proportionality claim. No default attributed to the latter is imposed on the former. Section 17-110.1(C) requires the arbitrariness and similar-crimes/defendants determinations; subsection (E) permits collection within the chosen period, requires use of available records as a guide and availability of accumulated records to circuit courts. It does not command collection of every prosecution.

**Operative remedy or transition:** A-732 is denied 6–3: Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support denial; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer oppose. The order creates no execution restraint, decides no accompanying petition or companion proceeding, and neither commands execution nor dissolves an unidentified order. No final habeas-merits, §2254(d), hearing or nonretroactivity ruling follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Coleman v. Thompson, 504 U.S. 188 (1992), and Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983), are applied to the separate review-prospect and irreparable-harm requirements, without creating an automatic capital stay.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991), Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478 (1986), and Reed v. Ross, 468 U.S. 1 (1984), supply default, external-cause and legal-novelty principles for the limited treaty assessment. No categorical treaty exception or prohibition is announced.
- Pulley v. Harris, 465 U.S. 37 (1984), rejects a universal comparative-review requirement; Lewis v. Jeffers, 497 U.S. 764 (1990), limits federal relief for state-law error alone; Hicks v. Oklahoma, 447 U.S. 343 (1980), preserves the distinct arbitrary-deprivation question. Their separate functions are retained.
- Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314 (1996), remains protection for identified, legally required habeas process that cannot be completed by lawful expedition, not an automatic stay of every first petition. Schlup v. Delo, decided January 23, 1995, retains its reliable-new-evidence probability gateway; an asserted lost opportunity for consular assistance does not itself satisfy it.
- Trest v. Cain, decided December 9, 1997, creates no duty to invent an omitted default defense and does not remove the treaty default actually asserted here. Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard, decided March 25, 1998, and Doe v. District of Columbia, decided June 2, 1997, establish neither a universal comparison database nor the absence of every independent judicial-process claim.
- Lambrix v. Singletary, decided May 12, 1997, and O'Dell v. Netherland, decided June 19, 1997, preserve claim-specific nonretroactivity analysis. O'Dell's confined future-dangerousness, legal-parole-ineligibility and denied-corrective-opportunity holding is not a general capital-procedure exception. No final nonretroactivity determination is made here.

#### Republic of Paraguay v. Gilmore; In re Breard

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1390 (A-738); No. 97-8660 (A-767); No. 125 Original (A-771).

**Decided:** 1998-04-14.

##### A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect of prospective relief for a current consular violation

**Holding:** Even assuming the invoked notice duties were binding and violated, and applying the applicants' favorable formulation requiring a reasonable probability that four Justices would grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, likely irreparable harm and appropriate consideration of competing equities, A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect that the older consular duties require the requested execution restraint as relief for a current violation. A continuing consequence of past nonnotification is not by itself a continuing denial of these applicants' presently available consular rights; the distinct most-favored-consul notification theory does not itself establish the requested remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, supplies eight direct joins to this complete rule and application. Stone-Zsela independently agrees that the current domestic duty and remedial showing is insufficient, without adopting the complete explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The older consular claim is examined on its own duty and remedy, without importing the new ICJ-order theory. Treaty enforceability, ultimate remedy and categorical immunity remain open. The 1859 most-favored-consul notification theory is distinct from Article 36’s request condition.

##### A-738's older civil claim requires no further protected jurisdictional interval

**Holding:** A-738 also supplies no basis for an administrative execution injunction merely to obtain additional coordinated consideration of the older treaty claim. Even assuming an otherwise lawful §2283 route in the applicants' favor, that claim's interim duty-and-remedy question can be assessed now; no necessary additional jurisdictional or injunctive-power inquiry is established by the prospect that execution-specific relief will become unavailable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela denies interim relief on his own stated ground and does not adopt this additional analysis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2283 covers execution of a state judgment; short duration, an officer caption and an administrative label do not themselves create an exception. Its three exceptions remain express authorization, necessity in aid of jurisdiction and protection or effectuation of the issuing court’s judgments. Actual independent-party status and any §1983 right, defendant and exception remain unresolved.

**Holding:** After independent consideration of the retained original-habeas route, A-767 presents no sufficiently substantial prospect of extraordinary domestic habeas relief and identifies no unfinished process actually required by governing law that demands interim protection. The stay is denied without denying the original petition, treating the new ICJ order as a defaulted or previously adjudicated claim, or finding an unverified Rule 20 condition unsatisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: five direct joins to the complete explanation. Stone-Zsela separately adopts the insufficient-exceptional-relief and no-identified-required-process grounds, supplying a sixth denial vote without joining every additional proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Circuit authorization is unnecessary for retained original habeas. Rule 20 still requires exceptional circumstances, inadequate relief elsewhere, reasons concerning district relief and exhaustion or the applicable unavailable/ineffective-state-process alternatives. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform discretion, with their direct force here reserved. No new ICJ-order default, final merits adjudication, found Rule 20 defect or automatic compulsory response/hearing is imposed.

##### A-771 lacks the present domestic showing for its requested interim injunction

**Holding:** A-771's emergency submission does not sufficiently establish the domestic right, ongoing duty, jurisdictional route and remedy needed for the requested injunction through the proposed original action. Denial resolves immediate protection only: the Court reserves categorical treaty enforceability, ultimate prejudice, final original jurisdiction, the separate cause-of-action questions and the leave motion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this interim insufficiency proposition in Part III; Stone-Zsela independently adopts the same proposition in his signed explanation. These five directly support it at this limited level. No whole opinion or additional §2283 necessity application has five denial-coalition joins, and no Marks synthesis supplies one.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the five-Justice limited interim-insufficiency proposition controls. The competing four-Justice §2283 necessity analyses supply no further rule. Categorical treaty enforceability, cause of action, original jurisdiction, prejudice and leave remain reserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** A-738 is denied unanimously. A-767 is denied 6–3, Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supporting. A-771 is denied 5–4, Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas supporting. These denials issue no restraint or administrative interval and terminate no accompanying petition or leave proceeding. They neither command execution nor establish that it occurred.

Union Gas is not overruled, and Monaco's foreign-sovereign/State issue is not resolved merely by an officer caption. Papasan and Milliken preserve relief for legally continuing violations without treating every remaining consequence as an ongoing wrong.

Coleman's certiorari-stage requirements remain separate from Felker's original-habeas channel and exceptional showing. Felker's independent consideration and original-§2244 reservation remain; Lonchar requires genuinely unfinished legally required process and an actual timing need, not automatic protection from filing. Schlup's innocence gateway is not established by a lost-consular-advice allegation. No new cause, prejudice or waiver ruling is made.

Shipp and United Mine Workers preserve a court's ability to decide a substantial authority question, subject to necessary scope and cessation when relevant power is found absent. Shipp's obsolete automatic-stay statute is not imported. Pennsylvania Bureau keeps §1651 auxiliary; Hill and Atlantic Coast Line preserve §2283's enforcement coverage and three exceptions. Hale and County of Imperial require the actual independent-party inquiry; Mitchum preserves the established §1983 exception and separate equitable limits. Baker's bounded recognition rule supplies no unproved nonparty status or treaty remedy.

Blessing separates a particular right, enforcement channel, defendant and fitted relief; official consular status does not automatically establish an individual §1983 entitlement. Aktepe requires examination of an independently manageable duty and supplies no categorical foreign-relations bar. Steel Co. remains a prohibition on deciding contested merits by assuming Article III power; this order makes no final merits adjudication on such an assumption. Woodard supplies neither a treaty cause nor a rule substituting clemency for otherwise available judicial review. No precedent is overruled.

## Authority: Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho

Source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1474.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

##### A disputed federal tribal title question does not create a sovereign-lands exception to prospective officer relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a claim for prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officials actually connected to an alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights merely because adjudicating that violation requires resolving a disputed federal title question or affects important sovereign interests. The decree must address proved unlawful future official conduct and may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or require the State itself to convey title.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Material application:** The pleaded land-regulating officials have a concrete connection to the alleged interference; a general law-obedience duty would not suffice. The actual request for exclusive use and exclusion of regulation is acknowledged, but the Ninth Circuit withheld a decree binding Idaho’s title and the case remains at the pleading stage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither generalized state-interest balancing nor a special sovereign-lands exception bars the route. The Tribe must establish the federal right, ongoing violation and lawful remedy. No ownership, navigability, prestatehood disposition or separate United States action is adjudicated; no general Article I abrogation rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm permission for the prospective-officer claims, 5–4; leave the State, agency and direct quiet-title or retrospective-relief dismissals undisturbed, 9–0. Further merits and tailored-remedy proceedings are permitted, without automatic injunction or property transfer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young supplies the connected-officer route; Tindal and Treasure Salvors distinguish officer conduct relief from sovereign title. Oneida supplies federal-question character. Edelman, Green and Pennhurst retain retrospective and state-law limits. Seminole’s actual IGRA officer holding is consistent but supplies neither a universal statutory route nor an abrogation rule, and no IGRA sequence is imported.

## Authority: Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

Source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-12.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### IGRA's binding good-faith adjudication is judicial despite later statutory steps

**Holding:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function when its judgment conclusively determines legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make that judgment advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens's serious doubt and Stone-Zsela's nonjoin supply no additional support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aetna and Nashville permit conclusive adverse declarations without an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial assistance to administration within its adversarial, conclusive, enforceable-order setting, without creating IGRA contempt power. Ferreira's revisable commissioner process is distinguished. Gordon's jurisdictional disposition supplies no delivered reasoned majority opinion requiring execution in every judicial case; Hayburn produced no Supreme Court merits decision, and its reported circuit communications remain historical materials. Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut: Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. The holdings do not establish an unrestricted power to reopen completed merits judgments and do not determine whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication ceases to be judicial because mediation or executive action follows.

##### A connected officer may be sued prospectively under IGRA within its complete statutory limits

**Holding:** Ex parte Young permits prospective official-capacity relief against Governor Chiles for an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiation duty, subject to IGRA's full sequence and remedial limits. The State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case; they do not authorize greater remedies through the officer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. No additional general clear-indication theory commands that coalition.

**Material application:** The Governor's representatives negotiated, supplying connection to the alleged violation without establishing that he alone can bind Florida to every term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over compact terms, and nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers §2710(d)(3)(A)'s good-faith duty; suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary action under (A)(iii) remain separate. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's good-faith burden under (B)(ii).

An actual adverse finding triggers an order to State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty days. The court may consider public interest, safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming, and shall treat direct-tax demands on the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. Failure to agree during that period requires last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the findings and order. Submission to both sides begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent produces an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. Failure to agree or participate during the ordered period produces statutory progression, not added contempt or an order compelling agreement; every separate obstruction sanction is not decided. Section (d)(4) creates no new taxing authority but preserves agreed regulatory-cost assessments under (d)(3)(C)(iii), separately from direct-tax demands' evidentiary effect. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.

Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain distinct. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction means approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA, and Federal Register publication remains necessary. Section 2721 severability supplies neither missing predicates nor an alternative program.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical Governor dismissal, 5–4, without finding bad faith or granting a compact, mediator, default proposal, Secretary procedures, damages, game-scope declaration or license. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 receive their own lawful treatment and are not vacated. The asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting power, Tenth Amendment defense and mediator-appointments question remain unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young reaches this connected officer and ongoing federal duty without a universal rule for detailed statutes. Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary remains; prospective costs alone do not establish retrospective relief. Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies appealability, not immunity. New York and Voting Rights Coalition do not decide the reserved IGRA constitutional defenses; X-Citement Video preserves appropriate respondent defenses without compelling their resolution here.

##### State amenability lacks a controlling rationale, despite agreement on direct-State statutory intent

**Authority of the judgment:** Florida's immunity-dismissal direction is set aside and remanded by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, 5–4. Stone-Zsela and Stevens would sustain direct-State amenability; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate without deciding it. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. No controlling State-amenability rationale exists under Marks: avoidance is not a logical subset of the affirmative constitutional grounds, and three unused conditional alternatives are not actual abrogation joins.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, State-and-Tribe order and successive State-directed steps. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Six actual positions across the separate writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The other three's conditional acceptance is not counted. This agreement concerns statutory intent alone.

**Current force:** The clear-statement proposition controls; no majority sustains or rejects Florida's constitutional amenability or requires a particular avoidance sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State-removal judgment establishes neither jurisdiction nor its absence; lawful jurisdiction is necessary before any State merits adjudication. The State claim is not terminated by immunity, and no abandonment, mootness or indefinite stay is presumed. No express or constructive waiver, reciprocal tribal/state consent or delegated United States immunity exemption establishes this suit. The United States appears only as amicus.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Union Gas is not overruled: its damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion retain their decided force without making every plurality sentence binding. Hans remains effective; separate common-law theories do not become law. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unchanged.

## Authority: Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal

#### Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-300.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### I. The circuit's threshold classification is reviewable

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary review of a circuit decision granting or denying authorization to file a second or successive application, but it does not bar review of this distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary. The Court may decide whether the circuit correctly placed this proceeding outside that mechanism; the circuit's label does not itself establish the answer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is adopted by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The circuit’s label is not conclusive; an actual authorization determination remains subject to Felker’s review bar.

##### II. This previously presented premature claim may receive its first federal merits consideration

**Holding:** A Ford claim actually presented in the initial merits application, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved for later consideration, is not a second or successive application when renewed after the impediment of prematurity has ended. This renewed claim may therefore return to the District Court without circuit authorization, subject to otherwise applicable habeas requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author; these seven directly establish the rule and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is confined to a Ford claim actually presented, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved. It does not exempt every competency-only application, a claim first raised after a prior rejection, other later-arising claims or all nonmerits dismissals. Competency and Arizona’s procedures remain undecided; no live hearing, writ or stay is guaranteed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed, 7–2, for the particular claim’s return to the District Court under otherwise applicable habeas procedures. All nine permit review of the threshold classification. No competency finding, stay, writ, hearing requirement or execution direction follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Felker v. Turpin: applied to actual authorization decisions; its review bar and distinct original-habeas route remain unchanged.
- Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil: distinguished because they concerned actual authorization refusals; this case creates no exception permitting ordinary review of those refusals.
- Calderon v. Thompson: its action-specific channel distinction is applied; its completed-merits recall rule is distinguished from a claim dismissed only as premature.
- Lonchar v. Thomas: its requirement of legally supported dismissal predicates informs the classification; no equitable power to disregard enacted successive limits is recognized.
- Ford v. Wainwright: supplies the incompetency-to-be-executed claim without deciding competency or procedural sufficiency here.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: their provision-specific temporal treatment is preserved; they do not independently exempt all later filings from AEDPA.

## Authority: Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

Source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Aktepe v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-03-10.

##### The pleaded duties cannot be adjudicated without choosing military training policy

**Holding:** A military setting alone does not make a damages action nonjusticiable. NATO participation, potential embarrassment and military status alone do not bar review. A materially different claim enforcing an independent manageable duty remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Political-question summary judgment is affirmed. The Court decides no negligence, damages, maritime statutory coverage, sovereign immunity or Feres question, and orders no further merits proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baker v. Carr supplies the claim-specific political-question inquiry; military subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Nixon v. United States preserves review of independently fixed legal limits while recognizing the confined nonjusticiability of an actually committed procedural choice; its impeachment holding is not military immunity.
- Gilligan v. Morgan, 413 U.S. 1, is applied to the policy-selection concern, while its reservation of specific-injury controversies remains intact and supplies no categorical damages exception.
- Weiss v. United States and Loving v. United States preserve constitutional and statutory review in military matters; their appointment and delegation holdings do not establish the maritime duty proposed here.
- Dalton v. Specter retains scheme-specific statutory preclusion and is not extended to these maritime statutes.
- Richenberg v. Cohen preserves substantial military weight within actual legal constraints, without creating a military-tort immunity. No precedent is overruled.

## Authority: Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.

Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1525.
**Decided:** February 21, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Preserved claims and disavowed legal arguments (The Court may consider this included legal argument despite its earlier disavowal); Federal Courts — Relief after reversal of governmental-actor status (Governmental status requires a remand, not an immediate display order).

##### Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental

**Holding:** When the Federal Government creates a corporation by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains authority to appoint a majority of its governing directors, the corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes notwithstanding a statutory declaration that it is not a governmental agency. Amtrak satisfies those combined conditions; ordinary operating discretion in its corporate officers does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- San Francisco Arts & Athletics, Inc. v. United States Olympic Committee, 483 U.S. 522 (1987): distinguishes federal charter and assistance to a private institution from special creation coupled with permanent governmental majority-appointment authority.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 (1991), and Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992): preserve attention to actual governmental legal machinery in their jury-selection settings; neither makes billboard advertising an exclusively governmental function or supplies a general rule for all publicly benefited corporations.
- Jackson v. Metropolitan Edison Co., 419 U.S. 345 (1974), and Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991 (1982): distinguish attribution of a private entity's particular act from identifying an institution that is itself governmental under the combined rule.
- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., decided November 14, 1994: keeps the distinct inquiry into an entity's state sovereign immunity separate; absence of immunity does not imply constitutional privacy.
- American National Red Cross v. S. G., 505 U.S. 247 (1992): preserves its express charter-jurisdiction rule, which supplies no constitutional actor classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal charter, subsidy, regulation, governmental property, public benefit, a single appointment, temporary investment, or merely the current ownership of shares is not independently sufficient under this rule. Permanent authority to appoint the governing majority, governmental objectives, and special governmental creation must be present together. The Court decides no universal agency status, sovereign immunity, Treasury obligation, federal tort-liability rule, or charter jurisdiction. ### 1. Notice of the seizure does not require an individualized guide to published return procedures

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process requires timely notice reasonably calculated to identify the seized property and its responsible custodian so that an owner may seek its return. When the owner receives that notice, due process does not additionally require personalized instructions describing adequate state return procedures reasonably ascertainable from generally available statutes and judicial decisions; publication alone does not establish meaningful access to an inadequate, concealed, misleading, obstructed or practically inaccessible remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other members of the seven-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They adopt both holdings and the procedural-due-process explanation. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment and the negative rule rejecting the additional legal-instruction duty, but withhold assent to deriving notice duties for criminal warrant execution from civil procedural-due-process decisions. The seven complete joins independently establish controlling authority; no fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that notice must inform the owner what the government has done and where to seek the property. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. supplies that practical notice principle. It does not require an official to provide a personal explanation of the law an owner may use. Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft involved an operative utility-dispute procedure that a customer could not discover simply by consulting generally available law. California's judicial return procedures present a different question. Mathews v. Eldridge requires attention to the actual interests and procedures; the modest expense of adding instructions to a receipt does not alone make those instructions constitutionally necessary. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property preserves an independent due-process inquiry despite a warrant, but concerned process before civil seizure of real property. It neither settles notice after criminal seizure of movable evidence nor requires a guide to judicial remedies. The receipt here identified the property, agency, issuing judge and court, relevant dates and officer contacts. The Court rejects the additional legal-guide requirement without independently adjudicating every aspect of California's procedures or their operation. Withholding information actually necessary to invoke a remedy, or misleading or obstructing the owner, would present a different properly preserved claim.

**Limits:** The holding does not validate an unlawful initial seizure, unreasonable retention, a failure to disclose the seizure or custodian, or an inaccessible return route. It adopts no comprehensive notice checklist, universal deadline or rule that a warrant satisfies every later process obligation. California Penal Code §1536's custody subject to court order, the nonstatutory return motion recognized in People v. Icenogle, and §1540's separate restoration conditions remain distinct; this case does not merge them or decide that every owner satisfies any one route.

### 2. The additional-notice judgment must be reversed without deciding an unproved obstruction claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's requirement of individualized instructions about published return procedures, and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground, must be reversed. The omitted warrant number does not sustain that judgment because the District Court found that respondents had not established the number was necessary to bring a return motion; the reported difficulties do not establish a different obstruction ground.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling explanation. Scalia and Thomas also support this application and its reservations through their concurrence in the judgment; all nine support reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit held the available California procedures constitutionally adequate but demanded additional instruction about using them. This Court rejects that legal demand; it does not independently endorse every procedure or its practical administration. Perkins reported difficulty finding the warrant, the issuing judge's absence, a mistaken number and a sealed warrant. Some officer statements were disputed. A public index connected the searched address to the warrant number, and the District Court's unrefuted finding was that necessity of that number had not been established. These circumstances do not permit the Court to replace the lower finding with a determination that officials withheld an indispensable identifier. Nor does rejection of the instruction theory extinguish a distinct, properly preserved challenge to actual obstruction or accessibility. Such a claim must rest on its own record and procedural basis. Return of most property in June 1994 does not itself dispose of a supported claim concerning an earlier deprivation. The judgment on individual qualified immunity is not reopened, and the Court adjudicates neither entitlement to the reportedly stolen starter pistol nor damages.

**Remand limit:** The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any preserved adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation issue remains subject to ordinary procedural and substantive requirements; no new claim, fresh hearing or exploratory discovery is ordered.

## Current public holdings: att_corp_v_iowa_utilities_board_merits_1999-01-25.md

## Holdings

### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's insertion of the local-competition provisions into the Communications Act brings those provisions within its implementing grant. Section 251(d)(1) directs implementation; §252(c) requires state commissions to apply §251 and the Commission's rules. National methodology and state determination of particular rates can coexist. Section 152(b) does not remove the new federal duties from the regulatory authority the amendments create. Louisiana Public Service Commission prevents a general grant from regulating matters Congress left wholly intrastate; it does not disable implementation of newly enacted federal obligations. MCI requires an actual delegation and preservation of operative limits, not a rule that every delegation permits only minor action. The contrary reading gives proper attention to the state role but treats methodology and particular determinations as mutually exclusive when the statute does not. The holding decides authority, not the substantive lawfulness of TELRIC, a confiscatory rate or every instruction the Commission might issue.

### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the general implementation rule does not adjudicate every asserted federal instruction. Section 251(f)'s exemption predicates remain for the actual assigned determinations; §§251(d)(3) and 252 retain their preservation and approval limits. MCI requires those specific statutory conditions to retain their force even when a general delegation exists. The lower court therefore must examine any unresolved objection to the rural burden-of-proof rule or preexisting-agreement requirements on its own substance. Rejection of exclusive state regulatory authority supplies no blanket validation of either rule and no permission for state commissions to disregard otherwise lawful federal requirements. The remedial disposition removes the categorical jurisdictional ground and returns the residual questions without selecting a new proof burden or deciding a particular agreement.

### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that operational-support systems, operator and directory services, and vertical-switching functions are not excluded merely because they are not detachable physical components or can also be described as services. The statutory definition itself supplies the answer. The physical-component-only construction would omit its express functional language. But definition and compulsory access are separate inquiries: the Commission must still apply the proprietary-necessity and general-impairment criteria to identify required elements. No general requirement to unbundle every technically available service follows.

### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding and operative rule:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the invalidity holding that the rule must make element-specific judgments giving necessity and impairment independent constraining force, and joins vacatur and agency remand; he does not supply a separate join in the more specific outside-alternatives requirement. Souter dissents from invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that any increase in cost or decline in quality cannot by itself establish the required impairment while external alternatives are categorically ignored. MCI preserves operative statutory predicates before agency deference. The agency's competitive-entry expertise remains substantial, but it cannot make every purchasing disadvantage a sufficient statutory obstacle. The proprietary standard of significant impairment or thwarting is not invalid simply because it is less than absolute indispensability; its defect includes the cramped alternative comparison limited to the incumbent's nonproprietary elements. The Court does not require access only to an irreplaceable natural monopoly, invent a numerical threshold or choose a replacement list. On remand the agency may explain real entry impairment, actual availability, costs, quality, service requirements and workable selection criteria under the enacted standard. The strongest contrary argument invokes the competitive-entry purpose and danger that theoretical alternatives will require disabling duplication. The Court accepts that real commercial availability matters; that is why an actual inquiry is required rather than either automatic access or theoretical replication.

### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combining elements into a service. The separate resale route in §251(c)(4) does not insert a minimum-property requirement into the unbundling route. Combining or buying elements entails obligations and commercial consequences distinct from buying a finished retail service for resale; the existence of both routes does not make one redundant. This holding promises no entrant the incumbent's whole network regardless of necessity and impairment and decides no assumed difference in particular rates.

### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3)'s direction to make elements available in a manner permitting the requester to combine them does not require the incumbent first to dismantle a configuration already joined. The Commission reasonably implements nondiscriminatory access by preserving that configuration subject to the requester's option to separate. The contrary reading confuses separately available elements with mandatory physical separation. This holding restores paragraph (b), not the distinct affirmative new-combination duties in paragraphs (c)–(f), and does not require an unestablished configuration to be built. Those duties receive their own disposition. Particular requests remain subject to the Act and valid technical and operational requirements.

### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to any interconnection, service or network element supports selection of an individual arrangement, not a requirement to accept the entire agreement. The entrant may not take an isolated favorable price while discarding its relevant obligations. The incumbent may establish that provision to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and the reasonable availability period protects against indefinite use of stale arrangements. The challengers argue that selective access undermines negotiated tradeoffs; the agency reached a contrary practical judgment with nondiscrimination and these safeguards in view. That disagreement does not establish that Congress commanded whole-agreement adoption or that the agency's incentive assessment is irrational. No unrestricted cherry-picking right results.

### VIII. Dialing-parity authority and numbering-cost ripeness remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. The separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge remains unripe because its asserted disadvantage depends on particular cost-recovery rate decisions not before the Court.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor agrees on the narrower §251(b)(3)/§251(d)(1) implementation ground stated in her separate opinion. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. All eight Associates join Part VIII's separate numbering-cost ripeness holding; Stone-Zsela agrees only in that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that federal implementation of §251(b)(3)'s substantive parity duty is authorized. The general implementing grant supports that conclusion, and the narrower substantive-duty ground also supports the parity disposition without resolving the separate state-pricing dispute. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain operative. The separate numbering issue concerns who will bear costs under future particular rate decisions. Reversal of the authority ground neither resolves those rates nor creates an accrued numbering injury. The California judgment therefore receives distinct dispositions rather than complete reversal by inference from the general-authority holding. No particular carrier's liability or cost share is fixed.

### IX. Technical feasibility cannot replace the required access criteria

**Holding and operative rule:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. The separately sustained definition of technical feasibility and other unaffected portions of the rule remain outside that invalidation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that technical feasibility may inform element selection: §251(d)(2)'s minimum criteria do not prohibit consideration of additional relevant matters. For elements beyond Rule 51.319's list, once technical feasibility is found, Rule 51.317 permits the state commission to decline unbundling only if either (1) the element is proprietary or would reveal proprietary information, and the requester could offer the same proposed service through other nonproprietary unbundled elements within the incumbent's network; or (2) denial would neither reduce service quality nor increase its financial or administrative cost compared with providing that service through other unbundled elements in that network. Those restrictions reproduce the limiting-standard defect in the required-element list. They do not allow realistic outside alternatives to perform their proper role. The result rests on this independent substantive flaw, not on the mistaken premise that feasibility can never matter. A corrected-delegation remand cannot preserve the existing defective criteria without agency reconsideration. The Court does not forbid every lawful presumption or invalidate all of Rule 51.317.

### X. Superior-quality duties return for review under the correct framework

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c) is vacated and remanded for reconsideration of the actual conditions and agency justification under the correct implementing authority. The Court does not decide their ultimate validity or order unconditional reinstatement.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part X's corrected-standard remand and its confined review directions. Thomas and Breyer do so under the Court's governing authority framework while retaining their dissent from Part I. Stone-Zsela agrees in the remand without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(2)'s requirement of at-least-equal quality establishes a floor; the absence of an express superior-quality command does not alone show that an implementing rule can never require more. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict objections nevertheless remain for review. Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns requested, technically feasible interconnection superior to that supplied to the incumbent, affiliates or other interconnection recipients. Rule 51.311(c) separately concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, placing technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission on the incumbent. Lesser quality at the requesting carrier's sole request remains permitted. On remand the actual improvement, statutory relationship and agency explanation must be examined. Compensation alone supplies no missing authority, and nondiscrimination alone does not command every upgrade. The lower court's allowance for modifications necessary to accommodate lawful access is preserved; neither an unconditional superior-facilities right nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

### XI. Affirmative new-combination duties require separate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f) is vacated and remanded for review under the proper implementing-power standard. Section 251(c)(3)'s direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requester; the particular affirmative duties and burdens remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part XI is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer's agreement is a remand under the governing authority framework, not a Part I join. Seven Justices control this remand; Stone-Zsela agrees only in its disposition, and Thomas dissents on his independent task-allocation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower construction treated enabling access as an exclusive assignment of all combining labor without adequately addressing the actual delegation, network control, conditions and agency explanation. That differs from preserving existing combinations, which the Court separately upholds. Subsection (c) addresses requested configurations the incumbent does not ordinarily make, only when technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) addresses combination with the requester's own elements in a technically feasible manner. The other-carrier condition expressly belongs to (c), not automatically to (d). Subsections (e) and (f) place the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission on the incumbent. MCI preserves specific statutory allocations, but the contested sentence does not alone establish the exclusive task allocation the lower court found. The remand neither approves every proof burden nor compels a particular configuration. Voluntary combination and necessary accommodation remain distinct from a mandated new combination.

### XII. The general section 208 announcement is not ripe for review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definite agency announcement of a possible enforcement forum is not ripe merely because the position is complete and the dispute predominantly legal. Because this announcement directs no present change in primary conduct, determines no carrier-specific liability and permits meaningful review of a concrete application, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice, without deciding enforcement authority.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Scalia's Part XII, supplying a controlling majority. Stone-Zsela agrees only in vacatur of the premature enforcement-merits adjudication and dismissal without prejudice as unripe; he adds no enforcement or ripeness ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that finality, standing, fitness and hardship are distinct; it decides ripeness, not independent standing or final-order questions. Ohio Forestry and Toilet Goods distinguish settled policy from presently consequential application. Paragraphs 126–128 definitively assert complaint authority even where a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplate enforcing agreement terms; a later expedited-procedure rulemaking does not make that assertion tentative. Yet the announcement changes no substantive carrier duty, identifies no operative agreement conflict, revises no state determination and orders no act or liability. A concrete complaint would distinguish statutory enforcement, agreement enforcement and a carrier proceeding that effectively revisits state action.

Footnote 236 says the Commission might decline financial penalties in some state-authorized circumstances; it establishes neither immunity nor a required penalty. The statement that judicially decided issues are unlikely to be revisited likewise creates no absolute preclusion rule. The absence of an absolute safe harbor is not itself withdrawal of an established protection or present liability. Bennett's operative legal-protection change and Franklin's completed determination are therefore different.

Section 208(a) supplies complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer and investigation under its conditions; §208(b)(3) makes an order concluding a covered investigation appealable under §402(a). Concrete application permits the authority objection in its actual setting. The prospect of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about forum allocation do not alone establish present primary-conduct hardship. Lee preserves review of presently burdensome civil duties, and Pic-A-State concerns an existing business actually constrained by a law; neither condition is established by this instrument. NAACP and Paradise require examination of the actual claim, not an automatic further inquiry into hypothetical applications. The Court creates no universal exhaustion rule, promises no future filing's standing or timeliness, and does not defer every challenge until penalties are collected. A presently binding or burdensome statement remains a different case. The Court decides neither carrier statutory-violation authority, agreement enforcement, review of state determinations under §252(e)(6), nor state-failure authority under §252(e)(5); no carrier complaint, agreement, state order or penalty is adjudicated.

## Current public holdings: your_home_visiting_nurse_services_v_shalala_merits_1999-02-23.md

## Holdings

### I. Refusal to reopen is not a new appealable reimbursement determination

**Holding and operative rule:** A fiscal intermediary's discretionary refusal to reopen a final reimbursement determination is not a new final determination as to the amount of total program reimbursement under 42 U.S.C. §1395oo(a)(1)(A)(i). Judicial review of the Board's final jurisdictional ruling under §1395oo(f)(1) permits deciding that question; it does not give the Board jurisdiction over the refusal or authorize direct judicial calculation of reimbursement.

**Authority:** Part I-A of Kennedy's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. Part I-B's additional regulatory construction and deference ground is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join that ground. Each stated rationale commands a majority at that scope.

**Controlling explanation — I-A, statutory ground:** The Court holds that the refusal leaves the earlier amount determination in place. Congress supplied a 180-day appeal period; the regulation supplies a distinct, ordinarily three-year opportunity to request reopening. Treating every refusal as a new amount determination would allow a provider to renew the statutory appeal simply by requesting reconsideration. The provider's contrary argument—that refusal finally leaves payment unchanged—confuses the effect of preserving an earlier decision with a new decision about its amount. The Board's authority under §1395oo(d) over matters in a cost report operates once the hearing conditions are met; it does not remove them. The credibility of the asserted cost error supplies no missing jurisdiction.

**Controlling explanation — I-B, regulatory support:** Section 405.1885(c) gives reopening jurisdiction exclusively to the body that last decided the matter; §405.1889 separately recognizes review when actual reopening produces a revision in the reimbursement amount. The Secretary's considered reading respects that distinction. *Auer* requires a fair and considered regulatory interpretation consistent with the text, and *Thomas Jefferson University* applies that bounded deference to Medicare regulations. The manual explains this arrangement rather than creating jurisdiction or independently amending law, consistently with *Guernsey*. The interpretation leaves the regulation's mandatory reopening commands intact. An agency's jurisdictional label is not conclusive, and deference does not replace the statutory analysis.

### II. The claim cannot proceed directly under federal-question jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** Incorporated §405(h) bars the asserted 28 U.S.C. §1331 route because this request seeks reopening for additional Medicare reimbursement and derives its standing and substantive basis from the Medicare scheme. Describing the requested order as procedural does not remove that claim from the statute.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, commands eight votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on this component. This same eight-Justice coalition controls Holdings III and IV.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the requested reopening is a means of obtaining payment under Medicare. *Heckler v. Ringer* supplies the arising-under inquiry and does not allow a claimant to escape statutory channeling by requesting a preliminary procedural step. *Michigan Academy* recognizes a presumption of review in its different Part B setting; it does not promise a new district-court route whenever the claimant has not used an original statutory appeal. This scheme offered an original Board appeal followed by judicial review. The missed deadline matters to that arrangement, but it is not treated as nonexhaustion of the separate, later refusal claim. No distinct colorable constitutional claim is presented, and no independently authorized constitutional channel is foreclosed.

### III. The APA supplies no independent jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** The Administrative Procedure Act does not independently confer subject-matter jurisdiction over this refusal to reopen. Its review standards do not supply jurisdiction missing from the asserted statutory routes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that *Califano v. Sanders*, which rejects the APA as an independent jurisdictional grant, governs. The provider expressly asks the Court to reconsider that rule based on Medicare's corrective-adjustment command. That command regulates lawful reimbursement; it does not amend the APA's jurisdictional character or create an exception when §1331 is barred. Section 1395oo(f)'s use of APA standards in an authorized action likewise does not establish that every proposed action is authorized. The Court neither decides the reimbursement claim nor adopts a separate factual conclusion about the salary evidence. No distinct colorable constitutional claim is presented; the treatment of such a claim is not decided away.

### IV. No clear duty to reopen these determinations supports mandamus

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that incorporated §405(h) does not universally preclude mandamus under 28 U.S.C. §1361, mandamus fails because no clear nondiscretionary duty to reopen these final determinations is established. The denial rests on the absence of that duty, not on failure to exhaust the available avenues concerning the refusal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that lawful reimbursement duties do not automatically require reopening closed reports. *Ringer* requires a clear nondiscretionary duty and exhaustion of other avenues. The provider exhausted the refusal claim. The reasonable-cost and comparable-compensation requirements govern reimbursement; *Good Samaritan* places corrective adjustments within authorized cost methods, and *Regions Hospital* distinguishes correction of historical inputs for future payments from reopening final annual payments. Neither supplies the requested duty.

Section 405.1885(a) permits reopening on a timely motion and ordinarily requires a request within three years of notice; reopening afterward is barred except under paragraphs (d) and (e). New evidence or alleged error alone does not command reopening. Paragraph (b) requires reopening and revision when HCFA, within those three years, notifies the intermediary that its determination or hearing decision is inconsistent with applicable law, regulations, or general instructions issued by HCFA in accordance with the Secretary's agreement with the intermediary. Paragraph (d) requires reopening and revision at any time when a party procured the determination or decision by fraud or similar fault. Neither trigger is established. A provider's allegation is not HCFA notification or proof of fraud.

Paragraph (e) applies paragraphs (a) and (b) to periods ending on or after December 31, 1971, and the three-year limit to earlier periods reopened after May 27, 1972; it changes nothing for 1989. Section 405.1887 requires written notice of reopening, an explanation when revision follows, and a reasonable opportunity for additional evidence or argument in the reopening. Section 405.1889 separately permits review of an actual post-reopening revision in reimbursement. Those provisions do not require granting every request. The manual's new-material-evidence, clear-error and inconsistency considerations cannot override the distinct regulatory commands. The compensation claim's possible accuracy therefore does not establish a clear present reopening duty.

### V. The affirmance decides the presented routes and preserves their limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed without a reimbursement award, reopening order, Board merits hearing or further remand. Timely initial appeals, actual reopening followed by a new determination, genuinely presented regulatory procedural violations and independently authorized constitutional channels retain their own requirements and are not decided here.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part V's stated disposition and reservations. The conclusions about the independent routes remain controlled by the distinct coalitions stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds only that the routes presented do not authorize relief on this record. An established mandatory reopening trigger, actual amount revision or independently supported constitutional claim would present its own question under the lawful channel governing it; none receives automatic review or relief. The possible absence of the intermediary as a party supplies no independent dismissal ground. Nor do four constituent reports establish an unraised amount-in-controversy or grouping defect. The decision does not determine the proper compensation amount, compel payment of a claimed cost, or declare all reopening-related actions unreviewable.
