### Anonymous individual political leaflets

#### McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-986.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Compelled identification on these political leaflets requires exacting scrutiny

**Holding:** A content-defined requirement that an individual identify herself on ordinary political issue leaflets burdens core political expression, including the choice to speak anonymously. The State must satisfy exacting scrutiny by showing narrow tailoring to an overriding state interest; viewpoint neutrality or the election setting alone does not lower that standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices; Thomas concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Talley v. California, 362 U.S. 60: applies protection of anonymous handbills to this individual political leaflet; the Court does not establish immunity for fraudulent communications.
- Burson v. Freeman, May 26, 1992: applies the independently majority-supported scrutiny distinction; its fractured polling-place validity judgment does not settle this leaflet requirement.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780: distinguishes the election-administration setting; it does not reduce scrutiny for every election-related speech restriction.

##### The asserted voter-information interest does not justify this identification demand

**Holding:** A general interest in helping voters evaluate a source does not justify requiring this ordinary individual to place her name and residence or business address on her independently produced, truthful political leaflet. The Court leaves candidate finance, campaign committees, organizational spending, and other materially distinct disclosure settings for separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices; Thomas concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Talley v. California, 362 U.S. 60: applies protection for the ordinary author's choice of anonymous publication.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1: distinguishes campaign-finance reporting and its specific interests; those disclosure holdings remain undisturbed.
- First National Bank of Boston v. Bellotti, 435 U.S. 765: distinguishes corporate-advertising source information; its observation does not resolve this private individual's leaflet.

##### Fraud prevention does not justify this application to truthful independent advocacy

**Holding:** Although preventing electoral fraud is a weighty interest, Ohio may not impose this categorical identification requirement on truthful, independently produced individual issue leaflets without the necessary narrow fit to that interest. The enforcement order and $100 fine must be set aside; properly tailored fraud, impersonation, and materially different disclosure rules remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices; Thomas concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Talley v. California, 362 U.S. 60: applies anonymous-publication protection while distinguishing measures directed at fraud.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1: leaves candidate-finance disclosure and corruption-related interests intact rather than treating them as dispositive of this ordinary leaflet.
- First National Bank of Boston v. Bellotti, 435 U.S. 765: leaves materially different corporate source-disclosure questions outside the individual sanction decided here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ohio's judgment is reversed, 8–1, and the enforcement order and $100 fine must be set aside. No damages or invalidation of every disclosure provision is ordered; candidate, committee, organizational and other materially different disclosure settings remain outside this application.

### Political-party campaign expenditures

#### Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission, 518 U.S. 604 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-489.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether the Senate party-expenditure ceiling may reach this independent advertisement

**Holding:** A political party's own campaign advertisement that is genuinely independent of a candidate may not be subjected to the Senate-election expenditure ceiling in 2 U.S.C. §441a(d)(3) merely because its speaker is an official political party. Party identity and a continuing electoral relationship do not, without more, justify applying that ceiling to this independent expression; the adverse enforcement judgment must therefore be vacated.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct votes.

**Material application:** The party’s $15,000 pre-nomination advertisement was genuinely independent. Its prior assignment of the whole $103,248 adjusted election allowance explains statutory excess, not constitutional validity. Legitimate corruption and circumvention concerns do not establish an actual candidate connection from party identity alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority adopts the complete narrower expenditure framework or its prudential reservation rationale (O’Connor, Souter, Breyer), or Kennedy/Scalia/Thomas’s broader coordinated-party-expression protection. No Marks combination is used. Coordinated expression, direct candidate cash, earmarks, republication, sham independence and actual conduits are neither generally immunized nor upheld by implication. No universal bribery-proof, express-advocacy formula, constitutional dollar floor, vagueness or administrative-deference rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Adverse enforcement/penalty mandate vacated and remanded, 6–3. The broader Senate-election counterclaim receives no majority constitutional determination; no fixed penalty, damages, refund or compelled new factual trial.

##### Whether party identity supplies the connection required by the alternative contribution theory

**Holding:** For the government's theory that this advertisement is a contribution under §441a(a)(7)(B)(i), party status alone cannot substitute for expenditure made in cooperation, consultation or concert with, or at the request or suggestion of, a candidate, the candidate's authorized political committees or their agents. On the accepted independence premise, that theory cannot preserve the adverse enforcement judgment under §441a(a)(2).

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice Part I coalition.

**Material application:** A categorical agency characterization is not a factual finding of consultation; competing assertions about novelty and noncontest establish neither forfeiture nor concession. Transfers among national, State, district, local and subordinate same-party committees are exempt from §441a(a)(1) and (2). Candidate-material dissemination, distribution or republication under (a)(7)(B)(ii), and original-source attribution for earmarked/intermediary contributions under (a)(8), have distinct predicates absent here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutionality of a transaction with an actual connection, all interactions with (d)(1)’s notwithstanding clause, presidential allowances and other House limits are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1:** Its distinction between independent expenditures and candidate contributions supplies the present independent-advertisement rule. Its treatment of the party provision's Fifth Amendment challenge did not decide this First Amendment question. The general distinction remains in force.
- **FEC v. National Conservative Political Action Committee, 470 U.S. 480:** Its protection of genuinely independent political advocacy supports the result; it supplies no categorical immunity for actual contribution channels.
- **Day v. Holahan, May 15, 1995:** Its controlling protection of meaningful intermediary association and preservation of realistic conduit regulation inform the required distinction. Its recipient category excluded political-party units, and its responsive-financing judgment had no unified controlling rationale. Neither is converted into an earlier decision of this party-spending question.
- **FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life, Inc., 479 U.S. 238:** Its protection of political expression and its nonprofit setting remain distinct; the Court does not rest today's judgment on a universal express-advocacy formula or extend the nonprofit exception to every entity.

### Student speech and curricular control

#### Settle v. Dickson County School Board

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 93-6207; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 25, 1996.

##### What genuine curricular grounds may limit a religious research topic

**Holding:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The teacher-only submission is not presumed publicly attributable to the school or an open forum. Objective inquiry may be distinguished from devotion or personal testimony. Genuine factual or evaluative mistakes can remain pedagogical judgments, while a legally impermissible categorical religious ban does not become valid as an honest academic mistake. The full policy permits objective, respectful, balanced academic religious treatment without promoting tenets or demeaning belief and accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief; neither provision guarantees approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal curricular viewpoint neutrality, unrestricted topic choice, religion-based exemption, perfect-accuracy duty or independent Free Exercise, Establishment or equal-protection holding.

##### Whether the summary-judgment record establishes lawful cause or the same-decision defense

**Holding:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Failure to secure permission was supported but its independent causal effect was disputed by the next-day no-penalty alternative, mixed reasons and original-choice answer, which also covered Buddha and Muhammed. Faith alone does not prove inability to research. Familiarity does not prove absence of new inquiry. Source requirements must reflect permitted secondary and outside sources, not a new Bible-only or school-library-only premise. Comparator descriptions are relevant without proving identical circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No pretext, hostility, final causation, liability or automatic defeat of a proved same-decision defense is found. Waters supplies its shared genuine-ground distinction, not an imported employment investigation code. Graduation proves no universal mootness; actual capacities, municipal attribution, immunity and remedies remain open. Conduct-date immunity notice concerns March–April 1991, not later decisions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0, for ordinary proceedings on the free-speech claim. No compulsory approval, reading, grading, credit, removal of the zero, damages, new discovery procedure or trial; another supported lawful disposition remains possible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

### Public-employee speech and factual error

#### Waters v. Churchill, 511 U.S. 661

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1450.

**Decided:** 1994-05-31.

##### A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor

**Authority of the judgment:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated and the case remanded, 7–2; defendants' summary judgment remains set aside. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support vacatur. No complete controlling rationale exists under Marks; the express majority propositions below have only their stated scope.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** A public employer does not violate the employee's substantive speech right by disciplining her because of a genuinely held, objectively reasonable account of speech that the Pickering–Connick balance permits it to discipline, even if that account is later shown mistaken. The protection requires actual reliance on the permissible ground; it does not protect a reasonable investigation used to conceal retaliation for different protected speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968): applies the public-employee speech balance, adding this limited factual-error permission.
- Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138 (1983): applies contextual public-concern analysis and reasonable prospective assessment of institutional disruption; personal embarrassment alone is insufficient.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): preserves private speech protection and the relevance of manner, time and place.
- Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): preserves actual causation; a potentially permissible ground is not proof of the ground actually used.

##### An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Discipline because of an unreasonable and incorrect report of speech that was actually protected violates the substantive speech right when the protected speech bears the required causal relation to the action, subject to distinct causation defenses and remedial immunity. This agreement does not establish liability for every investigative imperfection, for an unreasonable report of actually unprotected speech, or without proof of the underlying protected conduct and causal action.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering, Connick and Givhan: preserve the substantive protection of the actual public-concern speech within the employment balance.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): distinguishes a property-based hearing entitlement from this substantive speech claim.
- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): retains the distinct causation and same-decision requirements; unreasonableness alone is not an automatic recovery rule.

##### The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated, but defendants' summary judgment remains set aside because genuine reliance and retaliation for other protected criticism are materially disputed. On remand the employee must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred without it, and individual qualified immunity remains available and, if necessary, must be assessed as a distinct defense under the law relevant to the 1987 conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Givhan, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): apply the employee's causal showing and the employer's preponderance same-decision defense.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982): preserves individual qualified immunity as distinct from the substantive speech rule.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: requires considering relevant authority on the preserved immunity issue, without expanding the claim or supplying notice from later law.
- Rankin v. McPherson, June 24, 1987, and Anderson v. Creighton, June 25, 1987: may inform later doctrine but cannot themselves establish notice for the January and February 1987 actions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete factual-investigation theory commands five votes. The seven-Justice reasonable-and-genuine safe harbor and six-Justice unreasonable-false-actually-protected proposition are express agreements, not a unified Marks framework; the latter includes two judgment dissenters and is not selected as a judgment-supporting narrowest rationale. No universal freestanding investigation duty or rule for unreasonable investigation of actually unprotected speech is established. Personal immunity remains tied to law giving notice at the 1987 conduct, not the 1994 decision itself.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, without restoring defendants' summary judgment. Determine genuine reliance, actual motivation and the protection of causally relevant other speech under ordinary summary-judgment and Mt. Healthy rules. The completed investigation's conditional objective adequacy and the reported account's conditional permissibility are not reopened as wholly unresolved issues. Personal immunity is considered if necessary; no damages, reinstatement, municipal-liability finding or revived association claim is ordered.

### Public-employee speech and personal causation

#### Jeffries v. Harleston

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit No. 93-7876; no separate Supreme Court docket is supplied.
**Decided:** November 17, 1997.

##### Whether the employment balance supports final defense judgment for shortening this chair term

**Holding:** The actual speech and employment relationship do not support final defense judgment on the appellate court's balance here. Retained tenure and lack of entitlement to an extended chair term do not categorically defeat retaliation liability, and a reasonable prediction of disruption remains relevant without itself deciding the constitutional balance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Material application:** The off-campus, unaffiliated address addressed curriculum and racial history and was not assigned official work; use of Jeffries's chair title did not establish university sponsorship. His real administrative responsibilities included courses, tenure evaluations, records, meetings and budget work, but the record established no special policymaking, confidential or additional public-contact role. The recruitment, fundraising, faculty and community interests remain relevant. Their combination did not give the forecast and retained professorship the dispositive force the appellate court assigned them. The jury's findings of no realized disruption and reasonable expected disruption both remain operative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Prevention need not await actual harm. This application creates no categorical academic-speech immunity or exclusion of all viewpoint reactions, no demand for quantified institutional loss, and no comprehensive investigation procedure. It does not transfer the whole prospective-restraint burden to an individual personnel decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pickering and Connick require a contextual employment balance. Waters protects an actually relied-upon reasonable account only when that account describes lawfully disciplinable speech; no complete investigation framework is added. Perry, Umbehr and O'Hare distinguish entitlement to a benefit from unconstitutional retaliation in withholding it. NTEU's broad prospective-restraint rule and Settle's student-curriculum holding remain confined to their settings.

##### Whether nine successful Board votes automatically establish an independent lawful cause

**Holding:** When protected conduct is a substantial or motivating factor in an employment action, the inquiry must address each defendant's actual causal responsibility and any defense that the same action would have occurred without that conduct, proved by a preponderance. Different punitive-damages or animus findings and nine successful votes do not, without that inquiry, establish an independent lawful decision insulating all defendants.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins.

**Material application:** A proposal by a nonvoting officer may contribute causally, but procurement alone does not prove liability. Defeated removal votes and abstention neither prove nor eliminate other causal contributions. An actually independent lawful Board decision or a supported personal defense may remain decisive. Claims against Bernstein remain discontinued.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary competent direct and circumstantial evidence governs; no heightened motive-proof requirement applies. Punitive answers do not establish constitutional protection, causation or immunity. No individual liability is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mt. Healthy, Umbehr, O'Hare, Kimberlin and Crawford-El preserve ordinary proof of actual responsibility and an actual same-decision counterfactual, rather than a merely conceivable lawful explanation.

##### What further adjudication follows from rejection of the blanket defense judgment

**Holding:** The remand leaves verdict harmonization, sustainable personal defenses, capacity attribution and relief to ordinary adjudication; it does not require a new trial. Personal-damages immunity must be assessed specifically as of the late-October 1991 conduct, with a separate date for any properly preserved March 1992 claim, and later decisions cannot establish earlier notice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. Each part has its own majority, without a Marks synthesis.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices support vacatur—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—but only the five joining Part III adopt this bounded mandate. The sixth vacatur vote does not establish compulsory retrial.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate judgment is vacated without reinstating the two-year chair injunction or punitive awards. Trial, retrial or judgment without trial depends on the actual procedural record and governing law. No discontinued claim is revived and no March 1992 claim created. Prospective relief requires its own live remedy; elapsed time establishes neither mootness nor continuing chair eligibility.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton preserve particularized conduct-date immunity; later merits decisions do not give retrospective notice.

#### Brewster v. Board of Education

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 97-55203; no separate Supreme Court docket established.
**Decided:** 1998-08-17.

### Securities fraud and affirmative assistance

#### Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-854.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance

**Holding:** The established private action under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 permits recovery from a person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct, subject to proof of that fraud, the defendant’s qualifying participation, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. This is accessory responsibility within the received investor action, not a finding that the assistant made a primary misrepresentation or a rule imposing liability through professional status or control alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the existence of the confined action and its received-remedy rationale. No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6 — applied as recognition of the received private action; it did not itself decide accessory liability.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — applied for bounded development of the received action and distinguished as to its actual contribution holding; shared liability must still be established before allocation.
- Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Curran, 456 U.S. 353 — used by analogy for the significance of an established judicial remedy; its different statutory setting does not establish congressional enactment of this action.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — applied: the underlying wrong must involve the required manipulation or deception, not unfairness or fiduciary misconduct alone.
- Pinter v. Dahl, 486 U.S. 622 — distinguished: its construction of express seller liability under §12 does not settle this received §10(b) action; its own statutory holding remains intact.
- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Hochfelder itself did not decide that question.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Huddleston itself did not decide that question.

##### Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance

**Holding:** For affirmative substantial assistance, the defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the fraudulent character of the activity and the assisting role of its conduct; severe recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. No independent disclosure duty is required for that affirmative participation, but negligence, valuation concerns alone, professional status, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assistance, or mere silence without a relevant duty do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its exclusion of negligence remains controlling; its recklessness reservation is resolved only for this private affirmative-assistance setting.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — its deception-or-manipulation requirement prevents ordinary investment risk and professional error from becoming securities fraud.
- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — its confined affirmative-assistance and extreme-recklessness analysis is adopted for the bank claim; inconsistent knowledge-only or categorical duty requirements for such affirmative assistance cannot govern after this decision. Its separate rulings concerning Pring are outside this review.

##### Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award

**Holding:** Viewed favorably to the purchasers at summary judgment, the appraisal warnings, internal concerns and agreement postponing an already demanded independent review until after the offering permit a genuine dispute over severe recklessness and substantial affirmative assistance; the Tenth Circuit’s reinstatement of the pleaded bank claim is affirmed and the case remanded. The purchasers must still prove the underlying violation, the bank’s qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss under the ordinary civil burden; neither the default nor the bonds’ face value establishes fraud damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — affirmed insofar as it reverses summary judgment on the purchasers’ affirmative-assistance claim against Central Bank; triable issues are not findings of fraud or damages.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — the ordinary preponderance standard is preserved; neither institutional status nor information held by the defendant shifts that burden.
- Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 — its reliance requirement is preserved. Today’s decision establishes no presumption for these municipal bonds and, as part of the retained accessory action, requires reliance on the underlying fraud rather than on the assistant’s concealed participation.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — its shared-liability prerequisite and contribution limits remain effective; this remand establishes no entitlement or allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the full rules for duty-based omissions, duty-free inaction, agency attribution, a universal securities recklessness rule, other enforcement or statutory settings, or Stone's additional concurrence formulations. The §20(a) defense for a controller acting in good faith without directly or indirectly inducing the violation remains distinct. Reliance on the underlying fraud is required; investors need not personally rely on concealed assistance, and no reliance presumption is established for these bonds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit insofar as it reinstated the pleaded bank claim and remand, 5–4. The bank's summary judgment cannot rest on the rejected categorical grounds or the disputed assistance and culpability record. No liability, damages, automatic indemnity, unpleaded primary claim or adjudication of another defendant's claim is supplied.

### Primary and controller fraud, connected gains and prospective prevention

#### Securities and Exchange Commission v. First Jersey Securities, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** 96-1862.

**Decided:** 1998-09-28.

##### Proven direct deception supports primary fraud liability

**Holding:** A broker-dealer and the officer who knowingly directs its deceptive transactions are responsible for their proved primary securities fraud; office, ownership or assistance alone does not establish that violation. First Jersey's intentional concealment of its dominated markets, repurchases and coordinated splitting and resale plan, together with Brennan's knowing hands-on orchestration, supports the liability found under Securities Act §17(a)(1), Exchange Act §10(b) and Rule 10b-5.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part II; nine direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** The findings establish deceptive dealing in the six securities, rather than liability inferred from Brennan's title or wealth. The firm's transactions carried an implied representation that the prices reflected a competitive market. Its concealment of its own domination and coordinated repurchase, unbundling and resale plan made that representation misleading to both selling and purchasing customers; its duty was to disclose the facts needed to make those representations truthful. Its contemporaneous repurchase prices supply a supported market benchmark in that setting. Illiquidity within the firm's unilateral control does not establish an independent explanation defeating those findings. No automatic five-percent or ten-percent markup rule is adopted. Central Bank preserves its confined private affirmative-assistance action, but direct participation in the actual deception supplies liability here without enlarging that action or importing its private reliance requirements into SEC enforcement. O'Hagan preserves the need for actual deception, culpability and a connection to securities transactions; this case does not extend its source-deception theory to status alone.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Bank's private accessory rule remains separate and is not expanded.
- O'Hagan's primary-deception requirements are preserved without extending misappropriation liability.

**Independent alternative holding:** Section 20(a) permits the SEC, which falls within the Exchange Act's relevant definition of person, to enforce controller responsibility for the established §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 violation on proof of actual control and the deliberate culpable participation found here. Its defense requires both good faith and absence of direct or indirect inducement; Brennan cannot satisfy that defense on the supported findings.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part III; nine direct rationale votes. This is an independently sufficient statutory ground at the stated scope, separate from his primary participation.

**Material application:** Section 3(a)(9)'s definition includes governmental agencies, defeating the contention that the SEC cannot invoke this supported controller liability. Brennan exercised actual control and deliberately participated in the coordinated scheme. A compliance arrangement that deliberately failed to examine the relevant transactions does not establish good faith, and his active direction defeats the separate no-inducement requirement. The Court does not impose liability from passive shareholding alone, and the defense remains available to a controller who proves both good faith and absence of direct or indirect inducement. It need not settle every disputed culpable-participation pleading burden because that participation was proved. This Exchange Act controller ground does not become a rule supplying controller liability for every Securities Act claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Bank retains its distinct accessory domain and does not displace the express controller provision or its conjunctive defense.
- O'Hagan retains its distinct primary-fraud domain and does not displace the express controller provision or its conjunctive defense.

##### Equity may recover the connected gains once

**Holding:** In the SEC's injunctive enforcement proceeding authorized by Securities Act §20(b), 15 U.S.C. §77t(b), and Exchange Act §21(d)(1), 15 U.S.C. §78u(d)(1), traditional ancillary restitution permits recovery of the reasonably approximated unlawful gains connected to the proved fraud, with credits preventing duplicate recovery. Joint and several responsibility is proper for First Jersey and Brennan's proved collaboration and benefit in this enterprise, not because every owner owes every corporate debt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part IV; nine direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** Sections 20(b) and 21(d)(1) authorize the SEC's suit for injunctive enforcement; their words do not expressly grant disgorgement. Porter and Mitchell recognize ancillary restitution within that statutory equity unless Congress displaces it. Mertens construed a different, expressly limited ERISA remedial provision; it does not remove this traditional gain-recovery function. The six-security profit approximation was $27,288,099. Crediting the $5 million class-settlement payment leaves $22,288,099 to be disgorged. Brennan's deliberate collaboration, complete ownership, unrestricted access to the enterprise funds and corresponding enrichment connect him to those gains. The defendants' strongest objection—that equity cannot take unrelated wealth or impose another person's unrelated gain—states a real limit but does not describe these findings. The award removes the proved benefit once; it supplies no recovery of unproved transactions, unrelated profits, wealth as such or every recipient's gross receipts. Deterrence alone supplies neither the amount nor an additional punitive component.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Porter supplies traditional ancillary restitution within statutory equity.
- Mitchell supplies traditional restorative equity absent statutory displacement.
- Mertens is distinguished by its different statutory restriction, without altering its ERISA holding.

##### Retained-use value supports the interest award

**Holding:** Prejudgment interest may approximate the value of retaining unlawful gains, subject to the actual credit, causal connection and equitable fairness of the award. On these findings, the $52,689,894 award using the IRS underpayment measure is sustained, with interest on the credited $5 million ending when that amount was paid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part V; nine direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** The $52,689,894 award is the prejudgment interest fixed in the July 17, 1995 District Court judgment, not a recalculation through today's decision. The defendants retained use of the remaining gains during that prejudgment period; interest on the $5 million paid in January 1987 ceased at that payment. A borrowing-cost measure addresses that benefit more directly than the lower rate the Government pays a lender; no identified legal or calculation error requires substitution of the Treasury rate. A large ratio to principal, or the passage of years while the benefit remains held, does not by itself transform retained-use compensation into punishment. The actual cessation of interest on the paid settlement credit prevents charging for money no longer retained. The Court makes neither the IRS rate nor interest during every possible litigation delay universally mandatory. Porter and Mitchell's gain-restoration principle supports this confined use-value component, not an unexplained surcharge.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Porter's restorative-equity principle is applied to the connected benefit of retained funds, without a universal rate.
- Mitchell's restorative-equity principle is applied to the connected benefit of retained funds, without a punitive multiplier.

##### Prevention requires a supported risk of recurrence

**Holding:** Under Securities Act §20(b), 15 U.S.C. §77t(b), and Exchange Act §21(d)(1), 15 U.S.C. §78u(d)(1), a prospective securities injunction requires the proper showing of a supported risk of future violations; past misconduct or a defendant's refusal to confess does not alone supply that basis. The systematic intentional violations, relevant prior sanctions, ongoing market activity, retained personnel and continuing control support the injunction entered here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part VI; nine direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** The governing provisions authorize the SEC to seek restraint of a person engaged or about to engage in covered violations and provide for injunctive relief on a proper showing without bond. The actual findings connect the deliberate course of conduct to continuing means and opportunity for recurrence. The injunction therefore prevents supported future securities violations rather than punishing wealth, corporate office or defense of the suit. FEC v. Williams preserves the separate requirement for a genuine preventive basis and does not classify every governmental equitable remedy as a penalty. Neither an industry-wide ban nor relief against unrelated actors is ordered. The Court sustains this decree on its supported predicates without creating an automatic injunction from a completed fraud judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FEC v. Williams is applied to independent preventive support, preserving its limits on automatic prospective relief.

Related questions in Securities and Exchange Commission v. First Jersey Securities, Inc., 1998-09-28, dockets 96-1862, appear under Federal Courts — Claim preclusion and later distinct securities transactions.

# City of Cincinnati v. Kruse — December 16, 1998

## Event

City of Cincinnati v. Kruse, No. 98-454. Merits decision, December 16, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, reviewing 142 F.3d 907 and its affirmance of the judgment invalidating Cincinnati's mandatory candidate-expenditure ceiling. The question is whether the City's asserted corruption, fundraising-time and public-confidence interests, together with its other financing concerns, justify that ceiling. No oral-argument date is established.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Sixth Circuit's judgment invalidating Cincinnati's mandatory candidate-expenditure ceiling.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment invalidating the challenged ceiling and protecting against its enforcement remains in force. The separate contribution and disclosure provisions, voluntary-financing arrangements and any damages question are not adjudicated. No new ceiling or evidentiary hearing is directed.

All nine Justices join the full opinion. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### Candidate expenditures and the asserted municipal interests

**Holding and operative rule.** Cincinnati may not impose this mandatory ceiling on the total campaign expenditures of a candidate or candidate committee. A direct limit on the quantity of candidate advocacy receives the demanding First Amendment review applicable to expenditures; the accepted corruption, campaign-cost, equalization, official-work and public-confidence grounds do not justify this salary-linked, source-independent restraint.

**Authority.** Kennedy's opinion for the unanimous Court supplies the complete rule, application and limits. Every participating Justice joins it in full.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that accepting the City's evidence does not establish the constitutionality of the means it chose. Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes a donor's contribution from the candidate's expenditure and protects the quantity of campaign expression. Spending contributed money remains expenditure by the candidate; spending personal or family funds does not become a contribution restriction merely because the City calls the law a campaign-finance measure. Reducing costs or equalizing resources cannot justify this limit. Prevention of actual or apparent corruption supports properly fitted controls, but the record does not connect the cutoff of all candidate spending to the contribution danger asserted here.

Fundraising's interference with official work is a distinct concern. The ceiling nevertheless reaches challengers and personally financed advocacy as well as incumbent fundraising, and its link to salary does not supply the missing fit. General public confidence cannot cure that defect or revive the rejected cost and equalization grounds. That a meaningful campaign can be conducted below approximately $140,000 does not permit government to forbid additional advocacy. The 1995 spending evidence preceded the new contribution controls and does not establish their inadequacy; the Court imposes no mandatory empirical waiting period.

Colorado Republican distinguishes actual candidate-resource transactions from political expression; Kentucky Right to Life likewise requires attention to the particular receipt or intermediary restraint. Neither converts this total-spending ceiling into a contribution limit. The Court accepts the City's asserted facts for summary judgment and decides their legal sufficiency, without finding those submissions false. It leaves materially different measures addressing official duties, separate contribution and disclosure provisions, voluntary public-financing conditions and damages outside this decision.

**Application.** The invalid ceiling is three times annual council compensation and governs candidate or candidate-committee election spending. Its exclusions for independent expenditures, uncompensated volunteer services and the described party slate or sample-ballot publications identifying endorsed candidates do not cure the restriction on candidate expression. The Sixth Circuit's invalidation is affirmed; no further evidentiary proceeding or replacement ceiling is ordered.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1:** Applied to a mandatory candidate-expenditure ceiling; its contribution/expenditure distinction and rejection of cost and equalization grounds remain effective.
- **Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC, June 26, 1996:** Applied for its actual-transaction distinction; its independent-party-expression holding is not enlarged into a ruling on every coordinated expenditure.
- **Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel, September 14, 1998:** Distinguished because its receipt and intermediary controls regulate different transactions; their governing rules remain intact.
- **Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce, 494 U.S. 652:** Its distinct corporate-treasury rationale does not authorize this all-candidate ceiling; no broader treatment of Austin is adopted.
- **NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones, November 5, 1998:** Its vacatur leaves the lower financing merits unavailable as a completed holding; it decides neither the validity of this ceiling nor a wealth-primary theory.

## Law After Decision

The existing distinction between contributions and expenditures governs this municipal ceiling. Cincinnati cannot enforce the challenged limit. No general contribution, disclosure or voluntary-financing rule changes, and the decision creates no compulsory empirical-testing requirement or categorical ruling on every measure addressing officeholder time.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

Merits review is complete. The affirmed judgment continues to bar enforcement of the challenged ceiling. Ordinary proceedings implementing that judgment remain governed by its terms; no further Supreme Court return or new nonroutine act is ordered.

## Holdings

**Common authority.** Stevens's Court opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg throughout. Those six directly support Parts I, III-A and III-B. O'Connor and Breyer additionally join Part II, giving that component eight full rationale votes. Thomas concurs in every invalidity judgment on the distinct grounds described below; his judgment votes are not full joins in the Court's methodology. The rule's limits accompany each component.

### I. Registered-voter condition

**Holding and operative rule.** Colorado may not require otherwise qualified resident-adult initiative circulators to be registered voters on this record. Excluding otherwise qualified speakers because they are unregistered substantially burdens protected petition circulation, while the retained identification and enforcement mechanisms serve traceability more closely.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a person need not register to vote to engage in the protected political conversation involved in circulating a petition. Meyer v. Grant protects that interaction and the available pool of speakers. Colorado's condition excludes at least 400,000 otherwise qualified but unregistered voters. Easy enrollment does not eliminate that reduction or show that registration adequately measures residence, availability for process or honesty.

The State's strongest argument treats registration as an administrable qualification in a legally operative election process. Anderson and the governing Burdick rule permit appropriately fitted administration, but require attention to the actual burden and interest. Here the sworn identifying and residential-address affidavit, signature review, subpoenas and targeted fraud sanctions address traceability without the registration exclusion. Timmons's different candidate-nomination setting does not make this speaker restriction minor. The Court invalidates the registration condition, not the age floor, circulation period or residence rule whose validity is not presented. An affidavit cannot preserve the invalid condition merely by requiring a registration recital.

### II. Personal name displayed during solicitation

**Holding and operative rule.** Colorado may not compel every circulator to display a personal name on a badge during protected face-to-face petition advocacy when the retained post-circulation identification and enforcement measures serve the asserted interests with materially less speech burden. The personal-name component is invalid; standalone paid-or-volunteer and employer-information requirements are not adjudicated.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that compelled personal attribution at the expressive encounter requires a justification fitted to that burden. McIntyre protects individual political advocacy against inadequately justified identification demands; Meyer protects petition circulation, and Riley prevents payment from stripping a solicitor of speech protection. The trial record shows deterrence from the personal badge, particularly when the subject is unpopular. That is a burden on the actual political conversation.

Even assuming fraud prevention and detection are compelling interests, Colorado has not shown the necessary fit. The identifying affidavit, signature assurances, review, protests and fraud penalties allow officials to identify and investigate circulators without exposing their names to everyone approached. The later public affidavit differs in timing and accessibility from a worn badge; the Court creates no anonymity from lawful investigation. A witness's interest in reporting misconduct does not establish the needed additional benefit of this blanket personal-name display. This holding does not decide standalone paid-status, sponsor or employer attribution, nor invalidate lawful post-circulation verification.

### III-A. Final individual-circulator reports

**Holding and operative rule.** The challenged final-report requirements publishing each paid circulator's identity, address and individual compensation fail the required substantial relationship to Colorado's asserted informational and enforcement interests on this record. Disclosure of initiative proponents and aggregate spending remains distinct; the decision creates no categorical exemption from initiative-finance disclosure.

**Controlling explanation.** Buckley v. Valeo permits campaign-finance disclosure substantially related to important governmental interests. That inquiry requires attention to what the information reveals. Proponent identity and aggregate expenditures reveal who finances an initiative and the extent of paid efforts. The individual messenger is not, for that reason, a financing source or a candidate contributor. Colorado has not demonstrated the added benefit necessary to justify publishing every paid circulator's identity and compensation, without a threshold separating consequential financing from any paid messenger.

The State argues that payee information permits tracing and checking spending beyond aggregate totals. That is a legitimate subject of inquiry, but the retained sponsor, aggregate, amount-per-signature and verification mechanisms make the asserted incremental need insufficient here. Final reporting is less immediate than a badge; it still requires its own disclosure justification. Kentucky Right to Life's payer-identification rule concerns a different financing function and does not answer this demand for every messenger's information. A narrower individual-reporting rule supported under the governing scrutiny remains open.

### III-B. Monthly individual-circulator reports

**Holding and operative rule.** Colorado's challenged monthly requirements publishing each paid circulator's identity, residential and business addresses and individual amounts paid or owed are likewise invalid on this record. The Court decides that individual-information package, not the independent validity of every other monthly-report field.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court separately considers publication during the circulation campaign. Timely information can expose abuse and inform voters before qualification; that argument gives monthly reporting work that a final report cannot always perform. But timing does not cure the insufficient relationship between identifying each messenger and the State's demonstrated financing or enforcement need. It also exposes the individual while the political effort is underway. The retained sponsor and aggregate disclosures and enforcement mechanisms remain material alternatives. The decision does not hold that referendum disclosure has no informational value or that every individual challenge requires proof of reprisals.

The Tenth Circuit invalidated the monthly subsection as a whole. The Court affirms the reviewed invalidity of individual circulator disclosures and leaves the independent validity of its other prescriptions, including proponents and measure identity, undecided. It neither orders those fields conclusively reinstated nor independently declares them unconstitutional. Any ordinary remedial or severability implementation must respect the existing judgment and the scope actually decided.

**Common limits and remedy.** The reviewed registration, personal-name and individual-disclosure invalidations stand. Age, circulation timing, verification affidavits, fraud and signature-review mechanisms are outside the reviewed invalidations and remain governed by their own terms. This decision does not invalidate standalone paid-status or sponsor attribution, create a replacement initiative code, grant damages, destroy public records, authorize fraud or decide every future disclosure rule. Unreviewed requirements receive no automatic new constitutional endorsement.

- **McIntyre, April 19, 1995:** Extended to the personal-name demand during live petition advocacy at the stated narrow-fit scope; candidate-finance, organizational and independent post-circulation settings retain their distinctions.
- **Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781:** Applied to protection of paid solicitation and the burden of compelled speech during the encounter; its discussion supplies no adjudication of the reserved badge fields.

- **Kentucky Right to Life, September 14, 1998:** Its payer and source-identification distinctions remain; identification of the financer does not automatically justify identifying each messenger.

## Law After Decision

Registration cannot be used to exclude otherwise qualified resident-adult circulators; personal names cannot be compelled on the challenged solicitation badge; and the reviewed final and monthly individual-disclosure packages cannot be enforced. Governing law preserves distinct authority for lawful administration, verification, fraud controls, sponsor and aggregate disclosures. The independent validity and remedial treatment of unreviewed badge and monthly fields remain outside this judgment. No fractured-opinion or narrowest-ground synthesis is needed: each Court holding has at least six direct joins, and the badge holding has eight.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Thomas agrees that every reviewed restriction is invalid but applies traditional strict scrutiny to the direct speech and severe associational burdens.
