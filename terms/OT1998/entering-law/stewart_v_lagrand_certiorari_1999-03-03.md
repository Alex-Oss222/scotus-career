# Stewart v. LaGrand; No. 98-1412; distinct State application A-735.

Public reading copy from the Canonical Decision Record.

## Event

*Stewart v. LaGrand*, No. 98-1412. Certiorari grant, March 3, 1999. On petition for a writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. The State challenges the judgment enjoining Walter LaGrand's execution by lethal gas. The Ninth Circuit granted a certificate and the gas-specific injunction while separately denying a general stay of execution. No oral argument preceded this action. The State's application A-735 is distinct.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The petition for a writ of certiorari is granted to consider whether the gas-specific injunction may stand in light of the presented method-election, procedural-default and antecedent habeas-review questions. The grant does not encompass a new comprehensive empirical question about execution methods or authorize ordinary review of an actual §2244(b)(3) authorization decision.

## Judgment & Remedy

This petition action enters no judgment on the merits and neither grants nor denies the separate application to vacate the injunction. It creates no new restraint and vacates none.

## Opinion Topology

The Court issues an order granting review without an accompanying opinion or publicly disclosed individual poll.

## Holdings

The grant establishes no substantive holding.

## Precedent Treatment

The order changes no precedent.

## Law After Decision

The applicable habeas, certificate, procedural-default and execution-method rules remain unchanged. Taking review supplies no exception to the statutory bar on ordinary review of an actual successive-application authorization decision.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing accompanies the petition order.

## Procedure After Action

The case proceeds to further consideration of the accepted questions. The existing gas-specific injunction remains governed by its own terms. The grant sets no new briefing or argument date, adds no execution restraint and does not itself authorize a summary merits disposition. The separate State application requires its own disposition.

## Source Notes

The earlier district and appellate proceedings are reported at 883 F. Supp. 451 and 133 F.3d 1253. They distinguish the District Court's default analysis from the appellate prematurity treatment before method election. The present order supplies no additional term or duration for the later gas-specific injunction and makes no finding about the constitutionality of injection or gas.
