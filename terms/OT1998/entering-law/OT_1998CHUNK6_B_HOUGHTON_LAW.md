#### Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, 518 U.S. 938 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1691 and 95-1738.
**Decided:** July 1, 1996.

##### Whether probable cause and ready mobility require an additional federal exigency

**Holding:** Police may search a readily mobile automobile without a warrant when probable cause supports belief that it contains evidence or contraband; the Fourth Amendment requires no additional showing of unforeseen circumstances, imminent departure or insufficient time to obtain a warrant. Police custody, the ability to guard the vehicle or an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant does not itself impose that extra condition. The object sought limits the search to places and containers capable of containing it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** Carroll/Chambers supply automobile mobility; Carney adds reduced regulated-vehicle privacy; Johns preserves search authority during custody, with its separate unreasonable-delay limitation. Ross ties the permissible search to its object.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent authority to enter a home or protected curtilage, search an immobile structure as an automobile, omit probable cause or exceed object-based scope. Different residential uses and protected-premises access retain their own rules.

##### How that rule applies to the two reviewed searches

**Holding:** The accepted probable cause and adjudicated automobile circumstances bring these searches within the exception for purposes of the federal additional-exigency ground on which the judgments rest. The Court reverses that ground in each case and remands; its application is neither an unconditional approval of every entry and search act nor an instruction merely to reconsider whether the rejected urgency requirement should remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** Labron’s accepted probable cause arose from drug transactions and retrievals from an ordinary street-parked Lincoln. Its failed start on attempted impoundment and highway-legal exterior establish neither a running engine nor known pre-search incapacity. Kilgore’s accepted probable cause arose from the controlled purchase and truck connection; private parking, police custody and available guards do not add a federal urgency requirement. Lack of consent or visible contraband does not defeat an otherwise applicable automobile exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general known-mechanical-incapacity rule, lawful boundary-crossing finding, curtilage classification, conversion of house consent into truck consent, new consent/access hearing or revived Labron privacy-interest objection.

##### What the reversal and remand permit

**Holding:** Each judgment is reversed, and each case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's automobile and jurisdictional holdings. Pennsylvania may apply an actually adequate and independent state ground if properly available, and may address genuinely remaining scope or other distinct issues under its procedures; this mandate establishes neither their availability nor their merits and directs no automatic admission or prosecution outcome.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** The exception is applied to the actual reviewed additional-exigency issue, not left wholly undecided. Distinct genuinely remaining scope, access and independent state-law issues are subject to ordinary procedural predicates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new preservation, waiver, admission of evidence, conviction, retrial or automatic hearing is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Certiorari granted in each case; both suppression judgments summarily reversed and remanded, 7–2, in a reasoned decision without plenary argument. The undisclosed petition poll is not a merits tally. For jurisdictional holdings, see Federal Courts — Finality and adequate independent state grounds, these cases, July 1, 1996.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn:** The Court applies the third practical-finality category to these binding suppression determinations and the State's inability to obtain later effective review. No general interlocutory-review rule is added.
- **Michigan v. Long:** Its federal/interwoven-ground inquiry is applied separately to Labron and Kilgore. A State's power to afford greater protection remains, but possible state authority is not proof of an actual independent judgment.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Its Long holding remains controlling. Its separate exclusionary-rule exception and unresolved clerical-error attribution do not authorize these searches or require the same factual remand in a different case.
- **Sochor and Middlebrooks:** Their actually sufficient independent preservation and state constitutional grounds remain respected; the Court distinguishes them from the grounds under review here.
- **Carroll v. United States and Chambers v. Maroney:** The established automobile exception is applied without a new case-specific warrant-impracticability requirement. Police control does not by itself require a different federal rule.
- **California v. Carney:** Mobility and reduced automobile privacy support the rule; its actual setting and reservation concerning differently situated residential uses remain. It creates no general authority to enter protected property.
- **United States v. Johns:** Custody alone does not eliminate vehicle-search authority. The Court does not remove Johns's separate protection against unreasonable delay or decide a delay claim here.
- **United States v. Ross:** Probable cause's object determines the permitted places and containers to search; it is not a license for a search beyond that object.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Independent review of ultimate constitutional probability questions, clear-error protection for historical facts and due weight to grounded inferences remain. The case supplies no new fact, waiver or probable-cause presumption.
- **Whren v. United States:** Objective authority for the initial stop remains separate from search, manner, scope and duration. Its unanimity does not supply an additional vote or factual premise here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas and Pinson:** The distinct protections governing dwelling entry and the particular technological examination of domestic information remain. Neither is a general automobile rule or establishes Kilgore's property boundary or lawful access.

No prior decision is overruled. The Court applies the inherited automobile exception to reject the added federal condition; it does not adopt a new general mechanical-defect, curtilage or premises-consent rule.

#### United States v. Wellons

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-496.
**Decided:** May 30, 1995.

##### Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement

**Holding:** Absence from a rental agreement does not by itself defeat a driver’s Fourth Amendment privacy claim when he possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud or comparable wrongful possession. Courts must evaluate the actual legitimate relationship, including the right to exclude, the owner’s retained rights and any effective termination of possession; renter permission alone does not establish privacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined in this proposition by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rental company's impoundment request may bear on whether possession remained legitimate at search time; its effect is not found here. Permission alone, title alone or the contract's name list alone does not settle the individual's privacy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rakas v. Illinois, 439 U.S. 128: applies its requirement of the claimant’s own legitimate interest, rejecting proxy rights while refusing a contract-name shortcut.
- Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies the circumstance-specific privacy inquiry; possession or property alone is not conclusive.
- Minnesota v. Olson, 495 U.S. 91: extends its recognition that an invited user can have personal privacy without title to this bounded inquiry; it supplies no automatic rental-driver privacy.
- United States v. Padilla, May 3, 1993: applies the individualized search/seizure inquiry; no automatic derivative rights are created.

##### Independent privacy in closed personal luggage

**Holding:** A person’s lack of vehicle privacy does not by itself extinguish a legitimate expectation of privacy in that person’s closed luggage inside the vehicle. Ownership or lawful control and measures preserving privacy must be established independently, and a valid warrant exception reaching the container may still authorize its search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined on this separate proposition by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Each claimed container requires its own ownership or lawful-control and privacy inquiry, including any disclaimer. The Court makes no ownership or container-specific probable-cause finding and does not restore Sanders's displaced automobile warrant rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Chadwick, 433 U.S. 1: preserves personal closed-luggage privacy as distinct from vehicle mobility, subject to the later applicable automobile rule.
- Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies the requirement of a demonstrated personal privacy interest; ownership or lawful control and privacy measures require assessment.
- California v. Acevedo, 500 U.S. 565: preserves automobile probable-cause authority to search containers within its scope; personal container privacy does not impose a categorical warrant requirement.
- Arkansas v. Sanders, 442 U.S. 753: retains container privacy only consistently with Acevedo’s displacement of its contrary automobile warrant rule; that displaced rule is not revived.

##### Remedy after rejection of the categorical grounds

**Holding:** When the reviewed categorical privacy grounds are erroneous but personal interests and preserved search justifications remain unresolved, the judgment must be vacated and remanded for those determinations before a suppression consequence is imposed. A recognized interest does not foreclose lawful probable-cause search, consent, standardized noninvestigatory inventory after lawful custody, or another preserved warrant exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt this case’s remedial holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The company's request is not itself lawful impoundment or consent to search every personal bag. A dog alert does not establish legality of the preceding detention or sniff or unlimited probable-cause scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California v. Acevedo, 500 U.S. 565: applies the distinction between an interest and a lawful probable-cause search reaching a container; no automatic suppression follows recognition of privacy.
- United States v. Padilla, May 3, 1993: applies its individual-interest remand discipline without deciding unproved exception predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the conviction affirmance and remand for the claimant's legitimate vehicle possession and personal closed-luggage interests, including permission, theft, fraud, comparable wrongful possession or effective repossession. Determine lawful impoundment; detention and dog-sniff legality and consequences; probable cause and container scope; lawful consent and scope; standardized noninvestigatory inventory; or another preserved warrant exception. Suppression depends on supported findings and law, with ordinary harmlessness if applicable. No acquittal, automatic exclusion, new exclusionary exception or resentencing is ordered.

#### Maryland v. Wilson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1268.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Holding:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to the stop. This authority concerns the exit direction itself; it does not establish categorical forcible detention for the entire stop, permission to frisk or search, or power to prolong detention after the lawful traffic business ends.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether every passenger is seized at the initial stop or may be forcibly held throughout it. The observed movements and nervousness were not preserved as an independent justification; review does not cure that omission.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Removing the categorical individualized-justification requirement does not direct admission of the cocaine, reinstate a conviction or eliminate independent Maryland protections.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mimms's safety-and-incremental-intrusion analysis extends to the limited passenger-exit command. Terry, Brignoni-Ponce, Whren and Robinette retain their distinct intrusion, duration and search limits. Long and the Rakas concurrence are not treated as already deciding this issue; Ornelas supplies no universal review standard.

### Privacy in a rented vehicle and personal luggage

**Governing question:** Fourth Amendment interests of an unlisted driver and in separately protected closed luggage.

**Current rule:** Absence from the rental agreement does not alone defeat privacy where the driver possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud or comparable wrongful possession. The actual legitimate relationship, right to exclude, owner’s retained rights and effective termination of possession must be assessed; permission alone does not establish privacy. Lack of vehicle privacy does not extinguish an independently established legitimate interest in the driver’s closed luggage, supported by ownership or lawful control and measures preserving privacy.

**Authority by component:**

- Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement: Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement, 1995-05-30. Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined in this proposition by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt it.
- Independent privacy in closed personal luggage: Independent privacy in closed personal luggage, 1995-05-30. Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined on this separate proposition by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt it.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-30; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** A recognized interest does not defeat probable-cause search within its container scope, lawful consent, standardized noninvestigatory inventory after lawful custody or another established warrant exception. Effective repossession and wrongful possession remain material. Suppression follows only after the personal interests and preserved justifications are resolved, not automatically from rejection of the rental-list rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement; Independent privacy in closed personal luggage.

### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Governing question:** Whether an officer needs passenger-specific suspicion to direct a passenger to leave a lawfully stopped vehicle.

**Current rule:** An officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to a lawful traffic stop. The authority concerns the exit direction itself and supplies no categorical power to detain forcibly for the entire stop, frisk, search or prolong detention beyond the lawful traffic business.

**Authority by component:**

- Maryland v. Wilson — Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop, 1997-02-19; Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six), extends the limited Mimms exit-direction rule

**Present operation effective:** Maryland v. Wilson, 1997-02-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other seizure, search, frisk, duration and independent state-law requirements retain their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Maryland v. Wilson (1997-02-19) — Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop.

### Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

**Governing question:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause despite another investigative purpose.

**Current rule:** Objective probable cause of a traffic violation supports an ordinary initial stop notwithstanding an unrelated investigative aim. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce that violation absent that aim, or on compliance with customary assignment and enforcement policies. Manner, scope and duration remain independently constrained: a materially greater detention or search requires independent grounds. This rule does not remove purpose-specific conditions from inventory and administrative exceptions; inventories retain standardized criteria or established routine and genuine inventory purposes rather than investigatory rummaging. Purposeful racial selection remains independently prohibited by Equal Protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective initial-stop rule: Objective traffic probable cause supports this ordinary initial stop; independent execution and discrimination limits: Policy, execution and discrimination limits, 1996-06-10; Thomas’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Whren, 1996-06-10.

**Open question:** No discovery entitlement, immunity, suppression consequence or damages remedy for discriminatory selection is decided. Armstrong’s federal-charging threshold is not transferred to stop claims; traffic probable cause does not authorize every later intrusion.

**Holdings navigation:** Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806.
