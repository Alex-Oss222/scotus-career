# Federal Republic of Germany, Chrobog and Rudolph v. United States and Hull; No. 127, Original; A-736.

Public reading copy from the Canonical Decision Record.

## Event

*Federal Republic of Germany, Chrobog and Rudolph v. United States and Hull*, No. 127, Original; A-736. Original leave and interim order, March 3, 1999. The motion and accompanying requests were submitted directly to the Court; there was no oral argument and no judgment below.

Germany and its Ambassador and Consul General seek leave to file an original complaint and to prevent Walter LaGrand's execution under the ICJ's March 3 provisional-measures order. They request protection through the international case and, independently, a short interval for consideration of this Court's power and original leave. The March 3 measure is distinct from the acting ICJ President's March 2 communication. The United States and Governor Hull are distinct defendants; the operative prospective prayer concerns Hull.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated. Kennedy is the Ninth Circuit Justice; the original filing was directed to the full Court.

## Public Action

The request to dispense with printing is granted. The requested preliminary injunction and temporary restraining order, including the independent request for temporary protection pending consideration, are denied. The motion for leave to file remains under consideration.

The foregoing order records the action of the Court. The participating Justices' positions and the reasons they adopt are set out below. Certified for issuance: Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela, Chief Justice.

## Holdings

**Operative rule.** The present submission does not sufficiently establish the particular domestic right, ongoing duty, original enforcement route and available remedy needed for either the requested injunction through the ICJ case or the independent short restraint. The Court denies those requests without deciding final original leave, ultimate domestic treaty enforceability or the international force of provisional measures.

**Authority.** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's signed explanation, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies a direct five-Justice majority for this interim conclusion.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court considers the asserted new obligation on its own terms. The March 3 ICJ order is not merely a continuing effect of an old failure to notify consular officials. The actual diplomatic plaintiffs give the original-jurisdiction argument substance, and the Governor's alleged execution-related decision raises a concrete officer-connection question. Neither point completes the domestic enforcement chain. The international undertaking, its domestic judicial operation, an action by these plaintiffs, and the remedy against the named defendant remain distinct requirements. The United States' presence as a defendant supplies no consent to this action.

*Paraguay/In re Breard* requires a sufficient present domestic showing for an original-action injunction while reserving final leave and ultimate enforceability. *Seminole Tribe* and *Coeur d'Alene* preserve prospective relief against an officer actually connected to an ongoing federal violation; they do not supply the treaty duty or remedy asserted here. *Blue Fox* requires consent appropriate to the remedy against the United States, and *Aktepe* requires examination of a manageable duty rather than categorical refusal because foreign affairs are involved. Those inquiries cannot be replaced by comity, diplomatic importance or irreversible harm. The same deficient showing prevents the requested short protection; the Court does not adopt a general prohibition on protection while substantial authority is examined. The allegations concerning recent discovery also prevent treating all delay as unexplained. No final declaration against every possible domestic enforcement action is entered.

## Precedent Treatment

*Paraguay/In re Breard* is applied at its limited interim-showing scope; its reservations of original leave and ultimate treaty enforceability remain intact. *Seminole Tribe* and *Coeur d'Alene* retain their connected-officer prospective-relief rules, without creating this treaty remedy. *Blue Fox* retains its separation of substantive duty and consent to a particular federal remedy. *Aktepe* retains its claim-specific inquiry into a judicially manageable duty. *Shipp* and *United Mine Workers*, concerning protection while authority is determined, are not overruled or converted into a general entitlement to additional time. Section 2283's express-authorization, necessary-in-aid and judgment-protection exceptions remain distinct; the Court adopts no new majority construction of them here.

## Law After Decision

The decision resolves only the present requests for protection. No final rule of ICJ unenforceability, categorical immunity from treaty obligations, foreign-sovereign exclusion from prospective officer relief, or general exception to statutory injunction limits results. Original leave and the ultimate domestic enforcement questions remain open.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, Part II.** The Chief would decline this nonexclusive original action. He recognizes the actual diplomats' Article III and §1251(b)(1) category for considering leave and Hull's alleged concrete connection, but finds no established domestic command enforceable by the requested remedy. Article 94 addresses the member state's undertaking to the ICJ; the Optional Protocol identifies the international tribunal. Neither specifies this domestic action or makes the provisional measure a judicial stay binding the Governor. Article VI does not itself answer enforceability through this remedy, and the ICJ's reference to measures within national power does not enlarge that power. The United States has not consented to this distinct domestic suit. These grounds concern this pleading and remedy, leave the international obligation unresolved and do not foreclose a different properly authorized claim.

**Scalia, additional concurrence.** O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas join his conclusion that the Court can assess the present interim showing without an additional authority-preserving interval. The loss of the requested remedy does not by itself establish that extra protection is necessary to determine power. Scalia alone also requires a sufficiently clear basis for the affirmative original injunction; his stronger formulation commands no other join and creates no additional Court holding.

**Stevens, dissent, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** These Justices would restrain Hull and those actually acting under her authority until 5 p.m. Eastern Standard Time on March 5, 1999 or an earlier Court order, with earlier vacation upon determination that the relevant jurisdiction or injunctive power is absent. *Shipp* and *United Mine Workers* permit necessary preservation during a substantial inquiry into authority; *Pennsylvania Bureau* keeps §1651 auxiliary. Assuming §2283 applies, they find its necessary-in-aid exception satisfied by this particular new preservation duty, real diplomatic-party original action, concretely connected officer and finite inquiry. They do not rely on short duration or gravity alone, adopt automatic domestic treaty enforcement, or grant protection through the ICJ merits. Their proposed interval requires no new filing or hearing and carries no automatic extension.

## Procedure After Action

The present restraint requests are denied. Original leave remains pending for further consideration, without a fixed response or decision deadline. The proposed March 5 endpoint in the dissent creates no operative restraint or expiration. The order neither commands execution nor changes the separate Stewart application or any other restraint.
