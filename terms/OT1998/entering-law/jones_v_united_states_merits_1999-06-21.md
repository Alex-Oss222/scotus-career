## Event

Jones v. United States, No. 97-9361. Argued February 22, 1999; decided June 21, 1999. Certiorari to the Fifth Circuit's January 5, 1998 judgment affirming the federal kidnapping conviction and death sentence. This is the capital-sentencing case, distinct from the March 24 carjacking decision. The questions concern the requested deadlock instruction, the delivered sentencing choices and forms, and the nonstatutory aggravator and correction issues.

The sentencing jury found two statutory and two nonstatutory aggravators, rejected other proposed aggravators including future dangerousness, and found numerous mitigating circumstances. The charge required unanimity, said death was never mandatory and protected individual conscience, but directed nonunanimity toward a unanimous life-without-release form or a lesser-sentence form even though this kidnapping offense authorized no punishment below life without release. No actual deadlock or jury question occurred.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment sustaining the death sentence and the death sentence, and remands for a new penalty proceeding. The conviction remains intact.

## Judgment & Remedy

Sentence vacated and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm.

If the Government pursues death, the new penalty proceeding must accurately state the lawful alternatives and employ lawful selection procedures. A lawful nondeath disposition remains available. The Court orders no release, automatic life sentence, new guilt trial or execution timetable. A new hearing after reversible sentencing error is distinct from convening another jury merely because the first deadlocked.

## Holdings

### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

When completed penalty deliberations end without a unanimous recommendation, §3594's otherwise clause requires the judge to impose a lawful nondeath sentence. For this death-resulting kidnapping the alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C) does not authorize another attempt to obtain death solely because the jury deadlocked.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I-A has eight direct votes from all Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without joining that rationale.

Section 3593(e) requires a unanimous jury recommendation. Section 3594 directs the judge to impose recommended death or life without release and otherwise to impose an authorized lesser sentence. The Government's construction would confine otherwise to a unanimous lesser recommendation and use good-cause discharge to repeat the penalty hearing. That reading denies operative effect to the nondeath command when the jury cannot agree. Good-cause discharge has independent work, including disqualification, without treating failure of agreement itself as authorization for another death effort. Section 1201's offense-specific penalty fixes the available nondeath sentence; §3593's general reference to other lesser sentences creates none for this offense.

The Government's concession that this offense permits only death or life does not itself concede the deadlock question; the Court rejects the contrary new-jury construction on the statutes. No actual deadlock occurred. Reconsideration after reversible sentencing error under §3593(b)(2)(D) remains distinct and supports the present new-hearing mandate.

### There is no universal deadlock script, but a false sentencing choice must be corrected here

Neither the Eighth Amendment nor a supervisory rule requires a fixed deadlock-consequence instruction in every capital case. In this case, however, the preserved request for an accurate account of the lawful consequence required correction of the charge's materially misleading below-life alternative; refusing that correction requires a new penalty proceeding.

**Authority:** Part I-B's rejection of a universal script is unanimous. Part II's case-specific corrective duty and remedy are directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Court does not adopt every constitutional or plain-error reason stated in the separate concurrence.

Jurors ordinarily should deliberate and make their own punishment choice. Congress prescribed no mandatory deadlock script, and Simmons's parole-ineligibility protection does not dictate one. But this jury was choosing punishment, unlike Shannon's ordinary guilt jury, and it had to receive an accurate description of the lawful alternatives. Section 1201 allowed only death or life without release. Form C expressly required unanimity for life, while the direction toward C or D on nonunanimity and D's lesser option could communicate that failure to agree on life would permit the judge to impose a punishment below life.

The initial unanimity direction, optional death, conscience protection and instruction not to consider the judge's later sentence are considered together. They do not correct the specific false statutory choice created by that combination. Instruction 5 and repeated references to its accurate core identified the need for correction, notwithstanding counsel's imperfect lesser-sentence wording. The Court does not require the request verbatim, a nonunanimous jury life verdict or a reasonable-doubt burden for the ultimate moral choice. The shared ruling addresses the preserved refusal and the actual charge. A new accurately instructed penalty proceeding is necessary if death remains sought; the ruling rests on objective directions and findings, not juror affidavits or an invented deliberation inquiry.

### Independent aggravator validity and cure are not resolved for the Court

The instruction disposition removes this death sentence. No majority resolves whether factors 3(B) and 3(C) were independently invalid or harmless: O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas find contextual validity; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg find the specific formulations defective; Scalia, Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve validity. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would find narrowly assumed drafting error harmless; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject that cure, while Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve it.

**Authority:** These accounts identify the limits of the Court's decision, not additional factor holdings. No Marks synthesis supplies a validity or cure rule from the divided positions.

Any new proceeding remains governed by Tuilaepa's understandable relevant selection subjects and the requirement of a genuine lawful cure for an actually established invalid factor under Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and §3595. Overlap is not automatically unconstitutional, relevant victim-impact evidence is not categorically forbidden, and remaining valid eligibility does not by itself cure an independent selection error. The Court creates no new factor test, appellate moral-reweighing power or permission to repeat misleading instructions.

## Precedent Treatment

- Simmons v. South Carolina: preserves the effective opportunity to understand the only lawful nondeath alternative in its stated setting; supplies no universal deadlock script.
- Shannon v. United States: distinguishes an ordinary nonsentencing guilt jury; its allocation rule does not excuse an affirmatively misleading capital choice.
- Olano and Johnson: retain separate error, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary conditions. The four-Justice concurrence's alternative application changes no Court-wide rule and does not decide Johnson's reserved initially unsettled-law setting.
- Boyde, Mills and McKoy: preserve contextual examination of the charge and individual mitigation; no verdict-form typography alone decides the case.
- Tuilaepa/Proctor and Payne: retain understandable relevant individualized selection and permissible impact evidence within their limits; no factor-validity majority arises here.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa, Clemons and Chapman: preserve the distinction between valid eligibility, authorized reweighing and actual harmless-error correction. No new general cure or appellate-resentencing authority is adopted.
- Neder: retains its qualified actual-verdict inquiry; the divided factor applications supply no new universal capital harmlessness rule.
- Strickler: preserves distinct initial Brady materiality for guilt and selection; its burden and record do not decide this preserved instruction issue.

## Law After Decision

Actual federal sentencing deadlock invokes §3594's lawful nondeath command, with no replacement jury solely to seek death after that deadlock. There is no universal deadlock script. The specific misleading statutory choice in this charge, identified by the preserved corrective request, requires a new penalty proceeding. The conviction remains intact. The separate constitutional, plain-error, factor-validity and factor-cure applications retain only their stated voting support; existing capital-selection and review rules otherwise continue unchanged.

## Separate Writings

Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg in Part I, finds the requested correction preserved and the false below-life alternative constitutionally material. The Government cannot prove noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt where the actual penalty choice was contested, numerous mitigators were found and several proposed aggravators rejected. Alternatively, each Olano condition is met: the existing two-punishment statute makes the error clear, the concrete false choice creates a reasonable probability of affecting at least one juror, and allowing that distortion to support death independently seriously impairs fairness and integrity. They do not presume prejudice from capital status, invoke internal juror accounts or resolve Johnson's unsettled-trial-law reservation. Their four-Justice reasoning supports the Court's remedy without becoming its complete common rationale.

In Part II, Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg additionally find the two separately submitted personal-characteristic factors overlapping and inadequately definite. The actual vulnerability and family-loss argument strengthens the contrary reading but did not make that distinction obligatory; an instruction against counting headings does not remove extra weight authorized by repetitive factors. They reject direct cure because counsel's descriptions and the findings do not prove noncontribution of that overlapping authorization. They permit relevant impact evidence and would require proper §3595 correction if instruction relief were unavailable, without automatic life or a new appellate moral choice. Stone-Zsela and Breyer do not join this factor disposition.

Thomas, joined by O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy in Parts I and III, would affirm. Read as a whole, the initial unanimity rule, available life-without-release choice, optional death and conscience protection prevent a reasonable likelihood of the asserted inference. The C/D direction concerns recommendations and does not compel a genuinely divided jury to choose an unwanted sentence; foreperson-only signatures also recorded expressly unanimous factors. They distinguish the preserved refusal from forfeited delivered-charge objections and, assuming plain error, find no demonstrated substantial-rights effect. They need not decide unsettled-law timing or the fourth condition.

Those four also find narrowly assumed loose-drafting factor error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. The actual vulnerability and personal-loss explanations, separately described subjects, express findings and qualitative weighing instruction establish, in their assessment, the lawful distinct matters the jury actually considered; precise wording would have stated that understanding without changing the choice. This is an inference about noncontribution under the Government's burden, not a new finding of private thoughts, exclusive argument, overwhelming eligibility or an appellate choice of death. A different forbidden use or conflicting presentation would require separate analysis. In Part II, O’Connor and Kennedy join Thomas in additionally finding the factors valid in context under Tuilaepa, §3593(a) and Payne. Scalia reserves validity and relies on assumed-error cure.

## Procedure After Action

The death sentence and appellate judgment sustaining it are vacated. The case returns for a new lawful penalty proceeding if the Government continues to seek death, or a lawful nondeath disposition. The kidnapping conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed; the mandate orders neither release nor an automatic sentence or execution date.
