## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois

#### Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-783.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Program-wide participation opportunity

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies lawful delegated interpretation; authorizes no quota contrary to statute.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144: applies reasonable regulatory interpretation within its terms.

##### Independent constitutional scrutiny

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applies intermediate scrutiny to sex-conscious treatment.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applies genuine-objective and connected-disadvantage requirements.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547: its racial-treatment analysis supplies no changed sex standard or federal-compliance immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the University on the distinct Title IX and equal-protection claims. No reinstatement, damages or remand is ordered, and no broad section 1983 preclusion rule is adopted.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The inherited federal remedial standard sustains this challenge

**Holding:** Under Metro Broadcasting, a benign federal racial classification supported by Congress must serve an important governmental objective and be substantially related to that objective; Fullilove retains its supported judgment recognizing Congress's remedial authority at the scope actually sustained there. Applying that meaningful review of purpose, fit and burdens, the Court affirms the judgment rejecting this preserved challenge to the subcontractor compensation arrangement, without deciding every certification route or conferring a general exemption on measures called benign.

**Proposition-level authority:** Merits portion of Justice Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this rule and bounded application, not merely the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Metro's actual federal standard and Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): retains the important-objective/substantial-relationship standard for benign federal classifications at its actual scope.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains its supported remedial judgment and congressional-competence recognition; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): distinguished as state/local remedial classification law; its standard is not displaced or extended to erase Metro here.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to evidence, remedial fit and burdens; remedial purpose alone supplies no exemption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment rejecting the preserved constitutional challenge, 5–4. No remand, program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract follows.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Bragdon v. Abbott

#### Bragdon v. Abbott

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-156.
**Decided:** 1998-06-25.

##### HIV can impair before visible symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity

**Holding:** HIV disease is a physical impairment within the ADA before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. A claimant under the actual-disability branch must still show that the impairment substantially limits that individual's activity; diagnosis or reproductive-system impairment alone does not establish every element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the disability part. The other three accept or assume impairment for this case without joining its general rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual disability still requires an individual substantial limitation. No record-of or regarded-as holding, categorical HIV entitlement, or restriction of major activities to public, economic or daily activity is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arline's disease and individualized-assessment principles are applied within the ADA; Yeskey's distinction between coverage and individual entitlement remains.

##### Abbott's own reproductive activity is substantially limited on this record

**Holding:** Abbott's specific unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision not to bear a child, considered with the supported perinatal transmission danger, establishes substantial limitation of her reproduction. Complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, an attempted pregnancy or willingness to accept the danger is not an additional condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). The same six-Justice disability coalition directly adopts this application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The historical untreated and treated transmission estimates are evidence, not numerical legal thresholds or current medical findings. The case leaves the general treatment of mitigating measures open. Ordinary summary-judgment burdens remain, without a required attempted pregnancy, earlier childbearing plan or willingness to expose a child to danger.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The ordinary individual-definition and Rule 56 requirements remain; neither institutional coverage nor a diagnosis substitutes for the supported personal limitation.

##### Direct threat requires objective individualized assessment, not professional sincerity

**Holding:** A covered provider may exclude a person only for a significant risk to others' health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment based on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing the risk's nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation; sincere belief or professional status supplies no special legal deference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices directly support this common objective method.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relevant information is that reasonably available on September 16, 1994. Neither absolute safety, past confirmed transmission nor numerical quantification is universally required. The hospital offer supplies no independent defense or duty to accept extra charges; no comparative-safety theory was pursued or established. Competence limits and objectively justified precautions remain. No later breakthrough is found, and its possible relevance to prospective relief is not categorically excluded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arline's individualized inquiry is applied; Daubert and Joiner retain their foundation and fit roles without becoming substantive risk or automatic trial rules.

##### Public-health authority has founded, nonconclusive weight; the cited sources require bounded reconsideration

**Holding:** Reasonable medical judgments of public-health authorities receive special but nonconclusive weight, subject to a credible scientific challenge. That weight attaches only to a supported judgment within the source's actual role, date and scope; a private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment. The direct-threat judgment is vacated for reconsideration of the CDC and Association materials on those terms, without deciding whole-record triability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the public-health/source-remand part. Stone-Zsela joins the objective method and remand judgment, not this distinct ground; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas support the risk judgment on their contrary official-weight approach.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The CDC recommendation did not expressly determine residual risk or the need for further measures; the Association policy's private status requires attention to its scientific foundation. No source is declared worthless, no zero/significant-risk finding is made, and no whole-record trial issue is reached. The circuit may again enter summary judgment; no hearing, supplementation, expert appointment or treatment order is commanded. State-law merits remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arline's founded public-health weight is preserved; Joiner's source/inference discipline is applied within the granted question, leaving independent whole-record sufficiency undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The disability determination is affirmed. The direct-threat portion is vacated and returned through the First Circuit for reconsideration under the objective method and controlling source distinctions. The lower court determines the consequence for the judgment without a commanded trial, new evidence, hearing or treatment. State-law claims remain subject to ordinary lower proceedings without a new substantive state-law ruling.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County

#### Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County

**Docket or dockets:** 97-1115 and 97-1160.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### The local racial classifications remain subject to strict scrutiny

**Holding:** Local racial and ethnic contracting classifications must rest on a supported compelling justification and narrowly fitted means. General social inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not establish that justification; a supported local system of exclusion in which the County participates can permit fitted repair without invariably requiring a prior formal judgment of liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part II has all eight Associates for strict scrutiny and the federal/local distinction. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the affirmative remedial proposition; Scalia reserves the permissible extent of racial remediation beyond the grounds needed to reject these programs. Stone-Zsela does not join this local rule.

**Material application:** City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. governs local contracting preferences, and Hopwood v. Texas preserves supported justification, individual equality and narrow fit for state racial classifications. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña sustained its distinct federal arrangement under Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC's important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Adarand expressly preserved Croson; its federal rule cannot be transferred to Dade County merely because the County describes its purpose as corrective. The Court declines the proposed replacement of local strict scrutiny. This decision creates neither a right to proportionate contracts nor a prohibition on every properly supported local repair program.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Croson remains controlling for local racial classifications.
- Hopwood remains controlling for state racial classifications and fitted repair.
- Adarand retains its bounded federal application; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.
- Metro Broadcasting retains the different federal standard; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.

##### The BBE and HBE programs fail the independent narrow-fit requirement

**Holding:** Before using these racial and ethnic contracting preferences, the County must seriously consider reasonably available race-neutral measures responsive to the exclusion it identifies and provide a supported explanation of their inadequacy. This record does not satisfy that requirement for either BBE or HBE; it requires considered alternatives, not trial and exhaustion of every conceivable measure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part III has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand under a different proposed local rule. Four Associates also find the racial evidence insufficient, but that additional ground does not control.

**Material application:** Croson's narrow-fit requirement and Hopwood's fitted-repair principle require attention to measures addressing the actual problem. The record identifies procurement information, financing, bonding, payment and discriminatory-treatment obstacles. The County's limited technical and financial assistance and conclusory treatment of alternatives do not show serious assessment of responsive neutral measures; HBE lacks such consideration. Flexibility, waivers, annual review and five-year continuation review cannot supply the missing inquiry. The possibility that an existing BBE program reduced observed disparities matters to evidence, but cannot cure this distinct tailoring defect. The Court therefore need not decide whether the combined racial evidence would otherwise establish a sufficient remedial predicate. It does not constitutionalize a statistical-significance threshold, numerical parity, a requirement of a prior discrimination judgment or a duty to exhaust every possible neutral course.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Croson applies to the unperformed alternatives inquiry without making corrective contracting preferences categorically invalid.
- Hopwood applies its fitted-repair principle to that inquiry without categorical invalidation of corrective preferences.

##### The WBE program fails the distinct intermediate-scrutiny inquiry on this record

**Holding:** A sex-conscious contracting measure requires an actual important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the classification, with a demanding governmental burden. On independent assessment of the accepted findings and combined evidence, the County has not adequately substantiated the identified gender exclusion needed to justify this three-sector WBE preference; its invalidation is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). All nine accept the stated intermediate standard. Part IV's application and affirmance have five direct votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate and remand for a different independent assessment of the combined record.

**Material application:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees, Klinger v. Department of Corrections and United States v. Virginia require actual important purposes and a substantial relationship, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. The Court independently examines the constitutional sufficiency of the evidence while retaining subsidiary findings not shown clearly erroneous. Firm-size adjustments largely explain award-dollar differences; the surviving WBE SIC 15 disparity, women's entry disparity and accounts of refusing to deal with female owners matter but do not adequately justify this program's allocation across all three construction sectors. The subcontract denominator and broader marketplace pool do not reliably identify the relevant procurement comparison. The conclusion does not rest merely on calling the trial judge's ultimate constitutional view permissible. Nor does it impose a numerical minimum or rule out sufficient nonstatistical proof. The court of appeals rejected strict racial tailoring for WBE and found its flexibility capable of satisfying substantial relationship if the evidentiary predicate existed. The present failure is that predicate, not the racial alternatives defect, an excessive 11 percent goal, or an obligation to exhaust neutral measures. A later supported and differently designed women's program is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kelley retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejects stereotyped or merely numerical justification; it supplies no strict-scrutiny or statistical-only prerequisite.
- Klinger retains that distinct intermediate standard and actual-justification requirement, without a statistical-only prerequisite.
- Virginia retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejection of stereotyped justification, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means.

##### The existing injunctions remain confined to these programs

**Holding:** The judgments and permanent injunctions against the challenged BBE, HBE and WBE programs are affirmed on their respective grounds. The relief addresses the operative programs and established likely future unequal exclusion; it neither prohibits unexamined future corrective measures nor awards a contract, damages or an individual discrimination judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part V has the eight-Associate coalition for BBE/HBE and the five-Justice WBE coalition identified above. Stone-Zsela would maintain the racial restraints temporarily during his proposed remand, rather than affirm their present permanent disposition.

**Material application:** Concrete future exclusion, not past injury alone, supplies a preventive basis for relief. Hopwood's independent prospective-relief requirements and Federal Election Commission v. Williams's insistence on an actual preventive foundation are satisfied by the still-operative programs and stipulated future exposure. Williams supplies no campaign-finance limitation rule here. Lawful race-neutral outreach and procurement measures remain available. No court is directed to adopt a new preference program, and the separate evidentiary bases are not merged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hopwood supplies its independent prospective-relief limits without deciding a future unexamined scheme.
- Williams supplies the actual preventive-basis requirement without deciding a future unexamined scheme.

Related questions in Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County, 1998-09-21, dockets 97-1115 and 97-1160, appear under Federal Courts — Association standing to challenge unequal bidding opportunity.

## Source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer

#### Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1340.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### The 1986 amendment does not revive this preamendment relator action

**Holding:** The 1986 amendment removing the False Claims Act's former government-possession bar does not revive a private relator action based on alleged false claims and government disclosures completed before enactment, absent sufficient congressional direction for that new consequence. Former §3730(b)(4) therefore governs this action and requires dismissal because the Government possessed the material information within that bar.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's substantive opinion and dismissal direction, unanimous; eight join formal vacatur, while Stone-Zsela would reverse.

**Material application:** Both the alleged false claims and government receipt of material information preceded October 27, 1986. Removing a complete defense expands private enforcement rather than merely reallocating the forum; the jurisdictional label and later filing date do not change that consequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Which event would control if claims and disclosures straddled enactment is unresolved. The holding decides no fraud, loss, damages, Government-filed action, prospective amended-Act claim, purely forum change or new equitable-reliance rule. Amended public-disclosure and original-source requirements are not construed; the Attorney General exception remains distinct from the original-source requirements of direct and independent knowledge plus voluntary prefiling disclosure. Excluded constitutional objections remain unaddressed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, eight votes on form, with all nine requiring dismissal under the former government-possession bar. No further merits adjudication is authorized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products: applies the concrete inquiry into statutory direction and new consequences of completed events; the general framework retains its existing scope and joins.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express: applies the need for sufficient temporal direction despite an asserted corrective or enforcement purpose; new legislation is distinct from judicial construction of existing law.

## Source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Lindh v. Murphy

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

## Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md — United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

#### United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1306.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: applies intentional relinquishment; right-specific waiver requirements remain.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies limited plain-error authority and ordinary prejudice requirement.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997, resolves the intervening-law plainness reservation where settled contrary trial law changes before direct review ends: clarity at appellate consideration suffices. Initially unsettled law, structural classification and ordinary prejudice remain reserved.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Atkinson, 297 U.S. 157: applies the independent fairness, integrity and public-reputation standard.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies discretionary, circumscribed correction.
- Sawyer, June 22, 1992: distinguished; capital actual-innocence gateway is not a universal limit on appellate correction.

##### Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone

**Holding:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 24(c) waivability, errors first clarified on appeal, outcome-independent categories, appropriate prejudice presumptions, the preserved-objection burden, and Rule 606(b) hearing limits remain undecided. Because substantial rights were not shown, discretionary correction of a prejudicial intrusion is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the alternate-presence new-trial ruling and remand for remaining appellate issues. Gray counts V–VII and Olano counts VI and VIII, reversed for insufficient evidence, remain untouched and are not authorized for retrial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Parker v. Gladden, 385 U.S. 363, and Remmer v. United States, 347 U.S. 227: apply the focus on prejudicial influence, preserving appropriate presumptions in other circumstances.
- Smith v. Phillips, 455 U.S. 209: applies rejection of automatic retrial for every compromising contact.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200: applies the ordinary instruction-following presumption, not an irrebuttable cure.
- Zafiro, January 25, 1993: consistent record-specific instruction analysis; no blanket rule that instructions cure intrusions.

## Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md — Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, 512 U.S. 967

#### Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, 512 U.S. 967

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-5131 (Tuilaepa) and 93-5161 (Proctor).

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### Relevant individualized selection does not require fixed labels or numerical weights

**Holding:** On the unchallenged eligibility premise presented here, the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit consideration of understandable, relevant subjects for individualized penalty selection without requiring every consideration to be a yes-or-no factual element, exclusively aggravating or mitigating, or assigned a fixed numerical weight. This rule does not authorize forbidden or wholly irrelevant adverse considerations, exclusion of effective mitigation, or disregard of a separately established selection error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly adopt this bounded rule; Stevens and Ginsburg do not join Part I's additional framework discussion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful eligibility narrowing and individualized selection perform distinct functions under Gregg, Woodson and Lowenfield. Arave's authoritative limitation on an eligibility aggravator remains necessary in its setting. Eligibility does not cure a later unconstitutional weighing factor under Stringer and Espinosa. Romano's separate-death-decision responsibility rule and Simmons's future-danger/parole-ineligibility rebuttal rule retain their distinct operation. This decision neither extends nor retreats from those rules and does not approve the entire California capital scheme.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant individualized selection after unchallenged narrowing; it supplies no exemption for forbidden adverse considerations.

**Holding:** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic reduction to life, retrial of guilt, adjudication of unrelated aggravators, factor-by-factor jury finding, or collateral-review gateway rule follows. The petition's severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions are not granted or adjudicated in this limited summary action; no separate denial of them is invented. No prior Supreme Court decision is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court grants limited review and reverses the rejection of the sentencing claim. Remand requires lawful reweighing or Chapman review addressing the recommendation's influence, and new sentencing if no lawful cure is available. Guilt and the ungranted severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions remain outside the disposition; no separate denial or automatic life sentence follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. **Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is confined to the old-rule application established by Godfrey for this finality date. No Teague exception or unresolved procedural-default defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

##### What correction a constitutionally invalid aggravator requires in a capital weighing system

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine authorized reweighing and constitutional harmlessness are distinct corrective routes. The latter places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining valid aggravators or bare proportionality review alone do not cure the error; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Civil Rights — Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; Civil Rights — The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

##### Private production credit associations cannot borrow the sovereign's forum exception

**Holding:** Borrower-owned production credit associations suing in their own right and commercial interest do not acquire the United States' exception to §1341 merely from a federal charter, instrumentality designation or asserted substantive tax immunity. With the conceded plain, speedy and efficient Arkansas remedy, their federal injunction and declaration action must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous on institutional identity, adequacy and injunction; all eight Associates join the declaratory-relief application, with Stone-Zsela concurring in that judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The entity-specific rule establishes no universal United States-joinder requirement for every agency and decides no Federal Reserve or FDIC suit. The United States' amicus appearance does not make it a plaintiff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Department of Employment involved the sovereign and Red Cross as co-plaintiffs. Moe involved a separate statutory jurisdictional ground. Nash-Finch involved governmental regulation under §2283. None gives these commercial lenders the sovereign's exception.

##### Conceded state-remedy adequacy requires forum dismissal without tax merits

**Holding:** The associations' express concession that Arkansas supplies a plain, speedy and efficient remedy satisfies §1341's remedial condition on this record. Dismissal determines the federal forum only; the associations' substantive tax privileges and the validity of the taxes remain undecided in this action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous on adequacy and nonreach; eight Associate joins in the separate declaratory application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arkansas §§26-18-507 and 26-18-406 remain separate procedures with distinct conditions; no deadline is supplied. Section 2077's instrumentality designation and obligation-specific tax privileges do not confer a sovereign forum, and their substantive application remains undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for dismissal of the federal injunction and declaration action, removing the federal merits judgment. No refund, mandatory joinder, new factfinding or disposition of the parallel state action is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Private Truck Council's actual-adequacy limit and Reich's protection against withdrawal of an apparent postpayment remedy survive; neither defect is disputed here.
