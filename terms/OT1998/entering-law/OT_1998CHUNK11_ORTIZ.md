## Standards and Tests selection: Certification of settlement-only classes

### Certification of settlement-only classes

**Governing question:** Rule 23 prerequisites and the order of decision for a proposed settlement-only class.

**Current rule:** A settlement-only class must satisfy Rule 23(a) and the applicable Rule 23(b) requirements, including typicality, adequate representation and (b)(3) predominance. With no trial proposed, trial-management difficulties need not be examined. The bargain and settlement's actual operation inform the remaining requirements but do not relax them; Rule 23(e) fairness and settlement desirability do not substitute. Notice and opt-out rights cannot cure materially conflicting bargaining interests inadequately protected by common representatives and counsel. Common exposure or a common payment mechanism does not eliminate material governing-law, injury, causation, defense and claim differences for predominance. A nonmerits certification defect logically antecedent to class-dependent standing questions may be resolved first, without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate anyone's substantive claim; any individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Authority by component:**
- Antecedent certification and settlement requirements: Amchem, Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions and Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements, Thomas with all seven other participants (eight; O'Connor absent), 1997-06-25.
- Representation and predominance boundaries: Amchem, Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record and Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance, Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** Amchem, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal subclass architecture or categorical bar on nationwide, mass-tort, issue, future-claim or settlement classes is adopted. Constitutional notice and reserved collateral-review questions remain open.

**Related current rules:** Certification of multistate damages classes supplies the common Rule 23(b)(3) showing and trial-jury constraints; this entry supplies the distinct settlement-only application. Article III power before disputed statutory merits forbids assumed jurisdiction for substantive merits, without prohibiting the logically antecedent nonmerits certification resolution here.

## Standards and Tests selection: Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

## Standards and Tests selection: Certification of multistate damages classes

### Certification of multistate damages classes

**Governing question:** The legal and evidentiary showing required before Rule 23(b)(3) certification.

**Current rule:** Before certification, proponents must show from actual claims, applicable law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate, class treatment is superior to available alternatives and Rule 23(a) is satisfied. Conditional certification cannot postpone those showings. Novelty neither excuses rigorous analysis nor requires a history of individual verdicts. Material state-law variations require demonstrated common answers or workable groupings, not presumed uniformity or subclass labels. Proposed common issues must fit with individual reliance, causation, damages and defenses in lawful, manageable proceedings. A common punitive-ratio issue requires demonstrated state-law predicates and burdens, connection to established liability and coordination with individual proceedings; size or ordinary settlement pressure alone does not prohibit certification. Continuing issue certification must preserve substantive defenses and jury functions under actual governing state law. An average national negligence rule is impermissible; demonstrate materially equivalent law, lawful groupings or genuinely separable issues. Successive federal juries may not redetermine inseparable facts, although independent historical facts may be grouped.

**Authority by component:**

- Castano v. American Tobacco Co. — Castano v. American Tobacco Co. — I. Certification requires a demonstrated legal and evidentiary basis, Castano v. American Tobacco Co. — II. The present core-liability class cannot stand and Castano v. American Tobacco Co. — III. The punitive component and superiority finding also fail; Ginsburg, all nine Justices, January 14, 1997. 
- Continuing certification and jury constraints: In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer — In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer — Certification and jury limits remain binding, February 26, 1997; Stone-Zsela for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Castano, January 14, 1997; Rhone-Poulenc Rorer, February 26, 1997, for continuing certification and jury constraints.

**Exceptions and limits:** Decertification of an unsupported proposal does not categorically bar a materially narrower proposal meeting every applicable requirement. Concrete pressure generated by bypassing defenses or detached punitive assessment may reinforce established defects; it is not an independent numerical bar.

**Open question:** The relationship between action-wide predominance and issue certification is not fully resolved. Mandamus remains subject to the existing clear-and-indisputable entitlement, inadequate ordinary remedy and extraordinary-circumstances requirements; an unsuccessful writ does not validate certification.

**Related current rules:** Certification of settlement-only classes supplies the separate no-trial and representation rules.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

**Present operation effective:** Steel Co., 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule does not prescribe an exhaustive order among all nonmerits thresholds. Norton and Avrech retain their already-controlled-merits and distinct jurisdictional settings. EPCRA citizen-suit coverage of fully corrected reporting defaults remains undecided.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question.

#### Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-270.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions

**Holding:** A nonmerits Rule 23 defect that prevents this proposed settlement class from binding absent persons is logically antecedent to the Article III questions arising from those persons' inclusion in that class. The Court may deny certification on that ground without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate any person's substantive claim; an individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by all seven other participating Justices: eight direct votes. O'Connor does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No named or absent person's individual standing, future injury, right to sue or substantive entitlement is decided; broader constitutional notice issues remain unanswered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. forbids assumed jurisdiction to adjudicate disputed merits. Denial of this nonmerits representative device is distinguished and creates no general merits-first permission.

##### II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements

**Holding:** A settlement-only class must satisfy the applicable requirements of Rule 23(a) and (b), including typicality, adequate representation and predominance under Rule 23(b)(3). Because no trial is proposed, a court need not examine difficulties managing a trial; settlement's actual operation and bargain may inform the remaining inquiries, but their requirements cannot be relaxed or replaced by overall fairness or the desirability of settlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. The disagreement in the separate writing concerns the appellate application and remedy, not permission to dispense with certification requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Falcon requires rigorous application of actual prerequisites. Matsushita and Richards distinguish lawful representation from notice, objection opportunities and shared objectives. Castano requires an actual governing-law and proof showing without class-size or novelty prohibitions. Rule 23(e) fairness does not replace Rule 23(a) and (b).

##### III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record

**Holding:** This proposed undivided class fails Rule 23(a)(4) because the same representatives and counsel do not adequately protect the materially conflicting bargaining interests of currently injured and exposure-only claimants. Notice, the opportunity to opt out and the settlement's overall economic attractions cannot cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six direct votes with Thomas. Stevens and Breyer do not join this application.

**Material application:** Current claimants seek immediate payments, while exposure-only claimants require protection against inflation, diagnostic uncertainty, annual caps and surrender of future claims. Fixed categories and payments, caps and no inflation adjustment make the bargaining conflict concrete.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal subclass architecture or prohibition on future-claim settlements is announced. A differently structured proposal might provide independent protection, but none is approved. Constitutional notice and Matsushita's collateral-review reservation remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry requires genuine adequate representation. Matsushita preserves that prerequisite apart from notice and opt-out rights; Richards rejects a shared objective as a substitute, and Shutts's separate safeguards do not cure the conflict.

##### IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance

**Independent alternative holding:** The proposed class also fails Rule 23(b)(3) because its common asbestos-exposure and settlement objectives do not predominate over the materially different state laws, exposure histories, causation, injuries, defenses and individual claims. Removing trial-management concerns does not remove those substantive differences.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III and independently supports affirmance. Stevens and Breyer withhold this application as well.

**Material application:** Differences include product identification, latency, limitations, defenses, damages and applicable state law. A shared payment compromise does not establish that common substantive questions predominate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nationwide, mass-tort, issue and settlement classes are not categorically prohibited. No particular state-law claim or revised settlement is adjudicated; Metro-North's FELA holding does not establish the validity or injury status of these state-law claims.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Adequacy and predominance are independently sufficient grounds for affirmance, 6–2, leaving certification, settlement approval and the classwide injunction vacated. O'Connor does not participate. No new subclasses, compensation, approval or individual damages ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Castano's governing-law and proof inquiry and Falcon's actual-prerequisite rule apply despite elimination of trial-management issues. Rhone-Poulenc retains distinct extraordinary-writ limits, and Adams supplies no settlement merits ruling.

### Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, No. 96-270

Merits decision, June 25, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 18, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, whose May 10, 1996 judgment vacated certification, settlement approval and the related injunction. The question is whether settlement-only certification relaxes Rule 23's predominance and adequate-representation requirements and whether this asbestos class satisfies them.

The complaint and proposed settlement were filed together on January 15, 1993, without an intention to litigate. Nine named plaintiffs sought to represent currently injured persons, exposure-only claimants and family members with differing claims under many States' laws, without subclasses. The agreement prescribed disease categories and payment ranges, annual caps and no inflation adjustment, while restricting some state-law claims. The settling parties seek reinstatement; the objectors defend decertification. Class-related standing and notice objections are presented but do not require decision once certification fails.

Justice O'Connor took no part in the consideration or decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer.
**Not participating:** O'Connor.

Certification, settlement approval and the classwide injunction remain vacated. Stevens and Breyer would vacate the Third Circuit's judgment and remand for settlement-sensitive review of the district court's findings; they would not reinstate or approve the class or settlement. No individual damages claim or future agreement is adjudicated.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Affirmance | Entire opinion, Parts I–IV |
| Same lead opinion | Thomas | Stevens, Breyer | Threshold and governing-rule agreement only | Parts I and II; not the adequacy or predominance applications in Parts III and IV |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Breyer | Stevens | Would vacate and remand for appellate review | Settlement's real operation and proper review of the district court's findings; no approval of the settlement |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions

**Holding and operative rule:** A nonmerits Rule 23 defect that prevents this proposed settlement class from binding absent persons is logically antecedent to the Article III questions arising from those persons' inclusion in that class. The Court may deny certification on that ground without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate any person's substantive claim; an individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by all seven other participating Justices: eight direct votes. O'Connor does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. forbids assumed constitutional power to decide disputed statutory merits. The present order of decision does not exercise that power. Denying the representative device establishes that this proposed class cannot bind its absent members, without determining anyone's damages entitlement, future injury or individual right to sue. The standing objections specific to the proposed class therefore need not be answered to deny certification. This limited sequencing rule does not authorize a court to assume jurisdiction over an individual action or decide its merits first. The Court likewise leaves the broader constitutional notice objection unanswered because Rule 23 independently defeats this class.

##### II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** A settlement-only class must satisfy the applicable requirements of Rule 23(a) and (b), including typicality, adequate representation and predominance under Rule 23(b)(3). Because no trial is proposed, a court need not examine difficulties managing a trial; settlement's actual operation and bargain may inform the remaining inquiries, but their requirements cannot be relaxed or replaced by overall fairness or the desirability of settlement.

**Authority:** Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. The disagreement in the separate writing concerns the appellate application and remedy, not permission to dispense with certification requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Falcon requires rigorous attention to the actual prerequisites for representative adjudication. A settlement eliminates the need to manage a trial but still binds persons absent from its negotiation. The court must therefore examine who is represented and which claims and interests the bargain resolves. Rule 23(e)'s fairness review does not substitute for Rule 23(a) and (b). The settling parties' argument that the bargain creates a common interest in compensation does not make distinct substantive rights identical. Matsushita and Richards distinguish genuine representative authority from notice, opportunity to object or a shared objective. Castano requires the actual law-and-proof showing, without a categorical ban based on class size, novelty or settlement pressure. Settlement may bear on the real class and its interests; the Court does not treat it as relevant only to a mechanical manageability inquiry. Nor does the rule disfavor settlement classes that independently meet the prerequisites.

##### III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** This proposed undivided class fails Rule 23(a)(4) because the same representatives and counsel do not adequately protect the materially conflicting bargaining interests of currently injured and exposure-only claimants. Notice, the opportunity to opt out and the settlement's overall economic attractions cannot cure that defect.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six direct votes with Thomas. Stevens and Breyer do not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Hansberry requires genuinely adequate representation before absent persons may be bound. Matsushita preserves that requirement independently of notice and opt-out protections, and Richards rejects shared interests as a substitute for lawful representation. Here currently injured claimants seek ample immediate payments. Those who may develop disease later need protection against inflation, diagnostic uncertainty, annual caps and the loss of future claims. The agreement's fixed categories and payments, caps and absent inflation adjustment make these divergent bargaining incentives concrete. One undivided representative group cannot exchange the future protections for present benefits without independent protection of the interests surrendered. Shutts's notice and exclusion safeguards serve a different function and do not align those incentives. The availability of compensation therefore cannot answer the defect. The Court does not prescribe a universal subclass architecture, prohibit settlement of future claims, decide constitutional notice sufficiency or resolve Matsushita's reserved collateral-review question. A materially different proposal may use separately represented subclasses, narrower groups or another adequate structure, but no redesign is approved here.

##### IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance

**Holding and operative rule:** The proposed class also fails Rule 23(b)(3) because its common asbestos-exposure and settlement objectives do not predominate over the materially different state laws, exposure histories, causation, injuries, defenses and individual claims. Removing trial-management concerns does not remove those substantive differences.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III and independently supports affirmance. Stevens and Breyer withhold this application as well.

**Controlling explanation:** Castano requires a demonstrated grouping of the governing law and proof rather than an abstract common description. Common exposure does not resolve product identification, latency, injury, limitations, defenses or damages across the many covered claims and States. The agreement's common payment mechanism is a compromise of those rights, not proof that common questions predominate over them. Falcon's insistence on the actual certification prerequisites remains relevant even when no trial is contemplated. The contrary contention that settlement removes the need to try individual questions confuses trial management with the substantive predominance requirement. Both remain distinct under Rule 23(b)(3). This application establishes no categorical prohibition of nationwide, mass-tort, issue or settlement classes, and it decides neither the validity of any particular state-law claim nor the fairness of a future bargain. Metro-North's FELA limits do not resolve the state-law claims collected here or make all exposure-only claimants legally uninjured.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Steel Co. retains its prohibition on assumed jurisdiction to decide disputed merits; this nonmerits, class-dependent threshold ruling is distinguished.
- Falcon supplies rigorous application of Rule 23's actual prerequisites; settlement does not displace them.
- Hansberry supplies the requirement of genuinely adequate representation; conflicting settlement interests must receive real protection.
- Shutts preserves notice and opt-out safeguards, which do not independently cure inadequate representation.
- Matsushita preserves the constitutional representation prerequisite separately from settlement recognition and notice; its actual-adequacy and collateral-review reservations are not decided here.
- Richards supplies the distinction between shared objectives and lawful binding representation without making every federal class requirement a universal constitutional command.
- Castano applies the required governing-law and proof inquiry to this proposed class while retaining its rejection of categorical national-class or novelty prohibitions.
- Rhone-Poulenc retains its separate extraordinary-writ limits and does not dilute Rule 23; Adams supplies no settlement merits ruling.
- Metro-North remains a FELA decision and does not determine the distinct state-law claims in this settlement.

#### Law After Decision

Settlement-only classes remain available when Rule 23's applicable requirements are satisfied. The absence of trial eliminates the need to address trial-manageability problems, but neither supplies predominance nor cures conflicts in representation. This class fails each of those independent requirements. The decision neither decides absent persons' individual claims nor mandates a particular settlement architecture. No precedent is overruled.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Breyer, joined by Stevens, concurs in the nonmerits order of decision and the mandatory Rule 23 requirements but dissents from affirmance. Settlement's actual consequences matter to more than the absence of trial, and the appellate court must assess the bargain and the district court's findings with that setting in view. The differences between present and future claimants, including inflation and future-payment tradeoffs, warrant careful adequacy review. They do not warrant the Supreme Court's first resolution of the large record in place of proper appellate examination. Matsushita and Richards demand lawful representation without deciding this settlement's actual adequacy; Castano likewise does not adjudicate these findings. Breyer and Stevens would vacate and remand for settlement-sensitive Rule 23 review. They would neither restore the certification automatically nor direct approval, and leave notice and ultimate fairness unresolved.

The Court orders no new subclasses, compensation, approval or adjudication of individual damages rights and expresses no view on a revised agreement's fairness.

## Current-term public authority: South Central Bell, March 23, 1999

Source: terms/OT1998/entering-law/south_central_bell_v_alabama_merits_1999-03-23.md. Part II, unanimous; all nine join.

### II. Shared taxpayer interests do not authorize binding these nonparties

**Holding and operative rule:** Claim or issue preclusion cannot bind these taxpayers to earlier litigation merely because they share the earlier taxpayers' interests or were aware of the public dispute. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or other legally accountable representation, or another recognized relationship consistent with due process; the asserted common-interest rationale supplies none of those prerequisites.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards requires lawful authority to bind the person before the State may extinguish that person's own claim by another litigant's judgment. The earlier litigation concerned different taxpayers; shared objectives, vigorous advocacy and the public importance of a tax question do not establish representative authority over these taxpayers' personal liabilities. Awareness of another action creates no general obligation to intervene. Describing the proposed bar as issue preclusion rather than claim preclusion does not cure the missing relationship. The Court rejects the relationship asserted below without inventing a factual finding that every conceivable ground of control or representation has been disproved. Ordinary party preclusion, actual control, adequate class representation, fiduciary representation and authorized public representation retain their lawful operation. A prior decision may also supply precedent without conclusively binding a stranger to its judgment. The tax merits therefore require independent consideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Richards's due-process prerequisite is applied to both proposed forms of preclusion; its protection of lawful representative adjudication and ordinary precedent remains intact.
