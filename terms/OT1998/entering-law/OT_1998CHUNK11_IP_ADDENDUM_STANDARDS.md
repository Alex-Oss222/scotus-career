## Standards and Tests selection: State immunity of bistate compact entities

### State immunity of bistate compact entities

**Governing question:** When a bistate compact entity shares its constituent States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity.

**Current rule:** Exercise of governmental power alone does not confer the States’ immunity. The entity’s actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must give good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred. Exposure of the state treasuries is highly probative but not categorically indispensable; a separate, self-supporting enterprise responsible for its own obligations cannot claim immunity merely from its public functions.

**Authority by component:**

- PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity: PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity, 1994-11-14. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-14; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This compact-entity inquiry does not establish a universal sole-treasury test, decide abrogation or consent, or equate immunity with governmental status for constitutional-rights purposes.

**Holdings navigation:** PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Rehabilitation Act federal-program coverage and damages consent

### Rehabilitation Act federal-program coverage and damages consent

**Governing question:** Whether the federal operator of a §504 program has consented to compensatory damages.

**Current rule:** Sections 504(a), 505 and the 1986 Amendments' §1003 do not unequivocally waive United States immunity from compensatory damages for a federally conducted academy program. A substantive nondiscrimination duty differs from consent to Treasury liability. Section 505(a)(2)'s assistance-recipient/provider formulation, §505(a)(1)'s distinct §501 employment remedies, §505(b)'s discretionary prevailing-party fee authority and §1003's State-directed remedial comparison do not create a general federal-operator damages waiver.

**Authority by component:**

- Program coverage and missing remedy-specific consent: Lane — Federal program coverage does not supply the missing consent to compensatory damages, June 20, 1996; Souter for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and himself (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Lane, June 20, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independently authorized prospective relief survives. Differently situated federal assistance-provider claims, fee applications, new officer actions and implied damages causes remain undecided; the rule does not turn a program-enrollment claim into federal employment.

**Holdings navigation:** Lane v. Peña, 518 U.S. 187 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

### Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

**Governing question:** Whether Eleventh Amendment immunity affecting some claims defeats removal of an otherwise federal-question action.

**Current rule:** An action containing substantial §1331 federal claims is removable under §1441(a) even when Eleventh Amendment immunity bars particular State or official-capacity claims. Proper assertion of immunity prevents adjudication of the affected claim; it does not eliminate jurisdiction over the other federal claims or require remand of the entire action under §1447(c).

**Authority by component:**

- Immunity affecting one claim does not eliminate removal jurisdiction over remaining federal claims: Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-22.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-22.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule decides neither whether removal itself waives immunity nor every unasserted-immunity issue, and does not supply jurisdiction over a claim lacking its own necessary foundation.

**Holdings navigation:** Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381 — Removal jurisdiction and claim-specific state immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability

### IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability

**Governing question:** What Congress authorized, as distinct from its constitutional power to subject a State to suit.

**Current rule:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, order to State and Tribe and successive State-directed stages. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice. That clear statutory authorization does not establish constitutional amenability: no controlling OT1995 holding either sustains or rejects the direct-State action, imposes an abrogation-before-officer-relief sequence or overrules Union Gas.

**Authority by component:**

- Directly adopted statutory intent, distinct from the divided constitutional question: Seminole Tribe — Holding IV, March 27, 1996; six actual adopters across writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The three conditional alternative positions supply no additional actual constitutional joins.

**Present operation effective:** Seminole Tribe, March 27, 1996.

**Unresolved interaction:** The five votes removing the State-dismissal direction combine two affirmative-amenability grounds with three avoidance grounds; neither is a logical subset yielding a controlling constitutional rationale. Union Gas's actual damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion survive at their decided scope; Hans and Fitzpatrick remain unaffected. No general Article I abrogation permission or prohibition, waiver from negotiation, automatic reciprocal consent or delegated United States exemption results.

**Holdings navigation:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44.
