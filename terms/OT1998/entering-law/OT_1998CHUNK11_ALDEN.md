## Standards and Tests selection: IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability

### IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability

**Governing question:** What Congress authorized, as distinct from its constitutional power to subject a State to suit.

**Current rule:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, order to State and Tribe and successive State-directed stages. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice. That clear statutory authorization does not establish constitutional amenability: no controlling OT1995 holding either sustains or rejects the direct-State action, imposes an abrogation-before-officer-relief sequence or overrules Union Gas.

**Authority by component:**

- Directly adopted statutory intent, distinct from the divided constitutional question: Seminole Tribe — Holding IV, March 27, 1996; six actual adopters across writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The three conditional alternative positions supply no additional actual constitutional joins.

**Present operation effective:** Seminole Tribe, March 27, 1996.

**Unresolved interaction:** The five votes removing the State-dismissal direction combine two affirmative-amenability grounds with three avoidance grounds; neither is a logical subset yielding a controlling constitutional rationale. Union Gas's actual damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion survive at their decided scope; Hans and Fitzpatrick remain unaffected. No general Article I abrogation permission or prohibition, waiver from negotiation, automatic reciprocal consent or delegated United States exemption results.

**Holdings navigation:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective officer enforcement of IGRA negotiations

### Prospective officer enforcement of IGRA negotiations

**Governing question:** The available officer route and complete remedial progression under §2710(d).

**Current rule:** Ex parte Young permits a prospective official-capacity suit against an officer connected to an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiating duty, within IGRA's complete sequence and remedial limits. State-directed language and remedial detail do not exclude that route under this statute. A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers good-faith negotiation, not acceptance of preferred compact terms; a failure-to-negotiate action under §2710(d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's burden to prove good faith. Nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith. An adverse finding triggers an order to conclude a compact within sixty days. The court may consider public interest, public safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming; it shall consider a demand for direct taxation of the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith.

If that period passes without agreement, both sides submit last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the one best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the court's findings and order. Submission of that selection to both parties begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent makes it an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe, consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion in mediator selection is not repeated verbatim in the Secretary provision. Nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal, and failure to agree does not itself authorize contempt or an added order compelling agreement.

**Authority by component:**

- Connected officer action and enacted sequence: Seminole Tribe — Holdings II–III, March 27, 1996; Souter for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (five).

**Present operation effective:** Seminole Tribe, March 27, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No freestanding broader injunction, enlarged negotiating duty or retrospective Treasury compensation follows. Incorporating state gaming law into the federal duty does not itself turn the claim into a Pennhurst state-law action. Section (d)(4) grants no new taxing power, but preserves regulatory-cost assessments agreed under (d)(3)(C)(iii); this differs from the mandatory evidentiary treatment of direct-tax demands. Lack of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation. The effective-compact action under (A)(ii), Secretary enforcement under (A)(iii) and an asserted substitute administrative route remain distinct. Section 2721 severability satisfies none of the statutory predicates.

**Controlling construction:** Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain independent. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA violations, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction produces approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA; Federal Register publication remains necessary.

**Open question:** No general rule that every detailed federal statute preserves officer equity is adopted, and no rule for every separate obstruction sanction or automatic Secretary bypass is decided. Immunity removal proves neither bad faith nor the remaining merits.

**Holdings navigation:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44.

## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial effect of a staged IGRA enforcement decision

### Judicial effect of a staged IGRA enforcement decision

**Governing question:** Whether a binding good-faith-negotiation adjudication becomes advisory because later mediation and executive action follow.

**Current rule:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith dispute is judicial when it conclusively determines the parties' legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive implementation, or absence of a guaranteed compact, do not alone make that determination advisory. The legal effect of the judgment controls; neither an immediately executable coercive decree nor assurance of all requested relief is universally necessary.

**Authority by component:**

- Judicial-function holding: Seminole Tribe — Holding I, March 27, 1996; Part I for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Seminole Tribe, March 27, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** A declaratory label alone supplies no controversy, substantive cause or missing legal consequence. The holding manufactures no coercive remedy or guarantee of the claimant's preferred compact.

**Holdings navigation:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments states the distinct Morgan Stanley and Plaut rule. Those decisions do not determine whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication ceases to be judicial because mediation or executive action follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations

### Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations

**Governing question:** An exercise of ordinary federal commerce power making a State choose between administering federal instructions for private actors and assuming their property and liabilities.

**Current rule:** Congress may not require an unwilling State to choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting ownership and possession of their waste with associated liability. Two unauthorized commands do not create a lawful option. State officials' advocacy for the federal statute neither enlarges congressional powers nor estops a noncompacting State from challenging the command. This differs from authorized monetary or access inducements leaving the State free to choose whether and how to govern, where consequences of refusal fall on private generators rather than transferring their responsibilities to the State.

**Authority by component:**
- Compulsory-ownership alternative and officials' assent: New York v. United States, “5. Compulsory state ownership or regulation”, June 19, 1992; O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five), extending Hodel's legislative-compulsion boundary.
- Distinct actual monetary and access inducements: New York, “3. Monetary incentives” and “4. Access incentives,” June 19, 1992; O'Connor Parts III-A and III-B, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author, applying the received spending and commerce doctrines without creating a numerical coercion threshold.

**Controlling construction:** The 1985 Act's general state-responsibility language states an objective implemented through the specified incentives; it supplies no independently enforceable command to regulate. This is the five-Justice holding in New York, “2. No independent command in the responsibility clause,” not a generic avoidance rule.

**Present operation effective:** New York v. United States, June 19, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Generally applicable regulation of state activity under Garcia and Baker remains distinct and undisturbed. Actual compact obligations, state market conduct, state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law are reserved. The decision establishes no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule or power to direct other States to accept waste.

**Holdings navigation:** New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992) — Federal incentives and compulsory state administration.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporary state-executive records assistance under federal direction

### Temporary state-executive records assistance under federal direction

**Governing question:** Whether valid federal regulation of private transactions categorically excludes affirmative statutory duties of temporary records assistance for state executive officers.

**Current rule:** Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function under affirmative statutory direction when implementing otherwise valid direct regulation of private transactions, federal law supplies the substantive rule and federal authorities retain coercive-enforcement responsibility. The source, function and allocation of responsibility control this limited permission; convenience, low cost, temporary duration or the officer's oath alone supplies no power. The permission does not resolve fiscal or unauthorized-expenditure objections to the actual compulsory arrangement.

**Authority by component:**
- Confined source-and-function rule: Printz/Mack, Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States — Part I — Appropriate temporary executive records assistance is not categorically forbidden, Souter with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer (five), 1997-06-27.

**Present operation effective:** Printz/Mack, 1997-06-27.

**Open question:** Actual constitutional validity given unresolved fiscal objections, compensation entitlement, any compensation action or measure, and general permanent enforcement or field-investigation commands remain undecided. No majority fiscal rationale or Marks rule follows from the distinct five-vote remand.

**Related current rules:** “Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations” remains intact; valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation, lawful spending arrangements and the distinct sources of state-court and election duties retain their own boundaries.

**Controlling construction:** Section 922(s)(2) requires reasonable effort within five business days after the dealer notice, including available State, local and designated national records. It commands no arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. “Including” does not textually exclude every possible nonrecord act and supplies no independent investigative power. Under (6)(B), unless the transaction is determined unlawful, the transmitted purchaser statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the statement was made; disclosure is confined to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection and use to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Under the separate (6)(C), written reasons are due only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. These conditions do not establish an unconditional constitutional validation of every ancillary duty.

**Holdings navigation:** Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States (1997-06-27) — Whether appropriate temporary records assistance is categorically forbidden.

#### Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission, 502 U.S. 197 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-848.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Whether FELA liability of a state-owned interstate railroad remains enforceable in state court

**Holding:** The Federal Employers' Liability Act's established inclusion of state-owned interstate railroads remains enforceable in a competent state court. This rule does not revive constructive waiver of Eleventh Amendment immunity in federal court or establish a general rule for every Article I damages action against a State.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, and Souter; these five Justices supply a majority of the eight participants. Blackmun concurs in the judgment on a different ground and does not supply the controlling rationale.

**Material application:** The retained statutory construction governs the specialized railroad relationship. Congress retained the relevant statutory language, and South Carolina excluded FELA-covered employees from workers' compensation against that established background.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not reopen Hilton's dismissed federal suit, decide negligence or damages, or hold that entering commerce generally waives immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The state-court judgment is reversed and the case remanded so the FELA claim may be adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Parden's FELA coverage holding is reaffirmed as independent of its constructive federal-forum waiver rationale, which remains displaced by Welch. Welch expressly reserved the statutory coverage question. Will, construing a different statutory term, is distinguished. Howlett is applied: a competent state court may not use state immunity to defeat liability under valid federal law.

#### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-12.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### IGRA's binding good-faith adjudication is judicial despite later statutory steps

**Holding:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function when its judgment conclusively determines legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make that judgment advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens's serious doubt and Stone-Zsela's nonjoin supply no additional support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aetna and Nashville permit conclusive adverse declarations without an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial assistance to administration within its adversarial, conclusive, enforceable-order setting, without creating IGRA contempt power. Ferreira's revisable commissioner process is distinguished. Gordon's jurisdictional disposition supplies no delivered reasoned majority opinion requiring execution in every judicial case; Hayburn produced no Supreme Court merits decision, and its reported circuit communications remain historical materials. Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut: Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. The holdings do not establish an unrestricted power to reopen completed merits judgments and do not determine whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication ceases to be judicial because mediation or executive action follows.

##### A connected officer may be sued prospectively under IGRA within its complete statutory limits

**Holding:** Ex parte Young permits prospective official-capacity relief against Governor Chiles for an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiation duty, subject to IGRA's full sequence and remedial limits. The State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case; they do not authorize greater remedies through the officer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. No additional general clear-indication theory commands that coalition.

**Material application:** The Governor's representatives negotiated, supplying connection to the alleged violation without establishing that he alone can bind Florida to every term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over compact terms, and nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers §2710(d)(3)(A)'s good-faith duty; suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary action under (A)(iii) remain separate. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's good-faith burden under (B)(ii).

An actual adverse finding triggers an order to State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty days. The court may consider public interest, safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming, and shall treat direct-tax demands on the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. Failure to agree during that period requires last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the findings and order. Submission to both sides begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent produces an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. Failure to agree or participate during the ordered period produces statutory progression, not added contempt or an order compelling agreement; every separate obstruction sanction is not decided. Section (d)(4) creates no new taxing authority but preserves agreed regulatory-cost assessments under (d)(3)(C)(iii), separately from direct-tax demands' evidentiary effect. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.

Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain distinct. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction means approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA, and Federal Register publication remains necessary. Section 2721 severability supplies neither missing predicates nor an alternative program.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical Governor dismissal, 5–4, without finding bad faith or granting a compact, mediator, default proposal, Secretary procedures, damages, game-scope declaration or license. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 receive their own lawful treatment and are not vacated. The asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting power, Tenth Amendment defense and mediator-appointments question remain unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young reaches this connected officer and ongoing federal duty without a universal rule for detailed statutes. Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary remains; prospective costs alone do not establish retrospective relief. Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies appealability, not immunity. New York and Voting Rights Coalition do not decide the reserved IGRA constitutional defenses; X-Citement Video preserves appropriate respondent defenses without compelling their resolution here.

##### State amenability lacks a controlling rationale, despite agreement on direct-State statutory intent

**Authority of the judgment:** Florida's immunity-dismissal direction is set aside and remanded by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, 5–4. Stone-Zsela and Stevens would sustain direct-State amenability; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate without deciding it. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. No controlling State-amenability rationale exists under Marks: avoidance is not a logical subset of the affirmative constitutional grounds, and three unused conditional alternatives are not actual abrogation joins.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, State-and-Tribe order and successive State-directed steps. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Six actual positions across the separate writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The other three's conditional acceptance is not counted. This agreement concerns statutory intent alone.

**Current force:** The clear-statement proposition controls; no majority sustains or rejects Florida's constitutional amenability or requires a particular avoidance sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State-removal judgment establishes neither jurisdiction nor its absence; lawful jurisdiction is necessary before any State merits adjudication. The State claim is not terminated by immunity, and no abandonment, mootness or indefinite stay is presumed. No express or constructive waiver, reciprocal tribal/state consent or delegated United States immunity exemption establishes this suit. The United States appears only as amicus.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Union Gas is not overruled: its damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion retain their decided force without making every plurality sentence binding. Hans remains effective; separate common-law theories do not become law. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unchanged.

#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 91-543, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-558, County of Allegany, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-563, County of Cortland, New York v. United States et al. The same component-specific relief applies in all three dockets.

For the ripe-review holding in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563, see Federal Courts — Ripeness of future statutory obligations.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.

### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, No. 94-12

Merits decision, March 27, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, No. 94-12; 517 U.S. 44. October Term 1995. Argued October 11, 1995; decided March 27, 1996. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing its January 18, 1994 immunity judgment, 11 F.3d 1016.

The Tribe sued Florida and Governor Lawton Chiles in his official capacity under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. The district court denied immunity dismissal on June 18, 1992; the court of appeals reversed and directed dismissal. The questions concern congressional authorization of a prospective direct-State action under the Indian Commerce Clause and a prospective officer action under Ex parte Young. Alabama is not a party to this docket.

A separate September 22, 1993 merits summary judgment for Florida and the Governor and its stayed Eleventh Circuit appeal, No. 93-5256, remain outside this immunity review. The complaint alleges a January 29, 1991 negotiation request and September 19 filing; the United States describes a December filing. Either interval exceeds 180 days, but the discrepancy establishes no amendment history. The actual prayer for a compact within sixty days, later mediation and a game-scope declaration is distinct from the government's narrower proposed good-faith negotiation or declaratory relief.

The other three accept that proposition only in their unused alternative. This agreement concerns statutory authorization, not constitutional power. No aggregation of conditional votes or broader separate reasons supplies a controlling State-amenability rule.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The statutory judicial decision is not categorically advisory

**Controlling proposition:** Adjudicating IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function where the judgment conclusively determines the parties' legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. The prospect of later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make the court's decision merely advisory.

**Authority:** Part I has seven direct supporters: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens's serious reservation is not an eighth join; Stone-Zsela supplies no independent join.

**Controlling explanation:** A court decides an existing dispute over a federal duty and, upon the requisite finding, changes the parties' legal obligations and access to the next statutory stage. That supplies effective partial relief even though neither the desired compact nor every desired game is assured. The decision's legal operation matters; the answer does not rest solely on whether the Secretary can edit individual findings.

Aetna and Nashville reject a universal requirement that judicial relief take the form of an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial adjudication in aid of administration, while its actual enforceable-order setting prevents treating it as an answer to every intermediate proceeding. Ferreira and the relevant Gordon and Hayburn materials require attention to whether the purported judgment has legal effect or remains subject to executive revision. IGRA's binding determination and prescribed consequences satisfy that concern here.

Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut address Congress's ability to establish a new rule requiring renewed adjudication of specified completed proceedings without dictating the judicial resolution of underlying claims. Neither line determines whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication of a good-faith negotiation dispute ceases to be judicial merely because subsequent mediation or executive action remains. The Court neither determines every later preclusion question nor manufactures coercive remedies or a new cause of action to make the statutory decision judicial.

##### II. IGRA permits this connected officer action within its own limits

**Controlling proposition:** Ex parte Young permits a prospective official-capacity action against Governor Chiles to enforce an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiating duty, subject to the statute's full sequence and remedial limitations. IGRA's State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case.

**Authority:** Part II has five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The common rule is specific to IGRA; it does not adopt the three Justices' additional general clear-indication theory.

**Controlling explanation:** Young provides the established route against a responsible officer for an ongoing federal violation. The Governor's representatives participated in negotiations, supplying the required connection to the alleged failure. Rejecting categorical dismissal does not require proof that he alone can bind Florida to every compact term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over those terms.

Congress imposed a federal negotiating duty and prescribed how its enforcement progresses. References to the State do not, in this arrangement, exclude the official through whom its obligations are performed. The scheme limits the officer action rather than authorizing a free-standing injunction with greater remedies. This construction answers the objection that an officer could evade Congress's chosen restraints: both sixty-day periods, mediation, selection, consent and Secretarial conditions remain effective. It does not treat remedial detail as irrelevant or establish that every statutory right carries officer relief.

The State's involvement in negotiations establishes neither waiver nor unlimited compacting authority. Incorporation of state gaming law into the federal obligation does not itself convert the claim into a Pennhurst demand to enforce state law. Hafer's individual-capacity damages rule supplies no basis for this action, and Edelman's retrospective-relief boundary remains.

The actual request for a compact and declaration does not establish entitlement to either. A court cannot prescribe its preferred compact, enlarge the good-faith duty, bypass mediation or recast accrued compensation as an injunction. The statutory progression, rather than an added coercive order compelling agreement, governs failure to reach agreement. The categorical officer dismissal must therefore be reversed while the remaining merits and lawful relief receive their proper treatment.

##### III. Removing the officer bar does not establish the predicates for compacting relief

**Controlling proposition:** The remand permits only relief authorized by IGRA upon the actual statutory predicates. It neither resolves good faith or gaming scope nor disturbs the distinct merits judgment and appeal. The following conditions govern any relief through the recognized officer route.

**Authority:** Part III has the same five direct supporters. The four opposing the route preserve the same enacted conditions in their dissent; recognizing those conditions does not imply their agreement to reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** A valid duty, an available enforcement defendant and entitlement to a particular remedy are different questions. IGRA makes adverse findings, successive waiting periods and specified mediation and administrative steps do separate work. Removing immunity cannot substitute for any of them. Nor does the actual prayer enlarge the relief Congress supplied. The September 1993 merits judgment and its stayed appeal must receive their own lawful disposition; this immunity mandate decides neither. The Court accordingly preserves the statutory progression and independent gaming conditions below without ordering that progression to begin as though bad faith were already established.

**Complete statutory conditions and limits:**

- Under 25 U.S.C. §2710(d)(3)(A), a qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the relevant Indian lands triggers the State's duty to negotiate in good faith, not a duty to accept preferred terms. A failure-to-negotiate suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary's enforcement action under (A)(iii) remain distinct.
- Under (B)(ii), tribal evidence that no compact exists and that the State failed to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's burden to prove good faith. Nonagreement alone does not establish bad faith. An actual adverse finding triggers (B)(iii)'s order to the State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty-day period. The court **may** consider public interest, public safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming; it **shall** consider a demand for direct taxation of the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith.
- If no agreement follows during that period, (B)(iv) requires each side's last-best proposal to a court-appointed mediator. The mediator selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law, and the court's findings and order. Submission to both parties under (B)(v) begins a **second sixty-day period** for State consent. Consent makes the proposal an agreed compact under (B)(vi).
- Nonconsent during that second period triggers notice to the Secretary under (B)(vii). The Secretary **shall** prescribe procedures, consulting the Tribe and acting consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The express findings/order selection criterion appears in (iv), not verbatim in (vii). The Secretary lacks unrestricted discretion to withhold all action after the enacted predicates occur.
- State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. The government's qualified prediction supplies no textual default. The adopted construction adds neither contempt nor an order compelling agreement merely because agreement or participation during the ordered period fails; statutory progression is the consequence. No rule concerning every separate obstruction or disobedience sanction is decided.
- Section (d)(4) grants no new state taxing power while preserving regulatory-cost assessments agreed under (d)(3)(C)(iii). That exception is distinct from the mandatory treatment of direct-tax demands as bad-faith evidence. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.
- Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain separate. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA violations, other federal law **not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands**, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction produces approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA; Federal Register publication remains necessary.
- Section 2721's severability provision preserves qualifying remaining law but does not satisfy a finding, waiting period, mediation, selection or nonconsent condition and creates no alternative program. The distinct asserted Secretary substitute remedy remains unadjudicated. The Court neither endorses an automatic bypass nor reverses the lower court's separate substitute-remedy determination. Immunity dismissal alone satisfies none of the enacted predicates.

##### IV. Direct-State intent is agreed upon, but State amenability is unresolved

**Majority-supported statutory proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes a direct action naming the State. Its specified State-party action, State evidentiary burden, order to State and Tribe and successive State-directed steps satisfy the clear-statement requirement. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered references to States alone would not suffice.

**Authority and force:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and the four dissenters actually adopt this statutory construction across their writings. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept it only if their alternative State route must be reached. The six-position agreement establishes only the exact statutory-authorization proposition stated above; it creates no common State-amenability opinion. Statutory intent and constitutional authority remain separate: no controlling holding sustains or rejects Florida's amenability, and no majority opinion decides the constitutional question. It also supplies no sequencing rule requiring abrogation to be decided before officer relief.

**Common judgment, without common State rationale:** Five support setting aside the immunity-dismissal direction and remand. Two support affirmative direct-State relief; three support nonmerits vacatur; four oppose either change. Avoidance is not a logical subset of the two Justices' affirmative constitutional grounds. No narrower controlling constitutional rationale can be extracted by treating their different reasons as interchangeable.

**Other limits:** No express waiver, automatic reciprocal tribal/state consent, constructive waiver by negotiating, or delegation of the United States' immunity exemption establishes this suit; the United States is an amicus. The Court does not reach the distinct Tenth Amendment defense first raised on appeal and not decided below. The question-scope limitation is prudential: X-Citement Video permits an appropriate respondent defense without requiring resolution of this distinct constitutional issue. No universal forfeiture rule, jurisdictional incapacity or independent mediator-appointments invalidation is announced.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Young:** Applied to this connected officer and ongoing federal statutory duty. The recognized route carries IGRA's limits and establishes no universal rule that detailed statutes preserve officer equity.
- **Edelman:** Its prospective/retrospective boundary remains. An injunction cannot be used to obtain accrued treasury compensation; prospective compliance costs alone do not decide that classification.
- **Pennhurst:** Its restriction on federal orders enforcing state law remains. Incorporation of state gaming law as part of the federal IGRA obligation does not itself turn this action into a state-law enforcement claim.
- **Union Gas:** Not overruled. Its actual damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain precedent within their decided scope. The fractured rationale neither erases that conclusion nor makes every plurality sentence binding. Four Justices would overrule the constitutional conclusion; their dissent changes no law. Three unused constitutional alternatives cannot be aggregated with two affirmative State votes to create a present holding.
- **Hans:** Not overruled. The separate common-law accounts of its nontextual immunity do not become a Court holding, and the State set-aside does not abolish its rule.
- **Fitzpatrick:** Its distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unaffected; the decision establishes no new Reconstruction ground.
- **Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227:** Applied for the judicial character of specific, conclusive declaratory relief in an actual dispute over present rights. The declaratory statute is procedural; it creates neither a constitutional controversy nor an otherwise missing substantive action. The absence of a damages or injunction award is not alone disqualifying.
- **Nashville, Chattanooga & St. Louis Railway v. Wallace, 288 U.S. 249:** Applied for the proposition that an adverse, conclusive declaratory adjudication can be judicial without execution or coercive process. Its actual state-tax controversy does not establish that a declaratory label alone supplies legal effect or an IGRA remedy.
- **ICC v. Brimson, 154 U.S. 447:** Applied as support for judicial adjudication assisting administration, while preserving its ordinary adversarial proceeding, conclusive judgment and judicially enforceable order. Contempt there followed disobedience of the court's order, not merely the agency's request. It neither establishes an IGRA contempt remedy nor alone answers whether a differently structured intermediate judgment is judicial.
- **United States v. Ferreira, 54 U.S. 40:** Distinguished. The judge acted as a claims commissioner in a process lacking ordinary suit, parties, process and court record; no debt arose without Treasury approval, and the Secretary could revise the award. IGRA's conclusive adjudication and prescribed legal consequences differ. Ferreira's reserved appointments question supplies no mediator ruling; the appended Yale Todd account is a later inferential report, not a recorded reasoned holding.
- **Gordon v. United States, 69 U.S. 561, and the later appendix at 117 U.S. 697:** The actual disposition dismissed for want of appellate jurisdiction without a contemporaneous reasoned opinion. Taney's prepared paper, considered after his death and later printed, was not a delivered majority opinion; the contemplated Court opinion was never prepared. Its execution language informs the historical concern about legal effect contingent on another branch, but is not an unqualified holding requiring execution in every judicial case.
- **Hayburn's Case, 2 U.S. 409:** The reported Supreme Court proceeding produced no merits decision. The circuit judges' communications printed in the note explain objections to executive suspension and legislative revision of purported judicial decisions. They remain relevant historical materials, not a reasoned Supreme Court holding deciding IGRA's constrained later process.
- **Wisconsin v. City of New York:** Its concrete partial-redress holding supplies a limited analogy. It did not decide whether IGRA assigns a judicial function, eliminate Stevens's doubt or guarantee the claimant's preferred ultimate result.
- **Morgan Stanley/Plaut:** Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. - **Puerto Rico Aqueduct:** Applied to the immediate appeal of the conclusive immunity denial. Appealability resolves neither immunity nor waiver, abrogation or the officer route's merits.
- **Hess:** Its entity-specific arm-of-State analysis does not resolve Florida's own immunity or congressional abrogation.
- **Hafer:** Its personal-capacity damages holding remains separate from the official-capacity prospective action recognized here.
- **New York v. United States:** Its compulsory regulation-or-ownership rule and distinct permitted inducements remain effective. It does not resolve the separate Tenth Amendment defense left unadjudicated here.
- **Voting Rights Coalition:** Its election-power grounds and United States enforcement posture supply no general Indian Commerce abrogation or state-command rule.
- **X-Citement Video:** Its allowance for an appropriate prevailing-respondent defense is preserved without compelling adjudication of this distinct Tenth Amendment issue. The Court's question-scope limit remains prudential.

#### Law After Decision

From March 27, 1996, the controlling rules reject the categorical advisory-function objection to this legally effective IGRA adjudication and permit the connected prospective officer action within the complete statutory sequence. No general rule that every detailed scheme preserves or excludes Young is adopted. Six actual writers agree that IGRA clearly authorizes a State action, but the judgment creates no controlling rule on Congress's power to subject Florida to that action.

The State-removal judgment binds the parties without a controlling State-amenability rationale. Union Gas remains precedent; the decision neither adopts a general Article I abrogation power nor establishes the dissent's general Article I prohibition. Statutory duty, cause, defendant, constitutional power, merits and remedy remain separate. The judgment supplies no direct-State jurisdiction by default, Secretary bypass, new damages action or gaming authorization.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the officer holding and statutory limits and supports removal of Florida's dismissal direction, but would affirmatively sustain Congress's clearly enacted prospective direct-State action. Indian Commerce authority, the ongoing federal duty and unmistakable authorization support that route. Prospective compliance costs do not become accrued treasury compensation merely because payment is needed. Congress's express enforcement channel and the Tribe's continuing-duty claim warrant retaining a forum. Young independently reaches the connected officer, with the same statutory restraints. Neither route permits a preferred compact, bypassed mediation, enlarged duty or disguised retrospective damages; lawful relief should avoid double or inconsistent commands.

He expressly refuses to treat officer availability as sufficient reason to stop short of sustaining the State action. He retains Union Gas's actual damages judgment without adopting every broader abrogation rationale. He reserves materially new compensatory, punitive or restitutionary liability, compulsory state-court actions and different statutory schemes, while recognizing that already-decided Union Gas questions remain settled within its scope. He supplies no new waiver or Reconstruction ground.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts II–III and would sustain both routes. Outside the Eleventh Amendment's actual textual restriction, Hans immunity is judicial doctrine subject to displacement by valid congressional legislation. IGRA supplies the affirmative direction absent from Hans; Congress cannot amend the Constitution or exceed Article III. His rationale is not confined to prospective Indian Commerce litigation and does not require overruling Hans's actual result. He retains Union Gas's constitutional conclusion and leaves the textual issue concerning out-of-state or foreign-citizen plaintiffs open; the Tribe is not treated as such a plaintiff. His own remedial-intent analysis supports the constrained officer action, and he accepts lawful officer-only resolution as a narrower sufficient alternative without abandoning his affirmative State position.

He nevertheless retains serious doubt whether this intermediate judicial step, particularly if separate executive relief could make it dispensable, is fully judicial under Article III. That is a reservation, not a vote to dismiss independently. Wisconsin's partial-redress holding does not settle this different judicial-function issue. He does not formally join Souter's entire separate theory.

**Souter, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, respecting the State claim in Part IV.** They prefer the established officer route and vacatur of Florida's immunity-dismissal direction without deciding abrogation. Intricate remedies and State wording alone do not clearly exclude established prospective equity; Congress may limit statutory enforcement, but they find no sufficient indication of exclusion here. This is distinct from inventing a personal damages action. They would address whether State adjudication is necessary in light of lawful officer relief while preserving lawful jurisdiction before State merits adjudication or a proper nonmerits resolution. The Tribe might forgo its State claim or adequate officer relief might support denying duplicative relief, but neither fact is found. They prescribe no automatic abandonment, mootness, dismissal or indefinite stay. Their additional sequence is not the Court's mandate.

If Young were rejected and Florida's still-live immunity claim adjudicated, they would recognize the direct-State clear statement and uphold congressional displacement of Hans immunity under valid Article I power. They regard that nontextual immunity as federal common law, retained by stare decisis absent deliberate legislative displacement, rather than constitutional immunity Congress can never alter. Constitutional text, limited prior holdings and legislative control of common law independently support that account; it is not a wholesale adoption of Brennan's Union Gas reasoning. Their view is not confined to prospective relief or commerce, but decides no damages or unrelated statute. Because Young succeeds here, these constitutional views remain conditional alternatives, not actual joins in a State-amenability holding.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join the judicial-character ruling and find unmistakable direct-State intent, but would affirm both dismissals. Their constitutional rule is broad: original Article I powers cannot overcome the constitutional immunity limiting federal judicial power over a nonconsenting State. Prospective relief does not cure the defect; neither Indian/interstate commerce nor proprietary/governmental distinctions justifies retaining Union Gas. They would expressly overrule its constitutional conclusion while leaving its statutory construction and Fitzpatrick's later Fourteenth Amendment authority intact.

They acknowledge Union Gas's five-vote constitutional conclusion rather than declaring it never precedent. In their view its conflict with the constitutional immunity structure, fractured rationale and deficient integration with prior immunity decisions supply the special justification for correction. They reject the opposing distinction between a common-law default and congressional displacement, without assuming zero reliance or proof that clear-statement rules are empirically unworkable. Their premise does not adjudicate every other statute or remedy, grant immunity to every public entity, foreclose United States enforcement or eliminate ordinary Young relief.

Independently, they read IGRA as selecting the State-party action, reciprocal State/Tribe order and limited subsequent progression. An ordinary officer injunction risks judicial coercion Congress withheld, but their objection is not limited to unlimited contempt: even an officer action copying the deadlines changes Congress's selected defendant and enforcement form. The sovereign-compacting arrangement reinforces that inference. They recognize that Congress can authorize constrained officer remedies; they conclude it did not do so here. They establish no universal detailed-scheme bar, no categorical affirmative-duty exception to Young, and no power to rewrite the Act merely because enforcement otherwise has a gap.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The immunity decisions are 11 F.3d 1016 and 801 F. Supp. 655. The separate merits judgment and stayed appeal establish no finding of good faith in this review. The differing filing-date accounts establish no amendment history. The record does not resolve every game-scope issue or the Governor's complete state-law authority to bind Florida; a party's account of a possible administrative substitute is not enacted authority.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for proceedings consistent with the officer holding, shared State set-aside and preserved statutory conditions. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 require their own lawful treatment; this decision supplies neither reversal of that judgment nor a finding of bad faith. The State claim remains unterminated by the immunity disposition, with no majority determination of State amenability and no mandatory avoidance sequence imposed by the Court.

No immediate compacting order, mediator appointment, default proposal, Secretarial prescription, damages, game-scope declaration or license issues from this decision. The distinct asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting authority, Tenth Amendment question and mediator-appointments issue remain unadjudicated. No special mandate date or continuing Supreme Court supervision is ordered.

### I. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar review of Alabama's own judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Amendment does not prevent this Court from reviewing under 28 U.S.C. § 1257 the federal questions decided by Alabama's own courts. Such review is distinct from initiating a new federal damages action against the State.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Opinion of the Court, under the common authority stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Alabama's immunity objection concerns the character of this proceeding. The taxpayers seek review of the federal judgment entered in litigation in Alabama's courts. McKesson recognizes both this Court's authority to review such federal questions and the State's obligation to administer constitutionally adequate relief when required. Treating appellate review as a new action would insulate the state court's decision of federal law from the review Congress authorized. The Court rejects that objection without deciding an unpresented immunity question concerning a separate federal suit or awarding damages against the State.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson supplies the distinction between review of a state judgment and initiation of a federal action; it remains effective on that ground.

(b) Damages; right of action; attorney's fees and costs; termination of right of action


Any employer who violates the provisions of section 206 or section 207 of this title shall be liable to the employee or employees affected in the amount of their unpaid minimum wages, or their unpaid overtime compensation, as the case may be, and in an additional equal amount as liquidated damages. Any employer who violates the provisions of section 215(a)(3) of this title shall be liable for such legal or equitable relief as may be appropriate to effectuate the purposes of section 215(a)(3) of this title, including without limitation employment, reinstatement, promotion, and the payment of wages lost and an additional equal amount as liquidated damages. An action to recover the liability prescribed in either of the preceding sentences may be maintained against any employer (including a public agency) in any Federal or State court of competent jurisdiction by any one or more employees for and in behalf of himself or themselves and other employees similarly situated. No employee shall be a party plaintiff to any such action unless he gives his consent in writing to become such a party and such consent is filed in the court in which such action is brought. The court in such action shall, in addition to any judgment awarded to the plaintiff or plaintiffs, allow a reasonable attorney's fee to be paid by the defendant, and costs of the action. The right provided by this subsection to bring an action by or on behalf of any employee, and the right of any employee to become a party plaintiff to any such action, shall terminate upon the filing of a complaint by the Secretary of Labor in an action under section 217 of this title in which (1) restraint is sought of any further delay in the payment of unpaid minimum wages, or the amount of unpaid overtime compensation, as the case may be, owing to such employee under section 206 or section 207 of this title by an employer liable therefor under the provisions of this subsection or (2) legal or equitable relief is sought as a result of alleged violations of section 215(a)(3) of this title.
