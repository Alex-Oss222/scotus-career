#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

## Event

NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones, No. 97-1840. Decided November 5, 1998. Reasoned threshold decision after full merits briefing, on writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit; no argument date is established. The judgment under review is *NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones*, 131 F.3d 1317 (9th Cir. 1997), decided December 16, 1997, No. 96-56455, affirming dismissal of the constitutional challenge.

The presented questions concern whether privately financed judicial elections deny less wealthy voters and candidates equal protection under the wealth-primary theory and whether reimbursement for an optional candidate statement in a mailed sample ballot violates the First or Fourteenth Amendment. The antecedent issue is whether the court below could assume disputed Article III standing before deciding those merits. The plaintiffs seek prospective declaratory and injunctive relief; they expressly exclude an indigent-candidate claim, and the state-law claims were not preserved.

## Holdings

### Actual Article III standing before contested constitutional merits

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III standing to decide these contested constitutional claims about judicial-election financing and optional candidate statements. It must determine, with support appropriate to the litigation stage, whether the plaintiffs have a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury fairly traceable to the challenged conduct and likely to be redressed by the prospective relief they seek; the Ninth Circuit's merits judgment is vacated so that determination can be made on the actual pleadings and appropriate record.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices adopt this rule, its explanation and the remand limits below; it controls directly without any fractured-opinion inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the disputed jurisdictional question must be answered before these constitutional merits. *Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.* forbids deciding contested merits on assumed Article III power. *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife* supplies personal injury, fair traceability and likely redress, with support suited to the litigation stage; *Bennett v. Spear* applies those requirements at pleading without demanding proof appropriate only at a later stage. The District Court found no injury in fact as to the county defendants, but the Ninth Circuit expressly assumed standing to reject the constitutional theories. An unsuccessful claim to free publication or public financing does not itself establish an absence of factual injury, just as a claimed constitutional entitlement does not itself establish standing.

The apparent weakness of a subsidy claim supplies no jurisdictional shortcut. *Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith*, which examined the actual burden of a different election subsidy, does not settle these plaintiffs' standing or this optional-statement charge. *Norton v. Mathews* and *Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech* retain the confined settings, preserved in *Steel Co.*, in which other controlling decisions had already disposed of the merits; no such controlling resolution of this distinct dispute is established. The Court prescribes no exhaustive order among all nonmerits questions.

The available account identifies a former candidate and prospective relief but does not establish every material allegation in the complaint. The Court therefore neither invents a future candidacy nor treats an allegation omitted from that account as absent from the complaint. This uncertainty supports remand for an actual determination, not a finding that standing exists or a direction to dismiss every plaintiff. Both constitutional merits questions remain open if jurisdiction is established.

**Remand limits and questions not reached:** The lower courts must determine standing on the actual pleadings and appropriate record before any merits ruling. The Court orders no particular evidentiary hearing, standing discovery, document production or amendment entitlement. It neither approves the optional charge nor requires campaign financing or free publication. No indigent-candidate claim is decided, and the unpreserved state-law claims are not revived. No finding of racial intent or viewpoint discrimination, new future-candidacy allegation, ballot-access ruling or rule for a compulsory filing fee is supplied. Merits arguments about the wealth-primary theory and optional printing costs are not alternative holdings.

## Event

Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc., No. 97-1909. Merits decision, April 5, 1999. Argued March 1, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 125 F.3d 1396. The District Court denied Michetti's motion to remand. On certified review under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b), the Court of Appeals reversed and directed remand because it treated receipt of a courtesy complaint copy as starting the removal period.

Michetti faxed Murphy a courtesy copy of the filed complaint before formal service. Murphy removed more than thirty days after receiving the fax but within thirty days after formal service. The question is whether that pre-service receipt began the original-pleading period under §1446(b). This is review of a certified denial of remand, not an appeal from a covered district-court remand order.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The federal district court must treat the notice of removal as timely and address subject-matter jurisdiction and any other preserved remand ground. No contract merits or other removal defect is decided. Scalia and Thomas would sustain the appellate direction to remand to state court because receipt of the fax began the period under their construction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Ginsburg delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Justice Thomas dissents, joined by Justice Scalia. Each writing retains its complete statutory construction and remedy; there are no partial or judgment-only joins.

## Holdings

### Formal process and the statutory pleading events govern the original removal period

**Holding and operative rule:** Informal receipt of a courtesy copy before formal service, waiver or legally equivalent process has subjected the defendant to the court's authority does not by itself begin §1446(b)'s original-pleading removal period. Under its first branch, thirty days run when that process requirement and receipt, through service or otherwise, of the initial pleading setting forth the claim for relief have both occurred; the separate branch runs thirty days after service of summons if the initial pleading has then been filed in court and is not required to be served on the defendant, whichever period is shorter.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has the complete seven-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All seven directly adopt both statutory branches, their limits and the application to Murphy's removal.

**Controlling explanation:** Receipt of useful information and an obligation imposed by court process are different events. Section 1446(b) operates within the system in which formal service, waiver or a legally equivalent process subjects the defendant to judicial authority. The phrase permitting receipt otherwise than through service has work to do: pleading information may arrive separately from formal process. If the complaint arrives first, later process starts the first branch; if process precedes the required pleading receipt, that branch awaits receipt. The separate summons branch applies on its own stated conditions and does not invariably require actual receipt or service of the complaint. The dissent correctly observes that Congress expressly referred to receipt in the first branch and summons service in the second. Reading the paragraph as a whole, however, those different information events operate within the formal-process framework, rather than making an unsolicited advance copy a compulsory forum-election deadline. Henderson supports attention to service's actual procedural role without answering this statute or erasing its commands. Caterpillar preserves prospective removal requirements; no completed-trial cure is invoked here. Murphy had no obligation to appear or face default when the fax arrived, and its removal was within thirty days after the subsequent formal service. The notice was therefore timely under the statutory rule, not an equitable extension. This construction does not hold that Congress could never enact an earlier deadline or impose a universal clear-statement requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both original thirty-day periods and the shorter-period direction remain operative. A validly begun period is not extended, and service-evasion gamesmanship is not excused. The Court decides no unpresented waiver's precise timing or other process sequence, does not prohibit removal before service, and does not apply the separate later-removability paragraph. Timeliness supplies no subject-matter jurisdiction. Other preserved remand grounds, contract merits and fees are not decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- Henderson v. United States, decided May 20, 1996, supplies source-specific attention to service's procedural function. Its Suits in Admiralty Act holding does not itself define this removal trigger, repeal other service statutes or authorize a discretionary extension here.
- Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis, decided December 10, 1996, preserves the prospective duty to comply with removal requirements. Its bounded protection of an actually jurisdictionally sound completed judgment is distinguished; this case has no such judgment and applies no cure.
- Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, decided December 5, 1995, and Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., decided June 3, 1996, retain their covered-remand-review boundaries. Review here arose from the certified denial of remand, not appellate reconsideration of a district-court remand order protected by §1447(d).
- Omni Capital International, Ltd. v. Rudolf Wolff & Co. preserves the distinct role of authorized service in establishing personal jurisdiction. It does not independently fix the statutory time to elect removal.

## Law After Decision

A courtesy complaint copy received before process does not itself start the original removal clock. The first branch requires both the legally relevant process status and pleading receipt; the summons branch operates independently when its filed-pleading and no-service-required conditions are met, with the shorter statutory period controlling. The rule preserves timely statutory compliance without confusing the removal opportunity, duty to appear and subject-matter jurisdiction. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, would affirm the appellate remand direction. The first statutory branch expressly makes receipt of the filed initial pleading the triggering event and permits receipt otherwise than by service. Formal service is a means of receipt under that language, not an additional condition. The second branch expressly requires summons service when the pleading has been filed and need not be served, and the shorter period remains controlling. General service principles cannot add the second branch's process condition to the first. Congress may require an election of a federal forum before state process imposes a duty to answer; an expired removal period neither authorizes default without service nor establishes personal jurisdiction. Because Murphy removed more than thirty days after receipt of the fax, the dissent finds the removal untimely. It adopts no waiver, bad-faith or equitable-extension finding.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for otherwise authorized federal proceedings. The district court must reject the courtesy-fax timeliness ground, treat the removal notice as timely, and address subject-matter jurisdiction and any other preserved remand ground. The Court directs no merits judgment, fees or extension of another deadline.

## Event

El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie, No. 98-6. Argued March 2, 1999; decided May 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 136 F.3d 610. Navajo plaintiffs brought tribal-court claims alleging uranium-related injuries. The companies sought federal relief under Price-Anderson and appealed partial injunctions; the plaintiffs did not cross-appeal. The questions concern federal determination of statutory coverage before tribal exhaustion and the appellate enlargement of nonappealing plaintiffs' relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is vacated and the cases remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting both the coverage-first and cross-appeal rulings:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The federal district court must determine Price-Anderson coverage itself. The appellate enlargement dissolving unappealed partial injunctions is set aside; those orders return to their preappeal position, without a new merits adjudication of their propriety.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join both holdings and the complete remand.

## Holdings

### Federal courts determine Price-Anderson coverage before imposing tribal exhaustion

**Holding and operative rule:** When the invoked Price-Anderson federal proceeding requires deciding whether pleaded claims are covered public-liability actions, the federal court must decide that predicate itself before imposing prudential tribal exhaustion. Covered claims follow the Act's specific federal channel; uncovered claims retain their own jurisdictional, tribal-authority and exhaustion analysis.

**Authority:** Thomas's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding and the cross-appeal holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual ordinarily allow tribal courts to consider their authority first as a matter of judicial comity. Price-Anderson creates a specific federal cause and jurisdiction, together with removal and consolidation mechanisms for covered public-liability claims. That arrangement cannot perform its assigned work if a court-created exhaustion rule postpones the federal coverage decision. The removal provision expressly names state courts; the Court does not rewrite it to say tribal courts or invent a tribal removal filing. The relevant question is the federal court's already invoked coverage task, which the Act's jurisdiction and consolidation design place before ordinary comity. Quackenbush keeps jurisdiction distinct from discretionary surrender. Strate likewise requires an actual source of tribal governing authority; exhaustion itself supplies none. The Court does not decide whether these allegations establish coverage, whether every mining injury is a covered nuclear incident, or whether any uncovered claim may proceed in a tribal forum. Mille Lacs requires attention to actual instruments and rights, not a presumed extinguishment of unexamined treaty protections.

### Nonappealing appellees may defend a decree but may not enlarge it

**Holding and operative rule:** An appellee may defend the existing decree on an alternative preserved ground but may not, without a cross-appeal, obtain an enlargement of its own rights or reduction of the appellant's rights under that decree. The Ninth Circuit could not dissolve the partial injunctions for plaintiffs who did not appeal them; that enlargement is vacated.

**Controlling explanation:** American Railway Express draws the distinction between a ground supporting the existing judgment and a request for a better judgment. Respondents' proposed dissolution was the latter. Comity does not convert their defense into an appeal, and no independent jurisdictional defect supplies a basis requiring that dissolution here. The Court need not classify every cross-appeal omission as jurisdictional or decide every possible exception. Restoring the prior decrees also does not decide their substantive merits or authorize broader relief. Any additional coercive relief must have an actual jurisdictional and equitable basis, respect proper-party and immunity limits, and fit the coverage determination.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Farmers Union Insurance Cos. v. Crow Tribe and Iowa Mutual Insurance Co. v. LaPlante retain their ordinary prudential exhaustion rule; it yields to this specific federal coverage task.
- American Railway Express Co. v. Levee preserves alternative grounds for defending a decree but prohibits an unappealed enlargement.
- Strate v. A-1 Contractors requires a substantive source of tribal authority and preserves proper-party, immunity and equitable limits; it does not decide these mining claims by label.
- Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. keeps jurisdiction and prudential abstention distinct.
- Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band preserves instrument-specific rights; no unexamined treaty right is extinguished here.

## Law After Decision

Price-Anderson coverage is a federal threshold task in these proceedings. Covered claims cannot be delayed by ordinary prudential tribal exhaustion, while uncovered claims remain governed by their actual jurisdictional rules. Appellees still need a cross-appeal to obtain the favorable decree enlargement at issue. No precedent is overruled.
