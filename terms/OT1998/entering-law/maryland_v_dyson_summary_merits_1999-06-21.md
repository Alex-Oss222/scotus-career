## Event

Maryland v. Dyson, No. 98-1062. Reasoned summary merits decision, June 21,1999, after the separate same-date grant of certiorari. There was no plenary argument. The Court reviews 122 Md. App. 413, 712 A.2d 573 (1998), reversing a conviction for conspiracy to possess cocaine with intent to distribute after denial of suppression; Maryland's highest court declined review October 9,1998.

The state appellate court accepted abundant probable cause but required additional exigency because police had 13–14 hours to obtain a warrant before encountering the moving rental Toyota at 1 a.m. on July 3,1996. The search found cocaine in a trunk duffel. The question is whether that earlier warrant opportunity creates an additional Fourth Amendment condition.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The Court summarily reverses the state appellate judgment and remands for proceedings consistent with its opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 7–2. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Opposing: Stevens and Breyer, who would obtain qualified opposing presentation before deciding the merits. Their agreement with the federal automobile rule is not a vote for this immediate merits judgment.

The federal added-exigency ground is rejected. The Court orders no unconditional evidence admission, restoration of the conviction, acquittal, new trial or hearing. Any genuinely remaining issue must be properly available under state procedure and consistent with the federal holding; no independent state ground is presumed or adjudicated.

## Holdings

### Federal review and immediate summary treatment are available here

The final available state appellate judgment conclusively resolves the federal suppression issue, and the State could obtain no later effective review whether further proceedings ended in conviction or acquittal. Cox's third practical-finality category therefore permits §1257 review; the judgment's actual federal automobile ground supplies jurisdiction under Long, without an established adequate independent state basis.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela agrees with the threshold disposition without joining this rationale.

Labron/Kilgore applies Cox to a binding suppression issue that cannot effectively await later prosecution proceedings. The present opinion expressly relies on the Fourth Amendment requirement of further exigency. A State's ability to give greater protection does not establish that this judgment already rests on such a ground. Long and Evans preserve genuinely independent state law while permitting correction of this actual federal premise.

Rules 15.1 and 16.1 make opposition optional unless ordered in this noncapital matter and permit appropriate summary merits action. Respondent has filed no opposition, and counsel is not a member of this Court's bar and does not wish to become one. The Court regards the supported record and explicit settled-law error as adequate for immediate correction. It treats neither fact as respondent's consent, waiver or default, and creates no general rule dispensing with needed adversarial presentation.

### Probable cause and ready mobility require no additional emergency

A readily mobile automobile may be searched without a warrant when supported by probable cause; officers need not additionally show unforeseen urgency, imminent departure or insufficient opportunity to obtain a warrant. The accepted probable cause and moving Toyota satisfy that rule for the federal ground on which the state judgment rests, despite the earlier 13–14-hour warrant opportunity.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

Labron/Kilgore directly rejects the added warrant-impracticability condition. Carroll and Chambers establish automobile authority based on its settled predicates; Carney also recognizes the reduced privacy of vehicles in their regulated public setting. Johns makes custody alone insufficient to eliminate that authority. Earlier investigative time cannot restore the extra requirement rejected by those cases. This record supplies both actual automobile movement and accepted abundant probable cause, rather than merely a citation or an unverified suspicion.

The rule does not dispense with probable cause, justify searches of persons, authorize entry onto protected premises or resolve known mechanical incapacity. Knowles's rejection of a citation-only search leaves this independent probable-cause ground intact. No new balancing or exception is required, and this is an application of the rule to the actual erroneous suppression ground rather than an instruction to reconsider the same added condition.

### The object sought supplies the relevant scope limit

The cocaine object permits the challenged search of a trunk duffel capable of containing it within the justified automobile search. This application creates no authority to search a passenger's person or decide a different known passenger-owned-effect dispute.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela does not join this specific-container application.

Ross limits automobile search scope to places capable of containing the object supported by probable cause. The trunk duffel searched for cocaine satisfies that limit here. Houghton's separate passenger-effects disagreement supplies no additional automobile-exigency requirement, and no such ownership issue is established in this case. The Court neither infers a personal search nor decides a protected-premises or different execution claim. It preserves only genuinely distinct issues that remain properly available; it requires no new factual hearing to reconsider the settled automobile predicates.

### Reversal leaves only genuinely remaining matters for ordinary state proceedings

The state appellate judgment is reversed and the case remanded consistently with these holdings. Correction of the actual federal error does not automatically establish admissibility on every possible ground or a particular ultimate prosecution result.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the seven Justices supporting reversal.

Labron/Kilgore distinguishes application of the federal automobile rule from adjudication of every independent state or search-execution question. The State must follow the federal rule on remand and cannot reimpose the rejected urgency condition as federal law. An actually independent state ground or distinct properly preserved issue may receive its ordinary lawful treatment, but the Court establishes neither its existence nor its merit. No conviction, acquittal, new trial, reopening of a forfeited claim or new hearing is commanded.

## Precedent Treatment

- Labron/Kilgore: applies its settled automobile rule, Cox/Long framework and bounded remand; no additional federal exigency is introduced.
- Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn: applies the third practical-finality category, without creating a general interlocutory-suppression exception.
- Michigan v. Long and Arizona v. Evans: apply review of the actual federal ground and preserve genuinely independent state law; possible greater state protection is not itself an existing jurisdictional bar.
- Carroll, Chambers, Carney and Johns: retain automobile probable cause and ready mobility without an extra warrant-opportunity condition; independent delay and protected-premises limits remain.
- United States v. Ross: applies object-based scope to the cocaine search of the duffel.
- Knowles v. Iowa: distinguishes citation-only authority from independent automobile probable cause.
- Wyoming v. Houghton: preserves its specific detached passenger-effect rule; no new passenger-search issue is decided here.

## Law After Decision

The established automobile exception applies without a separate case-specific exigency or lack-of-time requirement. The actual federal suppression ground cannot stand; probable cause, ready mobility and object-based limits retain their force. No precedent is overruled or new search exception created. Ordinary state procedures govern genuinely remaining issues within the Court's mandate.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. Labron already supplies the operative rule, and the accepted probable cause and ready mobility defeat the state court's added exigency requirement. He joins the automobile and remand holdings but regards constitutionality of a separate specific-container or passenger search as analytically distinct and unpresented. He adopts no rule that automobile ownership eliminates privacy, no search authority without probable cause and no resolution of a different passenger-owned-container dispute.

Breyer, joined by Stevens, dissents from immediate summary disposition. They agree that the federal automobile exception requires no additional exigency, but would obtain qualified opposing presentation before changing this favorable criminal judgment. Known absence of an opposition and counsel's stated bar circumstances make that opportunity valuable for identifying any properly preserved threshold, scope or remedial concern. Optional opposition and authority for summary action permit immediate treatment without requiring it. Their position supplies no merits vote, final denial, additional Fourth Amendment requirement or entered response or representation order, and predicts no eventual response or disposition.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed judgment is reversed. The case returns to the Maryland courts for ordinary proceedings consistent with the federal automobile and jurisdictional holdings. The Supreme Court merits action is complete; no specific further hearing, evidentiary ruling or prosecution outcome is ordered.
