## Standards and Tests selection: The 1991 Civil Rights Act’s new liabilities and jury election

### The 1991 Civil Rights Act’s new liabilities and jury election

**Governing question:** Whether the 1991 Act’s new damages and contractual coverage reach preenactment conduct.

**Current rule:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive damages do not apply to conduct before November 21, 1991. Its jury election exists only when statutory damages under that section are available and requested; either party may then demand a jury, which must not be told the statutory caps. Section 102 damages supplement rather than duplicate §706(g) relief and exclude backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized there. Punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and are unavailable against governments, government agencies and political subdivisions. Section 101’s expansion of §1981 to contractual performance, modification, termination and enjoyment of benefits likewise does not govern preenactment conduct. Corrective purpose alone supplies no contrary temporal command; a defendant need not prove actual reliance on the former interpretation.

**Authority by component:**

- New damages, their limits and dependent jury election: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages. Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.
- Expanded contractual coverage and temporal reach: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Section 101’s new contractual coverage does not govern the earlier conduct; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; these five adopt the complete explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same temporal result through their separate relevant-activity and textual-command method. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Claims already available under the earlier law remain available on their own terms. Neither section supplies a new remedy for every earlier Title VII or §1981 wrong.

**Holdings navigation:** Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim; Section 101’s new contractual coverage does not govern the earlier conduct; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

## Standards and Tests selection: Rehabilitation Act federal-program coverage and damages consent

### Rehabilitation Act federal-program coverage and damages consent

**Governing question:** Whether the federal operator of a §504 program has consented to compensatory damages.

**Current rule:** Sections 504(a), 505 and the 1986 Amendments' §1003 do not unequivocally waive United States immunity from compensatory damages for a federally conducted academy program. A substantive nondiscrimination duty differs from consent to Treasury liability. Section 505(a)(2)'s assistance-recipient/provider formulation, §505(a)(1)'s distinct §501 employment remedies, §505(b)'s discretionary prevailing-party fee authority and §1003's State-directed remedial comparison do not create a general federal-operator damages waiver.

**Authority by component:**

- Program coverage and missing remedy-specific consent: Lane — Federal program coverage does not supply the missing consent to compensatory damages, June 20, 1996; Souter for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and himself (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Lane, June 20, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independently authorized prospective relief survives. Differently situated federal assistance-provider claims, fee applications, new officer actions and implied damages causes remain undecided; the rule does not turn a program-enrollment claim into federal employment.

**Holdings navigation:** Lane v. Peña, 518 U.S. 187 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

### Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

**Governing question:** Assessment and consequence of suppressed favorable evidence.

**Current rule:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses in the whole record, including defense preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently meet the standard; the defendant need not show that acquittal is more likely than conviction or that remaining evidence is legally insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence held by police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires. Personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Once a material Brady violation is established, a second duplicative prejudice or harmlessness inquiry cannot defeat relief by reassessing the same nondisclosure.

**Authority by component:**

- Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction: Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction, 1995-04-19. Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for the rule, application and conviction-level relief. The dissent accepts collective assessment but disputes materiality on this record.
- The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team: The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. The dissent’s consideration of the police-held material does not supply additional votes for an affirmative organizational rule.
- A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing: A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for this claim-specific ground. The dissent finds no material violation and adopts no independent harmlessness ground.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-04-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This case-team responsibility does not impute every government office’s knowledge or require unrestricted investigation or open-file discovery. Lawful corroboration, impeachment and investigation-related uses matter collectively; every nondisclosure is not structural error. Independent habeas gates and defense diligence concerning known evidence remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction; The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team; A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent psychiatric-assistance injury after surviving capital eligibility

### Independent psychiatric-assistance injury after surviving capital eligibility

**Governing question:** Whether a valid aggravator resolves an independently available Ake claim in a nonweighing capital system.

**Current rule:** Survival of a valid aggravator may preserve death eligibility but alone neither defeats a properly available claim of denied psychiatric assistance required by Ake nor establishes no effect on selection of death. Removing future dangerousness as an aggravator does not itself correct lost assistance in answering evidence heard by the jury. Relevant examination and help with evaluation, preparation, presentation, cross-examination, rebuttal and supported mitigation remain distinct from a right to a preferred psychiatrist or another examination merely on request. If an available violation stands, review must address the relevant penalty record and actual loss of those functions; counting examiners does not resolve adequacy.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent injury, eligibility/selection distinction and limits: A surviving aggravator does not dispose of the independent assistance claim, 1995-10-30; Stevens’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Tuggle, 1995-10-30.

**Open question:** Whether harmless-error review applies, the governing collateral standard, actual effect and ultimate relief remain reserved. Correcting a reviewing rationale supplies no immediate writ or hearing. Brecht’s Doyle and O’Neal’s personal-intent Chapman rules are not extended to this category. Review remains subject to the actual prior rulings and legally available procedural defenses.

**Holdings navigation:** Tuggle v. Netherland, 516 U.S. 10.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Carriger v. Stewart — Pending capital habeas and whole-record innocence review; Carriger v. Stewart — Conditional disclosure materiality and witness files; Calderon v. Thompson — Recall of completed habeas merits mandates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

### Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

**Governing question:** Facial review of indecency restrictions on a general-purpose communications network.

**Current rule:** A content-based criminal restriction fails the First Amendment when uncertain, substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the government does not establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. Knowledge that a minor is present, other available channels and scienter do not by themselves cure uncertain content categories or adult-access burdens. Age-verification defenses must afford workable protection on the actual record; cost, loss of anonymity and lawful adult exclusion remain material. A severability clause permits removal of a discrete unlawful textual branch but does not authorize judicial creation of a minor-only offense or an absent value exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Overbreadth, uncertainty and narrow fit: Reno v. ACLU, Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment; textual severance boundary: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (eight), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Reno v. ACLU, 1997-06-26.

**Open question:** Precisely drawn future controls with effective nonexclusionary age assurance, parental-approval questions, every Internet service's classification and extraterritorial scope remain open. The holding creates no independent Fifth Amendment rule and leaves obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful contact to their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union (1997-06-26) — First Amendment / Internet content restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Proof before a potentially indefinite complete parental-contact cutoff

### Proof before a potentially indefinite complete parental-contact cutoff

**Governing question:** The sexual-abuse finding triggering Louisiana Revised Statutes §9:364(D)'s severe parental-contact restriction.

**Current rule:** Sexual abuse of the parent's child or children must be proved by clear and convincing evidence before §9:364(D) imposes mandatory loss of all visitation and contact until an adversarial hearing establishes both successful completion of a program designed for sexual abusers and that supervised visitation is in the children's best interests. The statutory treatment requires evaluation and psychotherapy specifically designed for perpetrators of family violence and conducted by licensed mental-health professionals. Bare preponderance cannot supply the predicate for that complete, potentially indefinite exclusion.

**Authority by component:**

- Heightened proof for the specified deprivation: Clear and convincing proof must precede this statutory all-contact restriction, 1995-11-20; Stone-Zsela, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** A. St. P. C. v. B. C., 1995-11-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary investigation, emergency intervention, temporary separation and lesser custody or supervised-contact measures retain their existing requirements. The rule supplies no universal custody burden, periodic-review schedule, restoration procedure, treatment-admission condition or entitlement to contact.

**Holdings navigation:** A. St. P. C. v. B. C..

## Standards and Tests selection: Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions

### Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions

**Governing question:** The manner of state-compelled severance of an established caregiving relationship to implement otherwise lawful custody entitlement.

**Current rule:** The child has an independent liberty interest against serious, avoidable injury attributable to the manner of compulsory implementation. Before implementation, the child's representative must receive a meaningful, prompt opportunity to present concrete evidence of that transition harm and practicable means of avoiding it; the entitled parent must be able to respond, and the court must consider the issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. Mathews requires attention to the identified private interest, error risk and value of further procedures, and governmental interests and burdens. Procedures and protective measures must fit the actual showing, proceed promptly and preserve lawful parental custody entitlement.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded child interest: I. The child's own interest limits the manner of compulsory implementation; fitted process and relief: II. The required opportunity and relief must fit the actual transition claim, 1996-06-21; Stone-Zsela Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Baby Richard v. Kirchner, 1996-06-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Caregiving duration does not itself prove serious injury. No right to preserve an invalid adoption, defeat a fit parent through a best-interests comparison, or remain indefinitely with nonparents follows. Unsupported requests may be rejected and adequate consideration may support immediate implementation. There is no automatic evidentiary hearing, fixed delay, appointed-clinician requirement, prescribed proof burden or renewed custody contest. Independently lawful emergency protection remains available.

**Holdings navigation:** Baby Richard, by his guardian ad litem, v. Kirchner (In re Petition of Kirchner).

## Standards and Tests selection: Treatment refusal, suicide assistance and class-wide constitutional claims

### Treatment refusal, suicide assistance and class-wide constitutional claims

**Governing question:** Substantive due process and equal protection of intentional physician assistance in suicide for competent terminally ill adults.

**Current rule:** The Due Process Clause does not establish the asserted class-wide fundamental right to a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. A prohibition therefore receives rational-basis review on that claim, and may rationally protect life, prevent suicide, guard vulnerable people against error and pressure, preserve medical responsibility and contain risks of a legalized lethal practice. Equal protection permits a rational distinction between competent refusal of unwanted treatment and intentional suicide assistance based on bodily consent, legal causation, intent and professional responsibility. Death as a shared ultimate result and the practical option available to life-support-dependent patients do not compel permission for lethal assistance. Good-faith palliation may foresee a shorter life without adopting death as its purpose; labels or a patient's wish alone do not settle purpose or causation.

**Authority by component:**
- No fundamental class entitlement and rational review: Glucksberg, Washington v. Glucksberg — I. The asserted physician-assistance right is not fundamental and Washington v. Glucksberg — II. Washington's prohibition rationally serves its protective interests, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas (five), 1997-06-26.
- Equal-protection distinction: Vacco, Vacco v. Quill — New York may distinguish treatment refusal from intentional suicide assistance, Thomas with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy (five), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Vacco and Glucksberg, 1997-06-26, combined from the same start-of-day baseline.

**Exceptions and limits:** No exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties is adopted. Casey's defined reproductive right and complete regulation framework remain intact. The rule does not authorize forced treatment, displace competent refusal or valid directives, allow discriminatory denial of lawful pain care, or punish good-faith palliation merely for foreseeable death. Materially different individual claims and discriminatory administration remain open; the reservation recognizes no entitlement or automatic hearing. Rational review requires no proof that prohibition is the best policy or adoption of a proposed narrower scheme, and States may choose otherwise within constitutional limits.

**Holdings navigation:** Washington v. Glucksberg (1997-06-26) — Civil Rights / Treatment refusal and assisted death; Washington v. Glucksberg (1997-06-26) — Whether Washington's prohibition rationally serves legitimate protective interests; Vacco v. Quill (1997-06-26) — Civil Rights / Treatment refusal and assisted death.

## Standards and Tests selection: Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims

### Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims?

**Current rule:** A §2 single-member fragmentation claim requires a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. These are necessary, not alone sufficient; totality remains. Distinct groups' numbers cannot replace missing cohesion or bloc-voting proof. Coalition cognizability and the population denominator remain open. A State need not prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts; challengers initially must show unequal opportunity caused by the arrangement under the totality, while a federal court may order such districts only as necessary to remedy an established violation. Independent constitutional limits remain. Assuming an influence claim exists, majority bloc voting remains necessary; modification or elimination of the district-majority prerequisite and influence cognizability remain unresolved. The necessary voting conditions do not require maximization of majority-minority districts. Unequal electoral opportunity under the totality does not require discriminatory purpose. Relevant proportionality compares the group’s share of the relevant population with the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect preferred candidates; its weight depends on the other evidence. It is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone does not establish liability. Nominal district labels and officeholders’ race do not replace demonstrated opportunity. No proportional-election entitlement, racial floor or ceiling, universal geographic scale or population denominator follows. Opportunity elsewhere cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another electoral barrier.

**Authority by component:**

- 3. Fragmentation claims require proof of the causal voting conditions: Growe v. Emison — 3. Fragmentation claims require proof of the causal voting conditions, 1993-02-23. Scalia's statutory opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt the stated single-member extension and record application. No fractured-opinion or Marks inference is required.
- H1. Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts: Voinovich v. Quilter — H1. Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts, 1993-03-02. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II.B, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the complete proposition; it directly controls the rejection of the lower court's statutory prohibition and burden allocation.
- H2. The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting: Voinovich v. Quilter — H2. The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting, 1993-03-02. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts II.A and II.C, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt this independently dispositive rejection of the presented Section 2 claim and each stated reservation.

- No maximization duty and proportionality within the totality: Johnson v. De Grandy — 3. Necessary voting conditions do not require maximization; 4. Proportional opportunity is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor, 1994-06-30. Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the complete necessary-versus-sufficient distinction and rejection of maximization. Thomas and Scalia's statutory noncoverage position is a different rationale and supplies no join. Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this contextual rule and its explanation. Kennedy separately accepts the exact limited propositions that proportionality is relevant, that the necessary conditions alone do not establish liability, and that proportionality supplies no safe harbor; his concurrence does not join Part III's full rationale or Parts IV and V's affirmative applications. No combined rationale under Marks is needed.

**Present operation effective:** Johnson v. De Grandy, 1994-06-30, for the maximization and proportionality limits; the earlier Growe and Voinovich components remain effective on their original dates.

**Holdings navigation:** Growe v. Emison, 507 U.S. 25 (1993); Voinovich v. Quilter, 507 U.S. 146 (1993); Necessary voting conditions do not require maximization; Proportional opportunity is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA equitable categories and nonfiduciary compensation

### ERISA equitable categories and nonfiduciary compensation

**Governing question:** Available equitable categories and individual redress under ERISA §502(a)(3).

**Current rule:** Section 502(a)(3)'s appropriate equitable relief means traditionally equitable categories, not compensatory damages merely because equity could award them in trust litigation. Compensation from an admitted nonfiduciary for plan losses is unavailable under that provision. The rule does not prohibit every equitable monetary remedy or decide Mertens’s expressly disclaimed antecedent nonfiduciary cause-of-action question; restitution is not reopened. Sections 409 and 502(a)(2)’s plan-centered remedy does not categorically bar §502(a)(3)’s appropriate individual equity for an actual §404 violation. Adequate relief under another ERISA provision normally makes further equity unnecessary and inappropriate, without an absolute prohibition governing every alternative pleading. Relief must redress an actual ERISA or plan obligation; veil piercing alone supplies no freestanding violation.

**Authority by component:**

- The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief: Mertens v. Hewitt Associates — The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief, 1993-06-01. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.
- Varity — Part III, individual equity and qualified adequacy principle, 1996-03-19; Souter, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (five); Stone-Zsela’s stricter condition is noncontrolling.
- Peacock — ERISA enforcement requires an independently violated statutory or plan obligation, 1996-02-21; Thomas, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Varity, March 19, 1996, resolving the categorical individual-fiduciary-redress question; Mertens’s equitable-category limit survives.

**Holdings navigation:** Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248 (1993); Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489; Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349.

**Exceptions and limits:** Restoration supplies existing membership rights, not immutable benefits. The controlling rule does not classify every substituted monetary item as equitable or adopt all trust-law compensation. An adequate benefits remedy is not established merely because that statutory route exists in the abstract.

## Standing State selection: 3. Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.
