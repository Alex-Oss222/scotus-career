#### Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The appellate alignment is Johnson v. Miller; the three docket aliases identify one constitutional challenge and one reviewed judgment for Georgia.

For the other questions in Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson), decided June 29, 1995, Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929. see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury (Personal exposure and standing are distinct from success on the merits); Federal Courts — Direct review of three-judge merits judgments (The merits denial of an injunction is directly reviewable).

##### Racial predominance alone does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, even when ordinary districting criteria are subordinated, does not by itself establish the asserted equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. An independently cognizable deprivation remains governed by its own elements, burdens, justification requirements and remedy; it need not arise from a second governmental act or produce a later loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes, applying Shaw.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, inferior civic status and other recognized wrongs retain their own elements. The Court neither applies rational-basis review to a conceded racial burden nor immunizes policies labeled inclusive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw v. Reno: applies its territorial-design rule and independent-injury qualification; the rule is retained despite the dissent’s express proposal to replace it.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: preserves review of boundaries that themselves accomplish racial exclusion.
- Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co. and Adarand Constructors v. Peña: distinguished by the operative allocation of opportunities; their own governing standards remain intact.

##### The asserted sorting, status and representation inferences do not establish a distinct deprivation here

**Holding:** A status or participation injury can be complete when government itself imposes unequal racial treatment; no additional monetary loss, electoral defeat or later denial of service is necessary. But predominant composition of this shared electorate does not alone establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race, and these asserted inferences therefore do not establish a distinct violation on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part III, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A discriminatory participation or constituent-service practice requires its own examination; no such practice is established and no general entitlement to favorable political attention is created. Gingles's statutory proof is not subject to a new constitutional-injury gate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Martin: preserves protection against official racial electoral treatment without requiring a changed winner.
- McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents and White v. Regester: preserve meaningful equality and participation beyond formal eligibility; no comparable operative restriction is established here.
- Thornburg v. Gingles: preserves claim-specific use of voting evidence, not racial stereotyping or an added constitutional-injury gate for statutory claims.

##### Absence of federal compulsion does not itself prohibit voluntary inclusion

**Holding:** A State’s otherwise lawful consideration of racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity is not unconstitutional merely because no prior adjudication or federal statute requires the precise district chosen. This negative proposition leaves every independent constitutional and statutory restriction intact and creates neither a duty to maximize majority-minority districts nor a right to proportional officeholding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes for the limited permission-versus-compulsion proposition. Only Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that this plan survives the asserted constitutional challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All nine adopt only the absence-of-compulsion negative proposition; the plan's survival has five votes. No finding that this plan achieved inclusion or a newly proved remedial purpose follows. Sections 2 and 5 keep their distinct elements and channels, and no federal administrative action is set aside.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Voinovich v. Quilter: applies the distinction between lawful state choice and federal obligation.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: retains the no-maximization rule and claim-specific totality inquiry, without proportionality as a safe harbor.
- Beer v. United States and City of Pleasant Grove v. United States: preserve distinct nonretrogression and discriminatory-purpose requirements.
- Morris v. Gressette: preserves restrictions on reviewing federal preclearance determinations, distinct from section 14(b)’s designated channel; this judgment sets aside no federal administrative action.

##### District shape is evidence, not a threshold requirement

**Holding:** Bizarre shape is not a necessary gateway to an otherwise cognizable constitutional districting claim, and unusual shape does not itself prove the violation. Direct and circumstantial evidence may establish the facts material to the particular claim, subject to its own elements and proof requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-A, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes at this evidentiary level; they disagree about the substantive effect of proved predominance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purpose evidence can include shape, demographics, statements, instructions, departures from ordinary criteria and actual operation, with supported alternatives considered. No universal compactness cutoff, expert report, commissioned study or replacement map is required; any comparator or proof required by the actual claim remains necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: treats geography as evidence of an operative constitutional wrong, not a universal shape test.
- Wright v. Rockefeller: distinguished because this case accepts predominant racial purpose rather than finding it unproved.
- Shaw v. Reno: preserves ordinary pleading and claim-specific proof without an aesthetic threshold.

##### The supported predominance finding is accepted without dictating its legal consequence

**Holding:** Trial findings concerning districting purpose receive ordinary clear-error review, while the legal consequence of those findings is decided separately. The supported finding that race predominated and traditional criteria were subordinated is accepted here; no supported basis for setting it aside is supplied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-B, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes on acceptance and review of the finding; the constitutional consequence divides 5–4 as stated above.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Acceptance of racial predominance does not establish every constitutional element. The conditional statutory-compliance defense discussion, although joined on its hypothesis by all nine, is nonessential to the judgment and supplies no independent alternative holding, scrutiny trigger or invalidation of DOJ action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Bessemer City: applies ordinary clear-error review to the supported factual finding.
- Shaw v. Reno: controls the distinct legal consequence; changing an assumed predicate into an accepted finding does not itself displace the rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the three-judge district court's judgment for Georgia and denial of injunctive relief in Nos. 94-631, 94-797 and 94-929, without remand. No injunction or redrawing order is unwound and no new injury inquiry, map order, election cancellation or retained supervision is imposed. The judgment does not certify the entire plan or adjudicate separate claims against federal officials.

#### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-357.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, No. 92-357, see Federal Courts — Review of federal preclearance determinations.

##### Territorial racial design alone does not state the asserted deprivation

**Holding:** Alleged deliberate or predominant consideration of race in drawing territorial constituencies, even with departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself state this equal-protection claim where no racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation is alleged. This is a substantive disposition of the design-only theory, not heightened pleading, a universal requirement of additional downstream loss, or an added constitutional-injury prerequisite for an independent statutory claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary notice pleading applies; no expert proof or replacement map is required at pleading. Independent exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, civic-status and statutory claims retain their own elements. No statewide racial-strength defense, general replacement of Croson, predominance test or added constitutional-injury gate for §2 is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the design-only state claim (5–4); no injunction, new map or trial on that theory. Other properly presented claims and any properly sought amendment remain subject to ordinary procedure and preclusion. Leave federal-official dismissal undisturbed (9–0) on its separate channel and nonreviewability grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United Jewish Organizations v. Carey, 430 U.S. 144: its fractured reasoning supports a limited distinction, not a comprehensive controlling test or a statewide-white-representation defense.
- Wright v. Rockefeller, 376 U.S. 52: distinguished; racial purpose was not established there, so it did not decide the present assumed-purpose question.
- City of Richmond v. Croson, 488 U.S. 469, and Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1: personal equality retained; the Court declines to equate territorial design alone with Croson’s racial allocation of opportunities.
- Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399: official racial ballot labeling remains prohibited without proof that an election winner changed.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339: racial municipal disenfranchisement remains prohibited; Whittaker’s broader concurrence is not adopted as a rule covering every racial district adjustment.
- White v. Regester, 412 U.S. 755, and McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents, 339 U.S. 637: impaired participation may be unconstitutional despite formal access; no universal downstream-loss requirement.
- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41; Leatherman, March 3, 1993: ordinary notice pleading retained, no demand for expert proof or a replacement map.
- Growe and Voinovich: statutory prerequisites, state primacy and unresolved coalition/influence questions remain distinct; no additional constitutional-injury gate imposed on § 2.

## Event

Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson; Ninth Circuit Nos. 97-15030 and 97-15031, 122 F.3d 692. Decided November 10, 1998, on review of the Ninth Circuit's vacatur of a preliminary injunction against Proposition 209. The appellate opinion was amended August 21, 1997. The Court considers whether selective removal of authority over race- and sex-conscious measures violates equal protection, whether Title VII preempts that removal, and what preliminary relief is justified. Review proceeds on full merits briefing; no argument date is established. The rehearing writings concern this same proceeding.

## Participation

## Public Action

## Holdings

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion controls Parts I, II and IV with the five votes of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III has all nine Justices' support. The following four holdings carry those respective coalitions.

### I. Selective racial restructuring requires a political-process inquiry

**Rule:** A State cannot answer a supported claim that it selectively moved authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process merely by calling the foreclosed measures preferences. On the district court's preliminary findings, California's removal of those measures to constitutional amendment, while ordinary preferences remain available through ordinary officials, supports confined protection against the resulting unequal political burden; it neither requires a preference nor validates an unconstitutional program.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter addresses a special political hurdle for racial protection; Seattle applies that principle to selective relocation of decisionmaking over racial remedies even when the underlying measure exceeds federal requirements. Romer preserves that limited doctrine without promising favorable legislation at every governmental level. The district court found both lost opportunities and the special amendment hurdle. Equal words about all races do not dispose of how that allocation operates. Crawford permits withdrawal of a voluntary policy, but does not eliminate Seattle's distinct inquiry into selectively changing who may decide racial questions. The State can enforce the constitutional limits on each proposed program; its interest in preventing unlawful classifications does not alone justify excluding measures that satisfy those limits. Croson, Hopwood and Engineering Contractors continue to require justification and fit. The Court decides the basis for limited preliminary relief, not the permanent validity of every provision or any particular program, and finds no need to attribute animus to voters.

### II. The sex theory requires a distinct inquiry

**Rule:** The racial political-process rule does not by itself resolve a challenge to removing authority over sex-conscious measures. The lower court must examine the asserted sex-based barrier on its own terms, applying the important-objective and substantial-relationship standard to an actual sex classification without presuming that every statewide policy withdrawal creates such a classification.

**Controlling explanation:** Virginia and Engineering Contractors require a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and a sex classification; they do not treat a stereotype as justification. Hunter and Seattle concern racial political structure and do not automatically decide every sex-conscious policy. The appellate categorical treatment omitted the separate inquiry. Section 31(c) preserves bona fide sex qualifications reasonably necessary to normal governmental operations in the three covered fields; it is neither a general permission for sex preferences nor proof that the initiative rests on a stereotype. The Court remands the distinct theory without finding all sex-neutrality rules invalid or extending racial strict scrutiny to them.

### III. Federal permission does not establish blanket preemption

**Rule:** Title VII's permission for defined voluntary affirmative action does not alone preempt a State's withdrawal of that policy or guarantee every subordinate governmental entity power to adopt it. An actual conflict with a federal statutory duty or binding federal remedy remains governed by federal supremacy and the applicable savings provisions; no such conflict is decided categorically for every operation of this initiative.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber and Johnson recognize lawful voluntary measures; they do not make every such measure compulsory. Section 703(j) prevents treating numerical imbalance in employment, union membership or related categories, measured against community, geographic or available-workforce figures, as itself requiring preferential treatment. It does not excuse actual unlawful discrimination. Section 708 separately preserves state duties except where state law requires or permits an unlawful employment practice. Section 1104 disclaims occupation of the field and preserves state law unless inconsistent with a provision or purpose of the Civil Rights Act. Those provisions cannot be collapsed into an unlimited exception. Proposition 209 protects existing orders, necessary federal-program eligibility and maximum lawful implementation on its actual terms. The district court's broad statutory theory therefore cannot sustain its entire injunction. Concrete federal conflicts and any distinct question of deference to administrative guidelines remain undecided.

### IV. Preliminary relief must correspond to the supported injury

**Rule:** Interim relief must protect against the demonstrated threatened loss of access to otherwise available lawful racial remedies, while leaving independently lawful provisions operative to the extent severable under state law. The lower court must conform its order to that constitutional injury and the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements, separately resolve the sex theory and concrete federal conflicts, and avoid turning interim protection into a permanent entitlement to a program.

**Controlling explanation:** The district findings supply a present burden on ordinary political access for preliminary consideration, not merely disagreement with an election result. They do not resolve the lawfulness of every affected program, the exact threatened injury defining an injunction's scope, or all remaining equitable prerequisites. The original order extended to implementation insofar as the initiative prohibited or affected affirmative-action programs throughout employment, education and contracting. That formulation may reach beyond the supported injury and does not justify automatic restoration of every part of the order. Section 31(h) requires maximum lawful implementation and severance of an invalid portion. The distinct ban on discrimination, genuine operational qualifications and other independently valid applications must be respected. The Court vacates the appellate judgment that categorically foreclosed the constitutional claim and returns the matter for a fitted order and further proceedings. No injunction validates a program that fails Croson, Hopwood or Engineering Contractors.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 31 bars governmental discrimination and preferential treatment based on race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin in public employment, education and contracting. It applies only to action after its effective date. Its sex exception is limited to bona fide qualifications reasonably necessary to normal public employment, education or contracting. It does not invalidate an order or consent decree already in force on the effective date; this protection is not confined to federal decrees and does not automatically extend to every later decree. It preserves action necessary to establish or maintain federal-program eligibility when ineligibility would cost the State federal funds, rather than every federally permitted action. Its governmental coverage includes the State and its local governments, public university systems including the University of California, community-college and school districts, special districts and other political subdivisions or instrumentalities. Remedies are the same regardless of the injured person's race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin and are those otherwise available under then-existing California antidiscrimination law. The section is self-executing, must operate to the maximum extent federal law and the Constitution permit, and severs an invalid provision from the remainder. These conditions do not themselves establish whether a particular program is lawful.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hunter v. Erickson is applied to the special political hurdle for racial protection; its force does not depend on a right to win a policy dispute.
- Washington v. Seattle School District is applied to selective relocation of racial remedial authority; a voluntary underlying policy is not outside its concern for that reason alone.
- Crawford v. Board of Education is distinguished at its policy-withdrawal scope, which remains effective without erasing Seattle's structural inquiry.
- Romer v. Evans is applied as preserving those racial precedents while rejecting a universal entitlement to favorable law at every governmental level.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. remains the local racial-classification standard; the political-process ruling excuses no defective program.
- Hopwood v. Texas retains its supported educational-diversity justification and required fit; permission does not compel a program.
- Engineering Contractors retains local racial strict scrutiny and the distinct demanding intermediate scrutiny for sex classifications; its limits govern affected measures on remand.
- United States v. Virginia supplies the genuine-objective and substantial-relationship requirement for sex classifications, not an automatic racial political-process rule.
- Adarand Constructors v. Pena retains its distinct federal standard; that standard is not transferred to state or local programs.

## Law After Decision

The racial political-process rule applies to a supported selective relocation of authority over otherwise lawful remedies despite a substantive preference label. It does not require a program, permanently invalidate every part of Proposition 209, or displace the existing constitutional standards for racial and sex classifications. The sex challenge remains distinct. Title VII permission alone supplies no blanket preemption; concrete federal duties retain their force. Preliminary relief must track the supported injury and preserve severable lawful operation.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, agrees that voluntary-action permission does not establish blanket Title VII preemption, but would affirm vacatur of the preliminary injunction. The dissent treats Proposition 209 as a statewide substantive restriction on officials' classifications, leaving ordinary antidiscrimination protection available. Crawford preserves the State's choice to withdraw optional policies; lawful remedies under Hopwood and Engineering Contractors are not compulsory policies. Hunter concerned unequal access to ordinary racial protection, and Seattle concerned specially displaced desegregation authority. In the dissent's view, those decisions do not entrench every optional preference-making power at a subordinate governmental level. The reciprocal sex provision likewise does not establish the asserted facial denial of equal protection. This position preserves scrutiny of actual unequal administration, binding federal obligations and properly presented later applications, and does not declare every affirmative measure unconstitutional.

## Procedure After Action

The preliminary-injunction appeal returns for conforming relief and further proceedings. The lower courts must distinguish the supported racial structural injury from the unresolved sex theory and concrete statutory conflicts, respect the initiative's actual exceptions and severability, and apply existing standards to any affected program. No final liability judgment, damages award or mandatory preference issues.

# Lopez v. Monterey County — 1999-01-20

## Event

Lopez v. Monterey County, No. 97-1396. Argued November 2, 1998; decided January 20, 1999. On direct review of the three-judge Northern District of California's December 19, 1997 dismissal after the Court's November 6, 1996 mandate. The questions concern the County's administration of state-mandated judicial-election changes, the reach of the 1983 submission, prospective nonuse and the dependent judicial-tenure order. California defends dismissal; the County supports the appellants.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The dismissal is reversed and the case remanded; the dependent tenure-order disposition is set aside to the extent stated below.

## Holdings

**Authority and scope.** Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court. The eight Associate Justices join Parts I–V. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II and IV, and Part III only insofar as it rejects delay as authorization for prospective use and rejects the internal-administration characterization of a direct electorate change. He agrees in rejection of the collateral-designation defense and in the dependent tenure-order disposition without adding grounds to those parts. Each controlling part thus has at least eight direct joins. No separate constitutional justification for Section 5 is adopted.

### I. State compulsion does not exempt covered administration

**Holding and operative rule.** A covered political subdivision must obtain Section 5 preclearance before administering a postbaseline voting change within that subdivision, even when a noncovered State mandates the change and leaves no local discretion. The duty attaches to the County's administration; it does not require California to submit its laws in the abstract.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court applies its November 6, 1996 Lopez mandate. That decision expressly assumed complete state displacement of local discretion and nevertheless held that giving the change operative effect is administration. The lower court's renewed reliance on state compulsion cannot reopen the settled question. Neither the 1979 law nor the January 1995 elimination of justice courts supplies an exemption from the County's duty.

Young preserves the difference between the source of a policy and its actual administration, while reserving a different question about wholly federally compelled conduct. Foreman likewise rejects the proposition that an earlier authorization automatically clears subsequent local implementation. California's argument that the County cannot choose a different arrangement confuses authority to enact with authority to administer an unprecleared change. Practical difficulty affects lawful implementation, not whether the completed mandate governs. The Court decides no discriminatory purpose, retrogressive effect or entitlement to substantive preclearance.

### II. The actual submission controls the reach of approval

**Holding and operative rule.** Replacing district electorates with a countywide electorate for judges is a covered change in voting, and a submission does not preclear earlier operative changes it failed to identify. The District Court must determine the actual reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance; its attachment and the Attorney General's nonobjection do not establish blanket approval of the preceding consolidations.

**Controlling explanation.** The prior Lopez decision fixes the direct electoral character of the change and the treatment of the earlier unidentified ordinances. Presley distinguishes internal allocation of governmental authority from changes in who votes for an official; this alteration concerns the electorate itself. Allen and Perkins preserve coverage of such practical electoral changes.

McCain and Clark require identification of what was submitted. Young and Foreman preserve that submission-specific boundary. The 1983 additional-judgeships submission, attached final ordinance and nonobjection are relevant evidence, but they do not establish the operative terms needed to decide conclusively whether the final ordinance itself was approved. That question remains for determination below. The Court does not invalidate an actual approval, review the merits of the Attorney General's decision, or treat an unavailable source as a party's failure to comply.

### III. Delay and a collateral designation challenge do not authorize prospective use

**Holding and operative rule.** Delay does not authorize prospective administration of an operative covered change that lacks preclearance, and the effective Section 4(b) coverage determination cannot be collaterally retried in this Section 5 enforcement action. Statutory bailout remains a distinct route; reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on legally available retrospective relief or a lawful transition, without supplying clearance.

**Controlling explanation.** California's remaining defenses must be separated rather than treated as another version of compulsion. Lopez already resolved prospective nonuse and the improper collateral challenge to designation. Clark and Hampton County preserve attention to remedial circumstances without making delay an authorization to use an uncleared practice. The direct change in judicial electorates likewise cannot be recast as merely internal administration under Presley. These applications enforce the existing mandate; they announce no new facial constitutional holding, decide no bailout request and create no general retrospective equitable rule.

### IV. Nonuse and the confined judicial-plan alternative

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court must prevent prospective use of established operative changes lacking preclearance until approval is obtained through the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia. A necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by the federal court is distinct only if supported by independent lawful remedial authority, consistent with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, no more displacing of state law than necessity requires, and limited to the period before a lawful precleared arrangement becomes available.

**Controlling explanation.** Connor distinguishes a genuinely judicial plan from governmental policy, and McDaniel prevents a governmental plan from becoming exempt merely through judicial adoption. Lopez and Young apply that distinction while preserving nonuse. The lawful alternatives include the lawful precleared baseline, completed preclearance, or an independently justified judicial plan meeting the stated limits. The Court selects none here and does not authorize a court to adopt the County's policy as its own.

Clark left open whether extreme circumstances could justify allowing an election to proceed while preclearance remained pending. No such circumstance is established here, and ordinary administrative difficulty supplies no exception. The earlier ordinary six-year at-large elections did not qualify under the prior mandate. Shaw preserves substantive purpose-and-effect determinations for the designated preclearance forums. The enforcement court's task is coverage, actual submission and lawful implementation, not its own substantive approval.

### V. The dependent tenure order requires lawful reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The disposition of the judicial-term-extension order must be set aside insofar as it depends on the rejected exemption and reconsidered under the corrected legal premise and actual circumstances. Reversal does not automatically revive an expired arrangement, extend any judge's tenure, install or remove an officeholder, cancel a completed election or invalidate judicial acts.

**Controlling explanation.** The District Court vacated its September 25, 1996 extension order together with dismissal of the enforcement action. Once the exemption premise fails, that dependent disposition cannot stand on the same ground. Its correction nevertheless requires lawful authority and the actual tenure circumstances; the mandate did not select officeholders or create indefinite holdovers.

Prior preclearance of a four-division option resolves only its preclearance status. It does not settle independent state-law, electorate-and-jurisdiction linkage, population, divided-municipality, residence or remedial-authority questions. Johnson's rejection of a design-only racial injury likewise does not approve all features of a proposed arrangement. The District Court must apply the existing mandate to the actual remaining questions without treating preclearance as complete legal approval. No new submission deadline, compulsory hearing or automatic return to this Court is ordered.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Lopez v. Monterey County, November 6, 1996:** Enforced unchanged as the governing administration, direct-electorate, submission, nonuse and confined-remedy mandate; its state-compulsion question is not reopened.
- **Young v. Fordice, March 31, 1997:** Applied for actual administration, identified submission and the limits on judicial implementation; its wholly federal-compulsion reservation remains distinct.
- **Foreman v. Dallas County, June 27, 1997:** Applied to preserve actual baselines and submission-specific approval; authorization does not automatically approve later implementation.
- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission:** Distinguished because the electorate changes directly; its internal-governance boundary remains.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections:** Applied to direct changes affecting electoral choice, without deciding discriminatory purpose or effect.
- **Perkins v. Matthews:** Applied to practical electoral operation rather than the label or source of the change.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Applied to require identification of the submitted change; related material does not establish blanket approval.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Applied for specific submission and prospective nonuse; its reserved extreme-circumstance possibility is not established here.
- **NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission:** Applied to distinguish prospective compliance from properly bounded remedial attention to actual circumstances.
- **Connor v. Johnson:** Preserved for a necessary plan wholly formulated by the court within independent lawful authority.
- **McDaniel v. Sanchez:** Applied to distinguish judicial adoption of governmental policy from a wholly judicial plan.
- **Shaw v. Reno:** Applied at the entering law's stated boundary preserving designated substantive-preclearance channels, without a new constitutional merits ruling.
- **Johnson v. Miller:** Its rule against a design-only racial injury remains distinct from other constitutional, state-law and remedial objections to a proposed judicial-election plan.

## Law After Decision

The County's obligation to preclear administered changes is unchanged. State compulsion, delay and attachment of a related ordinance do not dispense with it. The actual scope of the 1983 approval remains to be determined. Prospective nonuse must be enforced, with any genuinely judicial temporary plan meeting every stated necessity, authority, legal-compliance and duration limit. The dependent tenure question returns for lawful reconsideration without automatic reinstatement or a selected replacement system.
