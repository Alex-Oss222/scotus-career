#### MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

#### NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. / Ludwig v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-1612; 93-1613.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### Reasoned approval of annuity brokerage falls within the banking power

**Holding:** The business of banking under 12 U.S.C. §24 Seventh is not exhausted by its enumerated activities, and the Comptroller's reasonable construction may include financial-intermediary agency sales of the fixed, variable and combined annuity products covered by this approval. The construction must remain within banking and its incidental powers and comply with enacted restrictions; this conditioned brokerage approval authorizes no underwriting, issuance or guarantee of the insurers' obligations and no unrelated commercial enterprise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clarke v. Securities Industry Association, 479 U.S. 388: applies its substantial respect for reasonable Comptroller banking-law construction and brokerage analysis to this agency activity.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies deference within the ambiguity left after the enacted banking boundaries are determined; clear prohibitions still govern.
- Investment Company Institute v. Camp, 401 U.S. 617: preserves substantive banking restrictions and the need for an actual reasoned agency judgment, which this approval supplies.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218, and Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115: preserve text-first review and actual statutory limits; neither displaces permissible construction within a genuine delegation.

##### Section 92 does not defeat this annuity classification

**Holding:** The Comptroller reasonably classifies the annuity agency products covered by this approval as investment instruments outside the insurance category of 12 U.S.C. §92 for this statutory purpose, so §92 does not invalidate the approval. The Court does not decide whether §92 exclusively limits genuine insurance-agency activity to banks in qualifying small places, and it neither repeals that provision's conditions nor determines classification under a distinct state insurance law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America, Inc., 508 U.S. 439: applies the statutory-survival holding and respects its reservation of scope and agency-construction questions; no repeal issue is reopened.
- Chevron, 467 U.S. 837, and Clarke, 479 U.S. 388: apply permissible, reasoned construction to this statutory classification after examining the text and product features.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Comptroller's approval separately requires advertising, promotional materials and customer descriptions to disclose that the annuities are not bank products and are not FDIC insured, together with a signed purchaser acknowledgment of agency status and lack of federal insurance. Brokerage does not issue, underwrite or guarantee an insurer's undertaking. Applicable banking, securities and insurance requirements otherwise remain.

When §92 applies, it requires the national bank to be located and doing business in a place of no more than 5,000 inhabitants under the last preceding decennial census; the insurer must be authorized by the State where the bank is located; and the agency must operate under Comptroller rules. It permits solicitation and sale of insurance and collection of premiums for agreed fees or commissions, but prohibits the bank from assuming or guaranteeing premium payment and separately prohibits guaranteeing the truth of the insured applicant's statements. Those statutory terms are not the same as this approval's disclosure conditions. Section 24's distinction between without-recourse, customer-order/customer-account securities transactions and own-account activity remains; no own-account investment exception is adjudicated. No individual customer's contract, universal annuity classification or separate state-law preemption dispute is resolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit is reversed in both consolidated dockets, 9–0, and the conditioned approval reinstated. Advertising, promotional materials and customer descriptions must disclose that these are not bank products and are not FDIC insured, with signed acknowledgment of agency status and lack of federal insurance. Otherwise applicable banking, securities and insurance rules remain; no new agency rationale, underwriting or bank guarantee is authorized.

#### National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank & Trust Co.; AT&T Family Federal Credit Union v. First National Bank & Trust Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-843 and 96-847.

**Decided:** 1998-02-25.

##### Unrelated occupational groups cannot replace the common bond

**Holding:** Section 109’s occupational route requires a common occupational bond connecting the membership of the occupationally defined credit union; it does not authorize aggregation of unrelated employer groups merely because each has its own internal bond. The challenged agency policy crosses that statutory boundary and receives no deference at Chevron’s first step.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Multiple employer groups may share a genuine common occupational bond; no single-employer rule is adopted. The Court does not decide the full breadth of an occupation or association, geographic charters, individual retention rights, or invalidate an interpretation merely because it changed.

##### The disposition enforces the challenged boundary without rewriting membership law

**Holding:** The statutory invalidation applies to the challenged unrelated employer-group additions; the other membership commands and permissions in §1759 remain intact. Affirmance supplies no independent enlargement of the injunction reported below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subscription to at least one share and payment of the initial installment remain required. An entrance fee is required only if the directors require it. Joint survivorship shares with a member’s designated person do not confer voting, borrowing or officeholding rights unless that person is within the membership field and is a qualified member.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both judgments invalidating the challenged unrelated employer-group additions are affirmed. No broader injunction, removal of members, liquidation, transition date or monetary award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron requires enforcement of the unambiguous statutory boundary before agency deference.
- MCI applies the prohibition on eliminating an enacted requirement through interpretation. NationsBank’s lawful-classification holding remains intact within statutory boundaries.

For this case’s other questions, see Federal Courts (These banks may seek review of the membership expansion).

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

### AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board; Nos. 97-826, 97-829, 97-830, 97-831, 97-1075, 97-1087, 97-1099 and 97-1141; including California respondents in the AT&T, MCI and FCC petitions. — 1999-01-25

#### Holdings

##### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's insertion of the local-competition provisions into the Communications Act brings those provisions within its implementing grant. Section 251(d)(1) directs implementation; §252(c) requires state commissions to apply §251 and the Commission's rules. National methodology and state determination of particular rates can coexist. Section 152(b) does not remove the new federal duties from the regulatory authority the amendments create. Louisiana Public Service Commission prevents a general grant from regulating matters Congress left wholly intrastate; it does not disable implementation of newly enacted federal obligations. MCI requires an actual delegation and preservation of operative limits, not a rule that every delegation permits only minor action. The contrary reading gives proper attention to the state role but treats methodology and particular determinations as mutually exclusive when the statute does not. The holding decides authority, not the substantive lawfulness of TELRIC, a confiscatory rate or every instruction the Commission might issue.

##### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the general implementation rule does not adjudicate every asserted federal instruction. Section 251(f)'s exemption predicates remain for the actual assigned determinations; §§251(d)(3) and 252 retain their preservation and approval limits. MCI requires those specific statutory conditions to retain their force even when a general delegation exists. The lower court therefore must examine any unresolved objection to the rural burden-of-proof rule or preexisting-agreement requirements on its own substance. Rejection of exclusive state regulatory authority supplies no blanket validation of either rule and no permission for state commissions to disregard otherwise lawful federal requirements. The remedial disposition removes the categorical jurisdictional ground and returns the residual questions without selecting a new proof burden or deciding a particular agreement.

##### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that operational-support systems, operator and directory services, and vertical-switching functions are not excluded merely because they are not detachable physical components or can also be described as services. The statutory definition itself supplies the answer. The physical-component-only construction would omit its express functional language. But definition and compulsory access are separate inquiries: the Commission must still apply the proprietary-necessity and general-impairment criteria to identify required elements. No general requirement to unbundle every technically available service follows.

##### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding and operative rule:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the invalidity holding that the rule must make element-specific judgments giving necessity and impairment independent constraining force, and joins vacatur and agency remand; he does not supply a separate join in the more specific outside-alternatives requirement. Souter dissents from invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that any increase in cost or decline in quality cannot by itself establish the required impairment while external alternatives are categorically ignored. MCI preserves operative statutory predicates before agency deference. The agency's competitive-entry expertise remains substantial, but it cannot make every purchasing disadvantage a sufficient statutory obstacle. The proprietary standard of significant impairment or thwarting is not invalid simply because it is less than absolute indispensability; its defect includes the cramped alternative comparison limited to the incumbent's nonproprietary elements. The Court does not require access only to an irreplaceable natural monopoly, invent a numerical threshold or choose a replacement list. On remand the agency may explain real entry impairment, actual availability, costs, quality, service requirements and workable selection criteria under the enacted standard. The strongest contrary argument invokes the competitive-entry purpose and danger that theoretical alternatives will require disabling duplication. The Court accepts that real commercial availability matters; that is why an actual inquiry is required rather than either automatic access or theoretical replication.

##### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combining elements into a service. The separate resale route in §251(c)(4) does not insert a minimum-property requirement into the unbundling route. Combining or buying elements entails obligations and commercial consequences distinct from buying a finished retail service for resale; the existence of both routes does not make one redundant. This holding promises no entrant the incumbent's whole network regardless of necessity and impairment and decides no assumed difference in particular rates.

##### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3)'s direction to make elements available in a manner permitting the requester to combine them does not require the incumbent first to dismantle a configuration already joined. The Commission reasonably implements nondiscriminatory access by preserving that configuration subject to the requester's option to separate. The contrary reading confuses separately available elements with mandatory physical separation. This holding restores paragraph (b), not the distinct affirmative new-combination duties in paragraphs (c)–(f), and does not require an unestablished configuration to be built. Those duties receive their own disposition. Particular requests remain subject to the Act and valid technical and operational requirements.

##### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to any interconnection, service or network element supports selection of an individual arrangement, not a requirement to accept the entire agreement. The entrant may not take an isolated favorable price while discarding its relevant obligations. The incumbent may establish that provision to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and the reasonable availability period protects against indefinite use of stale arrangements. The challengers argue that selective access undermines negotiated tradeoffs; the agency reached a contrary practical judgment with nondiscrimination and these safeguards in view. That disagreement does not establish that Congress commanded whole-agreement adoption or that the agency's incentive assessment is irrational. No unrestricted cherry-picking right results.

##### VIII. Dialing-parity authority and numbering-cost ripeness remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. The separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge remains unripe because its asserted disadvantage depends on particular cost-recovery rate decisions not before the Court.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor agrees on the narrower §251(b)(3)/§251(d)(1) implementation ground stated in her separate opinion. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. All eight Associates join Part VIII's separate numbering-cost ripeness holding; Stone-Zsela agrees only in that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that federal implementation of §251(b)(3)'s substantive parity duty is authorized. The general implementing grant supports that conclusion, and the narrower substantive-duty ground also supports the parity disposition without resolving the separate state-pricing dispute. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain operative. The separate numbering issue concerns who will bear costs under future particular rate decisions. Reversal of the authority ground neither resolves those rates nor creates an accrued numbering injury. The California judgment therefore receives distinct dispositions rather than complete reversal by inference from the general-authority holding. No particular carrier's liability or cost share is fixed.

##### IX. Technical feasibility cannot replace the required access criteria

**Holding and operative rule:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. The separately sustained definition of technical feasibility and other unaffected portions of the rule remain outside that invalidation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that technical feasibility may inform element selection: §251(d)(2)'s minimum criteria do not prohibit consideration of additional relevant matters. For elements beyond Rule 51.319's list, once technical feasibility is found, Rule 51.317 permits the state commission to decline unbundling only if either (1) the element is proprietary or would reveal proprietary information, and the requester could offer the same proposed service through other nonproprietary unbundled elements within the incumbent's network; or (2) denial would neither reduce service quality nor increase its financial or administrative cost compared with providing that service through other unbundled elements in that network. Those restrictions reproduce the limiting-standard defect in the required-element list. They do not allow realistic outside alternatives to perform their proper role. The result rests on this independent substantive flaw, not on the mistaken premise that feasibility can never matter. A corrected-delegation remand cannot preserve the existing defective criteria without agency reconsideration. The Court does not forbid every lawful presumption or invalidate all of Rule 51.317.

##### X. Superior-quality duties return for review under the correct framework

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c) is vacated and remanded for reconsideration of the actual conditions and agency justification under the correct implementing authority. The Court does not decide their ultimate validity or order unconditional reinstatement.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part X's corrected-standard remand and its confined review directions. Thomas and Breyer do so under the Court's governing authority framework while retaining their dissent from Part I. Stone-Zsela agrees in the remand without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(2)'s requirement of at-least-equal quality establishes a floor; the absence of an express superior-quality command does not alone show that an implementing rule can never require more. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict objections nevertheless remain for review. Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns requested, technically feasible interconnection superior to that supplied to the incumbent, affiliates or other interconnection recipients. Rule 51.311(c) separately concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, placing technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission on the incumbent. Lesser quality at the requesting carrier's sole request remains permitted. On remand the actual improvement, statutory relationship and agency explanation must be examined. Compensation alone supplies no missing authority, and nondiscrimination alone does not command every upgrade. The lower court's allowance for modifications necessary to accommodate lawful access is preserved; neither an unconditional superior-facilities right nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

##### XI. Affirmative new-combination duties require separate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f) is vacated and remanded for review under the proper implementing-power standard. Section 251(c)(3)'s direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requester; the particular affirmative duties and burdens remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part XI is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer's agreement is a remand under the governing authority framework, not a Part I join. Seven Justices control this remand; Stone-Zsela agrees only in its disposition, and Thomas dissents on his independent task-allocation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower construction treated enabling access as an exclusive assignment of all combining labor without adequately addressing the actual delegation, network control, conditions and agency explanation. That differs from preserving existing combinations, which the Court separately upholds. Subsection (c) addresses requested configurations the incumbent does not ordinarily make, only when technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) addresses combination with the requester's own elements in a technically feasible manner. The other-carrier condition expressly belongs to (c), not automatically to (d). Subsections (e) and (f) place the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission on the incumbent. MCI preserves specific statutory allocations, but the contested sentence does not alone establish the exclusive task allocation the lower court found. The remand neither approves every proof burden nor compels a particular configuration. Voluntary combination and necessary accommodation remain distinct from a mandated new combination.

##### XII. The general section 208 announcement is not ripe for review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definite agency announcement of a possible enforcement forum is not ripe merely because the position is complete and the dispute predominantly legal. Because this announcement directs no present change in primary conduct, determines no carrier-specific liability and permits meaningful review of a concrete application, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice, without deciding enforcement authority.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Scalia's Part XII, supplying a controlling majority. Stone-Zsela agrees only in vacatur of the premature enforcement-merits adjudication and dismissal without prejudice as unripe; he adds no enforcement or ripeness ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that finality, standing, fitness and hardship are distinct; it decides ripeness, not independent standing or final-order questions. Ohio Forestry and Toilet Goods distinguish settled policy from presently consequential application. Paragraphs 126–128 definitively assert complaint authority even where a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplate enforcing agreement terms; a later expedited-procedure rulemaking does not make that assertion tentative. Yet the announcement changes no substantive carrier duty, identifies no operative agreement conflict, revises no state determination and orders no act or liability. A concrete complaint would distinguish statutory enforcement, agreement enforcement and a carrier proceeding that effectively revisits state action.

Footnote 236 says the Commission might decline financial penalties in some state-authorized circumstances; it establishes neither immunity nor a required penalty. The statement that judicially decided issues are unlikely to be revisited likewise creates no absolute preclusion rule. The absence of an absolute safe harbor is not itself withdrawal of an established protection or present liability. Bennett's operative legal-protection change and Franklin's completed determination are therefore different.

Section 208(a) supplies complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer and investigation under its conditions; §208(b)(3) makes an order concluding a covered investigation appealable under §402(a). Concrete application permits the authority objection in its actual setting. The prospect of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about forum allocation do not alone establish present primary-conduct hardship. Lee preserves review of presently burdensome civil duties, and Pic-A-State concerns an existing business actually constrained by a law; neither condition is established by this instrument. NAACP and Paradise require examination of the actual claim, not an automatic further inquiry into hypothetical applications. The Court creates no universal exhaustion rule, promises no future filing's standing or timeliness, and does not defer every challenge until penalties are collected. A presently binding or burdensome statement remains a different case. The Court decides neither carrier statutory-violation authority, agreement enforcement, review of state determinations under §252(e)(6), nor state-failure authority under §252(e)(5); no carrier complaint, agreement, state order or penalty is adjudicated.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T* is applied to identify actual delegation and preserve enacted limiting conditions; it is distinguished from a categorical ban on consequential implementing rules. Its tariff-filing holding is not changed.
- *Louisiana Public Service Commission v. FCC* retains its protection of matters Congress left wholly intrastate; the Court distinguishes the newly enacted federal local-competition obligations from those matters.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC* supplies review within actual delegation and statutory limits, not authority to remove necessity or impairment from the selection inquiry.
- *Allentown Mack Sales & Service v. NLRB* remains authority for administering the actual announced lawful standard; no judicial substitution of an economic rule is authorized.
- *Ohio Forestry Association v. Sierra Club* and *Toilet Goods Association v. Gardner* are applied to the difference between settled policy and presently consequential application, and the fitness/hardship inquiry.
- *Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner* retains its fitness-and-hardship framework; a presently burdensome rule remains distinct from this forum announcement.
- *Lee v. Harcleroad* is distinguished because present civil duties can create hardship without criminal penalties; the announcement here itself imposes no such duty.
- *Pic-A-State PA, Inc. v. Reno* is distinguished because its claimant's existing business was actually stopped by the challenged law and real enforcement risk.
- *Franklin v. Massachusetts* and *Bennett v. Spear* are distinguished as completed determinations carrying concrete legal consequences; finality alone supplies no section 208 ripeness holding.
- *NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones* and *Wisconsin Right to Life v. Paradise* require attention to the actual jurisdictional claim and remedy; neither commands another factual inquiry after this instrument's threshold status is ascertainable.

### National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior / Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior; Nos. 97-1184 and 97-1243. — 1999-03-03

#### Holdings

##### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Operative rule.** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Authority.** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct six-Justice majority for this holding and the holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court must identify the authority Congress granted before deferring to an agency. *MCI* preserves statutory boundaries; *NationsBank* permits reasoned administrative choice where the enacted scheme leaves genuine discretion. Here the definition of a collective bargaining agreement is not expressly limited to the principal term contract. An agreement reached during its term can also fit that definition. Conversely, §7114's direction to negotiate toward an agreement and its reasonable-times and frequency requirements do not unmistakably prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty.

Section 7106 principally allocates authority by subject. Its management-right exceptions do not establish a prohibition on every other midterm proposal. The agency-election condition in §7106(b)(1) is not an election condition in (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105 assigns the Authority responsibility for administering bargaining obligations within those limits. The efficiency instruction in §7101 and the conditional impasse powers in §7119 inform that administration; they do not prove perpetual negotiations or inevitable imposition of this clause. A prediction of disruption cannot replace the statutory inquiry, just as the desirability of responding to new workplace needs cannot establish an unlimited duty.

The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. The Authority must explain the lawful regime; the Court does not select a universal duty or universal exemption, create a new factor checklist, or authorize reopening matters an agreement actually settles. The Court does not determine the treatment of a hypothetical future proposal under the Authority's covered-matter or waiver rules.

##### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Operative rule.** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Controlling explanation.** The Authority did give an independent reason: contractual proposals can be negotiable even when their substance is not a statutory entitlement. It also distinguished contract coverage from a categorical denial of midterm bargaining. Those are actual reasons, not an appellate lawyer's invention. But the current order continued to treat the clause as restating a statutory right, and its alternative explanation chiefly answered the circuit's contrary precedent. It did not fully exercise the distinct choice over the relationship among a general duty, an advance clause, contractual repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion was removed.

*NationsBank* requires reasoned exercise of genuine discretion, not an assumption that legal necessity supplies the agency's choice. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* separates administrative implementing power from the validity of particular duties and permits return under the correct legal allocation. *Boston & Maine* requires a discernible agency ground; that requirement is satisfied here, but it does not make every stated alternative sufficient after a governing premise changes. *Good Samaritan* permits considered interpretive change; the Court does not remand merely because the Authority changed its view. This is legal-framework reconsideration, not an evidentiary remand or punishment for inconsistency.

The Authority may adopt the same negotiability result if it explains a permissible construction. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C), excluding matters specifically provided for by federal statute, and 7117(a), preserving federal-law consistency, remain distinct limitations; neither is satisfied merely by statutory silence. The Court does not impose a clause, require agreement or concession, or adjudicate a Panel award. Agreed waiver, a duty to negotiate a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain separate questions; an employer's power to impose a comprehensive waiver is not presumed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Chevron* remains the framework for determining statutory meaning and the scope of permissible agency construction; no automatic deference follows from the agency label.
- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co.* supplies the requirement that operative statutory limits be identified and enforced before administrative discretion is recognized; its tariff holding creates no general rule restricting agencies to minor choices.
- *NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.* supplies the distinction between genuine statutory discretion and the agency's reasoned exercise of it. Establishing authority alone does not validate the existing order.
- *ICC v. Boston & Maine Corp.* supplies the discernible-agency-ground requirement. The Authority's contractual explanation is acknowledged rather than replaced with counsel's reasoning.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* preserves permissible considered changes of interpretation. Change alone does not disqualify the Authority's construction or compel this remand.
- *AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board* supplies the distinction between implementation authority, particular statutory restrictions and rules requiring agency reconsideration under the correct allocation. Its telecommunications rules are not transferred to federal labor relations.
- *Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala* preserves statutory construction as distinct from additional regulatory deference; an administrative label cannot settle statutory power.
- *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.* permits consideration of a fairly included legal argument within a presented claim. The general-duty question here is expressly presented and supplies an actual agency premise; no new question or relief is added.

#### Law After Decision

Federal labor law now rejects a judicial categorical prohibition on all union-initiated midterm bargaining and all advance contractual clauses requiring it. The Authority retains responsibility for a reasoned choice within Congress's actual limits. The Court establishes neither a universal duty to entertain every midterm proposal nor final mandatory negotiability of this particular clause.

The distinction between statutory entitlement and contractual negotiability remains legally meaningful, but the existing Survey order is returned for reconsideration rather than ordered enforced. Management rights, statutory consistency, lawful waiver and the agreement's actual coverage retain their independent force. The Authority's descriptions of express coverage, subjects inseparably bound up with covered subjects and matters fully explored during term negotiations are not findings about an unidentified proposal. No rule about compulsory negotiation or imposition of a comprehensive waiver clause is settled.

**O'Connor, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The agreement-directed duty in §7114, the separately structured continuing obligations and §7101's efficiency instruction together supply a statutory boundary.
