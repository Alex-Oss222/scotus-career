#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress need not prove a violation in every jurisdiction, make formal findings, use a prescribed quantity or form of evidence, require individualized proof for every prohibited act, or enact geographic limits or sunsets. Such design features may inform substantive fit but are not universal conditions. Independent Fifteenth Amendment power is not further resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.

#### Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1462.

**Decided:** 1998-01-21.

##### Article IV does not permit this inadequately justified categorical exclusion

**Holding:** When a State taxes another State's citizens on income earned within its borders, a discriminatory deduction rule must rest on a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship, and the discrimination must bear a close relationship to that reason. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical exclusion of qualifying alimony from deductions against New York-source income fails that inquiry on the source, personal-expense, initial-computation and family-income justifications offered here; genuine source allocation remains permissible, and identical resident and nonresident tax codes are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The complete formula matters: alimony reduces hypothetical resident liability and the income denominator but is denied in the New York-source numerator. Neither personal-expense labeling, the narrower nonresident tax base, a mismatched family-income justification nor another State’s tax choices cures the discrimination.

##### Relief follows the established tax-remedy predicates

**Holding:** The unconstitutional treatment of the challenged assessment requires reversal and remand for lawful state allocation, severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When the State compelled payment before meaningful review, it must provide adequate backward-looking correction; independently, a clearly held-out postpayment remedy cannot be withdrawn after payment, including voluntary payment, subject to ordinary proof and lawful previously announced conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same seven Justices join Thomas's remedial holding in full.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual payment, preservation, amount and lawful announced procedural conditions remain to be established. State allocation and severability choices remain, including lawful refunds, permissible nondiscriminatory retrospective equalization or a lawful combination when correction is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No Equal Protection or Commerce Clause claim, federal alimony-qualification dispute, hypothetical recipient-based alternative or duty to apportion every personal expense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 7–2, for lawful allocation and constitutionally adequate relief, without a fixed refund, interest award, exclusive replacement formula or reopening of unrelated final tax years.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Toomer is applied for substantial equality, a substantial independent reason and the close relationship required between that reason and the discriminatory treatment.
- Shaffer retains nondiscriminatory source taxation and justified corresponding deduction limits; it supplies no categorical exception for every deduction labeled personal.
- Travis is applied to distinguish legitimate source limits from inadequately justified unequal personal allowances. Its rejection of presumed offsets remains operative.
- Austin is applied to reject a cure dependent on another State's tax choices; permissible cooperative arrangements remain available.
- Goodwin's reported home-State property and medical-expense setting is distinguished; that different deduction setting does not resolve this alimony provision.
- Friedsam's earlier statutory construction was superseded by § 631(b)(6); it is not revived as the constitutional rule or imposed as the exclusive replacement formula.
- Fulton and Oregon Waste supply analogical support concerning actual tax operation and unsupported offset explanations. Their Commerce Clause tests and Fulton's limited treatment of Darnell remain confined to their fields.
- McKesson, Harper, Fulton and Reich are applied to preserve conditional backward-looking correction, lawful state remedial choices and the independent protection of a clearly offered postpayment remedy.

#### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1039.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class

**Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Its established class-specific disability lacks a rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests, without reliance on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, elimination of generally applicable protection, or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The authoritative state construction covers legislative, executive, and judicial channels of the State, agencies, schools, and local government, including public and private settings; it is not merely a quota prohibition or local preemption leaving the legislature free to act. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract, and public-service protections remain. It creates no suspect or quasi-suspect tier, general duty to enact nondiscrimination laws, nationwide code, quota entitlement, universal access to every lawmaking level, or presumption invalidating every group-affecting repeal, referendum, or constitutional policy reservation. Particular religious, intimate-association, and expressive defenses retain their own predicates; no municipal exemption is approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Beach* preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable generalizations together with the *Moreno/Cleburne* limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. *Heller*'s controlling proof-burden decision requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage; its separate family-participation holding preserves relevant generalizations and rejects invalidity based on narrower alternatives alone. *Hurley* protects concrete private expression without making ordinary service categorically expressive association; *Swanner* and *American Life League* retain their particular religious-liberty inquiries. *Bowers* remains effective on its specified-conduct question but does not justify this broader disability. *Adarand* adds no orientation tier, and *Klinger*'s treatment-specific comparison does not import *Armstrong*'s charging-discovery threshold into an express classification.

##### Resolution of the preserved alternative and complete injunctive relief

**Holding:** The fully presented rational-basis alternative permits affirmance without adoption of the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The defect permeates Amendment 2's inseparable common operation, warranting the complete existing injunction without a new merits remand or federal rewriting of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** State severability law treats orientation, conduct, practices, and relationships as descriptions of the same class; deletion of a description leaves the same disability. No independently lawful remainder is established. Failure under strict scrutiny is not itself rational-basis failure, and no unconditional State concession supplies the result. The ability to seek another constitutional amendment does not cure the unequal barrier to ordinary protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 7–2; the permanent injunction remains effective in full. No enactment, damages, factual inquiry, severance proceeding, or new hearing is ordered. Unappealed suspect-status treatment is not reopened, and independently preserved First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunter* and *Seattle* retain their racial political-restructuring settings without a new fundamental-right extension. *Valtierra*, *Gordon*, and *Crawford* prevent treating every referendum, neutral voting rule, or policy revision as exclusion. *Dalton* requires relief matched to the violation and preservation of independently lawful operation; no general facial formula, *Miller* abortion method, or *44 Liquormart* empirical burden replaces this analysis.

#### Nordlinger v. Hahn, 505 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1912.
**Decided:** June 18, 1992.

For the acquisition-value and reassessment-exception equal-protection holdings in *Nordlinger v. Hahn*, June 18, 1992, No. 90-1912, see Property and Economic Rights — Property-tax classifications.

##### Whether Nordlinger may obtain heightened review by asserting absent interstate travelers' rights

**Holding:** A taxpayer who alleges no interference with her own interstate movement may not obtain heightened review by asserting absent travelers’ rights when she shows neither a qualifying relationship nor an obstacle to their own litigation. Nordlinger therefore cannot use those unasserted injuries to challenge this scheme; the merits of a proper traveler’s claim remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Part II-A, joined by White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with seven of nine Justices. Stone-Zsela states the same refusal separately; Stevens does not reach the issue.

**Material application:** Nordlinger already lived in Los Angeles before purchasing her home, alleges no personal impediment to interstate movement and identifies neither a qualifying relationship nor a reason an affected traveler could not sue. Her own financial injury permits her own equal-protection challenge, not assertion of every theory available to another owner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A properly situated traveler's claim remains open; neither Zobel nor Memorial Hospital is adjudicated on behalf of absent claimants.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The absent-traveler claims are not entertained. Her own equal-protection challenge receives ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 — applied to preserve the ordinary limit on asserting others’ rights.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 — applied to distinguish personal injury from authority to assert absent persons’ rights.
- Caplin & Drysdale, Chartered v. United States, 491 U.S. 617 — applied; no qualifying relationship or obstacle is established.
- Zobel v. Williams, 457 U.S. 55 — its travel-related protection is not adjudicated on this absent-party claim.
- Memorial Hospital v. Maricopa County, 415 U.S. 250 — its travel-related protection is not adjudicated on this absent-party claim.

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable.

**Authority by component:**
- Enforcement boundary and method: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** City of Boerne, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independent Fifteenth Amendment power and other sources of congressional authority retain their own law. The rule creates no general hearing requirement or evidentiary checklist.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Boerne v. Flores (1997-06-25) — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right.

### Article IV limits on discriminatory nonresident income-tax deductions

**Governing question:** Differential deductions when a State taxes other States’ citizens on local income.

**Current rule:** A discriminatory deduction must rest on a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship and bear a close relationship to that reason. Examine the particular deduction and complete tax calculation rather than apply ordinary rationality alone or require mechanically identical codes. A narrower nonresident tax base and a deduction’s personal character do not automatically justify categorical denial. Genuine source allocation remains permissible, but another State’s credit, deduction or fiscal choice cannot cure established discrimination.

**Authority by component:**

- Article IV substantial reason, close relationship and full-scheme inquiry: Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal, 1998-01-21; Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general apportionment rule for every personal expense, strict numerical equality or Commerce Clause compensatory-tax test is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Backward-looking relief for unconstitutional taxes supplies the separate payment- and procedure-dependent remedial conditions.

**Holdings navigation:** Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal — Discriminatory nonresident income taxation.

### Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati, Inc. v. City of Cincinnati — 1998-12-03

Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. They would affirm the merits judgment, dissolution of the injunction and dependent fee vacatur. They accept that Article XII reaches ordinary nondiscrimination protection and that municipalities remain subject to equal protection. In their view, regulatory restraint, limited resources, private association and the electorate's authority to make a local policy durable rationally support the measure despite imperfect coverage. Higher-level and generally applicable remedies remain available, and a local body's control over its own delegated policies differs from Colorado's restriction of state and local channels.

The dissent maintains its disagreement with Romer's application of ordinary rationality and proposes the municipal distinction as an additional limit. It does not claim Romer's majority adopted that reasoning or that every affected protection is a quota. O'Connor's Coalition writing supplies support for withdrawal of optional policies, while Hurley preserves actual expression; neither is treated as making every orientation protection unlawful. The dissent rejects a new racial political-process extension and finds no duty to adopt or preserve these municipal protections. Its fee position follows its merits conclusion only and supplies no separate bar to a fee award resting on an independently established surviving basis.

### Central State University v. American Association of University Professors, Central State University Chapter; No. 98-1071. — 1999-03-22

#### Holdings

##### Faculty workload administration supplies a rational basis for this distinction

**Holding and operative rule.** Ohio may exclude public-university faculty workload standards from collective bargaining to promote classroom teaching without proving that bargaining caused each earlier reduction in teaching. An ordinary occupational classification must rationally serve a legitimate purpose; a challenger cannot establish irrationality merely by identifying a lack of empirical proof or a different possible means.

**Authority.** The eight Justices joining the per curiam adopt this rule, its application and the stated limits in full.

**Controlling explanation.** Beach supplies ordinary rational-basis review without an empirical-certainty requirement. Nordlinger permits reasonable generalization, while Heller's distinct holdings require a rational connection to the actual disadvantage and preserve legislative choice among rational arrangements. Faculty classroom workloads are the subject Ohio seeks to administer. Removing those workload standards from bargaining can enable universities to increase or standardize classroom teaching, whether or not bargaining caused every past decline. The fact that other public employees retain bargaining rights does not defeat that subject-specific connection. Equality Foundation requires examination of the law's actual reach; it does not equate this defined management measure with a sweeping exclusion from ordinary civic protection. The presented record supplies no forbidden-purpose or independently protected-right ground that defeats the rational connection. The Court decides neither independent Ohio constitutional limits nor other bargaining and association rights.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Beach is applied: ordinary economic and occupational classifications do not require empirical proof of the legislature's proposed causal account.
- Nordlinger is applied: reasonable generalization may sustain a classification despite imperfect fit.
- Heller's separate holdings are preserved: review examines the actual disadvantage without converting legislative choice into a necessity or empirical-certainty test.
- Equality Foundation is distinguished: the workload exclusion has a defined management relationship and does not enact the sweeping civic disability addressed there.

#### Law After Decision

Existing rational-basis doctrine governs this faculty-workload classification and sustains it against the presented federal challenge. No new scrutiny tier, general immunity for anti-union action or rule overriding independent state protections is adopted.

### Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson — 1998-11-10

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, agrees that voluntary-action permission does not establish blanket Title VII preemption, but would affirm vacatur of the preliminary injunction. The dissent treats Proposition 209 as a statewide substantive restriction on officials' classifications, leaving ordinary antidiscrimination protection available. Crawford preserves the State's choice to withdraw optional policies; lawful remedies under Hopwood and Engineering Contractors are not compulsory policies. Hunter concerned unequal access to ordinary racial protection, and Seattle concerned specially displaced desegregation authority. In the dissent's view, those decisions do not entrench every optional preference-making power at a subordinate governmental level. The reciprocal sex provision likewise does not establish the asserted facial denial of equal protection. This position preserves scrutiny of actual unequal administration, binding federal obligations and properly presented later applications, and does not declare every affirmative measure unconstitutional.
