#### Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1502.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the separate standing and constitutional-counting holdings in Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992, No. 91-1502, see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury and Election Law — Interstate congressional apportionment.

##### The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable

**Holding:** The Secretary's completed census determination supplies final agency action under APA §704 even though the President later transmits the resulting apportionment statement. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit the counting decision entirely to unreviewable discretion: the duty to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice to the Secretary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule while agreeing with reversal on their own reviewability ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presidential transmission under §2a does not confer an independent presidential choice of the population counted under §141. The President's own APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief remain undecided; Waterman's independent foreign-affairs decision structure is different.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): applied for reviewability and practical completion of an agency determination with concrete consequences; the statutory transmittal here does not postpone finality.
- Chicago & Southern Air Lines, Inc. v. Waterman Steamship Corp., 333 U.S. 103 (1948): distinguished because Congress there required an independent presidential foreign-affairs judgment; its rule for that decision structure is preserved.
- Japan Whaling Assn. v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): applied for review against statutory limits despite broad administrative judgment; methodological latitude is not absence of law to apply.

##### The statutory and administrative objections fail on this record

**Holding:** Section 141 permits the supported inclusion of temporarily overseas personnel with enduring state ties, and the Secretary's comparative choice of supplemented home-of-record information is not arbitrary or capricious on this record. The agency must supply a contemporaneous, reasonably discernible explanation connecting its available information and residence objective to its choice; imperfection alone does not require rejection of every available dataset.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. The other four Justices do not join the statutory or APA merits determination.

**Material application:** The contemporaneous record supplies supplementary personnel information and continuing residence reasons despite cancellation of the proposed survey. Home of record may identify enduring ties; tax residence and last duty station have different defects. The holding relies on actual agency reasons, not stricken later declarations or counsel's substitute explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No replacement allocation, judicially selected dataset, injunction against the President, or general APA status for the President is decided. No claim that another residence method necessarily restores a seat is found proved. The statutory and administrative holdings are record-specific applications; no earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): applied for consideration of relevant factors, reasoned explanation and review on the agency's actual rationale; no exemption from review accompanies census discretion.
- Bowman Transportation, Inc. v. Arkansas-Best Freight System, Inc., 419 U.S. 281 (1974): applied for a reasonably discernible decisional path despite imperfect exposition; a court may not substitute a new rationale.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether APA review is available for the §1536 claims and these interests

**Holding:** The consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels supply no other adequate court remedy for the administrative claims. Alleged water restrictions unsupported by the best available scientific and commercial data implicate interests at least arguably protected by the particular §1536(a)(2) data requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous; both APA holdings have nine direct joins.

**Material application:** The interest inquiry concerns the provision sued upon; sound scientific decisionmaking also protects affected persons against unsupported restrictions. The §1540(g)(5) savings clause preserves available rights without itself satisfying every APA condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 702 concerns relief other than money damages and retains other legal or equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. A mandatory or injunctive decree must identify responsible officers and successors by name or title. Section 704 retains final-action and no-other-adequate-remedy conditions; preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal postpones finality only when an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. No decree or unproved exhaustion prerequisite is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dalton’s integrated base-closure scheme supported its confined preclusion holding; no comparable scheme is shown here. Its four-Justice recommendation-finality rationale creates no general bar.

##### Whether the Biological Opinion completes agency action with legal consequences

**Holding:** This Biological Opinion is final because it completes the Service’s consultation decision and its incidental-take statement changes legal exposure under §1536(o)(2) when the statement’s conditions are met. The Bureau’s retained operating discretion does not reduce that completed, legally consequential action to tentative advice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** Practical influence and the Bureau’s announced compliance support causation; the conditional change in taking liability separately establishes legal consequences. Under §1536(a)(2), agencies must use the best available scientific and commercial data to avoid likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat unless the Committee grants an (h) exemption; none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must find no (a)(2) violation or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed to avoid it, and conclude incidental taking will not violate that subsection. Marine-mammal taking additionally requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The statement specifies taking impact, reasonable and prudent minimization, necessary marine-mammal measures and implementing conditions including reporting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section (o)(2) protects only taking compliant with those conditions, not every ESA violation; the separate (o)(1) exemption is not the source of this consequence. No marine-mammal finding is required for these fish. Not every report or prediction is final, and the biological merits, triggered duties, merits application of review standards and final relief remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed dismissal is reversed and remanded, leaving the separate NEPA dismissal undisturbed and ordering no substantive operational relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin permits review of a completed subordinate-agency determination despite a later governmental step; its reserved presidential-status question is not decided.

For Bennett v. Spear, No. 95-813, decided March 19, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Standing to challenge a biological consultation constraint,” and Environmental Law, “Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels,” for the separate constitutional and citizen-suit holdings.

#### Ohio Forestry Association, Inc. v. Sierra Club

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-16.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### The present substantive planning challenge is premature

**Holding:** A substantive challenge to a programmatic forest plan is unripe when the plan authorizes no immediate logging, establishes no sufficient present legal hardship, and leaves further project decisions and meaningful review before cutting. On this record that rule requires dismissal without prejudice of the plan/clearcutting, public-trust sales and timber-suitability-regulation claims, without deciding their substantive validity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices, supplies direct controlling authority for this rule and the limits below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither standing, APA finality, statutory or public-trust merits, nor an independent fee entitlement. It does not adopt the lower court's exceptional-circumstances formulation for even-aged management or equate an optimum-method inquiry with a resource-protection condition. A site-specific authorization, an immediately binding provision or regulation, or a properly presented completed procedural violation may receive review when its independent requirements are satisfied. Lujan's protection of a procedural right tied to a threatened concrete interest remains intact. No optional-appeal exhaustion requirement or future limitations ruling is adopted.

Assertions in later briefing concerning motorcycles, heavy machinery, road closures and hiking trails supply no established basis for resolving the reviewed substantive dispute differently. The Court does not find those activities or injuries proved, find intentional waiver, announce a general forfeiture rule, or adjudicate a distinct present-recreation claim on either ripeness or merits.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both lower merits adjudications are displaced, and all three substantive theories are dismissed without prejudice as unripe. No plan approval or invalidation, logging injunction, revision or fee award follows. Later review must satisfy its own requirements and need not await physical injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner and Texas v. United States: applied to fitness and present hardship; neither requires an advisory answer about an insufficiently concrete implementation.
- Toilet Goods Association v. Gardner: applied to distinguish completed agency action from ripeness for judicial review.
- Bennett v. Spear: distinguished at its supported present-consequences premise; later implementation discretion alone does not defeat review.
- New York v. United States and Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno: distinguished because their challenged rules imposed established present burdens; pre-enforcement review remains available on that basis.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: its concrete-interest procedural protection is preserved; this decision supplies no separate standing determination.

#### Lee v. Harcleroad

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-35804, 95-35805, 95-35854, 95-35948 and 95-35949; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-01-20.

##### Patient exposure and the distinct reduction-of-care theory

**Holding:** At summary judgment, a plaintiff seeking prospective relief must support a concrete, actual or imminent personal injury with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, allowing favorable reasonable inferences. Elsner's progressive illness and prior depression, even with statutory eligibility and the general medical assertions assumed, do not establish sufficiently impending exposure to an unwanted assisted death; likewise, an assumed reduction of medical-care protection does not establish injury without supported personal exposure to changed care or threatened harm from that reduction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B, addressing the separate care-protection theory, has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in that disposition only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The summary-judgment rule requires neither certainty nor a numerical probability, particular document or proof that safeguards never fail. The construction of §4.01(1), retained negligence and misconduct liability under §4.02, substantive safeguards, patient-claim traceability and redressability remain undecided.

##### Continuing controversy, representation and the proposed class

**Holding:** Dutson's personal prospective controversy does not continue after his death on this record, which establishes no surviving personal claim, substitute or certified class. A provider's relationship to patients and a proposed class never certified cannot supply the represented patients' missing injury or an independently continuing controversy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, controls with eight votes; Stone-Zsela agrees in these dispositions only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical ban on provider representation, universal class relation-back rule or damages-pleading condition is adopted.

##### Compelled participation: injury and ripeness

**Holding:** Absence of an express criminal or statutory penalty does not by itself defeat Article III injury: an actually applicable legal obligation or supported threatened civil enforcement may burden present conduct. Fitness and hardship must both be examined; predominantly legal questions do not alone establish hardship, but the possibility of a later civil-enforcement suit does not categorically require postponing every challenge to a presently burdensome legal duty. The participation claims must be reconsidered under these principles without a present finding of standing or ripeness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-A states the legal boundary with all eight Associate Justices joining. Part III-B's application and remand command six direct votes: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas oppose the remand; no combination of separate rationales is needed for controlling authority.

**Material application:** Further analysis must distinguish the express freedom to refuse participation under §4.01(4) from its transfer-on-request duty upon care transfer, the attending-physician setting in §3.01(2), and the long-term-care witness rule in §2.02(4). Actual duty, present burden, proper defendant, traceability, redressability, fitness and hardship must be established; no duty to prescribe or recommend assistance is found.

##### Facility association

**Holding:** The facilities' challenge to §4.01(2)'s alleged restriction on excluding participating physicians lacks actual or imminent injury where no intending participating physician at the facilities or present operational restriction is supported. That separate dismissal remains operative despite remand of the compelled-participation claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV has all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An actual physician-participation conflict or supported present burden would present a different case. Association and religious-freedom merits and conclusive statutory construction remain open.

##### The District Court's declaration and injunction remain vacated

**Holding:** Limited corrective jurisdiction permits clearing a constitutional merits judgment unsupported by a live injured claimant, and the equities here require leaving vacated the District Court's patient-based equal-protection declaration and permanent injunction. Reconsideration of a distinct, previously undecided provider claim does not restore that judgment or establish a new entitlement to relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part V has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in leaving the judgment and injunction vacated only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Vacatur is equitable, not automatic whenever jurisdiction is disputed; §2106 supplies corrective authority, not merits jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm patient, reduction-of-care, representation/class and facility-association dismissals without prejudice; preserve vacatur of the patient-based declaration and injunction. Vacate the participation dismissal for claim-specific injury and ripeness analysis, 6–3. No assisted-death merits, restored injunction or automatic hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lujan is applied for specific summary-judgment support, reasonable favorable inferences and the concrete-interest requirement. Its ordinary injury, traceability and redressability elements remain intact; its noncontrolling absent-agency application is not adopted.
- Lyons and Whitmore retain the requirement of supported actual or imminent personal exposure; severity of a possible harm does not replace that connection.
- Powers is applied without combining its own-stake, close-relationship and hindrance requirements or creating a universal bar to provider representation.
- Arizonans is applied for continuing controversy and limited jurisdictional correction. Bender and Corrick retain their corrective role; Bancorp retains equitable vacatur, and Munsingwear its role in clearing judgments in the proper circumstances.
- Abbott Laboratories is applied to both fitness and hardship; Toilet Goods is confined to its actual enforcement and review setting. Babbitt supplies protection against requiring completed prosecution, without establishing that these conditional duties actually apply.
- Vacco and Glucksberg remain decisions permitting their specified prohibitions. Neither requires prohibition, validates Oregon's safeguards or supplies opponents' standing.
- Tanford's actual continuing exposure, Jeffries's live-relief boundary, Chicago's distinction between jurisdiction and merits, and Rouse's supported-threat requirement remain intact; none decides this statute's duties.

##### The stopped business presents a concrete and ripe challenge

**Holding:** A business presents a concrete, ripe pre-enforcement challenge when a criminal statute directly reaches its existing activity, it has stopped that activity but intends to resume, serious penalties create a sufficiently real enforcement risk, and the requested relief would remove that present federal constraint. Those conditions are met here; neither an actual prosecution nor resolution of every independent state-law restriction is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Government's prediction of unlikely prosecution is not a disavowal; lack of disavowal alone is not sufficient in every case. Independent state restrictions need not all disappear for federal relief to redress the federal barrier.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abbott Laboratories supplies fitness and hardship; Bennett requires personal injury, causation and likely redress at the relevant stage. Younger supplies no abstention ground without a pending state prosecution.

### Present civil obligations and pre-enforcement ripeness

**Governing question:** Whether a legal duty can create injury without an express statutory or criminal penalty.

**Current rule:** A presently applicable legal obligation or supported threatened civil enforcement may burden conduct and establish Article III injury even without an express statutory or criminal penalty. The claimant must establish concrete personal application, traceability and redressability. Ripeness separately examines fitness and hardship: a predominantly legal question alone does not prove hardship, but possible later civil enforcement does not categorically require postponing review of a presently burdensome duty.

**Authority by component:**

- Compelled participation: injury and ripeness: Lee v. Harcleroad, 1998-01-20; Stevens Part III-A with all seven other Associates; Part III-B’s remand with O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Conditional statutory duties, an asserted obligation or an abstract fear do not themselves establish present application. The rule creates no presumption of provider standing, statutory duty, violation or automatic hearing.

**Related current rules:** Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing supplies the ordinary injury and litigation-stage proof requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Lee v. Harcleroad — Pre-enforcement injury and ripeness.

### APA review of completed species-consultation determinations

**Governing question:** APA review and finality of completed §1536 consultation determinations.

**Current rule:** The ESA does not itself preclude APA review of administrative consultation claims for which its citizen-suit channels supply no other adequate judicial remedy. Claimed injury from restrictions unsupported by the best scientific and commercial data is at least arguably within §1536(a)(2)'s protected interests. A Biological Opinion is final agency action when it completes the Service's consultation determination and an incidental-take statement carries legal consequences by conditioning protection from taking liability on compliance. The operating agency's retained discretion does not by itself turn that completed, consequential determination into tentative advice.

**Authority by component:**

- Bennett v. Spear — The consultation claims have an APA route and a provision-specific interest, 1997-03-19; unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies the APA route and provision-specific interests
- Bennett v. Spear — The Biological Opinion is completed action with legal consequences, 1997-03-19; the same all-nine coalition supplies consultation finality and conditional legal consequences

**Present operation effective:** Bennett, 1997-03-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 702 addresses relief other than money damages and preserves other legal and equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. Any mandatory or injunctive decree must identify by name or title the responsible federal officers and their successors. Section 704 requires final action and no other adequate court remedy.

**Controlling construction:** Section 1536(a)(2) requires avoidance of likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat using the best scientific and commercial data available, unless the Committee has granted an exemption under (h). Under (b)(4), the Secretary must conclude that the action will not violate (a)(2), or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed not to violate it, and conclude that incidental taking will not violate that subsection. For a marine mammal, taking also requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The resulting statement specifies the taking’s impact, reasonable and prudent minimization measures, any required marine-mammal measures, and implementing terms including reporting. Section (o)(2) protects only taking that complies with those terms; it is not unconditional immunity from every ESA duty. The separate exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not the source of this statement’s effect.

**Open question:** The statutory and scientific merits, actual designation, triggered duty and ultimate relief remain for adjudication.

**Related current rules:** APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals states the independent §704 finality and required/inoperative-agency-appeal rule; Equitable restraint of officers acting beyond statutory authority addresses the separate non-APA officer remedy.

**Holdings navigation:** Bennett v. Spear (1997-03-19) — Administrative Law / Review of completed biological consultation; Bennett v. Spear (1997-03-19) — Whether the Biological Opinion completes agency action with legal consequences.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Scalia, joined by Thomas, agrees that absence of an express penalty is not a categorical injury bar, but would affirm the participation dismissal. Assuming an obligation that arises upon a possible future request does not establish that these plaintiffs presently confront it. The reported absence of a concrete trigger, present burden or supported enforcement threat defeats injury, and absence of supported current hardship independently defeats ripeness despite predominantly legal fitness. The appellate court's conditional observation about imminence does not establish present application. Their position does not foreclose a later action supported by concrete jurisdictional facts.

Stone-Zsela would affirm dismissal without prejudice in full. The patient chain depends on future eligibility, requests, independent medical decisions, safeguard failures and ingestion contrary to true wishes. The providers and facilities identify no present request, concrete duty, compliance cost or credible enforcement threat. In his view that unformed chain prevents identification of injury, causation or a ripe dispute, so remand is unwarranted. He reaches no assisted-death merits and preserves a later appropriate challenge by an eligible patient facing imminent application, a provider confronting a concrete statutory duty and credible enforcement, or a facility with an identified participation conflict.
