## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

**Authority by component:**

- Appropriate relief in an existing action and availability of Title IX compensation: Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools — Compensatory relief in the established Title IX action, February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of the categorical funding-notice bar: Franklin — Notice does not bar damages for the pleaded intentional violation, February 26, 1992. Both holdings rest on White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supply judgment votes only on a narrower statutory ground.

- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response, 1997-05-27; five direct joins supply the teacher-harassment recipient rule

**Present operation effective:** Franklin, 1992-02-26, for the remedy-availability rule; Kinman, 1997-05-27, resolves the separate teacher-harassment recipient standard.

**Open question:** Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX supplies Kinman's controlling recipient standard. Recipient attribution outside that setting, punitive relief, unintended discriminatory effects and genuinely ambiguous funding conditions remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992) — Statutory discrimination remedies. Kinman v. Omaha Public School District (1997-05-27) — Civil Rights / Teacher harassment and institutional responsibility.


## Exact selected Holdings entry: Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort v. Ash, 422 U.S. 66 (1975) — applies its inquiry into an implied private remedy, with congressional intent decisive.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11 (1979) — applies its requirement of congressional intent to create the particular remedy asserted; statutory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992, decision — preserves the distinction between the existence of a cognizable action and appropriate relief within it; no Title IX holding is narrowed.

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. The reporting ground therefore cannot independently sustain the challenged caseworker injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-A, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this separate statutory holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wright, 479 U.S. 418 — applies the need to identify a privately enforceable statutory right; its distinct rent holding remains in force.
- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. Rejection of this distinct statutory vehicle does not decide private enforcement of §671(a)(16).

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the ground rejecting direct implied enforcement of the reporting claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains open as to enforceable right, implied action, violation and additional lawful relief. No constitutional claim is revived or nationwide three-day deadline imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort, 422 U.S. 66, and Transamerica, 444 U.S. 11 — apply the congressional-intent requirement to the distinct implied reporting action.
- Franklin, February 26, 1992, decision — applies its action/remedy distinction without changing the established Title IX action or relief available within it.

## Exact selected Holdings entry: Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

## Exact selected Holdings entry: Blessing v. Freestone

#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove that categorical ground without creating an individual right or changing the preserved conclusion that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The respective statutes apply to actions pending on October 20 and October 31, 1994 and those filed thereafter, including this action. Other established grounds governing private actions remain except the state-plan ground the legislation removes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Suter*'s actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights rulings and separate implied-action rulings remain, together with its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim. The statutory reference imports no broader state-plan immunity.

##### Whether a general prayer for relief eliminates Madrid's particular distribution allegation

**Holding:** Madrid's allegation of nondistribution under former §657(b)(1) requires its own inquiry despite the broad programwide prayer. Each actual claim must be matched to its provision, conduct, remedy, governing version, and operative dates; an obsolete or not-yet-effective duty cannot silently justify an injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former distribution language is more specific than the aggregate benchmark, but the Court decides no individual right, eligibility, payment fact, defendant, or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not award a separate $50 for every installment regardless of timing. The rule applies to covered approved-plan collections for fiscal years after September 30, 1976, subject to the distinct foster-care rules in subsection (d).

**Material application:** The foster-care sequence first reimburses current maintenance; qualifying excess up to ordered support goes to the placement agency for the child's interests; the remainder reimburses unrepaid earlier foster-care or family-assistance payments with appropriate federal reimbursement; any balance goes to the agency for the child. The agency may reserve its payments for later needs or make them available to the person providing daily care. No applicable exception or payment fact is established for Madrid.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remand must apply the following distinct statutory dates and qualifications, without inferring an Arizona implementation event or default:

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.
- The former §654(24) operational-computer deadline was changed from October 1, 1995 to October 1, 1997 by the October 12, 1995 amendment; the old date establishes no present default. The 1996 text separately requires compliance by October 1, 1997 with requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and by October 1, 2000 with requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996. The latter deadline extends one day for every day the Secretary misses the final-regulations deadline of two years after August 22, 1996. Implementation deadlines remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates.
- Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), takes effect October 1, 1998. Section 312(d)(2) separately permits a State processing receipts through local courts on August 22, 1996 to continue that receipt processing through September 30, 1999, at its option, through the courts already processing those payments by enactment. That narrow exception concerns §654b(b)(1)'s unit receipt-handling requirement; it is not a wholesale extension of every computer-system obligation. No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

##### Whether relief may follow general administrative deficiencies without a proved individual violation

**Holding:** Relief requires an actual individual right, an available §1983 channel, a proper defendant, breach, causation, and a fitted lawful remedy. General administrative failure alone supports neither an undifferentiated compliance injunction nor a prescribed statewide staffing or computer redesign; rejection of the broad theory does not require dismissal of every concrete claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for provision-specific adjudication. No specified payment, damages award, structural injunction, or wholesale dismissal is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No directly implied Title IV-D action is decided. The Eleventh Amendment argument and proposed overruling of *Thiboutot*, neither raised below nor included in the questions granted, are not reached; defendant-specific issues remain subject to their own inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Maine v. Thiboutot* remains undisturbed. *Livadas*'s separate remedial inquiries prevent both an automatic structural decree and categorical rejection of particular claims.

## Exact selected Holdings entry: Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274

#### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1866.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive attribution route

**Holding:** A funding recipient's responsibility for a teacher's intentional discrimination cannot invariably be defeated solely because no official empowered to correct the harassment had actual notice. Kinman is limited insofar as it made that particular notice route exclusive; employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical regulatory breach, standing alone, still does not establish recipient liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this limited negative proposition. They do not adopt a common complete damages framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Limited insofar as actual notice to a corrective official was the exclusive recipient-attribution threshold. Its actual-notice, deliberate and unreasonable disregard and causal-injury route remains available, together with the other Title IX elements. Its rejection of automatic teacher-status attribution and a technical policy breach as sufficient by themselves remains. No earlier sole-Justice awareness inference becomes Court law merely through this decision.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as allowing appropriate compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. The present attribution holding does not establish a damages award or unintended-effects liability.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** The established private action remains intact; no new regulatory cause of action is recognized.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Its distinction between a directly shared negative proposition and unadopted complete affirmative frameworks supports keeping the exclusivity holding separate from the limited Marks analysis.

##### Authority materially enabling discrimination, combined with the recipient's causal fault, supplies a narrower attribution route

**Holding:** A recipient may be held responsible when school-conferred authority materially enables a teacher's intentional discrimination and the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This is a sufficient route for attributing the discrimination beyond the actual-corrective-official-notice route; it establishes neither the underlying discrimination nor causal loss, damages or the defeat of a supported defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has no other formal joins. Its stated attribution component supplies the narrower controlling ground under Marks v. United States and Cipollone's component-specific application. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently support attribution whenever actual delegated educational power materially aids intentional harassment, without Stone-Zsela's additional recipient-fault requirement. School-conferred authority must actually and materially enable the wrong; title or opportunity alone is insufficient under either ground. Every case meeting the narrower conjunction therefore meets that independently sufficient broader attribution route. Those judgment-supporting positions total five, without converting the four Justices into joins in Stone-Zsela's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authority, recipient-fault and causal-continuation conditions are conjunctive. The alternative involving reporting failure requires willful blindness and deliberate indifference, not constructive notice or negligence alone. The Court does not adopt ordinary agency liability as a complete general rule, the four-Justice negligence route or every Title VII attribution principle. Discrimination, institutional fault, causation, compensable injury, damages and defenses remain unresolved. Official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability and state-law remedies are outside this holding. The separate regulatory duties to adopt and publish prompt, equitable grievance procedures under 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b) and give nondiscrimination notice under §106.9(a) are not converted into strict private damages liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Marks v. United States:** Applied to the particular sufficient attribution ground, without treating every reason or remedy proposed by a judgment supporter as controlling.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group:** Its component-specific logical-subset inquiry supports the limited attribution holding despite the absence of whole-opinion joins; separate proof, defenses and damages questions remain distinct.
- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson, Andrade and Faragher:** Their employment-attribution principles are not transferred wholesale to Title IX. Faragher's unresolved effective-protection/nonuse defense is not resolved here.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its funding-notice principle remains; neither it nor Title IX's administrative enforcement sequence becomes a literal exhaustion requirement for this private action.

##### The appealed Title IX summary judgment is reversed for ordinary further proceedings

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this formal reversal and ordinary remand. Stone-Zsela alone adopts Part III's application of his recipient-fault reasoning to the disputed warning and reporting evidence; the other four support further proceedings on their separately stated grounds. Their judgment agreement is not a five-Justice finding of actual knowledge, deliberate disregard, willful blindness, negligence, material aid, causation or liability. The Court orders neither compensation nor a compulsory trial. The unappealed constitutional, personal and state-law claims are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the district on the appealed Title IX claim, and the case is remanded. The lower courts must determine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused it to continue, while preserving the actual-notice route and the remaining elements, ordinary procedures and defenses.

## Exact selected Holdings entry: New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 91-543, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-558, County of Allegany, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-563, County of Cortland, New York v. United States et al. The same component-specific relief applies in all three dockets.

For the ripe-review holding in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563, see Federal Courts — Ripeness of future statutory obligations.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to the distinction between an authorized conditional arrangement and compulsory state regulation; it supplies no blanket Guarantee Clause justiciability rule.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.
