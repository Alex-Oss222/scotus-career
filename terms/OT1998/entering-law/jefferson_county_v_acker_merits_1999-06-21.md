## Event

Jefferson County v. Acker, No. 98-10. Argued March 29, 1999; decided June 21, 1999. Certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit's en banc judgment, 137 F.3d 1314, sustaining the federal forum and holding the county occupational levy invalid as applied to the respondent federal judges. The questions concern federal-officer removal, the Tax Injunction Act, the operation of the levy under the federal salary-tax consent, and the renewed source-discrimination defense.

Jefferson County brought state-court collection actions against federal district judges William Acker and U. W. Clemon, who removed under 28 U.S.C. §1442(a)(3). Ordinance 1120, effective January 1, 1988, imposes one-half of one percent of compensation attributable to work within the County, with apportionment for work elsewhere. It includes federal, state and local officeholders, exempts persons paying specified state privilege, license or occupational taxes, and declares working without payment unlawful. No actual county restriction preventing a federal judge from sitting is established.

The district court rejected source discrimination and also accepted a distinct Compensation Clause ground. The earlier appellate account reports no challenge to the discrimination rejection; respondents renewed that defense here as an alternative ground for the same favorable judgment. The en banc merits disposition did not reach the Compensation Clause, which remains outside the questions decided here. The County first contests the federal forum and alternatively seeks rejection of the immunity judgment. The United States supports a different jurisdictional position: no Tax Injunction Act bar, but an insufficient official-act nexus for removal.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court sustains the federal forum, rejects the intergovernmental-immunity and source-discrimination defenses, and reverses and remands with the distinct Compensation Clause question undecided.

## Holdings

### Federal-officer removal is proper for these collection actions

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal judge may remove a county collection action under the event-applicable §1442(a)(3) when the action has the required connection to official duties and the judge asserts a colorable federal defense. The work-linked levy and official-duty immunity defense establish those requirements here; the defense's eventual failure does not defeat removal.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, has six direct votes for this rule, explanation and scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Mesa requires a federal defense in addition to federal office, and Willingham preserves a federal forum for a colorable official-duty defense without requiring the officer first to prevail on it. The County's demand arises from the compensation attributable to these judges' performance of federal judicial duties, and the ordinance makes engaging in the work the asserted basis of liability. Their claim that this exaction impermissibly burdens those duties supplies the necessary connection and a nonfrivolous federal defense. The United States' contrary nexus argument correctly separates personal tax liability from an official act, but gives insufficient weight to the particular work-linked ordinance and defense presented here. Federal employment and a compensation measure alone would not authorize removal of every personal obligation. This holding neither dispenses with the office nexus nor imports a later expansion of the removal statute. Characterizing the defense as colorable decides the forum, not whether federal law ultimately exempts the judges from this levy.

### The Tax Injunction Act does not bar the defensive adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1341 does not prevent a federal court with removal jurisdiction from adjudicating the federal defenses in a taxing authority's own collection suit. These collection actions seek payment, rather than anticipatory federal restraint of assessment, levy or collection by taxpayers.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II has all nine Justices' joins, with Scalia, Souter and Thomas accepting the Court's removal ruling as the predicate for this independent question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act limits federal injunctions, suspensions and restraints of state-tax administration; a tax collector's suit to obtain payment is a different proceeding. Removal changes its tribunal without transforming the defendants' defenses into an affirmative taxpayer action. Rejecting the County's asserted liability would resolve its own claim, not grant a separately requested anticipatory injunction. Farm Credit distinguished substantive tax immunity from access to a federal forum and involved private associations seeking affirmative injunctive and declaratory relief with an adequate state remedy conceded. Its entity-specific rule does not decide these removed judicial-officer collection actions. The Court therefore need not find a universal federal-officer exemption from §1341, treat the United States' amicus appearance as party joinder, or supply a new finding about the adequacy of Alabama remedies. Taxpayer-initiated federal restraint remains subject to its own statutory limits.

### The levy operates as compensation taxation within §111

**Holding and operative rule:** The levy asserted in these collection actions operates as a tax on compensation for federal personal services within 4 U.S.C. §111, notwithstanding its occupational-license label. Section 111's consent does not authorize discrimination because of the compensation's federal source or a local requirement of permission to exercise federal office.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven directly adopt this compensation-tax rule on the jurisdictional predicate stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Graves and §111 require attention to what the exaction does, rather than a constitutional exemption created by its state-law title. The County seeks a percentage of compensation attributable to services performed within its boundaries, with apportionment for work elsewhere. It has not established or exercised a local veto over the judges' performance of their duties. The unlawful-work language and exemptions for occupations paying other license charges are relevant to the ordinance's operation, but together do not convert the revenue demand presented here into an exercise of licensing control over federal adjudication. A compensation measure alone would not save every direct federal-function license; the actual demand and operation are decisive. Blaze preserves the distinction between legal incidence and indirect economic effect, while reserving direct federal burdens and actual instrumentalities; federal judges are not equated with independent contractors. The Court decides no hypothetical restriction preventing a judge from sitting and need not decide the County's alternative Buck Act theory. The separate Article III Compensation Clause question is not resolved by this intergovernmental-immunity holding.

### The renewed discrimination defense may be considered on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Respondents may defend the existing favorable judgment on the renewed, record-supported source-discrimination ground without seeking greater relief, and the Court elects to consider that alternative here. Its earlier rejection and reported intermediate nonpursuit do not establish an automatic cross-appeal defect, jurisdictional bar or intentional waiver.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV has the same seven Justices' direct joins for this confined decision to reach the defense. The permission to consider an alternative is distinct from an obligation to consider every omitted ground.

**Controlling explanation:** X-Citement Video applies American Railway Express's rule allowing a prevailing party to support the same judgment on a ground in the record, including one rejected below, without enlarging its rights or reducing its opponent's rights under that judgment. Here the district court adjudicated discrimination and respondents expressly renewed it in their merits brief. They seek the same exemption judgment, not additional relief. The intermediate nonpursuit remains relevant to review discretion, but the established tax and comparison record permits decision without a new factual theory or findings. Zafiro's prudential treatment of an individual petitioner's unpursued claim does not erase this favorable-judgment posture. AT&T declined to construct an unadvanced independent theory; this one is actually presented. Lebron preserves bounded discretion over included legal arguments, not a duty to revive every abandoned ground. Dandridge's remand option and West's distinct alternative-ground remand likewise create no mandatory postponement of this established and renewed legal defense. The Court chooses present consideration without finding an intentional waiver or assuming that the lower rejection establishes the defense's substantive failure.

### The presented source-discrimination defense fails

**Holding and operative rule:** On this ordinance and record, taxing the respondent judges' compensation while exempting occupations subject to other specified state licensing charges does not discriminate because their compensation comes from a federal source. The intergovernmental-immunity judgment must be reversed, with remand consistent with that determination and the reservation of the separate Compensation Clause question.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies seven direct votes for the source-discrimination determination and limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Davis forbids unfavorable treatment attributable to the federal source of compensation and requires attention to the relevant comparison. This levy includes federal, state and local officeholders; the record establishes that state judges pay it. The exemption for occupations paying other state or county licensing charges does not, on this record, distinguish state compensation from federal compensation. Federal judges' inability personally to fall within that alternative-license category does not by itself establish the forbidden source classification. The Court independently rejects the presented comparison rather than presuming the district court correct or treating all possible exemptions as necessarily valid. No unadmitted workforce percentages, individual licensing facts or withholding practices supply the conclusion. An exemption favoring comparable state officials, an actual federal-source distinction or a genuine local restraint of judicial performance would present a different question. The separate Compensation Clause ground was accepted below but not reached by the en banc court and is not decided here. Remand leaves its proper treatment below without reopening discrimination, quantifying a tax obligation or directing collection regardless of that remaining ground.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Mesa v. California:** Applies the distinct federal-defense requirement; federal office alone is insufficient and merits success is not required to establish a colorable defense.
- **Willingham v. Morgan:** Applies the protective federal-officer forum and the connection to official duties without demanding advance victory on the defense; the particular nexus remains necessary.
- **Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas:** Distinguishes its affirmative injunction/declaration action by private associations and conceded adequate state remedy. Its separation of substantive immunity from forum entitlement remains intact; no universal federal-officer exception is created.
- **Graves v. New York ex rel. O'Keefe:** Applies the rejection of categorical immunity for federal compensation taxation and the need to assess the tax's actual operation, subject to governing nondiscrimination and federal-function limits.
- **Davis v. Michigan Department of Treasury:** Applies federal-source nondiscrimination and relevant comparator analysis; it does not require identical treatment of every differently licensed occupation regardless of the reason for classification.
- **Arizona Department of Revenue v. Blaze Construction Co.:** Preserves legal-incidence analysis and the distinction from indirect economic effect; its independent-contractor result is not extended to erase the separate protection of federal judges or direct federal functions.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video and United States v. American Railway Express:** Apply the prevailing respondent's ability to renew a record-supported alternative for the same favorable judgment without seeking enlarged relief.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Preserves bounded review discretion and limits concerning distinct claims, questions, relief and missing facts; no universal duty to consider abandoned grounds follows.
- **Zafiro v. United States:** Distinguishes a petitioner's personal unpursued claim from this renewed respondent alternative; its prudential preservation rule remains.
- **AT&T v. Central Office Telephone:** Distinguishes refusal to construct an independent theory that the respondent had not advanced; this defense was adjudicated below and renewed here.
- **Dandridge v. Williams and West v. Gibson:** Preserve lawful remand for appropriate unresolved alternatives without compelling remand of this already adjudicated, record-supported discrimination question.

## Law After Decision

Effective June 21, 1999, these federal judicial officers may defend the County's work-linked collection suits in the federal forum because the office nexus and colorable federal defense are established. Section 1341 does not bar the defensive adjudication in that collector-initiated posture. The particular occupational levy is compensation taxation within §111 and the presented source-discrimination defense fails. No categorical exemption for federal judges or local power to license their duties follows. The independent Compensation Clause question and proper calculation or procedural matters remain for their lawful treatment below. The discrimination issue is resolved, not remanded for reopening. Existing limits on affirmative taxpayer suits, actual federal-function restraints, source discrimination and distinct review questions retain their force; no precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Souter and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The collection actions seek payment of the judges' personal tax obligations, and the sued-on nonpayment is not an official judicial act merely because the tax is measured by judicial pay. A colorable immunity defense supplies one removal requirement but cannot substitute for the statutory office nexus. They would set aside the federal adjudication and return the actions to state court, where the defenses could be heard. Their objection turns on the event-applicable removal text and the actual relief sought, not on denial that the tax defense is colorable. Once the Court establishes removal, however, they accept that resolution as the predicate for joining Parts II–IV: §1341 does not bar this defensive collector action, the levy operates as compensation taxation, the renewed discrimination defense fails, and the intergovernmental-immunity judgment should be reversed with the Compensation Clause reserved. Those conditional joins decide the independent questions without disguising their disagreement about the proper forum.

**Breyer, joined by O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that removal is proper and §1341 poses no bar, but would affirm because the ordinance's cumulative operation imposes a direct charge on performing federal judicial functions. Liability is tied to engaging in work and entitlement to compensation; work without payment is declared unlawful; apportionment follows where the work is done; and occupations subject to other licensing charges are exempt. The argument does not rest on the license label alone or require proof that a county officer has already stopped a judge from sitting. Section 111 consents to compensation taxation, not to a local charge on the performance of federal functions merely measured by pay. The Buck Act addresses federal-area taxation and supplies no independent consent to that direct burden. Their reading of the operative ordinance, consistent with the direct-burden reservations in Farm Credit and Blaze, would sustain the existing immunity judgment. Because they find §111 consent inapplicable, they need not separately determine the source-discrimination defense or order an alternative Compensation Clause remand. They prescribe no universal checklist for every occupational levy.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower court for proceedings consistent with the Court's compensation-tax and nondiscrimination holdings. Proper treatment of the distinct, unreviewed Compensation Clause ground remains below. No unconditional tax-collection order, tax amount, new evidence requirement, reopening of discrimination or local licensing authority is supplied by the mandate. The federal forum remains available under the Court's threshold rulings.
