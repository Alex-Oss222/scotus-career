### Department of the Army v. Blue Fox, Inc. — 1999-01-20

Courts must identify the claimant's actual entitlement and the operation of the requested remedy before treating a monetary demand as specific relief under §702. A private-debt substitute does not become authorized merely through an equitable lien, a named project account, notice or the Government's failure to obtain a required bond. A genuine identified-property claim, enacted payment mandate, properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against an actual Miller Act surety or another unequivocal waiver remains governed by its own requirements. None is awarded here.

## Current-term law

### Cunningham v. Hamilton County, No. 98-727. — 1999-06-14

#### Holdings

**Complete separation from the merits.** A monetary Rule 37 discovery sanction against counsel during ongoing litigation is not immediately appealable under §1291's collateral-order doctrine merely because counsel's representation has ended. The category does not satisfy the requirement of an important question completely separate from the merits when review of discovery relevance, responses and compliance remains intertwined with the case's substantive issues and litigation record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently requires dismissal of the interlocutory appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require every collateral-order condition, not conclusiveness alone. Assuming the monetary determination is conclusive, evaluating whether discovery was relevant, responses sufficient or orders obeyed may require review of the underlying dispute. Counsel's personal financial interest differs from the client's but does not detach those questions from the litigation. Digital Equipment requires category-level assessment rather than an exception whenever one order looks especially separate, and Swint rejects convenience as a substitute for appellate jurisdiction. Cunningham's disqualification ended her representation; it did not end the civil-rights action or alter the connection between the discovery sanction and that action. The Court decides appealability of the $1,494 order, not whether the sanction, its amount or the disqualification was correct.

**Effective review after final judgment.** Independently, this monetary discovery sanction remains effectively reviewable after final judgment: the attorney personally subject to it may challenge her own sanction even though she no longer represents the client. Ordinary payment, delay and professional or reputational burdens do not establish an important right that will be irretrievably lost without immediate review.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently defeats the collateral-order appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Digital Equipment distinguishes the costs of delayed review from the loss of a right sufficiently important to justify immediate appeal. Later reversal can vacate the monetary obligation and direct repayment or other correction. Cunningham's own right to seek review does not depend entirely on her former client's decision to appeal. Behrens preserves qualifying legal-immunity appeals, but this sanction establishes no immunity or right to avoid the trial itself. The Sixth Circuit reserved effective unreviewability; the Court resolves it as a legal question here. Ending representation alone does not make later relief ineffective. The holding does not resolve a materially different final-withdrawal situation establishing genuine independent nonparty finality, another sanctions regime, or a contempt adjudication carrying its own finality. Section 1292(b), extraordinary mandamus and genuine independent contempt routes retain their own requirements; none is satisfied or granted by this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cohen retains the narrow collateral-order exception and its cumulative conditions.
- Coopers & Lybrand preserves those cumulative requirements; conclusiveness does not excuse a failure of complete separation or effective unreviewability.
- Digital Equipment supplies category-level discipline and preserves adequate final review despite irretrievable ordinary litigation costs; those principles apply to this monetary discovery sanction.
- Swint rejects convenience-based appellate jurisdiction and distinguishes ordinary liability from legal immunity.
- Behrens retains otherwise qualifying legal-immunity appeals, not immediate review of every personal monetary or professional burden.

#### Law After Decision

The final-judgment rule governs this monetary Rule 37 sanction even after representation ends through disqualification. The attorney retains review of her own sanction after final judgment. Different statutory appeal provisions, extraordinary remedies and genuinely distinct finality categories retain their existing prerequisites. There is no general attorney-status exception and no holding that every order against an attorney is unappealable.

### Cunningham v. Hamilton County, No. 98-727. — 1999-06-14

Kennedy joins both Court grounds and stresses that exceptional circumstances need not leave an attorney without a remedy. If a trial court refuses to stay enforcement and exceptional resulting hardship is itself likely to cause injustice, mandamus might permit review of the sanction or the refusal to stay under that remedy's demanding requirements. A genuine contempt adjudication against a person whose interests are not congruent with a litigant's may raise a different finality question. He identifies no such hardship, contempt finding, stay entitlement or established alternative route here; the reservation creates no automatic right of immediate appeal.
