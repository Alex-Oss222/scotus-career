#### Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, 518 U.S. 938 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1691 and 95-1738.
**Decided:** July 1, 1996.

##### Whether probable cause and ready mobility require an additional federal exigency

**Holding:** Police may search a readily mobile automobile without a warrant when probable cause supports belief that it contains evidence or contraband; the Fourth Amendment requires no additional showing of unforeseen circumstances, imminent departure or insufficient time to obtain a warrant. Police custody, the ability to guard the vehicle or an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant does not itself impose that extra condition. The object sought limits the search to places and containers capable of containing it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** Carroll/Chambers supply automobile mobility; Carney adds reduced regulated-vehicle privacy; Johns preserves search authority during custody, with its separate unreasonable-delay limitation. Ross ties the permissible search to its object.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent authority to enter a home or protected curtilage, search an immobile structure as an automobile, omit probable cause or exceed object-based scope. Different residential uses and protected-premises access retain their own rules.

##### How that rule applies to the two reviewed searches

**Holding:** The accepted probable cause and adjudicated automobile circumstances bring these searches within the exception for purposes of the federal additional-exigency ground on which the judgments rest. The Court reverses that ground in each case and remands; its application is neither an unconditional approval of every entry and search act nor an instruction merely to reconsider whether the rejected urgency requirement should remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** Labron’s accepted probable cause arose from drug transactions and retrievals from an ordinary street-parked Lincoln. Its failed start on attempted impoundment and highway-legal exterior establish neither a running engine nor known pre-search incapacity. Kilgore’s accepted probable cause arose from the controlled purchase and truck connection; private parking, police custody and available guards do not add a federal urgency requirement. Lack of consent or visible contraband does not defeat an otherwise applicable automobile exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general known-mechanical-incapacity rule, lawful boundary-crossing finding, curtilage classification, conversion of house consent into truck consent, new consent/access hearing or revived Labron privacy-interest objection.

##### What the reversal and remand permit

**Holding:** Each judgment is reversed, and each case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's automobile and jurisdictional holdings. Pennsylvania may apply an actually adequate and independent state ground if properly available, and may address genuinely remaining scope or other distinct issues under its procedures; this mandate establishes neither their availability nor their merits and directs no automatic admission or prosecution outcome.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** The exception is applied to the actual reviewed additional-exigency issue, not left wholly undecided. Distinct genuinely remaining scope, access and independent state-law issues are subject to ordinary procedural predicates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new preservation, waiver, admission of evidence, conviction, retrial or automatic hearing is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Certiorari granted in each case; both suppression judgments summarily reversed and remanded, 7–2, in a reasoned decision without plenary argument. The undisclosed petition poll is not a merits tally. For jurisdictional holdings, see Federal Courts — Finality and adequate independent state grounds, these cases, July 1, 1996.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn:** The Court applies the third practical-finality category to these binding suppression determinations and the State's inability to obtain later effective review. No general interlocutory-review rule is added.
- **Michigan v. Long:** Its federal/interwoven-ground inquiry is applied separately to Labron and Kilgore. A State's power to afford greater protection remains, but possible state authority is not proof of an actual independent judgment.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Its Long holding remains controlling. Its separate exclusionary-rule exception and unresolved clerical-error attribution do not authorize these searches or require the same factual remand in a different case.
- **Sochor and Middlebrooks:** Their actually sufficient independent preservation and state constitutional grounds remain respected; the Court distinguishes them from the grounds under review here.
- **Carroll v. United States and Chambers v. Maroney:** The established automobile exception is applied without a new case-specific warrant-impracticability requirement. Police control does not by itself require a different federal rule.
- **California v. Carney:** Mobility and reduced automobile privacy support the rule; its actual setting and reservation concerning differently situated residential uses remain. It creates no general authority to enter protected property.
- **United States v. Johns:** Custody alone does not eliminate vehicle-search authority. The Court does not remove Johns's separate protection against unreasonable delay or decide a delay claim here.
- **United States v. Ross:** Probable cause's object determines the permitted places and containers to search; it is not a license for a search beyond that object.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Independent review of ultimate constitutional probability questions, clear-error protection for historical facts and due weight to grounded inferences remain. The case supplies no new fact, waiver or probable-cause presumption.
- **Whren v. United States:** Objective authority for the initial stop remains separate from search, manner, scope and duration. Its unanimity does not supply an additional vote or factual premise here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas and Pinson:** The distinct protections governing dwelling entry and the particular technological examination of domestic information remain. Neither is a general automobile rule or establishes Kilgore's property boundary or lawful access.

No prior decision is overruled. The Court applies the inherited automobile exception to reject the added federal condition; it does not adopt a new general mechanical-defect, curtilage or premises-consent rule.

#### United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1180.

**Decided:** 1993-12-13.

##### Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property

**Holding:** Before seizing real property for civil forfeiture, the Government must ordinarily give the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not itself satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment requirement. To bypass prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as notice of the pending claim recorded against the property, a restraining order, or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction, or continued unlawful use; emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Pre-seizure process portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. These six adopt the same rule, its exigency qualification, and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Soldal v. Cook County: applied to preserve the independent operation of constitutional protections; its possessory-seizure rule remains intact.
- Mathews v. Eldridge: applied to the process required before this deprivation; no new general balancing formula replaces it.
- Connecticut v. Doehr: applied for the value of notice and an opportunity to contest a prejudgment property deprivation.
- Fuentes v. Shevin: applied for the value of notice and an opportunity to contest a prejudgment property deprivation.
- Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co.: distinguished because movable property and exceptional governmental needs can justify delayed process; that exception remains available where its justification exists.

##### Mandatory administrative promptness does not supply an unstated dismissal sanction

**Holding:** Noncompliance with the distinct reporting and commencement duties in 19 U.S.C. §§1602–1604 does not require dismissal of a forfeiture action filed within §1621's five-year limitations period. Section 1602 requires immediate reporting by customs agents to customs officers; §1603(b) requires prompt reporting to the United States attorney when the specified seizure or discovery requires proceedings by that office; and §1604, for recovery requiring district-court or Court of International Trade proceedings, requires those proceedings to be commenced forthwith when the prescribed probable basis for a fine, penalty, or forfeiture appears, subject to the Attorney General's preserved determination, after inquiry, that such proceedings probably cannot be sustained or that public justice does not require institution or prosecution, in which event the facts are instead reported to the Treasury Secretary for direction. These remain mandatory duties within their stated conditions, and the ruling does not extinguish an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or other source-authorized relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Statutory timing portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and reversal of the contrary ground below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brock v. Pierce County: applied to distinguish a mandatory timing duty from an unstated loss of governmental authority.
- United States v. Montalvo-Murillo: applied to reject an automatic substantive remedy that the timing provision does not prescribe; its distinct hearing duty is not diluted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The notice holding concerns civil real-property forfeiture, not criminal forfeiture or ultimate forfeiture liability. Statutory promptness remains mandatory; an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or otherwise authorized relief is not extinguished.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The hearing ruling is affirmed, 6–3, and the statutory-dismissal ground reversed, 9–0; the case is remanded. The timely forfeiture action may proceed lawfully. Restoration, accounting or other consequences of the interim seizure require an independently authorized basis; no award, automatic dismissal, return of title or sovereign damages is ordered.

### Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property

**Governing question:** When the Government may seize real property for civil forfeiture before notice and hearing.

**Current rule:** The Government ordinarily must provide the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before civil forfeiture seizure of real property; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment duty. To proceed without prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as recorded notice of the claim, a restraining order or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use. Emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process. The protection includes control and income from rented property, not only physical occupancy.

**Authority by component:**

- Prior process and Government exigency showing: United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property — Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property, 1993-12-13. Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Pre-seizure process portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. These six adopt the same rule, its exigency qualification, and its application.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 1993-12-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Movable-property exceptions retain their actual justification. No criminal-forfeiture rule, final ownership determination, immunity waiver or automatic compensation remedy follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property.

## Event

Wyoming v. Houghton, No. 98-184. Argued January 12, 1999; decided April 5, 1999. On certiorari to the Wyoming Supreme Court, 956 P.2d 363, which suppressed evidence from a passenger's purse because probable cause directed to the driver and vehicle did not authorize searching her separate effects.

The officer observed driver David Young's syringe and received his admission that he used it to take drugs. Vehicle-level probable cause to search for drugs is conceded. The question is whether the automobile exception reaches a purse found on the rear seat and identified as the passenger's before it was opened. The officer learned of the false-name discrepancy only after opening the purse's wallet; later discoveries cannot supply earlier facts.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4.

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, who would affirm the reviewed suppression judgment.

The passenger-ownership ground cannot sustain the judgment under the automobile rule applied here. The remand does not direct unconditional reinstatement of a conviction, decide every later intrusion or erase properly available independent issues.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Breyer | Reversal and remand, 5–4 | Complete detached-container rule, actual application, person/effect limits and bounded remand |
| Dissent | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg | Would affirm the reviewed suppression judgment | Complete common proposed known-passenger-effect qualification, effect-or-passenger connection to probable cause reaching the effect, independent search grounds and application |

## Holdings

### Vehicle probable cause reaches this detached passenger-owned purse

**Holding and operative rule.** When police have probable cause to search an automobile for drugs, the automobile exception permits searching a detached passenger-owned purse found inside the car if it can conceal the drugs. Known passenger ownership does not alone remove that container from the object-based search; this holding does not authorize searching the passenger's person or decide the treatment of an effect worn or carried on the person.

**Authority.** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, counting the author within that five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** Labron applies the automobile exception without a separate exigency requirement and measures its container scope by the object sought. Ross and Acevedo likewise permit a properly supported search of places and containers capable of concealing that object. Wellons recognizes a personal privacy interest in luggage but preserves otherwise valid search exceptions. Those propositions coexist: a protected interest does not defeat an established justification for searching it. Houghton's purse was a container within the searched vehicle and could conceal the drugs. Its acknowledged ownership does not change that capacity or establish an exception to the rule. Di Re's protection of the passenger's person remains distinct; the Court does not equate a detached purse with the passenger's body. Nor does it presume Houghton guilty or engaged in a common criminal enterprise. Young's syringe and drug-use admission supplied the conceded vehicle probable cause; that existing authority justifies the initial purse intrusion. The false-name discrepancy and later discoveries play no role in justifying the initial intrusion. Searches of clothing or attached personal effects remain outside the decision.

## Precedent Treatment

- Labron/Kilgore is applied without modification: actual vehicle probable cause supplies object-based container authority without an additional exigency requirement.
- Ross and Acevedo are applied: a capable container within a justified automobile search is not exempt solely because a passenger owns it.
- Wellons is preserved: separate personal privacy and an applicable search exception are distinct questions.
- Di Re is distinguished: authority over a vehicle does not by itself authorize a bodily search.
- Knowles retains its rule that a citation alone supplies no general search authority; the independent probable cause conceded here presents a different ground.
- Carter/Johns retains its personal-right, justification and causal limits; the Court makes no new independent-source finding.

## Law After Decision

The existing automobile exception reaches this detached passenger purse. The four dissenting Justices support the same proposed ownership qualification and suppression judgment; that qualification is not adopted. Known ownership neither eliminates personal privacy nor, by itself, defeats the applicable exception. Person searches and attached effects remain separate questions.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would qualify Labron for a closed personal effect known before intrusion to belong to a passenger when vehicle probable cause derives from someone else. Location and capacity alone would not authorize the search. They would require particularized facts connecting the effect or the passenger to the object sought, establishing probable cause reaching the effect, or another independently applicable search ground. Passenger-related facts can supply that connection; the evidence need not originate from the effect itself. Independently supported probable cause reaching the passenger's effect, consent and other established exceptions remain available.

Wellons supplies the distinct privacy interest, and Di Re supports refusing to transfer suspicion merely from the car to the passenger; neither already establishes their proposed exception. They acknowledge Ross, Acevedo and Labron as the principal contrary authority. A uniform container rule, in their view, gives insufficient protection to an identified person's private effects. They answer the concealment and administrability concerns by requiring concrete indicators of transfer, shared ownership or common concealment rather than presuming those circumstances from companionship. The qualification preserves ordinary vehicle compartments, unclaimed or shared containers and independently justified searches. It creates no blanket passenger immunity, universal luggage-warrant rule or separate automobile-exigency test.

Young's syringe and admission supplied vehicle probable cause but no reported fact tied Houghton's effect or her participation to the drugs before entry. Her claimed lack of identification did not by itself create narcotics probable cause; the false identity was discovered through the intrusion requiring justification. The later pouch, track marks and admission about the black container likewise cannot justify the first search retrospectively. All four would adopt the stated limited modification and affirm the reviewed suppression judgment. They make no independent-source finding and propose no acquittal or unqualified disposition of every later intrusion.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Wyoming courts for proceedings consistent with the controlling automobile-search rule. No independent-source finding, new trial, acquittal or unconditional conviction-reinstatement order is entered. Properly available issues outside the resolved passenger-ownership ground retain their ordinary procedural and remedial treatment.

The operative chronology places Houghton's ownership claim before the initial purse intrusion and the false-name discovery afterward.

## Event

Knowles v. Iowa, No. 97-7597. Merits decision, December 8, 1998. Argued November 3, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Iowa Supreme Court, 569 N.W.2d 601 (1997), which sustained the search and Knowles's conviction under a state rule authorizing a full search after issuance of a citation.

After stopping Knowles for speeding, the officer issued a citation and then searched the automobile, finding marijuana and a pipe. No custodial arrest preceded the search; Knowles did not consent, and the officer lacked probable cause to search. The question is whether unexercised authority to arrest and issuance of a citation authorize the full search-incident-to-arrest exception.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Iowa Supreme Court's judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The search and dependent judgment cannot stand on the citation-search theory. The Court returns for application of the corrected constitutional rule and ordinary, properly available remedial law; no alternative search justification is established here. Stone-Zsela separately would direct suppression of the search's fruits and further proceedings on the remaining admissible record. No acquittal, release, replacement sentence or civil liability is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous constitutional rule and reversal | Actual-custody requirement, application and preserved independent search grounds. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Court, eight, on the remedial formulation | Return for ordinary properly available remedial law without an automatic evidence-item disposition. |
| Opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice joins | Joins Part I and reversal; would give an express suppression instruction | Suppression of this search's fruits and proceedings on the remaining admissible record. |

All eight Associate Justices join both parts. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and states his more specific remedy separately.

## Holdings

### I. A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception

**Operative rule:** Issuing a traffic citation without a custodial arrest does not authorize the full search incident to arrest; unexercised authority to make an arrest cannot substitute for the custody that triggers that exception. A full search requires an independently applicable recognized basis, and the citation, general roadside risk and observed speeding supply none for this search; supported exit orders, protective measures, consent searches and searches based on independent probable cause retain their own conditions and scope.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support. Part II's ordinary-remedial-law return has the eight Associates' support; Stone-Zsela would require the specific suppression stated in his concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** Chimel ties arrest-related search to officer protection and preservation of evidence. Robinson permits a categorical full search after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest without a new necessity showing in every case. Belton addresses the automobile setting of such an arrest; none converts every citation into custody.

The officer chose to issue the speeding citation before searching. Transport and station-custody risks had not arisen, and the observed speeding offense supplied no further evidence ordinarily to be preserved by a full automobile search. The Court does not invent a later arrest to validate the intrusion retrospectively. State permission cannot by itself establish Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

Iowa correctly emphasizes that danger can remain at roadside and that the officer had authority to arrest. Those considerations may support recognized limited precautions, not transfer the full arrest-search category to an encounter lacking its predicate. Whren preserves independent limits on an intrusion beyond the initial stop; Robinette distinguishes lawful traffic business from later detention or consent. Maryland v. Wilson's limited exit-direction authority is not automatic frisk or search authority. Ricci likewise distinguishes enforcement power from justification for an added restraint without deciding a general prohibition on arrest for this offense.

A Terry frisk requires supported reasonable suspicion that the person is armed and dangerous. A Michigan v. Long protective vehicle search requires its own reasonable belief, based on specific facts, that the person is dangerous and may gain immediate control of weapons, and remains limited to areas where a weapon may be hidden. Voluntary consent and the automobile exception retain their independent predicates. None supplies an established justification for this search. The Court neither assumes every stop safe nor imposes least restrictive means, and it leaves Robinson's rule intact for actual qualifying custody.

### II. The dependent judgment returns under the corrected constitutional rule

**Operative rule:** The citation-search theory cannot sustain this search or the dependent judgment, which is reversed and remanded for proceedings under the corrected constitutional rule and ordinary, properly available remedial law. No alternative justification is established here, and the Court orders no acquittal, release, replacement sentence, civil relief or universal exclusion rule for traffic encounters.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but would add the express suppression direction stated in his separate opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The question decided concerns the asserted authority for this search. Rejecting that authority removes the ground sustaining the judgment and requires reversal. The receiving court must apply that determination in further proceedings; it cannot sustain the search merely by repeating the citation rationale. The Court establishes no alternative search ground or new remedial exception and makes no automatic disposition of every evidence item. Stone-Zsela would give a more specific instruction to suppress the fruits and proceed on the remaining admissible record. His additional direction is not part of the eight-Justice remand formulation.

## Precedent Treatment

- Chimel v. California: applies the officer-safety and evidence-preservation purposes of arrest-incident authority; actual custody matters to the exception's reach.
- United States v. Robinson: preserves the categorical full-search rule after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest; no individualized necessity requirement is added to that setting.
- New York v. Belton: distinguishes the vehicle search incident to arrest from a full search after citation alone.
- Whren v. United States (June 10, 1996): preserves objective justification for the ordinary initial stop and independent limits on later search, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette (November 18, 1996): preserves the distinction between traffic business, subsequent restraint, voluntary consent and any prior illegality.
- Maryland v. Wilson (February 19, 1997): preserves the limited passenger-exit authority without creating automatic frisk or search power.
- Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights (May 4, 1998): applies the distinction between state enforcement authority and the reasonableness of an added intrusion; no actual-custody or immunity question from that case is decided here.
- Terry v. Ohio: preserves the limited protective frisk on its own supported danger conditions.
- Michigan v. Long: preserves a limited protective vehicle search on its distinct danger, weapons-access and scope predicates.

## Law After Decision

An issued citation and unused arrest authority do not trigger the full arrest-incident search exception. Actual lawful custodial arrests retain Robinson's categorical rule, while independent protective, consent and probable-cause search grounds retain their own requirements. Iowa's authorization cannot sustain this search on the citation theory, but the decision does not invalidate every application involving an independently constitutional search or establish general roadside arrest immunity.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins the constitutional holding and the reversal but would make the remedy explicit: the Iowa courts should suppress the fruits of this citation-only search and conduct further proceedings on the remaining admissible record. The search followed no custody, found no evidence needed to preserve the speeding observation and rested on no particularized protective or other independent ground. On that established scope, he would not leave treatment of its fruits to a general remand formulation.

His concurrence concerns this search's fruits, not a universal remedy for every traffic encounter. He preserves lawful exit directions, supported Terry protection and independent probable-cause searches. He would not order acquittal, assumes no civil liability and supplies no new holding limiting searches after actual lawful custodial arrest.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Iowa courts for proceedings under the corrected search rule and ordinary properly available remedial law. The citation-search rationale no longer supports the search or dependent judgment; no alternative justification is established by this decision. No acquittal, release, replacement sentence, civil remedy or additional Supreme Court event is directed.

The ruling concerns this search and its asserted citation-based justification; it supplies no hypothetical arrest, new danger finding or alternative ground for the search.

# City of West Covina v. Perkins — 1999-01-13

## Event
City of West Covina v. Perkins, No. 97-1230. Argued November 3, 1998; decided January 13, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 113 F.3d 1004 (May 9, 1997). The question is whether due process requires individualized instructions about state-law procedures to recover property seized under a criminal warrant. The Ninth Circuit held the California return procedures adequate, affirmed the individual officers' qualified immunity, and directed judgment for Perkins against the City on an additional-notice theory. Review resolves that instruction theory.

## Participation
All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action
The judgment requiring individualized instructions about published return procedures is reversed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any distinct, preserved challenge to adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation remains subject to its own record and ordinary requirements. No new hearing or discovery is ordered, and the individual qualified-immunity judgment is not reopened.

## Opinion Topology
| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reversal and remand | Entire opinion, including the procedural-due-process source explanation and both holdings |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Reversal and remand | Agrees with the negative no-instruction rule, record application and reservations; does not join the lead opinion's derivation of criminal-warrant notice duties from civil procedural-due-process cases |

## Holdings
### 1. Notice of the seizure does not require an individualized guide to published return procedures

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process requires timely notice reasonably calculated to identify the seized property and its responsible custodian so that an owner may seek its return. When the owner receives that notice, due process does not additionally require personalized instructions describing adequate state return procedures reasonably ascertainable from generally available statutes and judicial decisions; publication alone does not establish meaningful access to an inadequate, concealed, misleading, obstructed or practically inaccessible remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other members of the seven-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They adopt both holdings and the procedural-due-process explanation. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment and the negative rule rejecting the additional legal-instruction duty, but withhold assent to deriving notice duties for criminal warrant execution from civil procedural-due-process decisions. The seven complete joins independently establish controlling authority; no fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that notice must inform the owner what the government has done and where to seek the property. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. supplies that practical notice principle. It does not require an official to provide a personal explanation of the law an owner may use. Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft involved an operative utility-dispute procedure that a customer could not discover simply by consulting generally available law. California's judicial return procedures present a different question. Mathews v. Eldridge requires attention to the actual interests and procedures; the modest expense of adding instructions to a receipt does not alone make those instructions constitutionally necessary. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property preserves an independent due-process inquiry despite a warrant, but concerned process before civil seizure of real property. It neither settles notice after criminal seizure of movable evidence nor requires a guide to judicial remedies. The receipt here identified the property, agency, issuing judge and court, relevant dates and officer contacts. The Court rejects the additional legal-guide requirement without independently adjudicating every aspect of California's procedures or their operation. Withholding information actually necessary to invoke a remedy, or misleading or obstructing the owner, would present a different properly preserved claim.

**Limits:** The holding does not validate an unlawful initial seizure, unreasonable retention, a failure to disclose the seizure or custodian, or an inaccessible return route. It adopts no comprehensive notice checklist, universal deadline or rule that a warrant satisfies every later process obligation. California Penal Code §1536's custody subject to court order, the nonstatutory return motion recognized in People v. Icenogle, and §1540's separate restoration conditions remain distinct; this case does not merge them or decide that every owner satisfies any one route.

### 2. The additional-notice judgment must be reversed without deciding an unproved obstruction claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's requirement of individualized instructions about published return procedures, and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground, must be reversed. The omitted warrant number does not sustain that judgment because the District Court found that respondents had not established the number was necessary to bring a return motion; the reported difficulties do not establish a different obstruction ground.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling explanation. Scalia and Thomas also support this application and its reservations through their concurrence in the judgment; all nine support reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit held the available California procedures constitutionally adequate but demanded additional instruction about using them. This Court rejects that legal demand; it does not independently endorse every procedure or its practical administration. Perkins reported difficulty finding the warrant, the issuing judge's absence, a mistaken number and a sealed warrant. Some officer statements were disputed. A public index connected the searched address to the warrant number, and the District Court's unrefuted finding was that necessity of that number had not been established. These circumstances do not permit the Court to replace the lower finding with a determination that officials withheld an indispensable identifier. Nor does rejection of the instruction theory extinguish a distinct, properly preserved challenge to actual obstruction or accessibility. Such a claim must rest on its own record and procedural basis. Return of most property in June 1994 does not itself dispose of a supported claim concerning an earlier deprivation. The judgment on individual qualified immunity is not reopened, and the Court adjudicates neither entitlement to the reportedly stolen starter pistol nor damages.

**Remand limit:** The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any preserved adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation issue remains subject to ordinary procedural and substantive requirements; no new claim, fresh hearing or exploratory discovery is ordered.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306:** Applied to notice of governmental action that permits a meaningful response; it does not impose a general duty to teach published law.
- **Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft, 436 U.S. 1:** Distinguished because notice of an operative utility-dispute procedure not reasonably discoverable in public legal sources differs from instruction in published judicial remedies. Its protection of meaningful access survives.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applied to the actual notice function and available procedures; low administrative cost alone does not establish an added constitutional duty.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property (December 13, 1993):** Its independent due-process protection is preserved. Its civil real-property, pre-seizure rule is distinguished from notice following criminal seizure of movable property; no warrant-based displacement of all due process is adopted.
- **People v. Icenogle, 164 Cal. App. 3d 620:** Its recognition of a nonstatutory return motion identifies an available state-law route. The Court does not convert that route into §1540's different statutory restoration conditions or decide a particular owner's entitlement.

## Law After Decision
An owner with timely notice of the seizure and custodian has no federal entitlement to personalized instruction about adequate, reasonably ascertainable published return procedures. Meaningful notice, actual access and a distinct supported obstruction claim remain separate questions. The ruling changes the Ninth Circuit's instruction requirement while leaving Good's civil real-property protection and California's separate return routes intact. It resolves no independent immunity, warrant-validity, damages or title question.

## Separate Writings
**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** The concurrence agrees that officials need not give personalized instruction about generally available return law and that the missing warrant number does not sustain the judgment on this record. It withholds assent to the Court's use of civil procedural-due-process decisions as the source of notice requirements when officers execute a criminal search warrant. Mullane's notice setting and Good's civil real-property seizure do not, without further analysis, establish the duties attending execution of a criminal warrant. That source objection does not require approval of secrecy or obstruction, a finding that the Fourth Amendment affirmatively commands a particular inventory, or a holding that it displaces every later due-process protection. The concurrence leaves the constitutional source, timing and detailed content of any separate notice obligation unresolved and joins the limited reversal and remand.

## Procedure After Action
The case returns to the lower courts with directions to reject the additional individualized-instruction theory. Any properly preserved distinct claim remains governed by its own procedural posture and proof. The decision does not establish obstruction, entitlement to the reported stolen pistol, a damages award or invalidity of the original seizure.
