## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory state executive administration of federal private-party regulation

### Compulsory state executive administration of federal private-party regulation

**Governing question:** Whether Congress may command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons.

**Current rule:** Congress may regulate private conduct within its enumerated powers, preempt conflicting state law and use federal institutions to enforce federal obligations. The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses do not authorize it to command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative investigative, screening or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons, including the records research and implicit required acceptance of the dealer's notice and the purchaser's statement that §922(s)(2)'s mandatory inquiry entails. The defect is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not the number of hours required or the absence of federal reimbursement. The rule rests on structural federalism and is categorical: no case-by-case balancing of burdens and benefits applies. Reasonable-effort limits, a designee option, temporary duration, modest effort or retained local discretion do not make the duty voluntary, and the violation does not depend on proof of cost, a state-law prohibition or personal liability; an assumed promise of reimbursement would not cure it.

**Authority by component:**
- Categorical structural rule and its application to the mandatory inquiry: Printz/Mack, Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States — Part I — Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons, Thomas with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy (five), 1997-06-27. O'Connor does not join Part I's reach to purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements.

**Present operation effective:** Printz/Mack, 1997-06-27.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not prohibit direct federal regulation of state activity or generally applicable laws, duties assigned to state courts as courts under the Supremacy Clause, voluntary cooperation or otherwise valid conditional federal spending, and it does not limit Congress's authority to regulate the underlying transactions. Garcia, Baker, Testa and Howlett are preserved, and FERC v. Mississippi remains within its bounded regulatory and adjudicative setting. The rule is not a physical-occupation rule, an individual-rights scrutiny standard or a totality test, and it rests on no Take Care or unity-of-the-executive holding. Duties under constitutional grants specifically involving state governmental action, including federal elections; actual consent to cooperative arrangements; statutory compensation agreements; and the complete rights of nonparty dealers remain unresolved. The decision creates no compensation rule, no individual Second Amendment holding and no new substantive severability rule.

**Open question:** Whether purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements imposed on unwilling state executive officers fall within the prohibition has no Court answer: O'Connor reserves it, and Part I's reach to such duties is the opinion of four Justices only (Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Kennedy).

**Related current rules:** “Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations” remains intact, and this rule extends its structural prohibition from compulsory legislation and ownership to compulsory executive administration; valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation, lawful spending arrangements and the distinct sources of state-court and election duties retain their own boundaries.

**Controlling construction:** Section 922(s)(2) requires reasonable effort within five business days after the dealer notice, including available State, local and designated national records. It commands no arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Under (6)(B), unless the transaction is determined unlawful, the transmitted purchaser statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the statement was made; disclosure is confined to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection and use to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Under the separate (6)(C), written reasons are due only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. With the mandatory checks removed, these conditional provisions compel no participation from officers who decline the program and are not separately declared invalid; their operation after voluntary participation is not decided, and no officer receives a general license to retain, use or disclose information.

**Holdings navigation:** Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States (1997-06-27) — Part I — Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons; Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States (1997-06-27) — Part I — Reach to purely ministerial record-processing (opinion of four Justices).

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution

### Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution

**Governing question:** Whether use of uncharged conduct to select a sentence constitutes prior prosecution or punishment for that conduct.

**Current rule:** Considering relevant uncharged conduct to choose a sentence within the authorized statutory range for the offense of conviction is not itself prosecution or independent punishment for the uncharged offense. That sentencing use therefore does not alone bar a later distinct prosecution under the retained Grady rule. A disguised separate penalty, punishment beyond statutory authority and independent sentencing safeguards remain outside this permission. A general acquittal also does not categorically prohibit reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance in ordinary Guidelines sentencing within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. That use supplies no new conviction, disguised separate acquitted-offense punishment, excess punishment or removal of an actual offense element from the jury. Independent due-process, evidentiary, Guidelines and Rule 32 requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here: Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here, 1995-06-14. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, constitutional-characterization discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this rationale. Scalia and Thomas affirm only in the judgment on the narrower distinct-prosecution ground. 
- General acquittal and ordinary conviction-based sentencing: United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra — United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra — I. A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings, January 6, 1997; per curiam opinion joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This resolves Witte’s acquitted-conduct reservation only at that scope.

**Present operation effective:** Watts and Putra, January 6, 1997, for the ordinary acquitted-conduct rule; Witte’s uncharged-conduct and successive-prosecution boundaries survive.

**Related current rules:** Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt retains the governing successive-prosecution law. Conditional coordination of undischarged sentences addresses a later sentence separately; permission to prosecute orders no conviction or punishment.

**Holdings navigation:** Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here. United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra (1997-01-06) — A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings.



**Open question:** Extraordinary sentence magnification remains unresolved. Neither mandatory Guidelines alone nor a below-maximum sentence establishes a universal element-classification rule. The Commission’s power to change the acquitted-conduct policy remains unresolved; separate opinions do not settle it.

## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.
