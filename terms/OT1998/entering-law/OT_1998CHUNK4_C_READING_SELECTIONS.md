#### Reno v. Catholic Social Services, Inc., 509 U.S. 43 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1826.
**Decided:** June 18, 1993.

##### The individual-determination channel does not foreclose all rule challenges

**Holding:** Section 1255a(f) channels review of individual legalization determinations but does not categorically eliminate district-court review of a challenge to generally applicable regulations where no individual determination supplies an effective review route. A regulatory label alone establishes neither ripeness nor a right to district-court reconsideration of an individual denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, The individual-determination channel does not foreclose all rule challenges portion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McNary, 498 U.S. 479 — systemic-review distinction applied; individual denials remain distinct.
- Bowen v. Michigan Academy, 476 U.S. 667 — presumption of review applied, not converted into a factual burden.
- Section 1255a(f)(2) — late-filing denial bar preserved; no general permission to evade it by never filing.

##### A concrete application barrier must be established

**Holding:** An actual refusal to accept a timely application under the challenged rule supplies concrete application sufficient for a ripe district-court challenge where front-desking blocks the individual-review route. Mere awareness of an adverse rule is insufficient on this record; further facts may establish that the front-desking policy substantially caused a comparable inability to apply even without a personal front-desk rejection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, A concrete application barrier must be established portion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An individual denial ordinarily follows the statutory administrative route and judicial review connected to deportation; §1255a(f)(2)’s late-filing bar is not evaded by a regulatory label. Eligibility, substantive rule validity and general equitable tolling remain unresolved. Processing a timely tender is distinct from extending the deadline.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate undifferentiated relief and remand through the appellate court to identify actual front-desking, adequate class representation and any comparably concrete policy-caused inability to apply. No automatic extension or lawful-status award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories, 387 U.S. 136 — fitness and hardship applied to concrete effects.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 — concrete application and immediately operative burdens distinguished.
- McNary, 498 U.S. 479 — effective-review concern applied to refused filings.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Civil Rights — Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; Civil Rights — The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-386.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Whether the dispositive nonparty bar is final for limited federal review

**Holding:** Section 1257(a) permits review under Cox Broadcasting's first finality category because the preclusion ruling conclusively bars Richards and Hill's remaining federal claims and leaves its implementation preordained, despite the state interlocutory label. Review concerns only nonparty due process; separate Buck Act proceedings and the removed tax-merits question do not enlarge it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court. All nine participating Justices join Parts I–IV and every following holding and qualification; there is no separate writing.

**Material application:** State Counts 3 and 4 had already been rejected. The ruling disposes of these petitioners' remaining federal claims, while the remaining Buck Act claim belongs to a separate federal-employee group. The actual orders leave implementation preordained; state certification alone establishes neither federal finality nor a presumed later judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reversal permits litigation to continue, so the fourth-category arbitration route used in Southland and Casarotto does not supply this jurisdiction. Quackenbush's §1291 routes concern federal appellate review, not this state judgment under §1257. Neither tax constitutionality, Buck Act liability nor entitlement to a particular tax remedy is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469, supplies its first category for practically concluded claims and preordained implementation. Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1, is distinguished as a fourth-category arbitration case. Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto, May 20, 1996, preserves attention to actual proceedings rather than certification alone, but its arbitration finality and substantive FAA holdings do not decide this case. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., June 3, 1996, retains its distinct §1291 and abstention holdings and the separate tax-comity setting; it supplies no reopening of the earlier federal tax dismissal.

##### Whether shared interests alone authorize binding absent claimants

**Holding:** A State's ordinary preclusion rules remain subject to due process: essentially identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources and public importance alone do not authorize adjudication of absent persons' own legal rights. Binding may rest on constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process; finality policy and a virtual-representation label cannot replace that prerequisite.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Guardian–ward and trustee–beneficiary relationships illustrate genuine privity; bankruptcy and probate illustrate special arrangements that may lawfully foreclose successive litigation. Each person need not appear individually, but common objectives cannot alone confer representative authority. Section 1738 does not separately command Alabama to bind these strangers to its own earlier judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes without deciding that adequate representation might cure absent notice in some class or representative proceedings. Even on that assumption, this representative basis fails. The assumption neither makes representation an automatic cure nor establishes a universal individual-notice requirement. Conversely, notice, objections or opt-out opportunities do not independently cure inadequate representation, and mere notice of another person's suit supplies neither representative authority nor a general intervention duty.

Ordinary state preclusion and valid finality remain. Correction of this extreme application supplies no general retrial of ordinary state-law errors and decides none of Matsushita's full collateral-review reservation. Absence of a Rule 23 class is relevant here but does not itself invalidate every source-authorized representative judgment; Bedingfield remains effective among persons properly bound.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry v. Lee, 311 U.S. 32, supplies the constitutional requirement of genuine adequate representation while preserving lawful representative adjudication. Martin v. Wilks, 490 U.S. 755, supplies nonparty hearing protection with adequate-representation, control and due-process-consistent special-remedial exceptions; notice alone creates no general intervention duty. Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996, distinguishes ordinary recognition from constitutional authority to bind, including representation independent of notice and opt-out protections; its complete collateral-review reservation remains unresolved.

##### Whether the stated public-interest rationale binds these direct personal tax claims

**Holding:** The representative rationale used below does not constitutionally bind Richards and Hill to Bedingfield's disposition of their own tax liability and claims for paid sums. Shared taxpayer objectives and fiscal reliance do not establish the required relationship; genuine representative public-right litigation and ordinary reliance on precedent remain distinct and preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Richards and Hill were nonparties to the nonclass Bedingfield action. The earlier litigants' common constitutional objective, resources and vigorous advocacy, together with revenue and bond-financing reliance, do not establish authority over these absent taxpayers' personal claims. No deficient advocacy finding is necessary. Petitioners' no-notice assertion is not an appellate finding; no participation or control finding supplies the rationale used below, and every possible connection need not be resolved or found absent to reject it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A direct levy on a taxpayer's own funds differs from a challenge to misuse of public funds or government action affecting the taxpayer only indirectly. The Court assumes wide state latitude over standing and repeated proceedings in the latter setting. Genuine public-right representative judgments remain possible; neither refund relief nor lack of formal class treatment independently invalidates every judgment, and federal class-action requirements are not wholesale constitutional commands to the States.

The finance director's participation does not establish that public officials always adequately represent constituents' personal rights; that broader question remains open. Supposed representation of city taxpayers cannot without more establish representation of these county taxpayers. The stated rationale establishes no exclusive public-action remedial scheme, and common interests and revenue reliance cannot create one by assertion. No cause of action or tax-merits holding follows. A later court may apply precedent without conclusively binding a claimant to another person's judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brinkerhoff-Faris Trust & Savings Co. v. Hill, 281 U.S. 673, supports an opportunity to contest a direct levy without deciding this tax's validity. Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422, supplies the protected status of an otherwise available individual claim, or chose in action, reinforcing the same hearing right; it creates no new cause of action or universal state-remedy scheme. Bedingfield retains its proper force among persons lawfully bound and as precedent; only its constitutionally unsupported preclusive use against these petitioners is rejected.

##### Whether narrower issue preclusion sustains the judgment and what reversal requires

**Holding:** Respondents' narrower issue-preclusion theory cannot sustain this judgment because it also requires a constitutionally sufficient basis to bind these nonparties. The dispositive bar on the reviewed Richards–Hill federal claims is reversed and the case remanded, without deciding whether the exemption issue was actually and necessarily determined or awarding relief on the tax.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Claim and issue preclusion have distinct requirements, but both require lawful authority to bind the affected person. Narrowing the proposed bar to an exemption issue supplies no missing representative relationship. Earlier pleadings of federal theories, an opportunity to litigate them, and their actual necessary determination are distinct predicates. The binding defect makes resolution of that disputed determination unnecessary; the Court does not find that no relevant issue was ever decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Uncertainty about every notice, authorization or control fact requires no exploratory remand or adverse factual finding. A genuinely available defense or constitutionally valid binding relationship remains subject to its own proof, preservation and procedural requirements. No new theory or finding that one exists is guaranteed. No state-count ruling, earlier Tax Injunction Act dismissal, properly binding judgment or separate Buck Act claim is reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0, as to the dispositive nonparty bar on Richards and Hill's reviewed federal claims. Ordinary legally available defenses, valid preclusion and remedy conditions remain; there is no mandatory discovery, representative-adequacy hearing or opportunity to construct a new preclusion theory. No immediate trial, tax invalidation, refund, damages, injunction or eventual recovery is ordered or guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** No Supreme Court precedent is overruled; the constitutional prerequisite to binding these nonparties defeats both proposed forms of preclusion without extending collateral review or reopening otherwise binding judgments.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

#### Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-270.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions

**Holding:** A nonmerits Rule 23 defect that prevents this proposed settlement class from binding absent persons is logically antecedent to the Article III questions arising from those persons' inclusion in that class. The Court may deny certification on that ground without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate any person's substantive claim; an individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by all seven other participating Justices: eight direct votes. O'Connor does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No named or absent person's individual standing, future injury, right to sue or substantive entitlement is decided; broader constitutional notice issues remain unanswered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. forbids assumed jurisdiction to adjudicate disputed merits. Denial of this nonmerits representative device is distinguished and creates no general merits-first permission.

##### II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements

**Holding:** A settlement-only class must satisfy the applicable requirements of Rule 23(a) and (b), including typicality, adequate representation and predominance under Rule 23(b)(3). Because no trial is proposed, a court need not examine difficulties managing a trial; settlement's actual operation and bargain may inform the remaining inquiries, but their requirements cannot be relaxed or replaced by overall fairness or the desirability of settlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. The disagreement in the separate writing concerns the appellate application and remedy, not permission to dispense with certification requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Falcon requires rigorous application of actual prerequisites. Matsushita and Richards distinguish lawful representation from notice, objection opportunities and shared objectives. Castano requires an actual governing-law and proof showing without class-size or novelty prohibitions. Rule 23(e) fairness does not replace Rule 23(a) and (b).

##### III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record

**Holding:** This proposed undivided class fails Rule 23(a)(4) because the same representatives and counsel do not adequately protect the materially conflicting bargaining interests of currently injured and exposure-only claimants. Notice, the opportunity to opt out and the settlement's overall economic attractions cannot cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six direct votes with Thomas. Stevens and Breyer do not join this application.

**Material application:** Current claimants seek immediate payments, while exposure-only claimants require protection against inflation, diagnostic uncertainty, annual caps and surrender of future claims. Fixed categories and payments, caps and no inflation adjustment make the bargaining conflict concrete.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal subclass architecture or prohibition on future-claim settlements is announced. A differently structured proposal might provide independent protection, but none is approved. Constitutional notice and Matsushita's collateral-review reservation remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry requires genuine adequate representation. Matsushita preserves that prerequisite apart from notice and opt-out rights; Richards rejects a shared objective as a substitute, and Shutts's separate safeguards do not cure the conflict.

##### IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance

**Independent alternative holding:** The proposed class also fails Rule 23(b)(3) because its common asbestos-exposure and settlement objectives do not predominate over the materially different state laws, exposure histories, causation, injuries, defenses and individual claims. Removing trial-management concerns does not remove those substantive differences.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III and independently supports affirmance. Stevens and Breyer withhold this application as well.

**Material application:** Differences include product identification, latency, limitations, defenses, damages and applicable state law. A shared payment compromise does not establish that common substantive questions predominate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nationwide, mass-tort, issue and settlement classes are not categorically prohibited. No particular state-law claim or revised settlement is adjudicated; Metro-North's FELA holding does not establish the validity or injury status of these state-law claims.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Adequacy and predominance are independently sufficient grounds for affirmance, 6–2, leaving certification, settlement approval and the classwide injunction vacated. O'Connor does not participate. No new subclasses, compensation, approval or individual damages ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Castano's governing-law and proof inquiry and Falcon's actual-prerequisite rule apply despite elimination of trial-management issues. Rhone-Poulenc retains distinct extraordinary-writ limits, and Adams supplies no settlement merits ruling.

#### Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-783.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Program-wide participation opportunity

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies lawful delegated interpretation; authorizes no quota contrary to statute.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144: applies reasonable regulatory interpretation within its terms.

##### Independent constitutional scrutiny

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applies intermediate scrutiny to sex-conscious treatment.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applies genuine-objective and connected-disadvantage requirements.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547: its racial-treatment analysis supplies no changed sex standard or federal-compliance immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the University on the distinct Title IX and equal-protection claims. No reinstatement, damages or remand is ordered, and no broad section 1983 preclusion rule is adopted.

#### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1866.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive attribution route

**Holding:** A funding recipient's responsibility for a teacher's intentional discrimination cannot invariably be defeated solely because no official empowered to correct the harassment had actual notice. Kinman is limited insofar as it made that particular notice route exclusive; employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical regulatory breach, standing alone, still does not establish recipient liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this limited negative proposition. They do not adopt a common complete damages framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Limited insofar as actual notice to a corrective official was the exclusive recipient-attribution threshold. Its actual-notice, deliberate and unreasonable disregard and causal-injury route remains available, together with the other Title IX elements. Its rejection of automatic teacher-status attribution and a technical policy breach as sufficient by themselves remains. No earlier sole-Justice awareness inference becomes Court law merely through this decision.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as allowing appropriate compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. The present attribution holding does not establish a damages award or unintended-effects liability.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** The established private action remains intact; no new regulatory cause of action is recognized.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Its distinction between a directly shared negative proposition and unadopted complete affirmative frameworks supports keeping the exclusivity holding separate from the limited Marks analysis.

##### Authority materially enabling discrimination, combined with the recipient's causal fault, supplies a narrower attribution route

**Holding:** A recipient may be held responsible when school-conferred authority materially enables a teacher's intentional discrimination and the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This is a sufficient route for attributing the discrimination beyond the actual-corrective-official-notice route; it establishes neither the underlying discrimination nor causal loss, damages or the defeat of a supported defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has no other formal joins. Its stated attribution component supplies the narrower controlling ground under Marks v. United States and Cipollone's component-specific application. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently support attribution whenever actual delegated educational power materially aids intentional harassment, without Stone-Zsela's additional recipient-fault requirement. School-conferred authority must actually and materially enable the wrong; title or opportunity alone is insufficient under either ground. Every case meeting the narrower conjunction therefore meets that independently sufficient broader attribution route. Those judgment-supporting positions total five, without converting the four Justices into joins in Stone-Zsela's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authority, recipient-fault and causal-continuation conditions are conjunctive. The alternative involving reporting failure requires willful blindness and deliberate indifference, not constructive notice or negligence alone. The Court does not adopt ordinary agency liability as a complete general rule, the four-Justice negligence route or every Title VII attribution principle. Discrimination, institutional fault, causation, compensable injury, damages and defenses remain unresolved. Official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability and state-law remedies are outside this holding. The separate regulatory duties to adopt and publish prompt, equitable grievance procedures under 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b) and give nondiscrimination notice under §106.9(a) are not converted into strict private damages liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Marks v. United States:** Applied to the particular sufficient attribution ground, without treating every reason or remedy proposed by a judgment supporter as controlling.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group:** Its component-specific logical-subset inquiry supports the limited attribution holding despite the absence of whole-opinion joins; separate proof, defenses and damages questions remain distinct.
- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson, Andrade and Faragher:** Their employment-attribution principles are not transferred wholesale to Title IX. Faragher's unresolved effective-protection/nonuse defense is not resolved here.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its funding-notice principle remains; neither it nor Title IX's administrative enforcement sequence becomes a literal exhaustion requirement for this private action.

##### The appealed Title IX summary judgment is reversed for ordinary further proceedings

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this formal reversal and ordinary remand. Stone-Zsela alone adopts Part III's application of his recipient-fault reasoning to the disputed warning and reporting evidence; the other four support further proceedings on their separately stated grounds. Their judgment agreement is not a five-Justice finding of actual knowledge, deliberate disregard, willful blindness, negligence, material aid, causation or liability. The Court orders neither compensation nor a compulsory trial. The unappealed constitutional, personal and state-law claims are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the district on the appealed Title IX claim, and the case is remanded. The lower courts must determine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused it to continue, while preserving the actual-notice route and the remaining elements, ordinary procedures and defenses.

#### Reno v. Flores, 507 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-905.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### Statutory authority for the juvenile release regulation

**Holding:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits the Attorney General to use the family-custodian and discretionary-release arrangements in 8 C.F.R. §242.24 for temporary juvenile custody pending deportation proceedings; the regulation is not facially beyond that authority because it does not mandate release to every willing responsible adult. Custody remains subject to the statute's reasonable-dispatch limitation, its habeas remedy upon the requisite conclusive showing of unnecessary delay, the regulation's release avenues and the existing conditions agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six votes for this rule. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower statutory and facial-review ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carlson v. Landon, 342 U.S. 524 (1952): applies discretion bounded by the immigration statute; it does not authorize indefinite custody.
- National Center for Immigrants' Rights, December 16, 1991, decision: applies general immigration-release authority with preserved individual safeguards; does not enlarge that decision into universal detention authority.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): applies facial-review limits to the whole-regulation remedy.

##### The facial substantive-due-process challenge fails

**Holding:** The Constitution does not give a juvenile without an available parent, guardian or close adult relative a fundamental right to placement with a willing unrelated adult instead of temporary, nonpunitive government care under this regulation and the binding conditions agreement. On this facial record, the family preferences and other release avenues reasonably serve care and attendance interests; individual unconstitutional confinement and enforcement of the agreement remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. O'Connor and Souter also explain the substantial liberty interest in avoiding institutional confinement in a separate concurrence; Stone-Zsela does not join this formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the distinction between protective juvenile custody and punishment.
- Parham v. J. R., 442 U.S. 584 (1979): applies the special circumstances of juvenile care; does not extinguish a child's liberty interest.
- Youngberg v. Romeo, 457 U.S. 307 (1982): preserves constitutional limits on institutional care.
- Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992, decision: distinguishes psychiatric confinement after its predicate ends; no universal custody test is inferred.

##### Request-based review is not facially inadequate

**Holding:** The described examination, notice and opportunity to obtain immigration-judge review on request do not facially require replacement with an automatic hearing for every detained juvenile. That result depends on the available procedures and the absence of a record establishing general inability to invoke them; it does not approve ineffective notice, denial of requested review, improper delay or unlawful custody in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela agrees with setting aside the universal automatic-hearing remedy but reserves a distinct Mathews-based implementation inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fare v. Michael C., 442 U.S. 707 (1979): applies the recognition that juveniles can make a knowing waiver; no universal finding of comprehension follows.
- The Japanese Immigrant Case, 189 U.S. 86 (1903): applies aliens' entitlement to due process in deportation proceedings.

##### The challenged classifications do not establish facial unequal treatment

**Holding:** The regulation's distinction between juveniles with available family custodians and those without them survives the facial equal-protection objection because family availability bears a rational relation to safe placement and attendance. The separate federal juvenile-delinquency release scheme does not require identical release rules for immigration custody, and this ruling does not resolve unequal administration in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component and adds no ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the legitimate relevance of protective care in juvenile custody; no general exemption from equal protection follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b)(1) requires release in order of preference to a nondetained parent, legal guardian, or adult brother, sister, aunt, uncle or grandparent unless detention is required to secure timely appearance before the Service or immigration court, or ensure the juvenile's safety or that of others. If the only listed custodian is detained, (b)(2) provides discretionary case-by-case consideration of simultaneous release. Under (b)(3), a parent or guardian detained or outside the United States may designate another capable and willing person by sworn affidavit before an immigration or consular officer; that person must agree to care for the child and ensure presence at all future proceedings. Subsection (b)(4) permits other agreeing adult custodians only in unusual and compelling circumstances, at the district director's or chief patrol agent's discretion. These are distinct release avenues, not one undifferentiated exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1987 conditions agreement remains enforceable and is not a finding that each facility complies. Custody review under §1252(a)(1) permits habeas upon a conclusive showing of failure to proceed with reasonable dispatch warranted by the particular facts. For a juvenile not seeking voluntary departure, the described 24-hour process uses a different examiner unless no qualified alternative is available; English/Spanish notice allows review on request and withdrawal of waiver. No universal ruling on voluntary-departure safeguards, 30-day custody cap or approval of defective notice, ineffective assistance, delayed review or unconstitutional individual/systemic implementation follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial juvenile-release and automatic-hearing injunctions, 7–2 in judgment, and remand consistently with the six-Justice rationale. No particular child’s release is ordered; conditions enforcement and properly supported implementation challenges remain.

#### Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-896.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applied to discern congressional review allocation from the statutory scheme.
- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): distinguished because that scheme preserved supplemental judicial review; the presumption favoring review does not require every forum.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): distinguished because its collateral systemic challenge lacked meaningful review through individual proceedings; that protection remains.
- Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Inc., December 3, 1991: its scheme-specific channel rule supports the analysis, without extending its express bar to the Mine Act by assumption.
- Darby v. Cisneros, June 21, 1993: preserved; limits on judicially added APA exhaustion do not displace Congress's allocation of original review.

##### The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908): distinguished on practical access; its protection against a penalty structure effectively foreclosing judicial review remains intact.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): its requirement of meaningful practical review is preserved and satisfied on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed; the district-court injunction remains unavailable. The representation/posting challenge must use the authorized Commission and appellate process. No labor-law merits determination, penalty assessment, guaranteed stay or new administrative outcome is ordered.

#### Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, 512 U.S. 504

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-120.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The redistribution provision reasonably limits newly shifted educational costs

**Holding:** Under the governing rule giving an administering agency's interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with that regulation, the Secretary reasonably construes 42 C.F.R. §413.85(c) to bar increased reimbursement resulting from transferring previously medical-college-borne educational costs to the hospital's patient-care account, even when the underlying clinical education is generally eligible. That construction sustains the challenged denial for the reporting year ending June 30, 1985; it does not establish a universal historic-dollar ceiling, make every newly measured expense ineligible, or decide the separate community-support ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. Five participating Justices directly adopt this regulatory construction, its application and its limits.

**Material application:** Section 413.85(a)(1) provides that allowable cost may include the net cost of approved educational activities under paragraph (g), apportioned for Medicare utilization under §413.50. Permission to include those costs is not an unconditional payment command. Paragraph (g) deducts tuition revenue and includes trainee stipends, teacher compensation and other direct and indirect educational-activity costs under Medicare cost-finding principles. The recodified paragraph (a)(2) directs payment for approved residency programs in medicine, osteopathy, dentistry and podiatry to §413.86 for cost reporting periods beginning on or after July 1, 1985. That later-period exception does not itself disallow the reporting year ending June 30, 1985, and the Court fixes no later-period payment or base-year amount.

Section 413.17(a), subject to the separate exception in paragraph (d), includes the cost of services, facilities and supplies furnished by organizations related through common ownership or control at the related organization's cost, capped at the price of comparable items that could be purchased elsewhere. It does not independently make an otherwise excluded educational cost allowable. No party's entitlement under paragraph (d) is decided, and its separate exception is not a premise of this holding. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)'s reasonable-cost system takes direct and indirect costs into account within authorized methods and included-item rules, excludes unnecessary costs in efficient delivery, and bars shifting Medicare costs to non-Medicare patients or the reverse. It does not guarantee payment of every asserted actual expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co., 325 U.S. 410 (1945): applies controlling weight to a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation; the plainly-erroneous-or-inconsistent limit remains operative.
- Udall v. Tallman, 380 U.S. 1 (1965): applies the same own-regulation standard; an alternative reasonable reading does not by itself displace the Secretary's permissible construction.
- Gardebring v. Jenkins, 485 U.S. 415 (1988): preserves the limit where an alternative reading is compelled by the regulation's plain language or other indications of the Secretary's intent at promulgation; no such compulsion is established here.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144 (1991): applies its account of reasonable own-regulation interpretation and administrative expertise; its allocation of interpretive authority under the Occupational Safety and Health Act remains specific to that statutory structure.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves reimbursement within lawful cost methods and limitations, including authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods; actual expenditure alone does not establish entitlement here.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., June 17, 1994: preserves the requirement to respect operative statutory limits; its mandatory-tariff holding does not invalidate this textually supported regulatory restriction or replace the own-regulation standard.
- Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: preserves the distinction between delegated authority and its actual exercise; its treatment of a general reasonable-doubt policy does not render every statement of regulatory intent nonoperative or create a Medicare proof burden.
- Ohio State University v. Secretary, Department of Health and Human Services, 996 F.2d 122 (6th Cir. 1993): rejects its contrary construction insofar as it excludes this transfer of otherwise eligible clinical-education costs from the redistribution provision; its separate community-support analysis is not resolved.

##### The actual agency ground survives the asserted inconsistency

**Holding:** The Administrator's expressed redistribution ground and the established history of college-borne costs support this denial; a memorandum's silence about redistribution and an intermediary's prior allowance do not, on this record, establish an authoritative contrary interpretation that defeats it. A considered, otherwise permissible interpretive change is not automatically invalid, but contrary text, genuine inconsistency and the required agency explanation must be examined, and a reviewing court may not substitute a new ground or missing finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this answer to the consistency and actual-ground objections; it supports application of the redistribution holding rather than furnishing an alternative reimbursement bar.

**Material application:** Letter 78-7 requires that faculty-service costs would be allowable if incurred directly, be allocated consistently among recipient components, comply with related-organization and comparable-price limits, and satisfy the applicable patient-care relation, documentation, reasonable-allocation and reasonable-amount requirements. Its identified additional nonfaculty costs are limited to direct and space costs. It does not permit duplicative additions where the hospital maintains its own corresponding library, administrative structure, office or clerical support. Those conditions survive; the Court does not read silence about redistribution as an unconditional promise of all overhead. The record does not explain the failure to claim reimbursement before 1974, and the Court does not turn that silence into a finding of community support or an estoppel rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court affirms on redistribution alone. It neither endorses nor rejects the separate construction treating all non-Medicare funding as community support; decides no merits of the Administrator's additional university general-and-administrative-cost ruling under §§413.9, 413.85 and Letter 78-7; and orders no reimbursement, penalty, recoupment, reopening of the settled faculty-compensation amounts, or relief on the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim. The original $2,861,247 disputed difference is not adjudicated as an amount remaining after settlement. Materially different retrospective applications, later §413.86 calculations and entitlement under §413.17(d) remain unresolved. No independently sufficient alternative holding is adopted, and no fractured-rationale synthesis is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: applies its rule that considered interpretive change is not automatically disqualifying while preserving scrutiny of text, delegation and reasons; unsupported litigation positions receive no automatic controlling weight.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: applies the requirement of a discernible actual agency rationale; the express redistribution ground satisfies it without a new judicial explanation.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943): preserves review on the agency's own ground rather than a substitute invented on review.
- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): preserves required reasoned agency decisionmaking and the prohibition on appellate rationalizations supplying missing findings.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): preserves the rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; this decision rests on the Administrator's stated rationale and decides no new retroactive-rulemaking question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The challenged reimbursement denial is affirmed, 5–4, on redistribution alone. No additional reimbursement or remand is ordered. The separate community-support construction and general-and-administrative-cost ruling are neither independently sustained nor rejected; settled faculty amounts and the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim remain untouched.

#### Shalala v. Guernsey Memorial Hospital

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1251.
**Decided:** March 6, 1995.

##### The accounting regulations do not compel immediate recognition of this loss

**Holding:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A) and 42 C.F.R. §§413.20 and 413.24 do not make every generally accepted accounting recognition rule conclusive for Medicare reimbursement or require immediate recognition of this advance-refunding accounting loss. Provider records remain the accounting base, while lawful reasonable-cost allocation may distribute the relevant expense elements across the periods benefited, subject to the distinct existing allowance and calculation conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 — lawful cost methods and allocation remain distinct from a demand for individualized correction of every claimed shortfall.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala (June 24, 1994) — its different education-cost regulation does not resolve these accounting terms; no general change to its existing review rule is adopted.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. (June 17, 1994) — agency action must remain within actual legal authority; no power to contradict a binding regulation is recognized.

##### The manual explains a lawful allocation rather than changing binding law

**Holding:** PRM §233’s application to the advance-refunding timing at issue is interpretive because it explains a permissible application of the existing reasonable-cost regulations rather than imposing a conflicting new legal obligation; APA §553(b)(A) therefore does not require notice and comment for that explanation. The Secretary’s actual adjudicative allocation ground is sustained within existing law, not as authority to evade a binding regulatory command.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lincoln v. Vigil, 508 U.S. 182 — interpretive or policy material is distinguished from a new binding substantive rule; form does not authorize evasion of statutory procedure.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 332 U.S. 194 — lawful adjudication and the agency’s actual stated ground matter; a court cannot supply a different administrative decision.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala — reasoned implementation remains subject to governing methods and legal limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A) defines reasonable cost by actual incurred cost, excluding unnecessary cost in efficient delivery of needed services, under regulations specifying methods and included items. The Secretary must consider accepted accounting principles, not necessarily adopt them. Methods may differ with circumstances and employ estimates, limits, per-unit measures, or charges where reasonable. Direct and indirect costs must be allocated so Medicare does not bear non-Medicare costs and the converse; suitable corrective adjustments operate within lawful methods. Section 413.20(a) requires sufficient standardized financial records and statistical data, says accepted accounting and reporting practices are followed, says changes to those systems will not be required to determine payable costs, and uses basic accounts as the starting point for equitable and proper payment. Section 413.24 requires adequate verifiable data, approved cost finding, and accrual accounting: revenue when earned and expense when incurred, regardless of collection or payment. Accrual accounting alone does not compel GAAP reimbursement timing. Sections 413.9 and 413.153 retain reasonable patient-care cost and necessary-and-proper-interest limits.

PRM §233.1 covers advance refunding through new debt initiated on or after July 1, 1983; recall without new debt is excluded and referred to §215. Initiation is provider or official issuer action clearly intending refunding; board authorization, hiring an underwriter, or applying to the issuer are examples, not cumulative requirements. Under §233.2, legal defeasance need not mean the old holders have received principal. For necessary patient-care debt defeased or repurchased through advance refunding, §233.3 treats expense elements separately: new debt issue costs amortize from incurrence to scheduled maturity of the new debt; old unamortized discounts, premiums, and issue costs amortize from new issuance until old holders receive principal, with proportionate treatment for serial bonds; call premiums or penalties are allowed when old holders receive principal, prorated by principal payments for serial bonds. Redemption and miscellaneous expenses and annual authority/trustee fees are allowed as paid or accrued subject to the separate §2305 qualification. Interest on both debts is allowed annually as paid or accrued; new discount/premium amortization adjusts interest, and income from investment of new proceeds offsets interest whether earned directly or through a trust. Individual expense elements, rather than the accounting loss as an undifferentiated sum, determine reimbursement.

Section 233.4 retains necessary-and-proper and reasonable-cost requirements. Available sinking funds count in necessary borrowing, and existing funded depreciation counts when excess borrowing purchases depreciable assets. Ordinarily net allowable refinancing costs cannot exceed costs allowable without refinancing. Compelling factors, such as necessary cash flow or removal of a restrictive borrowing covenant, may justify excess costs upon a showing satisfactory to the intermediary; absent that showing, the annual ceiling is the cost without refinancing. Section 233.5 permits qualifying debt and proceeds in equity-capital calculations but excludes debt and related assets to the extent interest is disallowed under §233.4. No equity-capital award is decided. Exact §2305 allowance conditions are not established, and no expense-specific entitlement dependent on them is adjudicated. Medicare allocation, ordinary calculation limits, and these distinct periods remain in place; no universal amortization period or new amount is fixed.

The Manual lacks regulatory force. APA §553(b)(A)’s interpretive-rule exemption is distinct from the benefits exemption, on which the decision does not rely. The later §1395hh rulemaking provision does not govern this preexisting manual dispute. No agency may use interpretive form or adjudication to contradict binding statutes or regulations.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit's advance-refunding timing judgment is reversed and remanded consistently with sustaining the Secretary's determination, subject to existing allowance and calculation rules. The separate interest-offset affirmance is undisturbed. No dollar award or expense-specific entitlement is fixed.

#### Regions Hospital v. Shalala

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1375.

**Decided:** 1998-02-24.

##### Authority to determine the lawful base

**Holding:** Section 1395ww(h)(2)(A) permits the Secretary to correct the allowable fiscal-1984 graduate-medical-education cost figure used in the later statutory per-resident reimbursement formula. This authority to determine a lawful historical input does not itself reopen the final payment for the base year or authorize departure from the cost rules governing that year.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I-A: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part I-B’s construction and permissible-deference rationale independently controls with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Authority to correct the historical input is distinct from reopening the annual payment. The Secretary must apply the cost rules governing the base year, not a newly invented allowability standard.

**Material application:** The reasonable-cost context and the new enduring reimbursement formula mean that the statutory recognition language does not unambiguously freeze every expense previously reimbursed. The five-Justice construction permits excluding costs unallowable under existing principles while preserving the closed annual payment.

##### Antecedent facts, later payments and finality

**Holding:** Applying the existing base-year cost principles to antecedent data for future reimbursement does not impose a new legal consequence on the closed 1984 payment and is not impermissibly retroactive. A later already-paid year may be corrected only if its own payment determination remains independently open under governing reopening rules; closed determinations cannot be revised or recouped under this holding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part II: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, on the specific later-payment analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance permits a corrected base for future calculations and later determinations independently open under their own reopening rules. It permits no recoupment or revision of a closed payment, including the 1984 award.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This later-payment application recognizes neither general retroactive-rulemaking authority nor general power to revise final agency decisions.

##### Reasonableness and the asserted reliance burden

**Holding:** The challenged reaudit rule is reasonable on the supported administrative record because it corrects an enduring formula input under existing cost law and provides the stated two-direction corrections and documentation accommodations. This facial conclusion does not validate every particular expense disallowance, allocation or future evidentiary accommodation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** The rule permits requested inclusion of GME costs misclassified as operating expenses and an upward hospital-specific operating adjustment for costs improperly classified as GME. When original physician-time records are unavailable, later reporting-period evidence may document allocation; Regions used 1989 and 1990 studies.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The facial reasonableness determination approves no particular expense disallowance, indefinite serial reaudit, new base-year standard or defective procedure. Reliance and evidentiary accommodation in a materially different application remain open.

##### The intermediary notice does not establish adjudicated issue preclusion here

**Holding:** On this record, the intermediary's final annual reimbursement notice and payment-finality regulations do not conclusively adjudicate every underlying cost fact for the distinct statutory base determination. No Provider Reimbursement Review Board adjudicatory hearing resolved the reasonableness of these costs, so the asserted issue-preclusion objection does not bar this use.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part IV: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No adjudicatory Board hearing resolved the cost reasonableness issue on this record. The Court does not hold administrative adjudications universally nonpreclusive or permit reconsideration of completed Article III judgments.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown requires statutory authority before deference; the agency may not add a condition contrary to the governing text.
- Landgraf is applied to the particular consequence for later payments; its rule against unauthorized new retrospective burdens remains intact.
- Bowen preserves the limit on retroactive legislative rules and rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; neither authority is expanded.
- Good Samaritan preserves correction within authorized methods and legal boundaries, not an entitlement to every claimed historical expense.
- Thomas Jefferson supplies the distinct limits on interpreting an agency's own regulation and on reliance on its actual ground; that standard does not replace statutory construction here.
- Guernsey preserves lawful accounting allocation without permission to contradict a binding regulation.
- Chenery preserves review on the agency's actual rationale.
- Allentown requires use of the actual announced standard and the whole record; no substituted base-year cost standard is approved.
