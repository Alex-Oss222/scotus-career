A corporation, of course, cannot act for itself. It may 
only act through its agents. Agents are the officers, directors, 
employees, or other persons who may be authorized to act for 
the corporation. A corporation is legally responsible for those 
acts or omissions of its agents made or performed within the 
scope of their employment. 


To find a corporate defendant guilty, you must find 
beyond a reasonable doubt that all the essential elements of the 
offense, as set forth in these instructions, are present as to the

To be acting within his employment, the agent first must 
have intended that his act would produce some benefit to the 
corporation. Second, the agent must have been acting within 
his authority. An act was within the agent’s authority if it was 
directly related to the performance of the type of duties that the 
employee had the general authority to perform or if outsiders 
could reasonably assume that the agent had the authority to 
perform the act, judging from his position with the company, 
the responsibility previously entrusted to him, and the 
circumstances surrounding his past conduct. 

If the agent was acting within the scope of his 
employment, the fact that the agent’s act was illegal, contrary 
to his employer’s instructions, or against the corporation’s 
policies will not relieve the corporation of responsibility for it.

approves or engages in conduct that is consistent with approving 
the act. 


A corporation is legally responsible for any act or
