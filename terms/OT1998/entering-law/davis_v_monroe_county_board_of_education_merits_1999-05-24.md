## Event

Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education, No. 97-843. Argued January 12, 1999; decided May 24, 1999. On certiorari to the en banc Eleventh Circuit, 120 F.3d 1390, which affirmed dismissal of the district's Title IX damages claim. The question is whether the funding recipient may be responsible for its own deliberate response to serious student-on-student sexual harassment within its substantial control.

A fifth-grade student allegedly endured repeated touching and sexual propositions by a classmate over several months. The child and her mother reportedly informed teachers and the principal; officials allegedly failed to protect her while the conduct continued and interfered with education. The harasser's later guilty plea is a procedural recital, not proof of every civil element or earlier institutional notice. The case concerns pleading sufficiency, not findings of recipient fault or damages.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed insofar as it affirms dismissal of the appealed Title IX damages claim against the district, and the case is remanded for ordinary further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The recipient claim may proceed under the stated conditions. Knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based educational deprivation, causation, compensable injury and any supported defense remain to be proved or decided. No compulsory trial, damages award, particular punishment, individual-capacity recovery or reopening of unrelated claims is ordered.

## Holdings

### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins establish this sufficient route.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination but leaves recipient responsibility to be established. Kinman's actual-notice route identifies a recipient's own deliberate disregard of a known educational danger. The Court extends that sufficient route to peer conduct where the school has substantial remedial control; it does not claim Kinman already decided peers. The school's knowing, culpable continuation of educational deprivation supplies the institutional wrong, answering the objection that a student cannot personally bind the funding recipient. Mere discipline authority or an ineffective result does not suffice. Actual notice must reach an official empowered to correct the danger; generic constructive notice, the harasser's own knowledge and technical policy noncompliance do not establish this route. A reasonable response is lawful even if harassment continues, and courts prescribe neither a selected punishment nor a victim's preferred remedy. Constitutional and statutory limits on discipline inform control and response. Gebser's school-delegated teacher authority does not transfer to classmates; its conjunctive teacher route and the other teacher holdings remain intact. The Court neither adopts ordinary negligence or general agency law nor rejects every distinct theory of direct official discrimination. Receipt of assistance remains separately necessary under NCAA v. Smith. Causal injury cannot be inferred solely from harassment predating legally sufficient recipient notice.

### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, directly controls with five votes. Stone-Zsela does not join this disjunctive threshold; no combination of separate rationales is needed for its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Kinman adopted severe-or-pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced educational harassment without mandatory psychiatric injury, measurable grade loss or a rigid list of factors. Its reliance on Meritor and Harris preserves attention to welcome, objective and experienced abuse, and the actual setting. The peer relationship affects the contextual application and recipient-control inquiry; it does not silently replace the substantive standard with a requirement that conduct be both severe and pervasive. Age, relationship, educational setting and actual effect may matter without becoming a mandatory checklist. Neither physical exclusion nor total loss of schooling is invariably required, and no one proxy is sufficient by itself. Ordinary teasing or offense fails without the substantive and institutional requirements. No universal rule determines whether one incident always suffices or never can. These allegations involve repeated touching and propositions over months with educational interference, a materially different claimed environment. The Court decides no ultimate fact and does not treat every unwelcome interaction as actionable discrimination.

### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support this allegation-stage application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged response concerns the district's own use of authority after reports of a known danger, rather than automatic responsibility for the classmate. The asserted repeated abuse and educational disruption permit the necessary elements to be tested. Allegations that reports reached the principal do not finally establish every official's corrective power, knowledge or opportunity; the criminal plea supplies no earlier notice or complete causal showing. The plaintiff must prove the institutional and substantive conditions, including postnotice causal injury. Existing compensation principles therefore require ordinary further adjudication, not immediate damages or judicial control of discipline. No compulsory trial follows if ordinary procedure establishes a missing element or valid defense. The decision reaches only the appealed district Title IX claim and leaves unrelated and individual claims outside the mandate.

## Precedent Treatment

- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools is applied to available compensation for proved intentional discrimination, with recipient responsibility, causation and loss still distinct.
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District is extended at its sufficient actual-notice/culpable-disregard route to substantially controlled peer conduct. Its severe-or-pervasive contextual educational-harm standard remains; the peer extension is stated expressly.
- Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District retains the limited teacher-authority-plus-recipient-fault conjunction and its rejection of exclusivity in teacher cases. Neither delegated teacher power nor that complete route is imputed to peers.
- NCAA v. Smith preserves the distinct federal-assistance recipient requirement; economic connection or control alone does not establish receipt.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson informs the distinction between participation and welcome through Kinman without importing employment attribution wholesale.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems informs the contextual objective-and-experienced abuse inquiry through Kinman, without mandatory psychiatric injury or any indispensable factor.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman retains its funding-notice principle; the controlling recipient-own-wrong route concerns intentional educational discrimination, not automatic liability for a third party.

## Law After Decision

A funding recipient's own actually informed, deliberately unreasonable response to peer harassment within substantial control can support damages when all independent discrimination, educational-harm and causal-injury elements are established. The controlling environment threshold remains severe or pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced as abusive, with impaired educational access or benefit. Teacher-attribution rules remain separately bounded. Ordinary negligence, policy breach, unsuccessful reasonable correction and peer attendance alone do not establish liability. The Chief's separate reporting-failure extension does not become a controlling peer rule, and no complete alternative-attribution or defense code is decided.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment:** The Chief joins the actual-notice recipient-fault route and the reversal on these allegations. The school is answerable for its own deliberately indifferent failure to use available authority after reliable notice, with meaningful control, clearly inadequate response, sex-based educational deprivation and causal injury independently proved. Kinman's sufficient notice route remains available, while Gebser's teacher authority cannot simply be attributed to a classmate. He would require severe, pervasive and objectively offensive peer harassment depriving the victim of equal educational access, and therefore withholds assent from the Court's severe-or-pervasive threshold. He additionally would recognize the recipient's willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causing continuation as a sufficient institutional ground. That proposed reporting-failure extension is his separate position, not the Court's peer rule. Neither misconduct nor negligent supervision alone suffices; officials retain disciplinary discretion so long as the response is not deliberately indifferent. He requires no particular punishment and makes no liability finding.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting:** They would affirm the appealed peer-harassment dismissal because Title IX's funding condition does not sufficiently establish this institutional attribution. Franklin makes compensation available after recipient discrimination is shown; it does not identify the recipient's wrong. Kinman concerned a teacher, and Gebser's additional route still requires materially enabling delegated teacher authority. A nonagent pupil does not have that link. These Justices address the Court's own-response theory directly: disciplinary power and knowing nonresponse to a student's discrimination do not, on their view, supply the necessary statutory duty or clear funding notice. Legal limits on school control and the breadth of the proposed obligation reinforce the objection. They neither find the allegations false nor approve the conduct. A supported claim that school officials themselves enforce rules differently because of sex remains distinct; they decide no such new claim. Existing teacher law and remedies for established recipient discrimination remain intact.

## Procedure After Action

The appealed Title IX dismissal is reversed and the case returns for ordinary proceedings under the controlling recipient and educational-harm rules. The lower courts determine the actual facts, elements, supported defenses and any compensable injury. No damages, compulsory trial, disciplinary measure, reopening of unrelated claims or additional Supreme Court date is directed.
