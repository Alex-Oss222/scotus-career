# El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng — 1999-01-12

## Event
**El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng, No. 97-475.** Argued November 10, 1998; decided January 12, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 122 F.3d 99. After a bench trial, the District Court dismissed Tseng's personal-injury claim; the Court of Appeals allowed local tort proceedings because it regarded her preboarding security search as no accident under Article 17. The question is whether local law permits personal-injury recovery within the Warsaw Convention's covered carriage when a treaty liability condition is unsatisfied. The separate $1,034.90 baggage award is outside review.

## Participation
All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action
The Second Circuit's judgment on the personal-injury branch is reversed, and the case is remanded for a conforming judgment restoring dismissal of that claim.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justice Stevens.

The New York personal-injury claim must be dismissed. The separate baggage award remains undisturbed; the Court orders no further tort trial or baggage relief.

## Holdings
#### Part I — Local law cannot avoid the Convention's conditions within its covered field

**Operative rule.** When a passenger's personal-injury occurrence falls within Article 17's temporal and substantive field, Article 24 subjects an action against the carrier, whatever its legal foundation, to the Convention's conditions and limits. Failure to satisfy the accident or bodily-injury condition does not itself remove the occurrence from that field or permit an independent local damages action; occurrences outside that field remain outside this holding.

**Authority.** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court has the eight direct joins identified in Opinion Topology and controls this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation.** Article 24 makes the Convention's conditions apply to actions founded on local law as well as the treaty. Reading a failed condition as permission to escape that condition would deprive it of its limiting force. The reference to Article 17 confines the rule to the personal-injury field of covered international carriage; it does not make liability under the Convention a prerequisite to the operation of its restrictions.

Saks distinguishes the injury from the external event causing it. Floyd enforces Article 17's bodily-injury condition while leaving this exclusivity question open. Zicherman permits domestic law to identify compensable losses and beneficiaries subject to treaty conditions; it does not permit domestic law to create liability when those conditions fail. The absence of a uniform treaty damages code therefore does not establish a separate local source of recovery. Uniformity supports this reading but does not enlarge the treaty's field.

Tseng's search occurred in the operations of embarking for her international flight, not merely somewhere in an airport. The accepted absence of qualifying bodily injury defeats the reviewed personal-injury claim. The Court need not decide whether the search was an accident. It does not categorically exclude every physical manifestation of emotional harm, decide events outside embarking, carriage or disembarking, or alter the baggage award. Part I leaves Article 25's operation open.

#### Part II — The Article 25 argument does not change the result on this record

**Operative rule.** The asserted Article 25 route cannot alter the disposition where the trial's finding of no treaty willful misconduct remains undisturbed. Intentional touching alone does not establish that misconduct; this application decides neither contested preservation nor Article 25's full effect when its misconduct predicate is proved.

**Authority.** Part II has the seven direct Associate joins identified in Opinion Topology. Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation.** Assuming that Tseng may present her Article 25 argument despite the disputed preservation objection, its factual predicate is absent. The trial court found no treaty willful misconduct, and this record supplies no basis for disturbing that finding. The deliberate character of a search does not itself prove the greater culpability required by the asserted treaty provision. The Court therefore need not determine how Article 25 affects exclusions or limitations when willfulness is established, and does not restrict that provision to monetary ceilings. It also does not infer that intentional conduct can never be an Article 17 accident.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S. 392:** Applied to distinguish the external event from injury; its flexible accident rule is unchanged and the classification of this search is not decided.
- **Eastern Airlines, Inc. v. Floyd, 499 U.S. 530:** Applied for Article 17's bodily-injury requirement. Its reserved local-law question is now answered within the treaty's covered personal-injury field.
- **Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.:** Preserved as allowing domestic loss categories and beneficiaries subject to treaty conditions; its reserved general-exclusivity issue is resolved in the stated field. It supplies no domestic liability despite a failed treaty condition.

## Law After Decision
Within Article 17's actual personal-injury field, a claimant cannot obtain local damages merely because the Convention's accident or bodily-injury condition is unsatisfied. Domestic law continues to supply losses and beneficiaries where permitted by Zicherman, subject to treaty liability conditions. Outside-field occurrences, baggage questions outside this review, and Article 25's full operation on proved willfulness remain unresolved here.

## Separate Writings
**Justice Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens reads Article 24's reference to Article 17 as covering injuries arising from an accident. He agrees that an accident causing only psychic injury remains governed by the Convention and cannot support a separate local recovery. He disagrees that the same displacement follows for a nonaccident: the text does not clearly extinguish local remedies for that residual category. Saks separates the event from the injury, Floyd leaves independent local claims open, and Zicherman's limited domestic supplementation does not settle the distinction. Uniformity cannot extend the treaty beyond the class its language identifies. Taking the Second Circuit's nonaccident determination as the premise of this review, without independently approving it, he would leave local proceedings available. He would award no treaty recovery and decides no battery, false imprisonment, loss or local defense.

## Procedure After Action
The case returns for a conforming judgment dismissing the personal-injury claim. The baggage judgment remains in place. No accident-classification inquiry or new willfulness hearing is ordered.
