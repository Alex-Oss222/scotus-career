#### Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-405.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### A private settlement does not supply the required collateral-order importance

**Holding:** An order refusing effect to an ordinary private settlement does not qualify for immediate appeal under §1291 merely because the agreement promises freedom from trial. Assuming conclusiveness and separateness, the category lacks the public importance required to excuse final-judgment review; irretrievable litigation burdens and the particular agreement's value, clarity or alleged violation do not enlarge appellate jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for affirmance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541: applies its narrow category of collateral decisions rather than a general hardship exception.
- Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay, 437 U.S. 463: applies the cumulative conclusiveness, separateness, importance and effective-unreviewability requirements.
- Lauro Lines s.r.l. v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495: applies category-based finality to a contractual claim despite practical burdens of litigation; its importance rationale distinguishes private bargains from recognized public immunities.
- Abney v. United States, 431 U.S. 651: distinguishes the constitutional interest against a second criminal trial, leaving its immediate-review rule intact.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: distinguishes the public-law immunity from suit without expanding its substantive coverage.
- Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, January 12, 1993: preserves immediate review of qualifying Eleventh Amendment immunity denials; a private settlement is a different category.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even apart from the dispute over importance terminology, ordinary settlement rights of this category remain adequately vindicable after final judgment and therefore fail the collateral-order effective-unreviewability requirement. Review of protection against ultimate liability, together with lawful contract enforcement and district-court protections when their own requirements are met, is adequate in this setting despite the irretrievable experience and expense of litigation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither alternative ground resolves the validity of settlement rescission, actual fraud, breach damages, sanctions, the district court's power to reopen, or trademark liability. Section 1292(b) remains separate: it requires written district certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, possible material advancement of termination, timely application and appellate permission. No satisfaction of those conditions is found and no such appeal is certified here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lauro Lines s.r.l. v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495: applies adequate postjudgment vindication despite loss of an asserted contractual litigation advantage.
- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541: applies effective unreviewability as a distinct limitation on immediate review.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, May 16, 1994: preserves the independent jurisdictional requirements for contract enforcement and its reservation of Rule 60(b) reopening; no breach remedy is found available on these facts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the interlocutory appeal is affirmed. No settlement-rescission merits ruling, contract remedy, trademark judgment or §1292(b) certification or permission is entered.

#### Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1636.
**Decided:** March 1, 1995.

##### Ordinary municipal liability remains reviewable after final judgment

**Holding:** The denial of this municipal defendant's ordinary liability defense does not qualify for immediate collateral-order review under §1291 because the defense remains effectively reviewable after final judgment. The officers' distinct immunity from suit does not give the municipality a corresponding right to avoid trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. retains its cumulative collateral-order requirements. Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. is applied through its independently controlling adequate-final-review ground. Mitchell v. Forsyth is distinguished because the officials' immunity from suit is not the county's liability defense. Monell v. Department of Social Services supplies no municipal immunity from all trial.

**Independent alternative holding:** An order that expressly leaves the county's final-policymaker question open for further presentation and a later legal ruling does not conclusively determine that question under Cohen. This failure independently defeats collateral-order review; the mere denial of the pending motions does not resolve a question the court expressly reserves.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Part III, eight Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen is applied to the express reservation of the disputed question. Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay is applied to the requirement of a conclusive, rather than tentative, determination. Neither decision is enlarged into a rule admitting every later definitive ruling to immediate appeal.

##### A proper immunity appeal does not authorize this separate county appeal

**Holding:** A court of appeals may not decide a separate party's nonappealable liability defense merely because a related officers' immunity appeal is properly before it and resolving the defense would save litigation. The county attribution issue here is neither inextricably intertwined with nor necessary to meaningful review of the officers' qualified-immunity questions, and no other authorized appellate route is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part II, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abney v. United States and United States v. Stanley are applied to limits on adding otherwise unauthorized issues to a proper immediate appeal. Cohen and Digital Equipment remain the source of the separate collateral-order inquiry. Earlier examples of connected review are not overruled or converted into a general pendent-party jurisdiction doctrine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Part IV of Ginsburg’s opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices, expressly leaves undecided whether genuinely inextricable or necessary connected review is available at all and what its scope would be. Stone-Zsela’s affirmative view is separate and noncontrolling. Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written statement that an order involves a controlling question of law, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that an immediate appeal may materially advance the litigation's ultimate termination. Application must be made within ten days; the court of appeals may then permit the appeal in its discretion. Application alone does not stay District Court proceedings unless the District Judge, the court of appeals, or a judge thereof orders a stay. No qualifying certification or permission is established here. Section 1292(e) permits additional interlocutory-appeal rules prescribed under §2072; §2072(c) permits rules defining finality under §1291. Those are rulemaking authorizations, subject to §2072's prohibition on abridging, enlarging, or modifying substantive rights, and are not an operative rule allowing this appeal. No mandamus relief is sought or granted here; that distinct route retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Commission's appellate disposition under §§1983 and 1985(3) is vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss its interlocutory appeal. Its surviving federal claims return to their preappeal District Court posture without a liability or summary-judgment ruling. Officers' immunity dispositions and separately declined City and individual state-law appeals are undisturbed; no county state-law count remained.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Clinton v. Jones, May 27, 1997, permits connected review of the categorical trial protection and its alternative docket-control basis within the same integrated temporal-immunity order. Independent liability questions and ordinary later scheduling orders remain outside that holding.

#### Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1244.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen's cumulative conditions and Mitchell's legal-immunity category apply. Digital Equipment's importance and category discipline remains; Johnson supplies no numerical ceiling and retains its factual boundary.

##### An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, unanimous. Stevens and Breyer condition agreement on an otherwise authorized appeal and do not find successive authorization here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson and Kimberlin preserve assumed-fact review without factual selection. Elder requires relevant conduct-date law but creates no jurisdiction or retrospective notice. Swint rejects convenience-based expansion; Doe's factual-sufficiency exclusion remains.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No immunity, constitutional violation, property interest, factual truth, evidentiary sufficiency or damages issue is decided.

##### A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join; Stevens and Breyer continue to oppose successive appeal as of right.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell protects against defending the claim to which immunity applies; Swint prevents a proper appeal from carrying unrelated matters and leaves its necessary-connected-review reservation open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the categorical Ninth Circuit dismissal and remand to identify proper assumptions favorable to the nonmovant and any actually reviewable legal issue, dismissing factual-sufficiency and otherwise unavailable issues. The summary-judgment denial is not reversed, immunity is not awarded, and neither the separate United States negligence claim nor earlier substitution ruling is reopened. No stay or permission is granted.

#### Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-386.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Whether the dispositive nonparty bar is final for limited federal review

**Holding:** Section 1257(a) permits review under Cox Broadcasting's first finality category because the preclusion ruling conclusively bars Richards and Hill's remaining federal claims and leaves its implementation preordained, despite the state interlocutory label. Review concerns only nonparty due process; separate Buck Act proceedings and the removed tax-merits question do not enlarge it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court. All nine participating Justices join Parts I–IV and every following holding and qualification; there is no separate writing.

**Material application:** State Counts 3 and 4 had already been rejected. The ruling disposes of these petitioners' remaining federal claims, while the remaining Buck Act claim belongs to a separate federal-employee group. The actual orders leave implementation preordained; state certification alone establishes neither federal finality nor a presumed later judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reversal permits litigation to continue, so the fourth-category arbitration route used in Southland and Casarotto does not supply this jurisdiction. Quackenbush's §1291 routes concern federal appellate review, not this state judgment under §1257. Neither tax constitutionality, Buck Act liability nor entitlement to a particular tax remedy is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469, supplies its first category for practically concluded claims and preordained implementation. Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1, is distinguished as a fourth-category arbitration case. Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto, May 20, 1996, preserves attention to actual proceedings rather than certification alone, but its arbitration finality and substantive FAA holdings do not decide this case. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., June 3, 1996, retains its distinct §1291 and abstention holdings and the separate tax-comity setting; it supplies no reopening of the earlier federal tax dismissal.

##### Whether shared interests alone authorize binding absent claimants

**Holding:** A State's ordinary preclusion rules remain subject to due process: essentially identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources and public importance alone do not authorize adjudication of absent persons' own legal rights. Binding may rest on constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process; finality policy and a virtual-representation label cannot replace that prerequisite.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Guardian–ward and trustee–beneficiary relationships illustrate genuine privity; bankruptcy and probate illustrate special arrangements that may lawfully foreclose successive litigation. Each person need not appear individually, but common objectives cannot alone confer representative authority. Section 1738 does not separately command Alabama to bind these strangers to its own earlier judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes without deciding that adequate representation might cure absent notice in some class or representative proceedings. Even on that assumption, this representative basis fails. The assumption neither makes representation an automatic cure nor establishes a universal individual-notice requirement. Conversely, notice, objections or opt-out opportunities do not independently cure inadequate representation, and mere notice of another person's suit supplies neither representative authority nor a general intervention duty.

Ordinary state preclusion and valid finality remain. Correction of this extreme application supplies no general retrial of ordinary state-law errors and decides none of Matsushita's full collateral-review reservation. Absence of a Rule 23 class is relevant here but does not itself invalidate every source-authorized representative judgment; Bedingfield remains effective among persons properly bound.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry v. Lee, 311 U.S. 32, supplies the constitutional requirement of genuine adequate representation while preserving lawful representative adjudication. Martin v. Wilks, 490 U.S. 755, supplies nonparty hearing protection with adequate-representation, control and due-process-consistent special-remedial exceptions; notice alone creates no general intervention duty. Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996, distinguishes ordinary recognition from constitutional authority to bind, including representation independent of notice and opt-out protections; its complete collateral-review reservation remains unresolved.

##### Whether the stated public-interest rationale binds these direct personal tax claims

**Holding:** The representative rationale used below does not constitutionally bind Richards and Hill to Bedingfield's disposition of their own tax liability and claims for paid sums. Shared taxpayer objectives and fiscal reliance do not establish the required relationship; genuine representative public-right litigation and ordinary reliance on precedent remain distinct and preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Richards and Hill were nonparties to the nonclass Bedingfield action. The earlier litigants' common constitutional objective, resources and vigorous advocacy, together with revenue and bond-financing reliance, do not establish authority over these absent taxpayers' personal claims. No deficient advocacy finding is necessary. Petitioners' no-notice assertion is not an appellate finding; no participation or control finding supplies the rationale used below, and every possible connection need not be resolved or found absent to reject it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A direct levy on a taxpayer's own funds differs from a challenge to misuse of public funds or government action affecting the taxpayer only indirectly. The Court assumes wide state latitude over standing and repeated proceedings in the latter setting. Genuine public-right representative judgments remain possible; neither refund relief nor lack of formal class treatment independently invalidates every judgment, and federal class-action requirements are not wholesale constitutional commands to the States.

The finance director's participation does not establish that public officials always adequately represent constituents' personal rights; that broader question remains open. Supposed representation of city taxpayers cannot without more establish representation of these county taxpayers. The stated rationale establishes no exclusive public-action remedial scheme, and common interests and revenue reliance cannot create one by assertion. No cause of action or tax-merits holding follows. A later court may apply precedent without conclusively binding a claimant to another person's judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brinkerhoff-Faris Trust & Savings Co. v. Hill, 281 U.S. 673, supports an opportunity to contest a direct levy without deciding this tax's validity. Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422, supplies the protected status of an otherwise available individual claim, or chose in action, reinforcing the same hearing right; it creates no new cause of action or universal state-remedy scheme. Bedingfield retains its proper force among persons lawfully bound and as precedent; only its constitutionally unsupported preclusive use against these petitioners is rejected.

##### Whether narrower issue preclusion sustains the judgment and what reversal requires

**Holding:** Respondents' narrower issue-preclusion theory cannot sustain this judgment because it also requires a constitutionally sufficient basis to bind these nonparties. The dispositive bar on the reviewed Richards–Hill federal claims is reversed and the case remanded, without deciding whether the exemption issue was actually and necessarily determined or awarding relief on the tax.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Claim and issue preclusion have distinct requirements, but both require lawful authority to bind the affected person. Narrowing the proposed bar to an exemption issue supplies no missing representative relationship. Earlier pleadings of federal theories, an opportunity to litigate them, and their actual necessary determination are distinct predicates. The binding defect makes resolution of that disputed determination unnecessary; the Court does not find that no relevant issue was ever decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Uncertainty about every notice, authorization or control fact requires no exploratory remand or adverse factual finding. A genuinely available defense or constitutionally valid binding relationship remains subject to its own proof, preservation and procedural requirements. No new theory or finding that one exists is guaranteed. No state-count ruling, earlier Tax Injunction Act dismissal, properly binding judgment or separate Buck Act claim is reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0, as to the dispositive nonparty bar on Richards and Hill's reviewed federal claims. Ordinary legally available defenses, valid preclusion and remedy conditions remain; there is no mandatory discovery, representative-adequacy hearing or opportunity to construct a new preclusion theory. No immediate trial, tax invalidation, refund, damages, injunction or eventual recovery is ordered or guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** No Supreme Court precedent is overruled; the constitutional prerequisite to binding these nonparties defeats both proposed forms of preclusion without extending collateral review or reopening otherwise binding judgments.

#### Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1594.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading solicitation concerns lawful CPA services, the State must demonstrate that the harms supporting its restriction are real and that the restriction alleviates them to a material degree. Florida’s unsupported predictions about direct solicitation of business clients do not satisfy that burden; the ruling neither requires empirical studies in every case nor immunizes deceptive or coercive solicitation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No strict scrutiny, universal empirical-studies requirement, or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted. Fraud, coercion and materially different professional settings remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the judgment and injunction against the challenged CPA business-solicitation prohibition; no damages or new licensing rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the real-advancement burden.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: retains reasonable fit without least restrictive means.
- Ohralik v. Ohio State Bar Assn., 436 U.S. 447: distinguished by professional setting, vulnerability and pressure.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: alternative time/place/manner characterization cannot cure deficient advancement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: consistent application; no expansion to strict scrutiny.

#### United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co., 509 U.S. 418 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-486.
**Decided:** June 25, 1993.

##### Meaningful reduction and reasonable fit in a border broadcaster’s application

**Holding:** Assuming truthful advertising of lawful activity, the licensing-State lottery restriction satisfies Central Hudson as applied to Edge because it meaningfully reduces lottery promotion reaching North Carolina residents and reasonably accommodates the different policies of lottery and nonlottery States. Complete exclusion of cross-border advertising is unnecessary, but the actual advancement and reasonable-fit requirements remain; no categorical exception for vice advertising or bar on as-applied challenges follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The first Central Hudson inquiry is assumed in the station’s favor. Merely marginal advancement, a categorical vice exception, a bar on as-applied review and the greater-power/lesser-power theory are not adopted. The restriction’s unopposed construction excludes noncommercial lottery information.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, removing the as-applied First Amendment basis for the injunction; genuinely unresolved preserved equality or other alternative claims remain. No facial approval of every application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: four-part scrutiny applied, with the first inquiry assumed in Edge’s favor.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: reasonable fit applied; no least-restrictive-means requirement.
- Discovery Network, March 24, and Edenfield, April 26, 1993: unrelated classifications and unsupported harms distinguished, their scrutiny retained.
- Posadas, 478 U.S. 328: no overruling and no adoption of the greater-power/lesser-power argument.

#### Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, Board of Accountancy, 512 U.S. 136

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-639.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### An active CPA credential is not false because its holder disputed regulation

**Holding:** The Board may not reprimand Ibanez for truthfully identifying her active Florida CPA credential on these law-practice communications on the unsupported theory that the title falsely implies submission to regulation. Her earlier legal challenge to the Board's authority does not falsify the credential or prove misleading advertising; the CPA ground for the reprimand must be set aside, with otherwise lawful professional regulation preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the CPA ground and its limited remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies protection for truthful, nonmisleading commercial information and preserves regulation of actual deception.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: applies the distinction between factual professional information and misleading advertising; no immunity from appropriately justified regulation follows.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies the requirement of justification in the relevant professional setting, without imposing an invariable empirical-study requirement.

##### The private CFP credential is not inherently deceptive on this record

**Holding:** The Board may not sustain this reprimand merely because Ibanez's authorized private CFP credential, displayed beside CPA without naming the private certifier, could be mistaken for government recognition. On this record the Board has not established actual or inherent deception or a constitutionally adequate justification for suppressing that truthful certification information; the CFP prohibition ground must be set aside, without validating every private credential or every form of its advertisement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices directly adopt this as-applied holding; O'Connor dissents from it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the distinction between protected factual information and actually or inherently misleading speech.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: preserves regulation of deception without treating possible confusion as automatically justifying suppression.
- Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91: applies its protection against the certification ban within the distinct plurality and concurrence rationales; no unanimous rule against certification regulation is attributed to it.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies its real-harm and material-advancement inquiry to the actual asserted professional deception.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: preserves reasonable fit between the selected restriction and its asserted harm without requiring regulation of every source at once.
- United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co., June 25, 1993: preserves supported incremental regulation and reasonable fit; it supplies no presumption that this credential is misleading.

##### The actual immediate disclosure demand does not justify this reprimand

**Holding:** Paragraph (j)'s demand for an immediately adjacent disclaimer that the certifier is neither affiliated with nor sanctioned by state or federal government, together with the certifier's recognition requirements including but not limited to education, experience and testing, cannot sustain this reprimand without adequate justification for that actual burden. The Board has not justified it on this record; the holding preserves supported, proportionate factual clarification and neither prescribes a replacement disclaimer nor creates an exception to paragraph (i)'s separate prohibition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices reject this disclosure justification as applied; O'Connor would independently sustain it if the advertisement is merely potentially misleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Paragraph (g) addresses stated or implied recognition as a public-accountancy specialist unless the recognizing agency is Board-approved; paragraph (i) independently prohibits recognition by another entity using certified; paragraph (j) separately imposes the actual disclosure just analyzed. Compliance with (j) is not an exception to (i). The Court resolves the sanction on this record, not all applications of those provisions, the withdrawn unlicensed-firm charge, future advertising, licensing jurisdiction, or the validity of a differently justified shorter disclosure. An expired credential, fictitious certifier or express false claim of state endorsement presents a different case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, 471 U.S. 626: applies the distinction between reasonably related factual clarification and unjustified or unduly burdensome disclosure.
- In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191: preserves the possibility of adequately justified clarification of potentially misleading professional information.
- Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91: preserves the separate concurrence's limited-disclosure rationale without converting its permission into a duty to supply exhaustive standards.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies record-sensitive justification without requiring empirical studies in every professional-regulation case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The CPA basis for the reprimand is reversed unanimously, and the CFP basis and actual disclosure justification are rejected by eight Justices. The case is remanded to set aside the reprimand on both challenged grounds. Licensing jurisdiction remains intact; no new disclaimer, damages, credential revocation or finding on the withdrawn unlicensed-firm charge is ordered.

#### Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1631.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### The challenged scheme does not materially advance its asserted health interest

**Holding:** The Government may not suppress truthful, nonmisleading numerical alcohol-content statements on lawful malt-beverage labels unless the restriction directly and materially advances a substantial interest. Preventing strength competition and its health harms is substantial, but the challenged scheme fails that requirement on this record; predictive judgment is permissible, while abstract concern and unexplained inconsistencies do not establish the necessary connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stevens concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies its substantial-interest and direct-advancement requirements.
- Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993: applies the requirement of responsive justification for the actual commercial-speech restriction.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993, and Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado, April 18, 1995: apply the requirement that the selected speech restriction address the demonstrated harm, without an all-sources-at-once rule.
- United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co., June 25, 1993: distinguishes its supported state-based scheme; its holding creates no categorical vice-speech exemption.

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently of the failure of material advancement, the categorical suppression of accurate numerical beer-label information lacks a reasonable fit to the asserted strength-competition harm in light of its unexplained breadth and workable measures directed more closely at that harm. Reasonable fit requires proportionality, not the least restrictive imaginable means, and the Court prescribes no particular substitute regulation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stevens concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: applies reasonable proportional fit and preserves rejection of a least-restrictive-means requirement.
- Discovery Network and Lanphere: apply their distinct fit inquiry to the burden and supported alternatives here.
- Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, June 13, 1994: applies the need for justification responsive to the actual truthful-information burden.

##### The asserted need to assist state policy supplies no alternative justification on this record

**Holding:** Assuming the Government’s state-assistance rationale is properly considered, its unsubstantiated assertion that federal suppression of accurate label information is needed to facilitate state alcohol policy does not justify this restriction. Legitimate accommodation of differing state policies remains possible when its need and operation are supported; federalism and the Twenty-first Amendment do not alone replace First Amendment scrutiny.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stevens concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edge Broadcasting: preserves the legitimacy of supported state-policy accommodation while distinguishing the missing demonstrated mechanism here.
- Central Hudson: requires the asserted justification to support the actual speech restriction rather than stand as an unexamined label.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the injunction against the challenged numerical label ban is affirmed, 9–0. The unappealed advertising judgment and separate state, descriptive-strength and design provisions remain undisturbed. No national format, alcohol ceiling, tax or replacement regulation is prescribed.

#### 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, 517 U.S. 484

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1140.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Material advancement and reasonable fit of truthful-price restrictions

**Holding:** Even under ordinary *Central Hudson* review and treating temperance as substantial, Rhode Island has not demonstrated that these truthful-price restrictions directly and materially reduce consumption. The holding concerns this record and restriction, not every possible effect of advertising, prices, or demand; supported prediction remains permissible without a mandatory state-specific numerical study or experimental certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five. Stevens affirmatively adopts this independently sufficient conditional application without accepting consumer ignorance as legitimate or ordinary scrutiny as the complete constitutional account.

**Material application:** The State's search-cost/price-dispersion mechanism and opposing experts' concessions of price responsiveness and theoretical plausibility are material but do not overcome the trial finding of no significant consumption effect. General-advertising studies with control problems, missing estimates connecting this restriction's projected price change to consumption, and possible market-share gains without aggregate consumption gains fail to establish material advancement. Constitutional sufficiency is independently assessed without turning appellate economic inference into a new trial finding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming material advancement, continuously suppressing this category of truthful retail-price information is disproportionate to the temperance objective on this record. Concrete direct price and tax alternatives expose inadequately justified fit; they are neither mandatory replacements nor a least-restrictive-means rule.

#### Gray v. Maryland

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8653.

**Decided:** 1998-03-09.

##### Obvious deletions that retain a direct accusation

**Holding:** When a nontestifying codefendant's confession replaces a defendant's name with a conspicuous blank, spoken deletion, symbol or equivalent substitution that directly identifies the defendant from the statement and its immediate trial context, admitting that confession at their joint trial with only an instruction to consider it against the declarant violates the other defendant's right of confrontation under Bruton. Richardson continues to govern a confession neutral on its face that becomes incriminating only through later evidentiary linkage; this decision does not condemn every neutral pronoun, redaction or joint trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt the rule and this application. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. This is direct majority authority, not a combination of unlike rationales under Marks.

**Material application:** The blanks and spoken deletions preserve an obvious accusation in its immediate joint-trial context; identifying the omitted accused does not require Richardson’s later independent evidentiary linkage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The prosecutor’s follow-up question is not decided as a separately preserved wrong. The rule does not condemn every neutral pronoun, lawful redaction or joint trial, prescribe substantive rewriting, or decide independently admissible or cross-examined statements.

##### Constitutional error and the remand remain separate

**Holding:** The Maryland judgment reinstating Gray's conviction is vacated and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the confrontation holding. Any properly available harmless-error contention requires the State to establish beyond a reasonable doubt under Chapman that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict; this Court makes no harmlessness finding and orders neither an unconditional new trial nor acquittal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the preservation-qualified remand. Stone-Zsela supplies the sixth vote to vacate and remand, but would affirmatively direct the state court to determine harmless error and does not join Part II's narrower instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur and remand preserve only properly available Chapman review; the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict. No harmlessness finding, automatic retrial or acquittal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bruton v. United States, 391 U.S. 123, is applied to obvious name deletions retaining a direct accusation; its limiting-instruction exception remains bounded to that confrontation risk.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200, is distinguished because its redaction removed reference to the defendant's existence; its rule for neutral statements requiring later evidentiary linkage remains effective.
- Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534, preserves specific confrontation protection alongside discretionary joint-trial management; it supplies no universal instruction cure.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, supplies the State's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness burden for constitutional trial error, without an application finding here.
- Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594, preserves separate analysis of error and remedy; its assertion-specific evidentiary holding and different nonconstitutional harmlessness standard are not displaced.
- White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346, retains its exception-specific confrontation rules; no new categorical testimonial definition or exception for an accusatory police confession is adopted.

#### Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5256.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Each material assertion must be against the declarant's interest in context

**Holding:** When an unavailable declarant's statement is offered under Rule 804(b)(3) as a statement against penal interest, each offered material assertion must, in the circumstances when made, so tend to expose that declarant to criminal liability that a reasonable person in that position would not have made it unless believing it true; a generally self-inculpatory account does not carry an independently neutral, noninculpatory or self-serving assertion into the exception. This is contextual assessment, not grammatical atomization: an apparently neutral detail or identification of another participant can qualify if its actual effect sufficiently incriminates the declarant, while neither joint inculpation nor custody alone establishes admissibility or exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. These seven participating Justices adopt the same rule, contextual qualification and explanation. Justices Kennedy and Thomas favor a different construction; their judgment votes add no support to this rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Salerno, decided June 19, 1992: applies its requirement that the selected hearsay exception's actual conditions be satisfied; its former-testimony and similar-motive holdings remain unchanged and do not themselves resolve Rule 804(b)(3)'s unit.
- Lee v. Illinois, 476 U.S. 530 (1986): draws on its warning about selective unreliability in an accomplice's accusation; does not turn its confrontation analysis into automatic evidentiary exclusion or decide the constitutional claim here.

##### Vacatur preserves the distinction between admissibility error and relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with the assertion-specific Rule construction, without a controlling determination that all of Harris's account qualifies or all of it fails; any properly available harmless-error argument must be considered under the standard applicable to the error actually found. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos place the applicable harmlessness burden on the Government and require the reviewing court, considering the whole record, to have fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, with grave doubt preventing a harmlessness finding; Chapman instead requires harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt only if constitutional trial error is actually reached and found, a new trial follows if error requiring relief is established, and neither acquittal nor automatic retrial follows from rejecting whole-account admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven Justices adopt these shared judgment-consequence limits while retaining the application disagreements described below. All nine Justices vote for vacatur and remand, but Kennedy and Thomas concur in that judgment under their different Rule construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record, substantial-influence inquiry to any established preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error; no present harmlessness result is reached.
- United States v. Olano, decided April 26, 1993: distinguishes its ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error; neither forfeiture nor intentional waiver is inferred here.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves the Government's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness burden for a constitutional trial error if one is reached and found; does not apply that standard merely because the Rule was violated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling application rationale under Marks classifies the whole Harris account; agreement on vacatur does not settle admissibility of particular assertions. The Confrontation Clause claim, firm-rootedness and a nationwide prosecution-side corroboration requirement remain undecided. Rule 804(b)(3)'s express corroboration sentence concerns penal-interest statements offered to exculpate the accused. The separate Harrell/Taggart circuit requirement remains applicable on its own terms, without becoming national law. Neither corroboration nor unavailability substitutes for genuine penal adversity; the abandoned coconspirator theory is not revived and no other exception is held satisfied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded unanimously, subject to the seven-Justice Rule construction and error-consequence limits. No common classification of every assertion or present harmlessness finding is made. Properly available harmlessness arguments, independently applicable corroboration and the separately preserved constitutional question retain their own requirements. The abandoned coconspirator theory is not revived; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, with no acquittal or unconditional retrial ordered.

##### The announced reasonable-doubt polling threshold is permissible

## Event

**Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Kravitz, No. 97-1768.** Decided November 12, 1998. On review of the Third Circuit's en banc judgment reversing summary judgment for the securities firms in consolidated customer litigation. The question is whether firms that execute customer orders at the national best bid and offer despite allegedly better reasonably available terms may be liable for primary fraud under § 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act and Rule 10b-5. No argument date is supplied.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Third Circuit's judgment reversing summary judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0. The Third Circuit's reversal of summary judgment stands. The customers' claims return for further proceedings on the actual undertaking, reasonably available execution terms, contemporaneous knowledge and all remaining private-action elements. The Court awards no damages, establishes no liability and orders no automatic trial of every issue regardless of ordinary lawful dispositive procedure.

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None.

| Opinion of the Court, Parts I-III | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Entire opinion: the knowing-deception sufficient ground, application to the disputed execution evidence, preservation of all private-action elements and limited disposition. |

## Holdings

The following two holdings are supported by the opinion of the Court and all nine Justices. They resolve the knowing-deception theory and the summary-judgment record. They do not establish a general primary-fraud recklessness rule.

### A knowingly false execution undertaking can be primary securities fraud

**Operative rule.** A broker-dealer that accepts a customer order without price instructions while knowingly intending to disregard the best-execution undertaking it thereby represents can commit primary deception under § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 when the representation is materially false and the other requirements of a private securities-fraud action are proved. Breach of care or fiduciary duty, inferior execution or the broker's status alone does not establish such fraud; the customer must establish legally sufficient reliance, causation and actual loss as well as the required deception and culpability.

**Controlling explanation.** The parties agreed below that accepting an order without price instructions implies an undertaking consistent with best execution and that acceptance with an intention to breach that undertaking would be a material misrepresentation. Their agreement does not establish what the undertaking required in each transaction or concede any defendant's liability.

First Jersey recognizes a broker's own knowing deception through implied transaction representations. That principle reaches a knowingly false execution undertaking, but First Jersey's proved facts and SEC-enforcement posture do not establish these private customers' reliance, injury or damages. Santa Fe Industries v. Green excludes fiduciary unfairness without deception, and Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder excludes negligence. The present theory respects both limits because it requires a false undertaking made with knowledge of its falsity, rather than a later failure to exercise due care.

The securities firms contend that customary execution at the national best bid and offer defined their undertaking and defeated culpability. Such custom is relevant to the actual representation and state of mind. It cannot conclusively immunize an undertaking that the firms understood to be false when made. The customers must prove the representation actually made, including material instructions or disclosed limits, and every other private-action element. A less favorable price creates no presumption of reliance or automatic damage award.

The evidence permits the knowing-deception theory to proceed. The Court therefore does not decide a general primary-liability recklessness standard or transfer Central Bank's separate affirmative-assistance standard to this direct claim.

### The disputed contemporary execution evidence precludes the categorical summary judgment

**Operative rule.** Execution at the national best bid and offer, or through the Small Order Execution System, does not by itself defeat a primary-fraud claim where evidence supports a genuine dispute that better terms were reasonably available and the firm knowingly accepted the order on a false undertaking. Reasonable availability is assessed under contemporary conditions, including order size, the security's trading characteristics, speed, clearing costs and the expense and difficulty of market access, as well as quoted price; later adoption of a rule or development of technology does not itself establish earlier duties, access or culpability.

**Controlling explanation.** The claims concern transactions between November 4, 1992, and November 4, 1994. Customers identify three possible sources of better terms: SelectNet or Instinet quotations, crossing opposing customer market orders, and matching customer orders with in-house limit orders. Expert and industry declarations, evidence of the firms' own use of alternative systems and evidence of particular trades with contemporaneous better quotations support a factual dispute. The firms' evidence of expense, delay and ordinary industry practice supports their defense, but does not conclusively resolve that dispute in their favor.

The relevant question is the best terms reasonably available to the customer. A nominally better quotation need not improve the customer’s execution after practical costs and timing are considered. Conversely, a customary published benchmark is not conclusive when a firm knowingly ignores a feasible improvement inconsistent with its undertaking. Small-order execution through the Small Order Execution System is part of that practical inquiry; the record establishes no universal safe harbor, and not all orders used that system.

This reasoning applies separately to the asserted electronic quotations, crossed market orders and in-house limit orders. It mandates no particular venue, guarantees no best possible price and imposes no universal search of every quotation for every order. Later execution regulations cannot be imposed retroactively on these transactions. A later study of these markets may supply reliable retrospective evidence of contemporary feasibility; adopting a later rule is not itself proof of earlier conditions. Particular-trade evidence supports a real damages controversy, but does not prove injury in every trade, determine class certification or establish reliance, causation or recoverable amounts. The ordinary rules for resolving the remaining issues continue to apply.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Securities and Exchange Commission v. First Jersey Securities, Inc.:** Applied for knowing primary deception through a broker's implied transaction representations. Its established findings and SEC-enforcement posture are distinguished from the disputed facts and private-action elements here.
- **Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank of Denver:** Its separate culpable-affirmative-assistance route remains intact. Its severe-recklessness standard for that route is not made a general primary-fraud standard, and assistance liability is unnecessary to this disposition.
- **United States v. O'Hagan:** Its requirement of actual deception connected to a securities transaction remains intact. The Court does not extend its distinct source-deception theory to supply the representation here.
- **Santa Fe Industries v. Green, 430 U.S. 462:** Followed. Fiduciary unfairness or breach alone does not constitute federal securities fraud.
- **Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185:** Followed. Negligence is insufficient; the Court does not treat Hochfelder as resolving every primary-liability recklessness question.
- **Basic Inc. v. Levinson:** The requirement of legally sufficient reliance is preserved. A worse execution price supplies no automatic reliance presumption in this case.

## Law After Decision

A customer's claim may proceed on evidence of a broker's own knowingly false best-execution undertaking. Neither a published quotation benchmark nor industry custom categorically forecloses that claim. Practical execution circumstances and the full requirements of private fraud remain essential. The Court leaves the broader primary-recklessness question unresolved, and no new execution regulation or automatic price guarantee follows.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring; no joins.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's knowing-deception holding and affirmance. He would also hold that reckless, materially misleading acceptance of customer orders under the existing best-execution undertaking can constitute primary § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 fraud when every ordinary private-action requirement is proved. Acceptance communicates an intention to honor that undertaking; an actionable false representation need not be confined to a defendant's express statement. His broader culpability position goes beyond the sufficient ground the Court adopts and does not become the Court's rule.

He agrees that best execution depends on terms reasonably available under contemporary market conditions, that industry custom informs but does not conclusively resolve culpability, and that later SEC rules cannot create retroactive liability. Later market studies may corroborate evolving feasibility without supplying a retroactive legal command. He would leave knowing or reckless falsity, reliance and actual damage for case-specific proof. Neither breach of fiduciary duty alone, execution at the national best bid and offer nor the existence of a better price automatically establishes fraud, and the concurrence recognizes no reliance presumption from price disparity.

The Third Circuit's reversal of summary judgment remains in effect, and the litigation returns for further proceedings consistent with the Court's holdings. The actual undertaking, practical alternatives and contemporary knowledge remain disputed. Reliance, causation, injury, class issues and recoverable amounts remain subject to the applicable law and proof. No compensation or regulatory injunction is ordered, and no automatic trial or favorable merits determination is guaranteed.

## Holdings

### I. Reservation of the federal cause does not erase a necessary, fairly litigated state finding

**Operative rule:** Section 1738 gives the Oregon judicial determination its lawful state preclusive effect: reserving a federal takings cause does not exempt an identical factual issue actually and necessarily litigated with a full and fair opportunity by parties bound in proceedings Oregon recognizes. Those requirements establish preclusion of profitable timber use here, but not of the entire federal claim, every federal element, a precise residual valuation or the legal assessment of partial deprivation.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires lawful rendering-state effect without a general public-policy exception; Allen applies that principle to actually litigated issues. England preserves federal questions following federal abstention, not a unilateral right to contradict a necessary finding in separate state compensation litigation. Williamson County's then-governing compensation requirement does not create the claimed exemption, and the Dodds completed the state process. Richards requires lawful authority to bind and adequate opportunity; these owners were actual parties.

Each Oregon condition is satisfied at the proper level of specificity. The existence of profitable timber use is the same factual issue relevant to total federal deprivation. The competing appraisal and forester evidence were presented, and the use finding was necessary to the final state no-taking determination. Available evidentiary procedures, including a hearing available on request and witness examination, together with actual presentation and judicial review, supplied a full and fair opportunity. The Dodds themselves were parties. The LUBA adjudication and ensuing judicial review are proceedings Oregon recognizes for this purpose. The reported failure to request the available hearing does not eliminate the opportunity; Kremer distinguishes adequacy from use of every available procedure.

The federal cause remains distinct. The state finding establishes retained profitable use, not the federal significance of every restriction or a particular dollar value. The Court therefore rejects complete preclusion and separately addresses the federal grounds. No rule makes every administrative proceeding fair or every reserved federal issue precluded.

### II. Profitable timber use defeats only the complete-deprivation category

**Operative rule:** The binding finding that this parcel retains profitable timber use defeats a Lucas claim that the restriction eliminated all economically beneficial use. It does not itself decide partial deprivation under Penn Central, and no background-property limitation need be found when the necessary complete loss has not been established.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support; Stone-Zsela does not join this federal merits determination.

**Controlling explanation:** Lucas supplies a categorical compensation rule after complete deprivation, subject to a substantiated antecedent property or nuisance limitation. Profitable timber use prevents the owners from establishing that categorical premise. The Court need not decide that Oregon's Goal 4 restriction already excluded the proposed dwelling from title or shift Lucas's background-law burden before total deprivation is shown. Prior enactment or a legislative description of harm would not alone satisfy that burden in a case that reached it. Del Monte Dunes preserves the separate partial-taking inquiry; the failure of the total-loss category does not end that inquiry here.

### III. The independent contextual federal inquiry supports this summary judgment

**Operative rule:** A partial-taking claim requires consideration together of economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action; retained value, acquisition under an existing restriction or a legitimate regulatory objective alone is not dispositive. On this undisputed record, continued profitable timber use, the existing necessary-and-accessory dwelling condition, nonassuring County communications and the forest-management character of the restriction support rejection of the claim; the restriction also bears the required relationship to the specified forestry purposes under the received Agins inquiry, without a demanded conveyance triggering Dolan.

**Authority:** Part III has the eight Associate Justices' direct support, including its distinct contextual and purpose-relationship applications; Stone-Zsela does not join those merits grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Penn Central requires the combined inquiry, not an equation between any remaining use and constitutional validity. The property continued to support profitable timber production. The claimed retirement dwelling was subject at acquisition to an existing statewide requirement that it be necessary and accessory to forest use. County communications did not promise the required permits. Those facts bear on the reasonableness of this particular expectation; they do not establish that later purchasers lose constitutional protection.

The regulation restricts forest use to serve actual forestry, fire-protection and chemical-application objectives. It neither occupies the parcel physically nor demands a property conveyance. Its actual relationship to those purposes satisfies the received Agins inquiry at the bounded scope preserved in Del Monte Dunes. Dolan's special nexus-and-proportionality burden for a demanded conveyance therefore does not govern.

The owners correctly distinguish their federal cause from the narrower Oregon finding and insist that retained use alone cannot answer their claim. The Court addresses the remaining legal restriction, actual communications, continuing use and governmental character independently. It neither fixes a percentage diminution nor calls an unknown loss trivial. The reported undisputed record supplies no material factual conflict requiring another trial under the governing standards. Del Monte Dunes protects a properly supported factual taking claim while retaining judicial sufficiency review; it does not guarantee a trial on a precluded fact or unsupported dispute. Personal residential plans receive ordinary property protection, and the result does not depend on labeling them noncommercial.

### IV. Later permission limits the claimed duration without curing a prior taking

**Operative rule:** The July 1, 1996 building authorization ends treatment of this dispute as an ongoing prohibition but does not itself defeat compensation for an earlier proved taking. Because the independently considered total- and partial-taking grounds establish no antecedent violation here, the actual-period claim supplies no compensation or basis for new factfinding.

**Authority:** Part IV has the eight Associate Justices' direct support; Stone-Zsela would reserve and remand the federal issues rather than join this final resolution.

**Controlling explanation:** First English, Lucas and Del Monte Dunes preserve compensation for the attributable duration of an actual taking. Permission issued later does not make an earlier unconstitutional deprivation disappear. Equally, delay in permission does not establish the antecedent violation without the substantive inquiry. That inquiry has been resolved against the owners on the stated record. The Court therefore awards no compensation, orders no new permit and requires no additional factfinding. Criticism of how quickly the owners pursued development supplies no constitutional forfeiture rule.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt each following holding and its qualifications; no separate-rationale aggregation is required.

### I. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar review of Alabama's own judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Amendment does not prevent this Court from reviewing under 28 U.S.C. § 1257 the federal questions decided by Alabama's own courts. Such review is distinct from initiating a new federal damages action against the State.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Opinion of the Court, under the common authority stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Alabama's immunity objection concerns the character of this proceeding. The taxpayers seek review of the federal judgment entered in litigation in Alabama's courts. McKesson recognizes both this Court's authority to review such federal questions and the State's obligation to administer constitutionally adequate relief when required. Treating appellate review as a new action would insulate the state court's decision of federal law from the review Congress authorized. The Court rejects that objection without deciding an unpresented immunity question concerning a separate federal suit or awarding damages against the State.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson supplies the distinction between review of a state judgment and initiation of a federal action; it remains effective on that ground.

### II. Shared taxpayer interests do not authorize binding these nonparties

**Holding and operative rule:** Claim or issue preclusion cannot bind these taxpayers to earlier litigation merely because they share the earlier taxpayers' interests or were aware of the public dispute. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or other legally accountable representation, or another recognized relationship consistent with due process; the asserted common-interest rationale supplies none of those prerequisites.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards requires lawful authority to bind the person before the State may extinguish that person's own claim by another litigant's judgment. The earlier litigation concerned different taxpayers; shared objectives, vigorous advocacy and the public importance of a tax question do not establish representative authority over these taxpayers' personal liabilities. Awareness of another action creates no general obligation to intervene. Describing the proposed bar as issue preclusion rather than claim preclusion does not cure the missing relationship. The Court rejects the relationship asserted below without inventing a factual finding that every conceivable ground of control or representation has been disproved. Ordinary party preclusion, actual control, adequate class representation, fiduciary representation and authorized public representation retain their lawful operation. A prior decision may also supply precedent without conclusively binding a stranger to its judgment. The tax merits therefore require independent consideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Richards's due-process prerequisite is applied to both proposed forms of preclusion; its protection of lawful representative adjudication and ordinary precedent remains intact.

### III. Alabama's franchise-tax bases discriminate against foreign corporations

**Holding and operative rule:** Alabama's franchise-tax structure discriminates against interstate commerce by taxing foreign corporations on actual capital employed in the State while allowing comparable domestic corporations to use a par-value base that may be set well below actual value. The favorable domestic base cannot be justified by comparing nominal rates or tax labels while disregarding the actual competitive burden.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste requires scrutiny of an express geographic tax preference, and West Lynn Creamery requires examination of linked economic operation. Fulton applies those principles to corporate tax classifications. Here, the ability of domestic corporations to employ the favorable par-value base places a materially heavier franchise-tax burden on comparable foreign corporations. Calling both charges franchise taxes does not equalize their bases. Nor does the State's collection of revenue from domestic corporations in other forms establish that the challenged distinction is evenhanded. Tracy's different regulated-service comparison supplies no exemption for this domestic/foreign classification on the comparable-business facts presented. The Court decides this discriminatory structure, not the validity of every regulation with differing economic effects or every tax imposed on corporations with some common product.

**Precedent treatment:** Oregon Waste's origin-discrimination rule, West Lynn Creamery's practical comparison and Fulton's treatment of corporate tax preferences are applied. Tracy's distinct actual-service comparison is distinguished, not displaced.

### IV. The domestic shares tax does not establish a compensatory defense

**Holding and operative rule:** A compensatory-tax defense requires an identified actual intrastate burden, an interstate charge that roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and substantially equivalent taxable events capable of functioning as mutually exclusive proxies. Alabama's domestic shares tax reaches a different event and does not establish the equivalence needed to compensate for the discriminatory foreign-corporation franchise tax.

**Authority:** Part IV of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste and Fulton make these requirements cumulative. A domestic tax does not compensate for a foreign-business disadvantage simply because the two taxes relate economically to corporate activity or raise revenue for the same government. The shares tax concerns a different taxable event from the challenged franchise burden, and Alabama has not established substantially equivalent events serving the claimed compensatory purpose. That failure independently defeats the defense. The Court does not hold that different nominal taxpayers always prevent compensation or prohibit genuinely complementary charges. It requires the substantive equivalence that the asserted defense has not demonstrated here.

**Precedent treatment:** Oregon Waste and Fulton supply the cumulative actual-burden, nonexcess and equivalent-event requirements. Their allowance for genuinely complementary taxes survives; fiscal correlation alone remains insufficient.

**Independently sufficient alternative holding:** Alabama also fails to establish an actual matching domestic burden and a roughly equivalent, nonexcessive interstate burden by invoking other domestic taxes or an aggregate fiscal balance. This separate deficiency defeats the compensatory defense even apart from the absence of equivalent taxable events.

**Authority for the alternative holding:** The same unanimous Part IV coalition.

**Controlling explanation for the alternative holding:** Fulton distinguishes a demonstrated matching burden from a general description of corporate taxation. The present justification does not establish that the domestic burden actually corresponds to the disadvantage imposed on comparable foreign corporations. An assertion that the overall system balances revenue cannot replace that showing. The Court supplies no numerical ratio, new formula or finding that every conceivable tax comparison necessarily fails; it holds the asserted comparison insufficient on this record.

**Precedent treatment for the alternative holding:** Fulton's independently sufficient actual-burden and approximate-equivalence ground is applied without creating a universal calculation.

### V. Alabama must provide adequate relief through lawful state remedial choices

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for constitutionally adequate relief under state law. When payment was required before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, McKesson requires meaningful backward-looking correction; independently, a State may not withdraw a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after payment and substitute an unpursued prepayment route.

**Authority:** Part V of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** McKesson permits a lawful refund, constitutionally permissible retrospective equalization, or a permissible combination when backward-looking relief is required. Reich separately protects the postpayment remedy the State clearly held out, including after voluntary payment; ordinary proof, reasonable previously announced conditions and prospective changes with fair notice remain possible. Fulton preserves those rules while leaving actual payment, preservation, state procedures and severability for lawful state administration. Alabama may choose a remedy within those constraints. Prospective correction may govern future taxation, but cannot by itself replace backward-looking relief when the stated conditions require that relief. A genuinely adequate prepayment system presents a different remedial setting. The Court does not select a replacement tax base, order assessments against particular domestic corporations, fix a refund amount or declare every taxpayer entitled to recovery. Those matters depend on the actual claims and lawful state choices, which must cure rather than preserve the unconstitutional disadvantage.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson's conditional duty of backward-looking correction and Reich's independent no-withdrawal rule govern the remand. Fulton confirms the distinction between invalidating a discriminatory tax and choosing its precise lawful remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exact refund, replacement assessment, severability ruling or particular taxpayer's satisfaction of payment and preservation conditions is decided. Ordinary valid preclusion and genuinely equivalent compensatory taxes remain possible under their own prerequisites. The Court does not reopen otherwise binding judgments or decide a new federal damages action.

## Holdings

### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

Stevens supports the common severability direction while adhering to his contrary disposition of the city-registration classifications; Stone-Zsela would affirm the broader relief below. ## Holdings

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens joins every part except Part IX. Accordingly, eight Justices directly adopt Parts I-VIII and X-XI; seven directly adopt Part IX. Stone-Zsela's component judgment votes add no opinion join. Each holding below is majority law on that direct support; no fractured-decision inference is needed.

### I. The identified owners may seek present review

**Holding and operative rule:** Owners who identify existing possession affected by a criminal ordinance, a closed exemption window and an express enforcement threat may obtain pre-enforcement review without inviting prosecution. P.R.O. may seek the common prospective relief through affected members whose injury is concrete, whose interests are germane to its purpose, and whose claims and requested relief require no individualized participation.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lujan* requires actual injury, causation and redressability; *Abbott Laboratories* and *Babbitt* permit review of a concrete present restraint backed by threatened enforcement. These owners face local surrender or storage elsewhere versus prosecution, rather than merely unsupported plans to obtain unidentified weapons. Their requested declaration and injunction would address that restraint. *Hunt* and *United Food* preserve member injury, germaneness and the distinct participation condition; the latter's statutory exception does not abolish that condition here. A possible future Ohio narrowing decision does not eliminate this present controversy. Neither a completed prosecution nor an advisory judgment about a wholly hypothetical weapon is required or authorized.

### II. The enacted fault rule and the meaning of the offense are distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.31(A)'s silence does not plainly negate section 2301.21(B)'s enacted recklessness default: culpability is unnecessary only where the offense plainly indicates strict liability; otherwise recklessness suffices for the silent offense. In applying these definitions, the City must prove heedless indifference and perverse disregard of a known risk that the prohibited circumstance was likely to exist; that fault condition can address ignorance of an intelligibly defined characteristic but cannot supply an undefined legal boundary.

**Controlling explanation:** Deletion of the former express knowledge term, while the magazine offense retains one, establishes different wording but does not by itself erase the express default. The lower court's strict-liability conclusion was its construction, not an enacted command. The City's first invocation of the default on appeal and its inconsistent proposed knowledge gloss are distinct: the Court applies the enacted default, not a prosecutor-created amendment. *Fargo* and *Women's Medical* require separate examination of definition, fault and substantive validity. *Grayned* supplies notice and practical guidance; *Kolender* independently requires standards constraining enforcement. *Hoffman Estates* makes context, sanctions and protected activity relevant without automatically invalidating every criminal prohibition. *Staples* separates knowledge of characteristics from knowledge of illegality but does not impose its federal actual-knowledge construction on this ordinance. *Love* defeats the premise that personal keeping has no constitutional protection; it supplies neither First Amendment overbreadth nor the abortion-specific *Casey* facial test. The following rulings depend on their express constructions, not on an invented earlier authoritative Ohio interpretation. No universal knowledge-of-illegality or actual-knowledge minimum is adopted.

### III. An existing compatible rifle magazine supplies an intelligible criterion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(1) identifies a semiautomatic center-fire rifle or carbine accepting an existing detachable magazine with capacity of twenty rounds or more, whether or not its owner possesses that magazine. A merely imaginable future magazine does not establish that physical characteristic, and the City must separately establish the enacted fault condition; the presented categorical vagueness challenge fails on that construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The words describe the weapon's actual capacity to accept an existing magazine, not the owner's purchase package. Walker's affidavit identifies a compatible thirty-round magazine; his preference for five- or fifteen-round magazines does not erase that objective characteristic. Smolak's four-round rifle and lack of knowledge of a larger magazine establish neither a nonexistent larger-magazine fact nor the required fault. The principal objection combines future manufacture with strict liability for unknowable facts. Requiring actual compatibility and separate fault answers that objection without selecting a numerical capacity different from the enacted threshold. *Grayned* does not convert disputed physical proof into an absent legal rule. Neither owner is found liable, and no duty to predict future manufacture is imposed.

### IV. Shotgun capacity concerns actual compatible ammunition

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(2) covers a semiautomatic shotgun whose magazine accepts more than six rounds of actual compatible ammunition, subject to the enacted fault condition. Different lengths of compatible shells do not make that physical-capacity criterion facially unintelligible; imaginary ammunition or an unproved alteration cannot establish it.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners urge a single measuring shell. The enacted criterion instead asks whether the magazine can hold more than six compatible rounds of an actual length. That inquiry is measurable despite different available lengths. Fault remains separate, so an owner's unknowing encounter with a shorter shell is not strict liability. *Fargo* and *Women's Medical* distinguish an intelligible criterion from proof of fault about its application. No new measuring standard, universal knowledge requirement or hypothetical weapon alteration is supplied. A disputed individual application remains subject to proof of every applicable element.

### V. Both modification branches lack the relationship that defines the offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(3)(a)'s two branches—semiautomatic handguns that are modifications of the referenced rifle or of an automatic firearm—are invalid as written because they fail to identify whether actual conversion, design descent or resemblance makes the handgun prohibited. Recklessness does not cure that missing legal relationship, and dependent restoration or parts references cannot independently impose liability through those invalid branches.

**Controlling explanation:** A person may know a handgun's features and history yet lack a rule deciding which kind of modification the ordinance means. The expert's reported account presents this concrete uncertainty. The City's absence of a provision-specific defense does not create litigant default; the undefined criterion supplies the defect. *Kolender* requires minimum enforcement guidance independently of whether an officer can identify an apparently clear example. Physical conversion may be such an example, but listing it leaves the same undefined relationship governing design descendants. The ambiguity affects the central classification, protected ordinary keeping and criminal exposure, rather than merely an obscure edge. No authoritative Ohio construction selects one meaning, and a federal limitation to personally converted weapons would substitute a contested category. Branch-wide relief therefore follows from the actual classification defect, not *Casey*, First Amendment overbreadth or a universal rule invalidating statutes with uncertain margins. Ohio severability governs the remaining provisions.

The local automatic-firearm predicate includes a firearm designed or specifically adapted to fire successive cartridges through one trigger function and separately a semiautomatic firearm designed or specifically adapted to fire more than thirty-one cartridges without reloading. That second branch excludes a firearm chambering only .22-caliber short, long or long-rifle cartridges. It is not silently confined to federal machineguns. The printed reference to division (a)(1), which does not exist, is understood as the referenced rifle provision (G)(1), as in the judgment below; that cross-reference does not supply the missing modification relationship.

### VI. Original handgun design is distinct from undefined modification

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(3)(b) asks whether a semiautomatic handgun was originally designed to accept a detachable magazine holding more than twenty rounds, subject to separate fault. That objective historical criterion is not facially invalid merely because design-history proof may be difficult, and it does not extend to every subsequently conceivable magazine.

**Controlling explanation:** Original design fixes both the relevant time and characteristic. *Posters 'N' Things* distinguishes objective article design from a person's relevant knowledge; its complete federal culpability rule is not imported. The lower court's derivative invalidation from the rifle definition and its broader premise that inaccessible design history is necessarily vagueness do not establish a missing rule here. Unknown facts can matter to culpability and proof without destroying the criterion itself. Ownership alone establishes neither the design condition nor fault, and the Court makes no finding about any plaintiff's handgun.

### VII. Restoration requires a former condition and actual capacity of return

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(4) reaches an existing firearm capable of restoration to a former operable condition satisfying an independently valid (G)(1), (G)(2) or (G)(3)(b) definition, with separate fault. It does not permit enforcement based on an invalid modification predicate, raw materials treated as a restored firearm, or unsupported theoretical reconstruction.

**Controlling explanation:** Restoration denotes return to a former condition, and the word may states actual capability. The absence of a readily qualifier broadens the range beyond easy repair; it does not erase that intelligible relationship. *Grayned* does not demand a numerical standard for every practical criterion. The City must establish the former covered condition and real capability of return. The Court does not invent a universal time, cost, skill or machine-shop threshold. Disagreement about necessary work can present an application question, but the lower court's blanket invalidity does not follow. References to the invalid modification branches remain unavailable as independent predicates.

### VIII. The two parts branches retain different conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The first branch of section 2323.11(G)(5) requires a part or combination designed or intended to convert a firearm into a weapon satisfying a valid (G)(1), (G)(2) or (G)(3)(b) predicate; objective conversion design or the identified conversion purpose must connect the parts to that result. The second requires a combination from which such a weapon may readily be assembled, possessed or controlled by the same person; practical assembly and separate fault remain necessary, and invalid modification predicates cannot independently support either branch.

**Controlling explanation:** Generic unrelated parts do not establish conversion design or purpose, and remote possibilities requiring rebuilding do not establish ready assembly. Common possession or control constrains the second branch; the City may not select arbitrary combinations from unrelated owners. *Grayned* permits practical standards without a numerical assembly formula. *Posters 'N' Things* illustrates the need to identify the article's bounded connection, but supplies neither an NFA parts-package result nor its own exclusive intended-use construction. Borderline practical applications differ from the absent modification relationship in Part V. No personal-use or general gunsmithing immunity is created. No plaintiff's parts, purpose, control or fault is found.

### IX. The former-city-registration selectors survive the presented equality challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.31(B)(3) may exempt a person who lawfully possessed and actually registered that particular assault weapon under former section 2323.05 in 1989, and section 2323.32(B)(2) may exempt a magazine belonging to or forming part of an assault weapon registered under former section 2323.05(C). Protecting that documented registration reliance while limiting the exempt stock rationally supports those selectors under the presented ordinary equality challenge, despite the former ordinance's invalidity and the expired window.

**Controlling explanation:** *Nordlinger* and *Dukes* permit protection of actual reliance without an immutable contractual entitlement; *Beach* permits imperfect classification, while *Heller* and *Romer* require a relation to the actual distinction. The earlier notice defect prevents punishment under the invalid law but does not erase actual registration and its documented reliance. The strongest objection is that lawful owners newly covered by the replacement law could not have known to register during the thirty-day November 1989 window. That exclusion is real, but ordinary review permits the narrower documented class rather than requiring a benefit for every prior owner. Registration of another weapon does not satisfy the firearm exception, and the magazine clause retains its particular related-weapon requirement. No proof that each registrant studied the ordinance is required. The ground is actual records and finite stock, not praise for compliance with invalid law or blame for nonregistration. *Hiley*'s different conviction-relief selectors do not decide this classification. The Court does not resolve an independent substantive arms claim or prescribe a replacement registry.

### X. The other magazine exceptions retain their full alternatives

**Holding and operative rule:** Against the presented ordinary equality challenge, section 2323.32(B)(2) may exempt a large-capacity magazine belonging to an NFA-registered firearm or possessed by the owner of such a firearm; the owner alternative requires neither that the magazine itself be registered nor that it fit that firearm. The separate inert-object branch covers a magazine belonging to or forming part of an assault weapon rendered totally inoperable or inert, which cannot readily be rendered operable or activated and is kept as a trophy, souvenir, curio or museum piece; ownership of an unrelated inert object is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** The NFA branch protects identified regulated ownership and reliance. Its coverage of magazines beyond those used with the registered firearm makes it imperfect, not irrational under *Nordlinger*, *Dukes* and *Romer*. Ordinary equality review does not require exact device-by-device tailoring. The inert-object branch preserves a different qualifying relationship and its express protection against ready reactivation; it is not a general owner's exemption. Neither alternative is rewritten to cure the challenged city-registration distinction. Section 2323.11(F) separately defines the magazine as a box, drum, clip or other container holding more than twenty rounds for continuous feeding into a semiautomatic firearm, excluding a magazine designed only for .22-caliber rimfire cartridges. Section 2323.32(A) expressly requires knowing possession; that requirement is distinct from section 2323.31(A)'s default recklessness and from the rifle definition's twenty-or-more threshold.

### XI. Relief follows the invalid branches and actual Ohio severability

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidity of the two modification branches does not itself eliminate every other definition or exception. The lower courts must determine Ohio severability of those branches and their dependent references, then conform declaratory and injunctive relief to that determination and the component judgments; no enforcement may rest independently on an invalid modification predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** *Women's Medical* requires actual state-law severability rather than automatic whole-enactment invalidation. Ohio's independent-operation, legislative-intent and no-judicial-insertion principles govern. Reversal of the categorical notice rulings establishes only the stated construction-dependent answers, not every firearm's coverage, every prosecution's proof or substantive arms validity. The Court neither creates an offense nor reopens registration, orders arrests or directs blanket enforcement. Stevens's contrary city-selector disposition does not alter his agreement with the common severability principles. Relief against the established modification defects remains; the lower court must determine the proper scope of the surviving ordinance under Ohio law.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Lujan:** applied for concrete injury, causation, redressability and the actual record; unsupported future plans remain insufficient.
- **Abbott Laboratories** and **Babbitt:** applied to present restraints and credible enforcement threats, without requiring prosecution first.
- **Hunt** and **United Food:** applied for representative prospective relief at the actual member-injury scope; United Food's statutory participation exception is not generalized.
- **Grayned:** applied to fair notice and practical enforcement standards; a factual dispute or borderline application is not automatically an absent legal criterion.
- **Kolender:** applied to the independent lack of enforcement guidance in the two modification branches, supporting their invalidity as written.
- **Hoffman Estates:** applied with its context, fault and protected-activity qualifications; its commercial formulation does not erase those premises.
- **Fargo** and **Women's Medical:** applied for separate definition, fault and substantive-validity inquiries; their abortion-specific facial-review rules are not transplanted. Women's Medical also supplies actual Ohio severability analysis.
- **Staples:** applied for characteristic-specific culpability analysis, without importing its federal statutory knowledge requirement or a knowledge-of-illegality rule.
- **Posters 'N' Things:** applied to objective design and distinct relevant knowledge; its complete federal knowledge rule and disputed exclusive intended-use construction are not imposed on the ordinance.
- **Love, Hale and Rybar:** preserve personal arms invocation and the distinct inquiry into coverage and regulation; their remands supply no automatic substantive result here.
- **Wright:** establishes Core-Preservation Review, including practical-burden tiers and the categorical protection against abolishing meaningful ordinary defensive possession in the home. That arms framework does not replace the notice inquiry or the ordinary review of the particular grandfathering classification; no independent substantive arms claim is decided.
- **Fraternal Order of Police:** applies Wright's intermediate tier to a defined domestic-violence disability and rejects a compelled official-use exception; it does not adjudicate this municipality's weapon restrictions under the arms guarantee.
- **Nordlinger:** its independent reliance holding supports protection of documented expectations, without contractual immunity or a requirement that every grandfather classification survive.
- **Dukes:** applied for a bounded grandfather protection of established reliance.
- **Beach:** applied for rational legislative latitude without converting the test into permission for an unrelated distinction.
- **Heller** and **Romer:** applied to require a relation between the actual classification and its stated purpose, without exact tailoring or a new comprehensive scrutiny tier.
- **Hiley:** its conviction-relief classifications remain in force, and its abandoned arms claim remains unadjudicated. Neither its equal-protection analysis nor FOP's official-use ruling resolves the different municipal registry on its own.
- **Springfield Armory**, the earlier Sixth Circuit decision: its invalidation of the former ordinance remains the historical legal setting; its suggested possibility of clearer generic definitions is not advance approval of this ordinance. **Arnold v. City of Cleveland** resolved a distinct Ohio constitutional challenge and supplies no controlling vagueness construction here.

No Supreme Court precedent is overruled. The lower court's collective-only, no-incorporation premise is displaced by Love; its broad strict-liability and categorical-definition reasoning is rejected at the stated scopes.

## Law After Decision

Effective October 15, 1998, the two undefined handgun-modification branches remain invalid, together with any attempted independent reliance on them through restoration or parts references. The other challenged definitions survive the stated categorical vagueness theories only under the Court's express constructions and the enacted fault conditions. All actual elements and distinct thresholds remain necessary; personal possession alone does not establish liability.

The two former-city-registration exceptions and the distinct NFA and inert-object magazine alternatives survive the presented ordinary equality challenges. No new registry, individual exemption adjudication or substantive assault-weapon ruling is created. Love's personal protection remains governing. A materially different construction or prosecution cannot claim validation from this decision. Ohio severability and conforming relief remain for the lower courts.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He joins the Court's threshold, fault, definition and noncity-exception analyses and its common severability principles. He would affirm invalidity of both former-city-registration selectors. His Nordlinger objection to entrenched ownership and family preferences and his Heller and Romer positions require a meaningful relation to an impartial public purpose. Lawful pre-existing owners who registered and those unable to identify the invalid old duty are alike in the ownership reliance invoked here. Owners first covered by the replacement ordinance had no meaningful opportunity to enter the only registry. For him those defects disconnect the closed registry from the claimed protection, even though actual registration occurred. A clear prospective cutoff or an opportunity for all covered prior owners would present a different case. He does not require universal grandfathering, invalidate the independent NFA or inert-object exceptions, or design a replacement scheme.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He would affirm the Sixth Circuit's relief against all five challenged definition categories and both former-city-registration distinctions, while sustaining the remaining NFA magazine exception. Smolak and Walker's actual possession, closed registration window and threatened prosecution justify present review; P.R.O. seeks common relief for similarly situated members. No advisory judgment about a wholly hypothetical weapon is warranted.

In his view, the rifle definition does not choose among actual magazine possession, commercial availability and merely possible manufacture; the shotgun definition does not select a measuring shell length. Undefined modification and inaccessible original handgun-design history compound the handgun defect. Restoration and parts provisions inherit those uncertainties and add uncertain assembly conditions. Criminal exposure without a clear culpability safeguard makes those defects consequential for ordinary owners and grants enforcement discretion the text does not constrain. The City's conflicting suggested knowledge standard cannot rewrite the ordinance. He therefore does not adopt the Court's default-fault and definition constructions. Love reinforces intelligible notice for protected keeping but establishes neither a right to every listed weapon nor a judicially chosen capacity limit.

He rejects grandfathering based on success in guessing an invalid 1989 registration duty. That objection reaches section 2323.31(B)(3) and the matching city clause in section 2323.32(B)(2). NFA registration instead supplies an independent, intelligible reliance basis. Its broader coverage than magazines individually used with the registered firearm survives ordinary rationality under Romer without exact tailoring. He also agrees with the judgment sustaining the separate related inert-object magazine exception, adding no ground on that component. He would implement the declaratory and injunctive relief for the invalid provisions, preserve separable valid exceptions, and enact neither damages nor a fresh registry. He decides no unpresented substantive arms claim and joins no part of the Court's opinion.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment is affirmed in part and reversed in part, and the cause is remanded through the Sixth Circuit for Ohio severability and conforming declaratory and injunctive relief. The two modification-branch defects remain established. No independent enforcement can rest on those predicates, and reversal of the other categorical rulings does not itself establish any owner's criminal liability or direct blanket enforcement. No filing deadline, mandatory new evidentiary hearing or later Supreme Court return is ordered.
