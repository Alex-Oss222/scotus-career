## Standards and Tests selection: Race-conscious local public-contracting preferences

### Race-conscious local public-contracting preferences

**Governing question:** Constitutional justification and fit for local racial or ethnic contracting classifications.

**Current rule:** Local racial or ethnic contracting preferences must satisfy Adarand's common substantial-justification standard. General inequality, imbalance and good intentions alone are insufficient; a supported local system of exclusion in which the government participates may permit fitted repair without an invariably required prior liability judgment. Before adopting preferences, the government must seriously consider reasonably available race-neutral measures responsive to the identified exclusion and explain their inadequacy; it need not try and exhaust every conceivable measure.

**Authority by component:**

- Engineering Contractors Holdings 2–3: application of Adarand's common standard, supported repair and neutral alternatives: Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County — controlling Holdings, 1998-09-21.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-09-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** No numerical constitutional minimum, exact-parity condition or categorical bar to future supported remediation is adopted. The additional racial-evidence-insufficiency rationale lacked a majority; it supplies no further rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County — Association standing to challenge unequal bidding opportunity; Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County — Local contracting preferences and separate racial and sex scrutiny.

## Standards and Tests selection: Program-specific prison comparisons and sex classifications

### Program-specific prison comparisons and sex classifications

**Governing question:** Equal-protection claims concerning particular prison program opportunities.

**Current rule:** Assess whether prisoners are similarly situated in relation to the challenged opportunity. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resources may distinguish a particular program, but whole-institution differences cannot defeat every comparison categorically. An actual sex classification in terms or administration requires the State to establish an important objective and substantial relationship to the sex distinction. For a genuinely sex-neutral choice, plaintiffs must prove that it was made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and knowledge alone do not establish purpose, although circumstantial evidence may do so. Segregated housing does not settle which branch governs a program decision.

**Authority by component:**

- Opportunity-specific comparison and separate classification/purpose branches: Similarity must concern the challenged opportunity and Actual sex classifications and neutral decisions require distinct inquiries, 1995-12-04; O’Connor’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Klinger, 1995-12-04.

**Exceptions and limits:** No identical-services right, prison exemption from sex-classification scrutiny, impact presumption, least-restrictive-means duty or entitlement to a selected program is created. Unequal programs and separated housing alone do not establish liability; immunity, entity responsibility and remedies remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Klinger v. Department of Corrections.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Carriger v. Stewart — Pending capital habeas and whole-record innocence review; Carriger v. Stewart — Conditional disclosure materiality and witness files; Calderon v. Thompson — Recall of completed habeas merits mandates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prisoner civil-rights fee awards under the original PLRA

### Prisoner civil-rights fee awards under the original PLRA

**Governing question:** An action brought by a confined prisoner in which §1988 authorizes fees.

**Current rule:** Fees may be awarded only to the extent directly and reasonably incurred proving an actual violation of rights protected by a fee-authorizing statute, and proportionately related to court-ordered relief for that violation or directly and reasonably incurred enforcing the ordered relief. When a monetary judgment is awarded, a portion not exceeding twenty-five percent must satisfy fees assessed against the defendant; the enacted text provides that if the fee award is not greater than 150 percent of the judgment, the excess shall be paid by the defendant. The hourly rate may not exceed 150 percent of the §3006A appointed-counsel rate. The prisoner may agree to pay more personally, rather than through a §1988 defendant-paid award.

**Authority by component:**

- Original fee predicates, judgment contribution, conditional excess language and rate ceiling: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), original §1997e(d), April 26, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** PLRA, April 26, 1996.

**Effective transition:** The statute was enacted April 26, 1996. Section 802's express all-prospective-relief transition is not generalized to §1997e or §1915; disputed application to pending matters remains unresolved unless an applicable authority separately resolves it.

**Exceptions and limits:** This states the original conditional 150-percent language without replacing it with a later judicial gloss. The statute does not itself establish prevailing-party status, compensable work or any award; §1997e(h)'s prisoner definition applies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

### Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

**Governing question:** Whether a privately owned federally chartered lender shares the United States' exception to §1341.

**Current rule:** Borrower-owned production credit associations suing for their own commercial interests do not receive the sovereign's federal forum merely because they have a federal charter, instrumentality designation or claimed substantive tax immunity. If a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists, §1341 bars their federal injunction and declaration action. Institutional identity, substantive tax immunity and federal-forum entitlement are separate questions.

**Authority by component:**

- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas — I. These associations cannot claim the sovereign's federal forum, 1997-06-02; Breyer and the seven other Associates (eight total) on complete rule; Stone-Zsela joins identity, adequacy and injunction grounds

**Present operation effective:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services, 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other federal agencies, special statutory jurisdictional grants and genuinely inadequate state remedies remain unresolved. The forum dismissal does not decide the substantive exemption.

**Holdings navigation:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas (1997-06-02) — Federal Courts / State-tax equitable relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction

### Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction

**Governing question:** HIV impairment, reproduction and the ADA’s actual-disability branch.

**Current rule:** HIV disease is a physical impairment before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. Actual-disability coverage still requires a substantial limitation of that individual’s activity; diagnosis alone is insufficient. Substantial reproductive limitation does not require complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, attempted pregnancy or willingness to incur the supported transmission risk.

**Authority by component:**

- Holdings 1–2: impairment, major life activity and individual limitation: Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other disability routes, unrelated major life activities and liability remain separate from this actual-disability showing.

**Holdings navigation:** Bragdon v. Abbott — ADA disability and direct-threat assessment in public accommodations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

### Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

**Governing question:** When health or safety risk permits a covered provider to exclude a person.

**Current rule:** Exclusion requires a significant risk to others’ health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment using current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation. The assessment uses information reasonably available to the provider and profession when service is refused, not a subjective sincerity test; neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission is universally required. Sincere professional belief receives no special legal deference. Reasonable public-health judgments receive special but nonconclusive weight subject to credible scientific challenge, only within the source’s supported role, date and scope.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 3: unanimous objective method; Holding 4: five direct votes for founded nonconclusive public-health weight: Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment; neither an inferred official conclusion nor later information substitutes for the actual relevant source. An alternative hospital offer alone supplies no independent defense or duty to accept it; service outside professional competence is not required and objectively justified precautions remain permissible.

**Holdings navigation:** Bragdon v. Abbott — ADA disability and direct-threat assessment in public accommodations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

### Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

**Governing question:** When a capital defendant must be allowed to explain the only lawful alternative to death.

**Current rule:** When the State puts future dangerousness in issue and the only lawful alternative to death is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. No single verbal formula is required. Speculative clemency does not make ordinary parole available. On direct review, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that denying the explanation did not contribute to the jury’s selection of death; where it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it continues to seek death. The error is not classified as structural.

For federal collateral review, Gardner and Skipper dictated this confined effective-opportunity protection before October 3, 1988; Teague does not bar that claim as new. Independently, even if classified as new, it falls within the watershed exception when future dangerousness is an operative basis for death, the sole lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and no accurate defense presentation or adequate instruction affords an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise. Neither retroactivity ground establishes the predicates of a violation, its error consequence or other genuinely applicable habeas conditions. Prison dangerousness remains a permissible State argument, without an automatic exception to the information protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Trigger, effective means and error remedy: Simmons v. South Carolina — Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case; The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued, 1994-06-17. Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary. Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.
- Old-rule holding: O'Dell, O'Dell v. Netherland — I. The opportunity to answer this future-danger case was dictated at finality, Stevens with O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five); independent watershed alternative: O'Dell v. Netherland — II. Independently, the confined protection satisfies the watershed exception if treated as new, Stevens with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five); separate relief predicates: O'Dell v. Netherland — III. Retroactivity does not by itself establish a present sentencing writ, five-Justice Part III including O'Connor and excluding Stone-Zsela, 1997-06-19.

**Present operation effective:** O'Dell v. Netherland, 1997-06-19, for retroactive availability; Simmons's operative protection and direct-review consequence date from 1994-06-17.

**Open question:** The decision does not establish the instruction requirement when the State has not raised future danger or when the legally available alternatives materially differ.

**Holdings navigation:** Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case; The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued. O'Dell v. Netherland (1997-06-19) — Criminal Procedure / Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Individualized capital selection without fixed weights

### Individualized capital selection without fixed weights

**Governing question:** What instructions are required for the discretionary selection decision after lawful death eligibility.

**Current rule:** After the defendant is lawfully eligible for death, the jury may consider understandable, relevant circumstances in making an individualized selection without mandatory binary aggravating-or-mitigating labels or numerical weights. The circumstances of the crime, relevant violent criminal activity and age may supply sufficiently definite subjects in context. Age is relevant but is not automatically aggravating merely because the defendant is not young. This selection discretion does not relax eligibility narrowing, authorize a forbidden adverse consideration, exclude relevant mitigation or cure a separate weighing error.

**Authority by component:**

- Selection discretion and bounded contextual factors: Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California — Relevant individualized selection does not require fixed labels or numerical weights; The circumstances-of-the-crime factor survives both presented challenges; The violent-criminal-activity factor is sufficiently definite in Tuilaepa; Age is a relevant contextual subject, not an automatic aggravator, 1994-06-30. Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly adopt this bounded rule; Stevens and Ginsburg do not join Part I's additional framework discussion. Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight find no error on each docket's factor-(a) challenge; none of these joins is a harmlessness vote. Part IV of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly sustain factor (b) on no-error grounds; Blackmun dissents on this factor as well as the others. Part V of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight adopt the rule and no-error application; Part VI gives the corresponding affirmances without a new penalty hearing.

**Present operation effective:** Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, 1994-06-30.

**Holdings navigation:** Relevant individualized selection does not require fixed labels or numerical weights; The circumstances-of-the-crime factor survives both presented challenges; The violent-criminal-activity factor is sufficiently definite in Tuilaepa; Age is a relevant contextual subject, not an automatic aggravator.

## Standards and Tests selection: Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

### Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

**Governing question:** Constitutionally invalid aggravation in a weighing State, whether used directly by the judge or transmitted through a jury recommendation carrying legally required great weight.

**Current rule:** Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure a constitutional weighing error, although an invalid weighing factor does not automatically negate death eligibility if valid eligibility findings remain. The state judicial system must provide genuine reweighing without the invalid factor where state law authorizes it, or constitutional harmless-error review proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not affect the sentence. Bare factor counting, proportionality review or a harmless-error label supplies neither cure; no particular verbal formula is indispensable if the substantive finding is actually made. A jury's inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction can infect the final death sentence when the judge must give the resulting recommendation great weight, even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. Correction must address that transmitted influence. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor disturbance of guilt follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Distinct weighing error and eligibility, with lawful correction routes: Stringer v. Black, “A weighing error needs a constitutionally adequate cure”, March 9, 1992; Kennedy for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor.
- Actual judicial reliance, substantive cure and no magic-words requirement: Sochor v. Florida, “The judge's documented coldness reliance requires correction” and “The existing appellate explanation does not cure the error”, June 8, 1992; respectively the eight-Justice Part III-B-1 and six-Justice Parts III-B-2/IV, applying Stringer, Clemons, Parker and Chapman without changing their correction standard.
- A legally influential recommendation and correction of its transmitted effect: Espinosa v. Florida, “A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator” and “Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence”, June 29, 1992; per curiam joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Espinosa*, June 29, 1992, extending the rule to the recommendation's legally required influence. The earlier direct-weighing rule remains supplied by *Stringer*, March 9, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** On the harmless-error route the State bears Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. That requirement is distinct from authority to perform a genuine reweighing and from collateral-review gateways.

**Exceptions and limits:** States retain latitude in assigning sentencing roles, but must give constitutional guidance to an influential recommendation. No factor-specific juror finding is presumed, and constitutional permission for a kind of appellate cure does not establish that state law authorizes it. Independent preservation, default and habeas barriers remain intact.

**Related current rules:** “Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations” preserves the distinct inference boundary for a legally available but evidentially unsupported jury factor. “Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims” governs access to review of otherwise barred claims, not correction of an available claim's weighing error.

**Holdings navigation:** Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992) — Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity; Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992) — Capital sentencing; Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992) — Capital sentencing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations

### Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations

**Governing question:** The effect of an evidentially unsupported but legally available ground when a general decision does not identify which ground was used.

**Current rule:** A general multiple-object federal conspiracy verdict need not be set aside merely because one lawful object lacks sufficient evidence if another is sufficiently proved under the unchanged beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. Factual insufficiency is distinct from legal invalidity; this rule does not preserve a verdict resting on a legally forbidden theory. In the separate Florida advisory-capital-jury setting, submission of a legally available but evidentially unsupported aggravator does not alone establish reliance when the general recommendation identifies no aggravator findings. That inference boundary neither finds actual rejection of the factor nor resolves a legally invalid instruction. Express judicial reliance remains distinct and subject to capital-weighing correction.

**Authority by component:**

- General multiple-object conspiracy verdict and factual/legal distinction: Griffin v. United States, “General verdict containing a factually unsupported lawful object”, December 3, 1991; Scalia, Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- Limited extension to an unparticularized advisory capital recommendation: Sochor v. Florida, “The general jury recommendation does not establish coldness reliance”, June 8, 1992; Souter's Part III-A joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Sochor*, June 8, 1992, for the advisory-recommendation component; *Griffin*, December 3, 1991, remains the operative authority for the conspiracy-verdict component.

**Exceptions and limits:** Failure of proof on every route, an element-diluting instruction, an independently presented unanimity defect and broader capital-jury error rules are not resolved. No fact-specific jury finding may be supplied from a general recommendation.

**Related current rules:** “Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error” governs constitutionally defective weighing and the influence of an inadequately narrowed jury instruction; the factual-insufficiency rule does not displace it.

**Holdings navigation:** Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991) — General verdicts; Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992) — Capital sentencing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

**Authority by component:**

- Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority: United States v. Olano — Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Correction remains discretionary: United States v. Olano — Correction remains discretionary, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone: United States v. Olano — Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

- Johnson v. United States — I. The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error, 1997-05-12; Stone-Zsela, all nine
- Johnson v. United States — II. Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting, 1997-05-12; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight

**Present operation effective:** Johnson, 1997-05-12, resolving plainness where trial law was settled to the contrary.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993). Johnson v. United States (1997-05-12) — The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error; Johnson v. United States (1997-05-12) — Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting.
