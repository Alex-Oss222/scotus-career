## Standards and Tests selection: Appropriate private placement and IDEA reimbursement

### Appropriate private placement and IDEA reimbursement

**Governing question:** When parents who reject an inadequate public program may obtain reimbursement for a private placement.

**Current rule:** When the public offer violates IDEA and the parents' unilateral private placement provides an education otherwise appropriate for the child under the Act, §1415(e)(2) permits equitable reimbursement even if the placement lacks state approval or does not satisfy every requirement in §1401(a)(18)'s definition of publicly provided free appropriate education. Parents act at financial risk and must establish both public inadequacy and private appropriateness. Neither parental choice nor progress alone guarantees relief. The court must consider relevant equitable circumstances and a reasonable amount; equity may support reduction or denial, and full reimbursement is inappropriate if private cost was unreasonable. No mandatory checklist replaces that discretion.

**Authority by component:**

- Private-placement eligibility, parental predicates and equitable amount: Florence County School District Four v. Carter — Appropriate parental placement and equitable reimbursement, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for this rule and affirmance. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Present operation effective:** Florence County School District Four v. Carter, 1993-11-09.

**Holdings navigation:** Appropriate parental placement and equitable reimbursement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Notice of statutory elements and collateral challenges to guilty pleas

### Notice of statutory elements and collateral challenges to guilty pleas

**Governing question:** When may a prisoner challenge a guilty plea based on a mistaken description of the statutory offense?

**Current rule:** A plea requires real notice of the essential charge; a materially mistaken description of an element may make it unintelligent. A statutory construction defining criminal conduct is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague. Failure to raise plea intelligence on direct review defaults the claim. Anticipated defeat or adverse precedent supplies no cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, a sufficient factual-innocence gateway is required, rather than legal error alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive interpretation, informed plea and direct-review default: Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516 — Holdings 1–2, 1998-05-18.

**Present operation effective:** Bousley v. United States, 1998-05-18.

**Related current rules:** Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims supplies the whole-record innocence inquiry and actual-plea-bargain qualification. Passage permits adjudication of plea intelligence; it establishes neither invalidity nor automatic vacatur.

**Holdings navigation:** Bousley v. United States — Collateral review of a misdescribed guilty plea.

## Standards and Tests selection: Reimbursed contractor taxes and derivative federal recovery

### Reimbursed contractor taxes and derivative federal recovery

**Governing question:** What instructions govern reimbursed contractor taxes and derivative federal recovery?

**Current rule:** Federal reimbursement or advance funding of an independent contractor's state taxes alone creates no federal common-law money-had-and-received action for alleged state-law assessment errors. Economic Treasury cost does not make the contractor's tax a direct federal tax. Subrogation takes a derivative right subject to pre-existing infirmities; where governing state law requires active assertion and the contractor's claim lapses before that assertion, later federal suit does not revive it. No universal choice between state and federal limitations for federal plaintiffs is decided.

**Authority by component:**

- Reimbursement does not create the asserted federal refund right: United States v. California — Reimbursement does not create the asserted federal refund right, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Later subrogation does not revive the expired claim: United States v. California — Later subrogation does not revive the expired claim, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-04-26; United States v. California, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. California, 507 U.S. 746 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Legal incidence of taxes on Indian-country tribal sales

### Legal incidence of taxes on Indian-country tribal sales

**Governing question:** State tax incidence on a tribe or members in Indian country.

**Current rule:** Without clear congressional authorization, a State may not enforce a tax whose legal incidence falls on the Tribe or tribal members for Indian-country sales. Incidence is determined from the enacted payment, remittance and credit arrangement, not merely economic burden or the identity of the person transmitting funds. The reviewed retailer-incidence motor-fuels tax is barred on that basis.

**Authority by component:**

- Legal incidence of the reviewed motor-fuels tax: Legal incidence of the reviewed motor-fuels tax, 1995-06-14. Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices support the fuel-incidence rule and application.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-14; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** A proposed incidence-shifting bill is not operative law. A later enacted shift requires independent analysis; this rule does not decide the amount of any refund or a different nonmember-incidence tax.

**Holdings navigation:** Legal incidence of the reviewed motor-fuels tax.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Appeal, certificate, district/circuit application and transfer procedures: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §§102–103, 28 U.S.C. §2253 and Fed. R. App. P. 22, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Hohn Holdings I–II: certiorari review and distinct certificate requirements: Hohn v. United States — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-15.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-15, for the new component; earlier component authorities retain their stated dates.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 22’s circuit-judge transfer language does not repeal the Supreme Court’s distinct original habeas authority; see “Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar.”

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Hohn v. United States, 524 U.S. 236 — Certiorari review of certificate denials.

**Former-rule note:** House v. Mayo no longer governs insofar as it denied §1254(1) certiorari jurisdiction over that judicial certificate determination; Hohn, 1998-06-15. Its distinct §1651 extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion and other holdings are not displaced.

## Standards and Tests selection: Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

### Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

**Governing question:** Judicial treatment of a responsible agency's interpretation of its own regulation, including a construction presented in an amicus brief.

**Current rule:** The responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Courts must assess the reading against the operative text, lawful allocation of interpretive responsibility and supported agency treatment. An amicus presentation neither disqualifies the construction nor establishes its correctness. Interpretive authority does not permit amendment of contrary text, unsupported litigation rationalization, automatic authority for an agency lawyer or deference to unproved employer-specific factual assertions.

**Authority by component:**

- Auer v. Robbins — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds, 1997-02-19; Kennedy Part III, joined by all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela's replacement proposal is noncontrolling

**Present operation effective:** Auer, 1997-02-19, for the amicus and factual-assertion boundaries.

**Related current rules:** Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary preserves its separate statutory and constitutional limits. Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution remains the distinct substantive reimbursement rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Auer v. Robbins (1997-02-19) — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds.

#### Aktepe v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-03-10.

##### The pleaded duties cannot be adjudicated without choosing military training policy

**Holding:** A military setting alone does not make a damages action nonjusticiable. These claims nevertheless cannot proceed because determining the alleged duties concerning drill notification, command terminology, live arming and questioning orders would require choosing among military training, obedience and safety policies without an independently manageable legal standard on the supplied record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the rule, application and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independently fixed operational command is established that separates the pleaded duties from policy selection; this is not a finding that none exists. NATO participation, potential embarrassment and military status alone do not bar review. A materially different claim enforcing an independent manageable duty remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Political-question summary judgment is affirmed. The Court decides no negligence, damages, maritime statutory coverage, sovereign immunity or Feres question, and orders no further merits proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baker v. Carr supplies the claim-specific political-question inquiry; military subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Nixon v. United States preserves review of independently fixed legal limits while recognizing the confined nonjusticiability of an actually committed procedural choice; its impeachment holding is not military immunity.
- Gilligan v. Morgan, 413 U.S. 1, is applied to the policy-selection concern, while its reservation of specific-injury controversies remains intact and supplies no categorical damages exception.
- Weiss v. United States and Loving v. United States preserve constitutional and statutory review in military matters; their appointment and delegation holdings do not establish the maritime duty proposed here.
- Dalton v. Specter retains scheme-specific statutory preclusion and is not extended to these maritime statutes.
- Richenberg v. Cohen preserves substantial military weight within actual legal constraints, without creating a military-tort immunity. No precedent is overruled.

#### Florence County School District Four v. Carter, 510 U.S. 7

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1523.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Appropriate parental placement and equitable reimbursement

**Holding:** When the public offer violates IDEA and the parents' unilateral private placement provides an education otherwise appropriate for the child under the Act, §1415(e)(2) permits equitable reimbursement even if that placement lacks state approval or does not satisfy every requirement in §1401(a)(18)'s definition of a publicly provided free appropriate education. Parents act at financial risk and must establish both public inadequacy and private appropriateness; neither their choice nor the child's progress alone guarantees reimbursement, the court must consider relevant equitable circumstances and a reasonable amount, equitable considerations may support reduction or denial of relief, and full reimbursement is inappropriate if the private cost was unreasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for this rule and affirmance. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *School Committee of Burlington v. Department of Education*, 471 U.S. 359 (1985): applies its statutory reimbursement authority, financial-risk rule, and equitable discretion; lack of state approval of the parental placement is not an additional categorical bar.
- *Board of Education v. Rowley*, 458 U.S. 176 (1982): leaves the standard governing appropriate public education intact; the public program's inadequacy and Trident's appropriateness are settled for this review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public inadequacy and Trident's appropriateness are settled for this review. The four public-education conditions in §1401(a)(18) are public expense, supervision and direction without charge; state standards; appropriate education at the relevant school level; and conformity with an individualized education program. Their wholesale transfer to the corrective parental remedy is rejected; the rule does not abolish public-placement standards or establish a constitutional funding right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit reimbursement judgment is affirmed. Equitable authority over reasonable amounts remains; no compulsory approval of Trident or new merits remand is ordered.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Bryan v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8422.

**Decided:** 1998-06-15.

##### Willfulness requires knowing unlawful dealing, not knowledge of the licensing rule

**Holding:** For the charged §922(a)(1)(A) dealing offense, §924(a)(1)(D) requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the firearms dealing itself was unlawful; it does not require awareness of the particular federal licensing duty or the statutory citation. Deliberate dealing alone, unrelated illegality or generalized bad character is insufficient, and the Government must still prove the actual dealing conduct, absence of the required license and all other offense elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven Justices. Scalia and Ginsburg dissent.

**Material application:** The straw-purchase, serial-number and street-sale evidence permits an inference of knowing unlawful dealing; it creates no irrebuttable proof or new appellate finding. Knowledge of a collateral offense cannot substitute for knowledge that the charged dealing is unlawful.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal known-source rule, dealer definition for every unlicensed owner, resolution of every business or license exception, or enlargement of another offense's mental state or penalty is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conviction judgment is affirmed against the preserved licensing-knowledge contention; no new trial, resentencing or unrelated count is reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994:** Preserved as requiring knowing unlawful structuring and residual lenity; distinguished from a universal requirement to know every specific legal source.
- **Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994:** Distinguished as factual knowledge of regulated weapon characteristics, without eliminating this statute's illegality requirement.
- **Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192:** Distinguished in its particular tax-duty setting; no universal known-source rule is drawn from it.
- **United States v. Lanier, March 31, 1997:** Preserved as an independent fair-warning constraint, not a substitute for statutory willfulness.
- **Brogan v. United States, January 26, 1998:** Its reservation of distinct knowledge-of-illegality questions is honored.

For Bryan v. United States, decided June 15, 1998, No. 96-8422, on the distinct unpresented instruction issue, see Federal Courts, Presented questions and distinct instruction objections.

#### Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492.
**Decided:** December 6, 1995.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** Both reviewed §924(c) use convictions receive the same statutory construction and bounded carrying remand.

##### Active employment under the use prong of §924(c)(1)

**Holding:** Use requires actual, active employment of the firearm as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment alone is insufficient; firing or use as a weapon is unnecessary, and Smith's actual firearm barter remains use.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Bailey's gun was stored in a locked vehicle trunk and Robinson's in her bedroom near drug proceeds. The possession-and-availability use theories and instructions cannot sustain the appellate affirmances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No closed list of uses, full carrying definition, retroactivity rule or additional knowledge-of-illegality element is adopted. Distinct possession offenses remain untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith's barter holding is applied while resolving its previously unaddressed passive-possession boundary; its original five-Justice authority is unchanged. Staples and Ratzlaf preserve the elements Congress enacted without creating a new mental element here or displacing ordinary interpretation with automatic lenity.

##### Independent proof of conduct and relation to the offense

**Holding:** Use or carrying must be established independently of occurrence during and in relation to the predicate offense. Actual or potential facilitation may establish the required relation under Smith but does not make mere presence or availability into active use.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith requires a trafficking purpose or effect beyond accident or coincidence and separately supplies active use through the actual exchange. Harris requires independent §924(c) conduct even when the predicate offense itself involves a firearm; its carjacking and cumulative-punishment holdings are unchanged.

##### Bounded consideration of an independently available carrying ground

**Holding:** The use-based appellate affirmances are reversed, and the D.C. Circuit may consider a legally sufficient and properly available carrying basis for either firearm conviction. Carrying language in the charges and instructions establishes neither lawful determination of all elements, harmlessness nor permission for a particular remedial course.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees to remand but dissents from retaining the trial convictions for this consideration without first vacating them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse each use-based appellate affirmance unanimously; remand without immediate Supreme Court vacatur of the trial firearm convictions, 8–1. No carrying conviction, acquittal or retrial is ordered. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments remain intact; no sufficiency, waiver or harmlessness determination follows from instruction excerpts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Robinson's aiding-and-abetting route requires an actual substantive firearm offense and intentional assistance; presence, association or awareness alone is insufficient. A charge label supplies neither element nor procedural availability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris's independent-conduct and intentional-assistance rules remain operative during the permitted carrying review; no new carrying or automatic remedy rule is announced.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Bousley v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8516.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct supporters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns the plea’s own informed validity, not every antecedent claim surrendered by a valid plea or a later disappointed expectation. Competence alone does not establish informed assent.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct supporters.

**Material application:** Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The district court has not determined factual innocence, and the Government does not assert a distinct waiver from failure to raise innocence below. The gateway precedes adjudication of constitutional invalidity and does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** The complete rule has the five direct supporters of Parts III and IV.A: O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela additionally joins Part III’s beyond-the-plea-record inquiry and IV.B’s use-only application and conditional remedy, but not IV.A’s affirmative burden concerning an actually forgone more-serious charge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This indictment charged use alone; no actually relinquished carrying or other identified more-serious charge is established. Carrying cannot be presumed to preserve this conviction. A successful gateway requires adjudication of plea validity, not automatic acquittal or vacatur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Seven Justices reverse the Eighth Circuit; the six joining Parts II–III and IV.B require an opportunity to establish factual innocence followed, if successful, by adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim. Stevens would vacate the firearm conviction immediately. The controlling mandate does not vacate that conviction now, affect the drug conviction, or require an oral hearing, release or dismissal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

#### Hohn v. United States, 524 U.S. 236

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8986.

**Decided:** June 15, 1998.

##### A judicial certificate-denial proceeding is reviewable under §1254(1)

**Holding:** An appellate court’s adjudication denying a certificate of appealability is a case in the court of appeals reviewable by certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), even though the denial prevents the underlying merits appeal from proceeding. House v. Mayo is overruled only insofar as it denies that statutory certiorari jurisdiction; its distinct extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion under §1651 and other holdings are not enlarged or displaced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. These six directly support both holdings, their explanations, the stated reservations and the mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This ground establishes review of the actual denial whether the circuit correctly or incorrectly required a certificate. The Court therefore reserves Hohn’s competing Lindh transition argument and the disputed preservation and scope objections. It holds neither that a certificate was required nor that the underlying appeal was automatically perfected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **House v. Mayo:** Its contrary statutory certificate-review holding is expressly overruled; its extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion under §1651 is neither expanded nor a substitute ground for today’s action.
- **Ex parte Quirin:** Its treatment of judicial business before completion of the ordinary appellate path supports statutory review of the threshold proceeding.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Its actual authorization-review bar and separately retained original-habeas jurisdiction remain intact, including their independent conditions.
- **Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil:** Their dismissals of ordinary authorization review are distinguished from review of a certificate denial.
- **Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal:** Its identification of an independently reviewable threshold classification supports the act-specific channel inquiry; it did not previously decide certificate review.
- **Lindh v. Murphy, Landgraf v. USI Film Products and Rivers v. Roadway Express:** Their provision-specific temporal inquiry remains governing. This decision reserves the certificate amendment’s application to this pre-enactment motion and later notice.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.:** The Court establishes its statutory review authority before granting the bounded vacatur; it does not assume jurisdiction to decide the merits.

##### Reconsideration of the certificate question does not grant collateral relief

**Holding:** The certificate denial is vacated and the matter returned for reconsideration in light of the Government’s changed constitutional characterization and governing law. If a certificate is required and is later issued, §2253(c) still requires a substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right and identification of the qualifying issues; this judgment itself establishes neither condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Material application:** Bailey defines the substantive reach of firearm use. Bousley distinguishes that statutory meaning from a possible constitutional claim and from satisfaction of the requirements for collateral relief. The statutory origin of an argument therefore does not alone dispose of every constitutional claim it may support. The Government’s changed position warrants reconsideration of the actual claim but proves no constitutional violation. The lower court must apply the proper law to the claim presented, including the unresolved certificate question. The Court finds no default exception, factual innocence, invalid conviction or entitlement to a certificate, and supplies no trial or plea facts not established in this proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit’s certificate denial is vacated. It must reconsider under applicable law and the Government’s changed position. The disputed transition argument remains unresolved; the Court neither directs issuance of a certificate nor orders consideration of the underlying appeal as automatically authorized. No collateral merits relief follows from this mandate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bailey v. United States and Bousley v. United States:** Their distinction between substantive statutory meaning, a constitutional claim and ultimate relief informs reconsideration without deciding Hohn’s claim.

# Department of the Army v. Blue Fox, Inc. — 1999-01-20

## Event
**Department of the Army v. Blue Fox, Inc., No. 97-1642.** Argued December 1, 1998; decided January 20, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 121 F.3d 1357, which permitted an equitable-lien claim against the Army. The question is whether APA §702 waives immunity for that remedy. The separately rejected SBA claim is outside review.

The Army obtained no Miller Act payment or performance bond for Verdan's contract. Blue Fox remained unpaid $46,586.14; after notice of the debt the Army paid Verdan $86,132.33. The later $84,910.52 project balance partly financed the $126,772.78 replacement contract. No earned Verdan balance remains withheld. Blue Fox limits its claimed lien to contract funds and argues that notice and its suit prevented subsequent disbursements from extinguishing the asserted charge.

## Participation
All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action
The Ninth Circuit's judgment permitting the Army lien claim is reversed, and the case is remanded with instructions to dismiss that claim for lack of an applicable waiver of sovereign immunity.

## Holdings
#### The requested lien supplies substitute compensation without an applicable waiver

**Operative rule.** Section 702 does not waive federal immunity for an equitable lien whose function is substitute payment from Government property for a private debtor's default when no identified retained property belonging to the claimant or specific statutory payment entitlement supports the requested recovery. The equitable form and identification of a project account do not establish consent; specific enforcement of an actual entitlement remains a different question under its own governing law.

**Authority.** Scalia's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins and controls the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 702's waiver excludes money damages. Bowen distinguishes substitute compensation from specific relief enforcing an actual entitlement, including a statutory entitlement to payment; it does not classify all monetary relief as damages. Nordic Village and Lane separately require consent to the remedy actually sought. A substantive duty, general jurisdiction or equitable description cannot supply missing consent.

Blue Fox expressly seeks contract funds and argues that its lien attached at notice, making later disbursements ineffective against it. But that attachment is the legal proposition to be established, not an accepted finding that the Army retained Blue Fox's property. No earned Verdan payment remains withheld, and the distinct completion balance is not shown to belong to Blue Fox. The requested recovery would replace Verdan's unpaid debt from Government property. Calling the Army a stakeholder cannot establish both the underlying entitlement and consent to that substitute obligation.

Henningsen and Pearlman concern surety or subrogation rights; Munsey Trust likewise supplies no general direct subcontractor right to recover against the Army free of immunity. The Miller Act's bond protection does not make the Government a guarantor when it fails to obtain the required bond. No bond exists here, and the Government's administration does not itself enact a payment mandate or waiver. That failure is not approved; it simply does not supply consent.

Immunity resolves this claim before independent equitable merits. The Court does not declare every equitable lien money damages or decide a genuine proceeding for claimant property under applicable authority. It also leaves the disputed agency-action, reviewability and adequate-remedy arguments under §§702 and 704, and the related preservation objection, unanswered. The money-damages limitation is sufficient.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Bowen v. Massachusetts, 487 U.S. 879:** Preserved and distinguished. Its specific statutory payment entitlement differs from compensation for this private debt; money alone is not the classification rule.
- **United States v. Nordic Village, Inc.:** Applied to require consent for the particular affirmative recovery and to reject equitable or property labels as substitutes. Genuine identified-property proceedings retain their own facts and authority.
- **Lane v. Peña:** Applied to separate a substantive governmental duty from waiver of immunity for the actual monetary remedy.
- **Henningsen v. United States Fidelity & Guaranty Co., 208 U.S. 404:** Distinguished as a surety/subrogation setting; it supplies no direct Army waiver for this subcontractor.
- **Pearlman v. Reliance Insurance Co., 371 U.S. 132:** Distinguished for its surety/property relationship; it does not establish Blue Fox's entitlement or consent here.
- **United States v. Munsey Trust Co., 332 U.S. 234:** Preserved in its governmental/surety setting; it creates no general right for an unpaid subcontractor to attach Army funds free of immunity.

## Law After Decision
Courts must identify the claimant's actual entitlement and the operation of the requested remedy before treating a monetary demand as specific relief under §702. A private-debt substitute does not become authorized merely through an equitable lien, a named project account, notice or the Government's failure to obtain a required bond. A genuine identified-property claim, enacted payment mandate, properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against an actual Miller Act surety or another unequivocal waiver remains governed by its own requirements. None is awarded here.

United States v. Griffin / United States v. Langley; Fourth Circuit Nos. 96-4803 and 96-4805. William Lumas Griffin and George David Langley, Jr., were arrested after breaking into a restaurant in Burlington, North Carolina. ## Holdings

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, is joined in full by all eight Associates. All nine Justices directly adopt each holding and its qualifications below; no judgment-only agreement or fractured-decision inference supplies authority.

### I. The supply restriction and recipient-possession rule supply the constitutional connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may regulate these qualifying convicted recipients' possession under section 922(g)(1) as a fitted counterpart to section 922(d)(1)'s convicted-person supply restriction, which forbids sale or other disposition of firearms or ammunition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has been convicted of a crime punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year. Preventing the recipients' acquisition and retention from defeating that supply restriction supplies the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clause connection; a pistol's former interstate movement, burglary or generalized dangerousness alone does not supply it.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lopez* requires the actual interstate mechanism before aggregation. *Franklyn*, Part II, directly identifies the section 922(d)(1)/section 922(g)(1) relationship for firearms and ammunition: permitting the prohibited recipient class freely to acquire and retain the restricted supply would permit evasion or material impairment through redistribution and concealment. Each defendant's pistol possession falls within that relationship; no separate commercial transaction by either defendant is needed for this constitutional ground. *Hale*, *Rybar*, *Kirk* and *Wright* explain analogous necessary inclusion in the different machinegun-stock regime, but do not automatically decide every prohibited-person statute. *Hiley* likewise supplies its distinct recipient-class application, not universal approval of every status disability. The objection that remote manufacture cannot alone support federal power is correct but does not defeat the independent mechanism here. The restaurant burglary makes the prosecution concrete without creating a federal burglary power or an uncharged commercial element. The enacted nexus remains independently necessary. The supplier's knowledge or reasonable-cause condition is not imported as the complete recipient-offense culpability rule, and statutory conviction exclusions or relief provisions are not displaced. No arms claim is adjudicated.

### II. The requested direct-effect instruction would add an element

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(1)'s retained nexus construction does not require contemporaneous interstate movement or an individually substantial or direct interstate effect from the defendant's possession. Because the regulated class has the independent constitutional connection stated in Part I, the court properly refused each defendant's requested additional direct-effect instruction; the prosecution still must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Controlling explanation:** *Bass* supplies the statutory requirement of a commerce connection, and *Scarborough* establishes that the retained nexus can be satisfied by the firearm's prior interstate movement. *Lopez* addresses congressional authority and does not silently add a per-defendant direct-impact requirement. Constitutional avoidance therefore supplies no reason to insert the requested condition: the actual supply-and-recipient mechanism resolves the constitutional objection without changing the statute. The jury decides the required offense facts, not whether Congress's national scheme is constitutionally sufficient. This holding rejects only the additional legal demand described in the lower opinion. It supplies no verbatim replacement charge, approves no unidentified wording in the instructions actually given and decides no different preserved instructional objection.

### III. The evidence independently supports the nexus for each pistol

**Holding and operative rule:** Viewed favorably to the verdict under *Jackson v. Virginia*, testimony that each pistol was manufactured in Connecticut, together with each defendant's possession of his pistol in North Carolina, permits a rational jury to find that firearm's prior interstate movement beyond a reasonable doubt. Neither a precise shipment date, carrier or seller nor a personal state-line crossing by the defendant is necessary to establish this nexus; both motions for acquittal were properly denied.

**Controlling explanation:** The expert's testimony concerned both weapons. Connecticut manufacture followed by North Carolina possession permits the interstate inference for Griffin's pistol and separately for Langley's. *Scarborough* identifies the statutory fact to be proved, while *Jackson* supplies the rational-jury sufficiency standard. Constitutional authority over the class does not itself prove that fact, and one pistol's history could not substitute for evidence about the other. The account supplies no disputed expert qualification, authentication failure or contrary-evidence defect; none is invented. Gonzalez's ammunition stipulation in *Franklyn* establishes no fact in these appeals. The absence of a detailed shipping record does not defeat this supported inference. The Court makes no additional credibility finding or ruling on possession knowledge, the prior-conviction element, conviction exclusions, weapon classification or sentence.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Lopez:** applied to require a constitutional connection independent of remote provenance, generalized crime costs or a jurisdictional label; it creates no additional individual-effect element here.
- **Franklyn**, Part II: directly applied to the same convicted-recipient supply and possession provisions. Its firearms-and-ammunition mechanism enters as law; its ammunition stipulation supplies no proof for either pistol.
- **Hale, Rybar, Kirk and Wright:** preserve necessary inclusion in the different machinegun transfer-and-stock regime; their holdings do not automatically validate all firearm restrictions or require an arms remand.
- **Hiley:** preserves its distinct domestic-violence-recipient application and separate enacted nexus; it supplies no universal status-disability holding.
- **Bass:** preserves the statutory commerce-connection requirement rather than permitting conviction without the enacted nexus.
- **Scarborough:** applied for the retained prior-interstate-movement nexus, separately from the constitutional justification for regulating this class.
- **Jackson v. Virginia:** applied to each pistol's evidence and the rational-jury, beyond-reasonable-doubt inquiry; the required proof is not supplied by the constitutionality of the statute.
- **Peoples Rights Organization:** its distinction between statutory definition, fault and substantive arms validity remains. Its municipal constructions do not amend this federal nexus or create an unpresented arms claim.

No precedent is overruled. The Court's constitutional ground is the identified supply-and-recipient relationship, not a universal rule that past interstate manufacture alone validates every later federal possession offense.

## Law After Decision

Effective October 29, 1998, the sections 922(d)(1) and 922(g)(1) supply-and-recipient mechanism governs these two local pistol possessions. The constitutional basis, the enacted nexus and evidence proving that nexus remain separate inquiries. No contemporaneous movement or individual direct-effect element is added, while proof beyond a reasonable doubt remains necessary for each firearm.

Both convictions stand on the expert testimony concerning both Connecticut-manufactured pistols and their separate North Carolina possession. The decision supplies no new arms claim, universal approval of every status restriction, recipient mental-state rule, replacement jury charge or sentencing holding.
