## Holdings

### Loss of at-will employment may be the injury caused by a forbidden federal-witness conspiracy

**Operative rule:** When two or more persons conspire to deter a party or witness by force, intimidation or threat from attending federal court or testifying there freely, fully and truthfully, or to injure a party or witness for having attended or testified, loss of at-will employment and wages caused by acts furthering that conspiracy may constitute injury to person or property under §1985's civil-remedy clause without a constitutional entitlement to continued employment. Haddle's allegations of concrete employment loss cannot be dismissed merely because the employment was terminable at will; the prohibited conspiracy and object, required federal-witness nexus, acts, causation, recoverable damages and properly presented defenses remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this proposition, its application to the categorical ground of dismissal and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute protects federal judicial proceedings from the specified conspiratorial intimidation and retaliation. Its civil-remedy language distinguishes injury to person or property from deprivation of a federal right or privilege. Requiring a separate constitutional employment entitlement for the injury branch would collapse that distinction. Kush v. Rutledge explains why the federal-process clauses must be read on their own terms rather than importing the equal-protection-purpose conditions of different clauses; Kush did not itself decide this damages question.

At-will status defines the employer's ordinary authority to terminate the relationship. It does not make the employee's existing work and wages valueless against prohibited interference. Truax v. Raich recognizes that distinction. Roth's constitutional-property rule concerns an entitlement protected against deprivation without due process, a different inquiry from injury caused by this statutory conspiracy. Darden's attention to common-law context supports reading the employment interest in its actual legal setting, without importing its separate employee-classification factors.

Respondents' argument correctly denies guaranteed employment but draws the wrong consequence for the alleged economic harm. Bray requires attention to the particular §1985 clause and does not make its distinct private-right requirements govern this federal-witness injury branch. Robinson and Inter-Modal likewise distinguish protection against conduct forbidden by a statute from a permanent right to the underlying job or benefit. Haddle alleges lost employment and wages from the forbidden object, which defeats this categorical pleading objection.

The Court does not find that the alleged conspiracy occurred or that these corporate agents are legally independent third parties capable of conspiring. It decides neither every witness-definition question nor damages amount, reinstatement or punitive relief. The statutory object, causation and other applicable requirements prevent this holding from becoming a federal remedy for every discharge.

## Precedent Treatment

- Kush v. Rutledge, 460 U.S. 719: applies its distinction between federal-process clauses and clauses requiring an equal-protection purpose. Its reserved damages question is not treated as already answering this injury issue.
- Truax v. Raich, 239 U.S. 33: applies the distinction between terminable employment and protection from wrongful interference with the economic relation.
- Board of Regents v. Roth: distinguishes constitutional entitlement from injury under this statutory civil remedy; its due-process rule remains intact.
- Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden: uses its attention to common-law content in statutory context without importing its ERISA classification test.
- Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic: preserves the requirements of the distinct §1985(3) deprivation clause; they are not transferred to the federal-witness clause.
- Robinson v. Shell Oil Co.: uses its distinction between specific statutory retaliation protection and permanence of employment, without importing Title VII's elements.
- Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co.: uses the distinction between lawful power to end a benefit and interference independently prohibited by statute; its ERISA coverage and remedy rules remain separate.

## Holdings
#### The requested lien supplies substitute compensation without an applicable waiver

**Operative rule.** Section 702 does not waive federal immunity for an equitable lien whose function is substitute payment from Government property for a private debtor's default when no identified retained property belonging to the claimant or specific statutory payment entitlement supports the requested recovery. The equitable form and identification of a project account do not establish consent; specific enforcement of an actual entitlement remains a different question under its own governing law.

**Authority.** Scalia's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins and controls the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 702's waiver excludes money damages. Bowen distinguishes substitute compensation from specific relief enforcing an actual entitlement, including a statutory entitlement to payment; it does not classify all monetary relief as damages. Nordic Village and Lane separately require consent to the remedy actually sought. A substantive duty, general jurisdiction or equitable description cannot supply missing consent.

Blue Fox expressly seeks contract funds and argues that its lien attached at notice, making later disbursements ineffective against it. But that attachment is the legal proposition to be established, not an accepted finding that the Army retained Blue Fox's property. No earned Verdan payment remains withheld, and the distinct completion balance is not shown to belong to Blue Fox. The requested recovery would replace Verdan's unpaid debt from Government property. Calling the Army a stakeholder cannot establish both the underlying entitlement and consent to that substitute obligation.

Henningsen and Pearlman concern surety or subrogation rights; Munsey Trust likewise supplies no general direct subcontractor right to recover against the Army free of immunity. The Miller Act's bond protection does not make the Government a guarantor when it fails to obtain the required bond. No bond exists here, and the Government's administration does not itself enact a payment mandate or waiver. That failure is not approved; it simply does not supply consent.

Immunity resolves this claim before independent equitable merits. The Court does not declare every equitable lien money damages or decide a genuine proceeding for claimant property under applicable authority. It also leaves the disputed agency-action, reviewability and adequate-remedy arguments under §§702 and 704, and the related preservation objection, unanswered. The money-damages limitation is sufficient.

#### General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service

**Operative rule.** General eligibility for workers' compensation does not by itself establish an unconditional constitutional right to constant payment of every disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. This rule neither extinguishes a protected claim to fair adjudication nor decides against a claimant an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient individual expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight direct joins control. Stevens supports the payment disposition on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennsylvania's substantive promise concerns reasonable and necessary care. General program eligibility therefore does not make every contested invoice presently due without regard to that condition. The facial demand for constant advance payment cannot rest on that inference alone.

Logan protects a statutory claim from arbitrary destruction; it does not equate a right to fair determination with unconditional payment before the substantive condition is established. Loudermill likewise prevents government from defining substantive property away through procedure. The Court preserves that distinction rather than making an insurer's objection conclusive or requiring a new adjudication before every payment.

The allegations concerning earlier orders and ongoing treatment are not resolved by assuming the orders concern different services or that no worker has an existing entitlement. Individual reliance on prior payments also remains open. Nor does reimbursement and interest necessarily replace medical care lost during an interruption. Those limitations prevent the confined negative rule from becoming a universal no-property holding.

The categorical advance-payment/severance requirement is set aside only to this extent. The Court does not decide every procedure adequate under Mathews, impose new exhaustion or proof requirements, or adjudicate unchallenged notice/input. Distinct preserved public-party and individual claims return for ordinary resolution under their own predicates.

## Separate Writings
**Ginsburg concurs in Part II and in the judgment.** Rejecting constant payment of every medical bill pending the reasonable-and-necessary determination is sufficient; she does not reach private attribution. Claims for medical benefits still require fair adjudication. She agrees with Stevens that the original absence of adequate notice and employee input was deficient and that the revised features answer that particular objection, without deciding every delay, lost-care or prior-award claim.

**Breyer, joined by Souter, concurs.** They join the private-attribution holding and the confined rejection of the facial payment demand. Prior payments may create a reasonable expectation of continuation in an individual case. The present ruling neither finds nor forecloses that property interest and does not assume that every interruption is brief or reparable with money. Their qualification is not an unconditional right to every disputed bill.

**Stevens concurs in part and dissents in part.** The substantive claim to reasonable and necessary care is property, and government must determine it fairly. The original procedure did not adequately warn of suspension or permit meaningful employee input. He would retain the notice and statement protections imposed below and reject additional categorical advance payment because withholding pending a fair determination is not inherently unfair. He declines the Court's attribution and entitlement rationales, does not declare the carriers governmental, and would leave the remaining actual claims for proper resolution. He dissents from the additional attribution adjudication while agreeing that further automatic payment requirements cannot stand under the revised procedure.

## Holdings

#### A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while the subject-matter issue is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first; the choice must respect applicable statutory remand commands and review bars and must decide no contested merits.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that declining authority over a defendant does not assume authority to decide liability. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. forbids assumed Article III power for contested merits, and NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones applies that boundary to constitutional claims. Neither exhausts the order among nonmerits thresholds. Personal jurisdiction protects a defendant's interest in being subject to the court's authority; its waivability does not make it an optional prerequisite to a binding merits judgment. Here choosing that straightforward ground avoids an unnecessary difficult subject-matter determination without deciding the tort claims. The State's forum interest and the possible consequences of jurisdictional determinations remain relevant to the choice, but do not establish an absolute order. If subject matter is as readily resolved and personal jurisdiction raises difficult state law, comity ordinarily favors deciding remand first. Murphy Brothers keeps removal timing distinct from actual jurisdiction, and El Paso Natural Gas gives effect to a particular statutory priority rather than a universal hierarchy. An established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence; an unresolved difficult issue is not the same thing. The Court leaves the correctness of the actual personal-jurisdiction dismissal to the Court of Appeals and decides none of the three asserted removal grounds.

## Holdings

Both holdings are adopted directly by Souter's opinion for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices join the complete rules and explanations.

### The EEOC may administer the compensation Congress expressly authorized

When federal-sector Title VII intentional discrimination falls within 42 U.S.C. §1981a's damages authorization, §2000e-16(b) permits the EEOC to award that compensation as an appropriate remedy. The agency must preserve §1981a's substantive conditions and monetary limits; its power does not depend on an administrative jury, and a proper court action retains the statutory jury election.

Section 717(b) delegates enforcement through appropriate remedies and introduces the listed equitable forms as examples. Congress subsequently added compensation to the remedies for intentional discrimination under §717. Reading those enactments together gives effect to both the express monetary authorization and the administrative mechanism Congress retained. Section 1981a's reference to an action does not silently confine §717 as a whole to its judicial subsection. Its jury clause regulates trial in court; it neither requires a jury in an administrative proceeding nor creates a governmental appeal from an adverse EEOC award. The Government's inability to demand judicial review therefore does not negate the separately authorized administrative remedy. This conclusion satisfies Lane's requirement of unequivocal enacted consent and Blue Fox's demand for a genuine statutory entitlement; administrative convenience supplies no independent waiver.

The compensation category applies to unlawful intentional discrimination, not disparate impact, and requires that the complaining party cannot recover under §1981. It excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by §706(g). The statutory caps remain applicable, and punitive damages are unavailable against governments, government agencies and political subdivisions. When available §102 damages are sought in court, any party may demand a jury; the court must not tell that jury the statutory caps. No damages under another statute or free-standing appropriations authority follow.

### Statutory authority does not decide Gibson's preserved procedural alternatives

The Seventh Circuit's no-power premise cannot sustain its judgment, but identifying an available administrative remedy does not establish whether Gibson exhausted it or has a properly preserved alternative answer to the exhaustion defense. The reviewed judgment must be vacated and remanded for consideration of proper presentation and, if appropriate, resolution of those alternatives.

McCarthy requires attention to the governing statutory scheme and the actual administrative remedy; its result involving a compensation-incapable prison process cannot resolve a claim involving an EEOC that may compensate. Gibson's asserted cash-award request, completed complaint procedures and inadequate counseling do not become judicial findings merely because the Court rejects the appellate statutory premise. The Court decides neither preservation nor actual exhaustion, estoppel, compensable loss or amount. It creates no categorical exception to an applicable exhaustion requirement. No new damages award or trial is ordered by this decision itself.

## Separate Writings

Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissents. Consent to a federal monetary remedy must encompass the forum and conditions in which liability is imposed. In their reading, §717(b)'s earlier equitable examples do not clearly authorize the later damages remedy, and §1981a's action language and any-party jury election attach conditions the agency cannot satisfy. The Government cannot obtain judicial review of an adverse EEOC award and so cannot secure the jury that the statute allows any party to demand. The definition of an eligible complaining party does not itself enlarge the Commission's power. The dissent would affirm the reviewed judgment on that statutory ground without denying judicial Title VII damages or determining Gibson's actual exhaustion, injury or amount.

## Law After Decision
Section 101 neither excludes inventions because they serve business nor establishes eligibility because they produce useful financial values. Courts must construe and assess the actual complete combination under the inherited calculation/application distinction. These six issued combinations are ineligible; that result establishes no categorical answer for all software, data structures or commercial inventions. Other patent requirements and the retained judicial/factual allocation continue to operate separately.
