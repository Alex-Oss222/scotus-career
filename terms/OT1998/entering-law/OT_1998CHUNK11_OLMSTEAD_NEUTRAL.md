## Standards and Tests selection: State prisons within ADA Title II

### State prisons within ADA Title II

**Governing question:** Whether state correctional departments and prison programs are within Title II.

**Current rule:** A state correctional department is a public entity under §12131, and its prison services, programs and activities fall within §12132. Custody creates no categorical exception: a qualified individual may not, because of disability, be excluded from participation, denied the benefits of those services, programs or activities, or otherwise subjected to discrimination by the public entity.

**Authority by component:**

- Title II covers state prisons and their programs: Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-15.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-15.

**Exceptions and limits:** Qualification, disability-based causation, reasonable accommodation and any independent constitutional or immunity issue retain their own predicates; statutory coverage alone establishes none of them.

**Holdings navigation:** Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, 524 U.S. 206 — Disability discrimination in state prisons.

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction

### Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction

**Governing question:** HIV impairment, reproduction and the ADA’s actual-disability branch.

**Current rule:** HIV disease is a physical impairment before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. Actual-disability coverage still requires a substantial limitation of that individual’s activity; diagnosis alone is insufficient. Substantial reproductive limitation does not require complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, attempted pregnancy or willingness to incur the supported transmission risk.

**Authority by component:**

- Holdings 1–2: impairment, major life activity and individual limitation: Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other disability routes, unrelated major life activities and liability remain separate from this actual-disability showing.

**Holdings navigation:** Bragdon v. Abbott — ADA disability and direct-threat assessment in public accommodations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

### Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

**Governing question:** When health or safety risk permits a covered provider to exclude a person.

**Current rule:** Exclusion requires a significant risk to others’ health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment using current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation. The assessment uses information reasonably available to the provider and profession when service is refused, not a subjective sincerity test; neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission is universally required. Sincere professional belief receives no special legal deference. Reasonable public-health judgments receive special but nonconclusive weight subject to credible scientific challenge, only within the source’s supported role, date and scope.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 3: unanimous objective method; Holding 4: five direct votes for founded nonconclusive public-health weight: Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment; neither an inferred official conclusion nor later information substitutes for the actual relevant source. An alternative hospital offer alone supplies no independent defense or duty to accept it; service outside professional competence is not required and objectively justified precautions remain permissible.

**Holdings navigation:** Bragdon v. Abbott — ADA disability and direct-threat assessment in public accommodations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

### Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

**Governing question:** Judicial treatment of a responsible agency's interpretation of its own regulation, including a construction presented in an amicus brief.

**Current rule:** The responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Courts must assess the reading against the operative text, lawful allocation of interpretive responsibility and supported agency treatment. An amicus presentation neither disqualifies the construction nor establishes its correctness. Interpretive authority does not permit amendment of contrary text, unsupported litigation rationalization, automatic authority for an agency lawyer or deference to unproved employer-specific factual assertions.

**Authority by component:**

- Auer v. Robbins — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds, 1997-02-19; Kennedy Part III, joined by all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela's replacement proposal is noncontrolling

**Present operation effective:** Auer, 1997-02-19, for the amicus and factual-assertion boundaries.

**Related current rules:** Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary preserves its separate statutory and constitutional limits. Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution remains the distinct substantive reimbursement rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Auer v. Robbins (1997-02-19) — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable.

**Authority by component:**
- Enforcement boundary and method: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** City of Boerne, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independent Fifteenth Amendment power and other sources of congressional authority retain their own law. The rule creates no general hearing requirement or evidentiary checklist.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Boerne v. Flores (1997-06-25) — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern equal protection of commitment proof and family participation?

**Current rule:** Kentucky's clear-and-convincing burden for adult intellectual-disability commitment, compared with reasonable-doubt proof for mental illness, fails rational-basis review because the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not justify the greater risk of mistaken confinement. ## Selected opening holding: Bragdon v. Abbott

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.

#### Bragdon v. Abbott

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-156.
**Decided:** 1998-06-25.

##### HIV can impair before visible symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity

**Holding:** HIV disease is a physical impairment within the ADA before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. A claimant under the actual-disability branch must still show that the impairment substantially limits that individual's activity; diagnosis or reproductive-system impairment alone does not establish every element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the disability part. The other three accept or assume impairment for this case without joining its general rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual disability still requires an individual substantial limitation. No record-of or regarded-as holding, categorical HIV entitlement, or restriction of major activities to public, economic or daily activity is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arline's disease and individualized-assessment principles are applied within the ADA; Yeskey's distinction between coverage and individual entitlement remains.

##### Abbott's own reproductive activity is substantially limited on this record

**Holding:** Abbott's specific unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision not to bear a child, considered with the supported perinatal transmission danger, establishes substantial limitation of her reproduction. Complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, an attempted pregnancy or willingness to accept the danger is not an additional condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). The same six-Justice disability coalition directly adopts this application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The historical untreated and treated transmission estimates are evidence, not numerical legal thresholds or current medical findings. The case leaves the general treatment of mitigating measures open. Ordinary summary-judgment burdens remain, without a required attempted pregnancy, earlier childbearing plan or willingness to expose a child to danger.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The ordinary individual-definition and Rule 56 requirements remain; neither institutional coverage nor a diagnosis substitutes for the supported personal limitation.

##### Direct threat requires objective individualized assessment, not professional sincerity

**Holding:** A covered provider may exclude a person only for a significant risk to others' health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment based on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing the risk's nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation; sincere belief or professional status supplies no special legal deference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All nine Justices directly support this common objective method.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relevant information is that reasonably available on September 16, 1994. Neither absolute safety, past confirmed transmission nor numerical quantification is universally required. The hospital offer supplies no independent defense or duty to accept extra charges; no comparative-safety theory was pursued or established. Competence limits and objectively justified precautions remain. No later breakthrough is found, and its possible relevance to prospective relief is not categorically excluded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arline's individualized inquiry is applied; Daubert and Joiner retain their foundation and fit roles without becoming substantive risk or automatic trial rules.

##### Public-health authority has founded, nonconclusive weight; the cited sources require bounded reconsideration

**Holding:** Reasonable medical judgments of public-health authorities receive special but nonconclusive weight, subject to a credible scientific challenge. That weight attaches only to a supported judgment within the source's actual role, date and scope; a private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment. The direct-threat judgment is vacated for reconsideration of the CDC and Association materials on those terms, without deciding whole-record triability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the public-health/source-remand part. Stone-Zsela joins the objective method and remand judgment, not this distinct ground; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas support the risk judgment on their contrary official-weight approach.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The CDC recommendation did not expressly determine residual risk or the need for further measures; the Association policy's private status requires attention to its scientific foundation. No source is declared worthless, no zero/significant-risk finding is made, and no whole-record trial issue is reached. The circuit may again enter summary judgment; no hearing, supplementation, expert appointment or treatment order is commanded. State-law merits remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arline's founded public-health weight is preserved; Joiner's source/inference discipline is applied within the granted question, leaving independent whole-record sufficiency undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The disability determination is affirmed. The direct-threat portion is vacated and returned through the First Circuit for reconsideration under the objective method and controlling source distinctions. The lower court determines the consequence for the judgment without a commanded trial, new evidence, hearing or treatment. State-law claims remain subject to ordinary lower proceedings without a new substantive state-law ruling.

## Selected opening holding: Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, 524 U.S. 206

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.

#### Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, 524 U.S. 206

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-634.

**Decided:** June 15, 1998.

##### Title II covers state prisons and their programs

**Holding:** A state correctional department is a public entity under 42 U.S.C. §12131, and its prison services, programs and activities fall within §12132. Custody supplies no categorical exception: a qualified individual may not, by reason of disability, be excluded from participation, denied the benefits of those services, programs or activities, or otherwise subjected to discrimination by the public entity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Material application:** The state correctional department falls within the public-entity definition, and its boot camp is a program offering a benefit. The statutory text supplies clarity even assuming Gregory applies; custody does not create an unstated exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage does not establish liability. A qualified individual must meet essential eligibility requirements, with or without reasonable modifications to rules, policies or practices, removal of architectural, communication or transportation barriers, or auxiliary aids and services. The Court decides neither that Yeskey has a statutory disability nor that he meets those requirements. It requires no particular modification or fundamental alteration and displaces no legitimate security or direct-threat defense. The question whether a cause of action or particular relief is available, the constitutional scope of congressional power, abrogation, sovereign immunity and damages are not decided. Those distinct questions cannot be resolved by treating the institution as outside the statutory definition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit’s reversal of the categorical dismissal stands. The case returns to the ordinary lower-court proceedings for unresolved matters. No finding of disability, qualification, unlawful exclusion, damages liability or entitlement to early release is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Gregory v. Ashcroft:** Assuming its clear-statement principle applies, the comprehensive public-entity definition satisfies it. The Court neither displaces that principle nor requires a prison-specific list.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Its limits on Congress’s Fourteenth Amendment enforcement power remain intact; statutory coverage alone determines no application’s constitutional validity.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida:** Its actual statutory and remedy holdings remain distinct from the coverage question; no controlling direct-State amenability rule is added.
- **Pennsylvania v. Union Gas Co.:** Its existing force is undisturbed; the Court does not decide abrogation or immunity here.

## Selected opening holding: Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S. 584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. ## Event
**Cedar Rapids Community School District v. Garret F., No. 96-1793.** Argued November 4, 1998; decided March 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 106 F.3d 822. The question is whether IDEA's related-services duty includes necessary continuous school-day nursing that does not require a physician.

Garret needs trained assistance within hearing distance to attend and benefit from school. His mental ability is unaffected, and his eligibility is not contested. The ALJ's formal award reimbursed 1993-1994 care; the discussion also identified the district's continuing responsibility.

## Holdings
#### Necessary nonphysician school-health services are covered despite continuity and cost

**Operative rule.** IDEA requires qualified nonphysician school-health services necessary during the school day for an eligible child to attend and benefit from special education, even when the required availability is continuous and the service is costly. The medical-services exclusion covers services requiring a physician, except diagnosis and evaluation; educational necessity and lawful qualifications remain required, and care appropriately furnished outside school hours need not be supplied at school.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins control the service rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation.** Tatro reads the same medical-services term consistently in its inclusion of diagnosis and evaluation and its exclusion of other physician services. The regulations separately include school-health services furnished by qualified nurses or other qualified people. The district's objection that the regulation defines only included physician services does not undo Tatro's statutory construction or the express nursing category.

Supportive services need not themselves teach a lesson. Garret can attend and benefit from his educational program if the necessary routine and emergency assistance is available; none of the disputed tasks requires a physician. Constant availability is not constant administration of each procedure, but coverage does not depend on understating the actual work or cost.

Tatro's account of why Congress could exclude costly physician care does not enact an additional local affordability, complexity, continuity or existing-staff test for covered nursing. The Anderson letter reports conflicting judicial decisions and reserves individual determination to the IEP team; it does not adopt every court's multifactor exclusion. Auer requires the responsible agency's fair, considered interpretation consistent with the text, not deference based on an amicus signature alone. The actual agency treatment retains individual necessity and the outside-school-hours qualification.

The rule governs Garret's IEP while its qualifying conditions remain satisfied. It does not require experimental treatment, services unrelated to educational access or physician care outside diagnosis/evaluation. Nor does it select a provider, override licensing rules or fix additional reimbursement. The independent Iowa-law ground is unnecessary to this federal holding.

#### The existing statutory service obligation supplies clear funding notice

**Operative rule.** The express IDEA related-services duty, the qualified school-health regulation and Tatro's established construction provide clear notice of the covered nonphysician service obligation at issue here. Continuous or substantial need does not by itself make that specified condition an unannounced funding obligation.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six direct joins control this independently stated answer to the Spending Clause objection. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejecting the objection without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennhurst and New York preserve the requirement that funding conditions be stated clearly. Tatro and the school-health regulation preceded the school-year expenses at issue. They identify the service category and retain educational necessity, rather than promising all medically useful care under a general educational purpose.

Suter, Franklin, Blessing and Gebser address distinct rights, enforcement or remedial questions; they neither abolish clear notice nor create categorical immunity from specific service conditions. This case uses IDEA's existing individual service and review framework. It does not imply a new section 1983 right, import Title IX attribution rules or create a general program-adequacy injunction. The district's contrary reading depends on rejecting Tatro's construction; it does not establish that continuity itself negates an otherwise stated condition.

## Separate Writings
**Thomas, joined by Kennedy, dissents.** Intensive continuing respiratory and bodily care can be medical by nature even if supplied by a nurse. The regulation's inclusion of physician diagnostic services does not, in their view, define every excluded service. They would disapprove Tatro's contrary general provider-based construction to that extent rather than treat it as never having addressed the question.

Their statutory reading also gives insufficiently clear notice of this continuing-care obligation as a funding condition. Auer cannot enlarge what they understand Congress to have authorized. They do not adopt an open-ended local cost test, find this district unable to pay, deny Garret's educational benefit or treat the Anderson letter as an express agency adoption of the district's proposal.

The ALJ found the costs manageable under an alternative burden inquiry and separately relied on Iowa law. The dissenters' categorical federal construction would make affordability insufficient for federal coverage, but it would not eliminate that independent state ground. They would remand for its resolution, including the asserted higher-state-standard route through IDEA and the district's contention that Iowa law tracks federal law. They would not order final dismissal or automatically extinguish reimbursement.

## Event

*National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior*, No. 97-1184, together with *Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior*, No. 97-1243. Argued November 9, 1998; decided March 3, 1999. On writs of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, reviewing its October 31, 1997 judgment, 132 F.3d 157, granting the Geological Survey's petition and denying enforcement of the Authority's bargaining order.

During term-contract negotiations, the union proposed that the employer undertake to negotiate on request about negotiable matters not covered by the agreement. The Authority found that the Survey's refusal violated 5 U.S.C. §7116(a)(1) and (5). It relied both on a general statutory midterm duty and on an independent contractual-negotiability ground. The Fourth Circuit rejected compulsory bargaining over the clause under its categorical no-midterm-bargaining interpretation.

The questions are whether the statute establishes a union-initiated midterm bargaining duty over uncovered, unwaived matters, and whether an advance clause creating that obligation is a mandatory bargaining subject in any event. Both questions were presented and supplied administrative grounds. FLRA's decision not to repeat an argument already foreclosed in the Fourth Circuit did not concede that ground. A report of renewed disagreement during renegotiation establishes no settlement, replacement contract or mootness.

## Holdings

### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Operative rule.** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Authority.** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct six-Justice majority for this holding and the holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court must identify the authority Congress granted before deferring to an agency. *MCI* preserves statutory boundaries; *NationsBank* permits reasoned administrative choice where the enacted scheme leaves genuine discretion. Here the definition of a collective bargaining agreement is not expressly limited to the principal term contract. An agreement reached during its term can also fit that definition. Conversely, §7114's direction to negotiate toward an agreement and its reasonable-times and frequency requirements do not unmistakably prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty.

Section 7106 principally allocates authority by subject. Its management-right exceptions do not establish a prohibition on every other midterm proposal. The agency-election condition in §7106(b)(1) is not an election condition in (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105 assigns the Authority responsibility for administering bargaining obligations within those limits. The efficiency instruction in §7101 and the conditional impasse powers in §7119 inform that administration; they do not prove perpetual negotiations or inevitable imposition of this clause. A prediction of disruption cannot replace the statutory inquiry, just as the desirability of responding to new workplace needs cannot establish an unlimited duty.

The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. The Authority must explain the lawful regime; the Court does not select a universal duty or universal exemption, create a new factor checklist, or authorize reopening matters an agreement actually settles. The Court does not determine the treatment of a hypothetical future proposal under the Authority's covered-matter or waiver rules.

### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Operative rule.** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Controlling explanation.** The Authority did give an independent reason: contractual proposals can be negotiable even when their substance is not a statutory entitlement. It also distinguished contract coverage from a categorical denial of midterm bargaining. Those are actual reasons, not an appellate lawyer's invention. But the current order continued to treat the clause as restating a statutory right, and its alternative explanation chiefly answered the circuit's contrary precedent. It did not fully exercise the distinct choice over the relationship among a general duty, an advance clause, contractual repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion was removed.

*NationsBank* requires reasoned exercise of genuine discretion, not an assumption that legal necessity supplies the agency's choice. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* separates administrative implementing power from the validity of particular duties and permits return under the correct legal allocation. *Boston & Maine* requires a discernible agency ground; that requirement is satisfied here, but it does not make every stated alternative sufficient after a governing premise changes. *Good Samaritan* permits considered interpretive change; the Court does not remand merely because the Authority changed its view. This is legal-framework reconsideration, not an evidentiary remand or punishment for inconsistency.

The Authority may adopt the same negotiability result if it explains a permissible construction. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C), excluding matters specifically provided for by federal statute, and 7117(a), preserving federal-law consistency, remain distinct limitations; neither is satisfied merely by statutory silence. The Court does not impose a clause, require agreement or concession, or adjudicate a Panel award. Agreed waiver, a duty to negotiate a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain separate questions; an employer's power to impose a comprehensive waiver is not presumed.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The agreement-directed duty in §7114, the separately structured continuing obligations and §7101's efficiency instruction together supply a statutory boundary. In their view, the broad definition of bargaining and the required frequency of meetings regulate negotiations already required; they do not authorize a comprehensive continuing union-initiated regime. The statute's public impasse system matters because bargaining may lead, after the statutory conditions are satisfied, to a binding lawful resolution. They do not assume that this particular clause will inevitably be imposed.

They accept that the absence of an entitlement ordinarily differs from a prohibition on negotiation. This general advance clause nevertheless recreates the compulsory regime they find inconsistent with the statutory structure and §7117(a). Its reference to negotiable uncovered subjects does not cure that temporal objection. They would affirm the refusal of enforcement while reserving genuinely narrower clauses and voluntary negotiations on particular subjects. They do not treat every statutory omission as a matter specifically settled under §7103(a)(14)(C), and do not revive the dismissed allegations against Interior.

**O'Connor, Part II, alone.** She adds that the Authority's court-induced reversal, continued assumption of a statutory mandate and limited independent explanation do not establish a fully reasoned administrative choice deserving acceptance. *Good Samaritan* permits diminished weight for a changed interpretation but does not automatically disqualify a considered permissible one; her objection concerns the actual reasons and asserted legal setting, not change alone. She would examine the text and structure without assuming that compliance with an earlier judicial interpretation supplies the agency's independent justification. Her statutory ground remains sufficient, and she does not announce a general displacement of *Chevron*. Scalia and Thomas do not join this additional ground.

## Event

United States v. Haggar Apparel Co., No. 97-2044. Argued January 11, 1999; decided April 21, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit, 127 F.3d 1460. The questions are whether the promulgated Customs regulation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation and whether its exclusion of permapressing reasonably implements the statutory allowance for operations incidental to assembly. The dispute concerns baking resin-treated fabric after assembly abroad to activate permanent-press characteristics.

## Holdings

### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Ginsburg, agrees with Parts I–III but would reverse outright. Chevron and NationsBank permit the agency to distinguish assembly and ordinary finishing from significant chemical treatment that imparts a new characteristic. In their view the actual ovenbaking activates a lasting permanent-press property and reasonably falls on the excluded side of that line. They preserve the regulation's introductory terms and do not treat every chemical effect or use of heat as disqualifying. Because review includes reasonableness and the supported process facts establish the application, they see no remaining reason to return validity or the challenged denial for decision. They would sustain that denial without calculating a new duty amount or deciding unrelated shipment issues.

### Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156

Merits decision, June 25, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 30, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the First Circuit, 107 F.3d 934, affirming summary judgment for Abbott. Bragdon refused routine office dental treatment after Abbott disclosed HIV on September 16, 1994 and offered hospital treatment carrying an additional facility charge.

The granted questions concern Abbott's disability under the ADA and the legal treatment of a provider's judgment under the direct-threat exception. The separate independent-review and whole-record trial questions were not granted. State-law claims are outside the federal grant.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |

#### Opinion Topology

O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas accept or assume the undisputed impairment premise; they do not join the affirmative general impairment rationale. Stevens and Breyer's affirmance preference is not an opposing remand vote.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. HIV can impair before visible symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity

**Holding and operative rule:** HIV disease is a physical impairment within the ADA before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. A claimant under the actual-disability branch must still show that the impairment substantially limits that individual's activity; diagnosis or reproductive-system impairment alone does not establish every element.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the disability part. The other three accept or assume impairment for this case without joining its general rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 12102(2)(A) separates impairment, major activity and individual substantial limitation. The Title III regulation, §36.104, expressly includes symptomatic and asymptomatic HIV and recognizes physiological disorders of relevant systems. Section 12201(a), except where the ADA otherwise provides, preserves at least Rehabilitation Act Title V standards and regulations. Arline's contagious-disease analysis does not turn its reservation about asymptomatic carriers into an exclusion from this statute.

The illustrative activity list is not confined to publicly performed, economic or daily activities. Reproduction's significance is not diminished because it is not continuously performed. The contrary reading would convert examples into a restriction the text does not impose. At the same time, a reproductive-system impairment and a substantial limitation of reproduction are separate inquiries. Yeskey resolves institutional coverage at its own scope and supplies no automatic individual entitlement here. No record-of or regarded-as holding is made.

##### 2. Abbott's own reproductive activity is substantially limited on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Abbott's specific unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision not to bear a child, considered with the supported perinatal transmission danger, establishes substantial limitation of her reproduction. Complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, an attempted pregnancy or willingness to accept the danger is not an additional condition.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice disability coalition directly adopts this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The reported risks were approximately 25 percent without treatment and 8 percent with treatment. Even the treated figure represents a substantial danger to the child on this historical record. These figures are evidence here, not a legal threshold or a conclusion about current treatment. The Court therefore need not resolve whether mitigating measures generally must be disregarded.

The general answer denying impaired life functions describes daily functioning and does not retract the specific childbearing account. The opposing argument correctly demands a limitation in this person's life, rather than an abstract medical risk. Abbott supplies that connection. Unknown prior plans do not defeat her specific unchallenged testimony or justify resolving credibility against her. Ordinary Rule 56 summary-judgment burdens remain; no automatic rule covers every person with HIV, every activity or every future medical condition.

##### 3. Direct threat requires objective individualized assessment, not professional sincerity

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered provider may exclude a person only for a significant risk to others' health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment based on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing the risk's nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation; sincere belief or professional status supplies no special legal deference.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly support this common objective method.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 12182(b)(3) and 36.208 distinguish significant risk from any imaginable residual risk. They require neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission as a universal prerequisite. The relevant standpoint is information reasonably available to the provider and profession when treatment was refused, September 16, 1994. Arline supports individual medical assessment but gives no private provider a subjective immunity. Daubert and Joiner require reliable foundation and fit; they do not decide the substantive dental risk or impose numerical quantification in every case.

The hospital offer creates no independent defense. The lower court reports that no comparative-hospital-safety theory was pursued or established, and the offer alone proves no privileges or safeguards. Section 12201(d) does not require Abbott to accept that accommodation or charge. The Court does not require service outside professional competence or bar precautions justified by objective evidence in another case. No later breakthrough overwhelmingly vindicating the concern is established; the possible relevance of such a development to prospective relief is not categorically foreclosed.

##### 4. Public-health authority has founded, nonconclusive weight; the cited sources require bounded reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** Reasonable medical judgments of public-health authorities receive special but nonconclusive weight, subject to a credible scientific challenge. That weight attaches only to a supported judgment within the source's actual role, date and scope; a private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment. The direct-threat judgment is vacated for reconsideration of the CDC and Association materials on those terms, without deciding whole-record triability.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the public-health/source-remand part. Stone-Zsela joins the objective method and remand judgment, not this distinct ground; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas support the risk judgment on their contrary official-weight approach.

**Controlling explanation:** Arline supports respect for reasonable public-health judgment while allowing supported scientific disagreement. The First Circuit largely used the correct objective method, rejected conclusive official consensus, permitted qualified contrary evidence and considered all eight proffers together. Its continuing rebuttable presumption was not an irrebuttable command. The Court does not invalidate every such presumption or find that cumulative review was omitted.

The CDC's 1993 recommendation says universal precautions reduce risk; it does not expressly assess the remaining risk's level or say further measures are unnecessary. The circuit drew those inferences. Their foundation must be considered before official authority is treated as resolving the defense. The Association's 1991 policy affirmatively describes safe office care, but it is a private professional policy whose scientific and professional-duty bases must be distinguished. The record does not establish that it was merely ethical, scientifically worthless or incapable of an implicit medical judgment. These source questions can affect the granted deference issue without deciding whether all evidence already requires trial.

The remand requires ordinary reconsideration of the existing sources and other evidence, without requiring reconstruction of a policy history, automatic supplementation or a new expert proceeding. It establishes neither zero risk nor significant risk. Personal sharp-injury observations and a scientific risk inference are distinct; the Court makes no finding that competent firsthand testimony was actually excluded. The lower court may again find summary judgment appropriate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Arline:** Supplies individualized assessment and founded respect for public-health judgment; neither its carrier reservation nor private-physician reservation creates an exclusion or subjective defense.
- **Yeskey:** Institutional coverage does not resolve this individual's disability; its reserved question remains distinct.
- **Daubert and Joiner:** Reliable foundation, fit and supported inference remain separate from substantive risk and whole-record triability. No automatic exclusion, expert appointment or numerical threshold follows.
- **Ordinary summary-judgment and question-scope rules:** Supported inferences and actual individual proof remain required; the granted method question does not itself enlarge review to the separately ungranted trial issue.

#### Law After Decision

Abbott is disabled on the established reproduction record. The general rule recognizes impairment before visible symptoms without treating every HIV diagnosis as complete statutory disability. The direct-threat inquiry remains individualized and objective, with significant risk and reasonable mitigation assessed at the relevant time. Founded public-health judgments receive special but rebuttable weight; a private policy and an inferred official conclusion require their actual foundation. Ultimate risk, whole-record triability, other disability routes and state-law merits remain unresolved.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the disability and common objective-method holdings and supports vacatur for assessment of contemporaneous objective evidence and reasonable precautions. Professional sincerity cannot replace that support, but the ADA permits refusal when a significant risk that reasonable measures cannot eliminate is proved. He does not adopt the separate public-health special-weight or specific source-foundation rationale, decide ultimate risk, require treatment outside professional competence or declare every person with HIV disabled.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, concurring.** They would prefer affirmance because the lower court applied the objective approach and thoroughly considered the evidence, including its cumulative force. They nevertheless join the entire Court opinion and the bounded source reconsideration, which leaves the lower court free to reach the same result. This is agreement with the remand entered, not an opposing vote or a trial mandate.

**Ginsburg, concurring.** She joins the cautious, informed remand and the complete disability analysis. HIV can affect family relations, employment potential, self-care and access to care before visible symptoms; invisibility does not justify discrimination. Those broader observations establish no additional individual finding, alternative regarded-as holding or automatic diagnosis-based rule.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting on disability.** They assume undisputed impairment but reject reproduction as the presented statutory activity: the illustrative functions concern repeated ordinary functioning, and naming reproductive-system disorders does not itself answer the separate activity question. Independently, even assuming classification, Abbott's post-diagnosis choice, unknown prior plans and general negative answer do not establish a present individual limitation to their satisfaction. They distinguish a risk affecting choice from demonstrated present loss and do not adopt utter inability as the standard. They would reject this actual-disability determination, without deciding every alternative claim or ordering a trial under the classification they reject.

**O'Connor, separately dissenting on disability.** Childbirth is not generally comparable to the representative recurring activities. She leaves open substantial limitation if reproduction were recognized and other intimate or family activities. Accepting impairment for this case is not an affirmative general impairment rationale or a finding that HIV is harmless.

**Scalia's risk-method part, joined by O'Connor and Thomas.** All three agree with objective, contemporaneous evidence and risk vacatur but reject special scientific weight from official endorsement in litigation between private parties. Credentials, foundation, fit and reasoning should stand on their own; review of an agency's own entrusted action differs. They view the circuit's continuing official presumption as an error requiring reconsideration, not merely a source-identification problem. Possible dental transmissions may have probative value without confirmation, and other occupational evidence cannot be rejected solely because its occupational name differs; actual exposure conditions and the link to this procedure still matter. The seven dental cases are possible, not confirmed, and the forty-two occupational cases are not dental cases. Their more favorable assessment of this evidence supplies no independent whole-record trial holding, proved significant-risk finding or mandated trial.

The complete lower opinion, 107 F.3d 934, supplies the cross-motion posture, specific and general testimony, actual continuing presumption, eight proffers and cumulative review. The relevant federal provisions are 42 U.S.C. §§12102(2), 12181(7)(F), 12182(b)(3), 12201(a), (d), and the contemporaneous Title III regulations, 28 C.F.R. §§36.104 and 36.208. The CDC recommendation, Association policy and court’s inference are distinct. Later categorical statements or later study descriptions do not become September 1994 medical knowledge. The historical risk percentages are evidence in this record, not contemporary medical advice or universal legal thresholds.

The disability determination is affirmed. The direct-threat portion is vacated and returned through the First Circuit for reconsideration under the objective method and controlling source distinctions. The lower court determines the consequence for the judgment without a commanded trial, new evidence, hearing or treatment. State-law claims remain subject to ordinary lower proceedings without a new substantive state-law ruling.

### Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, No. 97-634

Decision, June 15, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 28, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit. The question is whether Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act covers state prisons and their programs.

Yeskey, sentenced to 18 to 36 months, was recommended for a motivational boot camp whose successful completion could permit parole after six months. He alleges that admission was refused because of his history of hypertension. The District Court dismissed on the ground that the ADA did not apply to state inmates; the Third Circuit reversed that categorical ruling.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The categorical exclusion of state prisons cannot support dismissal. The remaining issues continue in the lower courts; the Court orders neither admission to the program nor early release.

#### Opinions and Joins

Justice O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join the statutory coverage rule, its application to the categorical dismissal, the reservations and affirmance.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Title II covers state prisons and their programs

**Controlling proposition:** A state correctional department is a public entity under 42 U.S.C. §12131, and its prison services, programs and activities fall within §12132. Custody supplies no categorical exception: a qualified individual may not, by reason of disability, be excluded from participation, denied the benefits of those services, programs or activities, or otherwise subjected to discrimination by the public entity.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the complete statutory rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 12131 includes state and local governments and their departments, agencies, special-purpose districts and other instrumentalities. A state correctional department falls within that definition. A prison’s boot-camp opportunity is a program offering a benefit; its custodial setting does not make that description inapplicable. Section 12132 contains no exception for involuntary custody.

Even assuming Gregory’s clear-statement principle applies, the enacted definition and program language supply the required clarity. Gregory addressed an ambiguous express employee exception, not a rule that Congress must list each covered institution by name. Neither the importance of prison administration nor an assertion that Congress failed to anticipate this application authorizes adding an exception to comprehensive text.

Coverage does not establish liability. A qualified individual must meet essential eligibility requirements, with or without reasonable modifications to rules, policies or practices, removal of architectural, communication or transportation barriers, or auxiliary aids and services. The Court decides neither that Yeskey has a statutory disability nor that he meets those requirements. It requires no particular modification or fundamental alteration and displaces no legitimate security or direct-threat defense. The question whether a cause of action or particular relief is available, the constitutional scope of congressional power, abrogation, sovereign immunity and damages are not decided. Those distinct questions cannot be resolved by treating the institution as outside the statutory definition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Gregory v. Ashcroft:** Assuming its clear-statement principle applies, the comprehensive public-entity definition satisfies it. The Court neither displaces that principle nor requires a prison-specific list.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Its limits on Congress’s Fourteenth Amendment enforcement power remain intact; statutory coverage alone determines no application’s constitutional validity.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida:** Its actual statutory and remedy holdings remain distinct from the coverage question; no controlling direct-State amenability rule is added.
- **Pennsylvania v. Union Gas Co.:** Its existing force is undisturbed; the Court does not decide abrogation or immunity here.

#### Law After Decision

Title II has no categorical state-prison exception. Its ordinary disability, qualification and discrimination requirements remain operative, and its application must respect otherwise governing defenses and remedial rules. The coverage holding determines no constitutional-power or sovereign-immunity question.

#### Source Notes

The reported record at [524 U.S. 206](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-206.pdf) supplies the sentence, boot-camp recommendation, alleged hypertension exclusion and lower-court posture. Sections 12131 and 12132 supply the definitions and operative prohibition. The alleged medical exclusion is not a finding that every statutory element is satisfied.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Third Circuit’s reversal of the categorical dismissal stands. The case returns to the ordinary lower-court proceedings for unresolved matters. No finding of disability, qualification, unlawful exclusion, damages liability or entitlement to early release is made.

**End of entry: Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, Decision, June 15, 1998.**

### Heller v. Doe, No. 92-351

Merits disposition, 1993-06-24

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued 1993-03-22; decided 1993-06-24. On writ of certiorari to Sixth Circuit, Doe v. Cowherd, 965 F.2d 109 (1992).

**Question:** Whether Kentucky’s different burdens of proof and family-participation rules for involuntary commitment of persons with intellectual disability and persons with mental illness violate equal protection, and whether family party status violates procedural due process.

**Material record:** Kentucky required proof of dangerousness, likely benefit from treatment, and a least restrictive placement predicate in the relevant commitment proceedings. For the category described in the then-operative statute as “mental retardation,” proof was clear and convincing and guardians or immediate family could participate as parties. For mental illness, proof was beyond a reasonable doubt and that party status was not supplied. The class challenged these differences. The medical and developmental distinctions offered as rationales concern typical onset, diagnostic history, prediction, and the information held by families; they are not individual findings that any class member is dangerous or incompetent.

**Relief sought:** Kentucky seeks reversal of the challenged federal rulings. The committed class seeks affirmance. Other district-court grounds outside the questions under review remain separate.

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

Affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. Unequal proof burdens invalidated, 5–4; categorical invalidation of family-party participation reversed, 6–3.

| Component | Disposition | Supporting Justices | Opposing or other positions | Remedy / scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unequal proof burdens | Affirm invalidation; remand for limited remedy (5–4) | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter | White, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Determine severability and lawful state choices to equalize safeguards; no national reasonable-doubt rule or blanket release. |
| Family/guardian party status: equal protection and categorical due-process invalidation | Reverse categorical invalidation (6–3) | Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Blackmun, Stevens, Souter | Party participation may continue with counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. Blackmun, Stevens and Souter would invalidate on equal protection and do not reach the independent due-process question. |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing / author | Joined by | Exact scope | Judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Souter | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor | Opinion of the Court on unequal proof burdens and limited remedial consequence. | Affirm proof invalidation |
| Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Opinion of the Court on rational family-party distinction and procedural due process; separate treatment from proof burdens. | Reverse participation invalidation |
| Kennedy | White, Scalia, Thomas | Dissent on proof burdens: diagnostic history, prediction and treatment differences each justify the different standards. | Would reverse proof invalidation |

| Blackmun | No other joiners | Separate dissent on participation, also concurring in the proof judgment: preserves preference for heightened review of disability classifications; that tier is not adopted. | Supports proof invalidation and would invalidate participation |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Controlling proposition:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory distinction must relate to the particular procedural disadvantage. Cleburne permits relevant distinctions between conditions but does not allow a label to substitute for that relationship. A longer history may make diagnosis or some predictions easier. Commitment, however, also requires dangerousness, likely treatment benefit and a least restrictive placement; the interests at stake remain physical liberty and protection from erroneous confinement. Addington explains the allocation of that error risk and supplies a constitutional minimum, not immunity from an equality challenge when a State provides greater protection to another class. The asserted difference in the intrusiveness of treatment likewise does not adequately explain the lower overall safeguard against confinement. Beach does not demand formal findings or perfect empirical fit, and the Court imposes neither requirement. It rejects the proffered relationship, not legislative generalization as such. The appropriate limited remedy must respect severability and the State’s lawful choices, while every continuing confinement still needs an independent lawful basis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S.432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S.418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Controlling proposition:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that gathering useful information is different from assigning a person less protection against erroneous confinement. A developmental condition and sustained family involvement plausibly give relatives and guardians knowledge of abilities, daily functioning and available care that assists the proceeding. Parham recognizes the value of family information without giving a relative authority to decide whether an adult should be confined. Cleburne requires a rational relationship, and that informational relationship supports the participation distinction here. Kentucky need not show that every family has useful knowledge or that no less extensive procedure could work. Nor does the existence of mental illness within some families eliminate the permissible general distinction. The Court does not presume that family members always share the adult’s interests. Their preferences or convenience establish none of the statutory grounds for confinement, and the independent adjudicator must evaluate their evidence under the lawful burden. This holding sustains party participation as a category, not every application of it.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S.432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S.584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S.221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Controlling proposition:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged party status must be examined within the actual procedural safeguards. Mathews requires consideration of the private interest, the risk of error and value of safeguards, and the government’s interests. The liberty interest is substantial. Kentucky nevertheless provides counsel, professional examination, an opportunity to testify and present evidence, cross-examination and independent adjudication. Relevant family information can improve accuracy when tested through those procedures. The possibility of adverse family interests requires such testing; it does not establish that adding a party inherently makes an accurate decision impossible. Parham does not permit the State to call an adult’s involuntary placement voluntary merely because a relative requested it. The adjudicator must find the statutory grounds under the lawful burden, regardless of the family’s preference. Proceedings that defeat effective contest or independent decisionmaking remain challengeable. The Court reverses the categorical due-process invalidation without determining that every class member’s hearing was fair.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S.319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S.584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.

#### Current-Law Effect

Effective June 24, the proof distinction is invalid under rational-basis review, with severability and lawful equalization left for remand. The family distinction and party status survive the categorical challenges subject to independent adjudication and effective contest. Addington, the substantive confinement predicates and the unresolved heightened-scrutiny question remain distinct. Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule and other unreviewed grounds are not decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy, joined by White, Scalia and Thomas, would sustain the proof distinction. They regard easier diagnosis, a longer behavioral history supporting prediction, and generally less intrusive treatment as independently sufficient rational grounds. Addington’s minimum is met, and ordinary review permits imperfect generalizations. Souter, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, would also invalidate family-party status: useful information can be obtained through witnesses, while party status adds an adversary able to call and cross-examine witnesses and appeal a refusal to confine. They find no rational connection between that additional burden and the different condition and therefore do not reach due process. Blackmun separately preserves his view that disability classifications and substantial restraints on liberty warrant heightened review, while accepting that the proof distinction fails the ordinary standard used here.
