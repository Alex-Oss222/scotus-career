### Calderon v. Coleman — 1998-12-14

Effective December 14, 1998, the properly available constitutional commutation-instruction claim affecting this jury's capital selection is governed by the State's duty to establish noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record. The protected-silence and personal-intent categories remain governed by their existing Chapman rules; no universal rule for every other constitutional-habeas category is adopted.

Error identification, actual effect, the governing burden and final relief remain separate. A substantive lower-court assessment can suffice without prescribed words, but the judgment here requires renewed actual-effect review. No present harmlessness or ultimate sentence-relief finding follows. The defined pending-capital temporal rule remains unchanged, as do genuinely independent habeas conditions and the distinction between constitutional harmlessness and authorized state resentencing.

### Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee; No. 97-1252; Ninth Circuit judgment, 119 F.3d 1367. — 1999-02-24

#### Holdings

##### I-A. The withdrawal reaches the named acts in these pending proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** From April 1, 1997, section 1252(g) reaches earlier, pending and future proceedings, and its named-action withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General's identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, rather than every legal challenge to completed adjudication. This selection challenge attacks commencement and the requested restraint of adjudication; neither its constitutional label nor its possible attachment to a later final-order petition moves it outside the named actions, and continuing proceedings is not a fourth statutory act.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight direct joins. The same eight-Justice coalition controls Parts I-B, II and III-A below. Stone-Zsela joins no part of the opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*, which distinguishes commencement of the new rule from the proceedings it reaches, and *Reyes-Hernandez*, which separates the named executive actions from specified legal challenges to completed adjudication. This suit seeks to restrain the former. Those decisions preserved the additional constitutional-access question; they did not create a general exemption for old proceedings or constitutional claims.

Corrected section 309(c)(1) generally saves the former procedure, subject to its exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of the new procedure only before the evidentiary hearing has begun and on at least thirty days' notice; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. Paragraph (4)'s transitional review rules require a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996. No election or final order is established here. The permanent scheme, including section 1252(b)(9), cannot be imported wholesale.

As *Salazar-Haro* and *Kolster* explain, section 306(c)(2) protects an actual judgment or order entered under former section 106, as amended, against paragraph (1)'s invalidation or compelled reconsideration. It grants no jurisdiction, reopens no merits review and does not bar ordinary review of a judgment's correctness. These collateral injunctions are not automatically qualifying section 106 judgments. The express transitions therefore defeat a blanket pending-case exemption without resolving the distinct question in Part III-B.

##### I-B. The individual-relief exception does not grant jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1252(f)(1)'s exception for application of the designated provisions to an individual alien against whom proceedings have been initiated limits that subsection's prohibition on lower-court injunctive relief; it does not independently confer subject-matter jurisdiction. Section 1252(f)(2)'s clear-and-convincing-evidence condition concerns enjoining removal pursuant to a final order and does not govern this case, which presents no such order.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I-B; the eight-Justice coalition stated in Part I-A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court rejects the Ninth Circuit's reliance on subsection (f) as a jurisdictional answer. An exception to a restriction on remedies does not establish the underlying power to hear a claim. The availability of individual relief may inform the surrounding statutory construction, but the original jurisdiction retained in Part III-B rests on section 1331 and the constitutional action against federal officers. Subsection (f)(2) addresses a different remedy at a different stage; its condition cannot be substituted for subsection (f)(1) or applied as though a final deportation order already existed. No categorical conclusion about every possible injunction application is necessary.

##### II. The proposed factual transfer does not make this final-order channel adequate

**Holding and operative rule:** Former section 1105a(a)'s incorporation of chapter 158 for final-deportation-order review does not authorize the proposed district-court development of this selection claim's necessary extra-record evidence: paragraph (4)'s administrative-record restriction, subject to the nationality-transfer exception in (5)(B), and section 2347's hearing conditions must both be satisfied. A required deportation hearing cannot become an action taken without a required hearing merely by treating the earlier charging decision as the action reviewed while using the final order as the jurisdictional trigger.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II; the eight-Justice coalition stated in Part I-A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the proposed route lacks the statutory capability attributed to it. Section 2347(a) uses the agency record when a hearing was held. Subsection (b) first requires that no hearing preceded the action reviewed. It then requires agency remand if a hearing was legally required; decision on pleadings and affidavits if none was required and no genuine material factual dispute exists; or district-court transfer if none was required and such a dispute exists. These conditions are not a general permission to develop any missing record.

The final deportation order follows a required hearing. The immigration judges and Board cannot adjudicate selection, but their inability does not erase the separate record-only command. The express nationality mechanism confirms that a general extra-record transfer cannot simply be assumed. Nor can the developed district-court record be imported merely because it exists.

Subsection (c) separately permits additional agency evidence upon materiality and reasonable grounds for the earlier omission. Any permanent or transitional prohibition of that subsection requires its own trigger; excluding (c) does not make (b)(3) available. The parties' accounts of a deleted Senate proposal are not adopted as legislative facts. The Government's proposed screening, explanation and material-dispute procedure, Rule 48, or a comparable future procedure cannot create missing authority. Its advocacy is neither a jurisdiction-conferring stipulation nor a concession that every selection claim constitutionally requires a forum.

This ruling concerns the necessary selection evidence, not every constitutional legal argument cognizable on a proper administrative record or a future constitutional remedy. *Kolster* preserves conditional section 2241 authority against AEDPA section 440(a); it does not decide subsection (g), establish custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion, filing, factual-review scope, stay or relief here. No new discovery or trial is required merely because the present statutory mechanism fails.

##### III-A. Access, statutory defenses, constitutional merits and interim protection are distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A substantial constitutional selection claim cannot be rejected merely because the actual immigration charges may be lawful or a statutory defense may avert deportation; those questions do not necessarily adjudicate selection, permit its necessary proof or repair an independently supported present injury. The developed preliminary findings make this access contention substantial, but establish neither ultimate discrimination, a general right to immediate review nor an automatic entitlement to stop the proceedings.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III-A; the eight-Justice coalition stated in Part I-A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes a forum from success there. *Thunder Basin* permits constitutional claims to follow an administrative route when actual judicial review is meaningful, despite the agency's inability finally to decide constitutional law. *McNary* identifies an unavailable factual record as a real barrier; its separate surrender-to-obtain-review obstacle differs from proceedings already pending here. *Catholic Social Services* requires an actual obstacle, not a collateral label. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* concerned an announcement imposing no present conduct constraint and leaving concrete statutory review; this case presents instituted proceedings, preliminary injury findings and an identified factual-review barrier. *Your Home* presented no distinct colorable constitutional claim and does not decide this access question. *Yoshina* separates a substantial maintained claim from its ultimate failure, without supplying any individual's surviving controversy here.

Ordinary defense costs, anxiety and undifferentiated chill do not justify stopping adjudication. *Hollywood Motor Car* distinguishes a dismissal remedy from a right never to undergo proceedings, but already had a trial court capable of hearing the constitutional motion. *Younger* and *Dombrowski* distinguish good-faith process from sufficiently supported continuing suppression; their state-federal comity rationale does not independently bar this federal action. *Oestereich*'s unequivocal exemption is absent; its broader timing concurrence is not controlling.

The preliminary association-based selection, disparate-treatment and present-chill findings are more than pleadings, but do not resolve the disputed conduct and immigration arguments. *Whren* separates objective grounds from forbidden selection; *Dawson* distinguishes abstract association from relevant evidence; *Armstrong*'s indirect racial-selection discovery rule does not decide this developed association claim's complete merits. *R.A.V.* controls on overbreadth, not its separate content-selection proposal. A successful statutory defense may leave no adverse final order without deciding retaliation; that possibility proves neither a future controversy nor entitlement to an advisory declaration. The selection merits, universal citizen-alien equivalence and immigration immunity remain undecided.

##### III-B. A confined construction preserves a capable constitutional forum

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1252(g) does not foreclose existing section 1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim when a claimant has a concrete personal controversy, the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate that claim, and the applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive the factual record necessary to decide it. A constitutional or collateral label, disagreement with enforcement discretion, or preference for earlier review is insufficient; this retained access does not itself entitle the claimant to an injunction.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five direct joins. The same five-Justice coalition controls Part IV. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this construction; Stone-Zsela also rejects present collateral jurisdiction. No narrowest-ground combination of the competing constructions is involved.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress has not unmistakably eliminated every capable forum for this substantial constitutional claim. *Webster* requires clear intent before reading a statutory restriction to exclude consideration of colorable constitutional objections. *Lincoln* preserves constitutional review separately from discretionary statutory administration. The actual-barrier reasoning of *McNary* and *Catholic Social Services*, and *Kolster*'s requirement that a retained route reach the substantial authority objection and effective relief, reinforce that distinction without automatically extending their different statutory holdings.

The contrary textual argument is substantial: this suit attacks the very actions subsection (g) names, and its notwithstanding clause and reference to any cause or claim are broad. The Court does not redescribe selection as a completed-adjudication claim. It construes the withdrawal narrowly only at the distinct point of otherwise unavailable meaningful constitutional adjudication. The statute's treatment of constitutional questions elsewhere and contemplated individual relief supply context, not jurisdiction or wholesale applicability of the permanent scheme. Section 1331 and the existing constitutional action against federal officers supply the original basis.

Here the agency cannot adjudicate selection, the applicable judicial record limitation excludes its necessary factual record, and the proposed transfer does not cure that defect. The preliminary findings of present association-based coercion make postponing even access to the only competent forum inappropriate merely because a statutory defense might succeed. The Court does not adopt every lower merits ground or treat every First Amendment injury as automatically irreparable. *Reyes-Hernandez* retains its named-action boundary; this decision answers its reserved constitutional-access issue at the stated narrow scope. Ordinary channeling remains where a capable route exists. Whether the Constitution would itself require the same access absent a permissible construction, or where an adequate later route actually exists, remains open.

##### IV. Retained jurisdiction requires separate reconsideration of each injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** The confined jurisdictional result is affirmed, but the appellate judgment's unqualified preservation of preliminary relief is set aside for individualized reconsideration of continuing controversy, entitlement and scope; the existing District Court orders remain temporarily effective, without enlargement, pending lower-court action on remand. The Six's refusal-to-dissolve posture and the Two's later initial injunction must be analyzed separately, and individual relief must be modified or dissolved if its continuing requirements are absent.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV; the five-Justice coalition stated in Part III-B.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court preserves the existing orders during reconsideration because constitutional jurisdiction remains and the record contains preliminary findings of present injury. This is a bounded interim consequence, not final approval of those findings or automatic relief from a jurisdictional label. *Webster* preserves ordinary equitable limits; *Catholic Social Services* requires relief fitted to concrete claimants and barriers.

The January 5, 1994 order protects the Six against further deportation proceedings. The Government's later resubmission of already available evidence does not alone reopen every preliminary issue as an initial application. But new statutory law, status changes, continuing controversy, actual ongoing injury and the need to restrain proceedings rather than afford narrower protection require consideration. The April 1996 order separately grants the Two's renewed initial application; their own showing, disputed conduct and immigration grounds must support that relief. The Six's modification posture cannot supply the Two's missing predicates.

Hamide and Shehadeh are permanent residents facing association/material-support charges; the Six's original grounds concern status violations. Barakat and Sharif obtained temporary resident status and cannot be deported on their original status grounds. That does not establish permanent residence, universal immunity or a new charge. Respondents' view that the injunction protects against new charges and their allegation of no governmental disavowal establish neither a particular charge nor an imminent individual threat. The lower courts must identify any actual surviving controversy; temporary preservation supplies no adjudicated protection against hypothetical charges.

The remand permits narrowing, modification or dissolution on the supported record. It does not require new discovery, a hearing, permanent protection, a fixed deadline, a removal order or a scheduled return here. Neither statutory deportability, specific intent, final discrimination nor a universal equivalence of citizen and alien rights is decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Salazar-Haro v. INS*, June 16, 1997 — applied: the actual-judgment savings provision grants no jurisdiction, and an unidentified collateral route is not an established adequate substitute.
- *Kolster v. INS*, June 23, 1997 — applied to effective review and the distinct transition provisions; its conditional section 2241 holding is preserved without extending it to subsection (g) or supplying habeas predicates here.
- *Webster v. Doe*, 486 U.S. 592 — its clear-intent requirement supports the confined review-preserving construction; ordinary discovery and equitable limits remain.
- *Lincoln v. Vigil*, May 24, 1993 — its preservation of separate constitutional review informs the distinction between executive discretion and constitutional access, without supplying a general immediate-review rule.
- *McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc.*, 498 U.S. 479 — its inaccessible-record reasoning informs the present barrier; its individual-determination text and surrender-to-obtain-review obstacle are distinguished.
- *Reno v. Catholic Social Services*, June 18, 1993 — its actual-barrier requirement and individualized remedial limits are applied by analogy; a collateral label remains insufficient.
- *Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich*, January 19, 1994 — preserved: meaningful review through an actual adequate channel remains permissible despite agency inability finally to decide constitutional law; the Mine Act's particular safeguards are not universal mandatory elements.

#### Law After Decision

Effective February 24, 1999, the named-action withdrawal and old-proceeding transitions remain in force. They now operate with the confined section 1331 construction for a concrete, substantial constitutional selection claim whose necessary factual adjudication is unavailable in the actual statutory channel. Agency inability alone, a constitutional label and a preference for immediate review remain insufficient. The proposed section 2347 route cannot supply this missing factual review under the applicable hearing and record provisions; the separate availability of constitutional legal arguments on a proper agency record is left intact.

Neither subsection (g)'s constitutionality nor the selection merits is decided. The two existing injunctions survive only provisionally and without enlargement while the lower courts address their distinct procedural and individual requirements. No habeas rule is added, no immigration status is granted, and no valid substantive deportation ground is immunized. The competing limited constitutional-exclusion inquiry and dismissal position are noncontrolling; neither enlarges the Court's construction.

### Peguero v. United States; No. 97-9217; OT1998. — 1999-03-02

#### Holdings

###### The issued certificate supplied the appellate authorization in this case

**Operative rule.** An authorized judge's actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal; the asserted error in that judge's assessment of section 2253(c)(2)'s constitutional-showing condition does not nullify the authorization for appellate-jurisdiction purposes here. The statutory showing remains a condition for proper issuance, and this decision neither finds it satisfied nor authorizes future certificates for bare rules claims without it.

**Authority.** Scalia's Part I has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with reaching the appeal without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2253(c)(1) requires a judicial certificate before an appeal may be taken. A certificate was issued and identified this Rule 32 claim. Subsection (c)(2) directs the judge's eligibility assessment; an alleged mistake in that assessment does not mean the judicial permission never existed. Section 1254(1) therefore supplies review of the resulting appellate case.

Hohn preserves certificate conditions while distinguishing the judicial proceeding and the appeal. It does not turn every dispute over an issued certificate into an absence of appellate power. The Government's earlier concurrence cannot create jurisdiction, and the Court does not rely on waiver of a jurisdictional objection. It resolves the statutory distinction before reaching the merits.

The rejected ineffective-assistance claim is not recharacterized to supply a constitutional issue. The Court also leaves aside how a timely direct challenge should correct an issued certificate in a different procedural setting. This entire motion and certificate process followed AEDPA; Hohn's separate temporal reservation does no work here.

###### A mandatory warning error does not restore an appeal that the omission did not cause the defendant to lose

**Operative rule.** The sentencing judge must give Rule 32's required advice despite a defendant's independent knowledge. But where actual knowledge and an unchallenged voluntary decision not to appeal establish no lost appeal attributable to the omission, that violation alone does not require section 2255 relief; causal loss is distinct from whether an appeal would probably succeed.

**Authority.** Scalia's Part II has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Hill and Timmreck distinguish a consequential collateral defect from formal noncompliance with a criminal rule. They do not excuse the judge's duty. They require the requested collateral remedy to address the injury for which relief is sought.

Peguero already knew of the appeal right. The unappealed findings establish that he chose cooperation and possible sentence reduction rather than appeal. Repeating the required advice would not supply the opportunity whose loss he asserts. A per se restoration rule would enforce the warning duty symbolically even though this omission caused no appeal loss.

Rodriquez protects a person actually deprived of an appeal without demanding proof that the appeal would succeed. The Court preserves that distinction: causal deprivation is not a meritorious-issue requirement. It does not adopt a universal clear-and-convincing burden for disputed knowledge, a presumption that cooperation waives appeal, or a procedure for future contested cases. No appeal is restored here, so the possible scope of a restored appeal remains undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hohn v. United States:** Applied to distinguish the judicial certificate process and resulting appellate case while preserving statutory issuance conditions; its different temporal reservation is not extended here.
- **Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424; United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780:** Applied to distinguish a consequential collateral injury from formal rule error, without excusing the underlying judicial duty.
- **Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327:** Preserved for actual deprivation of an appeal without a likely-success showing; it does not establish causal deprivation on these contrary findings.
- **Bousley v. United States:** Its separation of statutory meaning, constitutional defect and collateral remedy remains; this decision supplies no new constitutional claim or default exception.

#### Law After Decision

Proper certificate issuance still requires the statutory constitutional showing, but the disputed eligibility assessment does not erase the actual authorization here. Rule 32's judicial warning duty remains mandatory. Section 2255 supplies no automatic restoration for an omission that caused no appeal loss; actual loss and appellate success remain separate inquiries.

### Jones v. United States; No. 97-6203; OT1998. — 1999-03-24

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six Justices directly adopt the statutory construction, its ordinary element protections and the stated remedy. No aggregation of separate constitutional rationales is necessary.

##### I. Serious bodily injury and death define aggravated carjacking offenses

**Holding and operative rule:** The version of 18 U.S.C. § 2119 governing the December 1992 offense creates a basic carjacking offense and aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results. Serious bodily injury is an element of the grade carrying a twenty-five-year maximum, and death is an element of the grade carrying a maximum of life imprisonment; the basic offense carries a fifteen-year maximum.

**Authority:** Part I of the six-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the additional injury and death conditions as facts defining aggravated crimes. The numbered clauses prescribe sharply different limits on lawful punishment and use harm to the victim, a traditional distinction among robbery offenses, to select the applicable grade. The Government emphasizes the complete opening description of firearm carjacking, the common punishment command, dependent clauses and penalty headings. Those features support its reading, but they do not establish that the additional facts merely guide sentencing: aggravated offenses may share the basic offense's elements and add the fact that permits greater punishment. Almendarez-Torres supplies a statutory analogue in which placement within a punishment provision did not dispense with an aggravated element. The fairly available graded-offense reading also avoids serious constitutional doubt about assigning to a judge a current-offense fact indispensable to punishment beyond the charged verdict's authority. Gaudin protects the jury's determination of actual elements; neither it nor Almendarez-Torres already establishes a universal rule classifying every sentencing fact. Monge permits renewed proof of its particular noncapital serious-prior qualification for double-jeopardy purposes and expressly reserves independent charging and jury claims. It does not resolve this statute's construction. The Court decides the original enactment, without adding the later intent-to-cause-serious-harm language or death-penalty amendment.

**Precedent treatment:** Almendarez-Torres is applied as a statutory element-and-charging analogue, not as a general constitutional maximum-fact holding. Gaudin supplies the significance of identifying an actual element. Monge's bounded double-jeopardy classification is preserved, including its reservation of independent charging and jury questions. Holloway's attention to enacted offense conditions is consistent with this reading, but its construction of later statutory language is not imported into the earlier offense.

##### II. The existing indictment and verdict authorize only the basic carjacking range

**Holding and operative rule:** The aggravated injury or death element must be alleged in the federal indictment; absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission does not itself cure an indictment that omits the aggravated element. Because injury was neither charged nor established by Jones's verdict, the judge's later injury finding cannot authorize the aggravated range; the aggravated sentence must be vacated, and sentencing on the existing carjacking indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum.

**Authority:** Part II of the same six-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Hamling requires the federal charge to contain the offense's elements, and Gaudin places their determination beyond a reasonable doubt with the jury. Almendarez-Torres applies those safeguards to a statutory aggravator and holds that an admission to a fact cannot repair its omission from the charge. Here the indictment and general verdict establish only the basic carjacking offense. No valid admission or waiver supplies the missing aggravated charge and verdict. The judge's injury finding therefore cannot perform their work. Witte and Watts permit relevant-conduct findings to select punishment within authority supplied by the conviction; they do not supply additional statutory authority or remove an actual element from the jury. The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's authorization of the injury-based aggravated sentence, vacates that sentence and remands for lawful proceedings. The fifteen-year ceiling concerns this carjacking count on the existing charge and verdict. It neither fixes the sentence at fifteen years nor determines an aggregate sentence. No acquittal, harmless-error finding or automatic entitlement to another aggravated prosecution is entered; any further prosecution must satisfy its own procedural and constitutional requirements. Monge's completed-insufficiency and renewed-proof question is absent.

**Precedent treatment:** Hamling and Gaudin are applied to the aggravated statutory elements. Almendarez-Torres supplies the distinction between admitting a fact and properly charging the aggravated offense. Witte and Watts retain their ordinary within-authority sentencing operation and provide no basis for this increased statutory range. Monge supplies no independent remedy here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court's ground is statutory construction and the protections of the resulting elements. It does not decide a general constitutional rule for all maximum-increasing facts, bare prior-conviction status, mandatory minima within an unchanged maximum, every Guidelines classification or capital sentencing. McMillan's unchanged-maximum holding and Walton's capital rule are not overruled. The death grade is part of the statute's construction, not a finding that Jones caused death.

#### Precedent Treatment

Almendarez-Torres supplies the statutory aggravated-element and charging analogue; its result remains distinct from a universal constitutional rule. Hamling requires a federal indictment stating the offense's elements, and Gaudin requires the jury's determination beyond a reasonable doubt. Monge retains its noncapital serious-prior double-jeopardy classification and its express reservation of independent charging and jury claims; no renewed-proof remedy is drawn from it here. Witte and Watts retain their within-authority sentencing permission and do not authorize punishment beyond the charged conviction's statutory range. Holloway concerns later statutory language and does not amend the law governing this offense. McMillan and Walton are not overruled, and Custis's limits on collateral attack remain undisturbed.

#### Law After Decision

The original section 2119 creates aggravated injury and death offenses whose added elements receive ordinary federal charging and jury-proof protections. The existing basic conviction cannot authorize the injury range. The Court adopts no general constitutional maximum-fact rule, prior-conviction exception, new mandatory-minimum rule or capital-sentencing rule. The distinct constitutional positions in the concurrences retain their stated, noncontrolling scope.

### Mitchell v. United States; No. 97-7541; OT1998. — 1999-04-05

#### Holdings

##### I. The plea did not waive privilege concerning reserved quantity

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty plea does not itself waive the privilege against compelled self-incrimination concerning disputed sentencing facts while testimony creates a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment. This accepted plea, its limited admissions and its trial-silence warning did not waive protection concerning expressly reserved quantity.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Estelle preserves protection against sentencing incrimination. Brown prevents a voluntary witness from disclosing a subject selectively while refusing relevant cross-examination; it does not make a plea's limited factual inquiry consent to compelled testimony on unresolved quantity. Rule 11 permits inquiry into a plea's factual basis, and refusal to supply necessary information may leave that basis inadequate. Here the court accepted the plea and reserved quantity. Neither awareness of possible punishment nor admission of the offense establishes the missing waiver. The plea and actual voluntary admissions remain usable. When sentence is fixed, conviction final and further testimony presents no remaining adverse criminal consequence, the privilege has no continuing incriminating risk to protect; finality alone does not extinguish another genuine criminal exposure. Immunity-backed testimony is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Estelle's sentencing protection and Brown's scope-based testimonial-waiver rule are applied without making every plea statement inadmissible or establishing a permanent privilege.

##### II. Protected silence cannot prove the crime's circumstances at sentencing

**Holding and operative rule:** A sentencing court may not draw an adverse factual inference from a defendant's protected silence in determining the circumstances and details of the crime. The Government must establish disputed quantity through lawful affirmative evidence; permission for judicial sentencing findings does not authorize this source of proof.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six Justices including Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Griffin prevents using protected silence to supply criminal proof, and Estelle preserves the privilege when punishment remains unresolved. Their protection cannot depend on whether admitted guilt precedes determination of the crime's remaining punitive facts. Baxter's civil inference and Woodard's discretionary-clemency setting differ from this initial criminal sentencing determination. Witte and Watts permit certain sentencing information within lawful authority but retain independent constitutional limits; Dunnigan permits consequences for established willful material perjury, not an inference from giving no testimony. This holding concerns the source of proof, not whether every sentencing fact is an element or must go to a jury. It does not bar voluntary statements. Remorse and actual acceptance-of-responsibility claims remain undecided. The ruling does not prevent denial of an affirmative acceptance reduction for failure to carry a lawful burden; it creates no new burden or entitlement and decides no particular reduction ruling.

**Precedent treatment:** Griffin's no-inference protection is applied to proof of crime facts at criminal sentencing; Estelle supplies continuing protection. Baxter and Woodard are distinguished by their civil settings. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan retain their stated lawful operations.

##### III. Other potentially sufficient evidence does not establish harmlessness here

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that this constitutional error did not contribute to the affected sentencing determination. Because the judge expressly relied on silence in crediting disputed quantity testimony, the finding cannot be preserved merely because the remaining evidence could support it; the sentence must be vacated and quantity reconsidered without that inference.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same six Justices as Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman asks whether error contributed, not whether an appellate court could independently assemble sufficient evidence. The judge used silence to support the disputed testimony and indicated that a contrary privilege ruling could warrant closer examination. No independent conclusive quantity determination unaffected by that use is established. Wright independently requires a lawful sentencing basis established in the sentencing findings rather than a substituted appellate ground; its Guidelines holding is not converted into a universal constitutional rule. The District Court must reassess quantity from affirmative evidence admissible under the governing sentencing standards. The Court neither declares the witnesses unreliable nor guarantees a lower sentence. The pleas and admitted offenses remain undisturbed, and any lawful consequential sentencing adjustments remain for remand.

**Precedent treatment:** Chapman supplies the contribution-based constitutional harmlessness standard. Wright's existing lawful-basis requirement is preserved and applied at its actual sentencing scope.

#### Precedent Treatment

Estelle and Brown govern continuing privilege and actual waiver. Griffin now forbids adverse use of protected silence to determine crime facts at sentencing; its established trial operation remains. Baxter and Woodard do not authorize the present criminal inference. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan preserve lawful sentencing findings and consequences without removing independent constitutional safeguards. ### INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre; No. 97-1754; judgment below, 121 F.3d 521. — 1999-05-03

#### Holdings

##### Courts must respect the Board's permissible construction within actual delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§1103(a)(1) and former 1253(h), together with the Board's adjudicative assignment in 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), courts must respect a permissible Board construction of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception after independently enforcing enacted limits. Interpretation, application and factual review retain their distinct requirements; neither agency status nor a general appeal to foreign-affairs expertise supplies automatic correctness.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II are joined by all nine Justices and control this holding and the next two merits holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Haggar requires authority before deference and distinguishes a lawful interpretive choice from particular validity and application. The present statute and Board assignment establish authority independently of Haggar's tariff rulemaking setting. NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine ambiguity; MCI and NCUA prohibit erasing statutory conditions. The exception concerns serious reasons for considering that the applicant committed a serious nonpolitical crime outside the United States before arriving here. No criminal conviction is required, but the territorial, temporal and substantive conditions remain operative. The humanitarian importance of withholding supports careful enforcement of those limits, not an exclusively judicial factor code. Cardoza-Fonseca and Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; Elias-Zacarias preserves the separate standard for reviewing supported findings. The Board must apply its announced lawful construction to the actual record and explain its decision. The Court adopts neither unconditional deference nor an automatic new agency hearing whenever judicial review used a mistaken premise.

##### A properly established crime exception requires no additional persecution-risk balance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly established, former §1253(h)(2) makes the paragraph (1) withholding protection inapplicable without an additional balancing of feared persecution against offense seriousness. The Board may address that exception while assuming the applicant could show that life or freedom would be threatened because of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute separately states the protection and its exceptions. A greater threatened harm does not dissolve a properly established crime exclusion through an additional condition Congress did not supply. The Handbook paragraph 156 argument relied on below presents a serious humanitarian objection, but does not change those distinct statutory inquiries. The Court finds no absence of persecution risk and creates no general discretion to deny withholding to an otherwise eligible applicant. Relevant political circumstances remain material under the lawful crime analysis. Discretionary asylum is a different question and receives no automatic disposition from this withholding rule.

##### The political-criminal comparison does not require every judicially preferred subsidiary finding

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may compare an offense's political character with its ordinary criminal character and consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct in determining which predominates. The statute requires neither a separate atrocity finding in every case nor findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving its political objective; the Board must still assess actual political purpose, context and conduct under its announced standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Political motive alone does not establish a political offense, just as unlawfulness alone does not make one nonpolitical. The Board's explanation considered protest objectives alongside property destruction and civilian assaults and found the criminal character disproportionate. The Court reviews that actual explanation rather than adopting new factual findings from the contested testimony. The Ninth Circuit’s comparison with more severe conduct is a consistency objection, not necessarily a universal atrocity prerequisite. The Board’s actual disproportionate-conduct explanation answers that objection; the greater atrocity of another case is no minimum threshold for this alternative ground. Handbook paragraph 152 and the suggested McMullen comparison reinforce the importance of political context and disproportion, but the absence of a separate comparison with McMullen's atrocious acts does not defeat a determination resting on the supported political-criminal comparison. Atrocious means may defeat political characterization; atrocity is not necessary in every case. A clear causal relation to a political objective would not necessarily overcome a supported disproportion finding, and the Court reserves any independent causal-link requirement in a different case. Relevant comparisons and reasoned treatment of genuine inconsistency remain possible. No categorical rule about bus burnings, assault severity or unsuccessful protest is announced.

##### The late record-error contention remains for its existing agency procedure

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to decide the translation/transcription contention first raised here rather than before the Board or Court of Appeals; the applicant's existing BIA motion for a new hearing remains for determination through that lawful channel. If the BIA determines that modification of the record is necessary, it may determine whether the modified record warrants further consideration of withholding; no error, new hearing or outcome is presumed.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this component's disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged errors may be material if established, but this Court is not the first forum for resolving a contention not presented below. The existing motion supplies an actual avenue for that inquiry; its filing proves neither entitlement nor futility. The 1995 regulation's burden applies only if evidence indicates a withholding-denial ground; then the applicant must prove by a preponderance that the indicated ground does not apply. On the existing unrevised agency account, repeated gasoline-fueled bus burnings and civilian assaults, assessed against the political objective, indicate this exclusion and trigger that allocation. This uses conduct evidence, not silence, and makes no new credibility determination. The allocation is distinct from statutory serious reasons and judicial factual review. Failure to file a Board brief despite an extension is procedural history, not default, a concession, an adverse factual inference or proof that the conditional burden was unmet. The merits holdings rest independently on the lawful framework and stated Board explanation. A corrected record may affect their application without changing the statutory rule. The appellate remand itself commands no new Board hearing, stay or removal.

- Haggar preserves actual delegation, statutory boundaries and separate validity/application review; its tariff remand is not a universal agency-remand command.

### Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education; No. 97-843. — 1999-05-24

#### Holdings

##### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins establish this sufficient route.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination but leaves recipient responsibility to be established. Kinman's actual-notice route identifies a recipient's own deliberate disregard of a known educational danger. The Court extends that sufficient route to peer conduct where the school has substantial remedial control; it does not claim Kinman already decided peers. The school's knowing, culpable continuation of educational deprivation supplies the institutional wrong, answering the objection that a student cannot personally bind the funding recipient. Mere discipline authority or an ineffective result does not suffice. Actual notice must reach an official empowered to correct the danger; generic constructive notice, the harasser's own knowledge and technical policy noncompliance do not establish this route. A reasonable response is lawful even if harassment continues, and courts prescribe neither a selected punishment nor a victim's preferred remedy. Constitutional and statutory limits on discipline inform control and response. Gebser's school-delegated teacher authority does not transfer to classmates; its conjunctive teacher route and the other teacher holdings remain intact. The Court neither adopts ordinary negligence or general agency law nor rejects every distinct theory of direct official discrimination. Receipt of assistance remains separately necessary under NCAA v. Smith. Causal injury cannot be inferred solely from harassment predating legally sufficient recipient notice.

##### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, directly controls with five votes. Stone-Zsela does not join this disjunctive threshold; no combination of separate rationales is needed for its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Kinman adopted severe-or-pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced educational harassment without mandatory psychiatric injury, measurable grade loss or a rigid list of factors. Its reliance on Meritor and Harris preserves attention to welcome, objective and experienced abuse, and the actual setting. The peer relationship affects the contextual application and recipient-control inquiry; it does not silently replace the substantive standard with a requirement that conduct be both severe and pervasive. Age, relationship, educational setting and actual effect may matter without becoming a mandatory checklist. Neither physical exclusion nor total loss of schooling is invariably required, and no one proxy is sufficient by itself. Ordinary teasing or offense fails without the substantive and institutional requirements. No universal rule determines whether one incident always suffices or never can. These allegations involve repeated touching and propositions over months with educational interference, a materially different claimed environment. The Court decides no ultimate fact and does not treat every unwelcome interaction as actionable discrimination.

##### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support this allegation-stage application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged response concerns the district's own use of authority after reports of a known danger, rather than automatic responsibility for the classmate. The asserted repeated abuse and educational disruption permit the necessary elements to be tested. Allegations that reports reached the principal do not finally establish every official's corrective power, knowledge or opportunity; the criminal plea supplies no earlier notice or complete causal showing. The plaintiff must prove the institutional and substantive conditions, including postnotice causal injury. Existing compensation principles therefore require ordinary further adjudication, not immediate damages or judicial control of discipline. No compulsory trial follows if ordinary procedure establishes a missing element or valid defense. The decision reaches only the appealed district Title IX claim and leaves unrelated and individual claims outside the mandate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools is applied to available compensation for proved intentional discrimination, with recipient responsibility, causation and loss still distinct.
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District is extended at its sufficient actual-notice/culpable-disregard route to substantially controlled peer conduct. Its severe-or-pervasive contextual educational-harm standard remains; the peer extension is stated expressly.
- Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District retains the limited teacher-authority-plus-recipient-fault conjunction and its rejection of exclusivity in teacher cases. Neither delegated teacher power nor that complete route is imputed to peers.
- NCAA v. Smith preserves the distinct federal-assistance recipient requirement; economic connection or control alone does not establish receipt.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson informs the distinction between participation and welcome through Kinman without importing employment attribution wholesale.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems informs the contextual objective-and-experienced abuse inquiry through Kinman, without mandatory psychiatric injury or any indispensable factor.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman retains its funding-notice principle; the controlling recipient-own-wrong route concerns intentional educational discrimination, not automatic liability for a third party.

#### Law After Decision

A funding recipient's own actually informed, deliberately unreasonable response to peer harassment within substantial control can support damages when all independent discrimination, educational-harm and causal-injury elements are established. The controlling environment threshold remains severe or pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced as abusive, with impaired educational access or benefit. Teacher-attribution rules remain separately bounded. Ordinary negligence, policy breach, unsuccessful reasonable correction and peer attendance alone do not establish liability. The Chief's separate reporting-failure extension does not become a controlling peer rule, and no complete alternative-attribution or defense code is decided.

They accept Griffin and Carter at their established guilt-phase scope and decline the sentencing extension. Baxter and Woodard illustrate the distinction between inference, compelled answers and an automatic penalty for invocation; sentencing's different evidentiary rules, recognized in McMillan, remain relevant. They dispute the majority's separation of offense circumstances from other sentencing information. Their position permits neither compulsory testimony, treating the plea as an admission of reserved quantity, an automatic privilege penalty nor proof by silence alone. Scalia retains his broader Jones penalty-range position without deciding a new quantity-classification claim here. They find no error and therefore do not reach harmlessness as a ground for affirmance. Conditional acceptance of lawful relief under the Court's contrary rule adds no vote for reversal.

**Thomas.** Thomas joins Scalia's opinion but separately questions the constitutional foundations of Griffin and Carter: an ordinary inference does not itself compel testimony, and a statutory no-comment policy does not necessarily establish a constitutional prohibition. He would consider reconsidering those decisions in an appropriate case. He casts no vote to overrule them here, and no other Justice joins this prospective recommendation. Scalia instead preserves their established operation.

### Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education; No. 97-843. — 1999-05-24

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment:** The Chief joins the actual-notice recipient-fault route and the reversal on these allegations. The school is answerable for its own deliberately indifferent failure to use available authority after reliable notice, with meaningful control, clearly inadequate response, sex-based educational deprivation and causal injury independently proved. Kinman's sufficient notice route remains available, while Gebser's teacher authority cannot simply be attributed to a classmate. He would require severe, pervasive and objectively offensive peer harassment depriving the victim of equal educational access, and therefore withholds assent from the Court's severe-or-pervasive threshold. He additionally would recognize the recipient's willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causing continuation as a sufficient institutional ground. That proposed reporting-failure extension is his separate position, not the Court's peer rule. Neither misconduct nor negligent supervision alone suffices; officials retain disciplinary discretion so long as the response is not deliberately indifferent. He requires no particular punishment and makes no liability finding.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting:** They would affirm the appealed peer-harassment dismissal because Title IX's funding condition does not sufficiently establish this institutional attribution. Franklin makes compensation available after recipient discrimination is shown; it does not identify the recipient's wrong. Kinman concerned a teacher, and Gebser's additional route still requires materially enabling delegated teacher authority. A nonagent pupil does not have that link. These Justices address the Court's own-response theory directly: disciplinary power and knowing nonresponse to a student's discrimination do not, on their view, supply the necessary statutory duty or clear funding notice. Legal limits on school control and the breadth of the proposed obligation reinforce the objection. They neither find the allegations false nor approve the conduct. A supported claim that school officials themselves enforce rules differently because of sex remains distinct; they decide no such new claim. Existing teacher law and remedies for established recipient discrimination remain intact.
