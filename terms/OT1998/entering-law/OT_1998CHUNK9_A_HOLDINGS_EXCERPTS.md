#### Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6352.  
**Decided:** December 3, 1991.

##### Whether factual insufficiency on one lawful conspiracy object requires reversal of a general verdict supported on another object

**Holding:** A general guilty verdict on a federal multiple-object conspiracy need not be set aside merely because the evidence is inadequate for one legally valid object when another object is supported sufficiently under the unchanged beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. This rule does not govern a legally invalid object, failure of proof on every route, an element-diluting instruction, or a properly presented independent unanimity defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined on the proposition by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; seven of eight participants. Blackmun's judgment-only concurrence is not counted as a join in this reasoning.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the multiple-object conspiracy verdict. *Sochor v. Florida*, decided June 8, 1992, extends the factual-versus-legal distinction only to rejecting an inference of reliance on a legally available but factually unsupported aggravator from a general Florida advisory death recommendation; it does not settle broader capital-jury error rules.

**Material application:** Both alleged §371 conspiracy objects were legally permissible. The Government conceded insufficient evidence connecting Griffin to the DEA object, but the IRS object had sufficient evidentiary support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The excluded legal-invalidity, all-routes-insufficiency, elemental-instruction, and independent unanimity questions remain unresolved. Preferred withdrawal of an unsupported route on timely request creates no independent constitutional reversal rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is affirmed; the supported IRS object sustains the conviction against this challenge. No retrial or acquittal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Turner v. United States is applied to factual insufficiency. Stromberg v. California and Yates v. United States are distinguished and preserved for legally forbidden verdict grounds. In re Winship preserves proof beyond reasonable doubt, and Jackson v. Virginia preserves the rational-factfinder sufficiency inquiry.

**Later-authority backlinks:** *Sochor v. Florida*, 504 U.S. 527 (1992), No. 91-5843, decided June 8, 1992, question concerning inferred jury reliance on the coldness aggravator; see Criminal Procedure, the Sochor entry.

#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to this preserved Doyle claim with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman here; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. Exhaustion, preservation, other habeas gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: extends Chapman review to the preserved personal-intent claim arising from the combined instructions and prosecutorial argument; the State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden if a violation is found. The Doyle holding remains unchanged, and no general rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors is adopted. See Criminal Procedure — Constitutional trial error on habeas.

#### Sullivan v. Louisiana, 508 U.S. 275 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5129.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993, No. 92-5129, see Federal Courts — Finality of a completed conviction ruling.

##### A charge vitiating the reasonable-doubt standard leaves no valid verdict for harmless review

**Holding:** When an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt charge vitiates the jury’s governing standard for all factual findings, the defendant has received no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. That structural defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate court’s hypothetical verdict cannot substitute for the required jury determination, and this rule does not classify every instructional mistake as structural.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Cage defect is conceded. Different instructional errors, evidentiary sufficiency and collateral-review gates remain unresolved; the distinct penalty-phase relief is not reviewed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment and remand for proceedings permitting a new trial under a valid reasonable-doubt charge. No acquittal; prior penalty-phase relief survives and the original death sentence is not reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358: proof-beyond-reasonable-doubt requirement applied.
- Duncan v. Louisiana, 391 U.S. 145: jury’s constitutional role applied.
- Cage v. Louisiana, 498 U.S. 39: conceded defect taken as the predicate, not relitigated.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18: actual-verdict inquiry preserved; no substitute hypothetical verdict permitted.
- Rose v. Clark, 478 U.S. 570: separable presumption error distinguished.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: structural-defect category applied.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991: contested-charge framework preserved; no reopening of the conceded defect.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: preserved-Doyle habeas rule unchanged; not a Kotteakos constitutional-habeas rule.

#### O'Neal v. McAninch

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7407.
**Decided:** February 21, 1995.

##### Chapman governs the preserved personal-intent instructional claim

**Holding:** For a preserved constitutional trial violation, properly available on federal habeas, in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor's statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant's own intent required for the offense, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict. Kotteakos's substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt framework does not govern this claim; the standard for other reserved categories is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. The broader Part III extension has no controlling coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: extended from preserved Doyle error to the preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category; its original rule survives, while other reserved categories remain unresolved by the Court.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied with the State's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden and actual-verdict contribution inquiry intact.
- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): not extended to replace Chapman for this constitutional claim; its existing operation for genuinely nonconstitutional trial error remains unchanged.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 (1970): applied as the constitutional requirement that the prosecution prove the elements of the charged offense; no new personal-intent element is imposed on offenses that do not require it.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993: preserved as the distinct rule for a defect vitiating the jury's governing reasonable-doubt standard; the assumed error here is not declared structural.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt a general Chapman rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors. Proof of the violation, preservation, exhaustion, independent habeas gates, structural-error rules and a prejudice element defining another constitutional claim remain separate. Actual harmlessness is not decided.

**Later-authority backlinks:** California v. Roy, November 4, 1996, No. 95-2025: extends the State-borne Chapman rule to a preserved personal-intent omission without accompanying prosecutorial argument, while leaving a general extension to all constitutional trial errors unadopted.

##### The proper standard requires renewed review, not an automatic writ

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's substantial-influence assessment with a prisoner prejudice burden does not supply the required Chapman determination; its challenged judgment must be vacated for review of the combined claim under the correct rule. If an available constitutional violation is established and the State fails to prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the existing record that it did not contribute to the verdict, the writ shall issue for the affected conviction or convictions unless the State retries O'Neal within a reasonable time; no immediate writ or present retrial deadline is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. The broader Part III extension has no controlling coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: its whole-record remand and conditional-relief distinction are extended to this instructional claim, without automatic reinstatement of a prior writ.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied to the existing trial record; a weaker substantial-influence finding is not treated as the required finding beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991, and Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California, March 22, 1994: their whole-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry remains the rule for identifying an ambiguous instruction's constitutional defect; the Court does not decide that antecedent application here.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: its gateway for otherwise barred claims remains independent; neither passage through that gateway nor an innocence showing is invented for this claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is vacated and remanded, 7–2, with Chapman directions supported by five Justices. Review concerns the combined personal-intent instructional/argument claim, with District Court proceedings as appropriate. A lawful no-error ruling or a proper assumed-error/Chapman-harmlessness ruling permits denial of the writ. Otherwise the conditional writ issues for the affected convictions unless the State retries within a reasonable time; no present deadline, immediate release or reinstatement of the earlier conditional order is directed.

#### California v. Roy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2025.

**Decided:** November 4, 1996.

For the other questions in California v. Roy, No. 95-2025, decided November 4, 1996, see Federal Courts - Party status and questions presented (The existing official appellate party may seek review).

##### This personal-intent omission permits harmless-error review under Chapman

**Holding:** The preserved omission of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery is a constitutional trial error subject to harmlessness review, not an error requiring automatic reversal merely because an element was omitted. For this personal-intent violation properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict; neither collateral posture nor absence of accompanying prosecutorial argument substitutes a weaker burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Knowledge and affirmative assistance do not equal purpose to facilitate robbery. The rule does not extend Chapman to every constitutional habeas claim, revive resolved exhaustion issues, or decide AEDPA's temporal reach.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gaudin's jury-allocation rule is applied and its structural-error reservation respected; Sullivan's rule remains intact at its stated scope; O'Neal's protected personal-intent category applies to the omission without accompanying argument; Chapman's actual-contribution burden remains the governing burden.

##### Necessary equivalence is sufficient, but not the exclusive permissible inquiry

**Holding:** Under Chapman, the State may establish that this omission did not contribute to the actual verdict through supported whole-record inferences as well as findings necessarily embracing the omitted purpose; it may not substitute evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction, equate knowledge plus assistance with purpose, or infer purpose from general mental capacity. Under Yates, the court must examine whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting the proposed inference; if the omission kept the relevant personal-purpose question from the jury so that the inference cannot reliably address its actual verdict, strong evidence alone does not carry the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Review concerns the actual verdict rather than the sufficiency of a hypothetical correct conviction. A reliable inference must account for the omitted question's effect on what the jury considered; disputed witness accounts and related verdicts are not converted into new jury findings. No present harmlessness finding is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman and Yates are applied together to permit a supported actual-effect inquiry while preserving the jury's role; neither sufficiency alone nor a hypothetical verdict satisfies them. O'Neal's whole-record command remains subject to that actual-verdict requirement.

##### The limited remand preserves the record, argument and remedial boundaries

**Holding:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is vacated for a qualified Chapman review of the preserved personal-intent claim affecting the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions, with District Court proceedings if otherwise proper; no present harmlessness finding follows. If an available violation is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, conditional relief must allow the State a reasonable period to retry those convictions; proper harmlessness permits denial, and neither this remand nor the special-circumstance argument restores reversed punishment, affects the Clark conviction or orders immediate release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela separately supports the Chapman remand and reasonable-retrial remedy without joining Part III.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The conditional nonkiller intentional-aid instruction and the robbery-related purpose of a killing do not automatically establish Roy's personal aiding purpose; nor are the special-circumstance findings categorically unavailable. Genuine preservation and independent habeas gates remain. The reversed special-circumstance punishment and Clark conviction are outside the remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yee preserves the distinction between a new argument and a new claim without compelling disputed first-instance application; O'Neal supplies the conditional-relief and reasonable-retrial rule, without automatic release.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 6-3: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support the disposition; Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg would affirm. Part III's five-Justice rationale controls without treating Stone-Zsela's separate remand vote as a sixth opinion join. No present harmlessness finding, immediate release, new stay or fixed retrial deadline is ordered.

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt to facts establishing guilt.
- Sinclair v. United States: overruled to the extent it assigns a criminal pertinency element to the judge; unrelated propositions are not displaced.
- Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions. Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.

#### Rutledge v. United States, 517 U.S. 292

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-8769.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### The CCE concert element includes the same narcotics agreement

**Holding:** Section 848(c)'s requirement of acting in concert with five or more others requires mutual agreement in a common criminal plan or enterprise. In this narcotics setting, §846 conspiracy is a lesser included offense of CCE, and the same agreement supports both challenged counts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all seven other Associates: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins only the charged-and-tried application in which this CCE verdict necessarily established every element of this conspiracy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** CCE adds the continuing series, five-other-person, managerial and substantial-income-or-resource elements. Unwitting participants do not satisfy the agreement requirement; individual agreements with necessary participants may suffice without a universally prescribed organizational form. Subchapter-II-only enterprises are not brought within §846's subchapter-I coverage. Distinct agreements, different offenses, substantive predicates and successive prosecutions require their own analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shabani supplies knowing agreement beyond reasonable doubt without an overt-act requirement. Jeffers's formerly assumed included-offense premise is resolved. Blockburger compares elements rather than shared evidence.

##### Congress did not authorize both convictions and punishments for the included agreement

**Holding:** Congress did not authorize cumulative convictions and punishment under §§846 and 848 for the included agreement here. Concurrent imprisonment does not cure the unauthorized second conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela also joins Ball's second-conviction and concurrency propositions and the invalidity of these two judgments on his narrower charged-and-tried basis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hunter supplies the single-prosecution authorization inquiry; Blockburger and Whalen supply the elements comparison and presumption against cumulation. Jeffers's no-cumulative-punishment conclusion applies, while retention of its unreviewed earlier judgment establishes no contrary dual-conviction rule. Ball makes the second conviction consequential despite concurrency. Garrett and Felix preserve the distinct substantive-predicate and agreement/substantive-offense rules. Harris and Bishop/Stokes concern a different pairing expressly authorized by §924(c). Witte's authorized relevant-conduct sentencing is distinct from another conviction, and Dixon's retained Grady rule is unchanged. No independent constitutional punishment rule beyond congressional authorization is adopted.

##### The automatic assessment may be considered as a preserved dual-judgment consequence

**Holding:** The mandatory $50 assessment on the second felony conviction is actual additional punishment and may be considered in the preserved challenge to both judgments even though not separately challenged below. Vacatur must remove the selected count's assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins actual additional punishment and removal of the assessment, but not the separate presentation ruling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The automatic consequence answers the claim of no additional punishment; it creates no general preservation exception, freestanding refund claim or finding about payment, collection or expiration. Ball makes speculative collateral-consequence prediction unnecessary given the concrete assessment.

##### The District Court may choose which duplicate conviction to vacate

**Holding:** The District Court must vacate either Count 1 or Count 2, its entire corresponding life sentence and its $50 assessment, initially choosing within lawful discretion. Vacating only a sentence, staying its execution or combining labels does not remove the unauthorized second judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins required removal of one judgment, sentence and assessment without reopening Counts 3–6, but would require conspiracy-only vacatur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the dual-conviction ruling, 9–0; either-count discretion commands eight votes. The assessment total falls from $300 to $250. The surviving challenged count and Counts 3–6, including Count 3's independent life term, remain intact. No release, acquittal, trial, general resentencing or reopening of unrelated objections follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ball requires actual conviction vacatur and permits initial sentencing-court selection. Rutledge himself requested either-count relief; the Government's CCE preference does not compel a conspiracy-only mandate.

##### Qualified later entry of a valid lesser offense does not justify two present convictions

**Holding:** In an appropriate case, an appellate court may direct entry of judgment on a valid lesser included offense when reversal rests on a defect affecting only the greater offense. That corrective authority supplies no entitlement to retain two convictions now and no automatic later reinstatement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part V, joined by all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A defect tainting the agreement or lesser verdict cannot be ignored. Exact future procedural vehicle, limits, contested reinstatement, retrial, compelled lesser judgment and harmlessness remain open. Duplication vacatur is not an evidentiary acquittal. Earlier separate conspiracy prosecution, previously unknown CCE facts and a noncoterminous enterprise are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Section 2106 supplies corrective appellate power. Tinder illustrates bounded sentence correction while retaining the conviction, not revival of a vacated conspiracy. Ward's closer example included the defendant's reinstatement concession; that fact creates no universal consent requirement or irrelevance rule.

#### United States v. Wells

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1228.
**Decided:** February 26, 1997.

For the separate questions in United States v. Wells, No. 95-1228 decided February 26, 1997, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (The Court may review the statutory question actually decided below).

##### Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element

**Holding:** Section 1014 does not require an additional finding that a statement had a natural tendency or objective capacity to influence the institution's decision. The Government must still prove beyond a reasonable doubt the applicable conduct—knowingly making a false statement or report, or willfully overvaluing land, property or security—and the purpose of influencing an institution enumerated by the statute in a covered transaction.

The covered transactions are an application, advance, discount, purchase, purchase agreement, repurchase agreement, commitment or loan; a change or extension of one through renewal, deferment or otherwise; or acceptance, release or substitution of its security. The institutional requirement includes the FDIC-insured institutions relevant here; it is not displaced by calling a transaction financial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. Stevens dissents from this rule and vacatur.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purpose to influence is distinct from objective materiality and remains an element alongside the applicable knowing-false-statement or willful-overvaluation conduct and covered institution and transaction. Prosecutorial discretion, likely consequential lies and sentencing do not replace proof. No general materiality ruling for §1001 or every fraud statute follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The two-conviction appellate judgment is vacated 8–1. Part III's limits command all nine votes: acquittals cannot be reopened; remaining indictment, instruction, evidence, conspiracy and sentence grounds require their own availability and merits determinations. Neither a new trial, restored conviction or sentence nor harmlessness follows automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kay rejects actual-influence defenses within its predecessor setting; Williams preserves its conclusion that the checks were not factual assertions. Gaudin requires juries to decide actual offense elements but creates none. Roy distinguishes permissible error review from an established harmlessness result and does not automatically govern this different direct appeal.

#### Johnson v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-203.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the §1623 perjury offense and must be determined by the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; the judge's determination instead was constitutional error. Johnson's failure to object presents forfeiture under Rule 52(b), not an established intentional waiver extinguishing that error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, unanimous in Parts I and IV.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gaudin supplies the jury obligation for actual materiality elements and Griffith applies it on pending direct review. Wells's different §1014 construction does not eliminate §1623 materiality; Olano separates forfeiture from intentional waiver.

##### Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting

**Holding:** When the law at trial settled an issue against the defendant but controlling law changes before direct review ends, the error is plain if it is clear at appellate consideration. This resolves that intervening-law setting without deciding every case in which the law was unsettled at trial or dispensing with Rule 52(b)'s other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Scalia assumes plainness without joining.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's reservation about error first clarified on appeal is resolved for settled contrary trial law changed before direct review ends, without resolving initially unsettled law.

##### The independent discretionary condition does not warrant correction here

**Holding:** Even assuming an effect on substantial rights, this forfeited materiality error does not warrant correction under Rule 52(b) because, in the established circumstances, it did not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceedings. That conclusion rests on the clear materiality of the source-of-funds account in the particular investigation and the intervening change from settled trial law; it is neither a general overwhelming-evidence exception nor a substitute jury verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, unanimous in Parts I and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-rights impact is assumed; structural classification and ordinary third-step prejudice remain open. There is no innocence requirement or need for a second institutional wrong, no substitute element finding, and no universal overwhelming-evidence exception. Disputed materiality, less decisive proof or trial-strategy effects remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the conviction-sustaining judgment, 9–0, without a new trial, acquittal or sentence calculation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's fourth condition independently governs correction after forfeiture. Gaudin's classification reservation, Sullivan's vitiated-reasonable-doubt rule and Roy's distinct preserved personal-intent habeas inquiry remain intact.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Civil Rights — Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; Civil Rights — The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5015.
**Decided:** April 1, 1996.

##### General equitable disapproval of delay cannot replace the governing dismissal predicates

**Holding:** A first personal federal habeas petition satisfying jurisdictional and pleading requirements cannot be terminated solely for the reported delay and litigation purpose by an additional equitable sanction dispensing with habeas statutes and Rules. Delay may support dismissal under Rule 9 or another lawful source only when that source's predicates are satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2243's law-and-justice direction operates within the statutory scheme. Its initial writ-or-show-cause command excepts an application showing nonentitlement; Rule 4 requires prompt examination, summary dismissal with notice if nonentitlement plainly appears from the petition and exhibits, otherwise an answer or other appropriate action and service on respondent and the state attorney general. Neither requires a merits writ or evidentiary hearing.

Pre-AEDPA §2254 retains federal custody requirements and exhaustion, including absent or ineffective state-process exceptions. Rule 9(a) permits dismissal for filing delay prejudicing the State's ability to respond unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before prejudice arose; losing an execution date or generalized finality costs alone do not establish that prejudice, and no five-year presumption was enacted. Rule 9(b) separately requires repeated grounds after an actual prior merits determination or new grounds omitted through abuse. Failed next-friend captions alone establish neither successive status nor prior personal merits review.

The then-effective §2244(b) permission follows federal denial after a material factual evidentiary hearing or legal merits hearing. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated ground and no deliberate withholding or other abuse; new evidence is not necessarily a new ground, and absence of withholding does not eliminate every abuse. When both conditions hold and other barriers are absent, McCleskey requires consideration; clear, particular State abuse pleading precedes the prisoner's burden. No such subsequent-petition predicate is established here. Exhaustion, default, source-authorized sanctions and unpresented fraud or inherent-power issues are not erased or decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McCleskey's structured abuse conditions remain. Schlup and Sawyer retain their distinct innocence gateways without making innocence an entry condition for every first petition. Delo v. Stokes's successive-petition rule supplies no automatic first-petition merits or abuse determination.

##### Independent equitable stay discretion survives the rejection of an additional dismissal sanction

**Holding:** First-petition status does not exclude lawful independent consideration of State enforcement interests, unjustified delay and proved obstruction in deciding an execution stay. That discretion cannot sustain this appellate order on its erroneous premise that the petition could be dismissed through the additional sanction rejected above.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2251 permits the judge before whom habeas is pending to stay covered state proceedings before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal, involving the detained person and a matter in the habeas proceeding. Covered proceedings after a stay are void; without a stay they remain valid as if habeas were not pending. Filing alone suspends nothing. Serious claims and irreversibility alone do not compel late relief, and the reported conduct does not establish that required process is complete or a claim-specific disposition available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine set aside the appellate displacement of the district stay and require prompt district examination of the actual petition and proper State objections, with reasoned redetermination of necessary protection. Eight formally vacate and remand; Stone-Zsela uses reverse and remand with the same operative effect. The displaced district stay is restored subject to lawful modification or dissolution, without a separate Supreme Court stay, numerical term, indefinite protection, merits writ or compelled hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gomez remains an independent equitable rule even on an assumed open procedural route; its four prior petitions are not a minimum trigger. McFarland preserves denial for proved inexcusable disregard of a reasonable opportunity and obstruction while protecting actual statutory preparation. Neither creates an unsourced permanent dismissal.

##### Necessary protection must preserve genuinely required habeas process when lawful expedition cannot

**Holding:** If execution would prevent completion of process actually required by habeas law and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time, the court must provide interim protection necessary to make that identified opportunity meaningful. Mere filing, first-petition status and a capital sentence establish neither predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Kennedy joins only necessary opportunity, without deciding its extent here. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reserve the general proposition; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court must identify process still due. Rule 4 screening, lawful procedural dismissal and expedition remain; if they permit timely lawful disposition, no further protection follows. The rule guarantees neither every requested merits step nor an evidentiary hearing, and establishes no particular undisclosed claim or necessary period here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barefoot's meaningful-review principle is extended by analogy to genuinely required district process, without importing its initial-certified-appeal trigger. On an initial appeal with a certificate of probable cause, meaningful opportunity and an appellate merits decision remain required; if execution would moot review before considered disposition, a stay must issue, with adequate notice permitting expedited or simultaneous merits/stay disposition. Certification requires a substantial federal showing beyond nonfrivolity, not ultimate success or sentence severity alone. Successive-petition stays still require substantial grounds. Certiorari-stage protection separately requires reasonable probability of four votes to review, significant possibility of reversal and likely irreparable harm, adequate materials and prior resort to the court of appeals, whose action generally receives considerable weight. McFarland's actual-opportunity and obstruction limits and Gomez's independent equity remain intact.

#### Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-8836; associated application A-890.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether §2244(b)(3)(E) bars review of circuit authorization while preserving original habeas

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I-A, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 2241 still names the Court and its Justices; §2244(b)(3)(A) concerns advance authorization for district filing, and the amended transfer rule does not name this Court. Direct filing is procedurally original but constitutionally appellate; §1651 aids existing jurisdiction rather than creates it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unpresented certification, interlocutory and supervisory channels are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ordinary certiorari dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 9–0, without affirming the authorization judgment.

##### When the new authorization-review bar governs

**Holding:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine support the channel result; O’Connor’s Part I-B has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six. Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy use a distinct relevant-activity approach.

**Material application:** Landgraf’s provision-specific temporal sequence governs the majority reasoning; a procedural/jurisdictional label alone does not answer retroactivity. The May authorization and subsequent review proceedings began after April 24.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding determines every Title I provision’s temporal reach or Georgia’s chapter 154 qualification. Chapter 154’s express pending-case direction does not automatically govern other amendments.

##### What successive restrictions and discretionary conditions govern an original petition

**Holding:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** For statutory second/successive §2254 applications, (b)(1) dismisses previously presented claims. New claims under (b)(2) require either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both previously undiscoverable facts despite due diligence and facts which, if proved with the whole evidence, clearly and convincingly establish that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. A request to announce a new rule is not the first exception. Before district filing, (b)(3) requires circuit permission by a three-judge panel upon prima facie showing within thirty days; the determination permits no appeal, rehearing or certiorari. Under (b)(4), district dismissal is required unless the applicant actually establishes the conditions, despite authorization.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether (b)(1)–(2) bind this Court directly remains reserved; they inform the Court’s consideration. Rule 20 separately requires reasons for bypassing district court, exhaustion or satisfaction of §2254(b), exceptional circumstances and unavailability of adequate relief in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1) permits exhaustion, absence of available state corrective process or circumstances rendering it ineffective; (b)(2) permits, but does not require, denial on merits despite nonexhaustion; counsel’s express waiver is necessary under (b)(3). No exhaustion, process adequacy, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure fact is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Original relief denied, 9–0, on the exceptional-original-showing ground; no return, transfer or hearing is ordered.

##### Whether the channel and successive scheme suspend meaningful review or dictate an unlawful outcome here

**Holding:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The retained original route supplies actual independent claim examination and a sufficient traditional denial ground. Klein distinguishes changed law from a command to falsify facts or decide an unchanged rule in a prescribed manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No approval of every nominal forum, future exclusion of otherwise sufficient innocence claims, all AEDPA, §§2254(d)–(e), or unpresented supervisory restriction.

##### Whether the submitted medical evidence establishes the Schlup gateway

**Holding:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part IV-A, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela withholds this additional application.

**Material application:** Detailed new three-to-five-day estimates and physiological criticisms are considered with Whitaker’s independently favored earlier death estimate, his acknowledgment that the later interval was possible, and the disappearance, car, call and clothing evidence. The submission does not eliminate reasonable adverse assessment at Schlup’s probability threshold. No affidavit is declared false and other-offense evidence retains its motive/intent/state-of-mind limit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gateway passage would permit consideration of a trial claim, not prove it, establish statutory (b)(2)(B), or decide a distinct constitutional innocence entitlement.

##### Whether the separately pleaded constitutional innocence claim warrants exceptional original relief

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming a possible constitutional claim based on actual innocence, this considered submission does not establish exceptional circumstances warranting the Court's original habeas intervention. Original relief is denied without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence burden, rejecting every such constitutional claim, or requiring an additional return merely because new expert opinions are offered.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O’Connor’s Part IV-B, unanimous; independently sufficient original-writ ground.

**Material application:** Herrera supplies a particular-submission insufficiency holding, not one general constitutional innocence burden. The full medical dispute receives consideration; the conviction and circuit refusal alone do not replace examination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal freestanding-innocence threshold, categorical denial of every such right, presumed adequate Georgia remedy or mandatory further return from new expert opinions.

##### Whether A-890 warrants protection after claim-specific consideration is complete

**Holding:** Execution's irreversibility does not alone establish entitlement to a stay. Because the independently considered claims show no sufficiently substantial basis for relief through the available proceedings and no identified unfinished legally required process warrants protection, application A-890 is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part V, unanimous, in the reasoned combined threshold decision.

**Material application:** Bowersox’s substantial-ground assessment remains distinct from Lonchar’s protection of legally required process and McFarland’s counsel/preparation predicates. Neither the petition’s existence nor retained jurisdiction supplies relief by itself.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic lateness penalty, execution date, altered prior stay, exhaustion of all other possible avenues or execution legality determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pending stay request is denied, 9–0, after independent examination; no unfinished identified required process or sufficiently substantial relief basis remains.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Ex parte McCardle:** Applied to the specified withdrawn appellate route; no conclusion follows that every separate habeas source is repealed.
- **Ex parte Yerger and Ex parte Bollman:** Applied to the retained statutory habeas channel and its constitutional appellate character. They do not make issuance of an original writ automatic.
- **Landgraf and Rivers:** Their controlling provision-specific temporal inquiry remains in force; the application here concerns post-enactment authorization/review proceedings, not all pending habeas litigation.
- **McCleskey v. Zant:** Its recognition of lawful successive/abuse restrictions supports consideration of the new standards; it does not eliminate their separate textual requirements or settle their mandatory reach here.
- **United States v. Klein:** Its jurisdiction/rule-of-decision distinction is preserved; the present channel and standards do not direct a factual result under unchanged law.

#### Bousley v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8516.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct supporters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns the plea’s own informed validity, not every antecedent claim surrendered by a valid plea or a later disappointed expectation. Competence alone does not establish informed assent.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct supporters.

**Material application:** Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The district court has not determined factual innocence, and the Government does not assert a distinct waiver from failure to raise innocence below. The gateway precedes adjudication of constitutional invalidity and does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** The complete rule has the five direct supporters of Parts III and IV.A: O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela additionally joins Part III’s beyond-the-plea-record inquiry and IV.B’s use-only application and conditional remedy, but not IV.A’s affirmative burden concerning an actually forgone more-serious charge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This indictment charged use alone; no actually relinquished carrying or other identified more-serious charge is established. Carrying cannot be presumed to preserve this conviction. A successful gateway requires adjudication of plea validity, not automatic acquittal or vacatur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Seven Justices reverse the Eighth Circuit; the six joining Parts II–III and IV.B require an opportunity to establish factual innocence followed, if successful, by adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim. Stevens would vacate the firearm conviction immediately. The controlling mandate does not vacate that conviction now, affect the drug conviction, or require an oral hearing, release or dismissal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

#### Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-938.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Section 101’s new contractual coverage does not govern the earlier conduct

**Holding:** Section 101’s expansion of §1981’s making-and-enforcing-contracts coverage to include making, performance, modification, termination, and enjoyment of all benefits, privileges, terms and conditions of the contractual relationship does not govern conduct completed before November 21, 1991: it adds substantive legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. This provision-specific rule does not eliminate a claim already covered by old §1981 or decide a separate dispute over subsection (c)’s protection against nongovernmental discrimination and impairment under color of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; these five adopt the complete explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same temporal result through their separate relevant-activity and textual-command method.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, decided earlier April 26, 1994: applied to §101 independently of §102, preserving the provision-specific temporal inquiry and its five-Justice controlling explanation.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988), and Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to new substantive consequences for completed events; no constitutional bar against sufficiently directed civil retrospective legislation is adopted.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): distinguished in its collateral litigation-fee setting, not overruled.
- Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164 (1989): applied as the governing construction of the earlier text within its scope; an old-law enforcement claim is not extinguished by refusing the later expansion.

##### Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach

**Holding:** When Congress expands statutory liability in response to a judicial interpretation, its corrective purpose or use of a new definition does not alone make the amendment govern earlier conduct; sufficient temporal direction remains necessary under Landgraf, and Congress may supply that direction within constitutional limits. Judicial construction of the earlier statute remains distinct: Harper governs application of that construction to open cases, and a defendant need not prove actual reliance on a later-issued judicial decision before invoking the enacted amendment’s limited temporal reach.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Patterson: retained as the old-text construction relevant to the temporal question; Congress’s prospective expansion supersedes its narrower coverage for conduct governed by the amendment, without overruling it as the construction of earlier law.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): applied to distinguish judicial retroactivity from later legislation; the lower court’s selective Chevron Oil balancing is not adopted.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished because its express substitution of law for identified pending disputes supplied direction absent here.
- Frisbie v. Whitney, 9 Wall. 187 (1870), and Freeborn v. Smith, 2 Wall. 160 (1865): distinguished as instances of directed curative legislation; neither establishes automatic retrospective reach for a corrective amendment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress's prospective expansion supersedes Patterson's narrow coverage for conduct governed by the amendment while leaving it the construction of earlier text. The Court does not reconsider Patterson, endorse the lower court's whole-Act generalization or selective Chevron Oil balancing, or extinguish an old-scope enforcement claim. The distinct §1981(c) question remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit judgment is affirmed and the case remanded, 8–1, preserving the existing old-law contract-enforcement jury remand. That remand retains Lytle's protection against preclusion by later Title VII bench findings; it is not §102's new jury option. No discrimination finding, damages or dismissal of the preserved claim is ordered, and the unreviewed related matters are not reopened.

#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove that categorical ground without creating an individual right or changing the preserved conclusion that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The respective statutes apply to actions pending on October 20 and October 31, 1994 and those filed thereafter, including this action. Other established grounds governing private actions remain except the state-plan ground the legislation removes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Suter*'s actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights rulings and separate implied-action rulings remain, together with its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim. The statutory reference imports no broader state-plan immunity.

##### Whether a general prayer for relief eliminates Madrid's particular distribution allegation

**Holding:** Madrid's allegation of nondistribution under former §657(b)(1) requires its own inquiry despite the broad programwide prayer. Each actual claim must be matched to its provision, conduct, remedy, governing version, and operative dates; an obsolete or not-yet-effective duty cannot silently justify an injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former distribution language is more specific than the aggregate benchmark, but the Court decides no individual right, eligibility, payment fact, defendant, or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not award a separate $50 for every installment regardless of timing. The rule applies to covered approved-plan collections for fiscal years after September 30, 1976, subject to the distinct foster-care rules in subsection (d).

**Material application:** The foster-care sequence first reimburses current maintenance; qualifying excess up to ordered support goes to the placement agency for the child's interests; the remainder reimburses unrepaid earlier foster-care or family-assistance payments with appropriate federal reimbursement; any balance goes to the agency for the child. The agency may reserve its payments for later needs or make them available to the person providing daily care. No applicable exception or payment fact is established for Madrid.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remand must apply the following distinct statutory dates and qualifications, without inferring an Arizona implementation event or default:

- Pub. #### Kinman v. Omaha Public School District

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 95-2809; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** May 27, 1997.

##### Same-sex teacher harassment remains subject to every Title IX element

**Holding:** Same-sex teacher harassment may constitute discrimination because of sex under Title IX; the claimant must still prove sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, the required educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Sexual conduct alone does not establish those separate elements, and no opposite-sex comparator is invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No sexual-orientation coverage rule or universally required opposite-sex comparator is adopted; the reported evidence is not a final liability finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brown preserves causation because of sex. Oncale supplies the same-sex textual analogy without transferring Title VII's attribution rule.

##### Participation and later continuation do not settle welcome or educational harm

**Holding:** Voluntary participation and postgraduation continuation do not conclusively establish that earlier teacher advances were welcome. Actionable harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive; the full educational context controls without a psychiatric-injury, measurable-grade-loss or mandatory-factor requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The school's policy covering welcome conduct, off-campus or off-duty acts and two years after departure is not the federal test. Neither graduation nor continued participation erases a proved earlier violation or makes every later private act actionable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor distinguishes welcome from participation. Harris supplies contextual severity and pervasiveness without psychiatric injury, grade loss or an indispensable listed factor.

##### Recipient damages require actual corrective-official notice and culpable disregard

**Holding:** Damages against a funding recipient for teacher harassment require actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk, followed by deliberate and unreasonable disregard of the known danger causing injury or continuation. Constructive notice, the teacher's status or ordinary authority-assisted access, policy breach, and the wrongdoer's knowledge of her own misconduct alone do not satisfy that recipient standard; a reasonable response does not become unlawful merely because harm continues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part IIA, directly joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. This actual five-Justice proposition controls although four joiners oppose the remand; it is not a Marks synthesis.

**Current-force modification:** Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, No. 96-1866, June 22, 1998, removes this rule's exclusivity. Actual corrective-official notice followed by culpable disregard and causal injury remains a sufficient attribution route, and the other Title IX elements remain necessary. Gebser also establishes the limited school-authority-plus-recipient-fault route described in its attribution holding; no complete agency or negligence framework, automatic teacher-status liability or general awareness presumption becomes controlling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Stone-Zsela's additional circumstantial-awareness, obviousness and willful-avoidance formulations in Part IIB have no other joins and establish no Court inference or presumption. Section 1682's administrative notice, hearing, congressional-report and thirty-day provisions are not literal private-damages prerequisites. Administrative and injunctive standards remain distinct, and the recipient receives no personal qualified immunity.

**Material judgment:** The Title IX remand is retained, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. No shared application rationale supports that judgment: the latter four would use broader agency and negligence grounds, while O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would restore summary judgment under the adopted rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Title IX reversal and remand with instructions modified to the majority-supported recipient rule. Ordinary proof, causation and remedy remain; no liability, damages or mandatory trial is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin's compensation rule is preserved while its reserved recipient-attribution question is resolved. Andrade's Title VII agency and negligence rules do not automatically govern funding-recipient damages.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, No. 96-1866, June 22, 1998: nonexclusive actual-notice attribution and the narrower authority-plus-recipient-fault attribution route.

##### The particular supervisors' response does not establish deliberate disregard

**Holding:** Under Doe, personal supervisory liability requires the particular official's actual knowledge of abuse or a substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard despite protective authority and opportunity, and causal contribution. Even assuming sufficient awareness for Whitehouse and Mackiel separately, the supported response evidence does not establish deliberate disregard; their reviewed §1983 summary judgments are affirmed on the merits without a new immunity ruling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part IV, all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Material application:** Awareness is assumed separately for Whitehouse and Mackiel, without pooled knowledge. The interviews, attempted trace, testing, confrontation and later authentication and termination efforts are imperfect responses but do not establish knowing refusal to protect on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An investigation is not invariably sufficient; denials and polygraphs are not conclusive, and no confession or conclusive corroboration is demanded before protective action. A wrong-line trace allegation does not prove deliberate error. Later corroboration and later decisions do not supply earlier knowledge or conduct-date notice.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the personal §1983 summary judgments on constitutional merits. The Court makes no new qualified-immunity ruling and adopts no proposed replacement defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Doe controls personal knowledge, culpable disregard and causation; its distinct conduct-date immunity ruling is not constitutional approval. Harlow, Anderson and Elder remain unchanged.

##### The District lacks a proved policy, custom or attributable final decision

**Holding:** A school district is responsible under §1983 only for an unconstitutional policy or custom, or a decision attributable to an official with final policymaking authority, that causes the injury. This record establishes neither that policy/custom nor an attributable final decision; ordinary supervisory rank and the teacher's conduct do not supply the missing institutional link.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A broader pattern is not indispensable when an attributable final policymaker makes one decision; here that attribution predicate is unproved. The District receives no qualified immunity. Direct-teacher claims, the vacated default, unappealed Nebraska tort dismissal and damages receive no new disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the District's §1983 judgment on its separate institutional ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Monell requires the institution's own policy or attributable final decision and causal linkage. Nabozny preserves the distinction from individual wrongdoing and supplies no new selective-enforcement claim.

#### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1866.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive attribution route

**Holding:** A funding recipient's responsibility for a teacher's intentional discrimination cannot invariably be defeated solely because no official empowered to correct the harassment had actual notice. Kinman is limited insofar as it made that particular notice route exclusive; employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical regulatory breach, standing alone, still does not establish recipient liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this limited negative proposition. They do not adopt a common complete damages framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Limited insofar as actual notice to a corrective official was the exclusive recipient-attribution threshold. Its actual-notice, deliberate and unreasonable disregard and causal-injury route remains available, together with the other Title IX elements. Its rejection of automatic teacher-status attribution and a technical policy breach as sufficient by themselves remains. No earlier sole-Justice awareness inference becomes Court law merely through this decision.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as allowing appropriate compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. The present attribution holding does not establish a damages award or unintended-effects liability.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** The established private action remains intact; no new regulatory cause of action is recognized.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Its distinction between a directly shared negative proposition and unadopted complete affirmative frameworks supports keeping the exclusivity holding separate from the limited Marks analysis.

##### Authority materially enabling discrimination, combined with the recipient's causal fault, supplies a narrower attribution route

**Holding:** A recipient may be held responsible when school-conferred authority materially enables a teacher's intentional discrimination and the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This is a sufficient route for attributing the discrimination beyond the actual-corrective-official-notice route; it establishes neither the underlying discrimination nor causal loss, damages or the defeat of a supported defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has no other formal joins. Its stated attribution component supplies the narrower controlling ground under Marks v. United States and Cipollone's component-specific application. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently support attribution whenever actual delegated educational power materially aids intentional harassment, without Stone-Zsela's additional recipient-fault requirement. School-conferred authority must actually and materially enable the wrong; title or opportunity alone is insufficient under either ground. Every case meeting the narrower conjunction therefore meets that independently sufficient broader attribution route. Those judgment-supporting positions total five, without converting the four Justices into joins in Stone-Zsela's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authority, recipient-fault and causal-continuation conditions are conjunctive. The alternative involving reporting failure requires willful blindness and deliberate indifference, not constructive notice or negligence alone. The Court does not adopt ordinary agency liability as a complete general rule, the four-Justice negligence route or every Title VII attribution principle. Discrimination, institutional fault, causation, compensable injury, damages and defenses remain unresolved. Official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability and state-law remedies are outside this holding. The separate regulatory duties to adopt and publish prompt, equitable grievance procedures under 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b) and give nondiscrimination notice under §106.9(a) are not converted into strict private damages liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Marks v. United States:** Applied to the particular sufficient attribution ground, without treating every reason or remedy proposed by a judgment supporter as controlling.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group:** Its component-specific logical-subset inquiry supports the limited attribution holding despite the absence of whole-opinion joins; separate proof, defenses and damages questions remain distinct.
- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson, Andrade and Faragher:** Their employment-attribution principles are not transferred wholesale to Title IX. Faragher's unresolved effective-protection/nonuse defense is not resolved here.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its funding-notice principle remains; neither it nor Title IX's administrative enforcement sequence becomes a literal exhaustion requirement for this private action.

##### The appealed Title IX summary judgment is reversed for ordinary further proceedings

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this formal reversal and ordinary remand. Stone-Zsela alone adopts Part III's application of his recipient-fault reasoning to the disputed warning and reporting evidence; the other four support further proceedings on their separately stated grounds. Their judgment agreement is not a five-Justice finding of actual knowledge, deliberate disregard, willful blindness, negligence, material aid, causation or liability. The Court orders neither compensation nor a compulsory trial. The unappealed constitutional, personal and state-law claims are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the district on the appealed Title IX claim, and the case is remanded. The lower courts must determine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused it to continue, while preserving the actual-notice route and the remaining elements, ordinary procedures and defenses.

#### Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-896.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applied to discern congressional review allocation from the statutory scheme.
- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): distinguished because that scheme preserved supplemental judicial review; the presumption favoring review does not require every forum.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): distinguished because its collateral systemic challenge lacked meaningful review through individual proceedings; that protection remains.
- Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Inc., December 3, 1991: its scheme-specific channel rule supports the analysis, without extending its express bar to the Mine Act by assumption.
- Darby v. Cisneros, June 21, 1993: preserved; limits on judicially added APA exhaustion do not displace Congress's allocation of original review.

##### The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908): distinguished on practical access; its protection against a penalty structure effectively foreclosing judicial review remains intact.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): its requirement of meaningful practical review is preserved and satisfied on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed; the district-court injunction remains unavailable. The representation/posting challenge must use the authorized Commission and appellate process. No labor-law merits determination, penalty assessment, guaranteed stay or new administrative outcome is ordered.

#### MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

#### INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1342.
**Decided:** January 22, 1992.

##### Whether politically motivated coercive recruitment necessarily establishes persecution on account of the recruit's political opinion

**Holding:** Coercive recruitment by a politically motivated organization does not necessarily constitute persecution on account of the recruit's political opinion; the protected reason for targeting the applicant must be supported by evidence concerning the particular case. Direct evidence of motive is unnecessary, but the group's general agenda and the mere fact of refusal do not establish that connection by a categorical presumption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this exact proposition. Stone's agreement in rejecting the categorical premise is not a join in the Court's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Circumstantial evidence may establish motive. Political neutrality is not categorically excluded. The Court assumes for purposes of its reasoning that an attributed opinion could qualify, without resolving every imputation issue; persecution because of the attribution still requires evidentiary support. Asylum's and withholding's distinct probability thresholds remain unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. 421 — asylum's well-founded-fear threshold remains distinct from withholding's higher probability requirement; neither removes the protected-ground nexus.
- *INS v. Stevic*, 467 U.S. 407 — withholding's distinct mandatory standard is preserved.
- *INS v. Abudu*, 485 U.S. 94 — distinct reopening grounds remain; the statutory nexus question is not replaced by discretionary denial of mandatory protection.

##### Whether the existing evidence permits displacement of the Board's nexus determination under §1105a(a)(4)

**Holding:** Under §1105a(a)(4), a court may overturn the Board's supported factual determination only when the record compels the contrary finding, not merely because that finding could be supported. Here the evidence does not compel a finding of feared persecution because of the applicant's political opinion, so the Ninth Circuit cannot displace the Board's denial on its stated nexus rationale.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the evidentiary conclusion and the operative restoration of the Board's denial. Stone does not join this holding or its remedy.

**Material application:** The applicant explained fear of government retaliation for joining, and renewed visits established continuing recruitment pressure without compelling the inference that pressure arose because of his political opinion. The Court makes no credibility finding and requires no direct proof of motive.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The nexus-based review here does not establish one review standard for every prima-facie determination or every reopening denial.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must implement reversal and leave the Board's denial effective without the additional nexus or reopening review proposed by Stone. No asylum or withholding is awarded.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices—Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—support reversal of the categorical disposition; Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor would affirm. Only White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support the existing-record resolution that leaves the Board's denial effective; Stone would require further review. The five-member coalition supplies the controlling remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *NLRB v. Columbian Enameling & Stamping Co.*, 306 U.S. 292 — applied for the distinction between evidence permitting a finding and evidence requiring displacement of the agency's finding.
- *INS v. Abudu*, 485 U.S. 94 — preserved; the Court does not expand this case's nexus review into a universal rule for all reopening determinations.
- *INS v. Doherty*, simulated January 15, 1992 — its dispositions created no new controlling reopening rationale and supply no contrary rule or merits finding here.

#### INS v. Doherty, 502 U.S. 314 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-925.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether the Second Circuit could require reopening of the asylum application

**Authority of the judgment:** The asylum reopening requirement is reversed, 8-0, by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. #### Reno v. Flores, 507 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-905.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### Statutory authority for the juvenile release regulation

**Holding:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits the Attorney General to use the family-custodian and discretionary-release arrangements in 8 C.F.R. §242.24 for temporary juvenile custody pending deportation proceedings; the regulation is not facially beyond that authority because it does not mandate release to every willing responsible adult. Custody remains subject to the statute's reasonable-dispatch limitation, its habeas remedy upon the requisite conclusive showing of unnecessary delay, the regulation's release avenues and the existing conditions agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six votes for this rule. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower statutory and facial-review ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carlson v. Landon, 342 U.S. 524 (1952): applies discretion bounded by the immigration statute; it does not authorize indefinite custody.
- National Center for Immigrants' Rights, December 16, 1991, decision: applies general immigration-release authority with preserved individual safeguards; does not enlarge that decision into universal detention authority.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): applies facial-review limits to the whole-regulation remedy.

##### The facial substantive-due-process challenge fails

**Holding:** The Constitution does not give a juvenile without an available parent, guardian or close adult relative a fundamental right to placement with a willing unrelated adult instead of temporary, nonpunitive government care under this regulation and the binding conditions agreement. On this facial record, the family preferences and other release avenues reasonably serve care and attendance interests; individual unconstitutional confinement and enforcement of the agreement remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. O'Connor and Souter also explain the substantial liberty interest in avoiding institutional confinement in a separate concurrence; Stone-Zsela does not join this formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the distinction between protective juvenile custody and punishment.
- Parham v. J. R., 442 U.S. 584 (1979): applies the special circumstances of juvenile care; does not extinguish a child's liberty interest.
- Youngberg v. Romeo, 457 U.S. 307 (1982): preserves constitutional limits on institutional care.
- Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992, decision: distinguishes psychiatric confinement after its predicate ends; no universal custody test is inferred.

##### Request-based review is not facially inadequate

**Holding:** The described examination, notice and opportunity to obtain immigration-judge review on request do not facially require replacement with an automatic hearing for every detained juvenile. That result depends on the available procedures and the absence of a record establishing general inability to invoke them; it does not approve ineffective notice, denial of requested review, improper delay or unlawful custody in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela agrees with setting aside the universal automatic-hearing remedy but reserves a distinct Mathews-based implementation inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fare v. Michael C., 442 U.S. 707 (1979): applies the recognition that juveniles can make a knowing waiver; no universal finding of comprehension follows.
- The Japanese Immigrant Case, 189 U.S. 86 (1903): applies aliens' entitlement to due process in deportation proceedings.

##### The challenged classifications do not establish facial unequal treatment

**Holding:** The regulation's distinction between juveniles with available family custodians and those without them survives the facial equal-protection objection because family availability bears a rational relation to safe placement and attendance. The separate federal juvenile-delinquency release scheme does not require identical release rules for immigration custody, and this ruling does not resolve unequal administration in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component and adds no ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the legitimate relevance of protective care in juvenile custody; no general exemption from equal protection follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b)(1) requires release in order of preference to a nondetained parent, legal guardian, or adult brother, sister, aunt, uncle or grandparent unless detention is required to secure timely appearance before the Service or immigration court, or ensure the juvenile's safety or that of others. If the only listed custodian is detained, (b)(2) provides discretionary case-by-case consideration of simultaneous release. Under (b)(3), a parent or guardian detained or outside the United States may designate another capable and willing person by sworn affidavit before an immigration or consular officer; that person must agree to care for the child and ensure presence at all future proceedings. Subsection (b)(4) permits other agreeing adult custodians only in unusual and compelling circumstances, at the district director's or chief patrol agent's discretion. These are distinct release avenues, not one undifferentiated exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1987 conditions agreement remains enforceable and is not a finding that each facility complies. Custody review under §1252(a)(1) permits habeas upon a conclusive showing of failure to proceed with reasonable dispatch warranted by the particular facts. For a juvenile not seeking voluntary departure, the described 24-hour process uses a different examiner unless no qualified alternative is available; English/Spanish notice allows review on request and withdrawal of waiver. No universal ruling on voluntary-departure safeguards, 30-day custody cap or approval of defective notice, ineffective assistance, delayed review or unconstitutional individual/systemic implementation follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial juvenile-release and automatic-hearing injunctions, 7–2 in judgment, and remand consistently with the six-Justice rationale. No particular child’s release is ordered; conditions enforcement and properly supported implementation challenges remain.

#### INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-938.

**Decided:** November 13, 1996.

For the other questions in INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang, No. 95-938, decided November 13, 1996, see Federal Courts - Immigration review transition (Review under the corrected 1996 transition provisions).

##### Eligibility does not exclude the continuing fraud pattern from discretion

**Holding:** Former INA §241(a)(1)(H), 8 U.S.C. §1251(a)(1)(H), permits the Attorney General to consider a continuing pattern of immigration fraud when choosing among eligible waiver applicants, even though particular acts are connected with entry. Eligibility does not erase that conduct or compel a waiver; the Court neither permits automatic denial to every eligible applicant because of the qualifying fraud nor decides whether the minimum qualifying fraud alone could sustain a particular individualized denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not reweigh the agency's balance. A granted waiver reaches entry-inadmissibility grounds directly resulting from the fraud; eligibility and this consequence remain distinct from discretionary selection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

#### Salazar-Haro v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 96-3007.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### The saved immigration proceeding remains subject to AEDPA’s separate bar and the actual-judgment protection

**Holding:** The saved former procedure governs this old deportation proceeding, subject to applicable transition exceptions, while §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not itself withdraw review of the completed-adjudication claims presented here. That conclusion preserves no exemption from AEDPA §440(a), whose separate temporal operation must be decided. Section 306(c)(2) preserves an actual judicial judgment or order entered under former INA §106 (8 U.S.C. §1105a), applying §106 as amended by AEDPA §440, against an asserted operation of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate that judgment or order or require its reconsideration. The Third Circuit's September 13, 1996 dismissal falls within that protection, which neither independently confers jurisdiction nor reopens merits review nor bars ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness under applicable law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the transition disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Corrected §309(c)(1) saves proceedings begun before April 1, 1997, subject to its exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only before the evidentiary hearing begins, with notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; this earlier order does not qualify. Section 242(g) retains its special cross-temporal operation over commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing orders, but does not itself bar these completed-adjudication claims.

**Material application:** The September 13, 1996 judicial dismissal applied former INA §106 as amended by AEDPA §440(a). Section 306(c)(2) protects that actual judgment from invalidation or required reconsideration attributable to §306(c)(1), without insulating its correctness from ordinary appellate review. Public Law 104-302 made §306(c)(1) subject to §309 with September 30 effect and left paragraph (2) unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yang supplies commencement; Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto supply the corrected transition construction and the distinction between specified executive action and completed legal adjudication. Their savings do not nullify AEDPA’s independent restriction.

##### Section 440(a) withdraws this pending direct-review channel without a new disability attached to a protected completed event

**Holding:** AEDPA §440(a) applies to this qualifying final deportation order despite the January 17, 1996 petition because withdrawal of future ordinary appellate review attaches no new legal disability to a protected completed event established by this record. Filing, prior conviction, family hardship and an expectation of favorable review alone establish no such disability; the agency had already considered discretionary waiver under the former law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg: six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A surrendered colorable defense is one possible protected completed-event predicate, not a universal prerequisite. Section 440(f) concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). The agency considered waiver under the former law; the separate operation of §440(d) eligibility restrictions is not decided. Pendency alone gives no blanket exemption, and a jurisdictional label gives no categorical answer.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the jurisdictional dismissal, unanimously as to that disposition, without adopting the Third Circuit’s hypothetical merits footnote. No waiver, reopening, new hearing, transfer, stay or execution direction follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers require a provision-specific completed-event inquiry. Hincapie-Nieto controls the absence of a protected choice based only on filing and expectation; Reyes-Hernandez is distinguished on its actual surrendered-defense predicate.

##### The discretionary-balance record does not decide the availability of an unfiled collateral action

**Holding:** This record does not require preservation of ordinary appellate review for another weighing of discretionary equities, and a constitutional label or §1651 alone does not restore that jurisdiction. Affirmance neither approves eliminating every meaningful judicial forum for an actually presented legal or constitutional defect nor decides the availability, scope or custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates of immigration review under §2241.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, adopted in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela additionally joins the conclusion that this record presents no distinct legal defect requiring discretionary reweighing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No custody status, collateral filing, adequate substitute or satisfaction of statutory predicates is presumed. The holding neither recognizes affirmative immigration §2241 jurisdiction nor declares every meaningful forum closed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker concerned an actual retained original channel, not an assumed immigration substitute. Flores preserves its custody and facial-review safeguards without deciding this collateral issue. The Japanese Immigrant Case supplies antecedent due process, not a right to judicial reweighing of every waiver denial. McCardle, Yerger and Heikkila retain their channel-specific significance. Section 1651 assists jurisdiction already present; it does not create it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kolster v. INS, June 23, 1997, First Circuit No. 96-1194, resolves the retained §2241 route for substantial legal and constitutional objections when its actual jurisdictional and procedural predicates are satisfied, including a developed legal-eligibility objection. It does not authorize reweighing discretionary equities.

#### Kolster v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit No. 96-1194; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### I. The saved proceeding remains subject to AEDPA's independent restriction

**Holding:** Corrected IIRIRA §§306(c)(1) and 309(c)(1) preserve the former review scheme for this proceeding begun before April 1, 1997, subject to their particular exceptions; §242(g) reaches old proceedings but does not itself withdraw review of this legal objection to a completed adjudication. Section 306(c)(2) protects an actual judicial judgment or order entered under former INA §106, applying that section as amended by AEDPA §440, against an operation of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate it or require reconsideration; that protection neither supplies jurisdiction, reopens merits review nor bars ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with this transition disposition without joining its reasoning.

**Material application:** Section 309(c)(2) permits election only before the evidentiary hearing starts and with at least thirty days' advance notice; (c)(3) allows termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; the January 30, 1996 order does not qualify. The October correction made §306(c)(1) subject to §309, retained §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach and left (c)(2) unchanged. The First Circuit's December 4, 1996 dismissal applied former §106 as amended by §440(a), bringing that actual judgment within (c)(2)'s protection from paragraph (1) disruption.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yang supplies the commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez, Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro distinguish executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases and execute orders from review of a completed adjudication. Salazar-Haro supplies the full protection for qualifying §106 judgments without barring ordinary correctness review.

##### II. Section 440(a) governs this ordinary petition

**Holding:** AEDPA §440(a) withdraws ordinary appellate review of Kolster's final deportation order based on the admitted drug and aggravated-felony grounds, independently supported by the conviction documents, notwithstanding his February 28, 1996 petition. On the established facts, application of the channel restriction imposes no supported new legal disability on his plea, concession or another protected completed event; this conclusion establishes neither a universal actual-reliance requirement nor a rule that pleas or eligibility for discretionary relief can never support nonretroactivity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the dismissal on a different temporal ground.

**Material application:** Deportability was admitted and independently documented; no colorable defense surrendered is identified. Even Kolster's preferred domicile calculation left his September 1992 plea three years short of seven-year eligibility. Those facts establish no protected lost option without findings about subjective motives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A jurisdictional label does not alone determine temporal reach. Section 440(f) times subsection (e), not (a); §440(d)'s substantive eligibility amendment remains undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers supply the provision-specific temporal inquiry. Reyes-Hernandez's actual lost-defense premise is absent, without making surrendered defenses the only protected completed events. Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro reject filing and expectations alone as a basis for preserving the channel. Hughes Aircraft's new private enforcement exposure is distinguished.

##### III. Independent habeas jurisdiction survives for substantial legal and constitutional objections

**Holding:** Section 440(a)'s replacement of a particular INA review channel does not clearly repeal the independent jurisdiction enacted in 28 U.S.C. §2241. When custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates are satisfied, that jurisdiction permits consideration of a substantial legal or constitutional claim that executive custody or deportation lacks lawful authority, including a claim that an unlawful eligibility rule prevented consideration of discretionary relief, and permits effective relief if the claim warrants it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained route must be capable of reaching the substantial authority objection and effective relief when warranted; detention-only review required to assume the disputed eligibility rule valid is insufficient to answer that objection. The Court does not determine Kolster's habeas predicates, make every statutory error constitutional, reopen discretionary weighing or factual review generally, or define all constitutional minima. A demonstrated failure of the retained route remains reserved; §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker's distinction between a withdrawn review channel and retained statutory habeas is extended to §2241 in this immigration setting. McCardle and Yerger support channel-specific repeal analysis; Heikkila distinguishes ordinary immigration review from habeas. The legal-eligibility objection resolves the reservation left in Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro at this conditional scope; Flores's custody safeguards do not themselves answer review of deportation authority.

##### IV. The direct petition ends without a decision on eligibility

**Holding:** The First Circuit's dismissal of the direct petition is affirmed. The Court neither decides the lawful-domicile calculation nor orders a waiver, agency reconsideration, conversion or transfer of the petition, collateral relief, a stay or execution of deportation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The ordinary petition remains dismissed, unanimously. The sentencing recommendation does not bind the immigration decisionmaker. Any collateral challenge must establish its own jurisdictional and procedural predicates before legal eligibility or requested relief is decided.
