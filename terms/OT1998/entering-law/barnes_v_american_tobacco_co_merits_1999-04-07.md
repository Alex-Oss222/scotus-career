## Event

Barnes v. American Tobacco Co., No. 98-1489. Merits decision, April 7, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, whose November 12, 1998 judgment, 161 F.3d 127, sustained denial or decertification of a proposed statewide smoker medical-monitoring class under Rule 23(b)(2). The question is whether that proposal may be certified despite individual issues of addiction, causation and limitations. The proposed Pennsylvania class also presents materially varied exposure, monitoring need and substantive defenses. Five named claims received separate limitations judgments; the sixth received a judgment for lack of a monitoring need. Those individual merits dispositions are distinct from the class-certification question before the Court.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Third Circuit is affirmed insofar as it sustains denial or decertification of this proposed Rule 23(b)(2) class.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed as to the reviewed certification denial, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The five limitations judgments and the sixth plaintiff's lack-of-monitoring-need judgment remain undisturbed. The Court does not separately affirm their substantive merits, announce a new limitations or medical-necessity rule, or revive any named claim. No classwide monitoring, compensation, mandatory subclasses or renewed certification is ordered. Genuinely individual, timely claims remain subject to ordinary procedures only insofar as those procedures are independently preserved and available.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine join the complete rule, record application, limits and remedy. No separate writing is issued.

## Holdings

### The proposed monitoring class does not satisfy the applicable classwide-relief requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 23(b)(2), together with Rule 23(a), requires a supported showing that generally applicable defendant conduct makes final injunctive or corresponding declaratory relief appropriate for the proposed class; common allegations of tobacco conduct and a shared request for monitoring do not establish that showing by themselves. This proposal fails because the materially different exposure, addiction-related, causation, monitoring-entitlement and limitations issues cannot be resolved by the proposed classwide ruling while preserving meaningful individual proof and substantive defenses; the Court imports neither Rule 23(b)(3)'s predominance and superiority requirements nor a categorical prohibition based on individual differences.

**Authority:** Breyer's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls the complete rule, application, limits and remedy. All nine participating Justices directly adopt it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the requirements of the class device actually requested. Falcon requires examination of the applicable prerequisites. Castano requires an actual account of governing law and proof, and Amchem confirms that the attractiveness of aggregate relief cannot replace Rule 23's conditions. Their Rule 23(b)(3) applications do not create a predominance or superiority test under (b)(2). Here a common product or marketing campaign does not establish that every smoker needs the same screening, presents the same risk or faces the same limitations response. The stated differences concern entitlement to monitoring and preservation of substantive defenses, not merely convenient administration of an already established common remedy. Calling differential personal medical treatment one injunction cannot itself answer those questions. The proposal therefore lacks the cohesion necessary for the classwide relief it seeks on this record. Proponents correctly maintain that individual differences do not invariably defeat a common monitoring program. That proposition does not cure these demonstrated entitlement problems. Nor would an unexplained appeal to predominance or management difficulty suffice to deny a different supported (b)(2) proposal. The Court adopts no additional universal indivisibility requirement beyond the actual rule, decides no new medical fact or Pennsylvania claim element, and does not design a replacement monitoring program.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Medical-monitoring classes are not categorically barred, and tobacco deception receives no immunity. Rule 23(a)'s requirements remain applicable, but the Court makes no independent adequacy-conflict holding drawn from Amchem's different settlement facts. It decides no separate named claim, individual standing question, new limitations rule or medical-necessity rule. The class ruling creates no assumed jurisdiction over individual substantive merits. A different proposal would require an available procedural route, appropriate actual representatives and satisfaction of every applicable prerequisite; neither its availability nor its result is decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- General Telephone Co. of the Southwest v. Falcon supplies rigorous attention to the actual Rule 23 prerequisites; the Court applies that inquiry without turning certification into a prediction of ultimate merits success.
- Castano v. American Tobacco Co., decided January 14, 1997, supplies the actual governing-law and proof inquiry and preserves its rejection of categorical novelty or size barriers. Its (b)(3) predominance and superiority rules remain confined to their proper setting; its unappealed (b)(2) denial did not decide this medical-monitoring question.
- Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, decided June 25, 1997, supplies the requirement to satisfy applicable Rule 23 conditions and preserve lawful representation. Its settlement-specific conflicts and (b)(3) predominance application are not adopted as Barnes facts or requirements. Its limited antecedent nonmerits sequencing does not permit assumed jurisdiction to decide individual substantive claims.
- In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer, decided February 26, 1997, preserves lawful substantive defenses. The Court does not extend its distinct jury constraints into a new Seventh Amendment holding about this proposed equitable relief.

## Law After Decision

Courts must connect the differences among proposed monitoring-class members to the actual Rule 23(a) or (b)(2) requirement at issue. This proposal fails (b)(2)'s classwide-relief requirement because the stated individual issues affect entitlement and defenses. Individual differences, medical monitoring, class size and the equitable label are not categorical substitutes for that inquiry. Rule 23(b)(3)'s distinct requirements and the substantive law governing the named claims remain unchanged. No precedent is overruled.

## Procedure After Action

Denial or decertification of this proposed class stands. The separate named-plaintiff judgments remain in their existing posture, without a new adjudication of their merits. The decision orders no monitoring program, compensation, discovery entitlement, new evidence, subclass structure, replacement representative or automatic renewed certification. Any individual proceeding or materially different class proposal must have an independently available procedural basis and satisfy its applicable substantive and representative requirements.
